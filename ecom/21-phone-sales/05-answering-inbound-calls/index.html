Answering inbound calls

Section 21.5 · 5 of 26 in this chapter · 2 min read

In short

  • A person must answer the phone, not a machine.
  • Most callers want to know: is this a real business, and is the product in stock?
  • Before you sell, ask what worries the caller. Then calm that worry.
  • Return calls quickly and make sure someone answers every call.

Greeting and first contact

  • Answer every incoming call with a person. "Incoming" means calls that come in to you. Do not use AI to answer sales calls. A person should answer, even if they do not know every product answer right away.
  • Use this phone greeting. Adjust the wording to match how formal your company is. Use the same structure when opening chats. Make sure the staff member knows the next question to ask after the greeting.
Hello, thank you for calling [company name], this is [your name]. How can I help you today?
  • Staff with little phone experience should practice this greeting until it feels natural.

Understanding what callers want

  • Callers usually want to confirm the business is legitimate. "Legitimate" means real and trustworthy.
  • Or they want to check if a product is in stock before ordering. "In stock" means available now.
  • So the phone line also answers "pre-purchase" questions. These are questions asked before buying.
  • Most incoming calls are about stock or shipping times. "Shipping time" means how long delivery takes.
  • The real question is usually whether the store can be trusted.
  • Most early calls are simple checks. For example: "is this a real business" or "is this product in stock".
  • Answers should reassure the caller that a real person will help if something goes wrong. "Reassure" means calm their doubts.

Building trust on the call

  • A phone number builds trust.
  • Calls often convert to sales. "Convert" means the caller decides to buy.
  • Ask first what the caller's specific concerns are.

Trust-concern script. This is a set of steps for worried callers:

  1. Ask what their specific concerns are.
  2. Respond: "We are an authorized retailer for [Brand Name]. We have been since [year]." An "authorized retailer" is a seller the brand has approved.
  3. Ask which product they're interested in.
  4. Say: "Our website is hosted on Shopify. All of your payment information is completely secure." "Hosted on Shopify" means your store runs on Shopify, a company that provides online store software.
  5. Offer: "You can also purchase directly on our website without speaking to us if you prefer. Or, if you ever have questions, you can reach me directly at [your email]."
  6. Your goal: build confidence by proving you are legitimate and authorized. Most callers are satisfied once someone picks up the phone.

Answering questions about physical locations

  • If a customer asks whether they can visit a store or pick up in person, answer like this. "Pick up in person" means collecting the item yourself.
We do not have physical locations. We ship directly from our suppliers' warehouses to customers, and those warehouses are not open to the public.

Handling missed calls and voicemail

  • "Voicemail" is the recorded message a caller leaves when nobody answers.
  • Always have someone answer calls to the store. That person should handle the common questions.
  • An unanswered call can mean a lost sale.
  • Check voicemail and return calls quickly.

Treating callers as real prospects

  • A "prospect" is a person who may buy.
  • Treat each inquiry as a real prospect for a salesperson.
  • Give them the full story. Do not cut the conversation short.
  • A prospect who has already shown interest is worth more effort than a "cold" one. A cold prospect has not shown interest yet.
