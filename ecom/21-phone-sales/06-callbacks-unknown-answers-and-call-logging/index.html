Callbacks, unknown answers and call logging

Section 21.6 · 6 of 26 in this chapter · 5 min read

In short

  • If you do not know an answer, do not guess. Say you will check and call back with the right facts.
  • Before the call ends, get the caller's name and phone number or email.
  • Call the supplier first. Email only if they do not answer.
  • Write down every call that did not end in a sale, with a follow-up date.
  • Put each new answer on the item's page in your shop, so the next visitor finds it.

Promising accurate callbacks instead of guessing

  • When you do not know a product detail, do not guess. Tell the customer you will check with the manufacturer team (the team at the company that makes the product) and call back with accurate data (correct facts).
  • Answer unknown technical questions by promising a callback with accurate data, not by inventing an answer. This keeps trust and lets you collect the caller's contact details for follow-up (a later contact to check in).
  • A correct answer given hours later is better than an instant guess or no answer. Callers accept a follow-up within about 12 hours when they receive a correct answer.

Collecting caller details

Three phrasings for the moment you cannot answer at once. Each is a complete script from a different year.

Source A:

Thank you for calling [Company Name], this is [Your Name]. That is a great question, and I want to make sure I give you the correct answer. Could I get your name, phone number and email address? I will get back to you with the answer as soon as possible. [Optional: I will follow up by [email/text] within [timeframe].]

Source A:

Thank you for calling, that is a great question and I do not have the answer right now. Let me contact our supplier and confirm the answer with you, and I will get back to you as soon as possible. Can I have your name, phone number and email address, please?

Source A:

Good question, [customer name]. I don't know the answer at the moment, so I'll consult our team and return your call as quickly as possible. May I have your phone number and email address?

Handling unknown product questions

Pre-sales product question (a question asked before the customer buys), 2024 procedure:

  1. Ask for the customer's name, phone number and email.
  2. Tell them you will check and call them back.
  3. Call the brand contact (the person at the product's maker) about the product detail.
  4. Call the customer back with the answer.
  5. Send the answer by email and add the new detail to the product page (the page on your store that sells one item).

Phone first, email second: call suppliers (the companies that send you the products) for an answer. Follow up by email only if they do not answer.

Phone or chat question (a question asked in a live chat window) you cannot answer, 2020 procedure (Source A, older advice):

  1. Take the caller's name and phone number or email address.
  2. Tell them you don't know yet, but will find the answer and follow up as soon as possible.
  3. Ask the supplier the question.
  4. Add the answer to the website, so future visitors also see it.
  5. Contact the lead (a person who may buy) with the answer and follow up for the sale.

Note: the 2020 version does not name a channel (a way of contact, such as phone or email) for asking the supplier. The 2024 version calls the brand contact first and uses email only as the fallback.

Stock questions

Stock question (a question about how many items you have) you are not sure about, 2022 procedure (Source A, older advice):

  1. Tell them you need to confirm with the warehouse (the building where items are stored) before answering.
  2. Take their name and phone number and write them down, even if the call drops (the line goes dead).
  3. Check inventory (the count of items in stock) through the weekly stock sheet (a list of stock updated every week), the dealer portal (the supplier's online page for resellers), or by contacting the supplier.
  4. Call the customer back with the answer as soon as possible.

Evening and gap-time callbacks

Older advice:

  • In most countries it is considered acceptable for businesses to call customers until about 8 p.m. Evening callbacks are therefore a practical option when daytime calls are not possible.
  • At the start you may only have about two or three callbacks to return each day. A single half-hour block in the evening is usually enough to clear them.
  • Short gaps such as lunch breaks, a few minutes away from work, or a commute (travel to and from work) can be used to make callbacks. Calling while commuting is possible even on public transport.
  • A callback in the evening often feels to the customer like extra attention, which can build trust and improve the chance of a sale. Unexpected out-of-hours service (help outside normal working hours) is a useful way to create a memorable experience.

Callback timing at a glance:

Option When to use it Source
Evening block, until about 8 p.m. When daytime calls are not possible; about 2-3 callbacks a day at the start, one half-hour block
Gap time: lunch, short breaks, commute To make short callbacks during the day
Within about 12 hours After you have a correct answer for the caller

Logging unclosed calls

  • Log every inbound sales call (a call the customer starts to you) that does not close (end with a sale). Write down the call date and a follow-up date. Many callers who promise to call back never do, so the follow-up must come from you.
  • A Google Sheet (a free online spreadsheet) with columns for call date, emailed or not, and next follow-up date is enough to track early leads without a CRM (customer software that stores contacts and notes). Older advice.

Daily follow-up review

  • Review the follow-up list every morning, either yourself or through a team member. Contact each lead on its scheduled date. This recovers many leads that would otherwise be lost. Older advice.
  • Check the sheet daily to see who needs a reply. Older advice.
  • When a prospect (a person who may buy) calls in response to an offer, they have already been persuaded enough to take action. Follow up the inquiry quickly while interest is fresh. Delay and forgetting reduce the value of each inquiry.

Feeding answers back into product pages

  • Use the answers you learn from customer calls to update the product pages on your store. Future visitors then get the information without calling.
  • Add the new detail to the product page as the last step of the 2024 procedure.
  • Add the answer to the website as step 4 of the 2020 procedure, so future visitors also see it. Older advice.
