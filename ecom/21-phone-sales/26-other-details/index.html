Other details

Section 21.26 · 26 of 26 in this chapter · 1 min read

In short

  • Plan your sales steps for 10 years ahead, including who sells and how people are contacted.
  • Check any outside advice against where your own shop really stands before you use it.
  • Offer the buyer the option that gives the most value for the least cost and effort.
  • Ask shipping companies for a better price by sending them a competing offer.

Planning the sales process

  • Describe the sales process (the steps from first contact to a sale) as it should look in 10 years.
    • Say who sells.
    • Say how customers and prospects (people who might buy) are contacted.
    • Say whether a sales team works on the phone.
  • For B2B niches (markets where you sell to other businesses), also define who handles:
    • Prospecting: finding new people who might buy.
    • Offline conversion: turning a contact into a sale outside the website, such as by phone or in person.

Checking outside advice against your store

  • Check advice from an outside source against where your store actually stands right now.
  • General courses boil information down into one version for everyone. A personalized consultant (a person hired to advise you) tailors it to one specific store.

Matching the offer to the buyer

  • Example: for parents selling exam-prep products (products that help students get ready for tests),
  • a cheap ebook (a digital book you read on a screen) is more practical than an expensive class.
  • The class needs travel, parking and time away from home.
  • For this buyer, the ebook is more cost-effective (it gives more value for the money).

Negotiating with freight companies

  • Use this email to ask a freight company (a company that moves goods by truck, ship or plane) for a better price.
Hello, I am comparing quotes for this order. The current quote I have from [OTHER CARRIER/BROKER] is [AMOUNT], and the quote on your platform is [AMOUNT]. Is there any better pricing you can offer for this shipment? Thank you, [YOUR NAME].
