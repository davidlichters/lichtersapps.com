Measuring phone sales and ad attribution

Section 21.24 · 24 of 26 in this chapter · 1 min read

In short

  • If half or more of your sales happen by phone, Google misses many sales and undervalues your ads.
  • Fix it by linking phone sales back to the ad clicks that caused them, or by lowering the Google target ROAS.
  • Formula: Google target ROAS = overall target ROAS × (1 − phone share). With 50% phone sales and a 10x target, use 5x.
  • Before blaming the ad, check every step from click to sale.

Measuring offline and phone sales

When half or more of sales happen over the phone, Google sees too few conversions. A conversion is a sale Google can count. So Google undervalues your campaigns. Offline sales are sales made outside the website, such as by phone. You have two choices. Either attribute offline sales back to the clicks that caused them. Attributing means matching a sale to the ad click that brought it. Or lower the Google target ROAS to account for offline revenue.

Adjusting the target ROAS for offline sales

ROAS means return on ad spend: the revenue you earn for each unit of money spent on ads. Target ROAS is the return you aim for. Lowering it here makes Google count the offline revenue your ads help create.

Formula

Google target ROAS = overall target ROAS × (1 − share of offline or phone sales)

The "share" is the part of sales made offline. The "(1 − share)" part is the part made online.

Worked example

  • Share of sales made over the phone: 50% (written as 0.5 in the formula)
  • Overall target ROAS: 10x
  • Google target ROAS: 10 × 0.5 = 5x

Trade-off

  • This method avoids CRM work. A CRM is your customer database, the tool that stores customer and sales records.
  • It requires a reasonable estimate of the offline share.

Diagnosing the funnel before blaming the creative

The funnel is the set of steps a buyer takes, from seeing the ad to buying. The creative is the ad itself: its image, video, or text. Add-to-cart means a shopper clicks to put a product in their basket.

  • Do not assume that a single poor result is caused only by the creative. Doing so leads to missed funnel problems.
  • Even with a weak creative, one add-to-cart from a large spend means something in the setup is also wrong.
