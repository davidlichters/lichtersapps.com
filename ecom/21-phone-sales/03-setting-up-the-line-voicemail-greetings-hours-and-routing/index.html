Setting up the line: voicemail, greetings, hours and routing

Section 21.3 · 3 of 26 in this chapter · 2 min read

In short

  • Calls can reach different people at different times of day.
  • Missed calls go to voicemail (recorded messages from callers) or to the owner's personal phone.
  • Staff must check voicemail regularly.
  • Staff must always say their first name and the business name when they answer.
  • Voicemail transcripts (text versions of messages) let you reply faster without listening to recordings.

Routing calls by time of day

  • Your phone number sends calls to different people at different times of day.
  • The times follow your business time zone (the clock region your company uses).

Overflow to voicemail or personal phone

  • Overflow means a call moves on to a backup when nobody answers.
  • Unanswered calls overflow automatically to voicemail or the owner's personal phone.
  • No manual switching is needed.
  • Staff must check voicemail regularly during their answering periods.

After-hours handling

  • After-hours means the times outside your advertised business hours.
  • Outside those hours, the phone line plays a prerecorded message (a message recorded in advance) and goes to voicemail.
  • Voicemail left after hours is checked in the next business period.
  • Or: outside advertised business hours, the call diverts (is sent on) to the owner's personal phone.

Staff answering rules

  • Staff must always state their first name and the business name when answering a call.

Ringing several devices

  • Google Voice (a phone service from Google) can be installed on multiple phones.
  • All phones must be logged into the same Google account (your Google login).
  • Then calls ring on several devices at once.

Answering service

  • Older advice: Most number providers (companies that give you a phone number) offer an answering service (a system that answers calls for you).
  • When a call is missed, it plays a pre-recorded greeting (a message recorded in advance) that names your business and asks for a message.
  • The service then notifies you of the message by email or app.
  • So you can call back as soon as possible.

Voicemail transcripts

  • Quo (a business phone app) creates automatic voicemail transcripts.
  • A transcript is the text version of a voicemail.
  • Transcripts convert incoming messages to text, so you can reply faster to customer and supplier questions.
  • You do not need to listen to full recordings.
  • Example (Quo Starter plan, one of Quo's packages): The Quo Starter plan includes voicemail transcripts, which turn each voicemail into text.
  • A caller asking whether a product is in stock can be answered from the transcript later, so a sale is not lost while the owner is busy.

Recorded voicemail greeting

A greeting is the message callers hear before they leave a voicemail. Brackets in the text are placeholders (spots to fill in with your own details).

Current version:

Hello, you've reached [store name, e.g. storename.com]. Thanks for calling. Our team is currently away from the desk or helping other customers, so please record a voicemail including your name, email address, phone number, and the purpose of your call. We'll respond as soon as we're able.

Older version:

Thanks for reaching out to [store name]. Our team is either away from the phone or busy assisting another customer at the moment. Leave a voicemail stating your name, email address, and question, and we'll call back as quickly as we can. Alternatively, send us a message at [support email address].

Missed-call text reply

Hi, this is {store_name}, and I'm sorry I missed your call. I'm helping other customers right now, so I'll reply by text or phone as soon as I can. If your call is urgent, you can reply to this message with your order number and a short note about what you need.

{your_name}
