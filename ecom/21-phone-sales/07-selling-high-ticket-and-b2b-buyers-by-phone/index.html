Selling high-ticket and B2B buyers by phone

Section 21.7 · 7 of 26 in this chapter · 4 min read

In short

  • For products over $5,000, sell slowly and personally. Ask why the buyer wants it and when they need it.
  • Win with service (faster or careful delivery), not low prices.
  • Expect more questions and calls before a sale. Plan enough people to answer them.
  • Phone talks help close sales. Send a buyer a draft order (an order you build and send so they can pay) so they skip the normal checkout.
  • Business buyers often need many contacts over time, about 20 touch points (calls, emails or messages). Be patient and keep following up.

Qualifying high-ticket buyers

High-ticket means expensive. A qualified lead is a person who may really buy.

  • Use this sequence on a call with a qualified lead for products with an average order value (AOV, the average money one order brings in) of $5,000 or more:

    1. Ask why they want the product, where it will go, when they need it, and what they will use it for.
    2. Decide whether they are a real buyer by checking for a confirmed need and a real timeline.
    3. Offer service upgrades such as expedited delivery (faster shipping) or white glove delivery (careful delivery with extra handling) instead of discounts.
    4. Show that you want to earn the business through service, not price cuts.
    5. If calls become too many to handle, hire a VA (virtual assistant, a remote helper) to take them.

  • Expect more pre-sale questions (questions asked before buying) and inbound calls (calls the buyer makes to you) for high-ticket items, such as those priced at $10,000 and above or at $1,000 to $2,000. These products can still be sold, but expect more contact before the sale.

  • Before pitching a high-ticket B2B lead (B2B means selling to businesses, and pitching means presenting your offer), qualify in this order. The economic buyer is the person who controls the budget.

    Step Question Purpose
    1 Who holds the authority over the budget? Identify the economic buyer
    2 What is the need the buyer is trying to meet? Establish the need
    3 Where are they now? Establish the current state
    4 Where do they want to be? Establish the target state
    5 What is the impact of not reaching that goal? Establish urgency and cost of inaction

Avoiding discount tactics

Discount tactics are sales moves that rely on lower prices. Loss-leader pricing means selling at a loss to attract buyers.

  • Do not use loss-leader pricing or a discount-focused strategy for products of $5,000 or more.
  • Discounts signal desperation (a sign you need the sale badly) to customers with serious buying intent (serious plans to buy).
  • Focus on service upgrades such as white glove delivery and expedited shipping.
  • Verify genuine customer need and timeline, and show business credibility (trustworthiness).
  • Price questions from buyers often close the sale, but this requires constant commitment and fast responses. Older advice from 2019 states that high-ticket products of about $5,000 and above can sell well when someone answers live chat (text chat on the website) and the phone quickly and responsively.

Phone as a sales channel

  • Selling by voice on the phone is an effective way to close high-ticket sales. The chance of a sale rises significantly when customers can speak to the business.
  • Plan staff capacity (how many people and hours you have) for phone sales before entering the high-ticket or bulk segment (bulk means large orders). Calls will reveal product questions that you can then answer on the website.

Concierge-style selling

Concierge-style means personal, one-on-one service, like a hotel concierge.

  • High-ticket buyers are often over 50 and sometimes in their 60s or 70s. They value personal attention and clear answers to their questions. A concierge-style approach works best with this group.
  • Concierge-style phone sale for an expensive product:
    1. Answer every question the buyer has about the product, and pay attention to their specific needs.
    2. Create a draft order (an order you build in your system and send to the customer) so they can pay without going through the normal checkout (the usual website payment steps) for the first order.
    3. For repeat orders (new orders from the same buyer), keep the conversation going by email, text or voice memos (recorded voice messages), and send another draft order when they are ready.
    4. Keep the buyer updated on any back-order delays (delays when an item is out of stock and ships later) until the product arrives.
  • A personal concierge approach can turn a single high-ticket buyer into a steady source of revenue (regular income) over time. Draft orders avoid friction (trouble or delay) in checkout for buyers who prefer a personal sale.

Case: concierge sale with draft orders

  • A store sold products priced between $2,000 and $9,000, with the most expensive variant (version of the product) at $9,000.
  • One buyer called with many questions about her purchase. The store answered each one attentively and sent a draft order, so she did not have to go through checkout for the first order.
  • Two months later she placed a second order after only a few emails of prior contact, with no draft order needed. Over those two months she brought in about $13,000 in sales.
  • A separate $9,000 order was back-ordered for three months. The store kept her updated on the status until it was delivered.

Multi-person B2B buying

  • High-ticket B2B purchases involve several people:

    Role Description
    Economic buyer Controls the money
    Lead decision maker or product owner Leads the decision on the product
    Functional buyers For example, head of purchasing

  • Get in front of the key decision maker (the person who makes the final call) and build relationships with the others.

  • Expect the buyer to need strong proof and security (solid evidence and reassurance that the purchase is safe).

Long sales cycles

  • High-ticket products can require around 20 touch points (contacts such as calls, emails or messages) before purchase. Expect a long sales cycle (the full time from first contact to sale) and plan a sequence of follow-ups rather than expecting a sale after one contact.

Direct mobile numbers and repeat business

  • Giving a high-value buyer your direct mobile number can turn a one-time purchase into repeat business. The buyer may later text or send voice messages to request a new draft order.
  • Accept the channel the buyer prefers, including voice memos, and respond reliably.
