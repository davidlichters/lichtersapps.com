Retargeting for long buying cycles and competitive niches

Section 16.20 · 20 of 24 in this chapter · 3 min read

In short

  • Expensive products take a long time to sell, so keep following up with past visitors for months.
  • Keep paid ads running in slow months so future buyers still remember your store.
  • Retargeting (showing ads again to past visitors) helps you win, because the store a buyer sees most often often gets the sale.
  • In slow times, build lists of visitors and emails, then send heavy ads when buyers are ready again.

Retargeting for High-Ticket Products with Long Buying Cycles

  • High-ticket products (expensive items) usually have longer buying journeys (the time from first look to purchase), so follow-up must cover a longer period. Without follow-up for first-time visitors (people who look but do not buy), many will leave and buy elsewhere before they are ready.
  • High-ticket buyers, such as people furnishing a home, may research products up to six months before they buy. Keep paid traffic (visitors who come from paid ads) running in slow periods so these early planners enter your retargeting list (the group of past visitors you can show ads to) and recall your store when they are ready to purchase.
  • For some high-ticket products, customers shop months before they intend to buy, so retargeting must keep your brand present through that whole consideration window (the full time a buyer compares options) until they are ready to pay.

Keeping Paid Traffic Running in Slow Periods

  • For high-ticket products with long buying cycles, keep paid traffic running during slow periods. Customers may plan purchases months ahead, and retargeting lists keep your store in front of them until they buy.
  • The right level of paid traffic depends on your risk tolerance (how much loss you accept) and your long-term view. Some owners cut traffic in slow periods to save money, while others keep it running to build audiences (groups of people you can target with ads) for later.
  • Remarketing audiences built during slow economic periods compound in value (they grow more useful over time). Keeping consistent remarketing metrics (the numbers you track for these ads) during downturns (economic dips) means a higher audience size and message frequency (how often each person sees your ad) when the economy recovers, which multiplies campaign effectiveness.

Building Audiences in Slow Periods, Then Activating Them

  1. Collect customer emails through opt-in forms (sign-up boxes where people give their email).
  2. Create Google audiences (lists of people for Google ads) of all website visitors (website visits).
  3. Create Facebook audiences of website visitors and page engagement (people who interacted with your page).
  4. During periods of low purchase intent (economic downturns, when people buy less), build these remarketing lists without aggressive selling (pushy sales messages).
  5. Once consumer confidence (how willing people feel to spend) recovers, run heavy remarketing campaigns to these built audiences via email, Google, and Facebook.

Competitor-Aware Retargeting for Resellers

  • Resellers (people who sell other brands' products) face competitors selling the same items. Prospects (possible buyers) who research in advance see several sellers, and in long consideration periods the seller a prospect saw most during research often wins the sale, not only the seller with the best offer. Retargeting therefore gives a competitive advantage (an edge over rivals).
  • Retargeting brings back visitors who did not buy yet. It is one of the reasons a store wins repeat sales when competitors offer the same MAP price (minimum advertised price, the lowest price a brand lets sellers show in ads).

Retargeting in Ultra-Competitive Niches

  • In an ultra-competitive niche (a small market with very many sellers), retarget visitors on Google, Facebook, Instagram, Bing and Yahoo with higher bids (the higher amount you pay per ad click or view). Buyers often return days or weeks later, forget which store they researched, and buy from a competitor, so repeated exposure keeps your store in front of them.

Private-Label Products

  • Remarketing to your own visitors is a main traffic source for newly private-labeled products (products sold under your own brand name) that Google Shopping (Google's product ad listings) does not reach, because shoppers do not search for them.
