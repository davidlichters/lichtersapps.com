Playbook › 16. Retargeting

Common mistakes and operating rules

Section 16.23 · 23 of 24 in this chapter · 2 min read

In short

  • Do not run only dynamic product ads. Mix in other ad types.
  • Split visitors into groups by what they viewed and did on your site.
  • Change the ad after a person has seen it a few times.
  • Use each ad to teach, not only to bring people back.
  • Limit how long and how often each group sees ads.

Strategy Principles

Remarketing (also called retargeting) means showing ads to people who already visited your site. Three principles make it work:

  1. Stay consistently visible so customers do not forget you.
  2. Address the specific objections that stopped their first purchase. An objection is a reason someone hesitates to buy.
  3. Understand deeply what your target customer wants and why.

Product Ad Mix

  • Running only dynamic product ads is a common mistake. Dynamic product ads automatically show the products a visitor looked at.
  • They fit some visitors, but not all.
  • If someone already rejected a product, showing it again for 14 to 30 days rarely changes their decision.

Ad Angles and Segmentation

  • Retargeting works best when you vary the ad angle. An angle is the main idea or theme of an ad.
  • Segment audiences, meaning split visitors into smaller groups, by the pages they visited and the actions they took on the site.
  • Each ad type can show customers a different side of the business and its products.
  • Retargeting ads work best when they use different angles and say different things about the business and its products.
  • Each ad is a chance to educate customers, not only to bring them back to the site.

Repetition and Message Fatigue

  • Message fatigue means people get tired of a message they keep seeing.
  • Showing the same ad again and again over weeks, for example a full month, is one of the biggest mistakes. It is a common error.
  • Once a customer has seen an ad a few times, repeating it adds little value.
  • Instead, change the angle or message.

Exposure Limits

  • Retargeting visitors for too long and too often wastes budget and annoys people. A budget is the money you spend on ads.
  • Limit how long and how frequently each audience sees ads.

Operating Rules

  1. Do not run only dynamic product ads. Combine them with other ad types, because each type fits different visitors.
  2. Segment audiences by the pages visited and the actions taken on the site.
  3. Vary the angle. After a customer has seen an ad a few times, change the message instead of repeating it.
  4. Educate the customer with each ad, not only bring them back to the site.
  5. Limit how long and how frequently each audience sees ads.
  6. Stay visible, address the specific objection that blocked the first purchase, and understand what the customer wants and why.
