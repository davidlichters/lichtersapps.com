Capturing emails and cross-Channel follow-Up

Section 16.17 · 17 of 24 in this chapter · 6 min read

In short

  • Collect email addresses from visitors who read your content or sign up. This lets you follow up later.
  • Email follow-up is what makes busy-season sales strong.
  • Ads and personal messages work best together, and they should send the same offer.
  • Text messages (SMS) only work for customers who agreed to them, and only in some countries. Test them first.
  • Test each channel in each country before you depend on it.

Exit-Intent and Opt-In Capture

  • Exit-intent means a tool that notices a visitor is about to leave the site. Opt-in means the visitor agrees to receive emails. Both help you collect emails.
  • Capture email addresses from bounced visits (visitors who leave without buying). This gives you a second chance to turn them into buyers. It also shows what holds customers back and what they prefer.
  • Collect email addresses from every visitor who reads content or opts in, so you can follow up later. Email follow-up is what makes peak-season sales (the busiest buying time of the year) strong. **
  • Set up an email pop-up (a small box that appears on the page) to capture visitors who are not ready to buy. This extends the relationship beyond the visit.
  • A browse abandonment feedback survey (a question form sent to people who looked at products and left without buying) is an optional extra channel. It is not a required part of collecting customer feedback.
  • Set up a browse abandonment survey email only if the store already tracks browse abandonment.

Lead Magnets

  • A lead magnet is a free gift, such as a piece of content, given in exchange for an email address.
  • Run paid ads to a lead magnet landing page (a single web page built for one ad) with an email capture form (a box where visitors type their email).
  • Offer a piece of content or an ebook sample (a short part of a digital book) in exchange for the email address.
  • Use this approach to grow both the follower base (people who follow your social media accounts) and the email list at the same time.

Email Lists and Invisible Audiences

  • Audiences fall into two groups:
    • Email lists: people who opted in, for example kept in Klaviyo (an email marketing tool).
    • Invisible lists: audiences on ad platforms (ad companies such as Google and Facebook), built from site visits, purchases and social engagement (interactions such as likes and comments). They include people who never gave their email.
  • You need both, because only a small share of visitors ever give their email address.

Uploading Customer Lists to Ad Platforms

Remarketing means showing ads again to people who already visited your store.

Procedure for adding your Shopify email list to remarketing (Shopify is the software that runs your online store) (2024):

  1. Export your customer email list from Shopify.
  2. Create a customer list audience (a group of people the ad tool can target) from that file, in both Google Ads and Meta Ads (ad tools for Google and for Facebook). The list may include people who have not bought yet.
  3. Upload the list to both platforms.
  4. In your remarketing ad group (a set of ads that share the same audience and settings), stack (add together) the new customer list with your website visitor audience so both are included.

Procedure for improving Facebook remarketing performance (older: 2022):

  1. Set Shopify data sharing (sending visitor information to Facebook) to maximum.
  2. Upload the Klaviyo list of opted-in people who have not bought yet to a Facebook custom audience (a group you upload to Facebook so it can target them with ads).
  3. Add that audience to your remarketing campaigns.
  4. Check frequency (how often one person sees your ad). Reduce spend (the money you pay for ads) if the same people see the same ads repeatedly.

Syncing Email With Ads

  • Sync your ads with your emails (make them match) so that everyone in the broadcast email audience (the whole list that gets one email at once) sees the offer message, including people who do not open email.
  • Post each email promotion or piece of content to Facebook and run it as an ad at the same time. This way the list and the audiences see the same message, which builds the relationship and moves people closer to buying.
  • Run remarketing ads on Meta and Google, and email the visitor audience, to bring back people who visited the store. Use pixel-based audience data (data from a small tracking code on your site) to build these lists.

Retargeting Visitors and Abandoners

  • Retarget visitors who did not buy with ads and email campaigns (a series of emails sent toward one goal). Use messages that follow up on the page they viewed.
  • Follow remarketing visitors and abandoned carts (items left in the shopping cart without buying) from two angles at once: automated emails in Klaviyo and personal outreach by the owner. **
  • Set up retargeting for visitors who arrive from Pinterest (a picture-sharing site), so they can be reached again after leaving the site.
  • Retargeting is a two-part development stage (a growth step). **
    • Part one: retargeting ads on Google, Facebook and YouTube.
    • Part two: personal communication through email, SMS (text messages), push notifications (short alerts sent to a phone or browser) and Messenger (Facebook's chat app).
  • Smaller high-ticket businesses (businesses that sell expensive products) often neglect personal communication. **
  • Three main follow-up strategies exist: retargeting ads, email marketing and SMS marketing. **
    • Retargeting ads run mainly on a few networks (ad platforms).
    • Email is the classic follow-up channel.
    • SMS is an older channel that can still deliver strong returns (money earned back from the ad spend) when used well.

Personal Follow-Up Messages

  • Use this formula for personal follow-up:
    • Name the product.
    • Use the real time the visitor spent on that product page, taken from your tracking tool (software that records visitor activity).
    • Ask one simple question about whether they still want it.
  • The specific detail shows a real person is paying attention, so the message feels personal rather than automated. Keep it short and never reuse a fixed script (one set text sent to everyone).
Hi {customer_name}, you spent about 20 minutes looking at the {product_name} yesterday. Are you still interested, or can I answer a question for you?
Hi {customer_name}, I noticed you came back to the {product_name} page several times this week. Would it help if I checked its availability for you before you decide?

SMS and Push: Channel Limits

  • SMS retargeting works only for customers who tick the consent box (the box that gives permission to receive texts). If most customers do not tick it, the channel delivers almost nothing. Do not rely on it as the main recovery channel (the main way to win back lost sales).
  • SMS retargeting results depend heavily on the market (the country you sell in). In the reported experience it worked in the US, Canada and Australia, but not in Switzerland, Germany and Austria. Test the channel per market before relying on it.
  • Sources differ on SMS.
  • The older Source B material calls it a channel that can still deliver strong returns when used well.
  • Source C limits its use to consenting customers and to specific markets.
  • Push notifications (short alerts sent to a phone or browser) are one of the personal channels listed alongside email and SMS for follow-up. **

Multi-Channel Coordination

  • Multi-channel marketing (reaching people through several channels at once) works best when each message delivers value. The channels include email, SMS, push notifications and retargeting on platforms such as Google, Facebook, Pinterest and Snapchat.
  • Repeating the same product picture across channels mainly annoys customers. **
  • Run retargeting ads and personal messages together as one development stage (a growth step), rather than as separate tasks. **
  • Keep ad and email messages consistent so that the list and the audiences see the same offer.

More details
