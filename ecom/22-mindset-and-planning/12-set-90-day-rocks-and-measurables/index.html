Set 90-day Rocks and measurables

Section 22.12 · 12 of 26 in this chapter · 5 min read

In short

  • Success in dropshipping comes from simple repeated steps (systems) and steady work, not from testing random products.
  • A Rock is one big goal for the next 90 days. Keep it clear and countable.
  • Keep the number of Rocks small: no more than 7 per quarter.
  • Plan the first 90 days in six phases, in order. Spend no money on ads until Phase 5.

Why a 90-day plan matters

  • Dropshipping means you sell products that another company stores and ships to your customer for you.
  • Success comes from building systems and staying consistent. A system is a set of steps you repeat the same way each time.
  • Success does not come from testing random products.
  • A structured 90-day roadmap (a step-by-step plan for 90 days) gives clarity and focus.
  • It moves you from beginner to consistent revenue (money coming in from sales).
  • Rocks are the priorities for the next 90 days. A Rock is one big goal with a 90-day deadline.
  • Rocks come from the goals set for the business.
  • Rocks are 90-day goals. They break a one-year target into quarterly parts (three-month blocks).
  • Each quarter, ask which goals must be hit to reach the one-year target.
  • Rocks give the business momentum (steady forward movement) and traction (real progress).
  • This helps the owner feel progress even when growth is slower than hoped.
  • Long periods without visible momentum lead to stagnation (no growth), self-doubt and dislike of the business.
  • Recovering from that gets harder over time.
  • Setting Rocks is therefore a proactive (early, planned) way to avoid that spiral.

Writing a valid Rock

  • Every Rock must be specific (clearly defined), attainable (possible to reach) and measurable (countable with numbers).
  • Every Rock must be reviewed every 90 days.
  • A goal that is neither specific nor measurable is not a valid Rock.
  • Example of a goal that is not valid: more profit next quarter.
  • Example of a valid Rock: Make an additional $60,000 profit in the next 90 days.
  • This Rock is specific and measurable, so the result can be checked easily.
  • A vague goal about more profit fails both tests.
  • Keep Rocks realistic for the 90-day window.
  • People tend to overestimate what they can achieve in a short period.
  • People tend to underestimate what they can achieve over a long period.
  • A business with $100,000 in total sales to date should not set a Rock of $1,000,000 in one quarter.
  • Set no more than 7 Rocks per quarter, because more goals make focus difficult.
  • Use Rocks only for big-impact opportunities, such as:
    • a new product
    • promoting an existing bestseller (your top-selling product)
    • a new traffic source (a new way visitors find your store)
    • an email marketing system (a set of automatic emails to customers)
    • a hiring plan
    • a new business
  • Do not use Rocks for routine tasks, such as answering emails.

Breaking a one-year target into measurables

Older advice (2021-22). Worked chain:

  1. One-year measurable (a countable goal for the year): 600,000 extra organic search visitors (people who find you on Google without paying for ads).
  2. 90-day measurable: first publish 10 new pieces of content (articles, pages or posts).
  3. Each content piece becomes a Rock, such as hiring a writer or writing one piece every two weeks.
  4. Weekly to-do lists carry these steps: in week one, write the full piece; in week two, find images and upload it.

Barrier issues

  • Barrier issues are obstacles that could derail (break) a plan.
  • Write barrier issues down next to the Rocks and measurables.
  • When a barrier appears, check whether other measurables or Rocks need to change.
  • The goal is to free up the resources (time, money, people) the barrier needs.
  • For example, if there is not enough time for the content, adjust other commitments to make room.
  • If the owner does not know how to do a required task, that gap is a barrier issue.
  • Solve it by making the acquisition of the missing skill (learning it) a 90-day Rock.
  • Then add a later Rock for putting that knowledge into practice.
  • Common mistake: do not schedule an entire Rock into a single week.
  • A complex task, such as hiring and training someone, cannot be done properly within a week.
  • Only a truly urgent item should be squeezed into one week.

Rolling 90-day window and quarterly review

  • Set Rocks one quarter at a time.
  • Always work inside a rolling 90-day window (a 90-day period that moves forward as time passes).
  • Each Rock set is reviewed every 90 days.
  • A new set is defined every quarter.
  • The 90-day window can start at any time.
  • It does not have to match the start of a calendar quarter.
  • Standard financial quarters (Q1 to Q4, the four three-month blocks of a year) are optional.
  • The first set of Rocks should cover the starting point toward the one-year plan.
  • Build the 90-day sheet after the three-year picture and the one-year plan are filled in.

Linking Rocks to weekly and daily work

  • Once the Rocks for the quarter are set, they define the weekly and daily to-do lists.
  • Worked example: the content Rock is broken into weekly steps.
  • In week one, write the full piece. In week two, find images and upload it.

First 90 days: the Source D roadmap

Overall sequence. Run the six phases over 90 days, in this order:

  1. Days 1-14: Foundation
  2. Days 15-30: Product Research & Validation (finding and testing products)
  3. Days 31-45: Store Building
  4. Days 45-60: Creative Production (making ads and images)
  5. Days 60-75: Launching Ads
  6. Days 75-90: Optimization & Scaling (improving results, then growing them)

Note: as the source lists them, day 45 falls in both Phase 3 and Phase 4, and day 60 falls in both Phase 4 and Phase 5.

Phase 1 Foundation checklist (Days 1-14).

  1. Understand branded dropshipping (selling dropshipped products under your own brand name) as a business model.
  2. Set up Shopify (online store software) with a custom domain (your own web address) and email address.
  3. Configure Facebook Business Manager (Facebook's tool for running ads) and install your Pixel (a small tracking code that records what visitors do).
  4. Learn the product criteria (the rules a product must meet) for dropshipping.
  5. Learn advertising angles (the main reason a buyer should care) and how to identify testable products (products you can check with ads).

Spend zero budget on ads in this phase. Focus entirely on building knowledge and technical infrastructure (the tools and setup behind the store).

Rule for Phase 1. Phase 1 requires zero ad spend (no money spent on ads). All effort and resources go into technical infrastructure and knowledge. This makes Phase 5 ad spending efficient rather than wasteful.

Mistake to avoid. Starting Phase 5 (ad spend) before completing Phases 1-4 wastes budget on untested funnels (the path a customer takes from ad to purchase) and unrefined creatives (ads and images that are not yet tested). Each phase builds the knowledge and infrastructure the next one requires. Skipping steps multiplies risk.

Expected outcomes after the 90-day roadmap. Assets means ads, images and other creative files.

Area Outcome
Brand Built from scratch
Product One proven winner identified
Creatives 15-30 distinct assets launched
Data capability Ability to read and interpret ad metrics
Profitability First profitable days or weeks
System A repeatable process for future products
