Posting cadence, timing and hashtags

Section 11.9 · 9 of 22 in this chapter · 8 min read

In short

  • Post every day, at least once, and keep the rhythm steady. Steady beats bursts.
  • While you test, post 3 to 5 times a day to find which videos work.
  • Test your posting times against your target audience's time zone.
  • Never go above 9 posts a day. More can hurt the account.

Posting Cadence and Volume

1 min
  • Cadence means how often you post. It matters more than how many videos you post at once. One video per week, posted steadily, beats ten videos in one day or one burst per month followed by nothing. Pick a rhythm you can keep up.
  • Organic (free reach, no paid ads) short-video accounts should post at least once a day, even when older videos still bring sales. Older videos keep gaining views. New posts keep reach and sales from falling.
  • Organic growth depends on steady posting over time, not on one video. Early results, such as about 1,200 views and 18 new followers after two days, are best treated as a test of how steady your output is. Plan a regular posting rhythm from day one.

  • One per day keeps an established account active.
  • 2 to 5 per day fits testing.
  • 5 to 6 per day applies only while a video is viral.
  • 9 per day is the upper limit. Do not exceed it.
  • For a viral video, aim for 4 to 5 per day. Five to six is the ceiling during a spike.
  • The about 3 per day figure is for organic testing across several accounts. Each account needs its own content.

Bursts, Gaps and Spacing

1 min
  • During active testing, post in bursts (short runs of several posts close together), then leave gaps between them.
  • Upload in volume and in batches (many videos at once).
  • One video in a batch can go viral while the others do not.
  • One slipper-case account posted three to six times a day. One of its many videos produced most of its views.
  • Viral success depends on volume. Several uploads a day raise the chance that one video breaks through.
  • A single successful video can drive most of an account's results.
  • While a video is viral, space uploads about two hours apart. This avoids being flagged as spam (marked as unwanted repeat posting).
  • On an early account, space posts four to six hours apart.

Consistency and Patience

1 min
  • A viral video can take up to two weeks to appear after an account starts posting.
  • An account that posts irregularly may take longer to break through.
  • Consistent uploading shortens that wait.
  • Going viral usually needs sustained posting, because reach often disappears for stretches and then returns.
  • Do not decide after a dry period that the account is shadow banned (secretly limited in reach). Keep publishing consistently.

  • Align posts with US waking and lunch hours, since the US audience is the reference for these peaks.
  • Avoid posting near midnight.
  • Early-morning slots vary. A seller's 3 a.m. ET uploads worked well because the audience was awake at that hour in another time zone.
  • A personal account did well at 3 a.m. PT.
  • The midnight warning and these early-morning examples do not conflict. They describe different accounts and audiences.
  • In one account, a video posted around 6:30 a.m. ET got about 170 views.
  • Videos posted around 12:00-13:00 ET got about 300-400 views.
  • Compare view counts across your own upload times before choosing a schedule.
  • Always test upload times against the time zone of your target audience (the people you want to reach).
  • On a new account, test more than three upload times.
  • Varying upload times reaches different viewers, since people in other regions may be awake at odd hours.
  • Posting late at night in one region may still need local engagement (likes, comments and shares from nearby viewers) first.

Procedure: Finding the Best Posting Time for a Product

1 min
  1. Use different devices and accounts.
  2. Post the same product content at different hours of the day.
  3. Compare views and engagement (likes, comments, shares) by time slot to find the best window.

Drafting and the Content Pipeline

1 min
  • The content pipeline means the steps from making a video to posting it.
  • Draft 3 to 5 days of videos ahead of posting day, not on the day itself.
  • A buffer of drafts (spare finished videos kept in reserve) makes consistent uploading possible.
  • Drafting regularly signals to TikTok that you are a consistent, valuable uploader. This tends to lead to more reach.
  • Edit everything on the phone so the workflow stays quick.
  • For steady output while busy, use this routine:
    1. Produce three to nine videos per day in advance.
    2. Keep drafts a day ahead.
    3. Upload one per scheduled slot.
  • The one-day-ahead draft rhythm is the daily working routine.
  • The 3-5 day buffer is the reserve kept on hand.

Scheduling

1 min
  • The TikTok desktop uploader (the upload page on a computer) lets you set a scheduled upload time. This separates posting from phone activity.
  • The desktop uploader offers fewer editing options than the app, so add music and text before uploading from the computer.
  • TikTok Shop (TikTok's built-in store) now lets creators and brands schedule shoppable videos (videos that link to products for sale) ahead of time.
  • Shoppable content can therefore be planned and published at set times.

Hashtags

1 min
  • Hashtags (words after #) are not the main reason a video goes viral.
  • If no specific hashtag comes to mind, broad tags such as fyp (For You page, TikTok's main feed), viral or asmr (soft, relaxing sound videos) can be used.
  • The video itself matters far more than any hashtag.
  • Use no more than six hashtags per post.
  • Aim for about two niche (small, specific topic tags), two broad (big general tags) and two trending (tags in use right now).
  • A very large set can send a video to unrelated audiences.
  • For a new account with a target audience, use two niche hashtags, one of which names the target audience.
  • Test with and without hashtags, because their effect varies.
  • A typical set uses about five hashtags: the product hashtag, about two niche hashtags, one broad hashtag such as "satisfying", and one hashtag for the problem or complaint the buyer has.
  • Every hashtag should be relevant. Irrelevant hashtags narrow the audience to the wrong group.
  • Six is the maximum per post. About five is the typical set. Stay at or below six, and aim for about five.

Captions and Search Terms

1 min
  • TikTok autocomplete (the suggestions it shows while you type) can reveal what users want within a niche (a narrow market).
  • Use them in captions and content as an early SEO (search engine optimization, getting found by search) tactic on the platform.

Trend Monitoring

1 min

Procedure: Daily Trend Monitoring

  1. Spend about 30 minutes a day on the For You feed (TikTok's main scrolling feed) to see what is trending.
  2. Note the popular music, sounds and formats (the style or structure of a video) that repeat across several videos.
  3. When you find a trending audio (a popular sound clip), open its sound page (the page listing videos that use that sound) to see the top videos using it.
  4. Use the same audio in your own video, ideally soon after it starts trending.

Procedure: Finding Trending Audio and Formats

  1. Spend regular time on TikTok analyzing what is trending.
  2. Spot trends one to two weeks before they reach the viral charts (TikTok's trending lists) when possible.
  3. Use the trend format with your product. Older trends, even a few months old, can still perform well.

Seasonal and Q4 Planning

1 min
  • Consumer spending (the money shoppers spend) increases visibly around October 12-15 every year.
  • Spending stays high despite economic downturns because consumers rely on credit cards and buy-now-pay-later schemes (paying in installments).
  • Plan inventory (stock on hand) and campaign capacity for a volume surge in this window.

Procedure: Preparing for the Q4 Holidays

  1. Raise posting from one or two to three times per day.
  2. Watch for trending sounds, hashtags and filters (video effects) starting about 24 to 48 hours before Thanksgiving (a US holiday in late November) and the Friday after.
  3. Use them in your videos.

Handling Viral Momentum

1 min
  • While a video is viral, upload more good-quality content, aiming for four to five videos.
  • While a video is viral, space uploads about two hours apart.
  • When a video is gaining momentum, post several times a day.
  • The example was four uploads on the first day, followed by continued consistency.
  • Keep posting during the peak first week even if early views are low. One video can break out and lift the rest.
  • A viral spike usually fades within 24 hours to a few days.
  • A strong video can stay elevated for about a week, but only if you keep adding actions that reach new viewers.
  • A video that does not go viral on day one can still gain views later. TikTok sometimes reruns or pushes older videos (shows them again).
  • One reported video went from 14 million to 30 million views through reruns, passing 20 million on the way.
