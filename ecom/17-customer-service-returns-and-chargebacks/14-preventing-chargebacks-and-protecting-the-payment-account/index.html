Preventing chargebacks and protecting the payment account

Section 17.14 · 14 of 22 in this chapter · 8 min read

In short

  • A chargeback is when a customer gets their money back from their bank. Keep them under about 1 per 100 orders.
  • Answer customer messages fast and ship on time. Most chargebacks come from delays and silence.
  • Check product quality before you sell more. Bad products bring many chargebacks.
  • Keep the RDR setting (automatic refunds) on "Not active" in Disputely.
  • Make no unsupported health claims, and sell no banned products.

Chargeback causes

2 min
  • A chargeback is when a customer asks their bank or card company to take money back. In one documented case, late fulfillment (packing and shipping the order) was a main cause.
  • In that same case, customers thought the product was fake or not as described.
  • Some customers also disputed charges after the brand owner publicly called the seller a scammer.
  • A low-quality product can lead to many chargebacks. Enough of them can make the payment processor (the company that handles card payments for the store) shut the account down, even after sales are high. Check quality before scaling (growing sales volume).
  • Selling a seasonal product (one people need only at certain times of year) outside its season raises chargebacks. Buyers dispute orders for items they do not need in the current weather. Plan the sales window so the product sells only while its season matches the buyer's need.
  • Dropshipping (the supplier ships each order directly to the customer) with long delivery times and difficult returns carries more chargeback risk. Customers who wait weeks often dispute payments.
  • Customers file chargebacks through their bank or card issuer (the bank that issued their card), not through Shopify (the online store platform). The merchant gets a notification and can respond with evidence. The card network (Visa, Mastercard) or the bank decides the outcome.
  • Chargebacks stay rare when products arrive quickly, the store responds to customers, and the product matches what the customer expected. Most chargeback losses come from delays and unclear product expectations.
  • A chargeback can be lost even after delivery. This happens if a stolen card paid for an item shipped to a reachable address. Prevent this with fraud prevention (tools that block stolen-card orders) on the store. Evidence sent after the dispute is often not enough.
  • Higher order volume brings more returns, complaints and chargebacks. This is especially true on Shopify Payments (Shopify's own payment service) or Stripe (another payment service).
  • Products with thin margins (little profit per sale) become a risk as sales grow.
  • Orders paid with credit or debt carry higher chargeback risk.
  • The store's financial exposure (how much it could lose) is higher during a downturn, when customers are panicking.
  • In fashion and streetwear, younger UK buyers (about 21 to 30) converted very well (many of them bought). But refunds and chargebacks were much higher with them.
  • Older UK buyers were easier to sell to.
  • A supplier dispute or a quality problem can raise the PayPal dispute rate.
  • Changing suppliers can therefore cause temporary spikes in disputes.
  • Most German buyers pay with PayPal.
  • Poor customer support leads to many chargebacks in Germany.
  • Chargebacks rise during peak and holiday seasons.
  • A single chargeback can threaten a small merchant (a store that sells on its own account).
  • Unsupported health claims may pass for a while, but they eventually cause trouble with platforms.
  • When the promised result does not happen, customers get angry. They become more likely to return the product or dispute the charge.

Chargeback rate thresholds

1 min

The sources give different figures because they describe different contexts. Use the reference that matches your account and payment method.

The chargeback rate is the number of disputes divided by the number of orders.

Reference point Value When it applies Source
Visa and Mastercard expectation Below 1%, meaning no more than about 1 chargeback per 100 orders. Above that, card networks issue warnings and the processor may close the account. General card network rule
US Stripe and PayPal accounts About 1% dispute rate, described as the point of risk for holds or closure US accounts, account-specific
Hong Kong accounts Up to 6% described as tolerated (account-specific, verify before relying on it) Hong Kong accounts only
Reserve case 3.36% chargeback rate over the last 90 days led Shopify to place 10% of payouts (money sent to the store) in reserve (money held back) Shopify Payments store that had unfulfilled orders charged back
Example ratio 17.5% for the period without alert prevention Example from the source, not a benchmark
Occasional chargebacks Two chargebacks across more than 1,000 orders Treated as a normal cost of business that sales volume covers
Shopify shutdown trigger Three chargebacks in one week Can get a Shopify store shut down
  • Raising the average order value (the average amount a customer spends per order) spreads the same number of disputes over more revenue. This is the main practical way to bring the rate back below 1%.
  • Keep chargeback rates low. A high chargeback ratio is one of the main triggers for a merchant account (the store's account at a payment provider) to hold a reserve.
  • Excessive chargebacks can cause a payment provider to stop supporting a store.

Account holds and closures

1 min
  • Shopify rarely closes stores.
  • Closures mostly happen for prohibited products or serious chargeback problems.
  • Account closures for chargebacks are rare.
  • They usually follow from unanswered customers, missed shipments or repeated fraud.
  • To stay within the limits, respond to customers, ship on time and avoid prohibited products.
  • Three chargebacks in one week can get a Shopify store shut down.
  • Shopify may ban the store immediately, without refunding the money it holds.
  • A wrong Disputifier setup (an app that blocks chargebacks, explained below) can let chargebacks through. This can lead to this situation.

Case: Stripe hold. A seller's Stripe account was placed on a 30-day hold (the payment service keeps the store's money for a while). The seller contacted customers early to resolve complaints, and the disputes were resolved quickly. About 20 days after the hold, the money was released, the dispute rate fell, and no further holds followed.

Case: PayPal hold. A seller with PayPal holds at about 27,000 USD in volume uploaded fulfillment documents when requested. The funds were released about a week later.

The store also found that PayPal made up only about 5% of its payments. The seller paused PayPal for a few days and saw no drop in conversion (the share of visitors who buy). Judge holds against how much a payment method actually contributes to sales.

Prohibited products

1 min
  • Products on Shopify's prohibited list (items the platform bans from selling), such as supplements, can cause the processor to cancel the account and hold funds.
  • Check the prohibited products list on Shopify's legal page before listing a product.
  • Avoid unsupported health claims.
  • They create platform trouble and lead to angry customers who dispute charges.

Responsiveness and proactive communication

1 min
  • Not replying to customer emails is the most common cause of chargebacks.
  • Answer every message quickly, reassure the buyer and show empathy (understanding of how they feel).
  • A customer who receives no answer will often dispute the charge.
  • Ignoring customers who ask where their order is leads to chargebacks.
  • Even a reply that only explains a delay keeps the customer from disputing the payment.
  • Contact the customer as soon as a delay is known.
  • Most chargebacks from legitimate customers can be avoided this way, because the frustration that leads to disputes is prevented.
  • Most chargebacks may be prevented with better communication. This means clear product descriptions, accurate shipping times and a friendly, responsive contact channel (the way customers reach you).
  • Angry customers who dispute a payment often have unrelated problems. The store still controls what it communicates before the dispute.
  • Unanswered customer tickets (support requests) make customers angrier. This raises the share of refunds.
  • A rising share of refunds leads to a rising share of chargebacks, so the damage grows faster than sales.
  • Chargebacks and refunds mostly happen when customers are not answered in time.
  • A fast email reply with a discount code for the next order protects against chargebacks, even with long shipping.
  • Put in the shipping and refund policies that customers must contact the store before filing a chargeback.
  • Keep records of all customer emails.
  • A bank can use a screenshot of the customer's email and an unanswered reply as evidence.

Terms checkbox and evidence

1 min
  • Turn on the terms and conditions checkbox in the checkout (the payment step), so customers must agree before paying.
  • Link it to your terms pages using inline HTML (a short piece of web code placed directly on the page).
  • A recorded terms checkbox and a screenshot of it are evidence to include in a chargeback response.
  • They may not win every dispute.
  • For orders that were shipped as planned, enter the tracking number (the code that shows where the parcel is) as evidence to win the dispute.
  • Keeping proof of shipment is what makes this possible.

Disputifier, Disputely and RDR settings

2 min

Disputifier

  • Disputifier is an app that blocks chargebacks and helps handle disputes when orders go wrong.
  • Set it up before the store starts scaling.
  • A wrong setup can let chargebacks through.

Disputely

  • Disputely is a monitoring and dispute tool. It watches chargebacks and fights them.
  • It connects to the store, sends chargeback alerts and fights disputes to recover funds.
  • The source calls it one of the cheapest options.
  • Its billing page lists alert types (early warnings about disputes) RDR, CDRN and Ethoca. Prices are $16.00 and $25.00 per alert.
  • Each chargeback costs about $30 in total.
  • Fighting a chargeback with Disputely costs about $20 to $30.
  • The merchant pays these fees even when the chargeback is won.
  • The Disputely dashboard (the main overview screen) shows the chargeback ratio over time, chargebacks prevented, and the true cost of avoided chargebacks.
  • The Disputes view lists each case with a status such as Enriching or Evidence Ready (status labels) and a countdown to the deadline.
  • The tool submits evidence automatically about 5 days before the deadline. Review cases and add notes or extra evidence before that point.

RDR setting

  • RDR (Rapid Dispute Resolution, an automatic refund step before a chargeback) is an alert setting in Disputely Billing.
  • It must show Not active.
  • When it is Not active, the automatic actions read: No refunds will be issued and no subscriptions will be canceled.
  • If RDR stays active, customers can be refunded automatically even after the order has shipped. This happens when the order is seen as not fulfilled fast enough.
  • The merchant then loses the ad spend (money paid for ads), the cost of goods (what the product cost to buy) and the sale.

Procedure: stop automatic RDR refunds

  1. Open the Disputely billing page and check the RDR alert status.
  2. Write an email to Disputely support asking them to disable RDR on your account.
  3. Wait for confirmation, then check that the RDR alert shows Not active and that the automatic refund action reads No refunds will be issued.

Pre-dispute refunds and early resolution

1 min
  • Automatic refunds triggered by RDR are a loss, not a protection. Keep RDR at Not active.
  • Mitigator (a service that sends signals before a chargeback is filed) sends these signals to a Stripe or Shopify Payments account.
  • This allows earlier resolution.
  • Third-party gateways (outside payment connections) with better merchant protection were reported to cost around 400 to 500 (period and currency not stated).
  • Their lower acceptance rate can reduce conversion. So they fit mainly when holds or disputes already occur.
  • Replying quickly with a discount code, before a customer opens a dispute, is the earliest form of resolution available.
