Playbook › 17. Customer service, returns and chargebacks

Documentation, record-keeping and operational safeguards

Section 17.21 · 21 of 22 in this chapter · 4 min read

In short

  • Talk to the customer first in any refund or dispute, then to the supplier or carrier.
  • Save every order, shipment, refund and message. These records are your proof.
  • Never give customer emails to suppliers. Stay calm with suppliers, even when they caused the problem.
  • Turn on sales tax for each state before you take orders from that state.
  • Keep personal and money files out of any folder an AI tool can open.

Order and communication records

  • Contact the customer first in any refund or dispute (a disagreement about a payment). Then contact the supplier or carrier (the shipping company).
  • Keep records of all communication. They serve as chargeback evidence. A chargeback is when the bank takes money back from you after a buyer disputes a payment. Your records are the proof you show the bank.

Evidence for disputes

  • Keep complete records of every order, shipment and refund in Shopify (the online store software you use).
  • These records are the evidence you need to win a buyer appeal (a challenge a buyer makes against a decision).
  • Refund requests from platforms such as Facebook (sites where you sell or advertise) can be contested with these records.
  • Keep detailed records of tracking numbers (the code that follows a parcel).
  • Also keep detailed records of supplier legal names (the official registered company name), locations (including exact China addresses) and company names.
  • Payment processors (companies that move customer money to you) require these records in compliance investigations (checks that rules are being followed).

Supplier information

  • Do not share customer email addresses with suppliers without caution.
  • Suppliers will likely use them for marketing. Customers did not agree to receive supplier messages.
  • Send customers only the supplier's order email and return policy (the rules for sending items back).
  • The company does not create its own supplier contracts. Sign the supplier's agreement and return it right away. **
  • As the store owner, you handle customer complaints and payment disputes. The supplier does not.
  • Agree return and payment terms with the supplier in advance.
  • Set the defective return allowance (the share of faulty items the supplier takes back) on a supplier application (the form you fill in to become a supplier's customer) to 100 percent.
  • A defective (broken or faulty) product is always replaced. That is why the allowance is 100 percent.

Supplier relations and escalation

  • If a supplier does not respond after a few days, keep following up until you get a yes or no answer.
  • When an order is lost or damaged, do not yell, curse or send hostile emails to the supplier.
  • This applies even if the fault is theirs.
  • Work through the problem calmly and respectfully.
  • Suppliers who are treated with disrespect will not give you special treatment, even if you sell a lot.
  • If you cannot reach the decision maker (the person who makes the final call) at a supplier, ask the receptionist to connect you to a sales representative (a person whose job is selling and handling deals).
  • Sales representatives have more product knowledge (facts about the products) than call-center staff (phone staff who handle routine calls).

Insurance

  • The sources differ on whether general liability insurance (cover for claims from others, such as injury or damage) is required or only usual.
    • Optional view: general liability insurance is optional. It is not needed to open a business.
    • Optional view: it can be considered based on business assets (what the business owns).
    • Optional view: get quotes (price offers) from a large insurer (an insurance company) or from the insurer already used for a home policy.
    • Usual-type view: general liability insurance is the usual type for an online store. **
  • Be cautious with insurance brokers (middlemen who find policies for you). **
  • Review what you are actually charged over time. Premiums (regular insurance payments) can rise a lot after the first year. **
  • Limited liability insurance (a policy that limits what you could lose from a claim) becomes more sensible once you have assets (things of value) to protect. **
  • Consult a professional (an expert such as a lawyer or advisor) before deciding. **

Sales tax and payouts

  • Enable sales tax (a tax on sales, set up separately for each state) for each state before taking orders from that state.
  • An order placed before tax is set up may need to be cancelled and redone.
  • Refunds can cost real money, as in one case that cost 40 USD. **
  • A refunded or cancelled order can create a negative pending payout in Shopify. **
  • A pending payout is money Shopify has not sent to your bank yet. A negative one means you owe money back. **
  • After logging in, check the options shown in Shopify Payments (Shopify's own payment service) before paying out the balance. **

Refund exposure and cash reserves

  • Letting backorders (orders waiting for stock the supplier has not sent yet) stretch for months is a risk. **
  • Income keeps going to normal costs and owner pay (the money you pay yourself). This can leave no money for refunds or supplier bills. **
  • Track refund exposure (the money you may have to pay back) from pending orders against the cash set aside for them. **

Sensitive files and AI-accessible folders

  • Dragging personal or financial documents into an AI-accessible folder (a folder an AI tool can read) exposes them to the agent (an AI helper that acts on your files).
  • Dragging such documents into that folder creates real risk.
  • Keep such files outside the agent folder.
  • Keep such files out of any folder Codex (an AI coding tool) can open.
