# Coaching: the daily Lichters check-in

A short daily check-in with an AI or a friend keeps your six habits alive: one setup, then two minutes a day that end in one concrete action for today.

## What it is

A check-in is a tiny conversation, not a lecture. Someone asks you the same few questions every day, you answer honestly, and you leave with one clear thing to do next.

## Why

- **Being expected beats being motivated.** Most people don't fail from low motivation. They fail because nobody notices the skip.
- **A small daily look beats a big monthly plan.** You fix a problem on day two, not after three weeks of red.
- **One next action beats ten good ideas.** "Text a friend at 16:00 to play football" gets done. "Be more social" doesn't.

## How to start

**1. Do the setup once.** For each habit (f, e, b, t, o, s), write one line: what counts as done (the app's rule, not a softer one), the time, the place, and the one thing most likely to stop you plus your plan for it.

Example for b: "5 focused hours: 14:00 to 17:00 and 19:30 to 21:30, desk in my room, phone in the kitchen. Danger: YouTube on the laptop. Plan: block it during the timer."

**2. Check in once a day, at the same time.** Three questions:

- Which habits did I tick since the last check-in?
- For each miss: what got in the way, in one sentence?
- What is my one next action for today, with a time and a place?

**3. End with one action, not a list.** If you missed Eating three days in a row, the action is not "eat better". It is "put two apples and a carrot in your bag tonight at 21:00".

**4. With an AI: start a new chat for every check-in.** Paste your setup note plus today's answers. A long chat uses up your limit faster, because the whole history is read again.

**5. With a friend: same questions, by message.** Agree on a time, both send your answers, both reply with one honest sentence.

Many AI apps are 18+. If you're younger, use the friend or parent version. Never paste private details about other people into an AI chat.

## Mistakes

- **Ticking to save the streak.** A fake tick makes the whole tracker worthless.
- **Turning it into a diary.** Keep it to two minutes. If something heavy is going on, talk to a person you trust.
- **Asking for advice instead of an action.** "What's my one sleep action for tonight?" beats "How can I sleep better?"
- **Changing the rules when it gets hard.** Lower the plan, not the definition of done.
- **Punishing yourself with fines.** Fear makes people quit or lie. Use a person who checks.

## What happened when I tried it

I've used a habit tracker app since at least 2023. My honest results:

- In 2023 I kept a daily gratitude list. Later I admitted most of it wasn't true: I wrote things only to keep the streak. That's why honesty is rule number one.
- In autumn 2025 in Canada, I got a new friend to come to the gym with me every day, and soon my whole tracker was green again.
- Around new year 2026 I set a rule: if my week isn't 100% green, I give away 100 € on Monday. I wouldn't recommend it.
- In summer 2026 I worked hard for about a month mostly because someone expected a call from me at a fixed time.
- In September 2026 I asked an AI to coach me. Business had been green for years, but the other habits were almost empty. So the first goal wasn't more Business. It was one single week with every habit ticked.
