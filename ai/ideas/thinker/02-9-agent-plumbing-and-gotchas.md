## 9. Agent plumbing and gotchas

**Files (folder ~/thinker, as of 2026-10-14):** `lauf.mjs` one run (signals, 6 blind generators, sorting, checks: who already sells this, 2 pessimists, repairer; test card, control group) · `kern.mjs` one agent = one `claude -p` on the subscription, shared by run, calibration and improver · `stufen/` the VALID version: prompts plus `regel.mjs` (when an idea dies) · `versionen/vN/` every measured version v1 to v8 · `eichung.mjs` with `eichung/faelle.json` measures both errors (good ideas killed, forbidden ideas let through; `node eichung.mjs --bilanz`) · `grau.mjs` with `grau.jsonl` the grey list · `ideen.jsonl` every checked idea with its verdict · `testbuch.mjs` / `tests.jsonl` real tests, threshold fixed first · `bausatz.mjs` builds page and ad for a planned test, publishes nothing · `verbessern.sh` one improvement per night 03:30 · `wochenlauf.sh` full run Sundays 22:00. Both scheduled jobs (de.thinker.verbessern, de.thinker.wochenlauf) were unloaded on 2026-10-14 when David switched the Thinker off. Source: ~/thinker/CLAUDE.md.

- **One agent = one `claude -p` call** with the prompt on stdin, `--output-format json`, model Opus, API key env vars removed (so it uses the subscription, 0 EUR), 25-minute hard kill. The answer must END with a fenced json block; the last block counts. Every agent's raw text is saved, so any verdict can be audited.
- **Tool restriction (v6, 2026-09-28):** `--allowedTools` only ALLOWS, it forbids nothing, and the global settings allowed Bash, Agent, Workflow, Task, Monitor. On 2026-09-25, 6 to 7 of 10 agents in stages 1+2 pushed work into the background and ended without JSON; on 2026-09-27, 3 of 10 (a Bash call moved to background after 120 s, the process hung until the 25-minute kill). Fix: `--tools` with only the stage's tools plus `--strict-mcp-config` (removes MCP servers) and settings that disable workflows. Result 0/10 missing JSON twice.
- **Usage limits silently look like verdicts.** The 2026-09-25 run hit the 5-hour session limit: 14 checks got "You've hit your session limit" instead of JSON, giving 0 test cards and 14 grey. Marked invalid by hand. Lesson: always grep agent outputs for limit messages before reading a run as a result.
- **Exit code lies.** From 2026-10-07 every night logged `rc=0` while the output was only "Your organization has disabled Claude subscription access for Claude Code". The 2026-10-11 Sunday run "finished" with 0 signals, 0 ideas, 12 grey, every agent output 144 characters. A health check must read the content, not the exit code.
- **Blocked web search.** Since 2026-10-03 WebSearch/WebFetch are blocked for all agents (David's rule: research only via Perplexity). This silently disabled stage 4 (fixed in v8), made 30 of 47 generator ideas grey ("no price proof, web search blocked") and left the repairer blind (open at shutdown).
- **Context leak.** Agents running inside the home folder inherit David's global instructions and the LR status, so the "blind" generators were not blind. Blind mode: working dir outside home, `--setting-sources project`, read access only to given folders.
- **Parallelism:** default 3, Sunday run 2, because LR operations shared the subscription.
- Schedule (macOS launchd): full run Sundays 22:00, improver daily 03:30.

## 10. Test kit and test book

- **Test kit (`bausatz`):** from a planned test, an agent builds an honest landing page (mailto pre-order, own counter) plus ad text into `tests/tNNN/`. It publishes NOTHING. A hard check runs after the build: the page fails if the visitor counter is missing, any button lacks a counting attribute, the imprint is missing, the word "RIMA" appears, or a long dash appears. Counter: a free GoatCounter site "thinker-tests" that David never created.
- **Test book (`testbuch`):** one line per event. `--neu` takes a test card over (status planned, threshold copied from the card, cannot be changed later), `--start tNNN --kosten 50` when David starts it, `--ergebnis tNNN --wert N --bestanden ja|nein --kosten-echt N --beleg "<where to see it>"`. Feedback is automatic: a passed CONTROL becomes a "must live" calibration case; a failed test goes into the idea ledger. The test book stayed empty: zero tests ever ran.

## 11. Private signals it harvested (measured facts from our own data)

These are the inputs the generators worked from. They are real measurements from LR and our outbound work (as recorded in our files in Sept/Oct 2026) and are worth knowing on their own.

**From Liquidationsradar and the Bundesanzeiger archive:**
- Buyers react, creditors do not. Industrial liquidators and surplus buyers: 2 of 7 contacted reacted (one asked for the price). Craft businesses as creditors: 0 humans from 700+ deliveries. Clearance firms with a clearance text: 0 of 65. LR: 560 deliveries, 0 customers (as of 2026-09-25).
- The recovery industry's data channel hangs on insolvency administrators and does not see voluntary dissolutions: of 32,310 liquidation/dissolution events in our archive exactly 1 contains the word "Insolvenz". Roughly 93 % of liquidations are in no insolvency feed.
- 92.4 % of liquidation notices (73 of 79 on a full day) are only a form sentence without the liquidator's name; 5 of 79 have a postcode, 0 a deadline. The liquidator's name is retrievable free from the commercial register (12 of 12 found, 8 with name).
- The value is in the backlog, not the alert: on 2026-09-08, 31,409 blocking years (Sperrjahr, the 1-year wait after a creditor call before assets may be distributed, § 73 GmbHG) were running; a monitor starting that day would have seen 444; after 90 days still 25,689 unseen; median remaining 145 days.
- Deletion announcements for asset-less firms (§ 394 FamFG, 3-month objection period): only 18 of 1,639 HRB cases (1.1 %) were in the liquidation archive first, 98.9 % are new; 33.8 per day; visible only 8 weeks, no archive exists.
- Conversion notices (merger, split, change of form) are the biggest unused register source: 204.5 per day, about 74,625 per year, 2.37x the liquidations; 98.5 % carry court and register number; 0.7 % overlap with liquidations. 1,016 sole-trader-to-company spin-offs (e.K. -> GmbH) among 10,972 conversion notices from 2026-07-20 to 09-13 (Bavaria 227, NRW 168, Baden-Wurttemberg 150).
- Matching German company names without register number is half noise: 22.17 % of 5,305,406 register firms have an ambiguous name, 18.84 % across court boundaries; checked by register number, 10 of 23 name hits were another firm.
- Real hit density of a liquidation warning is about 2.4x lower than derived from public numbers: 0.904 % per GmbH/UG customer per year directly measured (152 of 16,814), 0.68 to 1.01 % on 12,000 names, versus 2.2 % derived. A typical craft business with 60 business customers (15 GmbH/UG) sees no hit in 85.9 to 90.3 % of years; a 74.5 % hit chance needs 135 to 201 GmbH/UG customers. Only 24.2 % of craft businesses are GmbH.
- Strong yearly wave: January 7,242 liquidations, December 3,917, May 1,698 (factor 4.3). 7,207 blocking years end in January 2027 (Berlin 515, Hamburg 376). Industries: construction/crafts 0.80x, retail 0.45x, but IT 1.78x, staffing 1.81x, holding companies 1.42x (orderly wind-downs, not failures).
- 67 firms in the archive were revived, 21 after the dissolution line; classifying per line instead of per firm calls them wrongly dead.
- Register memory is short: insolvency notices freely searchable 2 weeks, other notices deleted after 1 month, no free alert service. Our archive is the only time window, but anyone can rebuild it from public search back to 2012, so it is not a moat. Sell filter, matching and next step, never the notice.
- Real estate: 778 GmbHs in liquidation in 12 months with property words in the name after excluding admin/holding/project/broker words (Berlin 66, Hamburg 33, Munich 26, Dusseldorf 15, Frankfurt 13, Cologne 12); 511 of 1,355 broad hits contain "Verwaltung" (often the general-partner GmbH of a KG that owns nothing).

**From email and outbound:**
- SMTP address checks from a home connection measure your own IP, not the address: 5,385 of 7,132 "does not exist" verdicts (75.5 %) were blocks against our home IP (it landed on a Spamhaus list through our own RCPT-only checks); 201 of 714 targets were silently excluded.
- Deliverability tests measure themselves: warm-up mails 18 of 20 in inbox, probe mails to the same boxes 2 of 2 in spam; machine-like subject 2 of 3 spam, real cold text 5 of 5 inbox. Microsoft 365 put our buyer text into junk (SCL 5) from two different senders with SPF/DKIM/DMARC passing, also without links: the text is the cause.
- Our measuring tools lie more often than the results are wrong: an umlaut in an IMAP search term made 2 of 2 inbox mails "unfindable".
- Links in cold mail reach almost nobody: 1 click on 261 delivered mails with a short link (0.4 %), 0 replies on 304.
- Contact forms of US small businesses: of 12,784 send attempts, 3,417 (26.7 %) were hard-rejected by the form endpoint (404/500/403/400/409/419), 71.9 % of those failed again 3 to 11 days later. Of 2,639 submitted forms only 252 confirmed (9.5 %); strictly measured 39.8 % arrived; rented servers delivered 8 %, the home Mac 45 %. Forms lose messages silently (Contact Form 7 via API, unactivated formsubmit.co, JS forms).
- Among arrived forms the reply rate was 1.2 % (3 of 252). Contact-form channel: 0.47 % reply per business at 0.03 USD per send (n = 2,529), about 6 USD per owner reply.
- Counting replies naively is wrong both ways: 6 of 13 opened "replies" were machines (autoresponders, newsletter signups, echoes); 4 of 19 real humans were in no count (spam folder, below the first screen). Real: 1 reply per 630 to 1,100 sends, 64 to 113 USD per real reply; 63.51 USD per reply and 402.21 USD per person who wanted to talk in the personal-question test.
- Replies depend on industry: consulting/coaching 1 per 204 (4 of 915), fitness 1 of 176, salon 1 of 227. Median reply after 7.3 hours, 12 of 12 within 48 h; 2 of 7 real repliers read the question as an accusation.
- We own a list: 10.3 million domains crawled in 16 hours for 45 USD server cost, giving 68,577 US firms with a working contact form, by industry and state.

**From our AI pipelines and David himself:**
- 14,969 of 26,764 paid AI drafts (55.9 %) were never sent: the booking step to Google Sheets failed with HTTP 429 (60 writes/min vs up to 96 parallel writers) and the code then aborted the send.
- The pipeline's own cost book underestimated real card charges by factor 2.53 (n = 11,795; independently 2.7 at n = 1,324). The Anthropic console shows credit without 19 % German VAT. Auto-reload charged 14 times on 2026-08-18; the last 6 receipts were 322 USD, half the month in three days.
- LLM personalization from Instagram bios invents facts: Claude Sonnet 4.5 in 70.7 % of messages (n = 58), with a grounding rule still 38.6 %; gemini-flash-lite 98.3 % and 80.8 %. Fact check rejected 49 % of drafts and another 48 % were lost at sending; a send cost 1.63 USD instead of the planned 0.07; 400 USD ran up overnight.
- Cold Instagram DMs to consumers: 1 reply per 265 fully tracked first contacts (0.38 %); our own board showed 2.91 % because second contacts were missing from the denominator. About 1,000 hand-written DMs to US fitness targets gave 5 replies, 2 of them bots (0.3 % real).
- In our Instagram setter-bot conversations for fitness offers, 10 of about 400 counterparts were 14 to 17 years old and 6 of them received a payment request; the flow did not catch age before the payment step. (A mistake on our side, recorded so it never happens again.)
- Cheap vision models cannot sort profile screenshots: gpt-4o-mini kept 6 of 20 of Sonnet's top 10 %; Sonnet cost 0.00696 USD per image. A website in the bio speaks AGAINST a fitness coach's ideal customer (6.91 % vs 23.11 %, n = 9,266). Screenshots: about 1 image per second per window, 16 h/day without block, 0.375 GB RAM per window, max 10 windows on 16 GB; 63 % of follower profiles usable.
- New Instagram accounts were sometimes blocked before first login; all outreach second accounts were blocked after a few days of following and messaging without a profile picture.
- Reach without the right audience brings no money: 12 million TikTok views in under half a year brought about 300 USD; 1,600 followers were all Indian teenagers, zero usable leads; zero sales at a 3,000 USD offer.
- Public reference lists are sorted by solidity, the opposite of default risk: only 7.6 % of 316 business sites name any customer, 40 % of names are corporations, 0 of 30 named customers were in the liquidation data. Distance-sorted company lists are noise for a trade: of 26 firms near a drywall business only 5 could be clients, the first at position 12.


## 11.1 Why idea generation starts from our own data, not public text (measured 2026-08-27)

Night of 2026-08-26/27, 94 agents, 7.6M tokens (per the measured note of 2026-08-27; the Thinker code header says about 11 million, see section 1). Two methods were run end to end:
- **Searching existing things:** 71 candidates. An adversarial panel killed **12 of 14** because a cheaper substitute already served the buyer. Survivors were capped at **1,600 EUR a month**.
- **Deriving** (crossing buyer economics with AI cost collapses and computing the stranded surplus): 15 candidates, better shaped. Then checked: **15 of 15 already occupied**; in **5 of 7** cases even by a FREE option (nexxt-change/KfW, DocInfo.org/FSMB, Talnet, opentender.eu, Litify Referral Network). "Who already sells this?" killed 12 of 15 of these derived ideas (a separate count from the 12 of 14 above), and our pipeline had missed all five free options until asked directly, the expensive class of miss.
- **Why:** deriving returns what is legible. Every input is public text, so anyone with the same models reaches the same list the same afternoon; incumbents got there first because they had the same access plus a head start.
- **Rules since then:** (1) "Who already sells this, also free?" runs BEFORE any other work on an idea; it costs about twenty minutes. Search explicitly for free and trade-association-backed options. (2) An unoccupied opportunity has to come from something not in public text: a private archive, a measurement only we have taken, or a relationship. David's own business came from exactly that (his own measured send cap against his own capture cost), not from research. (3) Before building any lever, get the **market price of the finished service**, fetched yourself from the seller's price page (rule of 2026-09-17: when David asked what buying the whole mail sending would cost, Instantly 97 USD and Smartlead 94 USD a month were found in ten minutes, while Woodpecker had been in our files as a statistics source since 2026-09-11 and was never considered as a supplier). Buying replaces build time, not sender reputation or measurement.
- **Correction, David 2026-09-20:** "occupied" does not mean bad ("there are markets with hundreds of sellers where everyone gets rich because there is room for thousands"); Liquidationsradar itself is an occupied market. "Who sells this" stays mandatory, but as demand proof, not as a killer. Killers are only: no reachable buyer, one dominant seller plus high switching costs, a license requirement, or delivery that needs staff and capital.
