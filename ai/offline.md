# Offline: keep social media feeds under 30 minutes a day

Feeds are built to keep you scrolling. Every hour they take is an hour you don't spend on friends, training, sleep or your own business.

## The rule

Tick **Offline** when your social media feeds add up to **less than 30 minutes today**. Check Screen Time (iPhone) or Digital Wellbeing (Android) before you tick. Guessing doesn't count.

What counts as a feed:

- TikTok, Instagram Reels and Explore, YouTube Shorts, Snapchat Spotlight and Stories, X, Reddit, Facebook.
- Any endless stream of content picked for you by an app.

What doesn't count:

- Messaging a real person. That's talking, not scrolling.
- Posting your own content and answering comments on it. But the minute you watch other people's videos "for research", the clock runs.
- A YouTube video you searched for on purpose to learn something specific.

## Why

- **The 30 minutes come from a study.** In 2018, students who limited social media to about 30 minutes a day for three weeks felt less lonely and less depressed than students who used it as usual. A 2025 study found two weeks without mobile internet improved wellbeing and attention.
- **Time is the real cost.** Two hours of scrolling a day is about 730 hours a year.
- **It eats your sleep.** Phones in bed push your bedtime back.
- **It trains your attention to jump.** A new clip every 15 seconds makes two hours of focused work feel much harder.

## How to start

1. **Look at your real number first.** Check your daily average in Screen Time for the last week. It's usually more than you think.
2. **Set app limits.** On iPhone: Settings, Screen Time, App Limits, Add Limit, then Social, 30 minutes. Under 18? Ask a parent to set the passcode so you can't just press "ignore".
3. **Move the apps.** Put feed apps in a folder on the last page, or delete them and use the slower browser version.
4. **Pick a time for your 30 minutes.** After Business and Training are done. Ticks first, then feeds.
5. **Keep the phone out of your bedroom.** That's part of the Sleep habit too.
6. **Have a replacement ready.** A podcast, a book or a voice memo for your business ideas.

## Mistakes

- **"I'll just check one thing."** That's how 30 minutes turns into two hours.
- **Calling scrolling "research".** Write down what you learned. If you can't, it was scrolling.
- **Moving the scrolling.** Quitting TikTok for two hours of Shorts is the same habit.
- **Ticking without checking.** The number in Screen Time decides, not your feeling.
- **Going to zero, then crashing.** Under 30 minutes every day for years beats one perfect week.

## What happened when I tried it

In November 2023 I described a made-up 13-year-old, the kind of kid I wanted to help, and wrote down his normal day: gaming and YouTube for about two hours after school, two more in the evening, then lying in bed unable to sleep and grabbing the phone again. Next to it I wrote my own rules: no social media in bed, lowest brightness in the evening, read before sleeping.

In January 2026 my rules said: the bed is a phone-free zone, and every waking second goes into making content, not watching TikTok. I spent 1 to 3 hours a day recording my own TikToks. Making them was useful. Watching other people's was the trap.

In 2026 I also noticed two costs I didn't expect. My attention got really weak. And I got pain from bad posture, because I was always sitting at the phone or the computer.

That's why feeds under 30 minutes became a daily habit. A rule like "every waking second for content" is too vague to check. A number in Screen Time isn't.
