## 5. How David wants to work (standing decisions)

- **Decide and act, explain afterwards** (memory "decide yourself"). Ask only for sending messages, money, accounts and irreversible things. Product, design, price and order questions are decided by the AI with 1 to 2 sentences of data.
- **Repair, do not announce** (2026-10-25: "dumb sentence"). Never write "write X and I will fix it"; fix it immediately and only name a real human obstacle.
- **Everything is the AI's job** except what only a human can do. Before putting anything on David's list, try it yourself and measure live; at most 3 options.
- **Only real human tasks reach David**, and only as switches in his app (2026-11-06, refined 2026-11-07). No cards in chat (he does not see suggestion cards). Never send yes/no lists ("yes 1, 2, 3"); a course-based judge decides, David gets the result.
- **Reports to David: extremely short** (2026-11-01), grammar may suffer; never more than 3 lines on WhatsApp. Texts to companies stay proper.
- **No long dashes** in any text (they look like AI).
- **German companies get proper capitalization**; lowercase only for Instagram DMs and texts to Timo.
- **Mails to providers in the language of their last mail** (an English-speaking provider got a German mail on 2026-10-07 and it caused two tickets).
- **Never write to people David knows**; only drafts. Strangers and companies without asking, but since 2026-11-06 through a sending check (a check card and a second reader); money amounts and payment methods are asked, never fixed in the text.
- **A reply to someone who answered goes out after 5 review rounds**, not presented to David (a supplier waited 106 hours once).
- **Every draft to a person is checked 5 times against David's objections first.** Yes/no questions bring one bit; open questions are work for him.
- **Login codes:** every login or confirmation code from the mail app goes at the top of the answer immediately; David types it.
- **Business clients only in writing:** never calls, video meetings or meetings in company offers (his age would kill the deal).
- **Never delete what David built** (reviews, pages, texts) without asking, not even on the Musk judge's order (2026-11-02).
- **Never doubt David's statements**; if in doubt, ask one line, never rewrite his text (2026-10-30, a true family story was wrongly treated as an excuse).
- **Look before delivering:** every page is checked as a screenshot at 390 px width before it is shown; "Musk approved" only with every file opened.
- **Windows may be brought to the front** (since 2026-11-02; the older "screen belongs to the office" rule is lifted). Background remains first choice where it costs nothing.
- **Logins live in David's real Chrome profile**; never copy the Chrome profile (on 2026-10-31 that logged out all four Google accounts).
- **Tasks for David as WhatsApp polls** in a "ToDos" group was the October way; since 2026-11-06 his app replaces it.
- **Each answer ends with two Musk lines** ("Musk to Claude", "Musk to David") and then a compact to-do list (David 2026-11-02).
- **Legal questions are decided by the lawyer** (2026-11-02, 2026-11-03): never judge yourself; give David the question as a copy block (now: as a switch in the app); his lawyer's answers are not re-researched (2026-11-06).

### Reviews
- Never invented reviews in the real store (2026-11-02), even if David asks. Real reviews of the same product from other stores are fine (lawyer). The preview store with placeholder banner keeps its sample reviews.
- 2026-11-05 lawyer: choose freely; below 4.7, remove the most harmful bad reviews until 4.7.
- 2026-11-07 thought experiment on review counts (David's question, not an order to set a number): best range 5,000 to 10,000 per product [LIKELY]; above 10,000 no proven extra benefit; harm guessed from about 20,000 to 25,000 in an own store [GUESS]; David fixed 6,928 as "optimal" within the range. Evidence: no experiment varies only the count; PowerReviews 2021 observational lift vs 0 reviews: 1-100 +76.7%, 101-250 +143.2%, 251-500 +166.4%, 501-1,000 +172.1%, 1,001-5,000 +224.7%, 5,000+ +292.6%; survey N=9,012: 64% prefer over 1,000 vs 100, 54% over 10,000 vs 1,000; Northwestern: 5 vs 0 reviews +270%, almost all of the rise in the first 10; course voices: "once you get to 10,000 there's not really any point"; our 16 competitors at 50 to 100 USD: median 394, max 1,749. Reviews per buyer are 0.13 to 8.3%, so 100,000 reviews would imply at least 1.2 million buyers. In the real store only the real count of imported reviews is shown; a freely set count is not built.

### Research and knowledge

### Thinker rules (2026-09-20)
- Capital need is not an exclusion: an idea needing 10,000 EUR that returns 1 million gets a line on the test card (capital need, return, time to return, where the money comes from).
- "Occupied" is not an exclusion: someone selling it proves demand. Exclude only: no reachable buyer, one provider with crushing share plus high switching costs, licensing requirement, or delivery needs staff and capital we do not have. Many small providers with public prices are a plus.
- Price anchor is a mandatory, heavily weighted criterion: prefer niches where existing providers charge 200 EUR a month or more (on their price page, machine-checkable). 79 EUR must not anchor new ideas.

Other corrections from memory (same weight):
- **Numbers:** never report events as things. Check denominator, unique count and real invoice before quoting any number (67% duplicate sends and a 4x wrong cost both sat in data already available).
- **Deploys:** a fix is live only when the deployed hash matches on every machine (a fix ran on 0 of 5 servers all night).
- **Measurements:** compare only measurements of the same code version; an older file has a date but not the code state. A measuring tool can destroy its own input (a 91.3% score measured company names that the tool itself had cut apart); read generated test inputs yourself, and two numbers for the same quantity are a finding.
- **Documents cite documents:** one invented line became a Monday appointment in nine files. Always check against the mailbox, never against another document.
- **A screenshot does not prove the speaker:** two chats were read as one and four sentences attributed to the wrong person.
- **WhatsApp chat reader shows old messages:** check the preview before saying "no answer".
- **A true sentence can kill the sale:** "you can do this yourself in two minutes" stood above our own proof.
- **Clock times come from the clock:** on 2026-11-02 the AI wrote times up to 95 minutes in the future.
- **Paths:** a public GitHub repository for the store preview contained internal notes (lawyer, mails, foreign images, payment click path); excluded on 2026-11-02. Check what a public repo publishes.
