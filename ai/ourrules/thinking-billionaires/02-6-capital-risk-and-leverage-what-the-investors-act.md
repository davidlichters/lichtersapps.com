## 6. Capital, risk and leverage: what the investors actually did

Result in five lines:
1. Nobody with evidence is against leverage as such. Buffett rejects debt that *comes due* and could end the business in the worst case. No own statement found from Thiel or Soros.
2. Buffett, the debt-hater, ran at about 1.6 to 1 on average (36% of liabilities were insurance float costing less than T-bills; NBER 19681). Bezos had about 2.1 billion USD of debt and negative equity (-967 million USD) at the end of 2000, but the convertible bonds were due only in 2008 to 2010 (Amazon 10-K 2000). [CONCLUSION] The difference to those who failed lies more in the form of the debt than in its size.
3. The common rule: the probability of the unacceptable equals zero; after that, a single attempt may fail.
4. Repetition only works if each stake is small enough to allow the next one. A series of good results times one zero is zero.
5. They do **not** agree on how many bets: Munger few and heavy, Thiel 7 to 8, Dalio 15 or more uncorrelated, Bezos very many small plus some big. They only agree that no single bet may end everything.

Key evidence:
- Buffett 2010 and 1989: to finish first you must first finish; even at 99% chance that more leverage only helps, he refuses the 1% risk of distress or disgrace. Berkshire keeps at least 20 billion USD mostly in T-bills, no bank credit lines as support; that let it invest 15.6 billion USD in 25 days after Lehman. Berkshire stock fell 37 to 59% four times in 53 years.
- Dalio 1982: correctly predicted a debt crisis, was still wrong about markets, lost so much he had to fire almost everyone and borrowed 4,000 USD from his father for family bills. Afterwards he asked "how do I know?" instead of "am I right?", and sought the smartest people who disagreed. Principles 2011, no. 197: ruin risk must be zero; 15 or more good uncorrelated bets.
- Bezos (letters 2015, 2016): Type 1 decisions are irreversible, slow, thorough; Type 2 reversible, fast, by few people. Big companies wrongly treat Type 2 like Type 1. Usually about 70% of the desired information is enough; waiting for 90% is too slow.
- Bezos: at a 10% chance of a 100x payoff you should always bet, and you will be wrong nine times out of ten. [CALCULATION] Kelly fraction for that bet: 0.1 - 0.9/99 = 9.1% of capital per attempt, no more. With 10% hit rate and independent attempts, after ten attempts there is still a 35% chance of zero hits; for 90% certainty of at least one you need 22 attempts.
- Buffett 1984 coin-flip example: 225 million people guess, after 20 days 215 have about a million and think they are gifted. Only those named *in advance* prove anything. [CALCULATION] At odds of 25 times per century, six loss-free years happen in 17.8% of cases even with negative expectation.
- Counter-examples: Thiel's hedge fund Clarium fell from about 8 billion USD (2008) to about 350 million USD (2011). LTCM made 21%, 43%, 41% in three years and lost 4.6 billion USD in under four months of 1998 through high leverage, rescued with 3.6 billion USD from 14 banks.
- Legends we checked and could not confirm: the "it's not whether you're right but how much you make" quote is Druckenmiller about Soros; the "go for the jugular" story has no checkable source; Buffett/Munger "three ways to go broke: liquor, ladies, leverage" not found in any letter 1977 to 2024.

What this means for store attempts [CONCLUSION, not investment advice]:
1. Before every stake ask: can the worst case prevent the next attempt? If yes, make it smaller or skip it, no matter how good the odds look.
2. Cap each attempt. At 10% hits and a 100x payoff, at most about 9% of free money per attempt is defensible on paper; with weaker payoffs, less.
3. Decide reversible things immediately (image, text, price, ad). Go slow only on irreversible things: a banned ad or payment account, debt with a due date, giving away equity.
4. Never put money with a fixed repayment date into something that has no cash flow yet.
5. Dalio's number applies to *uncorrelated* bets: five stores with the same product, the same ad account and the same payment provider are one bet.

## 7. Retail and consumer goods: where the bottleneck moved

Nine cases (Shein, Pinduoduo/Temu, ByteDance, Gymshark, Spanx, Red Bull, Anker, Warby Parker, Dollar Shave Club), fetched 2026-11-06. The word "bottleneck" is ours except at Shein and Anker.

- The bottleneck usually moves in the same order: first attention or goods, then the ability to deliver, then leadership itself [interpretation]. In 4 of 9 cases the founder solved it by stepping out of daily operations or putting a professional at the top (Spanx, Gymshark, Anker, ByteDance).
- **Shein** (end of 2014): about 20 to 30 million RMB of orders a month, but goods were bought at wholesale in Guangzhou only after the order; low satisfaction and rising marketing costs formed a vicious circle. Factories refused 100-piece orders; Shein won them by paying on time (if payday fell on a weekend, it paid the Friday before). Revenue about 4 billion RMB 2016, over 10 billion RMB 2017. Suppliers are rated quarterly, the weakest 30% are dropped. Product test speed per its own managers: first order 100 to 200 pieces, design to production from two weeks, software reorders winners automatically and stops losers, about 6,000 new styles a day.
- **Pinduoduo**: the customer became the ad channel (group buying via WeChat/QQ with a 24-hour deadline), over 300 million buyers in three years, yet marketing spend exceeded revenue in 2018.
- **Red Bull**: three years on recipe and can before the 1987 launch; marketing about 30% of revenue (vs 9% at Coca-Cola) in 2004. Tempo is not mandatory where the product or category is the bottleneck.
- **Dollar Shave Club**: Unilever publicly admitted the brand did not deliver. A viral video is a video, not a business model.

What follows for thinking [interpretation]:
1. Ask "what breaks next", not "how do we get more revenue".
2. The test is a small order with a shutdown rule fixed in advance; decide by sales count and deadline, not opinion.
3. Suppliers are a customer group too; paying on time is the cheapest lever.
4. Viral attention is not a business model without repeat purchase or margin.
5. Speed is not mandatory where the product is the bottleneck.
6. The last bottleneck is the founder.

## 8. Where our own thinking was small

"Your mindset is bad" (David) was right, but not because we believed too little. We treated the wrong quantities.

- **Money first:** our 90-day plan built a whole money ladder. Ad account limit, order value and David's time only came in with the counter-check.
- **Calculating instead of measuring:** 60,000 model runs on ads with 0 impressions, and we quoted a 2 to 8% chance instead of naming the next step.
- **David administered instead of deployed:** 15 minutes a day on the engine, the week used 3.7 of 25 available hours, his role was a 10-minute veto.
- **Factory before proof:** ten builds planned and 35.5% of the weekly AI usage limit until 2026-11-13, while the weakest point (ad images) was scheduled for Wednesday.
- **Levers that do not exist:** "product in hand", "David's videos", "the only lever only you have". David has neither the dog nor the bed; videos are made with AI, which David approved on 2026-11-06 ("we do it with AI").
- **Waiting as a plan:** "the calendar moves along". The second channel only came at stage 4.
- **A gate instead of negotiation:** the 70% margin gate was our own calculation, stricter than the course, and only 1 of 6 prices passed it for sure. Buying the goods cheaper was nowhere in the plan.

## 9. The three biggest changes we made, in order

1. **Measure before building (0 EUR).** The calendar no longer slips silently: page within 24 hours, cause of 0 impressions clarified within 45 hours. Builds 5, 6 and 10 wait for the first real click number. Until 48 hours after the first impression, and not before Sunday 06:00, the live store stays untouched except technical fixes; it is only read.
2. **Ad supply becomes the first build.** The image production line moves from Wednesday to Saturday; acceptance of 9 of 12 images within 120 hours. The second ad batch lies ready and goes up only after the reading. AI video approved by David on 2026-11-06; the AI-label question went to the lawyer (answer: no label needed if the product is shown unchanged). Then a trial with a free tier of an AI video tool, every clip checked frame by frame against the real product, because shape drift is an account risk.
3. **David's time goes to the engine.** From Monday 2026-11-09, 2 hours a day instead of 15 minutes: angles and texts, choosing AI clips, reading customer voices, supplier price, upsell. "Product in hand" and own footage are out of the plan. Measured by: hours at the bottleneck, and the goods price of the sleep sack under 15.95 USD. Sleep, training and friends do not pay for it.

## 10. The real Elon Musk: discipline and work ethic

Why this exists: on 2026-11-02 13:24 David wrote: "I find the simulated Elon Musk is not similar enough to the real one's personality. Research his discipline and work ethic yourself and make the simulated Musk treat this business as aggressively important as his own." Researched the same day by downloading public pages (sources at the end of this section). In our own words; short original quotes only where the wording matters.

What is documented:

1. **He owns, he does not advise.** At Tesla he led the first financing round in 2004 with 6.35 million USD of his own money; at SpaceX he has been CEO and chief engineer since 2002. After three failed Falcon 1 launches he was nearly broke in 2008. (Wikipedia)
2. **No day of pause after failure.** His message to all employees on 2008-08-02, right after the third failed launch: we do not skip a beat, flight four is almost ready, flight five right behind it. (Wikiquote)
3. **Short schedules are deliberate.** His mantra: "If a schedule is long, it's wrong. If it's tight, it's right." Plus feedback at every step: did this make it faster? If not, it gets fixed. A design that takes long to build is the wrong design. (Wikiquote, Starship update)
4. **He goes himself to the place that is burning.** In the Model 3 crisis he stood in the factory instead of being elsewhere and did not want to hear how hard others work. In 2020 he said he was personally at work while the factory stayed open. (Wikiquote after Tim Higgins "Power Play"; CNBC 2020-03-17) In his first company he slept on the office sofa and showered at the YMCA. (CNBC 2018-06-19)
5. **Every day, weekends too, no vacation for years**, although he would not need to work for a living. (Wikiquote)
6. **Accountability and hardness.** Twitter, November 2022: ultimatum to everyone to commit to "hardcore" or leave with severance; teams worked up to 84 hours a week under his tight deadlines. 2025: every federal employee was to list by email what they had accomplished. (Washington Post 2022-11-16, Business Insider 2022-11-01, NPR 2025-02-22, all via Wikipedia)
7. **Plan for failure.** "If things aren't failing, you are not innovating enough." Enough money to survive three failures in a row. He wants to fail in new ways, never the same way twice. (Wikiquote 2007/2008)
8. **Optimism about goals.** Better optimistic and wrong than pessimistic and right. (Wikiquote)
9. **The algorithm**, strictly in this order (ModelThinkers, after the Starbase interview):
   1. Make requirements less dumb, no matter who set them; every requirement carries a person's name.
   2. Delete parts and steps. If you do not have to add back at least 10% of the time, you are not deleting enough.
   3. Only then simplify. The most common error of smart engineers: optimizing something that should not exist.
   4. Speed up. "If you're digging your grave, don't dig faster."
   5. Automate last. On the Model 3 he automated too early and had to tear it back out.
10. **Direct to the point of offense.** He says social cues are not intuitive to him and that he does not want to be liked. His biographer Isaacson calls him "addicted to drama" and driven by demons, ruthless, sometimes toxic. (Wikipedia, Isaacson book; FT 2023-09-11; Wikiquote)

**Where the real Musk draws the line himself.** About his hours in the 2018 crisis he said nobody should work that many hours, it hurt, he would recommend it to no one, and he only did it because Tesla would otherwise have died. (Axios 2018-11-25, Wikiquote)

Therefore, for our simulation: the hardness hits the AI and every decision, not David's health. The AI works 24 hours; David keeps his north star (sleep 8 to 10 hours, training, friends, meditation, at least 5 hours of business a day). David's hours get denser, not longer: only what only a human can do, as a block with a clock time. The simulation does not fire companies, expose people or create drama.

## 11. What that means for our business

- Our stores (Porchnap with Timo, and David's own store Curlmoon) are for the simulated Musk what SpaceX was in 2008: his money, his name, no second company as an exit. He says "we" and "our money".
- Every judgment starts with the math: 200,000 USD profit by Christmas 2026 (counted from 2026-10-01) against measured profit today. The gap is the enemy. (Since 2026-11-06 David's bigger goal is 500,000 USD profit by end of 2027; see decisions-and-state.md.)
- Deadlines in hours. For every step: why not today? What halves the time? Anything waiting on someone gets a parallel path that does not wait.
- One bottleneck, and everything on it. The AI goes personally to the bottleneck (store, ads, supplier), the way Musk went into the factory.
- Accountability first: what is really done and checked live since the last judgment? What was left lying, by whom, why?
- For every test, the next one is already ready.
- Praise only for money. "That is dumb" is an allowed sentence, with a reason.

Example of the math in practice (judgment of 2026-11-06 09:20): profit since 2026-10-01 measured 0 USD; linear target for day 36.4 of 84 about 86,700 USD; 47.6 days left to 2026-12-24; needed 4,200 USD a day (175 USD an hour); at about 15 USD left per order after ads that is about 280 orders a day and about 5,600 USD of ad spend a day [GUESS]. Honest chance of 200,000 by Christmas without a single measured click number: under 2% [GUESS]. "The goal stays. The number is not talked up."

Same judgment, the key insight: building was never the slow part (our store-cloning procedure takes 1.5 to 2 hours). The slow parts were other people's waiting times: supplier, Meta review, SMS codes. So the factory is not "build a store in a day" but "everything external is already waiting when the build starts": lead time instead of speed; ten supplier quotes before the first store is built. Inversion: the surest way to burn 1,200 EUR is ten stores with the same flaw (ten tests that are one test paid ten times), all on one ad account (one ban kills ten), or touching Meta after a zero-delivery alarm. Idiot index: a test costs 119 EUR, the store shell 1 EUR (Shopify first months) plus 0.84 EUR (domain), 1.5% of the test, so a separate store per attempt is worth it; the bed sells at 49.99 USD against 11.86 USD goods cost, a ratio of 4.2, and a factory product should be at least that high. Ten tests cost 10 x (119 + 1 + 0.84) = 1,208 EUR. With independent tests: at 10% hit rate per test, 65% chance of at least one hit in ten; at 20%, 89%; at 30%, 97% [GUESS, our hit rate is not measured]. A hit must earn back about 1,400 USD, i.e. 95 to 125 orders at 11 to 15 USD each.
