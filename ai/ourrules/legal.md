# Legal answers we got for our stores

> What our lawyer told us in November 2026 for our own case: a seller based in Germany, selling physical products by dropshipping to US customers through Shopify and Meta ads. This is our case, not legal advice for yours. Rules differ by country, and US sellers have to follow US rules (for example the FTC rules on reviews and health claims). Ask your own lawyer.

## How we handle legal questions

- We never judge a legal question ourselves. A new question goes to the lawyer in writing, the answer is written down, and then it is followed without re-asking.
- An answer from the lawyer is not re-researched afterwards.

## Product photos and videos

- Supplier photos and videos may be used in the store and in ads.
- Photos and videos of the identical product from other listings and stores may be used, and may be edited (background removed, color, text, AI rework).
- Where images come from is stated exactly once: one small line in the store footer. Ads carry no source line; a grey credit line in an ad image hurts it.
- Our own limit on top: never show a different product than the one the buyer gets, and never use another seller's video 1:1 (cut and mix instead; that is an ad-performance rule).

## AI-made ads

- AI-generated ad videos need no "made with AI" label in the video, as long as the actual product is shown unchanged and true to the real item. Animals, rooms and scenes around it may be generated.
- So every AI clip is checked against the real product photos before it runs.

## Claims

- Subjective words are fine: "feels calming", "cozy", "feels like a hug".
- Real customer voices are fine if they are recognisable as personal experiences.
- Products in kids' sizes may be sold if the product suits that age group.
- Our own limit: we do not write our own medical claim ("helps against ADHD or anxiety") without evidence, also because Meta's ad rules on personal attributes and health forbid it.

## Reviews

- We never invent reviews.
- Reviews from real buyers of the same product are shown under a neutral heading, with their origin stated once in the footer.
- We may choose freely which real reviews we show. If the average is below 4.7 stars, we remove at least as many of the most harmful bad reviews as needed to reach 4.7. Sort by harm, not by stars. The heading never says "all reviews".
- Newer store rule (David, 2026-11-07 18:15, not a lawyer answer): the displayed average must be 4.7 or 4.8, above 4.8 leave out some 5-star reviews, and no star bars. Details: section 18 in https://lichtersapps.com/ai/ops/store-building/03-14-restyling-a-live-shopify-theme-to-look-like-ano.md

## Books

- A book written fully with AI may be sold under your own name, without an AI label.
- Books from a supplier: we sell them only when the supplier confirms the rights for that title. Publishers can pass on distribution rights.

## Complaints

- If a platform or rights holder complains: remove the content, replace it, continue. A removal is not a verdict and costs nothing.
- A demand for money needs a legal basis; then the lawyer decides.

## Course knowledge

- Knowledge learned from bought courses may be rewritten in your own words, published and even sold as your own product. You don't have to reinvent every fact.
- Not allowed: copying the course platform or its videos and reselling them as the original course. That is why our e-commerce pages are rewritten, without course names or original material.

## Every lawyer answer in detail (moved here from decisions-and-state section 8 on 2026-11-07)

The sections above are the summary; these are the details of every answer. Same caveat: German seller, US customers, our case only. Every line was decided by David's lawyer (asked 2026-11-02 in writing and 2026-11-03 by phone, more on 2026-11-05 to 11-07). Follow strictly, do not question, do not re-ask, no own additions, use every freedom. New legal question: never judge yourself; put the question as a switch in David's app and write the answer down.

**Media, in detail:**
1. Supplier photos and videos: in the store and in ads.
2. Photos and videos from other AliExpress listings with the same product.
3. Photos and videos from competitor stores with the same product, including their own photos with dog and living room.
4. Customer photos from other people's reviews (AliExpress, competitors): in the store and in ads.
5. Other people's TikTok and Instagram videos showing the product: download and use as ads, whole or cut.
6. All of it may be edited: remove background, remove logo, change color, add text, regenerate with AI.

**Reviews, in detail:**
7. Reviews of the same product from other stores (AliExpress and competitor stores with provably the same product): copy texts verbatim; translate foreign-language reviews faithfully. No warning letter happens this way.
8. A neutral heading above the reviews; no source note on the individual review.
9a. Selecting (2026-11-05, almost an hour on the phone, confirmed in writing): we choose freely which reviews we show. While the average is below 4.7 stars, remove at least as many of the most harmful bad reviews as needed to reach 4.7. Harm decides, not stars: a 3-star review like "has nothing to do with the picture" hurts more than a 1-star like "ordered the wrong size, but good". Applies to every store.
- Very similar products (2026-11-06 evening): reviews may be transferred even for a very similar product. Our own limit (not a legal judgment): no reviews describing different goods than the buyer gets; if supplier data or a sample proves it is the same goods, use the normal way.

**Must:**
10. Origin of images and reviews stated exactly once: one line in the small print of the store footer, nowhere else. Pattern used: "Reviews and customer photos shown come from buyers of the same bed at other stores. Some product images, photos and videos come from suppliers and other sellers of this bed."
11. Complaint from a platform or rights holder: remove, replace, continue. A money demand needs a legal basis: then David and the lawyer.
9. Ad images without a source line (a grey origin text in the image very likely hurts strongly).

**Order for the next store:**
- Use the product image with Google Lens to find all stores with the same product; confirm via each store's public /products.json.
- Take their reviews (Judge.me stores expose them through the reviews widget endpoint) and customer photos (rows 7 and 8).
- Best photos and videos for store and ads from supplier, AliExpress, competitors, TikTok and Instagram (rows 1 to 6).
- Set the footer line (row 10); build ads without source line (row 9).

**Health and target-group claims (sleep sack, 2026-11-06):** question: can we say it helps with ADHD, sensory overload or anxiety at bedtime without a study; "feels calming"; real customer voices; kids' sizes? The lawyer's first answer said subjective wording is fine but concrete ADHD/anxiety/therapy claims are stronger health promises. His **second answer, which counts** (David: "The first time he got it wrong. The latest one is right, he says"): marketing may include concrete statements about possible personal effects; statements about ADHD, sensory overload, anxiety or sleep problems may be used as description of the expected product benefit, without a study; matching customer voices may illustrate it; kids' sizes are fine if the product suits the age group. Our implementation: name the target group ("made for sensory seekers"), real customer voices yes; the AI itself does not write a healing claim ("helps against ADHD or anxiety") for lack of evidence and because of Meta's rules on personal attributes and health.

**AI videos (2026-11-06):** AI-generated ad videos from real supplier photos (animal and room generated, the bed unchanged) may run in Meta ads to US customers without an AI label in the video, because AI is only a production tool and the buying decision depends on the product's properties. Duty: the bed stays unchanged and true to the product; every clip is checked against it.

**Books (2026-11-06 evening, David's summary after an hour's call):**
1. Book fully written with AI: sell, own name, no AI label, also from Germany via Shopify to the US.
2. Book with every sentence reworded: per the answer allowed, even same order and statements.
3. Book 1:1 with new cover, title, author: per the answer allowed.
4. Distance to the original: per the answer none needed.
5. Liability, customs: no special risk per the answer.
6. Coach or helper: allowed, no automatic liability.
7. Reviews, numbers, timers: the answer names appealing texts, images, real customer opinions, time-limited discounts; it does not clear invented numbers.
8. Children's books: no special checks.
9. A friend's book with his permission: settled.
Our implementation: the AI works on 1 and 9 fully. Points 2 and 3 with a protected book of a stranger as the source are outside the AI's work (they conflict with copyright as the AI understands it); David has the letter and his decision ("we do books") is his own, done without the AI. All listed suppliers told David by phone they hold rights to their books in part; do not ask them again. For the AI's own work, without proof for the individual title it does not count as a license. Product search for books takes own AI books, books with the author's permission, public-domain titles and titles with an existing license.

**Publisher titles from a China supplier (2026-11-07):** asked about three titles of a major US publisher (an advent calendar book and two cookbooks): in principle fine; if the supplier confirms he holds the rights, there is no reason to doubt the right to sell; publishers can pass on distribution rights; shipping from China to US customers with a company in Germany changes nothing, normal dropshipping. Condition: David's yes from his rights call with the supplier.

**Course knowledge (2026-11-07):** "You may learn the knowledge from the bought courses, prepare it, publish it on your website and develop your own product from it. You do not have to reinvent everything or phrase every single piece of information completely differently." Limit: not simply copying the whole course platform and reselling it as the original course. So lichtersapps.com/ecom may be public, indexed and sold (e-book, course, members area); no original videos, no 1:1 platform.

**Not legal questions, but binding:** course rule: never another seller's video 1:1; cut and mix with own clips (ad performance). David's ad rules stay (no "small pets", no deadline, no shipping). Invented reviews: the AI's own single limit, not a lawyer topic.

**Still open with the lawyer:** whether Meta ads are paid as a business or privately (VAT).
