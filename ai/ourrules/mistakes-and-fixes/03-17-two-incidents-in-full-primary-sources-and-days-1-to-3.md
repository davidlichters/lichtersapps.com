## 17. Two incidents in full: documents cite documents, and days 1 to 3

Added 2026-11-07 so the details are findable. Short versions: list items 14 and 29 on the map page, the entry "2026-08-27, motivation insight" in section 6 on page 2, and the rule "Primary sources only" in https://lichtersapps.com/ai/ourrules/working-rules/03-11-verification-before-you-say-done-or-quote-a-num.md

### 17.1 2026-08-24: a fabricated log line and the primary-source rule

Source: our memory note "documents cite documents", written 2026-08-24.

**What happened.** On 2026-08-24 I found the line **"Sounds good, talk to you then."** in our conversation log file (a notes file of sales conversations), attributed to a prospect and dated 2026-08-21 23:40. **No such email exists.** The real email thread ends on 2026-08-21 at 16:40 with "Let's set up a call".

**What grew from that one line:** a confirmed Monday 17:00 appointment, a Google Meet link that appears in no email, a **hard stop at 16:45 on another, real sales call** so the phantom meeting could start on time, and a line on a page built for an outside reader. It reached **nine places**: the "now" file (twice), the Monday plan (twice), the plan for the next morning (twice), the handover file, a prep file for the phantom call and a fix file for the real call.

**Cause.** Each file was written by reading the file before it. Nobody re-opened the mailbox. A claim copied four times looks four times more true and is still one unverified claim.

**Rule.** Before any date, appointment, quote or reply count reaches a document, re-derive it from the primary source (the mailbox, the ledger or the billing console), never from another document. When a claim already sits in several files, that is a reason for **more** suspicion, not less.

**Side effect of the cross-check.** Checking the conversation log against the mailbox also corrected the reply count: 15 replies were real, but 4 were polite goodbyes, and only **3 people actually asked to be contacted**.

### 17.2 2026-08-27: what drove the 33 unpaid days, and how to design days 1 to 3

Source: our memory note "obligation, not motivation" and the document "Designing days 1 to 3", both written 2026-08-27 from David's own 33-day record.

**The finding.** Reconstructed on 2026-08-27 from David's own **1,498 messages** in the July to August chat log: he was not motivated for 33 days, he was **obligated**. A prospect expecting a call at 16:00 pulled harder than any decision to work hard. Every dead stretch in the log is a stretch with nobody waiting on him.

**Why it matters.** When the first money signal is 30 to 90 days out, nothing internal bridges the gap. Dates owned by other people and a number that moves daily do. So: do not design stress. Design **obligations with dates** and **a counter that moves every day**.

**Day 1**

1. **A stranger's expectation on the calendar inside 24 hours.** Not "I will research this week", but "I told Mr X I would send him something Thursday." One real name, one real date. The strongest item on this list, and it costs nothing.
2. **One public artifact with his name on it.** A one-page site, a post, anything findable. Quitting silently gets harder once something exists in the world.

**Day 2**

3. **Build the scoreboard before the business.** Money is a 30 to 90 day signal, so it cannot carry week 2. Pick ONE proxy number that rises only on days work happens: conversations started, forms sent, replies received. David had sends per day. A daily-moving counter is the bridge until money arrives.
4. **Spend money that leaves a receipt.** Not the whole 1,000 EUR: 200 to 300 EUR, specific, non-refundable. Sunk cost was a real part of David's fuel, and it is the honest version of "need".

**Day 3**

5. **Engineer a fast no, not a slow maybe.** Ten conversations that each end in a euro figure or a written no beat three that end in "let's stay in touch". Rejection creates urgency, silence creates drift. David's dead days were the silent ones.
6. **Five written lines every night to one person who actually reads them.** David wrote a plan file for the next morning every night, and those held his month together. Reporting is not co-owning, so this survives a no-partnership rule.

**What will not work.** Manufacturing desperation in someone who is not desperate. The person will know it is invented, and invented pressure produces quitting faster than no pressure at all.

**What transfers.** Not the fear, but the structure: real people, real dates, a number that moves every day, and a loop short enough that progress arrives before patience runs out.

(This plan was written for a friend starting his first venture; it applies to any new venture.)
