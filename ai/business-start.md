# Start your first business at 13

Pick one skill, build something tiny, get real people to use it, and stay with that one model much longer than feels comfortable.

## What it is

A first business at 13 is not about money yet. It is a cheap way to learn what every business needs: making something, finding people who want it, and asking them to pay.

## Why

- **Experience is the real profit.** Most first attempts make nothing. What you keep is the skill: editing, selling, building, talking to strangers.
- **Small tests are cheap at 13.** A failed test costs a few weeks. At 30 it costs rent.
- **Results come late.** Many businesses look dead for months and then start working. Most people quit right before that point.

## How to start

1. **Pick one skill you can practise every day.** Video editing, drawing, writing, coding, fitness, fixing things.
2. **Turn it into one small offer.** Thumbnails for small YouTubers, a simple app, lawn mowing on your street, selling old stuff on eBay.
3. **Check that someone already sells it.** If others make money with it, there is demand. If nobody sells it, that is usually a warning, not a gap.
4. **Find your first 10 customers by hand.** Ask what they would pay for. Asking feels scary, but people notice you much less than you think, and one week of practice makes the fear of a "no" smaller. Write your first sentence down and count asks, not yeses. More: https://lichtersapps.com/ai/business.md
5. **Decide your test length before you start.** For content or a shop, think in months. Write the date down and do not switch before it.
6. **Measure one number every day.** Videos posted, people asked, sales, euros. If it never moves, change one thing, not the whole business.
7. **Bring your parents in early.** Under 18, payment accounts, store accounts and contracts usually need a parent. Check the rules in your country about selling and taxes.
8. **Protect the basics.** Sleep, school and training stay. More: https://lichtersapps.com/ai/business.md

## Mistakes

- **Switching every few weeks.** Every restart throws away what you learned.
- **Watching instead of doing.** Business videos feel like work. They are not.
- **Fake shortcuts.** Fake personas, fake reviews, lying about your age or results, spam, betting. They hurt people, get accounts banned, and teach you nothing.

## What happened when I tried it

I (David) started in 2022, at 12:

- **2022 to 2023:** Fiverr, a Minecraft YouTube channel, affiliate marketing, printed T-shirts, a bubblegum machine, Amazon Influencer, selling tennis balls. One Minecraft video got 100,000 views. Fiverr got no orders. All of 2023 came to roughly **minus 160 euros**. Only mowing lawns and selling old stuff on eBay worked a little, both a lot of work.
- **Two I regret:** a TikTok account where I pretended to be someone else to sell a ChatGPT course (about 300 followers), and "scamming scammers". Both were dishonest. Show your real self and sell something you actually know.
- **Aug 2023 to Mar 2024:** I watched a huge number of Hamza Ahmed videos and ran my own self-improvement YouTube channel. Watching counted as "business" in my head. It was not.
- **Spring 2024 to January 2025:** school got hard and almost nothing happened.
- **2025:** fitness coaching. First sale on 31 October 2025, about three years after I started: 25 euros for a training guide. Coaching came to about 374 euros by the end of 2025.
- **Late 2025 to 2026:** 1:1 coaching. In August 2026, about 5,800 messages through company contact forms: 15 replies, 0 sales. Basically spam, do not copy it. Then a business-alert service (0 paying customers).

What these years taught me: every test made the next one better, but I kept switching before anything had time to work. Stay with one honest model long enough.
