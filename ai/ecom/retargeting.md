# Retargeting

> Retargeting brings back people who already saw your video, your store or your cart: build warm audiences with clear time windows, change the message over time, keep frequency low and never push a first-sale deal to people who already bought.

## Rules

1. Retargeting means showing ads to people who already did something with your brand, like watching a video, viewing a product, adding to cart or starting checkout.
2. In 2026 the standard warm (BOF, "bottom of funnel") audiences are site visitors, video viewers, cart and checkout abandoners, page engagers and your uploaded email list.
3. If your product cannot sell without an explanation, warm cold people up with video ads first, because viewers who watch 50% or more flow into your warm pool on their own (2026).
4. People who are ready to buy should be sent right to the product page or the offer, skipping the long story (2026).
5. Keep frequency at 3 or fewer ad views per person per day, and lower it when you retarget on several networks at once (older advice).
6. Do not show the same ad to the same person for 30 days; switch the message, for example a new ad every 10 days (older advice).
7. Show dynamic product ads only for the first 7 days after a product view, then move to other ad types (older advice).
8. Never show first-purchase promotions to existing customers.
9. Do not rush to a discount: some people only need more time, and you still need profit (older advice).
10. Use only real offers and real deadlines: no fake countdowns, no fake stock numbers.

## Steps

1. Install the Meta pixel through Shopify's direct integration. A tag manager cannot track the Shopify checkout page.
2. Build the warm audiences: site visitors (7, 14 and 30 days), video viewers (25%, 50%, 75% watched), add to cart without purchase (30 days), checkout started without purchase (30 days), Instagram and Facebook page engagers (90 days) and your uploaded customer list.
3. Low traffic? Create the audience anyway. It starts working once enough people are in it. Begin with the biggest segment that works, for example everyone from the last 14 days or more.
4. Keep cold video ads running so new viewers keep filling the warm pool.
5. Make several different ads with different angles: product reminder, reviews, a short brand introduction, helpful content and a real deal.
6. Match each ad to what the person did. Someone who viewed one product should see that product, not a random one.
7. Set frequency caps and exclusions. Exclude existing customers from first-sale offers.
8. For a limited sale, give the ad a start and end date that match the real sale.
9. Retarget by email as well, not only with ads.

## Key numbers

- **7 / 14 / 30 days**: windows for site visitor audiences (2026).
- **25% / 50% / 75%**: video watch levels for video viewer audiences (2026).
- **50%+ watched**: viewers who flow into the warm pool automatically (2026).
- **30 days**: window for add to cart and checkout started without purchase (2026).
- **90 days**: window for page engagers and uploaded customer lists, which have high conversion and cheap CPMs (2026).
- **7 touchpoints**: average contacts before a purchase across e-commerce; some buyers need fewer, some more (older advice).
- **3 or fewer per day**: frequency cap for retargeting (older advice).
- **12 per day**: what a person can see if you set 3 per day on 4 networks at once (older advice).
- **1 per day per ad type**: cap when several retargeting ads run at the same time (older advice).
- **10 days per ad**: three different ads in a 30-day window (older advice).
- **Sequence**: product reminder days 1 to 7, review ad days 7 to 14, introduction ad days 14 to 21, a deal around days 14 to 30, content after 30 days (older advice).
- **30 days**: maximum length for running all ad types at once (older advice).
- **About 1,000 people**: usual minimum audience on Facebook; some Google ad types need only 100 (older advice).
- **30 seconds**: minimum time on site before the pixel counts someone; a 10-second visitor is rarely a buyer (older advice).

## Common mistakes

- One audience, like "everyone who viewed a product in 30 days", with one ad for all of them.
- The same dynamic product ad for a whole month. People tune it out.
- Frequency set per network but not across networks, so people see you 12 times a day.
- Counting every 10-second bounce in your audience, which wastes budget.
- Sending first-sale discounts to customers who already bought.
- Offering a deal too early and losing margin.
- Testing a product that needs explaining with warm ads only, before any video has warmed people up.
- Using fake urgency, like low-stock claims or deadlines that are not true.

## FAQ

**What is BOF?** Bottom of funnel. Ads for warm people who already know you and are close to buying.

**Are BOF ads only retargeting?** Mostly. They reach your video viewers and visitors, and can also catch shoppers who were looking at other brands (2026).

**Which audiences do I start with?** Site visitors, video viewers, cart and checkout abandoners, page engagers and your email list.

**My store is new. Is retargeting worth it yet?** Create the audiences now. They fill up as traffic grows.

**How often should someone see my ad?** 3 times a day or less, and fewer when several networks run.

**How long should I retarget one person?** Usually around 30 days, sometimes 60, with different ads along the way.

**Should I start with a discount?** No. Start with reminders and reviews. Save a real deal for later in the window.

**Do I retarget my customers?** Not with first-sale deals. For holiday sales you can include everyone, customers too.

**Long or short ad text?** Test both on Meta. Longer text often wins in many niches.

**Which button?** "Shop Now" for people close to buying, "Learn More" for content.

**Does retargeting work for cheap products?** Yes, retargeting usually converts very well for low-priced products.

**Why do expensive products need more retargeting?** Buyers often shop for months before they buy, so you need to stay visible.

## Where courses disagree

- **Manual sequences vs. simple warm audiences**: 2021-22 lessons build step-by-step sequences with excluded time windows. 2026 lessons use a short list of standard warm audiences and let video feed them.
- **Purpose**: 2021-22 lessons, built for expensive products, use retargeting mainly to stay visible until people are ready. 2026 lessons use it to harvest warm buyers.
