# Key numbers, finance and tracking

> Know your break-even ROAS before you spend a dollar, track profit every day in one sheet, judge tests over 3 to 7 days, and remember that profit is not the same as cash in the bank.

## Rules

1. Calculate your break-even ROAS before launch: average order value divided by gross profit per order (examples from 2026 lessons: 1.3, 1.32, 1.83).
2. Aim to run campaigns well above break-even: with a break-even of 1.5, one 2026 coach runs at about 2.5 and does not let ROAS fall below 2 while scaling.
3. Split every dollar of revenue into four buckets and check them against a benchmark: about 15% profit, 40% product cost (COGS), 30% marketing and 15% operating costs.
4. Keep product cost low: one 2026 lesson wants 60% or more gross margin, another wants 70% or more, which means COGS at 30% or less of the price.
5. Keep at least $50 of margin per sale.
6. Keep total ad spend at 30% of revenue or less.
7. Fill in a daily profit sheet (revenue, product cost, ad spend, fees); without it you cannot scale safely.
8. Run tests at least 3 to 4 days; $50 spent with zero sales on day 1 is not enough data.
9. Count Shopify fees, payment fees (about 2.9% plus $0.30), refunds and taxes as costs, not as profit.
10. Profit is not cash: a payout can reach your bank days after the order.
11. Use the pixel together with the Conversions API: advertisers using both saw about 17.8% lower cost per result.

## Steps

1. Put each product into a margin calculator: price, product cost, shipping, payment fees.
2. Write down your break-even ROAS and target cost per purchase (CPA), for example at 2x or 2.5x ROAS.
3. Enter product costs (COGS) in Shopify, or every Shopify cost report will be wrong.
4. Copy a daily P&L sheet. Only type into input cells, never into formula cells.
5. Launch the test and check soft metrics daily: cost per add to cart, CTR, CPC, CPM.
6. After 3 days, look at the last 3 days together, not just today.
7. Call it a proof of concept when you hold $75 to $100 profit a day for 3 to 5 days (another 2026 lesson says a steady $100 a day).
8. If results stay good for 5 to 7 days in a row, raise the budget step by step (one 2026 example went from $300 to $1,000 a day after ROAS doubled).
9. Log every change (price, budget, offer) to explain jumps in your P&L.
10. Regularly review the last 90 days of orders to find which cost eats your margin.

## Key numbers

- **Four-bucket benchmark (2026):** profit 15%, COGS 40%, marketing 30%, opex 15%. All four add up to 100%.
- **Contribution margin on the first order:** 15% to 25% target. A 15% target needed a ROAS of 2.48, a 25% target needed 3.38 in one example.
- **Opex:** under 15% of revenue.
- **Warehouse (3PL) pick fee:** about $1.50 to $2.50 per order, or $0 if you have none.
- **CPA example:** a $40 product should cost no more than $15 to $18 per purchase in ads.
- **CPC:** about $1 is good, $1.80 is fine, $2 to $3 is borderline or bad.
- **CPM:** $50 still acceptable, re-check ads at $60, $70 or more is bad.
- **CTR:** above 1% is good for one coach, 3% to 7% is ideal for another.
- **Funnel checks (2026):** click to landing page view: 70% to 80% is average, over 80% is good; losing more than 30% points to slow loading. Landing page view to add to cart: 5% to 8% is average, under 5% means page and ad do not match. Checkout to purchase: 45% to 60% is average, under 50% means a checkout problem.
- **Cart abandonment:** about 70% on average.
- **Ads:** beginners should launch more than 20.
- **Milestones (2026):** month 1, one day with $500 to $1,000 in sales; month 2, daily orders and a first $10,000 month; month 3, about $1,000 a day, which is about $30,000 a month.
- **Seasonality:** January and February sales usually drop.

## Common mistakes

- Looking at revenue instead of profit. A big sales number means nothing if nothing is left over.
- Forgetting that Meta and Google show revenue including tax. Take the tax out before you calculate ROAS.
- Paying 80% of the order in product cost: in one example this turned into a 10% loss.
- Killing a campaign after one red day. A few losing days between winning days are expected.
- Scaling without daily profit tracking, so you never notice losing days.
- Blaming the ads when ad numbers are fine. 20,000 visits and no sales means a store problem.
- Trusting tracking 100%. Some iOS users block tracking, and draft orders skip the normal checkout, so some sales never show up.

## FAQ

**What is ROAS?** Revenue divided by ad spend. $100 spent and $300 back is a ROAS of 3.

**How do I find my break-even ROAS?** Divide your average order value by the gross profit per order before ads.

**ROAS 2.5 but no profit?** The problem is outside the ad account: check product cost, fees, refunds, opex.

**How long should I test a product?** At least 3 to 4 days. Cut early only if soft metrics are bad and not improving.

**When can I scale?** When you see good data for 5 to 7 days in a row, or a steady $75 to $100 profit a day.

**Lots of traffic but no sales after 7 days?** Work on the website, not the ads.

**Which metrics matter most?** Profit per sale and cost per sale. In Meta, the ads with the most spend are the ones the system trusts to scale.

**What is a good conversion rate?** It depends on the product; see Where courses disagree.

**Is profit the money in my bank?** No. Profit is what is left after all costs. Shopify pays out a few days after the order, so your bank balance lags behind.

**Why are my Shopify cost reports wrong?** Usually because product costs were never entered there.

**What about taxes?** Set money aside for them and check the rules in your country.

**I am under 18. Can I do this?** Shopify, payments and ad accounts usually need an adult owner, so a parent must be the account holder.

## Where courses disagree

- **Gross margin:** 60% or more vs 70% or more (both 2026). Older high-ticket advice (2021-22): 20% minimum, 30% to 40% target.
- **Contribution margin:** 15% to 25% (2026) vs 35% in another 2026 report card.
- **CTR:** above 1% is good vs 3% to 7% is ideal (both 2026).
- **Conversion rate:** 2.6% in a 2026 example store vs 0.4% to 0.56% in an older high-ticket store (2021-22).
- **Target ROAS:** older advice from high-ticket Google Ads lessons (2021-22) needed about 10x; 2026 Meta examples break even near 1.3 to 1.8.
