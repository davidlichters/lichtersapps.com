# Influencers and UGC

> Real people filming your product on a phone earn more trust than polished ads, so gift widely, pay the best creators, and get written rights before you run their videos as ads.

## Rules

1. UGC (user-generated content) means simple phone videos from real people, and viewers trust them more than high-production ads.
2. Decide the goal first (brand awareness, sales, or community), because each goal needs a different type of creator.
3. The creator's audience must match your customer, and the creator must share your brand's values.
4. Pick one platform per campaign (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube or Snapchat), because each one has a different audience.
5. Start any collaboration with a gifting phase: send free product to many creators before you pay anyone.
6. Choose paid creators from the pool of people you already gifted who already posted about you.
7. A lasting partnership with a single creator beats 10 one-off deals with 10 different creators.
8. Send the brief only after you agreed on the collaboration and the rate.
9. Never run a creator's video as an ad without written usage rights that state ownership, duration and platforms.
10. Paid TikTok ads must use commercial audio from TikTok's library, not normal artist music.
11. Only post videos you made or have written permission to use, and never present a video as a real customer review when it is not.

## Steps

1. Write down your campaign goal and the one platform you will focus on.
2. Search by hand on Instagram Explore, TikTok Discover and YouTube. Paid tools often only show creators who signed up there.
3. From one good creator, check who they collaborate with and which accounts the app suggests.
4. Collect 30 to 50 creators in a Google Sheet with handle, followers and contact.
5. Check that each one fits your customer and your values.
6. Send short personal DMs. Use their name and mention something specific from their content.
7. Ship free product to everyone who says yes and ask for honest feedback.
8. Track who posts. Repost the best ones with credit in the caption, and ask for a collab post.
9. Pick the creators whose videos performed best and offer them a paid deal.
10. Send a brief with 5 parts: campaign overview, brand background, key message and call to action, content rules (format, text on screen, length), and creative freedom.
11. Set a timeline: product arrival, first edit, feedback, go-live.
12. Put usage rights in writing, including an option to renew.
13. Run the best videos as ads and test a different hook per platform. Add all creators to one group chat.

## Key numbers

- **1 to 100,000 followers**: a micro-influencer (2026). On TikTok, follower counts run much higher.
- **100,000 to 1 million followers**: a macro-influencer, for broader reach (2026).
- **Celebrities**: usually lower engagement, but they add a lot of trust (2026).
- **30 to 50 creators** in a sheet: a good starting list (2026).
- **50 DMs, 30 replies, 20 want the product, 10 post**: a realistic gifting funnel (2026).
- **30 days**: an example of ad usage included in a creator's rate; extra months cost more (2026).
- **250 USD per extra month**: an example renewal fee when you use a video as a paid ad for 60 days (2026).
- **6 months from the first post, worldwide**: an example usage term in a creator contract (2026).
- **2 years**: for long-running ads, pick evergreen videos that still make sense 2 years later, not trend or joke videos (2026).
- **3 main platforms** for reusing videos: Reels, TikTok and Shorts, with Snapchat Spotlight as a bonus (2026).
- **1 to 2 percent**: Amazon's affiliate rate in many cases after its cuts (older advice, 2021-22).
- **10 touchpoints**: minimum contact with a "Dream 100" partner before you pitch (older advice, 2021-22).

## Common mistakes

- Picking creators by follower count, not audience fit.
- No written usage rights, then running the video as an ad anyway.
- Using trending sounds or artist music in paid ads.
- The same upload everywhere without each app's own text tool and fonts.
- Jokes and trends from TikTok copied 1:1 to Facebook Reels, where the audience is older.
- "Buy my product" replies in community groups; most groups kick you out.

## FAQ

**How do I find more creators once I have one?** Look at their collabs and at the accounts Instagram suggests next to them.

**Should I use a paid creator search tool?** Manual search is still the best start, because many tools only list creators who signed up there.

**Do I ask for a post when I gift?** No. Send the product, ask for honest feedback, and see who posts.

**How many creators will actually post?** In one 2026 example, 50 DMs led to about 10 posts.

**What kind of creator sells best?** For sales, product review accounts. For community, creators who start lots of conversations.

**Where else can I find UGC creators?** TikTok Shop creators, or your own brand account acting as the creator (2026).

**My product needs explaining. What helps?** More UGC where people describe their own experience (2026 coaching call).

**When do I send the brief?** After you agreed to work together and agreed on the rate.

**How long can I use a creator's video?** As long as the contract says, with paid renewals. Some brands get rights forever, which is not great for the creator.

**What is whitelisting?** Running ads through a person who carries authority: either a personal one (a UGC creator or your own account) or a third-party expert, for example a doctor for a supplement (2026). Only with real people and their permission.

**Should I pay commission instead of a flat fee?** One example campaign gifted lots of product and paid affiliate commission only to people who posted videos (2026).

**Why build long-term partnerships?** Creators then make more authentic videos and are more likely to post about you unpaid.

## Checklist

- [ ] Goal and platform chosen
- [ ] 30 to 50 creators in a sheet
- [ ] Audience and values checked for each
- [ ] Personal DMs sent
- [ ] Free products shipped, nothing demanded
- [ ] Posts tracked, reposted with credit
- [ ] Best creators offered paid deals
- [ ] Brief with all 5 parts sent after rate agreed
- [ ] Usage rights in writing: owner, duration, platforms, renewal
- [ ] Commercial audio for TikTok ads

If you are under 18, Shopify, payments and ad accounts usually need an adult owner, often a parent. Contracts and disclosure rules for sponsored posts differ, so check the rules in your country.

## Templates

**Gifting DM**

> Hey [Name], the clip where you [specific moment] made me laugh. I run [Store], and we make [product] for [customer]. Want one, free? You owe us nothing, we just want to hear what you think. Send me an address if you are in.

**Paid offer**

> Hey [Name], your clip with our [product] did really well for us. We would like to pay you for [number] new clips and use them as ads on [platforms] for [duration], renewable later. How much do you charge?
