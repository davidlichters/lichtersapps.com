# Scaling, team and processes

> Scale only what already works, write a process (SOP) before you hand a task to anyone, hand off customer service first, and keep your team at about 80% of its capacity.

## Rules

1. Raise ad budget only when a product shows a clear breakthrough; if its numbers swing a lot, hold the budget (2026).
2. If profit margin drops after a 25% budget increase, scale back down (2026).
3. When sales stall, add new ads and keep the budget the same for a couple of days; adding works better than cutting (2026).
4. Only about 20 to 30% of new ads become winners, so test new creatives every week (2026).
5. Ten times more sales means ten times more problems (cash flow, support, chargebacks), so grow only as fast as your operations can handle.
6. Write an SOP for every task you hand off, before you post the job.
7. Your first hire is most likely a customer service person (2021-22).
8. Outsource only when you can afford it; never take on help that pushes you into a loss.
9. Any task over 1 hour a day, or one that keeps coming back over 30 days, is a candidate to hand off.
10. Plan your team so it runs at about 80% of its capacity, not 100% (2021-22).
11. Start every new hire with a trial at full pay instead of a fixed job.
12. Your data, creatives and ad account belong to you, never to an agency (2026).

## Steps

1. Reach steady profit first: a 2026 roadmap aims for steady $5,000 to $20,000 months.
2. Keep testing: launch a new product at least every 3 days (2026).
3. To scale a winner: remove weak spots in your funnel, make new creatives every week for the angle that wins, then add retargeting and bundles (2026).
4. Track your time for 30 days and mark long or repeating tasks.
5. Do the task yourself once so you understand it. Then write the SOP with screenshots, add a video walkthrough, and finish with a checklist that fits on one page.
6. Post a short job ad: role, tasks, proposed pay, hours, weekend work, tools needed, and one simple apply instruction.
7. Delete applications that ignore the instruction. After interviews, pick from the 2 or 3 people with no red flags.
8. Run a paid trial with the SOP, for example 1 week for customer service or 1 paid blog post for a writer. Expect good, not perfect.
9. Onboard: intro video about the store, a form for contact and payment details, a meeting at the end of the trial.
10. Set rules from day one: mostly messages, answers 1 to 2 times a day, one team group chat.
11. Review each person every 2 weeks at first, then monthly, about 30 minutes.
12. Automate repeating admin with workflows that start from a trigger, such as a new Shopify order (2026).

## Key numbers

- **25%**: budget step after which you check margin (2026).
- **20 to 30%**: share of new ads that should become winners (2026).
- **$10,000 to $20,000 revenue a month**: point where the business gets serious and you start building a team (2021-22).
- **80%**: target team capacity (2021-22).
- **50**: replies a job post often gets (2021-22).
- **1 to 2 months**: time until a new person does customer service as well as you (2021-22).
- **Older example pay (2021-22, assistants in the Philippines)**: customer service $1,000 a month, order processing $600, product upload $500. Check current rates on job sites.
- **5% of ad spend, 10% if they do more than media buying**: 2026 benchmark for agency pay, best as monthly base plus bonus, with caps.
- **30 to 90 days**: time to build a good workflow with an agency; do not fire it in the first 15 days (2026).
- **Under $10 million a year**: below this, an agency is usually cheaper than an in-house team (2026).

## Common mistakes

- Pushing budget while support, fulfillment or cash flow is broken.
- Hiring before the SOP exists, then blaming the person.
- Expecting perfect work in the trial. Nobody does a new task perfectly the first time.
- Giving freelancers full store access before a trial. One wrong click can break the store.
- Paying late. Pay on a fixed day, and pay project work as soon as it is delivered.
- Signing a long locked agency contract, or one that lets them delete your data when you leave.

## FAQ

**When should I hire?** When a task eats your time and you can pay for help without a loss. Never outsource at a loss.

**Who is the first hire?** Usually customer service. Later split it into customer service and order processing.

**What are interview red flags?** Taking 10 minutes to start answering, not clearly agreeing to pay and hours, or a full-time candidate who works for 10 other clients.

**Do I need a video call?** One teacher interviews only by text chat, because you keep a record and see attention to detail.

**Should I track my assistant with apps or daily reports?** One student checks only weekly results; asking for daily reports made about 90% of candidates disappear.

**Pay per hour or per project?** For content freelancers, one teacher pays for each finished piece and not by the hour (2021-22).

**How do I let someone go?** Remove all access first (Shopify, Drive, phone, group chats), then tell them by message.

**Agency or own team?** Under $10 million a year a good agency is usually cheaper (2026), and ending an agency is easier than firing a person.

**What do I check in an agency?** Who really works on your account, how many clients each person handles (often 3 to 5), a talk with a current client, and cancellation terms.

**Can AI replace team members?** A 2026 lesson says an AI agent can learn most trainable tasks, but some brands went back to human copywriters after bad AI copy.

## Checklist

- [ ] Store is steadily profitable before scaling.
- [ ] Budget raised in steps; margin checked after each 25% step.
- [ ] New creatives tested every week.
- [ ] 30 days of time tracking done.
- [ ] SOP (video, text, "do not do" list, checklist) ready before the job post.
- [ ] 3 to 5 people interviewed, paid trial done.
- [ ] Onboarding, communication rules and reviews set.
- [ ] Under 18: the store, payments, ad accounts and paying staff usually need an adult owner, so a parent. Check the rules in your country for hiring, contracts and tax.
