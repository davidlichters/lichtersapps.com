# Ad creatives and ad copy

> The message sells, not the format: use the customer's own words, test many simple creatives fast, and judge them by hook rate and website purchases.

## Rules

1. Have 12 to 20 creatives ready before you test a product, and never fewer than 10 (2026).
2. Start with 1 or 2 message concepts and make 3 to 5 creatives for each (2026).
3. Write 3 primary texts per ad, each aimed at a different awareness level (2026).
4. Win the first 1 to 3 seconds (the hook), because nobody watches the rest if it fails.
5. Use the exact words buyers use; in one 2026 survey, 17 buyers described the problem in almost the same words.
6. Focus each ad on one problem or one wish.
7. Make the primary text read like a normal post from a person, not a classic advert.
8. Study winning ads for ideas, but never post someone else's video, logo or brand as your own.
9. Never claim what you cannot prove, and avoid health promises, which can get ads blocked.
10. Use only real urgency, real reviews and real numbers.
11. Put at least 80% of your effort into low-effort, good-quality ads, not big productions (2026).
12. On cold traffic, start your copy at the problem-aware stage, one of the 5 awareness stages: people feel the pain but do not know your product yet.

## Steps

1. Research the buyer in forums, Reddit and comments under viral posts. Save pains, failed solutions, fears and exact phrases.
2. After your first orders, send buyers a short survey. Ask what they tried before, what moment pushed them to act, and how they would explain the product to a friend.
3. Pick the awareness stage. Problem ads can go to an advertorial or listicle; solution ads go to the product page.
4. Collect references in the Meta Ad Library: hooks, captions and structures of ads with high spend or many comments. A brand uploading 3 to 5 new ads a week is likely scaling.
5. Write 1 or 2 concepts. Concept = idea (the message) + style (how you show it). Example structure: problem, solution, product, offer.
6. Script videos: hook 0 to 3 s, problem 3 to 15 s, turn 15 to 25 s, solution 25 to 45 s, call to action 45 to 60 s.
7. Build 3 to 5 creatives per concept: videos of a real person using the product, and 1:1 images with a headline, the product and a few benefits.
8. Write the primary texts, then the headline. Let AI draft 5 options from your research and product page, keep the best 2, and edit them.
9. Name each ad by product, type and what is different.
10. Launch and read hook rate, drop-off and website purchases.
11. Take the creative with the most website purchases and remake its message in 2 new formats.

## Key numbers

- **12 to 20 creatives**: needed for a product test (2026); 10 to 15 is the absolute minimum.
- **15 per day**: new creatives one 2026 coach tests himself.
- **10 concepts x 5 creatives = 50**: rotation once you scale (2026).
- **Hook rate** (25% video plays / impressions): under 15% poor, 15 to 25% average, 25 to 40% good, over 40% excellent (2026). Another 2026 table is stricter: 20 to 30% only average.
- **Drop-off**: big drop at 25% of the video = hook problem, at 50% = middle, at 75% = offer; low completion after good retention = weak call to action (2026).
- **$75 to $100 per day**: revenue before you invest in long video sales letters (VSL) (2026).
- **$100+ profit over 3 days**: only then pay for custom ads or UGC for that product (2026).
- **80%+**: share of effort for low-effort, good-quality ads (2026).
- **1:1**: default image format; 9:16 for stories. A 2026 coach tested 4:3 and saw no difference.
- **Pick 3 to 4 of 12 formats** to start (2026 coaching call).

## Common mistakes

- Polishing one perfect ad. Many decent, different ads beat one flawless ad.
- Judging ads by ten small details instead of the big three: idea, style, hook.
- Running the top competitor ads everyone already runs.
- Copy full of adjectives, filler and jargon.
- Features without the benefit. People buy what the product does for them.
- Paying for custom productions before the product shows profit.
- An ad promise the landing page does not keep.
- Fake scarcity like "only 45 left" when it is not true.

## FAQ

**How many creatives to start?** 12 to 20, at least 10. One 2026 call called more than 6 the floor.

**Video or images first?** Images are easier to produce. One 2026 lesson tests cheaply and scales with videos. See below.

**Do I have to show my face?** No. Desk demos, voiceovers and real users holding the product work.

**Is a phone camera enough?** Yes. Modern phones film well; use daylight where you can.

**My click-through rate is low. What do I fix?** The hook. Better hooks are the main fix for a weak CTR.

**How long should a video be?** Up to about 60 seconds; one 2026 coach likes 90. Two minutes is too long.

**Price in the ad?** One 2026 coach leaves it out completely. Lead with the benefit and the deal.

**Can I reuse old ads for a holiday?** Yes. A 2026 call reused Black Friday winners for Christmas with new text.

**Should I buy ready-made ads?** Careful: one member bought 20 and only 1 scaled.

**Which call to action?** One per ad, tied to the product, for example "Stop the itching tonight" instead of "Shop now".

**Can AI write my copy?** As a draft. AI tends to give average text, so feed it research and edit.

**I am under 18. Can I run ads?** Ad accounts, Shopify and payments usually need an adult owner, so a parent. Check the rules in your country.

## Checklist

- [ ] Customer phrases saved from research or survey
- [ ] Awareness stage chosen, landing page matches
- [ ] 1 to 2 concepts, one problem or wish each
- [ ] 12 to 20 creatives ready (minimum 10)
- [ ] Strong first 3 seconds in every video
- [ ] 3 primary texts per ad, headline shows the main benefit
- [ ] No unproven claims, fake urgency or fake reviews
- [ ] All ads are your own work
- [ ] Hook rate and drop-off checked
- [ ] Winner remade in 2 new formats

## Templates

**Brief for a freelance video creator**

> Hi, I sell [product] to [who]. I need [number] vertical videos (9:16, under 60 seconds). Open with a hook about [main problem] in the first 3 seconds, show a real person using the product, end with one action: [action]. Customer phrases: [3 to 5 phrases]. No other brands' logos, unlicensed music or unproven claims. Budget [amount], deadline [date]. Can you share 2 similar examples?

## Where courses disagree

- **VSL or images first?** One 2026 lesson tests VSLs first and turns winners into images. Another 2026 lesson uses images until $75 to $100 daily revenue.
- **Competitor ads?** One 2026 lesson runs downloaded viral videos unchanged for speed; others say change the hook or body, or use them only as inspiration. Reposting someone else's video can break copyright and Meta's rules, so make your own.
- **Text on images**: older advice (2021-22) kept text under 20% of a Facebook image; 2026 lessons put a headline and benefits on images.
