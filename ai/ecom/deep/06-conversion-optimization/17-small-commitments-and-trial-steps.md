### Small commitments and trial steps

- Most buyers resist a costly decision and want time to think, so a small commitment now makes the larger decision more likely later. Tell the buyer that the big decision can wait, and ask only for the small step. [L1369, undated]
- Ask for a yes on a minor point first, such as color, size, delivery date or a sample. Agreement on a minor point carries the main offer along in about three cases out of four. Make the minor point a clear choice the buyer can make in seconds. [L1369, undated]
- Supply the impulse in this order: [L1369, undated]
  1. Identify the motive that makes the buyer want the offer.
  2. Choose a small first action linked to that motive, such as requesting a free trial, a sample or information, and make it cost nothing and oblige nothing.
  3. Make the next step easier than stopping, such as delivering the product with a trial period.
  4. Ask for the purchase only after the buyer has used or handled the product.

### Direct-response sales letters

- Four factors make a reader act immediately: they know and trust the sender, they like the product, they believe the urgency story and they find the premium exciting. Write these four factors on a card and check each one in every letter. [L1370, undated]
- Fortune-telling formula: make a specific prediction about the future of the buyer's market or situation, then present the offer as the way to prepare for or profit from it. People are fascinated by predictions and remember bold, provocative ones. [L1370, undated]
  - Store owner: "Shipping costs in your niche will climb again next spring. Stores that lock in rates now will keep their margins while others cut prices. Here is how to lock in your rates before the increase."
- Winners-and-losers formula: contrast two groups who started in the same place, one that acts on the offer and one that does not, and present the offer as the difference between them. It creates fear of being in the losing group and motivation to join the winners. [L1370, undated]
  - Online course: "Two shop owners opened the same month with the same products. One reads the weekly pricing report every Monday and now holds a steady margin. The other guesses, and is still discounting to stay afloat."
- Use a short question-based lead for a recipient who is buying something they already own or fear buying. Phrase the questions around the frustration and then state that you are on the reader's side. [L1370, undated]
- Sales sequence for a high-value product sold by mail or in person: [L1369, undated]
  1. Send a series of informative circulars that show the product's lifestyle benefit without asking for a purchase.
  2. Invite the prospect to a special demonstration or trial with a set date and time, and include the spouse or partner.
  3. Let the prospect operate the product and point out its features during the trial.
  4. Offer a customized option that matches the preference the prospect expressed.
  5. Ask for the signature once the prospect has experienced the product.

#### Further details

- Step 1: Research the subject thoroughly. Step 2: Write far more bullets than you'll need (at least 2-3x more than will appear in final copy). Step 3: Select the strongest, most intriguing bullets only. Step 4: Arrange selected bullets strategically, placing the most powerful ones at the beginning (front cover, headline area) and end (closing, call to action). Step 5: Use themed groups of bullets that hit the same topic from 2-3 different angles using different formulas. [L0068, undated]
