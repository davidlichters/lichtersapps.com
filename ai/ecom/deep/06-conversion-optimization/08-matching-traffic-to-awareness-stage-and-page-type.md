## Matching traffic to awareness stage and page type

### Awareness stages and the customer journey

#### The five-stage model and the pyramid

- ** Customers move through five awareness stages before buying: Unaware, Problem Aware, Solution Aware, Product Aware and Ninja Aware (also called Most Aware). Each stage is a mental step from first being in the market to deciding on a specific product. [L0904, 2021-22]
- ** A four-stage version of the same model runs problem aware, solution aware, product aware and most aware, in that order. A product page converts visitors who arrive at an earlier stage only if it walks them through all the missing stages, because a price or buy button means little to someone who has not yet accepted the problem or the solution. [L0473, 2021-22]
- ** The whole potential market fits into the awareness pyramid. The Unaware group is the largest segment at the base, and the segments get smaller toward the top, where Ninja Aware buyers are the fewest. [L0904, 2021-22]
- ** The customer awareness pyramid is also called the customer journey, and marketers use the two terms interchangeably even though they are not exactly the same. The sales funnel describes the same stages turned upside down, with top of funnel at the pyramid's base and bottom of funnel at its tip. [L0481, 2021-22]
- ** The awareness pyramid runs from completely unaware prospects at the bottom, through problem aware and product aware, to ninja aware buyers at the top, who are ready to purchase now. Traffic and landing pages should be matched to the stage each prospect is in. [L1392, 2021-22]

#### What each stage looks like

- **Unaware:** ** Unaware people match the profile of a future buyer but do not yet know they have a problem or desire that the product solves. Reaching them requires messaging that first makes them see the problem. [L0904, 2021-22]
- **Problem Aware:** ** At Problem Aware, people recognize a pain and start looking for a solution. Pain is caused by problems, and people either run away from pain or pursue pleasure, so the pain is the entry point for messaging. [L0904, 2021-22]
- **Solution Aware:** ** At Solution Aware, people know a broad type of solution to their problem and need to learn which option fits them, possibly among several solution types. This is the largest group in the market at any time, but most of them are not ready to buy because they still need education to feel safe purchasing. [L0904, 2021-22]
- **Product Aware:** ** At Product Aware, people know several specific products that solve their problem, including your product and competitor products, but have not yet chosen one. They are still comparing products before making a final buying decision. [L0904, 2021-22]
- **Ninja Aware:** ** At Ninja Aware, people have learned their options, the desire for a solution has been built, and they know the exact product they need. They are ready to buy at this stage. [L0904, 2021-22]

#### Why the stage decides the destination

- ** Roughly 10 percent of the total market is ready to buy at any given time, which is only the Ninja Aware segment. The earlier awareness stages hold much larger shares of the market. [L0904, 2021-22]
- ** Product pages that only target Ninja Aware visitors reach a small pool of people. Visitors at earlier stages land on those pages, find nothing that speaks to their situation, and leave without buying. [L0904, 2021-22]
- ** Product Aware and Ninja Aware visitors already know the solution, so for them the decision mostly comes down to price, the best offer and the best deal. Simple copy with a clear deal and a bit of urgency works only for this group. [L0904, 2021-22]
- ** Serving people with good copy and the right product information at each awareness stage increases the likelihood of purchase. This raises the store's conversion rate. [L0904, 2021-22]
- ** Buyers choose the type of solution before they compare specific products. For example, a beginner who needs a surfboard first decides between renting and buying, then compares products. Product pages and copy should answer the solution-level question before the product-level comparison. [L0904, 2021-22]
- ** The landing page a visitor reaches must match the awareness stage that visitor is in at that moment. Sending problem aware or completely unaware traffic to a hard-sell product page ignores the buyer's need for education and usually yields few sales. [L0481, 2021-22]
- ** Under the 40-40-20 principle, the first 40 percent is targeting, so the audience you target determines which awareness stage must be served on the landing page. [L0481, 2021-22]
- ** Modern version of the rule: the first 40% depends on reaching the right audience in the right place, at the right time, with the right destination. The destination must match where the visitor sits in the customer journey, so cold social media traffic should not land on a high-ticket product page with a direct buy request. [L0408, 2021-22]
- ** Sending cold, top-of-funnel social media visitors who are unaware of the solution straight to a product page selling a 3000 USD product is expected to yield an extremely low conversion rate. The traffic may match the market, but the destination does not match the buyer's awareness stage, so the visitor needs to be moved toward readiness first. [L0408, 2021-22]
- ** A product page converts cold traffic only if it educates visitors from problem awareness through solution awareness and product awareness to readiness to buy. When visitors who are still problem-aware land on a product page without that education, the mismatch is usually a bigger cause of low conversion than the page design. [L0473, 2021-22]

#### Worked example: the awareness ladder for one product

- ** Example for a home office worker with back pain from long sitting: the person is problem aware of the pain, then solution aware that a standing-capable desk could help, then product aware of the electric height-adjustable desk type. Ninja aware means they have chosen one exact model, and only then do they decide which retailer to buy it from. [L0481, 2021-22]

### Matching the headline and page to the stage

- ** Match the ad headline exactly to the primary promise on the destination landing page. If sending to a product page, use the primary promise from product copy; for blog or sales pages, match the page headline. Mismatches cause high bounce rates and wasted ad spend because users expect immediate visual confirmation of what they clicked. [L0241, 2021-22]
- Message match between the ad and the landing page matters: if the ad shows one thing and the link goes to a clearly different product or topic, visitors leave immediately. [L0691, 2024]
- Keep the product page visually close to the ad, so a GIF of the product in use in the ad is followed by the same GIF on the page. This one-to-one match reduces confusion for the visitor. [L1194, undated]
- Match your headline to the prospect's awareness of the product. A product-aware prospect should see the product named first, a desire-aware prospect should see the desire first, and a problem-aware prospect should see the problem stated specifically. Where a prospect stands on that scale decides how the copy begins. [L0523, 2015+]
- Relevance example: a golf-targeted ad for sports leggings should lead to a page that talks about golf performance, such as how the leggings support stance and swing, instead of generic sportswear benefits. A visitor who sees their own activity named on the page feels understood and is more likely to buy. Build this kind of audience-specific variant after the first landing page test shows a winner. [L0918, 2023]

#### Diagnosing a low-converting page

** Steps to fix a product page with a low conversion rate:

1. Identify the awareness stage of the traffic arriving on that page, which can be mixed; problem aware visitors can be recognized by search traffic about their problem.
2. Check whether the page moves visitors from their current stage to the next one.
3. If it does not, either segment marketing so problem aware visitors first reach a more suitable page, or add elements to the existing page that help visitors move through the stages.

[L0481, 2021-22]

#### Sequencing problem-aware and unaware visitors

** For a problem aware or completely unaware audience, the sequence is:

1. Send the visitor to a content page first, which explains the problem and why it exists and shows how to solve it and what solutions exist.
2. Move them through email, retargeting or another page that introduces your products as solutions.
3. Finally lead them to the product page.

[L0481, 2021-22]

- ** Problem aware visitors typically arrive from Google searches about their problem and from social media posts that promote a product solving that problem. Because of this, traffic from these sources should usually land on an educational content page, not a product page. [L0481, 2021-22]

### Traffic temperature and bridges

#### Hot, warm and cold traffic

- Hot traffic consists of people who already know you, such as email subscribers, podcast listeners and blog readers. Speak to them personally and share stories, opinions and details of your life, because they already trust you. Such personal messages can generate large revenue from hot traffic. [L0523, 2015+]
- Warm traffic reaches you through someone else's relationship, such as affiliates and joint venture partners. A personal endorsement from a trusted source, such as a lift letter, raises response greatly. In classic direct mail, list owners and sellers often split profits 50/50. [L0523, 2015+]
- Cold traffic consists of people who do not know you, such as Facebook or pay-per-click visitors. These prospects need the longest bridge, and most sellers fail here because they speak in insider language. Plan a separate path for cold traffic instead of sending it straight to the offer. [L0523, 2015+]
- Bridge length scales with coldness. Hot traffic needs only a quick link or email, warm traffic needs an endorsement from a trusted person, and cold traffic needs the longest bridge that educates the visitor before the offer. Choose the bridge from the traffic temperature. [L0523, 2015+]
- Not all clicks are equal, because the context a visitor came from determines how they respond to your page. A visitor who has just read a glowing recommendation converts far better than one who has just read a hostile warning about you. Control the frame your traffic arrives through wherever possible. [L0523, 2015+]
- Visitors from cold traffic often need several steps before they buy, so a path through trust-building landing pages can come before the purchase page. Plan the journey so each page answers one question the visitor has. [L0524, 2023]
- Traffic from different sources arrives at different awareness levels, so one landing page often cannot serve all of it. Plan up to three landing pages, one each for hot, warm and cold traffic, and send each traffic source to the page that matches its temperature. The lesson treats this setup as worth the extra effort. [L0523, 2015+]

#### Pre-frames

- A pre-frame is the mindset you establish before a prospect moves on to the following stage. Every stage of a funnel serves as the pre-frame for the next one, which is why each should be tuned for both selling and credibility. Visitors who arrive with a strong frame convert at a much higher rate than those who arrive with a weak one. [L0523, 2015+]
- The introduction a speaker receives strongly affects how much is sold at the back of the room. A bland introduction lowers energy and sales, while a strong pre-sell lifts them. Take control of the introduction with a short, prepared intro video that features endorsements and success stories, played just before going on stage. [L0523, 2015+]
- Sell testimonials as pre-frames. Pay customers or students to write blog posts about their results, or ask them to publish video reviews that include links and keywords, then drive traffic to those pages. Visitors arriving through someone else's endorsement convert much better. [L0523, 2015+]

#### Cold bridge pattern

Pattern: cold bridge page that translates a visitor's problem into the vocabulary of your offer. Start with a broad question about something the visitor wants, then explain the jargon in plain words, and finally show how the offer solves the problem with the new term.

Why it works: visitors only convert when they understand the terms the offer uses, so the bridge closes that knowledge gap.

- Example 1: "Want a free website that earns money?" followed by "To earn from it, people must visit, and visitors are called traffic. Here is how to bring them in."
- Example 2: "If you wake up with stinging feet at night, the usual cause is nerve damage called neuropathy. Here is what to do about it."

[L0523, 2015+]

### Traffic sources and their fit with awareness stages

#### Traffic foundation

- A single traffic source is not enough for a reliable e-commerce traffic base. A business should combine several sources to reach prospects across the whole awareness pyramid. [L1392, 2021-22]
- ** A full e-commerce traffic foundation rests on five components: paid search ads billed per click, unpaid search engine visibility, retargeting campaigns, building an email subscriber list together with email marketing, and marketing through social media platforms. [L1392, 2021-22]
- ** Traffic should reach prospects at every stage of the customer awareness pyramid, not only at the most aware stage. Each stage needs a page and message that matches where the prospect currently stands. [L1392, 2021-22]
- ** Targeting only the most aware buyers is a mistake. That group is small, the most competitive, and often hard to convert, and if you are absent when they reach that stage, a competitor can take the sale. [L1392, 2021-22]
- ** The goal is omnipresence: being available to prospects at any point in the awareness pyramid so the market's attention stays on your brand throughout their decision. [L1392, 2021-22]
- ** The earlier a prospect is reached and given useful help, the longer they have to experience the business. That early exposure makes the brand more likely to be remembered when the prospect reaches the most aware stage. [L1392, 2021-22]
- ** The industry average is about seven touchpoints, meaning interactions with a business, before a purchase in e-commerce. This is only an average, and the real number varies a lot between businesses, with some needing more and some fewer. [L1392, 2021-22]
- ** Prospects who have already interacted with a brand several times are more likely to buy when they reach the most aware stage, because trust has been built. A brand that appears only at the last moment must rely on having a clearly better offer than the alternatives the prospect has already seen. [L1392, 2021-22]
- ** Each traffic source must be matched with how the website engages visitors from that source. The content and landing experience should fit the awareness stage the traffic comes from. [L1392, 2021-22]
- ** Some businesses can take a prospect from first visit to purchase in a single session, but many e-commerce businesses cannot. Plan for multiple visits, because the commonly cited industry average is about seven touchpoints before a purchase. [L1392, 2021-22]

#### Traffic source fit table

** Traffic source fit by awareness stage (e-commerce, standard product pages):

| Traffic source | Completely unaware | Problem aware | Product aware | Ninja aware |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google Shopping | Not suited | Poor (sends visitors to a product page, which rarely moves them on) | Good | Good |
| Social media | Good (turns them problem aware) | Good | Weak | Weak, except retargeting |
| Paid search text ads | Too expensive for meaningful volume | Too expensive for meaningful volume | Suitable | Suitable |
| Organic search (problem-aware keywords) | Not the main fit | Good, at much lower cost than paid | Suitable | Suitable |

[L1392, 2021-22]

#### Platform-specific matching

- Google Shopping and paid search text ads point to product-level listings. Search text ads convert best when the landing page matches the search term closely, usually a single product page for a specific term or a narrow collection page for a broader one. A collection with hundreds of products makes the offer hard to find and lowers the chance of a sale. [L0880, 2021-22]
- Broad or long-tail keyword traffic often converts poorly at first, so the visitor must be guided through several steps of value before buying. Expect a lower immediate conversion rate after a traffic boost ends. [L0949, 2021-22]
- Smart links let traffic from specific ads or influencers land on a customized landing page built for that source. Matching the page to the visitor's entry point is meant to improve conversion from the first moment on the site. [L0593, 2025-26]
- For influencer campaigns, keep the landing page simple. Use pictures or GIFs of the influencer using the product, add customer reviews, and offer a coupon code that the influencer shares with their followers. [L0789, 2022]
- A website with only product pages engages only shoppers who are already ready to buy. Reaching people earlier in the customer journey, across the customer awareness pyramid, requires content. [L0500, 2021-22]

[L1019, 2026]

- Not every person is ready to buy immediately; some need more information first, so sending all traffic to a buy-now page is not always the best practice. [L1019, 2026]
- Problem ads aimed at unaware or problem-aware people should send traffic to an advertorial or listicle to educate them first. [L1019, 2026]
- Solution ads aimed at solution-aware or product-aware people should send traffic to a landing page or PDP to convert. [L1019, 2026]
- Retargeting aimed at most-aware people should go straight to the PDP or offer, without a long story. [L1019, 2026]
- For every ad set, write down its awareness level and check that its destination matches that level. [L1019, 2026]

#### Display and retargeting audiences

- Use website visitor audiences because they show prior intent; cold traffic on Display is nearly impossible to make profitable, so only run Display and Discovery to people who have already visited your store. [L0210, 2022]
- Retargeting is where the most-aware visitor is served the offer directly; see the ad-type table above. [L1019, 2026]
