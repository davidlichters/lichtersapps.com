### Hero, banners and sliders

- Place text on a hero image to tell the customer what the business does for them. Only add a promotional message when there is a real offer, and keep the wording clear enough to be changed later. [L1310, 2021-22]
- Use a hero image instead of a slideshow on the homepage, because slideshows rely on JavaScript and extra code that slows the site down. Turn off autoplay and keep one static image. [L1371, 2021-22]
- Reported research suggests only about 4% of visitors see or interact with the second and third slides of a slideshow. This is the reason for keeping only the first slide as a single hero image. [L1371, 2021-22]
- A slideshow as the main home page visual distracts from the product and is not necessary for a single-product or niche store. Use one clear image instead and remove the second slide. [L1331, 2025-26]
- When a homepage slider has several slides, each slide should link to a different collection. Do not link several slides to the same collection. [L0667, 2025]
- The Highlights banner section has four blocks as a starting point: Free shipping, Give us a call, Easy returns and Price match. Each block has a heading and text, and the section can hold up to four blocks by default. [L1310, 2021-22]
- Each Highlights banner block can use a preset icon or a custom one. Custom icons should be 50 x 50 px and in a similar style to the presets. Custom icons are optional. [L1310, 2021-22]
- Each Highlights banner block can link to a page on the store, such as shipping, contact us, returns or price match. Linking reinforces the benefit and gives the customer an easy path to details. [L1310, 2021-22]
- Use testimonial carousels and feature carousels that side-scroll, because they take less vertical space on mobile. Show a peek of the next slide so visitors see that the carousel scrolls. In PageFly the slideshow is set in the slideshow section of the element settings, under the General tab, where slides can be deleted or duplicated. [L1363, 2023]
- A feature block can show an image with a title and explanation, or, when no images exist, an icon, title and explanation. [L1363, 2023]

### Homepage sections and featured products

- Repeat the same key call to action in several places on the homepage, such as the header, a mid-page section and the footer. Different customers notice different parts of the page, so repetition increases the chance they act. [L1310, 2021-22]
- A search bar placed lower on the homepage helps shoppers who scroll past the header to find products directly. Faster product discovery usually leads to more purchases than browsing through several collections. [L1310, 2021-22]
- Featured product section, as a pattern: a heading and a subheading, a collection chosen to show a small product grid, a button labelled `Shop collection`, and an optional light background color to separate it from the white sections above. Example: `Featured Camping Gear` with the subheading `Our most-loved picks for the trail`, a collection of four products, and a `Shop collection` button. [L1310, 2021-22]
- Alternate business messages and product sections down the page instead of stacking product sections together. [L0409, 2021-22]

### Navigation, header and menus

- UX (user experience) measures how easily visitors navigate your store. Improving navigation reduces friction, builds trust, and increases conversions. [L0146, undated]
- Mega menus display multiple columns with images and featured products, replacing simple dropdowns for main categories. They help customers find products faster and improve product discovery. [L0146, undated]
- Reorder the header menu from best-selling to worst-selling category. [L1325, 2020-26]
- Turn the first menu item into a Shop dropdown with clear category names. [L1325, 2020-26]
- The store logo should link to the full collection of all products, while a Shop Now button should go straight to one product. This keeps the two navigation paths clearly separate for visitors. [L1013, 2026]
- Avoid adding extra header links such as Home or Shop Now above a product page, because they draw the visitor away from the purchase. Keep the visitor on one page so that more of the leads that arrive turn into sales. [L1013, 2026]
- A navigation bar on the product page distracts visitors who should stay on one landing page. Remove it so the page stays focused. [L1194, undated]
- A sticky header keeps the navigation, search bar, and cart available as the customer scrolls by shrinking into a thin bar with a hamburger menu. This means customers do not need to scroll back to the top to navigate. [L1371, 2021-22]
- Quick links and category shortcuts on the homepage should follow the same navigation logic as the menu. [L0197, 2025-26; L1325, 2020-26]
- Reduce the logo size and place it on the left side of the navigation. [L1325, 2020-26]
- Check the top promotion bar and add free shipping plus a percentage discount with a code if it fits. [L1325, 2020-26]

### Collection pages and filters

- Collection filters improve user experience by enabling faster product discovery and create a more professional appearance, signaling to suppliers that the store is designed for growth and customer navigation. [L0103, undated]
- Collection filters let customers quickly narrow results when browsing large catalogs, reducing frustration and increasing purchase likelihood. [L0146, undated]
- Filters help visitors reach the product they want with fewer clicks, which is the purpose of building them into the collection page. Filters are most useful when the store has many products, so a store with few products only needs a short list of filters. [L0369, 2020-26]
- Filters such as product type and color help shoppers find products faster within a collection, which improves user experience. Adding them is most useful once the store has several suppliers and enough products to filter through. [L0890, 2020-26]
- Filters that match real product attributes, such as product type and color, reduce the search effort for shoppers and improve the storefront experience. Tags only have value when they are kept consistent across all products, so each attribute should use one agreed spelling. [L0890, 2020-26]
- Collection filters work only on product tags: a product appears under a filter option only if it has the matching tag. Add the tags to each product page, then add the same tags to the tag lists in the collection sidebar. [L1010, 2020-26]
- Filter options should be built from the attributes customers use to sort products, and tags should be added to products as each new supplier brand and product is uploaded. Expanding the tag lists over time keeps filtering useful as the catalog grows. [L1010, 2020-26]
- Example: on a store with 500 products, a shopper who wants a blue longboard can narrow the grid by the Longboards type tag and the Blue color tag, so only matching products remain. Without tags on each product, this combination shows no results. [L1010, 2020-26]
- Pagination that splits products across several category pages, such as 12 products per page, hurts usability, conversion and SEO. Customers miss the second page and bounce, and Google may fail to crawl pages behind JavaScript pagination, causing indexation problems and lost link value. Disable pagination and show all products on one category page; expect roughly 3-5% more organic traffic plus a small conversion lift, though this can take about two weeks to show. [L0575, 2014]

### Product page layout

- Keep the product page to one purchase path: remove navigation and extra header links so the visitor's attention stays on the product. [L1194, undated; L1013, 2026]
- Customers often skip long product descriptions and look at images first, so place the visuals above the text they support. [L0773, 2023; L0774, 2023]
- Reviews should be visible and should answer typical buyer concerns, such as fit or size, in the review images. [L0774, 2023]

### Reference stores and review procedures

- When building or fixing a store, find an established store that sells in a related price range and product type, but not the same niche. Model its page structure and navigation, not its colors or exact design. [L1325, 2020-26]
- Include the store's default currency check in the review: check that the default currency matches the main traffic source. [L1325, 2020-26]
- Include the store's pricing display in the review: raise low prices, and show regular prices crossed out next to sale prices. [L1325, 2020-26]
- Expand the footer with customer care links, an about section and an email opt-in. [L1325, 2020-26]
- Add a brand story or about page. [L1325, 2020-26]
- Create collections for sub-styles and group matching products in each. [L1325, 2020-26]
- Review order for a store homepage:
  1. Check that the default currency matches the main traffic source. [L1325, 2020-26]
  2. Read the homepage headline and rewrite or remove it if a visitor cannot understand it. [L1325, 2020-26]
  3. Check the top promotion bar and add free shipping plus a percentage discount with a code if it fits. [L1325, 2020-26]
  4. Reduce the logo size and place it on the left side of the navigation. [L1325, 2020-26]
  5. Reorder the header menu from best-selling to worst-selling category. [L1325, 2020-26]
  6. Turn the first menu item into a Shop dropdown with clear category names. [L1325, 2020-26]
  7. Create collections for sub-styles and group matching products in each. [L1325, 2020-26]
  8. Add a brand story or about page. [L1325, 2020-26]
  9. Raise low prices, and show regular prices crossed out next to sale prices. [L1325, 2020-26]
  10. Add genuine customer reviews to product pages. [L1325, 2020-26]
  11. Expand the footer with customer care links, an about section and an email opt-in. [L1325, 2020-26]
- Procedure for modeling a reference store:
  1. Pick an established store that sells in a related price range and product type but in a different niche. [L1325, 2020-26]
  2. Look at its homepage and note what appears first and how sections are ordered. [L1325, 2020-26]
  3. Note its navigation order, category names and the dropdown structure. [L1325, 2020-26]
  4. Note how its collections group products. [L1325, 2020-26]
  5. Open its about or brand story page. [L1325, 2020-26]
  6. Check its footer links and whether it offers an email opt-in. [L1325, 2020-26]
  7. Check its Facebook and Instagram pages and how it posts there. [L1325, 2020-26]
  8. Copy the layout and structure, not the colors or design details. [L1325, 2020-26]

### Mobile and review checks

- Review each key landing page in both views before deciding it is fine:
  1. Load the page on desktop and confirm the headline and the main product image or intro text are visible without scrolling. [L1099, 2021-22]
  2. Switch to the mobile view and confirm the image does not push the title or first paragraph far down the screen. [L1099, 2021-22]
  3. Check that no pop-up, interstitial or chat bubble covers a significant part of the screen within the first 30 seconds. [L1099, 2021-22]
  4. Check that long text is split into chunks with subheadings, short paragraphs of two to three lines, and a readable font size of 18. [L1099, 2021-22]
  5. Note any issue, fix it, and recheck both views. [L1099, 2021-22]
- Most customers shop on mobile, so check that the homepage USP, the offer and the reviews appear on phone screens as clearly as on desktop. [L0409, 2021-22]
- Check the presentation of copy on mobile before judging its persuasive power. [L0925, 2021-22]
- Long walls of text on the product page are hard to read on mobile, so compress the product description into short sections that buyers can scan. [L1016, 2026]
