### Guarantees and risk reversal

#### Why guarantees work

- Risk reversal is everything a seller does to make sure the customer is not worse off after buying, such as a returns policy or a price match guarantee. Record every such element, since a returns policy is often the main form. [L0893, 2021-22]
- Money-back guarantees and hassle-free returns reduce the perceived risk of buying and build trust. Mention them near the call to action, because anything that lowers risk can increase conversion. [L0516, 2023]
- A money-back guarantee with a clearly stated period, such as 30 days, reverses the risk of purchase. The buyer knows they can return the product and get their money back, which reduces fear of loss and increases the likelihood of buying. [L1210, undated]
- Risk reversal works partly because of loss aversion: people weigh a possible loss more heavily than an equal gain, so a guarantee that removes the downside of buying addresses that fear. State the guarantee period clearly, such as 30 days, so the buyer can see exactly what they would lose or regain. [L1210, undated]
- Shoppers fear losing money, so the page should reduce the perceived risk of buying with visible guarantees and protection, such as a money-back promise, so the visitor feels safe to purchase. [L1363, 2023]
- Trust signals such as free shipping, a 30-day money-back guarantee and 'try risk-free for 30 days' near the top of the page remove purchase anxiety. The wording should make clear the company will refund an unhappy customer, which gives certainty rather than persuasion. [L0917, undated]
- A money-back guarantee tends to raise conversion rate because customers feel safer buying. Refunds may rise slightly, but the extra sales usually outweigh them. [L0683, undated]
- Trust badges and checkmark-style benefit lines add credibility to a product page when placed near the product or buy area. Only show badges and claims that are true for the store, because false credibility signals damage trust once a visitor notices. [L1380, 2022]

#### Writing the money-back guarantee

- Always include a 30-day money-back guarantee for first-time visitors to a store. This significantly increases perceived safety and conversion. Include email contact in the guarantee section so customers can claim refunds. [L0321, undated]
- Most stores that only say 'money back guarantee' without detail are weak. A better guarantee names the risk-free period (for example 30 days), states what the customer gets back (a full refund) and addresses returns and replacements directly. [L0577, 2023]
- Guarantee comparison from the lesson: a 60-day money-back guarantee was rated a medium-strength offer, and a generic 14-day guarantee covering only damaged or unwanted items was rated weak. A clearly stated 30-day risk-free period with full-refund wording was preferred. [L0577, 2023]
- Competitor stores state a money-back guarantee with a clear time window, most often 30, 60 or 90 days. Put the guarantee next to the buy button and make the window and refund terms easy to find. [L0600, 2026]
- Money-back guarantee copy to adapt for your own product. Replace the product name, and adapt the warranty length to your product. Do not copy it verbatim; rewrite the wording for your product and brand. [L0577, 2023]

```
Try the new [PRODUCT NAME] risk-free for 30 days. If you don't love it, we will simply give you a full refund. Our new [PRODUCT NAME] also comes with a 24-month warranty and no returns needed policy. If you have an issue with our product, we will simply replace it for you, no questions asked, and no need to return the item to us. The checkout process is guaranteed to be secure with Visa, Mastercard, Amex and Discover or PayPal.
```

  [L0577, 2023]

- Warranty lengths on guarantees: electronic products need at least a one-month warranty, while non-electronic products can use a shorter period such as six months or a one-year warranty. [L0577, 2023]
- Show the supplier's warranty on the product page, for example a one-year manufacturer warranty, because it helps buyers choose between similar stores. [L0667, 2025]
- Use a warranty only in the form the supplier actually provides, and state its length exactly as the supplier does. [L0667, 2025; L0577, 2023]

#### Where to place the guarantee

- Show the money-back guarantee and refund guarantee clearly on the checkout page, and also on product pages in a collapsible section placed above the product description. This is especially important during high-sales periods. [L0387, 2022]
- Place the money-back guarantee in several spots on the store: in a dropdown menu, within the product imagery and lower on the page. [L0577, 2023]
- Place trust badges directly below the add-to-cart button. Use small payment icons (PayPal, Visa, Mastercard), a free-shipping message and a 30-day money-back guarantee. [L0916, 2022+]
- Add a trust badge to the logo or checkout area. Then use a fresh pair of eyes to spot simple UX bugs (price, add-to-cart button, compare price) that are easily missed. [L0640, 2023]
- The four-column pre-footer trust row can show a free-shipping threshold, brand origin, fast and secure checkout and an easy-returns promise. Each column needs its own image and header, and a link is only added once the target policy page exists. [L1309, 2020-26]
- Pattern for a Highlights banner block: a short bold heading that names one reassurance (shipping, returns, price or service) and one brief line below it that answers the doubt behind that reassurance. It works because visitors always ask why they should buy from this store rather than a competitor, so each block removes one objection. Use blocks only for guarantees the store actually delivers. Two new examples: 'Order Tracking' with 'Follow your parcel from dispatch to your door,' and 'Secure Checkout' with 'Pay safely on every order, with no card details stored.' [L1310, 2021-22]
- A trust-element list for a product landing page: a 30-day money-back guarantee, payment badges, trust icons and star ratings, shown in a branded page layout with clear sections. [L1382, undated]
- Trust elements on the product page: a clean branded layout, a 30-day money-back guarantee, payment badges, trust icons and a star rating. [L1436, undated]
- Money-back guarantees and checkmark-style benefit lines work near the product or buy area. Place them where the buyer decides. [L1380, 2022; L1210, undated]
- Trust badges from well-known payment providers and security brands, placed near checkout, borrow the authority of those brands and reduce perceived risk. They should appear where the buyer decides to pay. [L1210, undated]

#### Returns statements

- Adding a free-returns policy statement directly below the Add to Cart button on product pages generates a +3.5% conversion-rate lift. [L0197, 2025-26]
- Shoppers fear losing money if they buy and become dissatisfied with the product. Displaying the returns policy at the point of decision reduces this perceived risk and lowers purchase hesitation. [L0197, 2025-26]
- A free-returns policy reduces the perceived risk of buying, because the customer knows they can send the product back if it is not right. Show it on the homepage right next to the free-shipping offer. [L0409, 2021-22]
- Show the return policy and shipping times prominently on the website, and display plenty of customer reviews. Buyers who distrust online stores, often after slow deliveries elsewhere, need these signals before paying. Reviews reassure them that others have bought successfully. [L1208, 2025-26]
- Leaving delivery areas, customer service contact and return terms off the product page makes visitors assume the purchase is risky. Buyers who cannot find a return policy often leave instead of buying. State these three points visibly on the page. [L1209, undated]
- Clear, published policies act as trust signals and reduce doubt at checkout. Stating return windows, shipping times and contact details up front lowers the chance of disputes and chargebacks later. [L1250, 2026]
- Short returns headline pattern: a no-cost signal, an ease word, the word 'returns' or 'swaps', and a plain permission to send the item back. It works because the buyer sees the risk removed within a few words and can scan it on any page. Examples: 'Free, simple size swaps: if it doesn't fit your room, send it back at no cost.' and 'Free, easy exchanges for any reason, with a full refund if you change your mind.' Only use wording that matches a policy you actually honour. [L0411, 2021-22]
- A returns policy visible only in the website footer effectively does not exist for customers, since only about 10% of visitors look in the footer. Any important policy must be visible on the page where the buying decision is made. [L0411, 2021-22]
- A friendlier return policy can increase returns, so track the returns rate alongside profit. [L0389, 2025-26]
- Show a no-return policy on the product page when a product cannot be returned. Then the customer can be pointed to it if a refund dispute or chargeback arises. [L0664, 2020]

#### Price match and price guarantee

- Show the price-match policy on product pages, for example in tabbed product sections, and also link it in the footer. Competitors that offer it on the product page have an advantage. [L0894, 2020-26]
- The price-match tab often seals the decision because visitors see they will get the best price. Showing it on the product page is therefore important. [L1130, 2020-26]
- A price guarantee page makes buyers feel secure and increases conversions. It is worth having even though few visitors will ever claim it. [L1130, 2020-26]
- Price-match guarantee template. Use it as the basis for your own policy, with the restrictions included:

```
We maintain competitive pricing as a priority. Within six months of purchase, if you locate the same item at a lower price from another retailer, we refund the difference. This policy also applies if our own website later shows a lower price for the same item you bought within that timeframe. Submit refund requests by emailing a product link from a competitor's site or our own within six months of your order date. Restrictions on this Price Match guarantee include: purchase must occur on our site; promotional items like rebates and BOGO offers are excluded; the item must be in stock at the competing retailer; competitors must be online-only (no physical store presence); discounters and auction sites (eBay, Overstock) don't qualify; the competitor must be an authorized retailer; and the guarantee covers item price plus shipping but excludes sales tax.
```

  [L0145, undated]

- Show the price-match tab in the product page for trust, and show the same restrictions there so the guarantee stays honest. [L0894, 2020-26; L0145, undated]

### Shipping, returns and policy pages

- Purchase friction increases when customers must guess about shipping, tracking, damage handling and refund eligibility. A clear Shipping & Returns page that answers all common questions before checkout prevents support inquiries and cart abandonment. [L0145, undated]
- A Shipping & Returns page must answer these six questions before checkout, or it risks cart abandonment. Every answer must be explicit and clear, because customers will not infer unstated details about payment timing, delivery speed or refund eligibility. [L0145, undated]
  1. When will my payment be charged?
  2. How soon will my items ship?
  3. Will I receive a tracking number?
  4. How can I track my order or get delivery updates?
  5. What happens if an item arrives damaged?
  6. What is your return and refund policy if I don't like the product?
- Make key business pages easy to find, such as About, Contact, Shipping, Returns and Price Match. Customers look at these before buying, and the more expensive the product, the more they rely on them to reduce perceived risk. [L1303, 2021-22]
- An order-tracking page can raise conversion rate, because it shows visitors that the store is reliable before they buy. [L1299, 2026]
- Phrase shipping positively on the site. Instead of 'free shipping', write 'we pay for shipping'. Instead of '10-12 business days', write 'we ship your item within 1-2 days' when the supplier ships within 24 hours. If delivery is slower, add a positive reason such as high demand and an assurance of tracking within 12-24 hours. [L1260, undated]
- Stating data-protection measures such as encryption and restricted access to payment data builds customer confidence. Keep these statements accurate, because they describe what the store actually does. [L1250, 2026]
- Display the shipping carrier logos in the shipping section of product pages, where buyers look when they check delivery. Recognised carriers reduce doubt before purchase. [L1050, 2020-26]
- Reduce a long row of trust badges to four or five of the most valuable ones, such as a money-back guarantee and express shipping. Too many badges overwhelm the customer. [L1225, 2025]

### Payment logos, badges and seals

#### Payment methods and footer signals

- Payment methods you accept (for example Shopify Payments or PayPal) work as trust signals when shown in the store footer, because visitors recognise the brands behind them. No special image is needed. [L1050, 2020-26]
- Shopify adds payment icons to the footer automatically. They show which payment options a customer can use and help build trust before purchase. [L1326, undated]
- Tick only the payment methods your Shopify store actually accepts, because every ticked method appears in the footer as a trust signal. Showing a payment method you do not accept misleads visitors. [L1050, 2020-26]
- Do not use large payment logos as trust badges, because they look scammy. Small, discreet payment icons look more professional and still signal that payment is safe. [L0916, 2022+]
- Display payment and security badges from brands you genuinely work with, such as McAfee Secure, Visa and Mastercard. Shoppers borrow trust from established names they already know. [L1207, undated]
- Show logos of well-known security or payment providers such as McAfee Secure, Visa or Mastercard to borrow their authority and trust. The buyer transfers trust from known brands to the shop. [L1209, undated]
- A local payment provider on the store builds trust with customers who already use it. In the lesson, the Swiss provider TWINT was shown, and only shops in Switzerland use it, so it signals a local store. [L0642, undated]
- Trust can be built by showing a recognised local payment-provider logo and a local flag on the store, especially in a country where customers know that provider. [L0650, undated]

#### Badges and seals: what the evidence shows

- Trust badges and credit-card icons below the add-to-cart button are optional now. They used to matter more and do not harm the store if used. (Recorded 2023.) [L1283, 2023]
- Trust badges increase conversion, and studies show the origin of the badge does not matter. Even a stock image labelled as a trusted store performed about the same as a well-known security brand. [L1111, 2020]
- Any trust badge is better than none. A study found that even a simple self-made security icon raised conversions about as much as expensive trust badges, and TrustGuard was mentioned as a badge provider. [L1002, 2019]
- Split tests of trust badges showed almost no conversion difference between badge types. If you use badges, choose ones that signal an encrypted or secure store, such as the badges Shopify provides, instead of generic 'trusted' images. [L0665, 2022]
- Trust badges such as authorised dealer badges or a trust seal can add credibility. A trust seal should only be installed after the store has consistent sales for at least three to six months. Before that, the store lacks the data to see whether the seal helps or hurts. [L0910, 2015]
- A free trust seal from an antivirus brand such as McAfee may reduce sales, because visitors may associate it with viruses. Check sales data before and after installing any seal, and remove the seal if conversions drop. [L0910, 2015]
- TrustGuard offered a 60-day money-back guarantee, so a store can test whether conversion rates improve before committing to the monthly price. If it does not justify the cost, cancel and ask for a discount. In the lesson, the monthly price was negotiated down from a higher rate to about 15 dollars. [L0910, 2015]
- Trust badges and credit-card icons: how the advice has changed.
  - 2019: any badge is better than none. [L1002, 2019]
  - 2020: badge origin does not matter. [L1111, 2020]
  - 2022: choose badges that signal a secure store. [L0665, 2022]
  - 2023: optional, harmless if used. [L1283, 2023]
  - Current rule: use badges that are true for your store, and check conversion data before keeping a seal. [L0910, 2015; L1380, 2022]

### Charity, authority and third-party claims

#### Charity pledges

- A charity pledge builds trust only when it names the actual organisation and shows its logo and information. Vague claims such as supporting abandoned dogs without naming a partner look shady to new visitors. [L0355, 2020-26]
- Even a very small local nonprofit is enough for a charity pledge. Name it, show its logo and include information about it, so visitors can look it up and verify the partnership. [L0355, 2020-26]
- A store that donates 10% of sales to a cause should state the 10% figure and name the beneficiary. The percentage alone, without a named partner, does not create credibility. [L0355, 2020-26]

#### Authority and press

- Authority signals include mentions of major publications and expert endorsements. An expert endorsement that also contains a benefit statement from a relevant professional gives both authority and social proof at the same time. [L0918, 2023]
- Press mentions, expert endorsements, notable customers and research-backed ingredients all build authority. Use each only when it is true for the store. [L0919, undated; L1363, 2023]
- Add third-party studies, reliable outside sources or a small self-run survey with transparent methodology to the page. Quote specific percentages from them, because statistics help buyers justify the purchase. [L1016, 2026]
- A named ingredient or component backed by published clinical research can serve as proof in a feature list, because the buyer sees a recognised mechanism behind the promised result. Use such a claim only when the evidence is real and can be cited, since an unsupported 'clinically proven' statement damages trust. [L1210, undated]
- Claims such as damage reduction, frizz control or clinical-style wording need concrete proof on the page before they can convert. Such proof includes how the technology works, test data or guarantee terms. An audit should flag homepage claims without this support as a trust gap. [L0886, 2025-26]
- Best-in-class claim pattern: formula superlative + category + location + measurable count or rating. It works because a bare superlative is disbelieved, while a verifiable number makes the claim credible. Examples: 'Most-reviewed sleep mask seller in Germany, with 4,800 verified reviews.' and 'Rated 4.7 out of 5 by 2,300 verified buyers across our pet bed range.' [L0411, 2021-22]
- Third-party certifications from independent organisations (such as organic certification confirming production standards) serve as proof. Display the certification badge and the organisation's name in product copy. [L0295, 2021-22]
- A product tested and rated by an independent testing organisation can carry the rating and test results in product copy as proof. Example: Choice, an Australian organisation that tests products for effectiveness and provides ratings. [L0295, 2021-22]
- Customers assume sellers will make positive claims about their own products, so skepticism is natural. Independent third-party proof points confirm that an emotional purchasing decision is also rational, which enhances the persuasiveness of all preceding copy. [L0295, 2021-22]
- Featured-in logo bars often come from paid sponsored posts or affiliate placements rather than organic press coverage. Treat such logos as paid authority signals, and only display them where the placement is accurate and can be backed up. [L1348, 2023]
- Do not fabricate press or media mentions. Show 'as seen in' logos only if the brand was actually featured. [L1363, 2023]
- Press mentions shown as a marquee of logos, rather than a large block. Keep reviews visible and repeated on other pages. [L1225, 2025]
- A low-cost alternative to paid press: get a review or feature from niche blogs or affiliate publishers, who then receive affiliate traffic, or create your own niche blog on WordPress that features the product. [L1363, 2023]
- Linking out from the landing page to outside press articles is debated. It can add authority and help SEO, but it takes visitors away from the purchase path. Decide per store. The lesson's own stance was not to link them. [L1363, 2023]
- Trust signals such as authorised retailer badges work best when added brand by brand, in this order:
  1. Add the authorised-retailer badge to the collection page of each brand.
  2. Add the same badge to the product pages of that brand.
  3. Add further signals (payment methods, carrier logos, memberships, charities, customer list) as they become available.

  [L1050, 2020-26]

- Authorised retailer badges raise trust gradually, not as an immediate jump in sales. [L1050, 2020-26]
