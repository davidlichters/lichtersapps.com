## Copywriting and messaging

### Copy foundations and the job of copy

- Copywriting is persuasive writing that aims at one specific action, such as a click, an opt-in or an add to cart. In ecommerce most of these actions are add to cart and purchase, so any product page text that pushes toward them is copywriting. [L0396, 2021-22]
- Content writing informs and educates, but a product description has to sell the product. Describing what the product is does not persuade on its own. [L0396, 2021-22]
- The marketing message can be delivered as text, video, images or any combination, and no single format is required. What matters is that every element of the marketing message is present, whatever the format. [L0396, 2021-22]
- Copy has to answer three questions:
  | Question | Where the answer comes from |
  |---|---|
  | Why buy this product? | Mainly the marketing message |
  | Why buy it from you? | Can be covered by a unique selling proposition of up to five points |
  | Why buy it now? | Mainly the marketing message | [L0396, 2021-22]
- Copy does not need to be the best copy in the world. It only needs to be more developed than the marketing messages of direct competitors to persuade customers to buy from you. [L0396, 2021-22]
- Marketing messages for the product (copy, videos and images) should be better than competitors' messages. This element is separate from the offer structure and gets its own treatment later. [L0411, 2021-22]
- Write website copy from the customer's perspective. Answer their questions before they ask them, such as shipping time, return policy and product materials, so they feel comfortable buying without uncertainty. [L0268, 2020]
- Education builds trust by giving useful information before asking for money. Explaining the problem and its causes gives the reader value up front and makes the product feel like the logical answer. [L0516, 2023]
- Consistency also means people want their actions to match the identity they show publicly. Copy that appeals to an identity, such as "of course you want the best for your pet", makes the reader more likely to act in line with it. [L0516, 2023]
- A complete landing page must surface the customer's problems, accentuate benefits, demonstrate the features that prove those benefits, show the desired outcome, and handle objections, usually in an FAQ section. [L0917, undated]
- Use an AI chat tool such as ChatGPT to rewrite benefits in a more striking or conversational style, and to produce problem-solution copy and feature-to-benefit lists from the product description. If the copy is weak, use such a tool to draft it. [L0917, undated]
- Specific, consumer-addressed wording in guarantees and product copy outperforms generic phrasing, because generic text reads as template text that buyers have seen many times. [L0577, 2023]
- Copy quality rarely decides a page alone. A poor headline or unclear page is a common reason for a 1 to 2 percent conversion rate, and small changes to landing page structure often produce larger gains than split testing. [L0387, 2022]

### Bottom-of-funnel and psychological framing

- The path to conversion runs through negative emotions, not around them. Bottom-of-funnel ads must address the psychological state of prospects by first acknowledging the problem they face, before presenting how your solution transforms their situation. [L0221, 2026]
- To generate bottom-of-funnel ads, ask Claude for ad copy and concepts built from all gathered market research, the emotional framework analysis and the target evidence metrics. Claude creates the ad copy and concepts from the customer psychology and language patterns you provide. [L0221, 2026]
- Competitor headlines pair a specific benefit with a time frame, which makes the promise measurable and sets clear expectations. Use the same formula of benefit plus time window, for example *Fall Asleep Faster in 14 Days* or *Feel Less Stiff by Week Three*. Only use time frames you can back with real customer results. [L0600, 2026]

### Mapping the product before writing

- Product copy is built from separate elements such as headline, benefits, features and proof, not written in one pass from top to bottom. Gathering these elements in a structured document before writing reduces the stall that comes from facing an empty product description box, and it shortens total writing time even though it adds a step. [L1160, 2021-22]
- Answer these questions about the product before writing any copy, in this order:
  1. Product name (used as the tab name and header, so the record can be tracked)
  2. Primary problem: the one main problem the product solves for most customers
  3. Secondary problems: any additional problems the product solves
  4. Unique mechanism(s): the attribute that solves the primary problem in a way competing products do not
  5. How the unique mechanism works: the process that produces the result
  6. Features: the attributes of the product you would tell a customer to justify buying it
  7. Benefit of each feature: a list that mirrors the feature list point for point
  8. How the product improves the ideal customer's life: a picture of what the customer experiences once they have used it
  9. Proof points: reviews, demonstration videos, third-party tests [L1160, 2021-22]
- Use a Google Sheets product mapping template for the exercise. Make a copy and save it to Google Drive, or convert it to Excel if you prefer to work offline. [L1160, 2021-22]
- Keep a master template tab in the mapping sheet. For each new product, right-click the template tab and choose Duplicate, then right-click the copy and choose Rename to set the product name. Each product then gets its own tab in one file, so products can be flicked between and compared. [L1160, 2021-22]
- The primary problem should be the biggest and most common problem the product solves for most customers, because people buy a product to solve a problem they have. Pick that one for the main slot and record any other problems as secondary problems. [L1160, 2021-22]
- Secondary problems can be recorded in addition to the primary problem. A product may solve several, and that is not a problem. Only the biggest one goes into the main slot when writing copy. [L1160, 2021-22]
- A unique mechanism is an attribute of the product that solves the primary problem in a way competing products do not. Ideally it is built into the product, such as a technology or a specially designed feature, and a product can have more than one. [L1160, 2021-22]
- Claiming a unique feature without explaining how it works is a mistake. After naming a unique mechanism, always state how it produces the result, otherwise the claim is not convincing. [L1160, 2021-22]
- Five product features is the recommended number to list. Three is acceptable if you want to focus, fewer than five is fine, and beyond five the value of each added feature drops. [L1160, 2021-22]
- Each feature must be paired with a benefit that answers why it matters to the customer. Write the benefit list as a matching list to the feature list, so every feature has its own benefit line. [L1160, 2021-22]
- The customer transformation describes what the ideal customer experiences in real use after owning the product, in concrete terms. It should show how daily life changes, not just restate the product's features. [L1160, 2021-22]
- Proof points are evidence that backs your claims and should be recorded in the mapping for use in copy and on the product page. Typical proof points are customer reviews, demonstration videos showing use and effect, and third-party tests that verify the results. [L1160, 2021-22]
- Worked example, a compact two-person infrared sauna for home use. This was technical and expensive, sold outside the field the mapper knew well.
  - Primary problem: the range of health benefits people seek from heat and infrared light, including better sleep, weight loss, relaxation, improved circulation, relief from joint and muscle pain, and improved skin.
  - Secondary problem: convenience, since users no longer have to travel to a gym or spa, pay repeatedly for sessions, or lose travel time.
  - Unique mechanism: a trademarked technology called True Wave II Full Spectrum Heating Technology. Its heaters produce near, mid and far infrared together at a full 1000W, which delivers the benefits of infrared saunas in less time.
  - Features listed: True Wave Technology, low EMF, 360 degree heating, non-toxic materials, easy assembly, lifetime warranty, and chromotherapy lighting.
  - Matching benefits:
    - full-spectrum heat in a shorter session
    - electromagnetic output of only 1mg against a recommended healthy limit of 3mg, which makes everyday use considered safe based on testing to date
    - consistent infrared access across the whole body from 360 degree heating
    - no toxic chemicals escaping from the materials
    - assembly by two people in about an hour with no professional installation needed
    - a lifetime warranty so the customer never pays again for a defective unit
    - full-spectrum light therapy linked to reduced swelling, pain relief and faster recovery
  - Customer transformation: the customer can practise heat therapy at home, which makes them more consistent and helps them feel better in both physical and emotional life.
  - Proof points: product reviews, a video demonstration of the product, and third-party testing of the heat sources. [L1160, 2021-22]
- An infrared sauna example listed 1000W of infrared energy as the full output, stated as 25x more than the closest competitor, and an electromagnetic field output of 1mg against a recommended healthy maximum of 3mg. These figures were used as concrete, checkable benefit claims in the copy. [L1160, 2021-22]
- Fill in a marketing message sheet for each product before writing its description:
  1. Write the primary promise or promises.
  2. Write the unique mechanism.
  3. List the features and their benefits.
  4. List the dimensionalized benefits to include on the page.
  5. Give the sheet to the copywriter together with the product data, or use it yourself as the first step before writing. [L0396, 2021-22]

### Problems, pain points and desire

- Problems are not always consciously recognized by the buyer. Status-related problems in particular are usually felt subconsciously, so copy has to name them even when the buyer has not put them into words. [L1157, 2021-22]
- A problem, also called a pain point, creates a desire for either a solution or a pleasant outcome. Copy should connect the problem to the result the buyer wants. [L1157, 2021-22]
- Buyers are motivated either by moving away from pain or by moving toward pleasure. Copy can address only the pain, only the pleasure, or both, depending on what fits the product and audience. [L1157, 2021-22]
- People buy for only two reasons: to escape pain or to pursue pleasure. Copy that triggers these feelings more strongly than competitor pages tends to sell more. [L0396, 2021-22]
- People buy a product to solve a problem that creates a desire, so the copy must first name the problem the product solves. Match it to the problem the target customer is actually experiencing. It is fine to address more than one problem the product solves. [L0396, 2021-22]
- After naming the problem, the copy must explain how the product solves it. Then it must describe how the customer's life changes in a way they can picture, which is what is meant by benefits. [L0396, 2021-22]
- Visitors on product pages already have a strong interest in solving a specific pain point. A headline that names that pain point or its direct outcome gets the most attention. [L0416, 2021-22]
- Copywriting should surface the customer's problem first, then present the solution as a benefit. Features are shown to prove the benefit, and the copy should ask what each feature means for the customer. [L0919, undated]
- For products that solve a health problem, name the specific conditions and the pain the customer wants to escape. When the problem is clear, the customer does not need false urgency to buy, only a direct call to fix it. [L0396, 2021-22]

### Emotion before logic

- Primary promise and unique mechanism trigger emotion, while proof, dimensionalized benefits and comparisons supply the logic that confirms the feeling is right. Specifications and features written clearly also serve as logic. [L0396, 2021-22]
- People buy an emotion and then justify it with logic, so each section of the page should add rational reasons that match the pain point the visitor is feeling. [L1016, 2026]
- When buyers are asked why they bought, they recall the logical reasons easily, but they often cannot name the emotion they felt. This effect is strongest for expensive purchases, so high-ticket copy must trigger the emotion before it offers the reasons to justify the purchase. [L0611, 2021-22]
- Once the headline and primary promise have hooked the visitor, the copy should trigger emotion before the product is pitched. People buy for emotional reasons that logic then supports, so an emotional response built early raises the chance of a sale considerably. [L0580, 2021-22]
- The first one or two paragraphs under the headline should name the main problem the primary promise addresses and show what life looks like once that problem is solved. Describe the feeling the customer will have after solving it, such as relief, happiness, excitement or satisfaction. [L0580, 2021-22]
- Getting the reader to picture the end result makes them start to experience the emotions tied to that result. If product images or video already created this picture, the copy reinforces and multiplies the effect, so it is still worth doing. [L0580, 2021-22]
- Two proven ways to create this emotional visualization are telling a story about the product and using thought commands. Story is the stronger choice when a real story exists; thought commands work for any product. [L0580, 2021-22]
- Before writing the imagined scene, build a short product map that lists the primary problem, the primary promise and the secondary problems. Take the scene's angle and vocabulary from these entries so the picture follows directly from the headline and keeps the copy consistent. [L0580, 2021-22]
- If the product page already has sales copy, the story or thought-command section can be added as a few extra paragraphs without rewriting the rest. Highlight the new text while editing so it can be reviewed and compared against the original version. [L0580, 2021-22]
- Concrete everyday details, such as a specific time of day, a short distance from the bed and one simple action, make a scene easier for the reader to picture than general benefit statements. Show a routine the reader can imagine happening in their own home. [L0580, 2021-22]
- Copy that opens with a thought or story and then moves into the offer is a structure to test. Add the copy elements one at a time (opening story or thought command, primary promise, unique mechanism, then benefits) and save each version. Comparing the versions shows whether each added element makes the text easier to read and more convincing. [L0465, 2021-22]
- A mechanism placed right after the primary promise keeps readers reading toward the call to action and at the same time builds the logical case for the product. Every section of the copy should keep the reader moving down the page. [L0465, 2021-22]

### Primary promise headlines

- Good copy has no effect if nobody reads it, so the headline's first job is to earn attention before any selling happens. A product page or sales page headline should make visitors read the next sentence, not decide whether to buy. [L0416, 2021-22]
- Headlines carry most of the copywriting weight, followed by short bullet points. Keep bullets short and benefit-driven, and add a testimonial section because shoppers look for reviews before they trust a site. [L0335, 2023]
- In a video sales presentation, the headline is the first one or two sentences spoken at the start of the video. Its purpose is the same as in text: to make viewers decide to keep watching. [L0416, 2021-22]
- Headline must include searchable keywords customers use. Example: *Spiralizer Envy Spiral Vegetable Slicer* includes both the brand name and the product keywords customers search for. (undated) [L0227, 2014]
- Place a unique selling proposition with the product name at the top or directly below it, and include a money-back guarantee in it. Keep the line short and benefit-led. [L0871, 2021+]
- Hero value-proposition formula: a short headline that states the outcome the customer wants, paired with a qualifier that makes it specific to the product, then a trust line directly beneath. Example pattern for a grooming product: *Remove all pet hair from your home easily and effortlessly*, followed by *Rated Excellent on Trustpilot*. Second example for a fitness recovery product: *Boost recovery and blood flow after every session*, followed by *Trusted by 10,000+ athletes*. [L0918, 2023]
- Procedure for writing a primary promise headline:
  1. Read the primary problem the product solves from the product mapping worksheet.
  2. Check whether the product sits in the health or finance space. If it does, do not put health or financial result claims in the headline.
  3. If it is health or finance, pick a secondary problem (such as convenience, time saved or money saved) or the biggest feature or unique mechanism as the basis of the headline. If it is not health or finance, start from the primary problem.
  4. Write the headline as a direct reflection of that problem, making the promise specific and including the how.
  5. If you use a number, make sure you have evidence for it and can explain where it comes from when asked.
  6. Make the headline bigger and bolder than the body copy, and include keywords if organic search matters.
  7. Split test the headline against at least one alternative and keep the version with the better time on page and conversion rate. [L0416, 2021-22]
- Case: infrared sauna headline. The product is in the health space and its primary problem is health benefits, so a health-based headline such as weight loss within a timeframe would put ads at risk and could not be proven. The copywriter chose the unique mechanism (full-spectrum infrared heating) as the basis, because the manufacturer provided heating-power and efficiency figures claimed to be backed by evidence, and avoided naming any health benefit. The headline promised 25 times more heating power and efficiency from the IS-2 sauna, with the product title and image kept above it and the headline placed as the first text element visitors read. No sales outcome is reported. The stated goal was to create enough curiosity that readers continue to the body copy, and the copywriter noted that the figure must be explainable if a customer asks where it comes from. [L0416, 2021-22]
- Outcome-led headline with a proof stack: the headline names the result the buyer gets, a short support line makes the product specific, and three perks or guarantees follow, such as free shipping, a free gift or a risk-free trial. The buyer reads the benefit first and sees low-risk reasons to buy right away, instead of a temporary discount.
  - Example 1: *Keep Your Knives Sharp Through Every Dinner Rush*, followed by a forged steel blade, free resharpening for a year and a 60-day return window.
  - Example 2: *Get Clean Sound Without the Cable Mess*, followed by a 30-hour battery case, free shipping and a 30-day money-back guarantee. [L0886, 2025-26]
- Headlines that name the specific risk a buyer fears and then offer one simple action to avoid it convert well. The formula is risk first, then the low-effort fix.
  - Example 1: *Don't book the trip before you check the seat map.*
  - Example 2: *Don't buy the sofa before you measure the doorway.* [L1016, 2026]
- Formula: compare the abstract benefit to a familiar professional service the buyer would normally pay for, so the product feels like a personal solution. This makes the benefit tangible and memorable in headlines and copy.
  - Example 1: *A masseuse for your back, in your pocket.*
  - Example 2: *A personal trainer on your wrist, without the monthly gym fee.* [L0919, undated]
