## Prioritizing and running the optimization plan

### Entry conditions: when conversion work starts

- Start optimizing as soon as the store makes its first sale, because that proves the checkout works and customers want the product. Before any sale, begin conversion work once the store reaches about 40 unique visitors per day. [L1293, 2014]
- At 40 unique visitors per day, the store should make at least one sale every other day. If it does not, fix the conversion problem before spending more on traffic. [L1293, 2014]
- Run these basic checks first: 1) confirm Google Product Listing Ads are running, 2) confirm Amazon Product Listing Ads are running, 3) confirm the store has at least 50 products. [L1293, 2014]
- Action plan from the lesson, in order: [L1293, 2014]
  1. Check traffic and confirm at least 40 unique visitors per day. [L1293, 2014]
  2. If traffic is low, confirm Google Product Listing Ads and Amazon Product Listing Ads are running. [L1293, 2014]
  3. Set up free bonus gifts for the top three products. [L1293, 2014]
  4. Brainstorm on paper or log into the shop and make the changes. [L1293, 2014]

### Order of operations

Work in this sequence. Each step points to the section that holds its full detail.

1. **Confirm entry conditions.** Traffic, basic ad checks and the 50-product minimum from the entry section come first. [L1293, 2014]
2. **Audit the store as a paid visitor sees it.** Run the cold-visitor review, then the full-funnel audit at about one and a half months in. [L1346, 2020-26; L1017, undated]
3. **Choose where to focus.** Rank products by traffic and work on the products that already get visitors. [L1446, 2020-26; L0208, 2024+; L0941, 2022]
4. **Build the first product page, then duplicate it.** Build the page structure on one product page first. Once its add-to-cart ratio meets the platform minimum, duplicate that page for the other products. [L1194, undated]
5. **Run the conversion setup in order.** Follow the setup sequence. [L0393, undated]
6. **Roll out the marketing message in stages.** Start with best-sellers, test, then extend. [L0396, 2021-22]
7. **Build the test backlog and run tests continuously.** [L1364, 2021-22; L0399, 2021-22]
8. **Hold the weekly routine.** [L1293, 2014; L1088, 2020; L0390, 2019]
9. **Change scale or strategy only on evidence.** Use the scaling, cut and maintenance rules. [L0686, 2021-22; L0744, 2021-22; L1228, 2021-22; L0639, 2023]

### Store audit procedures

#### Cold-visitor review

*Older (2021-22)? No. Source: one source, undated.*

Review a store the way a cold visitor from a paid ad sees it:

1. Note the product and offer promised by the ad that brought the visitor. [L1346, 2020-26]
2. Open the homepage and check within a few seconds whether it shows the same product type, and whether a stranger can tell what the store sells. [L1346, 2020-26]
3. Open a product page and check whether the featured image shows the product clearly, whether the description sells it with benefits, features and dimensions, and whether reviews, stock count, delivery estimate and live chat link are visible. [L1346, 2020-26]
4. Check the product page tabs or sections for the description, the shipping policy and any price match policy. [L1346, 2020-26]
5. Scroll to the footer and check that get-in-touch details, support hours, support email, live chat link, social media links and policy links are present and point to relevant pages. [L1346, 2020-26]
6. Check that the social media accounts exist and show recent activity. [L1346, 2020-26]
7. Write down every point where you would hesitate to buy, and fix those points first. [L1346, 2020-26]

#### Full-funnel audit at about six weeks

1. Once the store is built and ads are running, around one and a half months in, audit the whole store. [L1017, undated]
2. Go through every page and every step of the funnel from the ad click to the completed order, including minor details that tutorials may not cover. [L1017, undated]
3. Look for small problems that cause drop-off, such as a missing element or unclear step, instead of waiting for the student to report low conversion or many abandoned carts. [L1017, undated]

#### Standing assumption

- Assume your store has issues that need constant fixing, since even large companies have teams fixing bugs and reports every day. Regularly review your store with fresh eyes and check trends in your data. [L0390, 2019]

#### Marketing message rollout

*one source.*

1. Start with the best-selling products, not the whole catalogue. [L0396, 2021-22]
2. Test the new descriptions against the old ones. [L0396, 2021-22]
3. Optionally run conversion rate tests and compare the results. [L0396, 2021-22]
4. If the new approach works, implement it across the rest of the products. [L0396, 2021-22]

2. Test prices. [L0773, 2023]
3. Test lead magnets (free gifts). [L0773, 2023]
4. Add automatic discounts. [L0773, 2023]

### Choosing where to focus

#### Product pages first

- Improving product pages where you already receive traffic increases conversion rate without requiring additional ad spend, directly improving ROAS. This is the highest-leverage starting point for optimization. [L0208, 2024+]
- Improve product page optimization for products that already convert before scaling their traffic. Better pages turn the same clicks into more sales. [L0941, 2022]
- Optimize the top 20 percent of product pages first, since they usually produce about 80 percent of sales, and then work through the remaining products. [L0966, 2025]
- Optimized product pages support free Google traffic, Google Ads clicks and conversions, so optimizing the products already getting traffic gives the fastest return. [L0966, 2025]
- Start conversion work on pages that already get the most traffic, since these produce the fastest gains. Check Google Analytics for your most visited pages before deciding what to change. [L0972, 2025]

#### Ranking products by traffic

Two cut-offs appear in the sources. Both are kept here, and the sources do not say which applies to which store size.

1. Look at the product analytics and rank all products by the traffic they receive. [L1446, 2020-26]
2. Identify the top 100 products that actually get visitors. [L1446, 2020-26]
3. Focus optimization on those product pages rather than trying to perfect every page. [L1446, 2020-26]
- Focus optimization effort on the top 20% of products that receive traffic, since these are likely to be the initial money makers. Other products can wait until traffic data shows they matter. [L1446, 2020-26]

#### 80/20 across pages and channels

- Use the 80-20 principle for SEO optimization: start with the top 20% of pages by traffic. Optimize these first to boost conversions, then slowly expand to other pages. Do not try to optimize the entire catalog on day one. *Older (2022).* [L0249, 2022]
- Use an 80/20 approach when deciding where to focus: for an established store of about three to four years, paid search and organic search are almost always the largest traffic sources and therefore where conversion work has the most impact. Compare sources in Google Analytics to see which converts and which does not. *Older (2021-22).* [L1456, 2021-22]
- Focus on the top 20% of products by traffic and conversion first. (Derived from the two rules above.) [L1446, 2020-26; L0966, 2025]

#### Representative analysis

- Changing website elements at random based on personal opinion wastes effort. Improvements should follow from evidence of what customers need to see to move toward purchase, judged with empathy and in comparison with competitor pages. *Older (2021-22).* [L1456, 2021-22]
- When a competitor comparison reveals a problem on one product, the same problem usually exists on other products in the store. Analyse a few representative products in the comparison matrix instead of checking every product separately. *Older (2021-22).* [L1456, 2021-22]

#### Traffic versus conversion

- If traffic is high but conversions are low, improve the product page and retarget visitors. If traffic is low, first focus on getting relevant traffic. Conversion levers include social proof and compelling offers, and retargeting drove most of the owner's sales. [L1077, undated]
- When traffic already arrives but sales lag, improve the conversion rate before buying more traffic. A store's conversion rate is never finished and should be improved continuously. *Older (2019).* [L0663, 2019]
- On-site conversion changes produce small, incremental gains and do little if the traffic is poorly matched to the offer or the offer itself is weak. Fix the audience match and the offer before spending time on these details. *Older (2021-22).* [L1522, 2021-22]

### Checklists

#### CRO action checklist (full sequence)

*one source. Google Optimize and Neat are named as in the source; check that each tool is still available before use.*

1. Set up a CRO tracking system, such as a Trello board, spreadsheet, Google Sheet, Asana or another project management tool, to log every conversion test. [L0399, 2021-22]
2. Connect a CRO testing tool to the e-commerce site (Google Optimize or the Neat A/B testing tool on Shopify), and test with one or both. [L0399, 2021-22]
3. Fill the first column of the tracking system with a list of future CRO tests, based on what you think needs testing after reviewing the material. [L0399, 2021-22]
4. Review your competition analysis, covering competitors' offers and USPs. [L0399, 2021-22]
5. Compare your current offer with competitors and judge where you stand in your market. [L0399, 2021-22]
6. Improve the offer toward a strong C.U.R.V. offer where you are behind, and record it in the curve offer builder. [L0399, 2021-22]
7. Show the curve offer on the home page, product page and About Us page at minimum. [L0399, 2021-22]
8. Review product descriptions against the marketing message parts. [L0399, 2021-22]
9. Write a marketing message for one or more current bestsellers, or for high-traffic products with low conversion rates, and share the mechanism with any external copywriter. [L0399, 2021-22]
10. Implement the review multiplier call system, share the script with customer service, and schedule post-purchase calls. [L0399, 2021-22]
11. Add video review requests to every review request, with image reviews as a second option. [L0399, 2021-22]
12. Set up the off-site customer survey and the emails that invite visitors, opt-ins, pre-purchase prospects and existing customers to take it, choosing questions from the template list. [L0399, 2021-22]
13. Choose and implement one on-site survey mechanism, such as an online chat widget or Hotjar. [L0399, 2021-22]
14. Identify other on-site CRO tests, plan them and start testing. [L0399, 2021-22]
15. Review the list of smaller on-site factors and add the promising ones to the test plan. [L0399, 2021-22]
16. Keep the surveys and testing running continuously. [L0399, 2021-22]

### Test backlog and test design

- Keep at least one conversion test running at all times and treat testing as a permanent item on the to-do list. Even small tests add up to long-term change, and new test ideas should always be collected. *Older (2021-22).* [L1364, 2021-22]
- Conversion rate optimization is an incremental process built from many small, repeatable improvements. Waiting for one rare change that doubles conversion rate is not a plan for growth. *Older (2021-22).* [L1364, 2021-22]
- Make many small changes to the store, such as offer placement and wording, and test them one after another rather than waiting for one large redesign. Each small published change can noticeably move results, so check the effect of every change. [L0549, undated]
- Store design tests make sense only after the store already makes sales. Before that, use design changes only when the offer and the traffic are both confirmed to be working. [L0388, 2024+]
- Rewrite the marketing message first for products that already sell well. Then prioritize products with high traffic but low conversion rates, since they offer the biggest conversion upside. *Older (2021-22).* [L0399, 2021-22]
- Add the smaller on-site factors that may improve conversion to the test plan, and test them after the larger tests are planned. Use the tracking system for this list too. *Older (2021-22).* [L0399, 2021-22]
- Many popular conversion tips and hacks change very little on their own. The larger gains usually come from fundamental changes to the store, offer and purchase path, so these should be prioritized over minor tweaks. *Older (2021-22).* [L0598, 2021-22]
- Conversion improvements fall into two kinds: quick actions that can be taken immediately, and ongoing processes that take weeks or months to build and pay off. Both should be planned, and the ongoing ones should be treated as continuous work inside the business. *Older (2021-22).* [L0598, 2021-22]
- Conversion optimization is a foundational system. These 11 tasks form a foundational conversion system that requires continuous revisiting and refinement beyond initial implementation. [L0040, 2020-26]

### Quick wins versus tests

- Split the roadmap into quick wins (fixes to do now, such as making a guarantee, free shipping or free gifts visible) and testing ideas (hero positioning, risk reversal, comparison modules), and tag each with the metric it should move. [L0886, 2025-26]
- In an example 30-day roadmap, the first block (days 1 to 3) was labelled a quick win with high impact. The days 1 to 5 block included rewriting the homepage hero to explain the premium tool, its technology and its benefits (less damage, less frizz, less styling time); adding a comparison section against standard hot tools near the homepage and product page; and making the guarantee, free shipping and free gifts visible, which was rated low effort. The metrics to improve were homepage conversion rate, product page engagement, add-to-cart and checkout start. [L0886, 2025-26]

### Weekly routine and ongoing maintenance

#### Weekly cycle

- Repeat the full optimization process every week, for example every Monday, instead of doing it once, because a store either grows or slips backwards. [L1293, 2014]
- Conversion optimization is an ongoing process. After the initial setup, keep checking how ads and pages perform over time and review each optimization area again. *Older (2020).* [L1088, 2020]
- Monitor ad spend every two weeks (self-auditing), identify ads and products not generating profitable sales, and verify ROAS is stable or improving. *Older (2020).* [L0245, 2020]

#### Reading trends

- A small, short-term dip in traffic or sales is not always a problem. Check whether it continues into the next weeks before concluding something is broken. *Older (2020).* [L1088, 2020]
- Slow sales periods are a good time to work on conversion rate optimization, adding products and suppliers, improving content, and expanding sales channels. The aim is to be ready when orders return at a higher rate. *Older (2021).* [L0801, 2021]

#### Measuring compounded gains

- Improving each funnel step by 10% compounds: 1.1 x 1.1 x 1.1 = 1.331, so three separate 10% gains give about 33% total, not 30%. Funnel steps to optimize include the visit to next-page rate, the add-to-cart rate, the checkout click rate, abandoned-cart recovery, and pre-purchase emails. [L0572, 2014]

### Dashboard organization

- Organize the dashboard with decision metrics on the left, top-of-funnel metrics in the middle, and bottom-of-funnel metrics on the right. This structure allows you to see everything that drives daily decisions without scrolling, then use the funnel metrics (reach, impressions, link clicks, add to carts, checkouts, purchases) to quickly identify where drop-offs occur in the customer journey. Everything you need to make decisions should fit on one screen. [L0290, 2025-26]

### CRO team and agency compensation

- CRO agency success is harder to measure than paid media because conversion improvement is influenced by many factors. Structure compensation as base plus incentive tied to incremental revenue or conversion rate improvement, but beware revenue share percentages that favor the agency. [L0217, 2025-26]

### Ad-level and product-level cuts

#### First 24 hours (ad level)

- Within the first 24 hours, evaluate individual ads within the ad set (not the ad set level). Turn off any single ad that has spent $10 or more, shows a cost per unique link click (CPC) of $3.50 or higher, and has generated no add-to-cart events or purchases, because such CPC is not sustainable long-term. [L0229, undated]

#### Product break-even (product level)

- A product with no sales in its first two days can still become profitable after the landing page or offer is fixed and the ads have collected more data. Cut a product at the pre-set break-even limit rather than at the first disappointing day, and change one weak funnel stage at a time. [L0639, 2023]
- Changing the ad budget at product level: monitor, cut, redirect and verify. (See the reallocation procedure below.) [L0245, 2020]

#### Reallocating budget from losers

*monitoring every two weeks.*

1. Monitor ad spend every two weeks (self-auditing). [L0245, 2020]
2. Identify ads and products not generating profitable sales. [L0245, 2020]
3. Cut spend from underperforming products entirely. [L0245, 2020]
4. Redirect that budget to products that are converting or have historically converted. [L0245, 2020]
5. Verify return on ad spend (ROAS) is stable or improving. [L0245, 2020]

#### Recovering a store that almost breaks even

*Older (2022).* To improve a store that is almost breaking even after several months of ads:

1. Review all ad and analytics data to see which ads and products lead to the best results. [L0803, 2022]
2. Identify losing products because of low margin, supplier issues or high ad cost. [L0803, 2022]
3. Stop advertising losers, renegotiate with the supplier, or focus only on organic traffic for them. [L0803, 2022]
4. Reinvest the cut budget into the top 20 percent of products and optimize those product pages for higher conversion. [L0803, 2022]

#### Launch budgets

- Pouring large budgets into untested campaigns is a mistake. Start with small budgets to see if a campaign is profitable before scaling, and if results are poor, delete and restart the campaign rather than modifying it extensively. *Older (2020).* [L0210, 2022]

### Scaling and changing strategy

#### Scaling checks

- Cost structure example: £15 cost per £45 sale equals 33% of revenue spent on ads. Use this ratio when calculating whether to double budget during 4-hour scaling checks. [L0308, 2023]

#### Raising bids

- To justify a higher bid while keeping your target profit per sale, you must first raise the conversion rate. Confirm that the new bid is profitable at your current conversion rate before increasing it. *Older (2021-22).* [L0686, 2021-22]

#### Google Shopping ceiling

- Once the profitable Google Shopping limit is reached, raising conversion rate is the only remaining lever inside that channel, and it also has a ceiling. After that, keep the account profitable in maintenance mode and focus on other channels. *Older (2021-22).* [L0744, 2021-22]

#### Low conversion on paid traffic

*Older (2021-22).*

1. Check that the traffic targets the right audience. [L1228, 2021-22]
2. If the ads look good but conversions are low, reduce ad spend. [L1228, 2021-22]
3. Focus effort on the website and raise the conversion rate. [L1228, 2021-22]
4. Increase ad spend again only once the website can support the cost of the traffic. [L1228, 2021-22]

#### Scaling a product

- Build the page structure on one product page first, and duplicate it only after its add-to-cart ratio meets the platform minimum. [L1194, undated]
- Improve product page optimization for products that already convert before scaling their traffic. [L0941, 2022]
