## Email and phone capture: pop-ups, opt-ins and lead magnets

### Why capture contact details first

- A valuable free resource that matches what the visitor really needs turns cold and warm audiences into prospects by capturing name and email. [L0240, 2019]
- Research what the target audience genuinely wants, then build a free resource (eBook or report) that addresses those needs. Include company information and relevant offers alongside the value-driven content to introduce the business. [L0240, 2019]
- Older benchmark (2020): a new store with 100-150 qualified visitors per day performs acceptably. Five email subscribers from that traffic is below target and shows that email capture needs optimization. [L0245, 2020]
- Collect customer emails for follow-up, including abandoned cart recovery and bounce-back campaigns. Email follow-up significantly affects conversions. [L0249, 2022]
- A sign-up pop-up that offers a small discount for an email address can raise conversion and grow the list at the same time. Run it from the start of the store. [L0476, undated]
- Ask every visitor for an email address in exchange for something of value, such as a free report or video. Visitors unwilling to give an email address are unlikely to pay you later. Example: 1,000 daily visitors at a 30% opt-in rate produce about 300 interested leads. [L0523, 2015+]
- Few shoppers want another newsletter, but many will hand over an email address for something free. An opt-in bribe makes the trade worth it. [L0757, 2022]
- A new brand selling only on pre-order struggles to convert because it lacks trust and authenticity. Collecting sign-ups or notify-me requests is a softer way to capture demand. [L1016, 2026]
- Capture visitors' contact details and set up exit-intent overlays so visitors who leave can be remarketed to. [L1002, 2019]
- An exit-intent overlay captures visitor emails at the moment they leave and is a core tool for building the list early on. [L0963, 2022]
- Even with a small ad budget, use an exit-intent overlay to collect emails from visitors who are leaving. Captured emails feed the abandoned cart and promotion emails. [L0468, 2020]
- Combine a notification bar, an exit-intent overlay and email capture with an automated follow-up to turn first-time visitors into buyers sooner. Also set up abandoned cart emails. [L1088, 2020]
- Blog posts are a good place to collect email leads. Adding an email signup section to the blog posts template helps build the list. [L1265, 2020-26]
- For paid traffic, set the lead ad destination to a squeeze page that offers a free gift, a discount coupon or a popular product in exchange for an email opt-in. Sell offers to opted-in people over time by email. Enter the destination URL in the website URL field of the ad. [L0641, 2014]

### Where opt-ins go on the site

- Every content page should carry a content-based opt-in: blog posts, the blog index, the home page and collection pages. Use a sidebar opt-in, an inline opt-in below the content on blog posts and collection pages, and an exit-intent pop-up on all pages except product pages. [L0571, 2021-22]
- Offer opt-ins on every page of the site, not only in the footer or on the home page. Use different opt-ins for different parts of the site so visitors at different funnel stages get relevant offers and the message is not repetitive. [L0571, 2021-22]
- Footer-only opt-ins are seen by very few visitors, since only about 20 percent of visitors scroll to the bottom of a page. Scroll-mapping software can show this on most sites. [L0571, 2021-22]
- Running only one opt-in offer across the whole site is a common error. Visitors arrive at different stages of the buying journey, so one offer cannot match all of them. If an opt-in rarely gets sign-ups, it is usually the first thing to fix. [L0571, 2021-22]
- Blog layout: make the top third of each blog post a squeeze-style opt-in section where the only available action is entering an email address. Visitors from search, social or guest content see it first. This turns the blog into a conversion tool rather than only a content page. [L0523, 2015+]
- Place an inline opt-in in the middle of a blog post. An opt-in for a related downloadable guide on the same topic has a high opt-in rate, because the reader is already interested in that subject. [L0571, 2021-22]
- Place a related content opt-in below the author information at the end of the article. Readers who reach the bottom have just finished the article and are most interested in the topic. [L0571, 2021-22]
- In the Manhattan theme, the email signup section on blog posts can be given an accent color to make it stand out. The change is saved in the theme editor and is visible after refreshing the page. [L1265, 2020-26]
- Place an opt-in bribe in the website footer, offering a free item in exchange for an email address. [L0757, 2022]
- Replace the default Shopify newsletter opt-in in the footer or sidebar with a giveaway offer. Keep the same form, change the text to name a valuable prize, and state the entry rules. [L0923, 2015]
- Add an exit-intent overlay to the website, which appears as a visitor is about to leave, to capture emails or promote offers. [L0757, 2022]
- Place the consultation or bribe offer next to the opt-in form on the static site. [L0514, 2015]

Opt-in formats by page, as a reference:

| Opt-in type | Where it works | Display format |
|---|---|---|
| Content opt-in | Every content page: blog posts, blog index, home page, collection pages | Sidebar, inline below content, exit-intent on all pages except product pages |
| Discount offer | Product pages only, first purchase only | Pop-up after about 30 seconds, exit-intent pop-up |
| Quiz | Any page | Home, collection and product pages as pop-up; collection, blog and blog post pages as sidebar item |
| Survey | Any page | Same placements as quiz |
| Contest or giveaway | Any page | Pop-up, exit-intent, or static sidebar form |
| Sales event opt-in | Any page | Pop-up or exit-intent; collection, blog and blog post pages as sidebar item; shown only in the one or two weeks before the event |

### Offer and opt-in copy

#### The bribe

- A bribe is a free item that visitors already want, given in exchange for their email address. Email is low-friction to share, but visitors only give it when they trust the offer and expect it to be respected. [L0514, 2015]
- Match the bribe to the stage of the visitor. Someone who has not chosen a niche yet responds to a list of niche ideas. Someone further along responds to a narrow piece of the paid product. [L0514, 2015]
- Pattern for a converting bribe: offer one narrow, specific snippet of the paid product that gives an immediate, concrete benefit as a free download. It works because the visitor gets proof of value before paying anything. [L0514, 2015]
  - Formula: `[specific free piece] + [what it solves] + [quick result]`
  - Example 1: A free one-page checklist that shows how to fix your morning routine to cut afternoon fatigue within three days.
  - Example 2: A free 15-minute video on the three fastest ways to lower your monthly phone bill this week.
  - [L0514, 2015]
- Pattern for a bribe headline: `[number of tips] + [specific fast result] + [timeframe]`. Specific numbers and a time limit make the free offer feel concrete and quick to use. [L0514, 2015]
  - Example 1: Seven budget meal prep ideas that help you cut 150 dollars from your grocery bill in four weeks.
  - Example 2: Five quick desk stretches that ease neck pain in ten days.
  - [L0514, 2015]
- Physical bribes, such as a free product with a shipping charge, are more expensive but produce more valuable leads. People who pay for shipping give their full name, email and shipping address, which signals strong purchase intent. [L0514, 2015]
- Case: a company marketed to preppers, people who expect a disaster and buy survival supplies. It offered a credit-card-sized folding knife as a free bribe, and customers paid shipping. The bribe was expensive, but the leads were very valuable because they came with shipping details and a paid order. [L0514, 2015]
- A free consultation, such as a 10-minute video call on a specific topic, works as a bribe when you have expertise but do not want to create a digital or physical giveaway. It costs your time per lead, so it suits low volumes. [L0514, 2015]
- The opt-in form collects a name and email address. Pair it with a catchy, specific headline that names the free bribe. [L0514, 2015]
- Quizzes, surveys and giveaways are covered in their own section below.

#### Opt-in copy

- Make every opt-in specific and valuable by naming the exact item the subscriber will receive. Vague promises such as "our newsletter and discounts" attract almost no one, because the visitor cannot judge the value. (Quote removed; vague promises such as `our newsletter and discounts` attract almost no one.) [L0571, 2021-22]
- Each opt-in should solve one specific problem and promise one quick win, with one call to action. State the problem solved, the benefit and the time frame of delivery. [L0571, 2021-22]
- A lead magnet performs best when it solves one specific problem with a specific solution for a specific segment of the market. Name the segment in the opt-in copy so the right visitors recognize the offer as meant for them. [L0571, 2021-22]
- Formula: `[specific asset the visitor receives] + [the problem it solves] + [how and when it is delivered]`. Clear copy works because the visitor sees exactly what they get and can judge its value before entering an email address. [L0571, 2021-22]
  - Example 1: `Enter your email and get our one-page checklist for recovering abandoned carts, sent to your inbox today.`
  - Example 2: `Join the list and receive our 12-page guide to choosing the right running shoe width, delivered instantly.`
  - [L0571, 2021-22]
- Email signup headlines should match the brand voice, such as `Join Shaka Nation` or an equivalent branded call to action. Select a color scheme that matches your global theme to keep the design cohesive. [L0101, undated]
- Shorter content opt-ins often outperform long ones. A checklist, how-to guide or infographic that solves one problem is easier to accept than a 100-page ebook, because people's time is limited and large downloads put them off. [L0571, 2021-22]
- Content opt-in types include guides, PDF downloads of existing blog posts, infographics, ebooks, audio versions of content, checklists, cheat sheets, example collections, resource lists, inspiration files, recipe collections, calculators, spreadsheets, video courses, expert roundups and newsletters. Most can be reused from content already produced for promotion. [L0571, 2021-22]
  - Formula: take a piece of content, identify the problem it solves, and offer it in a different format as a download behind an email field. [L0571, 2021-22]
  - Example for an outdoor grill seller: `Get our top 10 ways to grill fish, with the recipes using our grill accessories, sent to your inbox.` [L0571, 2021-22]
  - Example for a homeware seller: `Download 50 living room designs that feature the products we sell.` [L0571, 2021-22]
- Sign-up success message: remove any line that tells the visitor to use the code to claim an offer, and state the code directly, for example with a line like `here is your discount code`. Keep the confirmation line about subscribing. [L0607, undated]

### Lead magnets and content upgrades

- Lead generation procedure:
  1. Choose distribution channels for your free resource: blog banner ads, community sponsorships, Facebook ads, or partnerships with industry influencers.
  2. Build the free resource (eBook or report) addressing your audience's stated needs.
  3. Create a landing page to capture email addresses.
  4. Set up email automation in Klaviyo to deliver the free resource and begin the follow-up sequence.
  5. Import all captured contacts directly into Klaviyo for ongoing campaigns and weekly offers.
  - [L0240, 2019]
- Buyer's guide PDF procedure:
  1. Build the guide in Canva, using its brochure templates as a starting point, or in a word processor.
  2. Add the text, and drag in images created in Canva.
  3. Save as PDF: in Canva, choose PDF when saving; in a word processor, use File > Download as PDF.
  - [L0360, 2024]
- Before a buyer's guide download, use an exit-intent overlay in Klaviyo that asks for an email before the visitor leaves. Set the overlay text to invite the visitor to enter an email for the buyer's guide. [L1281, 2022]
- Content upgrade template for a PDF version of a post:

```
Want a PDF version of this post? Enter your email below and we'll send you a copy you can save or print.
[Email field]
[Button: Download]
```

- Content upgrade template for an audio version:

```
[Button: Get the audio]
```

- A squeeze page is a simple opt-in page that asks for an email address in return for a free report or video. It removes ads, navigation tabs and every other distraction, so the visitor either subscribes or leaves. Its single goal is to convert visitors into subscribers. [L0523, 2015+]

### Discount types and offer rules

#### Fixed amount versus percentage

- A fixed-amount dollar discount tends to convert better than a percentage discount for sign-up offers, because shoppers can see the exact value they receive. This is the author's experience, not a measured result. [L0634, undated]
- A sign-up pop-up can use a fixed dollar amount instead of a percentage, such as `save $5 with your first order`, when a fixed amount feels like a clearer and bigger reward to the visitor. The matching discount code must be created in Shopify as a fixed amount with the same value, or the promise on the pop-up will not work at checkout. [L1297, 2023]
- Example from the lesson: a 5-dollar discount for an email address, with the headline changed to a question such as `Do you want 5 dollars off?` and a button reading `Sign up to get the deal`. The coupon is then created in Shopify Discounts as an Amount off order fixed amount of 5 dollars. [L0634, undated]
- Percentage offers used in practice: 10% for an email sign-up and 15% for an SMS sign-up. [L0418, 2025-26] [L1138, 2025-26]
- Sources give no measured comparison between fixed and percentage offers. Use the fixed amount where the reward is clearer that way, and make sure the Shopify code matches the promise. [L0634, undated] [L1297, 2023]

#### Channel-specific and first-purchase offers

- Use different discount offers by channel within one pop-up: 10% for email sign-ups and 15% for SMS sign-ups. Name the campaign so the offers are visible in the campaign name, which helps when comparing results later. [L1138, 2025-26]
- Discount opt-ins should be used only on product pages and only for the first purchase. Keep them time-limited, with windows such as 48 hours, 36 hours or one week, and do not make the window too long. Offer a meaningful to exceptional discount and deliver it by pop-up or exit-intent pop-up. [L0571, 2021-22]
- Example: a discount opt-in on a product page shows a 15% off next-order offer with an email field, a `Be the first to know about new products` line and a `No thanks` option. [L0571, 2021-22]
- Welcome offer only for new leads: filter for `Amount spent equals zero dollars` so the welcome offer goes only to new leads, not previous customers who already know the brand. [L0201, 2020-26]
- Example: a merch store set the pop-up to display after 60 seconds, offered discount code `save 10`, and automated welcome emails to new subscribers while automatically excluding customers with abandoned cart tags and prior purchase history. [L0201, 2020-26]
- An exit-intent overlay should offer something different from the header discount. Do not repeat the same percentage in the overlay, since visitors already see it and have no reason to enter their email. A vague wording such as `enter your email for a special discount` also works. [L1090, 2021]
- The Coupon Pop app for Shopify can show an expiring discount pop-up, such as 10 percent off, to collect email addresses. The same offer can stay in the store header, and the pop-up gives visitors a reason to leave their email instead of exiting. [L0923, 2015]
- Older rule (2020): an exit-intent overlay that offers a fixed discount or coupon in exchange for an email address is standard on large e-commerce sites. Giving a $50 coupon for signing up to a mailing list is legal. [L0967, 2020]
- Offer must be stated clearly in the headline: a pop-up with a clear offer converts better than a prettier pop-up without one. [L0418, 2025-26]

### Timing, triggers and frequency

- Never show a pop-up or any overlay on page load. Do not show an email pop-up immediately on page load. [L1213, 2023] [L0607, undated]
- Older rule (2021-22): do not show intrusive pop-ups or interstitials, including discount or newsletter pop-ups, within the first 30 seconds of a visit. [L1099, 2021-22]
- Triggering immediately is penalized by Google, and discount pop-ups should be shown only to visitors who show real interest in the offer. [L0571, 2021-22]
- Google can see page code loaded by pop-ups and interstitials that cover a significant part of the screen when it crawls a page, and may deprioritize such a page. Such overlays also reduce the chance that visitors stay. [L1099, 2021-22]
- An interstitial is any overlay that covers part of the page, including discount or newsletter pop-ups and intrusive chat widgets that open as large bubbles. Keep both kinds off the screen during the first 30 seconds of a visit. [L1099, 2021-22]
- Avoid showing the pop-up at the very start of the visit. [L0363, 2022+]
- Showing the pop-up immediately when a visitor arrives annoys them and makes them leave. Let them scroll or stay on the page first. [L0605, undated]
- A scroll-depth rule works as an engagement filter. A visitor who has read about half the page is more likely to want a discount or need reassurance, so the offer appears when interest is already shown. Pair the scroll rule with a delay of about 10 seconds and show the pop-up only when all selected rules are met. [L0607, undated]

| Source | Trigger | Condition | Use when | ids |

- Frequency rule: the pop-up shows a maximum of 2 times per visitor, with at least 1 hour between displays. Add it under Frequency > Add new rule. [L1138, 2025-26]

### Exit-intent overlays

- Exit-intent overlay: triggered when visitors show exit behavior, to capture visitors who would otherwise leave without engagement or conversion. [L0040, 2020-26]
- A bounced visit occurs when someone lands on the site but takes no action within a set time window. They may be distracted, on a call, or have the site open in a background tab. Interrupt them with a pop-up that exchanges a discount code for their email address, giving you another contact channel before they leave. [L0201, 2020-26]
- Configure the email pop-up to display after 60 seconds of visitor inactivity on the same page (no clicks or navigation) to catch them before they close the tab. [L0201, 2020-26]
- Page coverage conflicts between sources:
  - Alex-style setup: the email pop-up displays on all page types: homepage, blog posts, collection pages and product pages. Use this when the goal is maximum capture across the store. [L0201, 2020-26]
  - Older rule (2021-22): exit-intent on all pages except product pages. Use this when the product page should stay free of overlays. [L0571, 2021-22]
- Setup via the bounce visits module: use the traffic step (Bounce visits) to set up an exit-intent overlay that captures visitor email addresses before they leave the store. [L0208, 2024+]
- Setup procedure in one source's pop-up tool: go to Trigger > Add new trigger and choose `On exit-intent` (`When a visitor is about to leave your site`). Set Device to Desktop or Both devices. Exit-intent catches visitors who leave without converting and works on both mobile and desktop. [L1138, 2025-26]
- Use an exit-intent pop-up as the last chance to catch a visitor who is about to leave the site. Show it only when the visitor moves to leave. [L1213, 2023]
- An exit pop is a last-chance pop-up that appears when a visitor tries to leave without subscribing. It can include a special offer for subscribing. Since departing visitors rarely return, it is acceptable to be a little intrusive here. [L0523, 2015+]
- Do not run two exit-intent pop-ups at once, such as one from the theme and another from Klaviyo. Keep one and turn off the other. [L0855, 2023] (2023)
- Manual follow-up for opt-ins: if no exit-intent overlay is running, set one up. Manually email everyone who opts in (not automated messages) with the following text, which builds the relationship and recovers sales:

```
I'm the owner, noticed you on our site, happy to help with any questions.
```
