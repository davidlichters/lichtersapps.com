### Common product page mistakes

- Common product page errors include showing only one image, showing the sale price without the MSRP discount, stacking all content in one long page, and copying supplier features without explaining their benefit [L1130, 2020-26].
- Copying supplier features without a benefit leaves the buyer without a reason to buy. Each feature needs a benefit line that describes life with the product [L1130, 2020-26; L0409, 2021-22].
- Make sure the page does not become too crowded. Remove FAQ items that make the page feel crowded [L1331, 2025-26].
- Add FAQs to the product page with these steps [L1331, 2025-26]:
  1. Add a collapsible row block to the page.
  2. Add a collapsible content block inside it.
  3. Create one question and answer per collapsible item.
  4. Place the FAQ near the end of the page.
  5. Remove any FAQ items that make the page feel crowded.

### Product page and landing page structure

### Work order: offer first, then trust

- Converting a visitor into a buyer depends on two areas: the offer and the trust signals on the site. Work on both, starting with the offer, because visitors judge the page within seconds. Judge the product only after both are in place. [L1382, undated; L1436, undated]
- clarity, quick understanding of the product, a clear delivery of the core offer, and the marketing message matter most on a product page. Trust cues, bundles, and recommendations add value only once these basics work. [L1522, 2021-22]

### Hero and above the fold

- A visitor forms a first impression from the first image and the headline or product name, so both must be strong. [L1436, undated]
- The headline should be emotional and benefit-driven and address the visitor's current pain point. [L1436, undated]
- Supplement pages open with a hero headline that states a clear benefit, plus a discount banner (for example "Lowest price of the year" or "Up to 70% off until midnight"). [L1479, 2026]

### Section order

- Reference order from a top-selling supplement product page, top to bottom: [L1479, 2026]
  1. Hero headline with a clear benefit and a discount banner (for example "Lowest price of the year" or "Up to 70% off until midnight"). [L1479, 2026]
  2. Stock label such as "Low stock - selling fast". [L1479, 2026]
  3. Star rating with review count near the top (for example 5.0 with 3,749 reviews). [L1479, 2026]
  4. Price tiers for single, multi-pack, and subscription purchases. [L1479, 2026]
  5. Benefit bullets and a "Power through your day" style section. [L1479, 2026]
  6. "Backed by science" section with study and ingredient claims. [L1479, 2026]
  7. Supplement facts panel. [L1479, 2026]
  8. Customer photos and reviews. [L1479, 2026]
  9. How it works section. [L1479, 2026]
  10. FAQ. [L1479, 2026]
  11. Shipping information. [L1479, 2026]

### Mobile-first layout

- Most visitors look at the top of the page on mobile, so the hero must work on a phone screen. Use this sequence: offer, benefit, social proof, more benefits, add-to-cart, then a risk-free message. [L1363, 2023]
  1. Offer. [L1363, 2023]
  2. Benefit. [L1363, 2023]
  3. Social proof. [L1363, 2023]
  4. More benefits. [L1363, 2023]
  5. Add-to-cart. [L1363, 2023]
  6. Risk-free message. [L1363, 2023]

### Buy box content

- On a supplement product page, show the dosage per serving (for example 1300 mg per serving) and the supply length (for example a 30-day supply) close to the price and the star rating. Buyers can then judge value before they scroll down. [L1479, 2026]
- Showing the fit and shape of the item on the product page before checkout helps buyers decide with confidence. This supports the sale and reduces returns. [L1438, 2023+]

### Sticky add-to-cart

- A sticky add-to-cart button helps visitors who are ready to buy, including buyers who have already seen the full product description. It stays visible while the visitor scrolls, so the purchase is one tap away, and they can buy at any point without scrolling back through the description. [L1382, undated; L1436, undated]

### CTA placement and labels

- place a call-to-action button at the bottom of the product page and reconfirm the price there too. This helps mobile shoppers who scroll through the full description without scrolling back to the top. [L1522, 2021-22]

### Tabs, accordions, FAQs, and comparison charts

- A comparison table that separates the brand from competitors is useful once the product is known in the market. [L1363, 2023]
- Frequently asked questions address common buyer objections. [L1363, 2023]
- FAQ sits near the end of the supplement page, after the "How it works" section. [L1479, 2026]

### Templates and single-product pages

- Only remove sections of the template when needed. Keep every element in the top section (hero), keep at least one testimonial, the trust signals, the value adds, and the benefits somewhere on the page. Minimal designs are allowed once these are in place. [L1363, 2023]
- For a simple dropshipping product page, use a short intro paragraph at the top, show the product in use with a GIF near the top, and list five to six key benefits as bullet points. [L1380, 2022]
- Keep the homepage short and avoid extra content that slows the buying decision. [L1380, 2022]
