## Product page and landing page structure

### Where visitors land and what the page must do

- Most traffic lands directly on product pages rather than the homepage, so product page optimization deserves the most attention [L0894, 2020-26].
- Most paid traffic arrives on product pages through searches for a brand name, product name or SKU, so visitors already know what they want. Product pages must therefore work as complete sales pages on their own [L1130, 2020-26].
- The product page is a sales page. It must sell the product to a cold visitor who came from an ad and knows nothing about the store yet. If the page does not persuade, the visitor leaves without buying [L1346, 2020-26].
- Visitors who arrive from ads rarely read long text, so the default features and benefits must be clear enough that the visitor understands the product within seconds [L1346, 2020-26].
- Shoppers open several tabs and compare pages side by side. Product pages must stand out immediately with a clear title, price, delivery information and support options. A page that looks less convincing or less clear than a competitor's loses the sale [L0739, 2021-22].
- The goal of a product page is to remove every reason for the visitor to leave. Price, images, shipping, delivery timing, product details and answers to common questions should all be available in one place [L1098, 2019+].
- Place the manufacturer description, product specifications, customer reviews and a customer question-and-answer section on the product page, so visitors can make the buying decision without leaving [L1098, 2019+].
- A landing page has to deliver two things visitors look for: a clear offer and visible proof that the store can be trusted. Both should be visible early, because visitors decide quickly whether to stay [L0916, 2022+].
- Every element that does not help the buying decision adds friction and distracts the visitor. Remove or reduce anything that pulls attention away from the product after the click [L0916, 2022+].
- A landing page has to convey the offer informatively, compellingly and persuasively, and push the visitor toward one specific action. That action depends on the page's place in the funnel: buying, submitting an email, or downloading a lead magnet [L0919, undated].
- A clean, trustworthy design with fewer elements works better than a cluttered one. Product pages need optimized images, persuasive copy, urgency elements and trust badges to convert [L0491, 2026].
- The top of the page should give enough information to decide without overloading the visitor. Balance the amount of information in each section [L0709, 2017+].
- Put key information at the top: benefits, social proof and lower-risk elements such as guarantees. Put additional detail further down for visitors who want to research [L0709, 2017+].
- A common mistake is placing important product information far down the page, where visitors rarely scroll. Information should sit near the product image and the calls to action [L0709, 2017+].
- Each section should move the visitor forward by answering one question at a time: what is it, why should I believe it, what do I get, what if it does not work, and how do I buy [L0919, undated].
- The product page is built around the buying questions in order: what it is, how it works, what is inside, whether it is trustworthy, and what happens if it fails. Each block answers one of these questions with proof such as videos, reviews or a guarantee [L1188, 2026].

### Mobile-first layout

- Design mobile first. Create the mobile version of the page, then adapt it for desktop. Starting from desktop usually produces product pages where the add-to-cart button is buried on mobile [L0390, 2019].
- On mobile product pages where the add-to-cart button requires three to four scrolls before it appears, customers become frustrated and leave. Place the main call to action where it is visible without scrolling on mobile [L0390, 2019].
- Mobile share of traffic, two figures from two sources:
  - Over 70% of sessions typically happen on mobile, so optimize layout, navigation and above-the-fold copy for mobile first [L1016, 2026].
  - About 80-85% of customers buy on their phones, so the page must be optimized for mobile first. The first view on a smartphone is where the five key elements should appear [L0916, 2022+].
  - About 80-85% of visitors arrive on smartphones, so there is little time to convince them on the first screen. Put the most important trust element and the product image at the top [L1260, undated].
  - Use your own store's device data to decide which figure applies.
- On mobile, place as much as possible above the fold: product benefits, testimonials and trust signals, the offer, and how to buy. Balance this with enough white space so the page does not become too busy to read [L0755, undated].
- Five key elements for the mobile first view, in this order [L0916, 2022+]:
  1. Good-quality imagery, such as a lifestyle or user-generated image of the product in use.
  2. A bold headline or USP that states the product name and what the buyer gets.
  3. Social proof, such as a star rating line.
  4. The call-to-action button.
  5. Trust badges directly below the add-to-cart button.

  If the call-to-action or trust badges do not fit in the first view, place them in the first scroll [L0916, 2022+].
- Example of a social proof line: "4.8 out of 5 from 1,000+ happy customers," placed directly under the product name or headline in the first view. End it with a benefit phrase, such as "enjoying stress-free sleep," so the trust signal also carries an emotional message [L0916, 2022+].
- Reduce the size of the navigation bar and trim above-the-fold product copy to two or three lines. Move secondary points into bullet points so the critical buying information fits above the fold [L1016, 2026].
- On mobile, do not stack all features and testimonials vertically, because this creates a very long page. Use side-scrolling carousels for feature sets and testimonials so visitors can skip sections they do not need [L0918, 2023].
- Use accordions or drop-down menus for detailed features and ingredient lists, so visitors expand only the topics they care about [L0918, 2023].

### Above the fold: hero and buy box

- Place the key fundamentals above the fold: product title, a short two- to three-sentence description, variant selection, the add-to-cart button, and payment terms such as installment options [L1016, 2026].
- Use large, motion-rich hero imagery on the left side of the product page to create energy and immerse the visitor from the first screen [L1016, 2026].
- Show the review count and the number of answered customer questions near the top of the page. These signals reassure buyers early [L1098, 2019+].
- Show the price with the amount saved, the shipping cost (free shipping where offered), the estimated delivery date and the stock status near the top of the page. Buyers need these facts to decide quickly [L1098, 2019+].
- Show made-to-order status directly under the add-to-cart button, for example "product is custom made, estimated manufacturing and delivery time is four to six weeks," so buyers know the lead time before they purchase [L0806, 2024].
- Trust elements for the product page: star reviews (for example 4.8 out of 5), quoted testimonials, a secure checkout badge directly below the call-to-action button, and a benefit-driven semi-headline directly below the product name that states the problem or outcome [L1259, undated; L1260, undated].
- Product page structure: a headline as one benefit-driven line, followed by at most two lines of paragraph text, then an image or GIF. Long educational paragraphs are justified only for complex products where the target buyer needs a detailed explanation [L1259, undated].
- Use a unique custom product title that is not the generic supplier name [L1207, undated; L1209, undated; L1210, undated].
- Show the pack size or weight, for example "2.65 oz (75 g) net weight," near the product name and image [L0409, 2021-22].
- For a consumable product, show the yield or serving size in plain units, for example how many cups or servings one pack makes and how much one serving needs. This lets buyers judge cost per use and signals convenience. Tie the figure to the packaging so the claim can be checked [L0918, 2023].
- When selling to several countries, show both size systems next to each other, for example "EU 41 / UK 5.5," so buyers recognise their size immediately. Check the numbers against real conversion in each market [L1219, undated].
- Show portability, battery life, size and ease of setup on the page. These answer the practical objections about whether a product will be bulky, how long it will last, or where it can be used [L0919, undated].
- Use social cues, such as the most common choices, to pre-fill configurable products. Shoppers who start from a blank selection tend to hesitate and drop off [L1225, 2025].
- Headlines and copy should be plain and direct. Most visitors leave when they are confused about what they are looking at, so the hero headline must be obvious rather than clever [L0387, 2022].
- Put the product highlights at the top of the product section, the offer near the top right under the reviews, then the quantity or variant selector below the offer, and the add-to-cart button directly below that [L1225, 2025].
- Keep the top of the product description short, with about three bullet points, so the add-to-cart button stays visible above the fold. Move longer details into lower sections such as benefits, reviews and FAQs [L1331, 2025-26].

### Buy box content and order

- Recommended buy box order, from top to bottom [L1225, 2025]:
  1. Show product highlights at the top of the product section.
  2. Place the offer near the top, right under the reviews.
  3. Place the quantity or variant selector below the offer.
  4. Place the add-to-cart button directly below that.
- Product page layout in this order [L0409, 2021-22]:
  1. Product name, main image and pack size or weight, for example 2.65 oz (75 g) net weight.
  2. Variant choices such as scent, type or sensitive-skin version, shown as clearly selectable options.
  3. Price, with the one-time price and the subscribe-and-save price shown side by side.
  4. A large add-to-cart button in the single accent colour.
  5. Short benefit bullets directly under the button, for example "aluminum free, effective odor protection, goes on smooth, vegan ingredients."
  6. A headline that names the common problem with this product type and promises the solution.
  7. A section on the key ingredients or mechanism and what each one does for the customer.
  8. A feature list in which each feature is followed by its benefit.
  9. The review count, the star rating and a few review quotes, with a link to see more reviews.
  10. Related products at the bottom.

  [L0409, 2021-22]
- Specifications should be listed on the product page as part of the logic element that confirms the purchase. They are not a unique element of the copy, so they can be pasted in from the product data [L0396, 2021-22].
- Give the product page all options: variants, add-ons, removable items and multiple image angles. Offer two actions: add to cart to keep shopping, and buy now to go straight to checkout [L1098, 2019+].
- Show the ingredients or components of each product variant on the product page, because variants can differ in formula. A visual breakdown next to the variant selector helps buyers choose the right one [L0524, 2023].
- When one product comes in several intensity levels, such as four grit levels, list each level clearly and name it by the customer type it suits. This lets each customer find a matching option without guessing [L0524, 2023].
- If several products are similar but differ in tier, such as basic, pro and max versions, buyers usually choose one version rather than buying them together. Structure the page so each product can be selected directly and treat products as separate choices rather than a bundle [L0918, 2023].
- Show the comparison price next to the sale price with a simple discount label. State the discount as an exact percentage or plain amount [L1207, undated; L1209, undated].
- Under the add-to-cart button of a reseller page, a compact image-based block can explain the store's own USP, such as free shipping, a phone line, easy returns and a price-match promise. Keep it visually simple so it does not compete with the buy button [L0409, 2021-22].
- Add a USP block to each product page that shows why a buyer should choose you, such as shipping, returns or price promises. Visitors then see the reasons on the product itself, at the point where they decide to buy [L1145, 2021-22].
- USP icons can sit in the pre-footer or on product pages. Placing them near the buying decision lets buyers see the reasons to purchase where they decide [L1045, 2020-26].
- Short USP lines for skimmers, such as suitability for all users or no experience required, go directly beneath the headline and price [L1210, undated].
- Recommended sibling products on a conversion-focused product page should be removed or moved below the add-to-cart button. Upsells can come later when average order value is the goal [L1225, 2025].

### Buy button: label, placement and checkout route

- Make add-to-cart buttons large and repeat them, with one near the top and one lower on the page. A sticky button can also keep the cart reachable while the visitor scrolls [L0356, 2019].
- Test the wording of the purchase button, such as "Add to cart," "Buy now" or "Shop now," because buyers respond differently to each. Keep the main purchase button directly under the product title and images, where it is the first thing visitors see [L0352, 2022].
- Use "Add to cart" as the product page button label instead of "Order now" for dropshipping stores [L0665, 2022].
- Use a specific call to action tied to the product outcome, such as "remove dents now," rather than a generic "shop" or "buy" label. The call to action should link directly to the product page [L1225, 2025].
- Change the button text from "Add to Cart" to "Get My [Product] Now" and direct customers to checkout, rather than adding to cart, to reduce friction during purchase [L0321, undated].
- A direct payment button shortens the path to purchase for visitors who want to buy immediately with a provider they already trust. Visitors who are not ready to buy can still use Add to Cart and keep browsing [L0339, undated].
- For a single-product store, send the main call to action directly to checkout, with wording such as "Add to my bag" or "I want it now," instead of the add-to-cart page. This removes a step where visitors leave [L1259, undated; L1260, undated].
- Remove the accelerated checkout button, such as a buy-with-shop option, from product pages. In the lesson's tests it lowered conversion rates [L1225, 2025]. This conflicts with the direct buy options above ([L0339, undated; L1098, 2019+]). Use the direct buy option or the accelerated button only where your own test shows a gain.
- When a product page offers Buy It Now next to Add to Cart, both buttons should use the single accent colour, or Buy It Now should use a plainer style, so visitors see one clear group of purchase actions. Never let other bright coloured elements sit around either button [L0409, 2021-22].
- The call to action must be explicit and visible, especially in the hero section, because some visitors are already ready to buy and only need a clear button such as "Add to Cart." Do not assume visitors will work out the next step [L0919, undated].
- A call to action must be present on the page, because visitors who see value may still do nothing unless they are asked to act [L1363, 2023].
- Every product description needs a clear call to action at the bottom, such as "Scroll back up and click add to cart" or "Buy today," because many visitors read to the bottom and leave if nothing tells them what to do [L1293, 2014].
- The speaker estimated that up to 99% of visitors leave a product page without buying when the only call to action is the add-to-cart button at the top [L1293, 2014].
- Repeat the primary call to action at the bottom of the homepage and product page, so a visitor who scrolled the whole page has a clear next step [L0409, 2021-22].

### Sticky add-to-cart bar

- Sticky add-to-cart bar setup [L0635, undated]:
  1. Install a sticky add-to-cart app.
  2. Open the sticky bar settings.
  3. Set the checkout button to "Add to cart" or "Buy now."
  4. Configure the desktop and mobile versions separately.
  5. Keep the product name, variants, price and quantity; the compare-at price can be removed.
  6. Show the bar after 15% scroll on desktop and on mobile, so it does not appear at the top, where the main add-to-cart button already sits.
- A sticky add-to-cart bar increases the add-to-cart rate, because shoppers who are ready to buy while scrolling can act without scrolling back up. Show it only after about 15% of the page has been scrolled [L0635, undated].
- Use a sticky add-to-cart button that stays visible while scrolling. Place it unobtrusively so it does not cover the page content [L1016, 2026].
- Keep the sticky add-to-cart bar that stays visible while scrolling on mobile, because it helps conversion [L1225, 2025].

### Product page sections below the buy box

- Build the sections below the buy box in this order [L1331, 2025-26]:
  1. Add an image-with-text section above the other sections for the first key benefit.
  2. Write a heading, a short text block and a related image for each benefit.
  3. Duplicate the section for each further benefit and keep the same image size.
  4. Add an icon bar for quick features.
  5. Add reviews.
  6. Add a collapsible FAQ block near the end of the page.
- Pair each benefit with an image or short video that shows the feature, so each claim has visual proof. This listicle-style layout builds trust better than copy alone [L1331, 2025-26].
- Include an explicit what-is-included list and a specifications section. For example: one device, six massage attachments, one rechargeable lithium-ion battery and one charger, so buyers can see the full contents at a glance [L1016, 2026].
- Keep a short marketing message section separate from the detailed description, so visitors can scan the message first and read details later. Below the description, place reviews, shipping details and returns information [L0409, 2021-22].
- Product page layout for an ingredient-led or feature-led product, in this order [L1194, undated]:
  1. Benefits of using the product.
  2. How it works.
  3. How to use it.
  4. Who it is for.
  5. FAQ.
  6. Testimonials.
  7. Guarantee, if one is offered.
- Stacking content on a single-product page in this order keeps visitors on the page until they reach the buy button [L1013, 2026]:
  1. A short how-it-works block.
  2. A what's-inside list.
  3. A satisfaction guarantee.
  4. A see-it-in-action video.
  5. A customer reviews block, with reviewer names labelled "Verified Customer."
- Layout for a product with several ingredients or features [L0918, 2023]:
  1. List every feature or ingredient of the product.
  2. Write a one-line plain description of each.
  3. Add a credible quality reference for each, such as where it is sourced or which standard it meets.
  4. State how it works.
  5. State the resulting benefit for the customer.
  6. Place customer reviews below the feature list as social proof.

  Use accordion menus for the detailed list so visitors can expand only the topics they care about [L0918, 2023].
- Do not put buying guides or comparison charts on the product page. Keep them in a separate resource section [L0894, 2020-26]. This conflicts with the comparison-chart advice below ([L1076, 2019; L1330, 2025-26]). Use the separate resource section for guides; use a product-page chart only where a higher-end version is the logical next choice.
- Remove frequently-bought-together sections, unrelated product recommendations and extra navigation elements, so visitors stay focused on the main product [L1259, undated; L1260, undated].
- Upsell suggestions such as "you may also like" below the main product can raise average order value, but they may divert attention from a single-product page, which tends to convert better [L0693, 2024].
- Avoid "you may also like" or other unrelated product sections on a landing page, because they pull visitors away from the offer. If a related product adds value, include it only inside the bundle [L0916, 2022+].

### Descriptions: length by product complexity

| Product type | Description length | Where it goes | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple product | Short text and a clear image | Product page | [L0409, 2021-22] |
| High-ticket or complex product with long technical description | Long text, but not as a column beside the buy box | Full-width tabbed section across the bottom, usually done with an app | [L0370, 2021-22] |
| Short description on a conversion-focused page | About three bullet points above the add-to-cart button | Top of product description, with details lower on the page | [L1331, 2025-26] |
| Trimmed above-the-fold copy | Two or three lines, with secondary points as bullets | Above the fold | [L1016, 2026] |
| Above-the-fold description | Two to three sentences | Top of the product page | [L1016, 2026] |
| Headline with paragraph | One benefit line plus at most two lines of paragraph | Product page | [L1259, undated] |

- Simple products need only short text and a clear image, while complex or expensive products can use more text. The same clean layout works for both, and the number of elements should never exceed what the buyer needs [L0409, 2021-22].
- The depth of the description should match the product's complexity. A simple product needs only a short description. A complex product, such as a solar generator, needs detailed features, benefits and specifications [L0370, 2021-22].
- Long educational paragraphs are justified only for complex products where the target buyer needs a detailed explanation [L1259, undated].
