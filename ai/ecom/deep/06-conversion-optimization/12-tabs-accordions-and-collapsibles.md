### Tabs, accordions and collapsibles

- Set up tabbed product pages with three tabs in this order [L1130, 2020-26]:
  1. Product Description, which holds the item description.
  2. Shipping Policy.
  3. Price Match Policy.

  Visitors start on the description and can check shipping and price match without leaving the page.
- Placing description, shipping policy and price match in one long column wastes space and makes the page endless. Tabs keep the page short and let visitors reach the information they need quickly [L1130, 2020-26].
- Product pages can have as many extra tabs as needed to help the buyer, such as care instructions in addition to description, shipping and price match [L0667, 2025].
- Answer shipping and returns questions through tabs. Every time a shopper has to leave the page to find this information, the chance that they come back and buy drops. Product descriptions, images and videos answer product questions, while tabs answer delivery and return questions [L1300, 2021-22].
- Give tabs extra padding on each side and inside the tab content, so they are bigger, easier to tap and less cramped against the page edges. Check the result in the live preview before saving [L1300, 2021-22].
- Tabs for description, shipping, return policy and price guarantee that do not work are a major conversion problem, because visitors cannot find the information they need to buy [L0409, 2021-22].
- Use accordions or drop-down menus for detailed features and ingredient lists, so visitors can expand only the topics they care about. This avoids cluttering the page with text that puts off some visitors [L0918, 2023].
- Auto-opening a product feature section on page load draws attention to key product details that the shopper might otherwise miss. A fake click simulates a recorded interaction, such as clicking, typing or scrolling, so the change happens without shopper action [L0389, 2025-26].
- Auto-expanding product details sections on page load for first-time visitors only increases conversion rate by +2.1% [L0197, 2025-26].
- First-time visitors lack brand familiarity and need to understand all product benefits before deciding to purchase. Returning customers have already reviewed product details and built brand trust, so they do not benefit from expanded details shown automatically [L0197, 2025-26].
- Do not delete supplier-provided links from product descriptions without replacing them. Remove external links and recreate the warranty information directly on your product page using collapsible sections [L0253, 2024].

### Out-of-stock product pages

- Showing targeted product alternatives immediately below the add-to-cart button on out-of-stock product pages generates a +9.5% conversion rate lift [L0197, 2025-26].
- Shoppers who encounter out-of-stock products assume they will not find a suitable alternative and abandon the store. Prominent in-stock alternatives prevent this abandonment by redirecting purchase intent [L0197, 2025-26].

### Trust, reviews, guarantees and FAQs

- Include an FAQ section if the product solves a problem, and display a risk-free or 30-day money-back guarantee section [L1260, undated].
- Build the FAQ from the questions customers ask most, such as how soon results appear, how to take the product, the daily dose, what the product is made of and whether it is third-party tested. Place the FAQ below the product description, so buyers find answers before deciding [L0600, 2026].
- Answer common buyer questions, such as how the product works or how heavy it is, in a frequently asked questions section. This reduces doubt and helps the shopper decide [L1207, undated].
- Answer common buyer questions on the page in an FAQ section. Each answered question removes a doubt and gives the buyer more peace of mind before buying [L1209, undated].
- An FAQ section should answer the questions buyers typically have about how a product works, its size, weight or quality. Answering these on the page provides peace of mind at the point of decision [L1210, undated].
- Competitor stores use a timeline section to show what buyers should expect over time. Adapt the rows to the product's real results [L0600, 2026].

| Period | What the timeline shows |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | An early, noticeable first sign, for example less loss or less build-up in daily use |
| Week 2 to 4 | A stronger, steadier change, for example more normal routine results |
| Day 30 to 90 | The full result the product is designed to deliver, for example visible improvement |

- Show the rating and customer count, for example "4.8 out of five from a thousand happy customers" [L1207, undated].
- Testimonials worded as "customers like you," with a rating and review count [L1209, undated].
- Address the buyer as part of a like-minded group, paired with a satisfaction count [L1210, undated].
- Show happy customers as social proof, including customer testimonials and a star rating with the number of happy customers [L1210, undated; L1209, undated].
- Customer reviews placed near the end of the page, in detail [L1210, undated; L1209, undated].
- Place customer reviews below the feature list as social proof [L0918, 2023].
- Reviews and social proof section ideally with video testimonials. Image-based testimonials are more effective than text [L0321, undated].
- Most visitors will scroll past nothing: essential content such as reviews must not sit only at the end of the page. According to heatmap figures cited in the example, about 10% of product page visitors reach the bottom, and a well-performing page reaches about 20% [L0409, 2021-22].

Its description opened with the usual complaints about natural deodorants (they often fail to hide odor, go on chunky, and the market dislikes aluminum), then explained the ingredients' benefits and cited over 10,000 five-star reviews [L0409, 2021-22].
- In the desire section, add testimonials and reviews so visitors see that other buyers have the same experience. Also add a 30-day money-back guarantee to increase trust [L0916, 2022+].
- A comparison section shows how the store differs from competitors. An FAQ section clears the small doubts a buyer still has before paying. Reviews and a 30-day money-back guarantee serve as the final safeguards for a visitor who is not yet fully convinced [L0916, 2022+].
- Use a template with a proven layout logic, then replace copy and imagery with brand-specific content. The layout often follows psychological principles, so keep the structure and rewrite the content [L0918, 2023].

- If the product is complicated, or its use is not clear from the image, add a step-by-step usage section after the first or second section, such as Step 1, Step 2, Step 3. This section makes the purchase easier to trust [L0916, 2022+].

### Comparison charts

- Add comparison charts on product pages where a higher-end version is a logical next choice, showing features such as capacity or size side by side. Comparison charts can be built with Shopify HTML, a free app from the App Store, or a freelancer [L1076, 2019].
- A self-made comparison chart takes about 1 to 3 hours and can be made free inside Shopify or with a free app [L1076, 2019].
- A comparison table helps visitors compare products side by side, which can support purchase decisions on product pages and on the home page [L1330, 2025-26].
- A comparison chart can highlight the features where a competitor is weaker, or better terms such as a longer money-back guarantee, instead of comparing against the most famous brands. Comparing to a traditional product or an older version of your own product is also acceptable and less aggressive [L0387, 2022].
- Brand comparison with three to five unique benefit points, placed as a comparison section against competitors or an older product version [L0321, undated; L0387, 2022].
- Frame the product against competitors on the points where it wins [L1207, undated].
- Objection handling: address cheap copies and competitors before the buyer raises them [L1210, undated].

### Landing page structure: AIDA and section order

- Structure the landing page in AIDA order [L0916, 2022+]:
  1. Attention: show a bold, clearly visible headline (USP, bold claim or benefit line) and a clear product image in the first view.
  2. Interest: add benefit-driven points and emotional experience copy that describes life with the product.
  3. Desire: add testimonials, reviews and a 30-day money-back guarantee.
  4. Action: add a call-to-action button after every two to three sections and one before the end of the page.
- The attention phase has to work within the first 2 to 5 seconds of a visitor landing on the page. Avoid large blocks of text in the first view, because they cause visitors to leave [L0916, 2022+].
- AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action) is the structure used for landing page copy. In the attention part, the first screen should show the product name with its main USP, a testimonial or star rating, and payment trust logos such as PayPal and Visa [L1218, undated].
- The first screen of a landing page should show the product name, a clear USP, a star rating or testimonial, and payment trust logos before the visitor has to scroll [L1218, undated].
- Place a call to action button after every two or three sections and once right before the end of the page. Visitors should not need to scroll up and down to find the buy button [L0916, 2022+].
- Landing page guide: the visitor should be led through four questions in order: what is this, why should I pay attention, should I believe you, and is it a good deal at this price. Structure the page to answer these in sequence while the visitor scrolls [L0387, 2022].
- Section order for a conversion-focused landing page [L0387, 2022]:
  1. Hero bar at the top: a headline that states clearly what the product is (with authority), the offer (price, discount), a reviews element, and a Shop Now button.
  2. Why bar: three short points with icons and big, simple words that explain the value (for example safety, design, value), with small details below for readers who want more.
  3. Brag bar: credibility proof such as customer reviews, press mentions or a counter-example.
  4. Shop section: product details with description, price, add-to-cart button, clean pictures and main features in bold.
  5. Reviews section, ideally with video testimonials.
  6. How-it-works section: step-by-step explanation combined with a picture or GIF of the product in use. If not applicable, use an icon-based value proposition section instead.
  7. Brag bar 2 or comparison chart against competitors or an older product version.
  8. Shop section 2: a repeat of the shop section, so the add-to-cart button is reachable without scrolling far.
  9. Footer.
- Section order for a product landing page, top to bottom [L1225, 2025]:
  1. Header.
  2. Brag bar.
  3. User-generated content.
  4. Product box.
  5. Second brag bar.
  6. Comparison chart.
  7. Description.
  8. Footer.
- Build order for a landing page for a store with similar products [L0918, 2023]:
  1. Create a main hero with the big value proposition and an offer or call to action.
  2. Add trust signals immediately, such as a review rating or testimonial.
  3. Add authority and social proof, such as press mentions, expert endorsements or customer counts.
  4. Add striking product visuals with benefit overlays on the images.
  5. Add risk-lowering elements, such as a free trial, easy cancellation or guarantees.
  6. Add benefit-focused copy and objection handling.
  7. Add a features list with each feature translated into a customer benefit.
  8. Add reviews and testimonials.
  9. Repeat calls to action further down the page.
- A complete landing page sequence [L0918, 2023]: a big benefit call-out, trust signals, striking visuals, risk reduction, the offer and a call to action at the top; then benefit-focused copy and objection handling; then product features and social proof; then calls to action further down. The page should build trust and reduce risk while showing the desired outcome.
- The offer and call to action should appear at the top of the landing page. Further calls to action should be placed lower, after the reader has absorbed the benefits and trust content, because some visitors need that persuasion before buying [L0918, 2023].
- A strong landing page surfaces the desired outcome, adds social proof and authority, lowers the risk for the customer (for example with money-back guarantees), offers a clear deal and call to action, and then shows the collection [L1315, 2023].
- A landing page should convey the desired outcome, not only the product: keep the page simple and focused on the pitch and the offer, so visitors do not have to move around [L1195, 2026].
- A dedicated features section is optional. Simpler landing pages can skip it and fold feature-to-benefit explanations into the benefit bullets. More complex product brands may use a separate features section that states each feature and its benefit [L0917, undated].
- Use a template with proven layout logic, then replace copy and imagery with brand-specific content. The layout often follows psychological principles, so keep the structure and rewrite the content [L0918, 2023].
- Different visitors respond to different benefits, especially when paid traffic comes from different Facebook interest groups. A landing page should carry several benefit lines and several call-to-action points, so each type of visitor finds a reason to buy somewhere on the page [L0916, 2022+].
- Six core elements of a strong sales page: a hero section, persuasive copy, trust building, objection handling, an irresistible offer, and removal of anxiety and risk. Design each element deliberately rather than leaving it to chance [L0919, undated].
- A single-product sales page, working from top to bottom [L0919, undated]: a hero section with a benefit headline and social proof, a discount or offer, benefit-driven copy that surfaces the problem, features that prove the benefits, a comparison table, guarantees and risk reversal, FAQs, then a final block of customer reviews.
- Show the desired outcome and make the offer clear in one pass. A strong landing page surfaces the desired outcome first [L1315, 2023].
- Products with several similar offers: route the visitor to each product directly and avoid bundling unrelated items on the landing page [L0918, 2023; L0916, 2022+].
- A landing page must answer what the buyer will get in the first view. Show the offer clearly and keep the product name, benefit and call to action as the first elements seen [L0916, 2022+; L0919, undated].
- The hero section sits above the fold and must communicate the value proposition, striking visuals, early trust signals, the offer and a clear call to action on the first screen. Visitors should be able to scan it quickly and decide whether to buy [L0919, undated].

### Hero section build

- Six hero elements for a high-converting landing page: a clear value proposition, striking visuals, trust signals, risk reduction, an offer, and a call to action. Missing any one of them lowers the chance that a visitor with buying intent converts [L1363, 2023].
- A landing page hero needs a strong headline built around one big benefit claim, striking visuals, trust signals such as social proof, risk or loss reduction, an offer and a call to action. Copy is primarily the big benefit plus benefit bullet points placed directly beneath it [L0917, undated].
- Hero section build order used for a top-converting page [L1363, 2023]:
  1. Title that includes the free gift or offer.
  2. Benefit callout stating the desired outcome in plain language.
  3. Review badge (social proof).
  4. Offer line, for example 40% off plus free gift.
  5. Short testimonial.
  6. Trust line with free shipping and a 30-day money-back guarantee.
  7. Three benefit bullets written as what the product does for the customer, not features.
  8. Add-to-cart call to action.
  9. Secure checkout trust line and a risk-free note (30 days).
- The first screen should show the product name, a clear USP, a star rating or testimonial and payment trust logos before the visitor scrolls [L1218, undated].
- Copy is primarily the big benefit plus benefit bullets placed directly beneath it [L0917, undated].

### Landing page copy: case and benchmarks

- A conversion rate of 7% was achieved on the Shaper Loop template on TikTok. This is a tested benchmark after 3 days of traffic [L0321, undated].
- Starting point: the store's conversion rate was 1.85%. The owner rebuilt the landing page with the AIDA structure, added the five key first-view elements (imagery, bold headline, social proof, call to action and trust badges), replaced studio images with user-generated images, used a brand palette of black, white and one brand colour, removed distracting product sections, and added testimonials, a 30-day money-back guarantee and trust badges. After these changes the conversion rate rose to roughly 4.25%, and at that level daily ad spend of about 2,000 to 3,000 was possible. The lesson does not state the currency [L0916, 2022+].
- Trust elements on a product page can be built from the same set: star reviews, testimonials, a secure checkout badge and a benefit-driven subheadline [L1259, undated; L1260, undated].

### Single-product landing page templates

- Section order for a product landing page [L1210, undated]:
  1. Product title: use a unique custom product name instead of the supplier's generic product name, which can be less competitive in search.
  2. Headline: a bold benefit claim that states the desired outcome.
  3. Social proof: customer testimonials and a star rating shown with the number of happy customers.
  4. Price frame: show a comparison price next to the sale price and state the saving as a plain figure.
  5. Shorthand USPs: short benefit lines for skimmers, such as suitability for all users or no experience required.
  6. Trust and risk signals: payment and security badges, international delivery, secure payment, responsive customer service and easy returns.
  7. Peer-group line: address the buyer as part of a like-minded group, paired with a satisfaction count.
  8. Problem statement: a universal pain point, followed by the product as the fix, dramatized with a metaphor.
  9. Features as proof: each feature tied to the promised outcome.
  10. Three-step setup: usage or setup shown as three simple steps.
  11. Objection handling: address cheap copies and competitors before the buyer raises them.
  12. Guarantee: money-back guarantee with a clearly stated period.
  13. FAQ: answer common questions about how the product works, its size and its weight.
  14. Reviews: detailed customer reviews placed near the end of the page.
  15. Urgency and scarcity: cart timer, low-stock notice and time-limited offers.

  After checkout: a "today only" post-purchase upsell.

### Inverted product pages

- An inverted product page looks like a homepage but is built as a product page. It works for both warm and cold traffic. It lets you test products quickly without writing a separate advertorial or listicle for every angle [L0772, 2026].
