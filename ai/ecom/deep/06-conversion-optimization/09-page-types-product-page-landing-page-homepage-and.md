### Page types: product page, landing page, homepage and collections

#### Definitions and when each fits

- A product page is a short page with a specific product picture, description, price, and add-to-cart button. A landing page has many sections such as header, banner, bonuses, icons, features, and reviews. A hybrid combines both and reduces a step before checkout, but it is not always better. [L0387, 2022]
- Products that need explanation, such as health products (for example for back pain), often convert better with an advertorial landing page that explains how the product works before the product page, even though this adds a step. Impulse-buy products that sell on looks should have the shortest possible path to purchase. [L0387, 2022]
- The product page must do the selling through its product description, product images, and product copy. The site's call to action in e-commerce is primarily to buy the product. The offer is delivered and presented on the website, not in the ad. [L1507, 2021-22]
- A product page should receive visitors who are already solution aware or product aware, not problem aware or unaware ones. Visitors who do not yet know their solution should first be sent to content that educates them, which then links to the product page. [L0396, 2021-22]
- Traffic must match the page it lands on: a product page is only right for visitors close to buying, so top-of-funnel search terms should go to content or collection pages instead. High-ticket buyers rarely make impulse purchases and need a longer decision process, so expect a longer gap between click and sale for generic traffic and plan for retargeting. [L1456, 2021-22]
- A website converts only if the offer and page match what the buyer needs at that point; a product page should not be expected to educate buyers about the whole market. Where a generic search term has no good fit, the fix is often content that compares options and then links to the product page. [L1456, 2021-22]
- For direct response dropshipping the main focus is the product or sales page at the purchase stage, because it has to ask for the sale quickly. Funnels and retention pages come later as the business grows. [L0919, undated]
- Lead capture pages offer something of value, such as an ebook, white paper or webinar seat, in exchange for contact details. Long form sales pages suit higher-priced single products that need more explanation before purchase. [L0919, undated]
- Roughly 95% of the paid traffic that lands on a product landing page decides there whether to buy or leave, while only about 5-10% look at other products. The landing page is therefore the most important part of the funnel after the ad click. [L0916, 2022+]

#### Homepage versus product page

- Do not send paid traffic to the store homepage. Visitors cannot see the product right away or add it to the cart, which makes the buying path harder. Send them to a collection page such as a bestseller collection or directly to the product page. [L0562, undated]
- A product page can be a better landing page than the home page when most traffic goes to the product page. Test whether direct-to-product traffic converts better before building a separate landing page for each product. [L0918, 2023]

#### Digital products and collections

- Send traffic from a single digital product to a product page with bullet points, a screenshot or mockup, testimonials and instructions for use on tablet or phone. Send traffic for preset-type products to a collection page. [L1431, 2020+]
- Collection pages can serve as landing pages for new ad angles, such as a feature that customers care about, without building a separate landing page. The same pages can also be used for email segmentation and Google Ads campaigns. [L1389, 2025-26]

#### Variants and separate landing pages

- Individual listings for each variant work well when the variants look clearly different, for example vibrant colors that are easy to see. Send traffic to a collection page that shows these separate listings. [L1217, 2023+]
- A dedicated landing page works better when the product has emotional marketing angles, such as love or mother and father gifts, or targets a specific demographic. Use GIFs and images to add emotion to the copy. [L1217, 2023+]
- When product variants confuse shoppers or do not match what they expect, separate them into their own landing pages. This clarifies what is available and tends to raise conversion. [L1315, 2023]
- Case: A store that lacked currency settings and had confusing variants (one product page with mixed sizes or colors) had a conversion rate of 0.4% to 0.5%. After creating separate landing pages per variant group and setting up Shopify Payments with local currency checkout, the conversion rate rose to about 1.2%. A separate variant landing page helps when customers ask for options that are not available. [L1315, 2023]

#### Learning which page sends the most traffic

- Check each landing page's share of a brand's ads to see where its funnel really sends traffic. In the example, one product page received 125 ads (81.2%) and a second page received 8 ads (5.2%). [L0374, 2026]
- The Top Landing Pages list shows the share of a brand's ads linking to each page. In a drop shipping store the top landing page received about 3 percent of the traffic, while in established branded stores one landing page can receive around 30 percent. [L0892, 2026]
- A landing page that receives a large share of a brand's traffic is likely its best-performing page. Checking which page a competitor sends most traffic to is a way to find a proven lander. [L0892, 2026]

### Pre-sell pages: advertorials, listicles, editorials and presells

#### Gaps in competitor funnels

- If a competitor sends its paid traffic straight to product pages with no listicle, advertorial or pre-sell page, that gap is an opportunity. Build dedicated landing pages, advertorials or listicles that warm up cold visitors before they reach the product page. [L0374, 2026]
- When a competitor sends paid traffic straight to its product page, the gap can be an advertorial that agitates the problem further before presenting the product. Such a page can be a way to beat a direct-to-product competitor. [L0772, 2026]

#### Advertorials and editorial funnels

- An editorial funnel is a pre-sell page placed before the product page that mixes article-style content with advertising to turn cold visitors into warm prospects who are ready to buy. The reader gets useful information first and the product pitch comes last. [L0578, 2023+]
- An advertorial is an ad styled as an editorial article that does not ask for the sale right away. A pre-sale or advertorial page warms up cold traffic by educating about the problem and the product and building trust, then captures data such as an email so the audience can be retargeted. [L0919, undated]
- An editorial widens the reachable market because it answers objections and builds trust before any sales pitch, which converts people who would otherwise leave. It also reduces dependence on luck in ad targeting, since the page itself raises awareness of the problem and explains why the product is the best solution. [L0578, 2023+]
- Use the 70/30 rule: about 70% of the page provides information and story, and only the final 30% introduces the product and the buying option. The reader must read the whole problem and solution before the pitch, so they are warm when they click. [L0578, 2023+]
- Build the editorial on the AIDA sequence of attention, interest, desire and action, because each stage moves a cold reader one step closer to buying. Skipping a stage leaves the reader unconvinced at the point of the pitch. [L0578, 2023+]
- For the desire stage, handle objections by explaining why existing solutions on the market fail to work well and why the new approach is different. The product can be presented as a new method without naming the brand early on. [L0578, 2023+]
- Social proof in the desire stage should include a customer's photo and review, a results timeline (for example after one week and after two weeks), graphs or data, and a count of other customers who had good results. These elements make the claimed benefit believable. [L0578, 2023+]
- In the action stage, present the product in the final 30% of the page and end with a clear call to action that leads to the product page, such as "check current availability" or "choose your package". The earlier stages must already have built agreement that the problem is real. [L0578, 2023+]
- Putting the product image, price and buy button at the top of a page meant for cold traffic makes the visitor feel pushed, and the page does not build the trust needed to convert them. Lead with the problem and story instead. [L0578, 2023+]
- Pitching the product before the reader agrees on the problem wastes the editorial, because the reader has no reason yet to care about the solution. Always begin with the shared problem. [L0578, 2023+]
- A salesy tone in the first part of the editorial makes the reader leave, because the page stops feeling like useful content. Keep the sales language for the final part. [L0578, 2023+]
- Editorial funnels are an advanced step, so add one only after the current funnel already converts well at a good level. The editorial is then used to win the prospect group that product pages miss, not to fix a weak product page. [L0578, 2023+]

Case: A sleep-pain product store sent paid Facebook traffic to a story-style page instead of its product page. The headline promised a surprising solution to pain-filled sleep. The story then introduced a character who fits the ideal customer: someone who had a car accident years earlier and now has poor sleep and constant pain. The problem was agitated with photos, and the character then visited a doctor who recommended a foam pillow. The character tried it and her sleep improved over time, and the page showed other customers with similar results. The product was never pitched as the brand's own; the final button said "check current availability" and led to the product page. [L0578, 2023+]

#### Presell pages and third-party pre-frames

- Presell pages educate prospects before the sales page. A presell page is a longer article that provides background or explains a concept, such as what email marketing is and why it grows businesses. Affiliates use presell pages to warm up prospects before sending them to a partner's sales page. [L0523, 2015+]
- Articles placed on third-party sites serve as pre-frames for cold traffic from banner ads. Article types include a case study, a product explanation or a piece that frames the problem without mentioning you. Add the call to action as an inline link, a button at the bottom, a linked photo or a combination. [L0523, 2015+]
- News-style pre-frames borrow attention from current events. Linking your message to a timely story, such as an election or disaster, lifts attention while the story is hot, though the effect is short-lived. Designing a page to resemble a news page with a headline bar or sidebar can also boost credibility, but it must remain truthful. [L0523, 2015+]
- Blog posts are a pre-frame tool for almost any topic. A guest post on a high-traffic blog in your target market works better than your own blog, and the post is best published under the blog author's name. Pair it with a link to your offer and drive targeted traffic to the post. [L0523, 2015+]
- Pre-frame videos work well for testimonials and for educating viewers. Publish them on someone else's channel and place calls to action in the description or comments, as pre-roll or post-roll footage, or as annotations. The video should agitate a problem or teach a process that creates desire for your solution. [L0523, 2015+]
- Email pre-frames use solo ads or joint venture partners to endorse you to their list. The partner's endorsement borrows their credibility, and the call to action is a link in the email. In direct mail the same role is played by a lift letter from the list owner. [L0523, 2015+]

#### Educational content and the one-page versus many-pages choice

- Educational content such as guides and how-to articles helps customers choose the right product, and a site with strong guidance content turns visitors into buyers. Study content-rich stores that guide customers and adapt their approach to your niche. [L0487, 2018]
- ** Education-based marketing walks a prospect through the whole awareness pyramid, and this can be done on one long page, but it demands strong copywriting. Using several linked pages is usually easier, because each page needs less text. [L0481, 2021-22]
- A short quiz on the home page can ask a few questions about the visitor's situation and then recommend the right product or stage of the buying funnel. This helps undecided visitors who do not yet know which option fits them. [L1016, 2026]
- A Learn section with short decision guides, covering how to choose, how to understand the product and how to avoid costly mistakes, answers pre-purchase questions before the buyer leaves. Each guide should link to the matching product or kit, and the section should be finished before ads point to the site. [L1016, 2026]

#### Quizzes and free entry points

- Quizzes that give personalized results can serve as an entry point for a high-ticket or education-heavy product. In the example, the quiz ran on its own separate domain (neurotest.me), took about 20 to 25 minutes and ended with custom results and product recommendations. [L0334, 2017]
- Step: Free digital course as an entry point: build the funnel in ClickFunnels, with a short course that promises one clear result (in the example, eliminating procrastination in three days), three modules and one bonus module from a partner. End the course with product offers, and link the offer to a Shopify checkout by using direct add-to-cart, so fulfillment stays on one system; for high-ticket items, enter the fulfillment details manually instead. [L0334, 2017]
- Free trial entry points can be scaled only with your own fulfillment. Third-party logistics providers charge for each small task, which makes sample or trial packets expensive; a co-packer can assemble trial packets from bulk product, and the trial sign-up asks only for an email address. Test free-plus-shipping and free-only versions to see which converts better. [L0334, 2017]

#### Blog posts and content CTAs

- Blog posts that educate and then end without a next step waste the traffic they already earn. Add an on-topic call-to-action block or email capture so existing readers can continue into the funnel. [L1269, 2025-26]
- Choose a call-to-action based on the funnel stage of the article's reader, not because a product CTA seems expected. Readers of top-of-funnel articles have near-zero buying intent, so a high-priced product embed in an informational article will rarely convert. [L1269, 2025-26]
- Procedure: Map existing article content to funnel stages and CTAs:
  1. Ask the AI to classify each article by awareness stage, for example problem aware, solution aware or product aware, or by top, middle and bottom of funnel.
  2. Create a CTA for each stage, matching the reader's intent at that point.
  3. Map each stage to an email segment, so readers receive messages suited to where they are.
  4. Map each stage to product embeds or offers, placing products only where the reader is ready to consider them.

  [L1269, 2025-26]
- Example: A top-of-funnel article about a niche animal topic can carry an embedded signup for a topic newsletter, with the promise of regular practical tips on the subject. This captures interested readers instead of asking for a sale they are not ready to make. [L1269, 2025-26]
- On top blog posts, pairing an on-topic product block with an email capture works better than a generic store banner. The product should match the subject of the article, for example apparel for an ocean-themed article. [L1269, 2025-26]

### Funnel design and pre-frame structure

#### Funnel principles

- Revenue levels from six to eight figures depend mostly on whether you understand each phase of your funnel and can monetize each point along it. Products, traffic sources, copy, website design and launch methods matter, but they are not what separates the levels. Focus on engineering the path each customer takes. [L0523, 2015+]
- Problem-based marketing beats product-based marketing. Don't sell product features ("take this pill for focus"). Instead: (1) Top of funnel: make audience conscious a problem exists and share content about it, (2) Middle of funnel: show how life looks without the problem and how to overcome it, (3) Bottom of funnel: present your offer as the solution. Find problems via Google Trends, not products. [L0307, 2023+]
- Each stage of a funnel serves as the pre-frame for the next one. Use the seven-phase procedure below to structure the path:

  1. Determine traffic temperature (hot, warm or cold) and the awareness level of the prospect.
  2. Set up a pre-frame bridge matched to that temperature.
  3. Qualify subscribers with an opt-in offer that delivers something of value.
  4. Qualify buyers immediately after opt-in with a low-cost or free-plus-shipping offer.
  5. Identify hyperactive buyers through upsells and downsells on the following pages.
  6. Age and ascend the relationship over time, moving customers up the value ladder.
  7. Change the selling environment for high-ticket offers by moving to phone, direct mail or live events.

  [L0523, 2015+]
- There are 23 interchangeable funnel components, sorted into four stages: the pre-frame bridge, screening subscribers, screening buyers, and spotting hyperactive buyers. You can combine them in any arrangement to fit a given offer. Assemble the setup, run a test, change a single element, then test again. [L0523, 2015+]
- Send all paid traffic to a squeeze page with one goal: the visitor either gives an email address or leaves. Remove menus, ads and any other links so nothing distracts from the opt-in. Every paid click should become owned traffic. [L0523, 2015+]
- Track traffic and offers on the basis of the stage they are at: where a squeeze page captures an email, a pre-sell or advertorial page warms cold traffic toward a sale, and a product page closes a visitor who is already ready. [L0523, 2015+; L0919, undated]
- Ad-level funnels should match the path: a problem ad leads through educational content, a solution ad leads to a landing page, and retargeting leads straight to the offer. [L1019, 2026]

#### Pre-frames within the funnel

- The seven-phase procedure above treats each prospect's temperature as the first decision. [L0523, 2015+]
- Stage-matched copy and page choices reinforce the pre-frame: a visitor who arrives with a strong frame converts at a much higher rate. [L0523, 2015+]

#### Ad creative as a funnel stage

- If a Kalodata rip campaign is saturated or not working, test bottom-of-funnel image ads instead of pausing entirely. You may capture sales from an audience that is already solution-aware but fatigued from watching videos. Bottom-of-funnel is cheaper to test (quick image creation) than new video production. [L0316, 2026]
- Some stores build a separate product page per persona or angle, keeping the same layout and changing only the copy. Track each page separately to see which angle converts. [L1164, 2026]
- When one product is sold to several audiences, keep the landing page structure identical and change only the copy and headline for each angle. Then split-test the variants to see which angle converts best. [L0387, 2022]
- The landing page must match the marketing angle of the ad. With Page Fly, create one page theme per angle; with other themes, create separate product pages and see which converts best. [L1219, undated]

### Testing and scaling landing pages

#### Testing order

- Landing pages should be tested against each other after a first test, with more relevant variants built for specific audiences. Relevance between ad, audience and page is a key driver of performance. [L0918, 2023]
- Start with a standard original landing page. Build listicles, advertorials and whitelist pages only once daily revenue reaches roughly $500 to $1,000. [L1454, 2026]
- Below roughly $200 in daily revenue, do not spend time on listicles, advertorials or whitelist pages. Focus on getting first sales with the original landing page. [L1454, 2026]
- Do not build a pre-lander or pre-sales page until a single day has produced at least $500 in revenue. Before that threshold, pre-landers add work without enough sales data to justify them. [L1454, 2026]

#### Where the thresholds apply

The revenue thresholds from different units apply at different stages and do not conflict:

| Daily revenue | What to build |
|---|---|
| Below roughly $200 | Only the standard original landing page; no listicles, advertorials or whitelist pages |
| Roughly $500 to $1,000 | Listicles, advertorials and whitelist pages may be built |
| At least $500 in one day | A pre-lander or pre-sales page may be built |

[L1454, 2026]

- Plan up to three landing pages, one each for hot, warm and cold traffic, when several sources of different temperature are already running. [L0523, 2015+]
- An editorial funnel is an advanced step that comes after the current funnel converts well. [L0578, 2023+]

#### Test steps for a page

- Create at least two variants of a landing page and split-test them. Use the same page structure and change only the parts that differ between angles. [L0387, 2022]
- Test whether direct-to-product traffic converts better before building a separate landing page for each product. [L0918, 2023]
- For each ad set, record its awareness level and the page it lands on, then compare conversion by page. [L1019, 2026]

#### Further details

- Send cold traffic to the single simplified kit page rather than to a page with many options, because one clear problem and one clear solution convert best for people who have never heard of the brand. [L1016, 2026]
