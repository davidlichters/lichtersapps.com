## Designing bundles and quantity-break ladders

### Bundle types and definitions

- A bundle combines more than one product into one offer, such as "buy this and this item and get an extra 20% off." A bundle that contains several units of the same product is a quantity break, such as "buy one and get 20% off, buy two and get 30% off." [L0434, undated; L0435, undated]
- Bundle pricing sells two or more units for less than the sum of the single prices, so the customer gets an automatic saving and average order value (AOV) rises. The bundle price must still leave a margin that covers all costs. [L0436, undated]
- Quantity-break offers raise AOV because customers who buy more get a bigger discount, and the store earns more per order. [L0635, undated]
- Automatic quantity discounts raise both AOV and conversion rate because the total looks lower for multiple units. A short urgency line such as "only 5 left in stock" can strengthen the effect. [L0773, 2023]
- Bulk pricing or a discounted second unit raises AOV. Buyers who want more of the same product are easier to convert than new buyers. [L0378, 2019+]
- Bundles raise AOV because a customer who buys two items plus a free third spends more per order than someone buying one item. Use bundles to make the store more profitable per visitor, not only to win the first sale. [L0562, undated]
- Bundles and volume discounts increase the number of items per order. A free-gift option can reduce the perceived price without a full discount. Test which offer type fits the margin of your products. [L0476, undated]

### Bundle types in use

#### Quantity breaks and buy-X-get-Y

- **Buy two, get a percentage off the second unit or the whole order:** buy two and get an extra 30% off raises AOV and margin per order, and suits customers who want more units of the same item. [L0434, undated; L0435, undated]
- **Buy one get one free (BOGO):** strong with customers, because people respond to a perceived free item. [L0440, 2023+]
- **Buy two, get the third free:** a typical bundle offer; choose the version by testing which sells best while still leaving a good margin. [L0562, undated]
- **Buy two, get the third free, for products customers already buy in stores:** buy two get a third free raises order size and margin; buy two with free shipping also raises both. [L1176, 2023]
- **Buy any two, get the third 50% off:** encourages multiple items per order while keeping per-unit profitability. [L0232, 2026]
- **Buy-X-get-Y offers:** customers buy more items to qualify for the free item. They work best when the product is logically bought in multiples, such as home decor. [L0650, undated]
- **Tiered BOGO-style ladders:** Buy 1 Get 1 Free, Buy 2 Get 1 Free, Buy 3 Get 2 Free, with the savings amount shown under each tier. Keep the visual layout simple so customers are not overwhelmed. [L0232, 2026]
- **Common offers for low-priced products:** buy two get one free and buy three get two free. A store running several of these is usually testing which quantity customers accept. [L1172, 2023+]
- **Buy two, buy three or buy four and get one free:** works well for small, cheap products. Test several and keep the one that suits the margin. [L1171, 2024+]
- **Buy-two offer with a second item at a percentage off:** a gifting variant, used with free US shipping. [L1225, 2025]
- **Buy one at 20% off, with a second item at an extra 15% off:** one of the offer structures that work on TikTok. [L1183, undated]
- **Buy one and get the second at 50% off, with free shipping above a threshold:** a bundle structure, with free shipping above a threshold such as 55 dollars. [L0789, 2022]
- **Buy two get 50% off or 30% off:** tested structures among several others (see the list below). [L0650, undated]

#### Complementary bundles

- A complementary bundle pairs two different products that solve a related problem. Replace a second identical item with a complementary product, such as a sleep-related item with pyjamas or an organiser with a wallet, because a second identical item has little value to a buyer who needs only one. [L1131, 2018+]
- Complementary items work better than pushing customers to buy more of the same product to reach a free-shipping threshold. [L1172, 2023+]
- Complementary bundles work well when the add-on solves a related need and is cheap to ship. Shipping both items in one parcel is cheaper than two separate orders to the same address. [L1345, 2021+]
- Complementary-product bundles can convert better than standard discounts. A bundle with a complementary item made with the Bundle Bear app roughly doubled conversion rate compared with buy 2 get 20% off, with a 40 to 50 percent bundle take rate and about $40 extra profit per order. [L0650, undated]
- Complementary items are added when customers rarely buy more than one of the same product. [L0440, 2023+]
- Offer a complementary item when the offer complements the solution to the customer's problem or makes the product more attractive. For example, a candle seller can give two free when a customer buys four. [L0549, undated]
- For a cheap product that complements a main product, test buy two get two free instead of a small percentage discount, since this can raise AOV. [L0549, undated]
- Pair a main product with a part it needs from the same list, such as Fish Tanks with Aquarium Stands, Koi Ponds & Filtration Systems, and Above Ground Pools with Pool Filtration Systems and Pool Vacuums. Selling them together raises order value and saves the buyer a separate search. [L0546, undated]
- Bundle a product with a complementary product the same buyer needs. Example: a neck pillow for someone with neck pain who has trouble sleeping. [L0871, 2021+]

#### Build-your-own, sets, avatar and family bundles

- **Build-your-own:** up to 15% off, free shipping, and any combination of products the customer chooses. Show it as a pop-up offer that splits the screen so the product choices stay visible. [L0524, 2023]
- **Sets (sold together, older advice, one source 2021):** matching sofas and love seats should be sold together with the recliners they match, because customers buy complete sets. Keep unrelated products out of the store, with set-based exceptions. [L0801, 2021]
- **Sets, rule:** sell bundles only when the product naturally comes in sets, such as dining chairs sold in packs of four at about $200. Do not turn single items priced around $50 into bundles just to reach a higher price. [L0804, 2023]
- **Avatar bundles:** packages designed around a specific type of buyer, such as entrepreneur, traveler, biohacker, essential and longevity. Use them in promotions to match the offer to each customer type. [L0334, 2017]
- **Family packs:** bundles such as a family pack of 3 to 4 products work well and can be promoted on a specific collection page. [L0605, undated]
- **Two-family bundle:** instead of $70 for one unit, offer two units for $60 total. This appeals to families buying gifts for multiple households. [L0209, 2023]
- **Multi-pair bundles for shoes:** buyers often purchase several pairs in different colors or variants at the same time, so offer multi-pair bundles next to the single-pair option. [L1219, undated]
- **Subscriptions as a bundle layer:** offers can scale with quantity and subscription, with bigger discounts for more units, extra savings for a monthly subscription, and a "frequently bought together" add-on at a discount. [L0919, undated]
- **Buy More Save More:** suits direct-to-consumer consumable brands such as food, beverage and makeup. It can also lead first-time buyers into a subscription, because they try an offer before deciding to subscribe. [L1231, 2025-26]

#### Bundling with an upgrade

- Bundling combines a main product with an add-on you already sell as an upgrade and gives it one bundle price. Example: a $400 golf bag and a $100 trolley sold together for $450, promoted as a single package for a few days. [L1204, 2022]
- A bundle works as a re-engagement tool because it is a new offer the customer has not seen before. Its price should be close to the cost of the main product plus the upgrade, with low-cost accessories added, so the margin stays acceptable. [L1205, 2020-26]
- Case: a golf bag sold for $400 and a carrier upgrade for $100. For one month, the store offered the bag, the carrier, 24 golf balls, a glove and a cleaning kit together for $500. The extra cost to the store was only slightly higher than the bag and carrier alone, and the package gave a reason to contact old leads who had viewed the bag but not bought. [L1205, 2020-26]

### Choosing bundles by stage and starting point

- Start with one product. Add a bundle or a second product once the first one shows demand. Selling several products can also build trust, depending on the niche. [L0774, 2023]
- When starting out, focus on one product at a time rather than a bundle. Bundles are harder to sell and harder to test for a beginner. [L0772, 2026]
- Start with simple bundles that make sense for the product. Do not add complex bundle structures until the store is scaling. [L1299, 2026]
- Keep one clear offer per store rather than several different offers for different categories, to avoid confusing customers, unless the store is large enough to manage it. The overall goal is to raise AOV. [L0650, undated]
- Replace standard time-limited percentage sales with bundle offers, such as buy two get 20% off plus a free gift, or buy one and get a complementary product free. In one store this increased conversions and AOV. [L0549, undated]
- Changing an offer from 50% off plus free shipping to buy one get one free or buy two get two free made a major difference in one seller's sales compared with the earlier offer. [L0549, undated]
- Bundles at the first step of the shop raise AOV. A product priced at $39 can be sold as two or three units to bring the order value to $70 to $80. A bundle raises margin per order without raising the ad cost per click. [L0645, 2023]
- Bundles plus post-purchase upsells are the way to increase margin when a single product is priced too low. Reported results: about 70 percent of customers chose a bundle, and AOV rose from $39 to $110 across the store. [L0645, 2023]
- Bundle at the first step, then upsell: a single-product store that averaged $40 per order introduced buy two get extra 20% off, so two items cost about $70. Shipping per unit fell with two units shipped together, and AOV rose from $40 to $70 with higher profit per order. [L0434, undated]
- Expanding a single-product store into a multi-product store with bundles and extra items raises AOV. Multi-buy offers encouraged customers to buy more. [L0519, 2020+]
- Bundles raise AOV for the store, so a store with higher AOV can justify a higher cost per acquisition when scaling. Increasing AOV through bundles, upsells, higher-priced offers or a higher price raises profit per order. [L0999, 2023]
- Quantity bundles aim to raise average order value, revenue per customer and profit per order at the same time. They work as a conversion lever because each order contains more units. [L0860, 2026]
- Bundle structure matters more than the single-unit price: a visible quantity ladder with a clear saving per tier pushes multi-unit orders. Keep the ladder short so a buyer can compare the tiers in a few seconds. [L1188, 2026]
- Bundles raise AOV and also take up more space in Google Shopping ads, because each bundle is a different product listing. Bundles can be given their own UPC codes, which allows listing them on eBay. [L0920, 2018]
- A bundle is made by building one product page that includes several products, with the bundle price above the individual sum. No app is needed for a basic bundle. [L0855, 2023]

#### Bundle strategy by price point

- **Low-priced products (about $5 to $10) in paid ads:** a bundle can raise AOV to a level that covers ad costs. The bundle must still leave a margin of about $20 to $25 per customer. [L1174, undated]
- **Low-priced products in general:** bundle offers such as buy two, buy three or buy four and get one free work well for small, cheap products. Test several and keep the one that makes sense for the margin. [L1171, 2024+]
- **Low-priced kitchen products:** quantity discounts can encourage customers who want multiples to buy more. Test quantity offers with the product's natural use cases. [L1172, 2023+]
- **Competitor example at low price:** a competitor selling a cheap item priced one unit at $24.99, two units at $29.99 and a larger pack at a higher price used bundling to raise margin per order. The product was judged a potential winner because the competitor ran 280 active ads for it. [L1174, undated]
- **Cheap add-on check:** if a cheap add-on of about 2 to 3 dollars shows a good take rate of around 40 to 50 percent, a bundle with a total price up to about 40 dollars can make sense. The extra margin from the add-on can justify the bundle. [L0708, undated]
- **Mid-priced products:** a bundle offer for two items is typically priced about 20% below the single price. The second item can be a different variant of the same product, for example another size or color. [L1173, undated]
- **Higher-priced products:** a seller of a $79 device... see the bundle example below. Price the bundles so the best-selling option is the obvious deal. [L0789, 2022]
- **Thin-margin products (cheap items):** see the pricing procedure below. [L0298, 2023]
