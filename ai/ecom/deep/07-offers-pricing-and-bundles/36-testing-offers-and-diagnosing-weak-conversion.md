## Testing offers and diagnosing weak conversion

### Offer test method and budgets

#### Offer structures to test

- Offer types that improved conversion when pre-click metrics were strong but sales were low: BOGO (Buy One Get One Free), Buy 2 Get 1 Free, Buy 2 Get 50% Off, a free gift item bundled with the purchase (even a $1-$2 item), and tiered bundle packages labelled Single, Duo, or Trio. These create the perception of value and urgency, turning browsers into buyers. [L0229, undated]
- Free gifts often outperformed bundles and percentage discounts in one operator's stores. Buy-more-save-more offers (such as buy 2 get 10% off, buy 3 get 20% off) also worked. Choose a gift relevant to the audience's problem, and test many offers. [L1315, 2023]
- Buy 2, get the third free worked well for a collection of multi-item products. A single-product store with buy 2, get 2 free also worked where the product could be used in multiples. [L0388, 2024+]
- Buy-one-get-second-free or 50 percent off the second item worked best in countries such as Australia and New Zealand. In Europe, more refined offers were needed. [L1131, 2018+]
- Different bundle offers convert differently, so test several at the beginning instead of betting on one. [L1299, 2026]
- Test several offer structures, such as buy one, buy two and get one free, or buy three and get one free, because it is not possible to know in advance which offer converts best. Keep the margin check in place so every bundle is still profitable. [L0692, 2023]
- Keep offers simple: a quantity discount or a clear bundle is easier for customers than complex options. Test simple offers before complicated bundles. [L0773, 2023]
- Design many candidate offers relevant to the customer and test them against each other to see which one wins, rather than relying on one offer. [L0919, undated]
- Offer test with an introductory report: write two or more versions, each with a different angle or report name, and run them before choosing one. Change only the emphasis and naming, keeping the same product, so the result shows which angle the audience responds to. Keep the best performer and drop the others. [L1356, 2019+]

**Necklace case: four offer variants tested**

| Variant | Offer | Result | Id |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One necklace €19.90 alone | Minimal conversions | [L0233, undated] |
| 2 | One necklace €29.90 + second necklace free | Moderate conversions | [L0233, undated] |
| 3 | One necklace €29.90 + free gift + free styling ebook | Good conversions | [L0233, undated] |
| 4 | One necklace €29.90 + second necklace free + storage box + style guide + free shipping | Highest conversions ("game-changer") | [L0233, undated] |

- Variant 4 succeeded because customers perceived €80+ in value for €29.90 while the seller kept healthy margins. The bundled offer made saying no emotionally difficult. [L0233, undated]
- A necklace store ran ads with a good CTR but had no offer, and customers were expected to pay the full price of about $35. The owner switched to a buy-one-get-one-free offer, where buying the main necklace gave the second best-selling item free. Daily sales rose to roughly 1k to 4k per day (currency not stated, likely USD). The offer alone made the difference. [L1469, 2023+]
- Charging $30 for a single necklace with no bundle or compelling offer can stop a store from converting even when the audience is right. Test several offers to find one that makes sense to customers, especially for digital products, and check the conversion rate first. [L0678, 2023]

#### Price and copy variables

- Vary bundle structure and unit pricing together, not just the discount percentage. Test single-product pricing against bundle structure against per-unit price combinations. Example: a home decor product failed at $29.90 as a single price and at "buy 1 get 1 free," but succeeded at "buy 2 get 2 free" with an increased per-unit price. The new price point plus bundle structure created perceived value that matched customer expectations. The optimal offer combines discount depth, bundle structure, and unit pricing. [L0307, 2023+]
- Treat the name of a bundle offer as a variable you can test separately. Keep the same bundle, try different names, and keep the name that converts better. [L0549, undated]
- Stores A/B test price and offer together, for example $71.91 versus $58.46 for the same product. Test both variants and compare the results before choosing a price. [L1164, 2026]
- A lower average order value can still earn more if conversion rises enough. Test each offer and track AOV and conversion rate together, never AOV alone. [L1164, 2026]

#### Testing cadence and rotation

- Test a new offer roughly every two days during the testing phase to find which converts best, rather than keeping one offer for a long time. [L1216, undated]
- Run one offer at a time, not several side by side. Let each offer run for two to three days. Check the conversion rate and the CTR. If the CTR is high but there are no sales, replace the offer with a new one. Keep testing new offers about every two days until the store conversion rate is above two to three percent. [L0502, 2020+]
- Rotate offers every second day and track add-to-cart, checkout and purchase numbers:
  1. Day 1: lower the price.
  2. Day 2: create a bundle, for example a bundle at 50% off.
  3. Day 3: buy two, get a third free, for products where several units can be used.
  4. Day 4: buy one and receive a complementary product as a bonus.

  Keep the offer that produces the most add-to-cart actions and purchases. [L0388, 2024+]
- Judge an offer change over several days of data, not a single spike. One store changed its offer to a free second item while adding new interest audiences almost daily. The first morning brought about 250 sales, but sales then dropped to about one sale per day for the rest of the day. [L0642, undated]
- Offer tests can be run from day one, for example with buy one get one free, because a weak offer can be the reason a product fails. Changing the offer is a test to run before deciding to abandon a product. [L0648, undated]
- Example result: a store with 10 ad sets at $5 per day and about $30 total spend produced two sales and about $60 profit after ad spend and product costs. [L0650, undated]

#### Test setup

- Duplicate the product in Shopify, or build the offer in funnel software. Duplicate products make store analytics messy, so send all traffic to one product page to keep the data easier to analyse. [L0502, 2020+]
- Keep a winning product page running while testing a new offer. Duplicate the product and landing page, attach the new offer to the duplicate, remove the duplicate from collections so only ad traffic reaches it, and run traffic to it separately. [L1315, 2023]
- Offer test setup:
  1. Duplicate the product in Shopify.
  2. Duplicate the product page (landing page) or create a new template in Shopify.
  3. Attach the new page to the duplicated product.
  4. Remove it from all collections so only paid traffic can reach it.
  5. Run traffic to the separate product and compare it to the original. [L1315, 2023]

#### Judging results

- Treat the name of a bundle, the copy angle and the price as separate variables, and change one at a time so the result shows which factor responds. [L1356, 2019+; L0549, undated]
- Only judge the product after several offers have been tested over enough time. [L1131, 2018+]
- Test offers before giving up on the product, because the offer is the first lever to improve landing-page conversion. Work through BOGO, volume discounts, and complementary bundles one at a time. [L1345, 2021+]

#### Budgets per offer test

| Situation | Budget or rule | Id |
|---|---|---|
| Product costs about $30 | $10 daily per asset is suitable for testing; such products are much easier to sell than much more expensive ones | [L1107, 2023] |
| Product costs about $70 to $136 | $10 daily per asset is too small because sales come slowly; use a bigger budget per asset. Suggested testing budget: half of the break-even cost per purchase | [L1107, 2023] |
| Real testing budget | Run an A/B test of two completely different offers, then put more budget behind the winner. Do not test two small variations of the same offer | [L1299, 2026] |
| Tight budget | Run a basic offer already known to convert, such as BOGO, buy 1 get 2, or buy 3 | [L1299, 2026] |
| Per product test | Spend up to about $300 per product before judging it. Stop early at $10 to $50 if CTR stays below 1% or CPC is above €1 | [L1316, 2024+] |
| Early data good, no sales | If CTR and CPC look good but the product does not sell, change the theme and test different offers and prices before abandoning the product. A bad CTR or CPC early on is a reason to stop sooner | [L1316, 2024+] |

### Testing sequence

- Offer testing is a loop. Create an initial offer that bundles multiple value-adds (bonus items, free shipping, guarantees, discounts). Launch ads and monitor CTR and store conversion rate daily. If ads achieve high CTR but store conversion is low, the offer is weak and needs redesign. Test a completely new offer every 2 days. Continue until one converts consistently at acceptable margins. If lacking ideas, consult the resource PDF for more offer templates and examples. Once the offer converts reliably, proceed to price optimisation. [L0233, undated]
- Fix the offer and the fundamentals before any cosmetic changes. Improving the conversion rate of existing traffic is the lowest-hanging fruit and yields a better return before buying new traffic. [L0414, 2021-22] **
- When traffic is good but conversion is weak, first check whether the offer is a CURV offer. If it is not, fixing the offer comes before testing anything else. [L0473, 2021-22] **
- Compare the offer directly with competitor offers, because customers compare them anyway even when the business does not. Many businesses under-review this step. [L0473, 2021-22] **
- Check that the offer matches the traffic. If visitors search for something that is not visible in the offer when they land, the offer must change. [L0473, 2021-22] **
- If conversion is unsatisfying, test different offers before changing other parts of the funnel. Several offers should be tested until a good conversion rate is found. [L0678, 2023]
- Test the offer before changing the product. The offer (price, branding, USP, promotion, guarantee) plays a large role in conversion. Testing is the only way to know whether a bundle or promotion works for a niche. [L1259, undated]
- Test a price cut only after simplifying the page and removing hidden costs. In one case the seller had already lowered the price with little effect, and break-even was already tight at about 2.3x. [L0950, 2023]
- Bundle discounts can work. Test whether the bundle path is tracked correctly and whether customers see the bundle before deciding whether to lower the price. [L0950, 2023]
- Price and offer testing are central to finding profit. In one lesson's experience, around 20 offers were tested and only two were profitable enough to scale, so testing many offers is expected. [L0999, 2023]
- When a product with high CTR gets no sales, test new offers and prices, including a price below cost, to find out whether price is the barrier. If sales come, then improve the perceived value. [L1432, undated]
- When a product underperforms, test different offers, countries and age groups before dropping it. Focus budget on the countries that show sales and make sense for the product. [L1017, undated]
- Offer testing for a new product with weak sales:
  1. Define the core problem the product solves and the core promise.
  2. Test several offers, such as a price change, a bundle, or a free complementary product.
  3. Compare results per country, because offers that work in one market can fail in another.
  4. Only judge the product after several offers have been tested over enough time. [L1131, 2018+]
- A clear special offer made the difference between no sales and sales for a store with a weak website. Improving the offer, images and customer contact were done together. [L1439, 2024+]
- Test several offers for a product before deciding. Offer structure is not known in advance, so the test decides. [L0692, 2023]

### When to blame the offer, traffic or page

- High CTR with low store conversion means people see the ad and click, but the offer does not convince them. The offer, not the ad, is then the first thing to change. [L0502, 2020+]
- If ads get a CTR above 1.5% but produce no add-to-carts or sales, the offer is a likely cause and should be tested. Try different offers before assuming the ad or the page is the problem. [L1400, undated]
- If the ad metrics are poor but cost-per-click and clicks are fine, the offer itself may be weak. Compare your offer with competitors' offers, or ask a coach for feedback on improving it. [L0874, 2026]
- Note the two CTR thresholds: 1% CTR (with CPC above €1) is the early stop signal in a budget test [L1316, 2024+]; above 1.5% CTR with no add-to-carts points to the offer [L1400, undated].
- Check that the offer matches the traffic (see Testing sequence). [L0473, 2021-22] **

### Diagnosing carts without sales

- A store gets traffic and add-to-carts but no sales. Diagnose in this order:
  1. Check the Shopify orders for abandoned checkouts and see whether customers entered their contact details at checkout.
  2. If customers do not enter their information at all, test new offers and rotate a new offer every two days until one converts.
  3. If customers enter their information but do not complete the purchase, check that the payment providers are working.
  4. Also check the shipping options at checkout.
  5. Keep the offer test running for the period the store is spending on ads. [L1216, undated]
- Test a new offer roughly every two days during the testing phase (see Testing cadence). [L1216, undated]
- Test a price cut only after simplifying the page and removing hidden costs. [L0950, 2023]

### Testing by market

- Test geographic markets with one ad set per country, using a top-five analysis to see which country wins. Test English-language markets as well as local-language markets, because the sales approach determines which countries respond. [L1315, 2023]
- Buy-one-get-second-free or 50 percent off the second item worked best in Australia and New Zealand. In Europe, more refined offers were needed. [L1131, 2018+]
- Compare results per country, because offers that work in one market can fail in another. [L1131, 2018+]
- When a product underperforms, test different offers, countries and age groups before dropping it. Focus budget on the countries that show sales and make sense for the product. [L1017, undated]

### Pre-launch checklists

**Store review before launching ads**

1. Have an experienced reviewer check the store, including the product descriptions and the overall look of the store.
2. Check the price against the total dropshipping cost, including the product cost and shipping, and decide the launch price together with the reviewer.
3. Check the product reviews on the store.
4. Review the ad creatives and the video, and fix any that do not show the product clearly. [L0562, undated]

**Offer launch checklist**

1. Confirm the email flow for the offer is ready.
2. Confirm the Facebook ad assets for the offer are ready.
3. Confirm the website is ready and explains the offer clearly.
4. Plan the launch on the marketing calendar with enough lead time to prepare everything.
5. Check stock for each discounted product; exclude products that may sell out, or remove them from the site.
6. Set the order in which products are displayed on the website, since merchandising affects results.
7. Align the whole company, including customer service, on the offer, because a large discount generates support tickets.
8. Keep the offer and the web design simple so customers understand the deal without effort. [L1404, 2025-26]

- Before launching an offer, check that email, Facebook assets and the website are ready. Use the marketing calendar to plan enough lead time, and align the whole company, including customer service, because a sudden large discount generates tickets. Check stock levels, exclude products that might sell out, or remove them from the site. [L1404, 2025-26]
