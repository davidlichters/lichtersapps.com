### Bundle offers and where to put free shipping

Sources disagree on where free shipping belongs. Both values are given below with the condition under which each applies.

| Position of free shipping | Source | When it applies |
|---|---|---|
| Highest tier only; the lowest tier pays shipping (for example $5 on the lowest bundle tier) | [L0232, 2026] | Protects margins on budget purchases and uses free shipping as an incentive to upgrade |
| Never on the lowest tier (this is listed as a mistake) | [L0232, 2026] | Removing free shipping from the lowest tier keeps the upgrade incentive and avoids giving margin away to price-sensitive buyers |
| Middle tier, the two-pack labelled most popular | [L0434, undated; L0435, undated] | When the middle bundle is the one you want most buyers to pick because it carries the higher order value and better margin |
| Highest tier, the 3-pack, with free shipping as one of its extras | [L0860, 2026] | When the larger bundle should carry the most visible extras |
| Above a threshold, the free-shipping price (e.g. above 85 euros or above 55 dollars) | [L0789, 2022] | When the product price is high enough that a threshold steers buyers to a bundle |
| Free shipping on a buy-two offer | [L1176, 2023] | Raises both order size and margin |
| Free shipping on a gift variant of a buy-two offer, with free US shipping | [L1225, 2025] | Gift-season offers |
| Free shipping as a feature of the build-your-own bundle | [L0524, 2023] | Build-your-own offers |
| Free shipping on the top bundle tier of the necklace ladder | [L0502, 2020+] | The highest-value offer in the lesson |

- Free shipping on the lowest tier removes any incentive for customers to upgrade to higher tiers and wastes margin on price-sensitive buyers who would purchase regardless. [L0232, 2026]
- Charge $5 shipping on the lowest bundle tier and offer free shipping on the highest tier to protect low-end margins while using free shipping to incentivise upgrades. [L0232, 2026]
- Place free shipping on the highest-tier bundle, never the lowest. This protects margins on budget purchases while using free shipping as an upsell incentive. [L0232, 2026]
- Bundles with complementary items work better than pushing customers to buy more of the same product to reach a free-shipping threshold. [L1172, 2023+]
- Bundles reduce shipping cost per unit, since one parcel carries several items. This improves profit per order while the bundle price still feels like a saving to the customer. [L0435, undated]
- Bundle shipping saves money because two items shipped to one address cost less than two separate orders, so bundles can improve margin while appearing to be a deal. [L1259, undated]
- When an offer includes several items, ask the supplier to ship them in one package. Otherwise shipping costs multiply and the margin disappears; the extra cost should only be the product cost of the extra item. [L0502, 2020+]
- For a bundle with a complementary item, the bundle is shipped as one order to the same address to save on shipping costs. [L1259, undated; L1260, undated]
- Complementary-product bundles work well when the add-on solves a related need and is cheap to ship. [L1345, 2021+]

#### Gifts placement

- Free gifts go on the bundles, not the single-unit tier. Adding a free gift to the Buy 2 and Buy 3 tiers raises perceived value and conversion rate. [L1149, 2026]
- Offering free items or strong discounts such as buy one get one free or buy two get two free attracts customers, because people respond strongly to a perceived free item. [L0440, 2023+]
- The offer should either complement the solution to the customer's problem or make the product more attractive. For example, if you sell candles, give two free when a customer buys four. [L0549, undated]
- Place the free ebook on the 2-pack and the 3-pack, and place free shipping on the 3-pack. This gives the larger bundle the most visible extras. [L0860, 2026]
- Free gift on a bundle with a gift-friendly message: a single tier with "buy 1 and give 1 to someone you love" wording. [L1141, 2026]
- Free gifts raise perceived value, and the free-gift option can reduce the perceived price without a full discount. [L0476, undated]
- Gift-season offers should sound like a gift, not a bargain. [L1225, 2025]
- Gift-friendly niches can benefit from buy-one-get-one or buy-2-get-items offers, because customers can give the items to friends or partners. [L0650, undated]

### Bundle strategy by brand and niche

- Buy-one-get-one-free can downgrade a brand that sells a premium or identity-based product, because it cheapens the perceived value. Use a different offer for such brands. [L1131, 2018+]
- Buy-one-get-one-free can be a high barrier when many customers only want one gift, as seen with a previous Mother's Day mask offer that required buying two. Test percentage-off or a second-item discount, which gives more flexibility to single-gift buyers. [L1225, 2025]
- The best offer type depends on the product and niche. Gift-friendly niches can benefit from buy-one or buy-2-get-items offers. [L0650, undated]
- BOGO and similar offers are niche- and margin-specific and must be tested. Products that seem unlikely to sell in multiples often do sell that way when the offer is tested. [L1345, 2021+]
- Bundle offers such as "buy two, get one free" plus a clear black or multi-pack option (see the example above). [L0655, undated]
- Start a new store with one product and add a second product once the first one shows demand. [L0774, 2023]
- Products bought in stores: a bundle offer such as buy two get a third free works well for products customers already buy in stores. [L1176, 2023]
- Bundle the products that buyers would purchase together. Bundles work best when the products are related and buyers would purchase them together. Use a bundle app such as Bold Product Bundles for these combinations, though any bundle app works. [L1112, 2021]

### Sourcing and supplier bundles

- Create product bundles from the same supplier, such as kits made of components that come together, rather than making customers assemble cheap separate components to reach a price point. [L0671, 2021]
- Before building a bundle from related products, test a tiered quantity discount on the same product:
  1. One item at the normal price.
  2. Two items at a special discount price.
  3. Three items at a better discount price.

  This raises AOV with no extra product research, and the bundle idea can be tested later if it still needs a boost. [L0774, 2023]
- Test a second product as a bundle with the supplier before running ads on it:
  1. Choose the main product and a second product that is selling well for other stores.
  2. Ask the supplier agent to create a bundle SKU with the main product and the second product.
  3. Measure which bundle buyers pick most, then run ads for the second product on its own.

  [L0774, 2023]
- Discuss tiered pricing options with your supplier before launching a bundle. [L0240, 2019]
- Bundles and quantity levels with the supplier: ask the supplier for the cost of two or three pieces before pricing the bundle (see the pricing procedures). [L1182, 2026]

### Bundles and Google Shopping

- Bundles with a single item's page: create the product page for the single item that Google Shopping shows most, and offer quantity options such as 2, 4 or 10 on the same page. This keeps your listing aligned with what buyers already search for. [L0934, 2023]
- Do not repackage a product into a higher-priced bundle SKU, such as an $800 product sold as a $1,200 bundle. Google Shopping shows the higher price next to competitors at $800, which lowers click-through rate and sales. [L1109, 2018]
- Unique bundles raise AOV and also take up more space in Google Shopping ads, because each bundle is a different product listing (see the channel section above). [L0920, 2018]

### Bundles on TikTok and other channels

- Offer structures that work well on TikTok include buy one get one free, buy two get one free, buy one at 20% off with a second item at an extra 15% off, and bundles. Building a strong offer is the most important investment when creating a unique selling angle. [L1183, undated]

### Unbundling and debundling

#### Principles

- Unbundle high-priced products into separate components to lower the entry price. This improves click-through rates on Google Shopping ads while generating add-on revenue. [L0240, 2019]
- A debundled listing with a lower price can raise Google Ads click-through rate because it is cheaper than competitors' bundles. It is not false advertising when the listing photo and title show only the single item. [L1076, 2019]
- Unbundling sells the core product without an accessory at a lower price, and the accessory is offered as an optional add-on. Confirm the MAP limit with the supplier before listing it. [L1204, 2022]

#### Examples

- Product bundle: two separately priced items, one at $400 and one at $100, can be sold together as a single $500 bundle. The bundle packages the products into one purchase with a combined price. [L0757, 2022]
- Product un-bundle: a single higher-priced package is split into parts that can be sold separately. The lesson's example pairs a $1,000 price with an $800 price for the un-bundled version. [L0757, 2022]
- Paddle board example: a paddle board listed at 800 dollars without a paddle, which is 200 dollars below the 1,000 dollar competitors' price, with the paddle added for plus 200 dollars. Confirm the MAP limit with the supplier before listing it. [L1204, 2022]

#### Procedure: debundling a product for a cross-sell

1. Check the supplier price list for a single-item price, or ask the supplier for the separate MAP, wholesale and retail prices of the item sold in the bundle.
2. If the single item can be bought separately, list it at its lower price.
3. Use a photo of the single item only.
4. Let the customer add the accessory on the product page through a cross-sell offer.

[L1076, 2019]

### Procedures for complementary bundles from a low-cost accessory

1. On AliExpress, search for accessories related to the main product (for example fitness, health, jewelry, hair or nail accessories) and set a price filter from 0.01 USD to about 4 USD (for example min 0.01 and max 4 dollars).
2. Pick a low-cost complementary item (example cost around 2 USD) that solves a related problem.
3. Bundle it with the main product and ship as one order to the same address, which saves on shipping costs because two items ship to one address.
4. List the complementary item at a higher value (example 20 USD) while its cost is about 2 USD.
5. Offer the bundle with a visible saving, about 15% or a dollar-equivalent saving (example: main product 39.99 USD plus complementary product at 20 USD with a 15 USD discount, so buyers see the second item as almost free). Use wording such as "save big with our bundle."

[L1259, undated; L1260, undated]

### Testing and rollout

- Offers should be tested, and the one that makes the most sense for the margin should be kept. [L1171, 2024+; L0562, undated]
- Test BOGO and similar offers before committing, because the best structure depends on niche and margin. [L1345, 2021+]
- A quantity-break offer and a buy-two offer both raise AOV, so test which one lifts AOV more. [L0635, undated; L0434, undated]
- Use bundles to make the store more profitable per visitor, not only to win the first sale. [L0562, undated]
- Multiple bundle structures: several tested offers exist on the same product; keep the one that makes the most sense for the margin. [L0650, undated; L1171, 2024+]

### Mistakes to avoid

- Offering free shipping on the lowest tier removes the incentive to upgrade and wastes margin on buyers who would buy anyway. [L0232, 2026]
- Generic bundle names fail to create perceived value. [L0232, 2026]
- Repackaging a product into a higher-priced bundle SKU lowers click-through rate. [L1109, 2018]
- Buy-one-get-one-free can downgrade a premium or identity-based brand. [L1131, 2018+]
- A second identical item has little value to a buyer who only needs one. [L1131, 2018+]
- Bundling does not work when the store offers only one product in BOGO form (for example one book), so use Buy 1, Buy 2 and Buy 3 quantity tiers with progressive discounts instead. [L0860, 2026; L1149, 2026]
- Buy-one-get-one can be a barrier for single-gift buyers. [L1225, 2025]
- Turning single items around $50 into bundles only to reach a higher price is not advised. [L0804, 2023]
- Bundles can lose money if the bundle price does not cover product cost, acquisition cost and fees. [L0436, undated; L0233, undated]

### Build-your-own and offers that look like free items

- Build-your-own bundle: up to 15% off, free shipping, and any combination of products the customer chooses. [L0524, 2023]
- Show the add-on as almost free: list the complementary item at a visible value (e.g. $20) while its cost is about $2, and show a bundle discount so the add-on looks almost free. [L1260, undated]

### Pricing for Google Shopping and bundle take-rate

- Bundle take-rate and profit: complementary bundles with a 40 to 50 percent take rate and about $40 extra profit per order (see the complementary section). [L0650, undated]
- Bundling three related items into one set is a way to raise perceived value and average order size. [L1153, 2016]

### Bundle types

- **Complementary add-on bundles:** a low-cost accessory is paired with a main product and shown as one offer. [L1345, 2021+]
- **Quantity bundles (one, two and three packs):** each larger pack gives more savings per unit. These are a core way to raise order value, alongside volume discounts and cart upsells. [L1410, 2023]
- **Buy-one-get-one and free-item bundles:** a second unit or a second item is offered free with the first. [L1404, 2025-26; L1436, undated; L1439, 2024+]
- **On-page bundle packages:** a group of products tied to a core product, sold at one combined price. [L1409, 2021]
- **Value bundles:** one item is discounted to roughly break-even and profit comes from the other bundled items. [L1522, 2021-22]
- Bundles are often combined with percentage-off or dollar-off discounts. [L1404, 2025-26]

### Complementary add-ons

- Example: a complementary item costing about $2 is listed at $20. Bundled with the main product, the bundle shows a $15 discount on the add-on, so the buyer perceives the add-on as $5. [L1345, 2021+]
- A bundle discount of about $15 on a $20 complementary item is considered an attractive discount level. Set the actual discount according to margin. [L1345, 2021+]
- Create bundles for popular add-ons and accessories as separate Shopify products. This gives more listings in Google Shopping, where the single product and several bundles can appear together for one search. [L1357, 2018]
- Start with bundles for the best-selling accessories and test them before adding more. Bundles for every combination create too many options and overwhelm customers. The best approach is an educated guess followed by testing. [L1357, 2018]
- Bundling only works where the product is naturally bought with something else. It is not possible for products never sold alongside another item. Where it works, a bundle can add roughly one or two hundred dollars of profit per sale. [L1456, 2021-22]

- Derived from the same table: three packs at 79 save 38 against three single packs at 39 (117 − 79). This figure is not stated in the source. [L1412, 2023]
- Each extra unit is priced with a steeper discount so the bigger order looks clearly better. In a separate example, a 2-pack saved the buyer $35 and a 3-pack saved $62 at the time of that example. These figures do not match the ladder above, so recalculate them from the prices you actually use. [L1411, undated]
- Multi-pack offers can keep margin healthy even with a discount. When the same product ships to the same address, product and shipping cost per unit fall. Buyers also gain value and may gift the extra units. [L1411, undated]
- Volume bundles can be varied by pack count or by family size. The bundle should show the total price tiers clearly. Shown together, the tiers give the customer a reason to add more units. [L1412, 2023]

#### Which products get pack ladders

- For products priced under $50, a one-pack / two-pack bundle should always be implemented. For expensive products a bundle can still work, and the pack type can be changed to family packs. [L1410, 2023]
- For products priced under 50 USD, a bundle offer such as one pack, two packs or a family pack is recommended. Expensive products can also use bundles, but the strongest case is low-priced items. The two sources differ in strength: "should always" for under $50 in one, "recommended" in the other. Use the pack ladder for low-priced products first. [L1410, 2023; L1412, 2023]

### Tier naming and badges

- Name a bundle tier, such as Gold, by using the same product name plus a suffix that makes it sound like a better deal. [L1456, 2021-22]
- Multi-pack tiers with a "Best Value" tag and a visible savings amount raise the average order size. [L1479, 2026]
- Example from a 2025 US supplement page: a single bottle at $24.99, a "Buy Two Get 1 Free" multi-pack, a larger multi-pack tagged "Best Value" with a "You Save" amount shown, and an automatic refill option that saves 20%. [L1479, 2026]
- Pairing multi-pack tiers with an automatic refill discount, such as 20% off, adds repeat revenue from the same customer. [L1479, 2026]

### Buy-one-get-one and free-item offers

- Multi-purchase deals such as 2 for 1 or buy 1 get 1 free make the customer feel they get a great deal, which raises the chance of buying. This format has worked for years and still works today. [L1399, undated]
- Multi-purchase deals such as buy 2 for 1 or buy 1 get 1 free make shoppers feel they are getting a strong deal, which raises the chance of buying. This is an old promotion that still works well today. [L1400, undated]
- BOGO works best on the same product as the one purchased, because customers who want that item already have an emotional attachment to it. A variant such as Buy One Get One at 30% off is also possible. [L1404, 2025-26]
- A buy-one-get-one-free offer works well for the same product. It also works for very simple products that are understood from one image. For a different or complex product, the offer lowers conversion because the buyer must check the landing page. [L1412, 2023]
- Buy-one-get-one-free and "buy this, get this free item" bundles have been working for TikTok dropshipping. Buy-one-get-one may fail with electronics, while jewelry may suit it. [L1436, undated]
- Free-item example: buy one piece and receive a second piece for free. Customers who tagged the brand on Instagram also received a 50 discount (the source gives no unit) and a feature on the brand page. [L1439, 2024+]

### Cart-page quantity ladders

- On the cart page, a quantity ladder can reward larger baskets: one unit gets no discount and two units get 10% off. This gives a buyer already in checkout a simple reason to add a second unit. [L1412, 2023]

### on-page bundles and value bundles

- Example bundle on a product page:

| Item | Regular price (USD) | Bundle price (USD) |
|---|---|---|
| Golf simulator package | 7,799 | 7,299 |
| Tech bundle | not given | 1,899 |
| Projector floor mount enclosure | not given | 299 |
| Turf landing pad | 499 | 399 |
| Total | 10,496 | 9,896 |

The bundled total is $9,896 instead of $10,496 when bought separately. The $10,496 separate total equals 7,799 + 1,899 + 299 + 499. [L1409, 2021]

- On-page bundle boxes work better with several products tied to a core product, but a single product can also be offered this way. The customer sees a package with a combined price that is lower than the individual prices. [L1409, 2021]
- A value bundle groups products so one item is discounted to roughly break-even while profit comes from the bundled items. The bundle total must look cheaper than buying the items separately, and the page should explain the added value. [L1522, 2021-22]

#### Protecting margin on a low-margin product

1. Find a supporting product or accessory that customers buy alongside it. [L1456, 2021-22]
2. Create a named bundle tier, such as Gold, using the same product name plus a suffix that makes it sound like a better deal. [L1456, 2021-22]
3. Add the bundle as a selectable option on the product page, changing the price, or as its own product page that appears in ads. [L1456, 2021-22]
4. Use the same search traffic for both versions. Even selling the bundle to only part of buyers can recover a meaningful part of the margin lost. [L1456, 2021-22]

### Bundle strategy by price point and product type

- Start with the best-selling accessories and test them. [L1357, 2018]
- Under $50: use one-pack and two-pack bundles first. [L1410, 2023; L1412, 2023]
- Expensive products: bundles can still work, with family packs as an option. [L1410, 2023]
- Simple products understood from one image suit buy-one-get-one offers. Complex products lower conversion with those offers. [L1412, 2023]
- Electronics may fail with buy-one-get-one offers, while jewelry may suit them. [L1436, undated]
- Bundles only work for products naturally bought together. [L1456, 2021-22]

### Free shipping and gifts

- On multi-pack orders to the same address, shipping cost per unit falls, which helps keep margin healthy under a discount. Buyers may gift the extra units. [L1411, undated]
- A free second item can be attached to a purchase, and social actions can unlock extra rewards. [L1439, 2024+]

#### Further details

- A bundle offer on two units raises average order value. Use a simple discount code for single purchases to keep the entry offer easy to see. [L0492, 2023+]

- Progressive quantity discounts, with a larger discount for more units, raise the average order value and make the bundle feel like the better deal compared with buying one unit. [L1149, 2026]
