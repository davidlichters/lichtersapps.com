## Choosing a price range and ticket size

### Why price point changes the work

- Building and marketing a store takes the same time and effort whatever the average price. Niche selection, supplier research, website creation and ad investment stay constant, whether products average $10 or $1,000. [L0011, undated]
- At 10 sales per day, an average price of $10 gives $100 daily revenue and about $30 daily profit. The same 10 sales at $1,000 average give $10,000 daily revenue and about $3,000 daily profit. With constant work, price point is the primary variable determining profitability. [L0011, undated]
- At 30% net margin, a $10 cell phone case yields $3 per sale and a $1,000 stand-up paddleboard yields $300 per sale, a 100x difference per transaction. [L0012, undated]
- Higher-priced products need more visitors to convert into buyers, so their cost per acquisition is higher. Product price therefore determines what acquisition cost is acceptable. [L0440, 2023+]
- A $50 product with $20 profit needs many more sales than a $3,000 product with $900 profit to reach the same total. A high-ticket product with a clear price and a niche-matched audience makes every sale meaningful. [L0458, 2021-22]
- The higher the price point, the longer the customer journey and the more touch points a prospect needs before buying. Around $30 to $40, the whole journey can happen within minutes on a single site visit. [L0481, 2021-22]
- A very low price can signal low quality and lower perceived value. For products where value perception matters, a higher price can increase sales, and the right price depends on the product. [L0388, 2024+]
- For products buyers judge by price, a higher price can signal quality and increase demand. Know whether your category rewards low prices or premium signaling before setting the price. [L1261, undated]
- High prices create resistance and limit the reachable market, so price must match the scale of sales needed. High-volume products can be priced low, while low-consumption products need a high margin per sale. [L1261, undated]
- Narrowing the audience to a specific problem can support a higher price than broad positioning, because a specific audience accepts a price a general audience would not. [L1194, undated]
- Online buyers have a practical spending ceiling when paying by card without much deliberation, so very high prices reduce conversion. [L1147, 2020-26]

### Minimum price floors

The sources give different floors because they describe different models. Use the floor that matches your situation.

| Floor | Context | Source |
|---|---|---|
| $200 for core products (higher preferred) | Core niche of a dropshipping store; products at $50, $100 or $150 should be avoided even if they sell easily | [L0474, 2020-26] |
| $200 for small parcels (UPS/FedEx) | Shipping-cost profitability | [L0016, undated] |
| $1,000 or higher | Large or heavy freight items, per the same rule | [L0016, undated] |
| About $600 to $700 or more | Large and heavy items should ideally sell for this | [L0356, 2019] |
| About $600 or less not worth pursuing | Large heavy products sold with free shipping; price must cover COGS and full shipping and leave net profit | [L1180, 2020-26] |
| $200 minimum, about $80 exception | Products sold to customers (2025). Exception: add-on and cross-sell products on high-ticket pages, minimum around $80 | [L0472, 2025] |
| $90 minimum | High-ticket products; not recommended for beginners, viable for experienced operators with a sufficient testing budget | [L0209, 2023] |
| $20 bare minimum | Facebook and TikTok ad costs keep rising and thin margins leave no room for testing | [L0708, undated] |
| $25 minimum on paid platforms | Beginners: margin will not cover customer acquisition costs; aim for a 3x to 4x markup | [L1174, undated] |

Floor rules in more detail:

- Product prices should not go below 200 USD. [L0356, 2019]
- Do not list products below 200 dollars, and prefer prices higher than that. [L0968, 2021]
- Target minimum selling price is about $200 per product, so enough margin remains after shipping (2023). [L0855, 2023]
- As a guideline, the minimum product price is about $200 to keep margins solid. Large or heavy items should be above that. Avoid products that sell for around $50 through paid ads, since they are too hard to make profitable (2023). [L0804, 2023]
- Expensive items should be at least about $200 and ideally more. A range of $500 to $1,000 keeps customer service load manageable. Products above $2,000 bring more customer service work. [L0468, 2020]
- Only add products priced at 200 dollars or more. Items at 100 to 200 dollars were not worth the effort in that business. [L1279, 2021]
- Target a selling price between $25 and $200. The range leaves room for impulse purchases and enough margin for paid ads. [L1174, undated]
- Target about 50 USD or more where possible, because these products give better profit margins. [L0363, 2022+]
- Accessories and add-ons can be priced as low as about 50 dollars when they ship from the same supplier as the main product, adding no extra shipping or acquisition cost. Products that are hard to ship or source should not be priced that low. [L0665, 2022]
- Avoid individual products priced as low as $50. [L0852, 2020]

Where the floors conflict:

- The $200 floor applies to one source's core-product model, where a product receives paid traffic. [L0474, 2020-26; L0804, 2023]
- The $20 and $25 floors come from one source's paid-social context, where cheap products must be made profitable through higher average order value. [L0708, undated; L1174, undated; L1107, 2023]
- The $50 accessory floor applies only when the add-on ships from the same supplier and adds no extra cost. [L0665, 2022]
- The $80 add-on floor applies only on high-ticket pages. [L0472, 2025]

### Low-ticket versus high-ticket trade-offs

#### Low-ticket products

- In the US market, products in the 30 to 60 USD range are easier for beginners than high-ticket products. Cost per purchase for products around 70 to 100 USD is far higher than for cheap products around 20 USD or less. [L0693, 2024]
- Products around $10 are hard to scale, so higher-priced books are preferred. [L1175, 2026]
- A heavy book costing about $20 including shipping is not viable to test. Average order value must be much higher to leave room for ad costs and margin. [L1175, 2026]
- Target an average unit price of about $30 to $100. Go higher only with awareness that fewer products will be available at higher price points. This band is the filter used for the selection described here. [L1175, 2026]
- A low margin struggles to scale. Example: a product costing $26.99 and sold at $49.99 leaves about $23 profit, and an expected CPA of $15 to $20 eats most of it. [L0995, 2023; L0999, 2023]
- To fix a low margin, raise average order value through bundles, upsells or a free gift added to a higher price, as long as the gift cost is low. [L0995, 2023; L0999, 2023]
- Very low-priced items, such as a $7 wallet, only make sense with high traffic and high sales volume, since profit comes from many small margins. Test the same product at a higher price such as $19.99 in a different market. [L1185, 2023]
- Example: a $6.47 competitor product with a $1 wholesale cost, $1 shipping and $2.90 Amazon fees left $1.57 before ads. That is too little to pay for paid traffic, which shows why a higher price point and a bundle make ad spending workable. [L1153, 2016]

#### High-ticket products

- High-ticket products start at a $90 minimum price point. They are not recommended for beginners but are viable for experienced operators with a sufficient testing budget. [L0209, 2023]
- Avoid products above roughly $100 to $200 as a beginner. COGS becomes high, and payouts from sales can take time to reach the bank. The supplier may have to be paid from your own pocket. [L1174, undated]
- Upper middle-class pricing of $1,000 to $2,000 reduces pre-sale effort and post-sale service requirements compared to lower price points. [L0270, 2021]
- Sofa example: sell $1,500 sofas to middle-class customers with better creditworthiness and healthy margins, rather than $5,000 sofas that overextend customers on credit. Current market (2023): focus on $1,200 to $1,800. [L0274, 2023]
- High-priced products can sell online at $6,000 to $10,000, such as infrared saunas, when the product fits the buyer's interest and the store looks credible. [L0771, 2021-22]
- Example: a competitor sold a high-ticket product at 220 EUR, with a sourcing cost of about 50 EUR checked through CJ Dropshipping, leaving a margin of more than 150 EUR per sale before ad costs. A wide spread shows room for margin but does not prove the price converts, so test the product page and ads before scaling. [L0693, 2024]
- A high-ticket nursing book priced around 143 to 229 USD sold only about 87 units and was judged too thin and spiky to build on. A high price alone does not signal a good product, so check units sold per day before pursuing it. [L0354, 2026]

### High-ticket ranges and service load

- Start product selection in the $200 to $1,000 range to reduce the number of incoming calls. Products from $2,000 to $4,000 are possible from day one if you are willing to answer and return phone calls (2023). [L0835, 2023]
- Price ranges typically fine for a single order without heavy pre- and post-sale service are up to about $2,000. Around $2,500 is a borderline range where customers ask more questions before and after purchase, but it is not forbidden. [L0854, 2022]
- Products above $2,000 can still work, mainly for bulk buyers such as businesses or event planners. Expect more pre-sale and post-sale customer service in that range. [L0854, 2022]
- If a niche has a very wide price range, such as $30 to $12,000, focus on a mid-tier band such as $500 to $5,000. Mid-tier products usually have the most demand without the heavy service load of the top tier. [L0854, 2022]
- Sell all products in the niche priced between $2,000 and $4,000 if they fit the niche, not just the cheapest ones. Higher-priced items earn more net profit per sale. [L0468, 2020]
- List all higher-priced products you can source, even those in the $2,500 to $8,000 range. The worst outcome is that nobody buys them. The best outcome is that you start receiving inquiries and direct orders for high-value items. [L0358, 2020]
- There is no fixed price ceiling. Do not stop at around $1,000. Higher-priced products can be sold as long as the minimum margin and the other rules still apply. [L0960, 2019]
- Premium versions of a product can be sold above the niche's average price only if there is enough demand at that price segment. Check the segment's sales volume and suppliers' expected volumes before committing. [L0967, 2020]
- When a product has many variations, consider stocking the higher tiers, because customers who want custom or premium options will pay more for them. [L0801, 2021]
- To test higher-priced products in an existing store, add only the top two or three brands in the higher tier first. Measure them in the campaigns before adding more, so the budget does not spread too thin. [L0854, 2022]

### Testing higher-priced products

- Before scaling, test the product page and the ads, because a wide margin spread does not prove the price converts. [L0693, 2024]
- A low-value item sold at a price buyers see as too high for its usefulness struggles to convert. An ice cube listed at about 17 to 18 USD was hard to justify because cheaper alternatives exist. [L1444, 2022]

### Products below the floor

#### Do not list them as core products

- Products under 200 dollars are not recommended for listing. If they are listed, set their CPC to 0.01 dollars and do not run ads to them, so the budget goes to higher-priced products (2025). [L0857, 2025]
- Products under $200 should usually be sold as an upsell or add-on rather than as a standalone product. [L0852, 2020]
- Cheaper items below the core threshold should be sold as add-ons or upsells, not as core products that traffic is bought for. Keeping them off the main ad targets avoids spending on traffic that yields too little profit per sale. [L1147, 2020-26]
- Example: a $150 accessory is not a core product because it is below the $200 threshold. It is added to a higher-priced main product as an add-on, so the extra sale adds profit without receiving paid traffic. [L1147, 2020-26]

#### Keep them as add-ons and upsells

- Products below the core threshold are not dropped. They still earn profit as add-ons or upsells attached to core product orders. [L1147, 2020-26]
- For products under $200 that buyers usually purchase in multiples, check competitors' bundle or package prices to see typical quantities. If buyers commonly buy sets, the niche can work. [L0469, 2021]
- Do not use the loss-leader or make-money-on-backend model. For a low-ticket product, bundle it with a high-ticket item or use it only as an upsell or remarketing offer. [L0268, 2020]
- Add a quantity break discount so customers buy more units per order. This raises average order value on cheap products. [L1107, 2023]

#### Raise the price of very cheap items

- Raise the prices of very cheap items so they leave room for profit. A novelty ice mold priced around 7 USD was judged too low. The lesson suggested at least 10 to 15 USD for such items. [L1325, 2020-26]

### Building price-range collections in Shopify

Procedure: create a bounded price collection.

1. Create a new collection and enter the name, for example '$1,000 to $2,000'.
2. Set Collection type to Automated.
3. Keep Products must match on 'all conditions'.
4. Add condition 1: Price, is greater than, 999.99.
5. Add condition 2: Price, is less than, 2000.01.
6. Click Save.

Procedure: create an open-ended price collection.

1. Create a new collection and enter the name, for example '$2,000 and up'.
2. Set Collection type to Automated.
3. Change the condition field to Price.
4. Set the operator to is greater than and the value to 2000.
5. Click Save.

New products above the threshold are then added automatically. [L0371, 2020-26]

#### Further details

- For a US nursing study guide, a price near 24.99 USD was judged to leave too little margin, while a pocket guide format around 37 USD was judged the better price point. Check whether a low price leaves room for your unit cost before choosing it. [L0354, 2026]
