## Affiliates, influencers and partner offers

### Choosing which products affiliates may promote

- Offer affiliates your top 10 bestselling products, not your whole catalog. Affiliates cannot tell which items are worth promoting or consistently in stock if everything is open to them. [L0213, 2021-22]
- Do not give affiliates access to your entire catalog and expect them to find the winners. Offer your best sellers and tell affiliates which products sell, because affiliates want offers that convert. [L0719, 2021-22]
- Do not run affiliate offers on products that are frequently out of stock. Promoting unavailable inventory wastes affiliate traffic and effort, and you still owe commissions on cancelled orders without recovering the payout. [L0213, 2021-22]

### Setting affiliate commission rates

- Choose product-specific commission rates rather than a blanket rate across your entire catalog. [L0213, 2021-22]
- Set each rate from the profit margin of that product or brand, not as one blanket rate. The commission must still leave you profit and must be more attractive than alternatives such as the Amazon affiliate program. [L0719, 2021-22]
- Common ranges for high-ticket products are 5-10% of the sale price, or a fixed amount such as $50 per transaction. [L0213, 2021-22]
- Worked margin case: with a 30% supplier margin where you eliminate advertising costs, an affiliate commission of 8-10% still leaves healthy profit, because customer service and fulfillment remain your only variable expenses. [L0213, 2021-22]
- Reconciling the two figures: the 5-10% range is the general benchmark, while the 8-10% figure applies to a product with a 30% supplier margin and no advertising cost. Calculate from your own margins before using any benchmark. [L0213, 2021-22]
- Calculate affiliate commission rates from your product margins and cost structure before offering them. [L0213, 2021-22]
- Affiliate offers at 3-9% commission are not worth it for core products. Promote an affiliate product only when it helps customers reach the same goal and you do not sell it directly. [L0962, 2021]
- Example: a treadmill store can promote meal delivery kits as an affiliate because treadmill buyers often want to lose weight. The store does not sell the meals itself and earns a commission on promotion. [L0962, 2021]
- Example of a high commission on a digital product: a treadmill store offers a $37 ebook on abs with a 75 percent affiliate commission, which means about $28 per sale. [L0923, 2015]

### Cookie windows and affiliate motivation

- Longer cookie windows (60 days or unlimited) encourage affiliates to keep promoting, because they earn commission on delayed conversions. Affiliates stop promoting when sales do not trigger payouts within your cookie duration. [L0213, 2021-22]

### Onboarding a new affiliate

1. When a new affiliate joins, look at their site and the products they already recommend. [L0719, 2021-22]
2. Choose the products on your store that fit their audience and that you know sell. [L0719, 2021-22]
3. Send those products to the affiliate, or let them switch an existing link to your store if you offer a better rate. [L0719, 2021-22]
4. Keep the product list to products you can actually sell, since an affiliate will not sell what you cannot sell yourself. [L0719, 2021-22]

### Affiliates and MAP pricing

- Affiliate commissions do not violate MAP. The customer still sees the advertised price at MAP, and the commission paid to the affiliate is a separate business transaction. [L0276, 2024]

### Where to find affiliate programs

- For information products, ClickBank is the largest affiliate marketplace for digital products. For physical products, use Commission Junction, ShareASale or Amazon Associates. [L0923, 2015]
- If your niche has nothing on ClickBank, search for niche resources, forums, communities and blogs to see what your audience buys. [L0923, 2015]

### Partner and cross-promotional bonuses

- Partner with indirect competitors, meaning businesses in the same industry that sell something else, ideally a digital product or service. They gain audience, and you gain a bonus offer your margin could not fund alone. [L0469, 2021]
- Example: a store selling a $500 home-brewing kit partners with a homebrew conference whose tickets normally cost $100, offering buyers a 75 percent discount or a free pass. It also partners with an online brewing school whose $47 intro course is given free. Both partners gain exposure and members. [L0469, 2021]
- Partner bonuses are digital services from another company, such as a free first lesson, a free 14-day or 30-day trial, or an extended trial of a membership. These partners are usually happy to send you customers, so they often agree to free trials or extended discounts. [L1204, 2022]
- Cross-promotion within one store's funnel, using a treadmill dropship store as the starting point:
  1. Visitors have their email captured and are pixeled on Facebook and Google. [L0923, 2015]
  2. They are then offered a free weights store and a home gym store (both dropship stores). [L0923, 2015]
  3. They are offered the $37 ebook on abs with a 75 percent affiliate commission. [L0923, 2015]
  4. They are offered a Nutrisystem subscription, which pays recurring commission for as long as the customer receives the meals. [L0923, 2015]
  5. They are offered a three-week diet product on ClickBank that pays more than $100 per signup. [L0923, 2015]
  6. Smaller accessories such as jump ropes sit in an Amazon Associate store that is used only for retargeting. [L0923, 2015]

### Revenue sharing with partner stores

- In a brand partnership you receive a percentage of each sale, similar to a traditional affiliate relationship, and you do not handle fulfillment for those orders. The lesson does not state a specific commission rate. [L0869, 2021-22]
- Offer a partner store an affiliate-style cut only on products whose margin can cover that fee. Sharing revenue is viable only when you can afford the commission. [L0869, 2021-22]

### Setting up a low-cost affiliate funnel

- Starting costs for a bootstrapped affiliate funnel: domain $10, first-year hosting $12, and autoresponder free for 30 days, for a total of $22. After the trial, autoresponder prices were about $19 per month and rise as the subscriber count grows. [L0514, 2015]
- Example: an affiliate program paying $300 per referral for a digital course. If the site costs $22 to set up, the first sale nets about $278, and the payout rises when the course price goes up. [L0514, 2015]
- When you charge a small amount, such as $7, to filter out visitors who only want free information, place the payment step on your own affiliate site, either at the opt-in or on the page right after it. Do not run this paid step through Facebook ads. [L0514, 2015]

### Influencer and creator fee negotiation

- Pricing sequence for a creator fee:
  1. Define the usage terms and exclusivity you need. [L0785, undated]
  2. Open the negotiation and document each party's rights. [L0785, undated]
  3. Assess campaign complexity and the effort required. [L0785, undated]
  4. Define the exact deliverables and their scope. [L0785, undated]
  5. Evaluate the creator's track record and expertise, then negotiate the final fee with all of these factors included. [L0785, undated]
- Check whether a top-tier creator fits your budget before you pursue them. Influencer fees range from about $50 to about $1 million per campaign, so decide where your budget sits in that range first. [L0785, undated]
- Budget and negotiation sequence:
  1. Assess your overall marketing budget and set a reasonable share for influencer marketing that does not compromise your wider strategy. [L0785, undated]
  2. Prioritize creators who fit within that budget and your goals. If you cannot afford a creator, pick one within your range. [L0785, undated]
  3. Research industry standards for audience size, engagement rate, content quality and niche, so you neither overpay nor underpay. [L0785, undated]
  4. Adjust the market rate for expertise, campaign complexity and exclusivity, and reflect these adjustments in the contract. [L0785, undated]
  5. State your budget and constraints openly and communicate throughout the negotiation. [L0785, undated]
  6. Show flexibility, for example through long-term collaboration, mutual promotion or affiliate arrangements. [L0785, undated]
  7. Emphasize the mutual benefit of the partnership, since a brand that offers unique value to the creator's audience can get rate adjustments. [L0785, undated]
  8. Discuss a long-term partnership, where a multi-period deal can lower the rate, gives the creator steady income and gives you a reliable creator. [L0785, undated]
- Paying more or less than the market rate because you are uninformed about benchmarks for the niche and creator size leads to overpaying or underpaying. Research benchmarks before making an offer. [L0785, undated]
- Pursuing a high-profile creator who is outside your budget wastes time and money. Do not commit to a creator you cannot afford, even if they are your first choice. Find one who fits both your budget and your goals. [L0785, undated]
- Creators are often willing to come down to your budget when communication is good and they genuinely want to work with the brand. Be transparent about your budget limits from the start. [L0785, undated]
- Long-term partnerships usually lower the cost per campaign: the creator gets regular income, and the brand gets a creator who keeps working with it. Discuss a reduced rate in exchange for a longer commitment. [L0785, undated]
- A brand's first offer to a creator was $200 and was negotiated upward toward $400, and the full amount was paid via PayPal. Starting low leaves room to pay more and still close the deal. [L1379, 2020+]
- Set aside about $500 as a test budget for influencer campaigns. The minimum a creator typically accepts is about $200, and some campaigns will fail. [L1379, 2020+]
- Use a discount code for influencers only when your margin allows it. A 25% discount code was affordable only because the margin left enough profit on each sale, and the code tracked the influencer's campaign. [L0790, 2017]

### Maximum affordable fee and break-even

- Calculate the maximum affordable influencer fee before negotiating: multiply the follower count by the engagement rate, then by a conservative conversion rate such as 1 percent, and multiply the result by the product price. Compare the expected revenue against the cost of the promotion to see whether it is profitable in the worst case. [L0789, 2022]
- Example: an influencer with 75,000 followers and a 5 percent engagement rate reaches about 3,750 people per story. At 1 percent conversion, about 37 buyers at a $39 price bring about $1,462 worst-case revenue. A $600 investment is then covered on the first promotion. [L0789, 2022]
- Break-even formula: Break-even units = promotion fee / profit margin per sold unit. Example: a $500 fee with a $25 margin per unit requires 20 units sold to break even. [L0789, 2022]

### Influencer payment structures

- Cash-flow split when the brand funds all costs up front: 80 percent of the deal goes to the brand and 20 percent to the influencer for the first three months. After that, a 60/40 split in favour of the brand is mentioned as the next step. Use 80/20 for the initial period and 60/40 afterwards, depending on the phase of the partnership. [L0789, 2022]
- If you have no cash flow to fund the promotion, offer a 50/50 revenue split instead. The brand provides value upfront by showing results, using relevant case studies from other industries if its own track record is missing. Use 50/50 when the brand cannot fund costs, and 80/20 when it can. [L0789, 2022]
- For smaller creators, a free product exchange is often accepted if the product is good and the creator's niche fits. If they decline, offer payment instead. [L1379, 2020+]
- Payment options for a paid creator post:
  - Pay the full amount up front, or
  - Pay half up front plus the product, and the second half after the video is posted and has stayed live for the agreed period (for example 24 hours or longer). [L1379, 2020+]

### Affiliate-style creator incentives

- An affiliate commission for a creator can be a fixed amount per sale, for example $2 per order, or a percentage. Tiered commissions based on sales volume are also possible. [L1379, 2020+]
- Affiliate incentives let a creator keep earning from promoting you after the initial post, which builds a longer relationship. This is useful when cash budgets are limited. [L1379, 2020+]
