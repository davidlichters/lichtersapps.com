## What an offer is and how buyers judge value

### Definition of an offer

- An offer is the full set of what a visitor is shown to convince them to buy. A discount may be part of it, but the offer also includes everything else presented to persuade the purchase. [L0408, 2021-22]
- Anything that makes the buyer more likely to say yes, such as a bonus, a bundle or a guarantee, counts as part of the offer. An offer is more than a product on sale at a discount. [L0757, 2022]
- In marketing, an offer is the full package a buyer sees when deciding whether to buy from you: the product, the business behind it, and everything that persuades them. A discount or bundle is only one possible part of that package. [L0411, 2021-22]
- A complete offer has nine elements: marketing messages for the product, the product itself, product price, shipping or delivery, a USP package, risk reversal, customer service, trust factors, and bonuses with value delivery or perceived value. [L0411, 2021-22]
- Resellers compete mainly on the business. Own-product brands compete more on the product. [L0411, 2021-22]
- A total offer includes the product and its benefits, the marketing message, the price, the unique selling proposition, risk reversal (protection if something goes wrong), shipping or delivery arrangements, customer service, trust factors such as reviews, and any special offer such as a discount, sale price or free gift. A discount alone is only one small part of the offer. [L0414, 2021-22]
- The USP package is how the unique selling proposition is presented on the page. It can overlap with other offer elements, such as risk reversal, but it can also be entirely separate. [L0411, 2021-22]
- The offer is more than the price. Branding, the unique selling point, bundle or BOGO structure, and the perceived value of the whole deal all shape it. Add-to-cart rates depend on these elements. A weak offer is often the first reason a landing page does not convert. [L1260, undated; L1345, 2021+]
- Customers buy an offer, not just a product. The same item can look far more valuable with a free bonus, free shipping and quantity discounts, without any change to the item or its base price. [L0860, 2026]
- The offer must be relevant to the buyer's problem. High perceived value does not require adding more items. Speaking directly to the prospect's situation raises perceived value more than a generic "buy this" message. [L0643, 2015]

### The value equation

- Customer perceived value follows a ratio. Dream outcome and perceived likelihood sit in the numerator; time delay and effort and sacrifice sit in the denominator. This ratio determines purchasing decisions, and all four components influence whether a customer buys.
  - Sum form: `(Dream outcome + Likelihood of achieving it) / (Time required + Effort and sacrifice)` [L0233, undated]
  - Product form: `Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood of achievement) / (Time delay × Effort and sacrifice)`. Raise the first two factors and shrink the last two to make an offer more valuable. For a product, show customers how little time and effort they need. [L0502, 2020+; L0549, undated]
  - The worked example in [L0233, undated] uses the sum form.
- The four factors, judged from the buyer's point of view:
  - **Dream outcome:** what the buyer wants to become. [L0502, 2020+]
  - **Perceived likelihood:** how possible the buyer believes it is to reach that outcome. [L0502, 2020+]
  - **Time delay:** how long it takes. [L0502, 2020+]
  - **Effort and sacrifice:** what the buyer must invest. [L0502, 2020+]
- Dream outcome examples: a necklace promises looking stylish and expressing identity; fitness promises a great body. Identify your product's dream outcome and communicate it throughout the offer structure, naming and product description. [L0233, undated]
- Build perceived likelihood through high-quality customer reviews, before-and-after photos, testimonials and comparative evidence showing results. [L0233, undated]
- Worked example: a gym membership scores `(100) / (50) = 2`. Supplements for the same goal score `(100) / (10) = 10`. The outcome promise is identical, but supplements have 5x the perceived value because they require less time and effort. [L0233, undated]
- Gyms and supplements sell the same dream outcome, but supplements win larger demand because they promise faster results with almost no effort. A six-pack through training can take two to three years or more, which makes the time delay and effort very high. [L0502, 2020+]
- Numeric check: a numerator of 10 divided by a denominator of 2 gives 5. Lowering the denominator while keeping the numerator high raises the result. [L0502, 2020+]
- As time and effort approach zero, perceived value approaches infinity. Sources differ on how this compares with the other levers:
  - Cutting time and effort matters as much as strengthening the promise. [L0502, 2020+]
  - This asymptotic multiplier drives purchasing decisions more powerfully than improving the dream outcome or the likelihood of success. Products that need minimal time and effort will substantially outperform identical-outcome products with more friction. [L0233, undated]
- Shorten the time delay by promising short delivery, such as one to two weeks instead of months. Fast competitors such as Amazon can offer same-day shipping, so the gap matters. [L0502, 2020+]
- Sales copy should shrink the time, effort and sacrifice in the buyer's eyes, for example by showing a simple three-step process. This makes a high price feel justified. [L0919, undated]
- Buyers pay for the value they expect to get. If the offer delivers little perceived value, selling and convincing people to spend money on the store becomes very difficult. [L0502, 2020+]
- Buyer types differ:
  - Impulse buyers purchase quickly from a clear offer.
  - Comparison buyers check other stores.
  - Facebook and TikTok ads draw attention. Google Ads and comparison-shopping buyers look for the best deal.
  - The store offer has to convince both types. [L0502, 2020+]
- Before launching a landing page or offer, ask whether you would buy it yourself at that price with that offer, and why someone else should. Add as much value as possible until the answer is yes. [L0502, 2020+]
- Perceived value created through brand, presentation and marketing angles lets a product sell at a much higher price than its cost suggests. Prefer products with high perceived value potential. [L0708, undated]

### Making the offer valuable (CURVE and bonuses)

- A CURVE offer stands for Clear, Unique, Risk-free and Valuable. Each letter is a test the offer must pass, and the offer should be reviewed against all four. [L0411, 2021-22]
- **Valuable:** the customer perceives more value or benefit than from competitors. Improving one element while keeping the others equal can make the whole offer more valuable. [L0411, 2021-22]
- **Unique:** position the total offer so it is perceived as unique within the market. A different offer from competitors raises conversion more than changing a button colour. An average presentation can sell a great offer, but a poor offer cannot be saved by a great presentation. [L0414, 2021-22]
- **Clear:** simple offers convert best because shoppers spend mental effort understanding complicated deals. A complex offer such as "50% off your third item" loses buyers. A simple, obvious deal makes customers act quickly. [L1404, 2025-26]
- An offer works best when the buyer would feel unreasonable refusing it, usually through a clear saving or added value tied to their problem. [L1131, 2018+]
  - A bonus that duplicates the main product adds little, because a buyer who needs one item does not want a second one.
  - A complementary product that solves a related problem feels more valuable. Example: an organiser for a wallet buyer. [L1131, 2018+]
- Bundles and products with extra value, such as a case or accessories that help the buyer complete the job the product solves, increase the perceived value of the offer. [L1218, undated]
- Perceived value makes a product look more valuable than a competitor's without a large product change. Ways to add it:
  - bonus ebooks
  - a coupon code
  - a lifetime guarantee
  - bundling the product with a smaller item from the supplier [L1153, 2016]
- Irresistible offers maximise perceived value so the customer feels they are getting a bargain. Tools include discounts, free gifts, free resources, money-back guarantees and bundles that stack value. [L0919, undated]
- Discounts raise conversion rates because a visitor perceives a discount as a valuable offer. This is the underlying reason behind most promotion types used to increase sales. [L1400, undated]
- Explain the cost to develop the offer, such as expert teams, lab testing and the number of repetitions a product went through. This turns a feature list into a story of value. Identical features and benefits written with development detail sound more valuable than the same product described briefly. [L1370, undated]

### Offer types

- Offer types available to an e-commerce store, with their variants:

| Offer type | Variants or notes |
|---|---|
| Percentage off | Sitewide, specific SKUs, tiered (e.g. spend $100, get 20% off after that) |
| Dollar off | Sitewide or specific SKUs |
| Buy one get one (BOGO) | Free, or discounted (e.g. 30% off the second item) |
| Mystery box | Bundle of unknown contents sold as a 'trust us' pack |
| Free express shipping | Upgraded shipping at no cost, used before cut-off dates |
| Bundle deals | Multiple products sold together, often combined with % or $ off |
| Subscribe and save | Recurring orders with a discount |
| Gift with purchase | Free product from another range added to a paid order |
| Post-purchase upsell or cross-sell | Offer made after checkout |
| Lifetime warranty | Extended guarantee as the offer |
| 100-day free trial | Long trial period as the offer |
| Just pay shipping | Product free at checkout, customer pays shipping only |
| Exclusive memberships | Paid or sign-up access to exclusive deals |
| Loyalty program | Points or perks for repeat customers |
| Purchase to go in draw | Purchase entitles the buyer to a prize draw | [L1404, 2025-26]

- Monetary offer types with example forms:

| No. | Offer type | Example form |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Percent off each purchase | 10% off each purchase |
| 2 | Spend X, save X | Save $X when the order is over $Y |
| 3 | Buy one, get one free | Second item at no charge |
| 4 | Free expedited shipping | Faster shipping at no charge |
| 5 | Free white glove delivery | Premium delivery service at no charge |
| 6 | Free extended warranty | Longer warranty at no charge |
| 7 | Entered to win a prize | Entry into a prize draw |
| 8 | Make an offer | Shopper names a price | [L0757, 2022]

- The offer must fit the product, the audience and its price point. For low-ticket items a free second item can be affordable because the extra cost is small. Weak offers such as BOGO at 20 percent off are seen as boring and ineffective. [L1469, 2023+]
- Offers improve ad conversion. A brand running a promotion can afford to spend more on Meta ads than a competitor selling the same product without one, and still win the customer. [L1404, 2025-26]
- Offers reduce customer acquisition cost and lift revenue, clear slow inventory, create urgency and generate buzz and word of mouth. The main goal is revenue growth, driven by scarcity, urgency and the confidence buyers feel while a promotion runs. [L1404, 2025-26]
- Offers can roughly double revenue and make forecasting harder, because a well-matched offer can cause a sharp jump in sales. Plan for offers as a regular growth lever rather than an occasional event. [L1404, 2025-26]
- Strong offers are the primary lever for scaling. A well-designed offer combined with existing ad quality and conversion rates can increase ROAS from 2x to 4x overnight and CVR from 1.5% to 2.5%. [L0232, 2026]

### The no-brainer gap

- A no-brainer offer creates a large gap between perceived value and price. This is achieved by adding a lot of value, lowering the price, or a combination of both. Focus on increasing value, since a strong offer can make even average copy convert. [L1365, 2019+]
- Value stack: total value = sum of the stated value of each item in the offer, shown as value rather than price. The final price is a fraction of that total, so the gap between total value and final price makes the offer feel irresistible. Example: items with a stated total value of $12,000 offered for $990 show a gap of about twelve times the price. [L1365, 2019+]
- Bundle build-up: list every component of a bundle with its standalone price, add up the total hard value, then show a much lower total price. Name, value and state each bonus as free. Example: a training package whose parts were valued at over $5,200 but priced at $3,895 for a 24-month contract, with bonuses listed as free. [L1370, undated]
- Anchoring: build perceived value by anchoring a higher price against a comparable product that offers a similar result, such as a hedge fund strategy costing six figures, so your price looks like a good deal. [L1365, 2019+]
- Scarcity, by limiting the number of units available, raises perceived value and adds urgency. [L1365, 2019+]

### Why price alone is a weak lever

- Price is part of the offer, but competing only on price is a weak strategy. If the rest of the offer matches competitors, a slightly lower price can make the total offer stronger. [L0411, 2021-22]
- Cutting the price to attract buyers makes the product look cheap, since shoppers assume the best products in a category are not the cheapest. A lower price lowers perceived value and can reduce sales, which is why many businesses price themselves out of the market. [L1365, 2019+]
- Never build a store around the lowest price. Raise the price by increasing the value you provide, since higher prices often signal higher quality and build trust. [L0788, undated]
- Price should match what the buyer believes the product is worth. Compare against what competitors charge for similar products, and aim to make the buyer feel they get more for a higher price. Products with high perceived value include electronics, advanced products and items made from premium materials. [L1215, 2023+]
- Sources differ on when to price competitively:
  - Very simple products, such as a basic knife, cannot gain much perceived value, so price them competitively. [L1217, 2023+]
  - Products with several features or benefits can justify higher prices. [L1217, 2023+]
- Price shock: a price noticeably higher than competitors' or unfamiliar pricing can lead to abandonment. Check product pricing against the market and make the offer attractive before lowering the price. [L0640, 2023]

#### Further details

- The 40-40-20 principle (credited to Ed Meyer, adapted for e-commerce) splits the chance of conversion this way: 40% from reaching the right audience at the right time (traffic), 40% from the offer, and 20% from how the offer is presented (website design). Most people focus only on the 20% presentation part. [L0414, 2021-22]
