## MAP, MSRP and wholesale: reading supplier pricing

### Supplier price lists: wholesale, MAP and MSRP

- A supplier price list gives three prices: wholesale (your cost), MAP (minimum advertised price, your selling price) and MSRP (manufacturer's suggested retail price, your strikethrough compare-at price). [L0255, 2025; L0939, 2020-26]
- Example: wholesale $500, MAP $1,000, MSRP $1,200. [L0255, 2025]
- Example of a supplier's price sheet after approval, usually one spreadsheet with three rows:

| Price | Role for your store | Example (USD) |
|---|---|---|
| Wholesale | cost you pay the supplier | 1,000 |
| MAP | the price you sell at | 2,000 |
| MSRP | the crossed-out compare-at price | 3,000 |

  *Older (2019).* [L1110, 2019]
- Definitions:
  - MSRP is the manufacturer's suggested retail price.
  - MAP is the minimum advertised price you may display.
  - Wholesale is what you pay the supplier. *Older (2019).* [L0663, 2019]
- For decisions, wholesale and MAP matter most; MSRP matters less. *Older (2019).* [L0960, 2019]
- MSRP is a reference point in the price list, not the price to aim for, because most retailers sell below it. [L1146, 2020-26]
- MSRP is always higher than MAP. [L1115, 2024]
- MSRP and MAP example: MSRP 400, MAP 350, wholesale 175. MAP is shown as the sale price; MSRP is shown struck through. *Older (2022).* [L0665, 2022]
- Example: a studio microphone with MSRP $300 and MAP $250. If the brand lets resellers advertise at MAP, sell it for $250. *Older (2019).* [L0663, 2019]
- Use the supplier's price list. Upload each product at the prices the supplier specifies instead of choosing your own. [L1147, 2020-26]
- Use the supplier's MAP as the listed price on the product page, and calculate margin from that same price. [L0964, 2023]
- Use MAP controls: pick suppliers that set a minimum price so competitors cannot undercut each other to zero margin, and set your prices from the supplier's list. [L1147, 2020-26]
- Price lists may show net price, MAP or IMAP, and wholesale. MAP is the sell price and wholesale is your cost. To confirm MAP, search the product name on Google and check competitor listings. [L0667, 2025]
- If a supplier gives neither MAP nor MSRP, you set your own selling price and check it against competitor prices. *Older (2022).* [L0969, 2022]
- Record MSRP/RRP and MAP together with competitor prices, and whether competitors charge for shipping, to see where your list price sits. *Older.* [L1148, 2021-22]

#### MAP policy in three forms

- A supplier's MAP policy can appear as:
  1. a MAP column in the price list,
  2. a multiple of wholesale, such as 2x,
  3. a maximum discount below max retail price.

  Treat each form as the lowest price you may advertise. *Older (2018).* [L1109, 2018]
- Formula: MAP = wholesale × 2 when the supplier gives a multiplier. Example: $500 wholesale gives MAP $1,000. *Older (2018).* [L1109, 2018]
- Example: max retail price $148 with a 20% discount cap sets the lowest advertised price at $112. *Older (2018).* [L1109, 2018]
  - Check: 20% below $148 is $118.40, not $112. Confirm the exact figure with the supplier before using either number.
- Some lower-tier suppliers do not enforce MAP but still cap discounts at 20% below max retail price. Treat that cap as a MAP rule. *Older (2018).* [L1109, 2018]

### What MAP allows and forbids

#### Definition and minimum

- MAP is the lowest price a retailer may advertise or sell a product for, set by the supplier. Example: a $1,000 MAP on a standing desk means no retailer may list it below $1,000, which removes price competition between sellers. [L0486, 2020-26]
- MAP is the lowest price you may show in your store and in your ads. It is the default price on the product page and the price people see in an ad. *Older? No (2025).* [L0966, 2025]
- MAP sets only a minimum. A product can be listed above MAP but not below it. *Older (2020).* [L1111, 2020]
- MAP sets the lowest listed price. If MAP is $1,000, your listed price must not go below $1,000, the same as every other seller. *Older (2020).* [L0961, 2020]
- *Older (2022).* MAP is the lowest price a product may be advertised at, and the product page price must be at least MAP. [L1113, 2022]
- The price shown on a product page cannot be below MAP, because the "A" in MAP stands for advertised. [L0857, 2025]
- MAP is the lowest price a supplier lets retailers advertise. [L0969, 2022]

#### Allowed

- MAP has no maximum. You may list above MAP, but never advertise or display below MAP on the site or in ads. [L0667, 2025]
- Selling above MAP is permitted. Example: MAP $2,000, sold at $5,000, is allowed under the MAP policy. *Older (2020).* [L0358, 2020]
- Pricing above MAP is rare. It works only where few retailers sell the brand and competitors also price above MAP, which you can see on Google Shopping. *Older (2020).* [L1277, 2020]
- Prices should not be undercut by competitors, because suppliers enforce pricing policies. Work with brands where the margin is locked in. *Older (2020).* [L1277, 2020]
- "Add to Cart to See Price" may replace the price, as long as the lower price is revealed only after the buyer adds the item to the cart. *Older (2020).* [L0961, 2020]
- Lower prices with discount codes at checkout are a practical option unless the supplier agreement says otherwise. [L1285, 2024]
- A price-match guarantee matters only where the products come from MAP-enforcing suppliers. If the suppliers are not MAP suppliers, remove the price-match page from the store menu so shoppers do not see it. *Older (2022).* [L0429, 2022]

#### Forbidden

- Never list a product below MAP on the store, because it is advertised below the minimum advertised price. [L1285, 2024]
- Coupon codes do not change the rule: the advertised price is what draws clicks. *Older (2018).* [L1109, 2018]
- Bonuses count as a price. If the bonus is advertised below MAP, it breaks the policy. Example: a $100 MAP paddle with a bonus paddle advertised at $50 breaks MAP. *Older (2021).* [L0469, 2021]
- A MAP agreement, signed during supplier approval, means you may not advertise or sell below MAP. Every seller of that product must show it at or above MAP. *Older (2019).* [L1110, 2019]
- Once a supplier approves you, do not undercut other sellers. If a competitor's best price is $1,500, sell at $1,500, because price cutting eventually leaves no one with a profit. *Older (2020).* [L0961, 2020]

#### Why MAP exists

- MAP enforcement stops competitors from undercutting your price. Without it, competitors selling below your cost can drain your margin and sales. *Older (2019).* [L1275, 2019]
- Suppliers without MAP let competitors undercut, so avoid their products unless the margin is good. [L0855, 2023]
- Silver suppliers enforce MAP, so you compete only on service and brand, not price. Check that a supplier enforces MAP before you commit to its products. [L0486, 2020-26]
- MAP creates a level playing field among retailers. *Older.* [L0879, 2021-22]

### The 2x spread rule

#### Core rule

- MAP must be at least 2 times wholesale. Example: $500 wholesale needs a MAP of at least $1,000. Wholesale can be no more than 50% of MAP. *Older (2019-2021).* [L0247, 2021; L0468, 2020; L1086, 2019]
- The 2x is an approximation for typical items: MAP should be about 2x wholesale to maintain healthy margins. *Older? No (2025).* [L0255, 2025]
- For products that will receive ad spend, MAP should be at least 2x wholesale. Example: $500 wholesale implies $1,000 MAP. [L0970, 2023]
- List at 2x wholesale as long as the price covers all expenses. Example: $1,500 wholesale listed at $3,000. [L0970, 2023]
- Minimum threshold: MAP must be at least 2x wholesale. If margins are insufficient, negotiate wholesale down rather than selling above MAP, because customers will buy elsewhere at lower prices. [L0251, 2023]
- Minimum rule example: MAP $575 on a $500 product is a 15% markup, which is not worth pursuing. *Older (2019).* [L0960, 2019]
- Example: if MAP is 500, wholesale should be no more than 250. If wholesale is 400, MAP should be at least 800. [L0934, 2023]
- Minimum spread used to decide whether a product is worth carrying: wholesale no more than 50% of MAP. [L0934, 2023]
- Target a MAP about twice the wholesale price, a 100% gap between wholesale and MAP. *Older (2019).* [L0663, 2019]
- Wholesale should be no more than half of MAP. Suppliers above that leave too little margin. *Older (2022).* [L0854, 2022]

#### Spread target and formula

- Spread = (MAP − wholesale) ÷ MAP. The target is about 50%. [L0835, 2023]
- For advertised products, aim for a minimum 50% spread between wholesale and MAP. This leaves room for up to 10% of the price in ad spend, a 2.9% transaction fee, and shipping. [L0970, 2023]
- Example: wholesale $161, MAP $230 gives a spread of about 30%, which is too low. [L0835, 2023]
- Example: wholesale $1,200, MAP $2,000 gives a spread of 40%. It leaves $800 profit per sale, which can be acceptable. [L0835, 2023]
- The 50% target has one accepted exception: a 40% spread on a $2,000 MAP product with a $1,200 wholesale price, because the $800 dollar profit still makes the sale worthwhile. [L0835, 2023]
- Minimum listed price formula: minimum listed price = 2 × wholesale cost (the MAP). Example: 500 wholesale must be listed at no less than 1,000. *Older (2021).* [L1279, 2021]
- The 2x rule is calculated on product cost only, excluding shipping. Example: wholesale $500, MAP $1,000. [L0966, 2025]
- A wholesale of $700 on a $1,000 MAP product is too thin for ads. Rely on free organic and free Google Shopping traffic instead. [L0966, 2025]
- First step when reviewing a product's margin: check the wholesale-to-MAP multiplier. *Older (2021).* [L0671, 2021]
- The ratio can narrow somewhat at higher prices, but a solid spread is still needed to cover shipping, ads and card fees. *Older (2019).* [L1110, 2019]

#### Multiplier below 2x

- A multiplier of about 1.8 can occasionally be acceptable. *Older (2021).* [L0671, 2021]

#### Thresholds at a glance

| Case | Figures | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target | MAP 2x wholesale, spread 50% | Standard for products with ad spend | [L0970, 2023] |
| Near-minimum | MAP about 1.8x wholesale (spread about 44%) | Occasionally acceptable | [L0671, 2021] |
| Exception | Spread 40%, wholesale $1,200, MAP $2,000 ($800 profit) | Can be acceptable | [L0835, 2023] |
| Too low | Wholesale $161, MAP $230 (spread about 30%) | Too low | [L0835, 2023] |
| Markup | MAP $575 on $500 product (15%) | Not worth pursuing | [L0960, 2019] |
| Commission | 20 percent commission | Bad deal | [L1110, 2019] |

## Exclusivity, direct sales and channels

- With true exclusivity, you set the selling price yourself, so MAP does not limit it. Confirm exclusivity in writing before relying on it. *Older (2018).* [L1109, 2018]
- A brand that sells directly to the public is fine for a reseller, as long as the brand lets you advertise at its MAP. *Older (2019).* [L0663, 2019]
- Suppliers may sell direct to the public at MSRP while retailers advertise at MAP. Check the supplier's MSRP and MAP before deciding whether the brand's margin works. *Older (2019).* [L1086, 2019]
- A supplier's own website may sell at MSRP while your store sells at MAP. That is acceptable because you set your own price. Check the supplier's retail price before listing so your store is not underpriced by mistake. *Older (2020).* [L0468, 2020]
- A direct-to-consumer supplier can be carried only if it sells at MSRP and lets retailers sell at MAP, or if its direct sales effort is weak, for example no ads and an outdated website. [L0857, 2025]
- A supplier that lists a product below MAP on its own website while others sell at MAP:
  - If your MAP is lower than their website price, the product may still be worth selling.
  - If they sell below the MAP you intend to use, skip the product entirely. [L0251, 2023]
- Some brands sell directly to consumers and to online retailers at different prices. Brands may give online retailers incentives, so verify pricing with the supplier. [L0940, 2020-26]
- Online retailers often receive better wholesale prices than large box retail chains, which makes online sellers a preferred channel when comparing supplier pricing. [L0940, 2020-26]
- A brand selling on Amazon does not disqualify it for your store, provided your listed prices follow the supplier's price sheet and pricing policy. *Older (2021).* [L0834, 2021]

## MAP outside the United States

- MAP policies cannot be legally enforced in the EU, Australia or New Zealand. Brands there often still require retailers to sign an agreement not to list below a set price, and enforce it through the account relationship. *Older (2021).* [L0834, 2021]

### Supplier pricing with little or no margin

#### Warning signs

- Avoid suppliers offering only 15% off MAP. After transaction fees (2.9%), shipping and ad spend, these are nearly impossible to sell profitably. *Older (2022).* [L0249, 2022]
- A 20% supplier discount on a $1,000 product makes MAP $1,000 with insufficient margin. *Older (2020).* [L0268, 2020]
- A supplier offering 50% off MSRP while MAP is 25% off MSRP leaves too little margin. The deal works only if competitors sell at MSRP. *Older (2021).* [L0671, 2021]
- A supplier whose wholesale is above half of MAP leaves too little room for margin. *Older (2022).* [L0854, 2022]
- A 20 percent commission is a bad deal. *Older (2019).* [L1110, 2019]

#### Options when margin is thin

1. Check the multiplier and spread against the thresholds above. [L0671, 2021; L0970, 2023]
2. If the spread is 40% and the dollar profit is strong, the exception may apply. [L0835, 2023]
3. If margin is insufficient, negotiate wholesale down. Do not sell above MAP to compensate, because customers will buy elsewhere at lower prices. [L0251, 2023]
4. If the product is listed above MAP, it must go without paid traffic: list at a higher price and use organic or remarketing only. Example: a $1,000 product with only 20% discount is listed at $1,600 with no paid traffic. *Older (2020).* [L0268, 2020]
5. Above-MAP pricing works where few retailers sell the brand and competitors also price above MAP. Check Google Shopping before you rely on it. *Older (2020).* [L1277, 2020]
6. Without MAP, the retailer sets its own price and must check it against competitor prices. [L0969, 2022]
7. Products without MAP enforcement are avoided unless the margin is good. [L0855, 2023]

#### Negotiation email

Use this template when competitors are undercutting you and the supplier's terms leave too little margin:

```
We noticed that [competitor name] is selling [product name] at [competitor price]. At that price, there is not enough margin for us to drive traffic profitably. Do you offer different pricing tiers, incentives or wholesale discounts for a retailer in our position?
```

[L0966, 2025]

### Competitors selling below MAP

#### Monitoring

- Monitor competitors for MAP compliance:
  1. Check their current discounts for MAP violations.
  2. Report any violation as soon as you find it. [L0920, 2018]
- Monitor for prices below your MAP, including product-specific discounts that can push a competitor under MAP. Report violations quickly. A competitor who undercuts for a long time can build a war chest and outbid you in paid advertising. [L1357, 2018]
- Minimum advertised price policies protect your margin. Monitor competitor prices for your products, and report violations to the manufacturer to enforce the policy. [L1472, 2022]

#### Checking whether the supplier enforces MAP

1. Pick a specific product from the supplier.
2. Search its name on Google and open the Google Shopping results.
3. Compare prices across sellers and confirm the listings are the identical product.
4. If the same product sells much cheaper at some sellers, MAP is likely not enforced.

*Older (2021).* [L0968, 2021]

#### Do not guess: ask the supplier

- When a competitor sells a supplier's products below the MAP price sheet, ask the supplier's sales rep rather than guessing. Send a screenshot of the competitor's listing and the supplier's price list, and ask whether the price list is wrong. *Older (2021).* [L0801, 2021]
- Email to a supplier sales rep:

```
Email to a supplier sales rep about a competitor selling below MAP:

Hi [sales rep name], I am seeing [competitor store name] selling [product name] for [competitor price], which is below the MAP price on your current price list. I have attached a screenshot of their listing and a screenshot of your price list. Do I have an incorrect price list, and could you confirm the correct pricing for this product?
```

*Older (2021).* [L0801, 2021]

#### Reporting a violation

- Report a MAP violation to the supplier immediately by emailing your contact with a screenshot of the violating price. *Older (2021).* [L0968, 2021]
- Full sequence when a supplier breaks MAP:
  1. Take a screenshot of the Google Shopping result showing the lower price.
  2. Take a screenshot of their website showing the price.
  3. Email the supplier with the proof.
  4. Ask them to reach out to the retailer to adjust price per policy.
  5. Give the retailer a few days to a week to fix it.
  6. If they don't comply, the supplier should remove them from the approval list.

  *Older (2022).* [L0272, 2022]
- Shorter sequence for reporting a competitor who violates MAP:
  1. Email your sales rep or supplier contact right away.
  2. Include a screenshot of the violation and the link to the page showing the lower price.
  3. Ask the supplier to act on the violation.

  *Older (2019).* [L0357, 2019]
- Another sequence:
  1. Take a screenshot of the competitor listing that sells below MAP.
  2. Send it to your supplier contact, naming the competitor's domain and stating it violates MAP policy.
  3. Ask the supplier to have it resolved.

  *Older (2021).* [L1279, 2021]

#### Google Shopping and price gaps

- Do not raise bids to fight a large price gap. MAP exists to level the field, and lowering your bids cannot close a price gap in Google Shopping, a price comparison engine. *Older.* [L0879, 2021-22]
- Competing for top positions with a large gap wastes money. Shoppers still see the cheaper price and buy elsewhere. Clicks come from people checking the full price, but few convert. *Older.* [L0879, 2021-22]
