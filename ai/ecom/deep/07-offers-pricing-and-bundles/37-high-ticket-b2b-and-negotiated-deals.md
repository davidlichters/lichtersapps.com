## High-ticket, B2B and negotiated deals

### Deciding whether high-ticket and B2B fits your store

- High-ticket products are judged by the margin left after freight, returns and damage. A higher unit price alone does not guarantee profit. [L0660, 2019+]
- Above roughly 90 to 100 USD in the US market, the store, ads and offer must build trust before the sale, because buyers research reviews and search the brand before purchasing at this price. [L0693, 2024]
- A high-ticket product with healthy margin leaves room for a deeper discount when one is needed to close a larger deal, but discounts should never reduce the sale below a worthwhile profit. [L1068, 2019]
- Bulk orders from businesses can be given a larger discount and accepted at a lower margin, since total profit still stays high. [L1277, 2020]
- Lead-generation example: mobile-only lead generation worked up to about $997 per offer, and a $30,000 program converted through mobile leads. The advertiser found no limit on mobile above that point, and desktop was not required. [L0643, 2015]

### Concierge, VIP and trade access

- A concierge or VIP service can be your USP, especially in business-to-business niches. Customers placing very large orders value personal contact and custom quotes, and such orders can range from $10,000 to $100,000 or more. [L0413, 2016]
- Pattern: tell visitors on the site that a real person is available to talk, then provide custom quotes and work with them directly on large orders. This fits B2B niches, where single orders can reach $10,000, $50,000 or $100,000. [L0413, 2016]
- Create a separate trade or business customer page on the store, because business buyers often want bulk discounts and negotiation. Be ready to offer custom deals. [L0801, 2021]
- Show a phone number on the trade page so businesses can call directly. Discounts are based on order volume, and custom quotes are available. [L1088, 2020]

### Trade request procedures

**Procedure A: trade request with supplier quote** [L0834, 2021]

1. Add a trade or business-to-business form page to your store. [L0834, 2021]
2. Collect the customer's name, email, phone number, company name, products of interest, quantity, and the date they need the order by. [L0834, 2021]
3. Request a quote from the supplier for that exact quantity, delivery location and date. [L0834, 2021]
4. Pass the supplier's quoted price to the customer. Do not promise a trade discount upfront, because it varies from order to order. [L0834, 2021]

**Procedure B: trade pricing instead of a fixed coupon** [L0968, 2021]

1. Create a page on the store aimed at trade buyers, not at a product. [L0968, 2021]
2. List known business clients on the page. [L0968, 2021]
3. Add a contact form asking for name, phone, email, products of interest, units per product and purchase timeline. [L0968, 2021]
4. When a request arrives, ask your supplier for the best wholesale price on those quantities, then send the buyer a quote. [L0968, 2021]

**Example**

- A trade buyer asks for 10 units of item one and 10 units of item two. Ask the supplier for the best wholesale price on exactly that order, and quote the buyer a price for that order only. [L0968, 2021]

**Procedure C: trade page with volume-based discounts** [L1088, 2020]

1. Create a page for business customers and link it from the footer menu. [L1088, 2020]
2. Explain that discounts are based on order volume and that custom quotes are available. [L1088, 2020]
3. Show a phone number so businesses can call directly. [L1088, 2020]
4. Embed a contact form that asks for name, company name, phone number, email, products of interest and quantities. [L1088, 2020]

### Quoting prices to prospects

- Discuss price and delivery terms only after you know the prospect wants what you sell, subject to other issues being worked out. Confirm the product fit first, then address price, and do not let price questions replace the discovery process. [L0770, undated]
- Budgetary quotes are rough estimates based on expected production quantity, order size and other variables that often change before ordering. They can be prepared with little effort for a prospect who needs early pricing, but first evaluate the probability of winning his business. [L0770, undated]
- If the product meets the prospect's criteria, do not give a precise price for every quantity or option before he has committed to buy. Detailed quotes take the estimating team's time and rarely pay off when the prospect has not committed. [L0770, undated]

**Net saving formula for a new packaging or process**

- Labor savings + storage savings = maximum extra material cost the prospect will accept.
- If the material price stays below that amount, the net overall cost is lower and he can commit to an order. [L0770, undated]

**Example: concrete price test**

- A prospect wants extra advertising value on the outer packaging. The packaging costs about one dollar more per unit. Ask whether he will buy if the net cost stays in line with his current spending. This commitment is needed before any numbers are prepared. [L0770, undated]

### Volume-based business discounts

- Business discounts should depend on order volume. Very large orders, such as those over 50,000, can justify deeper incentives than orders around 2,000. [L1088, 2020]
- Do not give a large discount upfront in B2B deals. Start from your default discount and negotiate from there, with the size depending on margin and order size. [L1109, 2018]
- Example: one large B2B order of about $66,000 to $67,000 received a 15% discount after negotiation. A few orders of this size per year can add significant revenue and profit. [L1109, 2018]
- B2B and bulk-buyer discounts can use any agreed percentage, such as 2%, 5%, 10% or 12%, set per negotiated deal. The 12% level was used as the example for a large order. [L1322, 2020-26]
- Discounts shown on the standard income line do not include custom quotes made through draft orders. Large stores with bulk buyers may give steep negotiated discounts that never appear in the discount line, so check draft orders separately. [L1046, 2021-22]

### Personalised discount codes for business buyers

- A reusable discount code named after the business and given only to that business suits an ongoing account, such as an interior designer who buys for clients. (2025.) [L0472, 2025]
- A code limited to one use in total suits a unique offer to a single buyer, because nobody else can redeem it once used. This protects the agreed price from being shared or reused. [L1322, 2020-26]

**Procedure: personalised discount for one B2B buyer** [L1322, 2020-26]

1. Click Create discount and choose Amount off order. [L1322, 2020-26]
2. Name the discount code after the buyer, for example the buyer's first name. [L1322, 2020-26]
3. Set the value type to percentage and enter the agreed rate, for example 12%. [L1322, 2020-26]
4. Set maximum discount uses to once per customer. [L1322, 2020-26]
5. Under customer eligibility, choose specific customers and enter the buyer's email address. [L1322, 2020-26]
6. Set the end date to 24 hours from now. [L1322, 2020-26]
7. Click save discount and send the code to the buyer. [L1322, 2020-26]

**Template: personal discount email** [L1322, 2020-26]

```
Hi [CUSTOMER NAME],

I have created a personal discount code for you: [DISCOUNT CODE]. You can apply it at checkout on our store to get [DISCOUNT PERCENTAGE]% off your order.

This discount is valid until [END DATE AND TIME, TIME ZONE], so please place your order before then.

If you have any questions, just reply to this message.
```

### Deposits for custom and made-to-order products

- Taking a deposit on a custom-built or made-to-order product is common practice, similar to construction or home building. Deposit size matters. A large deposit, such as 50% or 60%, may discourage buyers because the deposit is non-refundable and they fear losing it. A small deposit, such as 20%, lowers the barrier to committing but raises the chance that customers back out, leaving the store with a built product. [L0721, 2021-22]
- State clearly that deposits on custom orders are non-refundable, in the checkout or product wording, before the order starts building. [L0721, 2021-22]
- Test the deposit model on the store by offering a few options and measuring which gets more interest, rather than relying on one theory. Options include split payment, pre-order and deposit schemes. Apps exist for pre-selling and pre-ordering. [L0721, 2021-22]

### Negotiated offers and make-an-offer promotions

**Make an Offer app on the store**

- Procedure: [L1204, 2022]
  1. Install the Shopify app called Make an Offer. [L1204, 2022]
  2. Set the promotion to run for a fixed window, for example three days. [L1204, 2022]
  3. A Make an Offer button appears under Add to Cart. [L1204, 2022]
  4. The shopper chooses an offer price and enters an email address and phone number. [L1204, 2022]
  5. You accept the offer, counter with a higher price, or decline it. You are never forced to accept. [L1204, 2022]
- Example on a 3,000 dollar product: offers within about 10 percent below the list price were accepted, while offers around 20 percent below were countered. Expect offers at both levels when you run this. [L1204, 2022]

**Make-offer promotion by email for a few days per month**

- Procedure: [L1205, 2020-26]
  1. Email the list and run Facebook, Instagram and Google remarketing ads to long-standing audience members, announcing that offers are accepted for a stated number of days. [L1205, 2020-26]
  2. Customers reply to the email or send a private message on Facebook with offers on specific products. [L1205, 2020-26]
  3. Accept, counter or decline each offer, since no offer has to be accepted. [L1205, 2020-26]
  4. Shopify App Store apps can add a make-an-offer button, but manual handling is recommended for the first attempt. [L1205, 2020-26]
- Mistake: the make-offer approach is time intensive because someone must respond to every offer. It can still produce many extra sales, so the team needs capacity to answer offers during the promotion window. [L1205, 2020-26]
- A make-an-offer or name-your-price email invites subscribers to propose a price, and you accept or counter each reply. This tactic runs only through email, not ads, and it is risky for large lists. [L1224, 2025+]

**Make-an-offer email procedure (2025)**

1. Send an email to the list inviting customers to make an offer or name their price on a product they want. [L1287, 2025]
2. Ask them which products they are interested in and how much they would pay. [L1287, 2025]
3. Accept offers that are fair and reply to the rest with a counter price. [L1287, 2025]
4. Use this for promotions that are not public, since it carries risk if the list is large. [L1287, 2025]
- Mistake: a site-wide make-an-offer button was removed after it caused problems with Google. Keep make-an-offer offers in email instead of a public button on the store. [L1287, 2025]

### Dealers, free goods and referral credit

- Avoid extra discounts or loading offers to dealers, because they often shift sales between stores rather than adding total demand. Spend that budget on acquiring new customers instead. [L1261, undated]
- Free goods given to dealers must be sold with advertising. Each free case means the ads must sell proportionally more to earn the same return. Figure the cost of free goods at your selling price before offering them. [L1261, undated]

**Template: referral credit letter for an existing customer** [L1261, undated]

```
Dear [CUSTOMER_FIRST_NAME], [REFERRER_NAME] tells us that you are one of her good customers. She says you pay as agreed, so we have opened a credit account for you with us, ready for use at any time. When you want anything from our range, just place your order and pay nothing in advance. We are glad to send it without any further investigation, since you were recommended to us in this way.
```

- Keep the recommendation as the reason for the account, and adjust the names and offer to match your product and terms. [L1261, undated]

### Where sources differ

- **Site-wide make-offer button:** an app can place a Make an Offer button under Add to Cart for a fixed window, such as three days. [L1204, 2022] A later source reports that a site-wide button was removed after it caused problems with Google, and that offers should run through email. [L1287, 2025; L1224, 2025+] Use the app only for a short, monitored window. Use email for the default channel. [L1205, 2020-26; L1287, 2025]
- **Discount code use limit:** a reusable code named after the business suits an ongoing account. [L0472, 2025] A single-use code suits a unique one-off offer. [L1322, 2020-26] The personal-code procedure sets the limit to once per customer, which is a per-person limit rather than a total limit. [L1322, 2020-26]
- **Upfront discount size:** a large discount should not be given upfront. [L1109, 2018] Depth of discount can rise when margin and order size support it, and can be used to close a larger deal. [L1068, 2019; L1277, 2020] Trade buyers should not be promised a trade discount before the supplier quote is known. [L0834, 2021]
- **Margin:** high-ticket products are judged by the margin left after all costs. [L0660, 2019+] Bulk orders may be accepted at a lower margin only when total profit still stays high. [L1277, 2020]
- **Trade request data:** Procedure A asks for quantity, delivery location and date, then requests a supplier quote for that exact order. [L0834, 2021] Procedure B asks for products, units per product and purchase timeline, then asks the supplier for the best wholesale price on those quantities. [L0968, 2021] Use the fuller data set for large orders where delivery terms affect the supplier price.
