## Promotion calendar, seasonal sales and Q4

### Offer arsenal and monthly rotation

- Build an offer arsenal: a list of at least four offers you can pull from whenever you need one. The list is the pool you rotate through over the year. [L0757, 2022]
- Brainstorm ideas without pricing them first, then check each idea's cost and margin before scheduling it. Keep the list written down so you can reuse, adjust and pull from it each month. [L1204, 2022]
- A monthly offer-brainstorming exercise can produce around 12 unique offers, more than the five needed for Q4. Keep a running list so you always have spares. [L1224, 2025+; L1287, 2025]
- Plan Q4 offers in this order:
  1. Brainstorm about 12 possible promotions. [L1285, 2024; L1223, 2024+]
  2. Select five and assign one to each holiday. [L1285, 2024; L1223, 2024+]
  3. Record the offer, eligibility and theme for each promotion in an offer calendar planned a year in advance. [L1285, 2024]
  4. For each promotion, check the historical best-performing email subject lines and ad headlines, ranked by opens, clicks and sales, and pick those that fit the brand. [L1223, 2024+]
  5. Use the same message in the email subject lines and in the remarketing ad headlines. [L1223, 2024+]
  6. Set up the email segment and the remarketing audiences, then launch. [L1223, 2024+]
- Promote one offer per month. Offers can be recycled and reused throughout the year. [L0757, 2022]
- Run only one offer per month. Proven offers can be recycled every quarter or every six months, ideally with a new twist, but not repeated every month. [L1205, 2020-26]
- ** Rotate offers monthly rather than repeating the same promotion. Two promotions in the same month, such as during November, are not too much. [L1088, 2020]
- ** Rotate the type of promotion each month, such as a bundle, a fixed percentage off, or something else, instead of repeating one offer. [L1281, 2022]
- Vary promotions across the year instead of repeating the same discount on each holiday. Choose the deal, such as a free gift, a free item, or a buy-one-get-one-free offer tied to a supplier deal, based on what the target customer wants. [L1076, 2019]
- Monthly promotions usually run for three to five days, announced to the email list and on social media. [L1281, 2022]
- Each month can follow a fixed theme. Example rotating offer calendar, one offer per month and reused across the year:

| Month | Offer |
|---|---|
| September | 10% off |
| October | Free white glove delivery |
| November | Bonus gift with all orders over $1,000 |

- Four promotion types exist: monetary promotions, bundles, physical product bonuses and digital product bonuses. Each is used to turn existing leads who have not bought into customers. [L1205, 2020-26]
- A sale or promotion section can be linked in the header and refreshed about once a month. Sales are allowed when a supplier is discontinuing a product or when a promotion bundles extra items. [L1281, 2022]
- Sales mainly during Black Friday, with otherwise selling out each collection before moving to the next. Do not discount constantly. [L1438, 2023+]
- Sources differ on how many sales a year to run. Monthly promotions are advised in the rotation rules above [L0334, 2017; L1094, 2023; L1205, 2020-26]. Sell-out-by-collection with Black Friday as the main sale is advised in [L1438, 2023+]. Use the monthly rhythm for a rotating offer arsenal, and the collection rhythm for a range sold in collections.

### Offer types and gift structures

- Percent-off offers are the simplest monthly promotion. Raise your usual coupon for a short window, for example from 5 percent to 10 percent off all orders. [L1204, 2022]
- Fixed-amount offers work best on higher-priced products, for example save 50 dollars on all orders over 500 dollars. [L1204, 2022]
- Free expedited shipping works well in November and December, when customers worry about delivery before the holidays. Promote a cutoff time, for example 5 p.m. for same-day or 24-hour dispatch, or a supplier ship-by date about a week before Christmas. It can be run for only part of the catalog if the supplier does not offer it store-wide. [L1204, 2022]
- Buy-one-get-one-free offers work especially well in November around Black Friday and Cyber Monday. Shoppers buy extra units as gifts, or buy two and keep one and give one away. [L1205, 2020-26]
- A bundle promotion can run for roughly one week within a chosen month, not the whole month. Example: a golf bag sold at $400 with a $100 carrier upgrade. A single $500 bundle that adds golf balls, a glove and a cleaning kit is a plausible one-week offer. [L1205, 2020-26]
- A bundle can include a free gift and a free ebook download, shown clearly in the ad so the customer sees the deal immediately. [L0692, 2023]
- Choice offer: the customer picks one of two free gifts. It keeps a product offer fresh and can rescue results when the old offer is worn out. Examples: "Pick your free gift: a travel pouch or a bottle of cleaner with any order." and "Choose one free bonus: an extra case or a matching cable with your purchase." [L0950, 2023]
- Seasonal relabelling: keep the same discount and shipping offer, but rename it for the season, such as Black Friday or Cyber Monday, to make it look new. Examples: 10% off plus free shipping for Black Friday; 10% off plus free shipping for Cyber Monday week. [L1085, 2018] *Older advice (2018).*
- Tie promotions to current events, holidays or seasonal moments where possible. The more timely and relevant the promotion, the more interest and urgency it creates and the more likely older leads will convert. [L1205, 2020-26]
- Any seasonal reason can justify a discount event, including Halloween, a spring sale or a holiday. Short, recurring sales give buyers a reason to act now. Keep the offer clear and time-limited so the reason to buy stays believable. [L1363, 2023]
- Non-discount promotions can compete with large retailers, for example free white glove shipping, free expedited delivery for holiday timing, a raffle entry, or a bonus gift valued at around 100 dollars. [L1113, 2022] *Older advice (2022).*
- Gift-with-purchase offers work during the holidays because the buyer can keep the gift or give it to someone else. The perceived value of the gift is often higher than its cost to the store. [L0467, 2019] *Older advice (2019).*

**Gift-with-purchase formula for expensive products** **
- Pattern: a percentage discount combined with a free gift above a spend threshold.
- Formula: Save [X]% and get a free [gift] on orders over $[threshold].
- Example 1: Save 10% and get a free gift box on orders over $500.
- Example 2: Save 15% and get a free set of RGB lights on orders over $1,500.
- It works because the discount keeps the price appealing, the gift adds value the buyer can also give to someone else, and the threshold raises the average order. [L0467, 2019]
- Case: a store with high-margin products ran a holiday promotion giving a discount plus a free gift. The gifts included an $80 item and a set of RGB lights costing the store about $50. The RGB lights offer was designed to push orders toward roughly $2,000 per customer. [L0467, 2019]

**Free gift threshold** **
- Pattern: a percentage discount plus a free gift on orders above a set amount, used only in peak sales periods by stores that already know their numbers.
- The gift should be worth about $50 to $100 and be something the buyer can keep or give away.
- Example 1: 10% off plus a free $80 gadget on orders over $1,000.
- Example 2: 15% off plus a free $60 accessory on orders over $500. [L1085, 2018]
- Use it only where the store knows its average order value and margin, and only around holidays and sale days, not all year. Set the threshold above the average order value so the gift is affordable. [L1085, 2018]
- Gift-friendly offers can sell products that would not normally be bought as gifts. During holiday shopping, buyers are already choosing presents for family, so a free gift removes one decision and pushes them toward your store. [L1085, 2018]
- *Current figures (2024):* A low-cost gift unrelated to the product, sent to buyers before the holiday deadline, can drive sales when inventory is limited. The gift must be worth more to the customer than its cost of about $80 shipped. [L1285, 2024]

### Recycling offers

- Recycle proven offers two to three times a year, or every quarter or six months, rather than always creating new ones. Repeat the offers that keep converting with the audience. [L1204, 2022]
- Sources differ on frequency. [L1204, 2022] says two to three times a year; [L1205, 2020-26] says every quarter or every six months, with a new twist; [L1404, 2025-26] says cycle offer types at most about once a year, and a BOGO that performed strongly at launch can fail on a repeat run. Use the once-a-year limit for offer types that have already faded.
- Over 90% of visitors to a dropship store are first-time visitors who came from a paid ad for a specific product. Because the audience is new each time, the same offer can be recycled very frequently without losing effect. [L1085, 2018] *Older advice (2018).*
- An offer that was used before can still work if it is packaged as a Black Friday or Cyber Monday deal. [L1223, 2024+]
- Vary offer types and cycle them at most about once a year. [L1404, 2025-26]
- Repeated offers stop converting after a few weeks. Use a different offer for each promotion instead of the same discount, such as 5% off. [L1223, 2024+]
- Repeating a 5 percent offer five times gets fewer responses because customers have already seen it. [L1285, 2024]
- Repeating the same discount on each promotion trains the audience to ignore it. Plan the offers in advance so none is recycled within the same quarter. [L1224, 2025+]
- A holiday promotion using a repeated discount with a holiday name, such as 10 percent off with a holiday name, loses impact after the first use. [L1287, 2025]
- Each promotion in the quarter needs its own unique offer. Thanksgiving has no separate offer when it runs together with Black Friday. [L1222, 2025+]
- Keep the offer you already run, such as free shipping, 5% off or 10% off, but change the coupon code to a holiday-related word. Announce the holiday promotion on Facebook and send the same promotion to your email list. Holidays are a sales opportunity and not a reason to pause making money. [L0357, 2019] *Older advice (2019).*

### Audiences for promotions

- A promotion works best when it is shown to a warm audience that has engaged for months, because the goodwill makes the offer feel natural. A buy-one-get-one or free-add-on promotion timed to a holiday converts far better in front of that group than cold. Examples in the same pattern: "Order a set of two candles this week and get a third free for the holidays" and "Buy the starter brewing kit now and get the seasonal ingredient pack free." [L0487, 2018]
- Time product offers to promotions and seasonal peaks such as summer, Black Friday and Christmas. Keep building the audience between them. Regular content can mention products with a link in the description without being a sales video. [L0487, 2018]
- Wait before making offers to a new audience so it can grow and trust the brand. The example suggested about six weeks. Making offers right away is possible if resources exist to sell and grow at the same time. [L0487, 2018]
- Promotions to move less popular products or seasonal items work best when sent to warm audiences such as your email list and remarketing audiences of past visitors. New visitors from cold traffic usually buy what they searched for, regardless of promotion. [L0801, 2021] *Older advice (2021).*
- Example warm audience size: around 2,000 people on your email list and around 10,000 people in a remarketing audience. Push promotions to those groups first rather than to new visitors. [L0801, 2021] *Older advice (2021).*
- Run a discount or flash sale every month, targeted at one segment of the email list. Revenue was higher in every month with a significant discount, so brands that fear discounting for premium image should test it. Online competition for attention and inbox space makes a sale a strong tool. [L0334, 2017]
- Sales event opt-ins can stop visitors from buying immediately, because they wait for the event. Run them close to the event rather than too far in advance to limit that effect. [L0571, 2021-22] *Older advice.*
- Run giveaways before Black Friday. This builds early interest at low CPM, and the engaged audience can then be converted during Black Friday. [L0797, 2025-26]
- Source conflict on channel priority: for Black Friday and Cyber Monday in the first month of a new store with a small email list, storefront promotions matter more than email campaigns. Make the store's promotions match the holiday, and add them once the first suppliers are live. [L1116, 2025] Warm-audience targeting in [L0801, 2021] applies to older stock and promotions aimed at existing buyers.
- Promotions aimed at the store's existing buyers did not move slower products in one 2021 case. Results were mediocre, and select brands on sale up to 20 percent off sold few units, while regular best sellers sold more. [L0801, 2021] *Older advice (2021).*
- Case, 2021: a store ran a tiered Black Friday promotion spending 400 dollars for 15 percent off, 800 dollars for 20 percent off and 1,200 dollars for 25 percent off, with average order value around 350 dollars. The next month without promotions still produced more sales than December. [L0801, 2021] *Older advice (2021).*

### Measuring and tuning promotions

- Store promotions perform differently from one another and affect monthly revenue, so plan a promotion calendar and track results per promotion. Use the best-performing promotions as a baseline for future months. [L0807, 2025]
- Coupon codes on the store were valid on Wednesdays and Sundays, chosen because traffic is similar each day. The host did not split-test the days. [L0804, 2023]
- Use coupon codes tied to the holiday season, such as Black Friday, Cyber Monday or Xmas. Promote them in email, on social media pinned posts and in cover images. [L1111, 2020] *Older advice (2020).*

### Promotion duration and timing rules

- Each offer promotion should run 3 to 5 days. Use the shorter end when quantity is limited, and the longer end when you need more hype or when the purchase is a large decision for the audience. [L0757, 2022]
- Each promotion runs for 3 to 5 days, and the rest of the month the normal percentage discount applies. Example: a promotion may run from January 1 to January 5 only. [L1205, 2020-26]
- Use shorter promotions when quantity is limited. Example: when a supplier allows only 20 units, the offer runs for three days or until the 20 units sell out, whichever comes first. [L1205, 2020-26]
- Use a five-day promotion for big purchase decisions, high-ticket items or when more hype needs to be built. [L1205, 2020-26]
- For larger purchases, send one pre-promotion email before the offer starts to tell the audience something big is coming and prepare them for it. [L0757, 2022]
- Monthly promotions of 3 to 5 days can be run, tied to a holiday or event relevant to the niche. Promotions are typically used with promo codes rather than price changes. [L1094, 2023]
- A promotion with a real deadline, such as a holiday, creates urgency. It can run for a single day or through a weekend, and it should be promoted with a sale announcement on the website and social media. [L0837, 2025]
- A 3-day Halloween promotion example: October 29 to October 31, 2024, with 10% off the whole store using the coupon code "happyhalloween", and one promotional email each day of the sale window. [L1105, 2024]
- A holiday promotion can be run as a short fixed window, such as Monday to Friday of one week, with a percentage-off discount code for all customers. Set the start and end dates before writing any promotional copy. [L1286, 2025]
- Source conflict on length: a 3-to-5-day window is standard; a five-day window is for big-ticket items [L1205, 2020-26]; a week-long window is used for Black Friday [L1115, 2024; L1224, 2025+]; a one-day window is used for Cyber Monday [L1224, 2025+; L1287, 2025]; a 48-hour window is used for flash sales [L1400, undated]. Each applies to its own case.
- Weekday rule: end offers on a weekday, and use Friday for the more expensive offers. Tie the promotion to current events or holidays where you can. [L0757, 2022]
- Plan promotions at least three months ahead, covering Halloween, Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year, with one specific offer per event. [L1113, 2022] *Older advice (2022).*
- Holiday promotion routine, from 2019, applies when you have no unique offer ready: rename the existing discount code, and keep the same offer under a holiday code. [L0357, 2019] *Older advice (2019).*
- A holiday promotion can still be worthwhile when planned only about one week ahead. In that case, a simple change such as renaming the existing discount code to a holiday word is enough, while a unique offer requires more lead time. [L0623, 2020-26]
- A holiday promotion can be assembled, configured, announced to your email list and posted to social media in approximately one hour. A discount of 5% or 10% paired with a holiday-specific code (for example "MemorialDay" instead of a generic discount) has measurable impact even with a small audience; converting one or two additional sales justifies the effort. [L0294, 2025+]

### Holiday planning for the US calendar

- U.S. holidays such as Memorial Day and the 4th of July, and monthly occasions, provide recurring promotional triggers throughout the year. [L0294, 2025+]
- A national holiday such as the 4th of July is a reason to run a special promotion that differs from everyday discounts. This applies only to stores that sell to customers in the US. [L0623, 2020-26]
- If there is no time for a unique holiday offer, the fastest starting point is renaming the existing discount code to a holiday word such as "July 4th", "freedom" or "fireworks". Set that code to expire at the end of the promotion window. [L0623, 2020-26]
- A holiday promotion in the lesson ran for five days, from Monday 1 July to Friday 5 July, with the code expiring at midnight on the final day. Normal promotion codes in the same business typically ended on Wednesday or Sunday. [L0623, 2020-26]
- 4th of July promotion plan:
  1. Create a holiday-themed promotion, or rename the existing discount code to a holiday word such as "July 4th", "freedom" or "fireworks". [L0623, 2020-26]
  2. Set the code to expire at the end of the five-day window, Friday 5 July in the example. [L0623, 2020-26]
  3. Show the code on the store and promote it to new visitors coming from ads. [L0623, 2020-26]
  4. Send promotion emails to the existing email list on every day of the window, from Monday to Friday. [L0623, 2020-26]
  5. Run static holiday remarketing ads to older website visitor audiences. [L0623, 2020-26]
  6. Run dynamic product ads to recent visitors who have not bought, excluding recent customers. [L0623, 2020-26]
- Start holiday promotions weeks early, since suppliers get busy, some close, and shipping carriers are backed up in the peak shipping period before Christmas. Holiday offers should state the gift benefit clearly, such as a Christmas gift or a holiday promotion. [L0467, 2019] *Older advice (2019).*
- Holiday promotions were run on all dropshipping stores, so promotional calendars should be planned per store. [L1002, 2019] *Older advice (2019).*
- Holiday-specific bundles can be promoted earlier than the holiday, for example during Black Friday or Cyber Monday, since many people shop for gifts early. [L1285, 2024]
- Holiday bundles or special products do not need to wait until the holiday itself to be promoted. Testing them earlier, for example during Black Friday or Cyber Monday, is worth doing because many people shop for Christmas during those events. [L1223, 2024+]
