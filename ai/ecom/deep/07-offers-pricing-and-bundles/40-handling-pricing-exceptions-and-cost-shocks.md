## Handling pricing exceptions and cost shocks

### Price Changes and Supplier Cost Increases

#### Tariffs and Wholesale Increases

- Tariffs can raise both the wholesale cost and the MAP. The margin per sale can stay the same, but a sudden large price jump can make customers stop buying (sticker shock). [L0626, 2025+]
- MAP is usually a multiple of the supplier's wholesale cost. When wholesale rises, MAP rises, and every seller of that product raises prices. [L0972, 2025]
- Rising supplier wholesale prices also raise MAP. A wider spread between wholesale and MAP helps cover extra costs, so cost increases should be passed on to every supplier's products at once. ** [L1092, 2022]
- When supplier prices rise, MAP rises too, which widens the margin. Update your prices right away when a supplier changes them, push the ad return, add paid upsells such as expedited or white glove shipping, and put more ad spend behind the high-margin products. ** [L1113, 2022]
- As an alternative to showing tariffs separately, add a percentage of the expected tariff cost into the sale price. [L1116, 2025]
- When a supplier publishes a new MAP policy with updated prices, such as a tariff-related price change, update the listed prices to match the new MAP. [L0857, 2025]

#### Adjusting Only the Affected Suppliers

1. Before raising prices, identify the suppliers where the gap between your price and wholesale price is too small to cover shipping. [L0801, 2021]
2. Adjust only those suppliers' prices instead of every product on the site, since most suppliers leave enough margin. [L0801, 2021]

#### What Not to Do Under Shipping Cost Pressure

- Do not respond to shipping cost pressure by raising prices above MAP, adding a large shipping fee when competitors do not, or dropping the brand first. Doing so can make the products unsellable, and money spent on their ads is then wasted. [L0852, 2020]

#### Worked Example: Free Shipping and Margin

- Free shipping means higher carrier rates are paid from the product margin, so margins shrink even when the selling price stays the same. Waiting for suppliers to raise their wholesale prices only restores the gap in part. [L0942, 2022]

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Wholesale before increase | $500 |
| Wholesale after increase | $600 |
| MAP (stays at 2x) | $1,200 |
| Left to work with | $600 |
| Shipping cost (at least) | $100 |
| Effective margin | about $500 (same as before the increase) |

[L0942, 2022]

### Supplier Dependence

#### Concentration Risk

- A store that made about 80 percent of its sales from one main supplier faced a supplier that raised sale prices for shipping cost increases, cut the store's margin from roughly 20 percent to about 10 percent, and started selling directly. [L1456, 2021-22]
- The response discussed was:
  1. Find additional suppliers over time.
  2. Lower Google Ads bids as an interim measure, while still aiming for at least break-even sales.
  3. Build bundles on low-margin products. [L1456, 2021-22]

#### Stock Shortages and Substitution

- Substitute an out-of-stock item with a similar, cheaper item that is always in stock when the original is on backorder, and tell the customer about the lower price. This can raise margin when the substitute brand is less well known but customers accept it. [L1076, 2019]
- Example: A supplier often had backorders on one item, so the store offered a similar, always-in-stock item that cost less, around 700 dollars instead of about 1,000 dollars. Customers who wanted the original got the price difference refunded, roughly 200 to 300 dollars, and the substitute shipped in about three days. Many customers accepted the substitute, and the margin on it was much higher because the brand was less known. [L1076, 2019]

### Mispriced Orders and Supplier Outreach

#### Handling a Mispriced Order

1. Check your supplier's current stock and pricing, and confirm whether the model is discontinued or the stock is a phantom unit that the inventory system wrongly shows. [L1472, 2022]
2. Email the customer promptly, explaining the stock or price problem with a clear, polite apology. [L1472, 2022]
3. Offer either the corrected price or a cancellation with a full refund, based on what your policy allows. [L1472, 2022]
4. Ask the supplier to renegotiate the price, using the dealer cost and the mismatch as the reason. [L1472, 2022]
5. Keep the full email thread saved, and check the status again until the order is closed. [L1472, 2022]

#### Customer Email Template (Corrected Price)

```
Hi {customer_name},

Thank you for your order of {product_name}. We have just discovered that {reason, for example: this model has been discontinued by the manufacturer or the item is no longer in stock}, and the price shown at checkout was entered incorrectly. The correct price is {corrected_price}.

You can either keep the order at this corrected price, or we can cancel it and refund you in full. Please let us know your choice by {deadline_date}.

We apologize for the mistake and appreciate your understanding.

{your_name}
{store_name}
```

[L1472, 2022]

#### Supplier Email Template (Renegotiation)

```
Hi {supplier_rep_name},

We have received order {order_number} for {product_name} at {price_paid}. The current price on your list is {list_price}, which means we are losing money on this order. Could you work with us on a revised price of {target_price}?

If we are not able to agree on a new price, we will have to cancel the order, but we would much prefer to keep working with you.

Thank you for your help.

{your_name}
{store_name}
```

[L1472, 2022]

#### Accepting Losses

- Taking a loss on a mispriced unit is sometimes unavoidable. Occasional losses on a discontinued brand can be negative by a small amount, such as about $50. Accept that these cases are part of selling, but try to negotiate before you take the loss. [L1472, 2022]

### Shipping Delays and Customer Retention

#### Gifts Instead of Discounts for Delayed Shipments

- Never offer discount codes for delayed shipments. Instead, include a free product with an actual cost of $1-2 but a perceived value of $15-20 in the parcel, which generates gratitude and repeat purchases. [L0324, 2025+]
- Offering a 10% discount for late orders fails because customers question why they would reorder from a late supplier. Free gifts create emotional value and willingness to buy again. [L0324, 2025+]

#### Concessions for Unhappy Customers

- Offer a discount or a credit to keep an order when a customer is unhappy, as long as the remaining margin stays comfortable. A small concession such as an extra $50 off or an additional 3% can be better than losing the sale entirely. [L1472, 2022]
- Offering a discount to retain a customer is better than making no money at all. Compare the cost of the concession with the loss of a cancelled sale, and choose a concession that still leaves a healthy profit margin. [L1472, 2022]

#### Escalating Resolution for a Delayed or Unhappy Order

1. Offer a first, smaller credit, such as $25, to keep the order.
2. If the customer still wants to cancel, offer a larger credit, such as $50.
3. Keep the total concession within a margin you can afford, so the order still makes a profit. [L1472, 2022]

### VAT and Market Entry

#### Testing a Market Before Registering for VAT

- Consider the VAT question before selling into a country such as the UK. With VAT added, customers may be surprised by an extra cost at delivery and return the product. Test demand first, then decide whether VAT registration is worth it. [L0950, 2023]

Test a market with VAT in this order:

1. Test demand in the market first without VAT changes.
2. Calculate break-even including the extra VAT (for example 20%).
3. Run ads only to that market separately.
4. If the result does not exceed the higher break-even, stop the ads for that market. [L0950, 2023]

#### Free Products From Missing Prices

### Agency and outsourcing costs

#### Defining the partner relationship

- Define the partner relationship you want before negotiating contract terms. Decide whether you need:
  - short-term tactical support,
  - a long-term scaling partner, or
  - a transition vendor, with a planned move to in-house within a defined timeframe.
- The relationship type shapes contract length, flexibility requirements, and training obligations. [L0217, 2025-26]

#### Matching cost to scope

- Cost structure must reflect the scope of work. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Managing only paid media (pulling levers in ad accounts) costs less than full agency-of-record responsibility, which covers strategy, creative sourcing, landing pages, email setup, and analytics. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Do not pay for breadth you do not need. [L0217, 2025-26]

#### Compensation structure

- Base compensation lets the agency budget for labor costs, even if you reduce ad spend for profitability reasons. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Percentage-of-spend compensation makes agency income volatile and creates resistance to spending discipline. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Rule of thumb: the base fee covers labor, and incentives cover upside. [L0217, 2025-26]

#### Hiring a virtual assistant

- When you are completely new to hiring a virtual assistant and only want to test a candidate, start with an hourly rate. [L1430, 2020-26]
- Once you have found a good candidate, switch to a fixed monthly price, typically between 300 and 500 USD per month for Filipino virtual assistants. [L1430, 2020-26]
- Offer changes can be delegated by stating the new offer directly, for example switching the offer to buy one get one free. [L1432, undated]

### Products, variants and inventory

#### Variants

- Set up variants such as colors and sizes in the product's variant options. For example, switch a color to black and add a size range. [L1331, 2025-26]
- For an early version with one color, a simple single-variant product is enough. Plan variants before launch. [L1331, 2025-26]

#### Pre-orders and uneven demand

- For product variants with uneven demand, use a pre-order model for low-volume variants. This reduces inventory risk and lets you market all variants without holding slow-moving stock. [L0324, 2025+]

#### Backorders

- Accept backorders for products with low current stock. [L0249, 2022]
- Tell the customer the expected delivery timeline and let them choose to wait. [L0249, 2022]
- This converts sales you would otherwise lose by removing the product or marking it out of stock. [L0249, 2022]
- Source age: older advice (2022). [L0249, 2022]

#### Economic timing and inventory planning

- A current economic recession does not meaningfully reduce Q4 sales, because consumer spending shifts to credit and installment plans. [L0309, 2024+]
- Credit crises typically strike 6-12 months after high spending periods. [L0309, 2024+]
- Plan inventory conservatively from mid-2024 onward. [L0309, 2024+]
- Consider shifting to high-ticket products aimed at wealthy buyers, who are less affected by economic cycles. [L0309, 2024+]

### Reviews and trust

#### Incentives for video and image reviews

- Video and image reviews require a significant incentive, because they are more demanding for the customer and worth much more to the business. [L1012, 2021-22]
- Examples of incentives:
  - a larger rebate on the reviewed order,
  - a cash-back payment,
  - a $100 Amazon gift card, or
  - another product sold at cost price, including delivery. [L1012, 2021-22]

#### Supplier reviews

- When a store has high volume but very few reviews, ask suppliers for reviews. Use them alongside store reviews to build trust. [L1115, 2024]

#### Review schema (Judge.me)

### Reciprocity and gifts

#### Reciprocation

- Give a valuable, unrequested gift first. The recipient feels obliged to respond with a purchase. [L0340, undated]
- The gift must be given without being requested. It should be valuable enough to feel thoughtful, not a cheap logo item. [L0340, undated]

| Business | Free gift |
|---|---|
| Dog groomer | Free flea and tick collar |
| Bakery | Free rye bread or a box of chocolate chip cookies |
| Bike shop | Free water bottle |
| Shoe repair shop | Free shine |
| Karate school | One month of free lessons |
| Printer | 50 free photocopies |
| Florist | Free bunch of flowers |
| Cigar shop | Free lighter |
| Pizzeria | Free slice |

[L0340, undated]

#### Example: moving service

- A moving-service business sends gift certificates to new neighbors on behalf of local restaurants and auto shops. Examples are a free pizza, a free entree, or a free oil change. [L0340, undated]
- The gift introduces the business and starts the reciprocation effect. Satisfied recipients often return and become long-term customers. [L0340, undated]

#### Common mistake

- A reciprocity gift only creates a sense of obligation when the recipient did not ask for it and it feels valuable. [L0340, undated]
- A gift given in exchange for an action the customer already took is a thank-you, not a reciprocity trigger. [L0340, undated]
- A service provider can offer something like 30 minutes of free time, with no obligation, to create this effect. [L0340, undated]

#### Price anchoring for high-ticket services

- Start from the price you feel comfortable quoting, then test a number roughly three times higher. [L0378, 2019+]
- If the higher figure still sounds credible when a prospect hears it, use it as your opening anchor for negotiation. [L0378, 2019+]

#### Entry products

- A low-priced digital template can be the first purchase for a buyer who later pays a much higher fee for a workshop. This works if the template solves an early step of the same process. [L0378, 2019+]
- Choose the entry product so that it demonstrates your method. This makes the higher-priced offer the natural next step. [L0378, 2019+]

### Case study: lemonade stand

Starting point: a lemonade stand run by a ten-year-old. Typical neighborhood stands brought in $10 to $15 per day and charged 10 to 25 cents per cup. [L0378, 2019+]

1. **Phase one (sourcing):** lemons were sourced at no cost, which kept cost of goods very low. [L0378, 2019+]
2. **Phase two (product range):** homemade cookies were packaged in packs of 1 and 3. Premium golf balls were collected and resold. [L0378, 2019+]
3. **Phase three (location):** the stand was placed on a busy street where regular cars and golf carts passed. [L0378, 2019+]
4. **Phase four (pricing and pitch):**
   - Lemonade: $1 for the first cup, 50 cents for a second.
   - Cookies: 1 for $1, or 3 for $2.
   - Golf balls: regular at 50 cents, premium at $2.50.
   - The pitch offered cookies with each drink, and golf balls to golfers who had lost balls. This matched a visible need. [L0378, 2019+]

**Result:** sales reached $63 in one day, about 4 to 6 times the usual stand income. $58 was left after paying a helper $5. [L0378, 2019+]

**Lessons:** small mistakes reduced results. These included missing samples, not adjusting pricing, and having a weak sign. [L0378, 2019+]

### Ad budgets and promotion

#### Daily budget guidance

- Beginners starting with dropshipping should start around $25 per day, not $500. [L1374, undated]
- A $500 daily budget fits established brands that already have proven organic success and reinvest profits into high-quality, trend-aligned content. [L1374, undated]
- The speaker says $10 per day is the lowest workable level, but $25 has performed better. TikTok's own recommendation of $100 is not considered necessary. [L1374, undated]
