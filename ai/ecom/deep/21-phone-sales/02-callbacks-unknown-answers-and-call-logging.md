## Callbacks, unknown answers and call logging

### Promising accurate callbacks instead of guessing

- When you do not know a product detail, do not guess. Tell the customer you will check with the manufacturer team and call back with accurate data. [L0934, 2023]
- Answer unknown technical questions by promising a callback with accurate data, not by inventing an answer. This keeps trust and lets you collect the caller's contact details for follow-up. [L0934, 2023]
- A correct answer given hours later is better than an instant guess or no answer. Callers accept a follow-up within about 12 hours when they receive a correct answer. [L0808, 2026]

### Collecting caller details

Three phrasings for the moment you cannot answer at once. Each is a complete script from a different year.

one source:

```
Thank you for calling [Company Name], this is [Your Name]. That is a great question, and I want to make sure I give you the correct answer. Could I get your name, phone number and email address? I will get back to you with the answer as soon as possible. [Optional: I will follow up by [email/text] within [timeframe].]
```

[L0808, 2026]

one source:

```
Thank you for calling, that is a great question and I do not have the answer right now. Let me contact our supplier and confirm the answer with you, and I will get back to you as soon as possible. Can I have your name, phone number and email address, please?
```

[L0805, 2024]

one source:

```
Good question, [customer name]. I don't know the answer at the moment, so I'll consult our team and return your call as quickly as possible. May I have your phone number and email address?
```

[L0934, 2023]

### Handling unknown product questions

Pre-sales product question, 2024 procedure:

1. Ask for the customer's name, phone number and email.
2. Tell them you will check and call them back.
3. Call the brand contact about the product detail.
4. Call the customer back with the answer.
5. Send the answer by email and add the new detail to the product page.

[L0971, 2024]

Phone first, email second: call suppliers for an answer and follow up by email only if they do not answer. [L0971, 2024]

Phone or chat question you cannot answer, 2020 procedure :

1. Take the caller's name and phone number or email address.
2. Tell them you don't know yet, but will find the answer and follow up as soon as possible.
3. Ask the supplier the question.
4. Add the answer to the website, so future visitors also see it.
5. Contact the lead with the answer and follow up for the sale.

[L1277, 2020]

Note: the 2020 version does not name a channel for asking the supplier. The 2024 version calls the brand contact first and uses email only as the fallback. [L0971, 2024; L1277, 2020]

### Stock questions

Stock question you are not sure about, 2022 procedure :

1. Tell them you need to confirm with the warehouse before answering.
2. Take their name and phone number and write them down, even if the call drops.
3. Check inventory through the weekly stock sheet, the dealer portal or by contacting the supplier.
4. Call the customer back with the answer as soon as possible.

[L1113, 2022]

### Evening and gap-time callbacks

*Older advice:*

- In most countries it is considered acceptable for businesses to call customers until about 8 p.m. Evening callbacks are therefore a practical option when daytime calls are not possible. [L0909, 2021-22]
- At the start you may only have about two or three callbacks to return each day. A single half-hour block in the evening is usually enough to clear them. [L0909, 2021-22]
- Short gaps such as lunch breaks, a few minutes away from work, or a commute can be used to make callbacks. Calling while commuting is possible even on public transport. [L0909, 2021-22]
- A callback in the evening often feels to the customer like extra attention, which can build trust and improve the chance of a sale. Unexpected out-of-hours service is a useful way to create a memorable experience. [L0909, 2021-22]

Callback timing at a glance:

| Option | When to use it | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Evening block, until about 8 p.m. | When daytime calls are not possible; about 2-3 callbacks a day at the start, one half-hour block | [L0909, 2021-22] |
| Gap time: lunch, short breaks, commute | To make short callbacks during the day | [L0909, 2021-22] |
| Within about 12 hours | After you have a correct answer for the caller | [L0808, 2026] |

### Logging unclosed calls

- Log every inbound sales call that does not close with the call date and a follow-up date. Many callers who promise to call back never do, so the follow-up must come from you. [L0949, 2021-22]
- A Google Sheet with columns for call date, emailed or not, and next follow-up date is enough to track early leads without a CRM. *Older advice.* [L0949, 2021-22]

### Daily follow-up review

- Review the follow-up list every morning, either yourself or through a team member, and contact each lead on its scheduled date. This recovers many leads that would otherwise be lost. *Older advice.* [L0949, 2021-22]
- Check the sheet daily to see who needs a reply. *Older advice.* [L0949, 2021-22]
- When a prospect calls in response to an offer, they have already been persuaded enough to take action, so follow up the inquiry quickly while interest is fresh. Delay and forgetting reduce the value of each inquiry. [L1261, undated]

### Feeding answers back into product pages

- Use the answers you learn from customer calls to update the product pages on your store, so future visitors get the information without calling. [L0934, 2023]
- Add the new detail to the product page as the last step of the 2024 procedure. [L0971, 2024]
- Add the answer to the website as step 4 of the 2020 procedure, so future visitors also see it. *Older advice.* [L1277, 2020]

## Selling high-ticket and B2B buyers by phone

### Qualifying high-ticket buyers

- Use this sequence on a qualified lead call for products with an average order value of $5,000 or more [L0268, 2020]:
  1. Ask why they want the product, where it will go, when they need it, and what they will use it for [L0268, 2020].
  2. Decide whether they are a real buyer by checking for a confirmed need and a real timeline [L0268, 2020].
  3. Offer service enhancements such as expedited delivery or white glove delivery instead of discounts [L0268, 2020].
  4. Show that you want to earn the business through service, not price cuts [L0268, 2020].
  5. If call volume becomes unmanageable, hire a VA to handle calls [L0268, 2020].
- Expect more pre-sale questions and inbound calls for high-ticket items, such as those priced at $10,000 and above or at $1,000 to $2,000. These products can still be sold, but expect more contact before the sale [L0808, 2026].
- Before pitching a high-ticket B2B lead, qualify in this order [L1315, 2023]:

  | Step | Question | Purpose |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | Who holds the authority over the budget? | Identify the economic buyer |
  | 2 | What is the need the buyer is trying to meet? | Establish the need |
  | 3 | Where are they now? | Establish the current state |
  | 4 | Where do they want to be? | Establish the target state |
  | 5 | What is the impact of not reaching that goal? | Establish urgency and cost of inaction |

### Avoiding discount tactics

- Do not use loss-leader pricing or a discount-focused strategy for products of $5,000 or more [L0268, 2020].
- Discounts signal desperation to customers with serious buying intent [L0268, 2020].
- Focus on service enhancements such as white glove delivery and expedited shipping [L0268, 2020].
- Verify genuine customer need and timeline, and show business credibility [L0268, 2020].
- Price questions from buyers often close the sale, but this requires constant commitment and fast responses. Older advice from 2019 states that high-ticket products of about $5,000 and above can sell well when someone answers live chat and the phone quickly and responsively [L1110, 2019].

### Phone as a sales channel

- Selling by voice on the phone is an effective way to close high-ticket sales. The chance of a sale rises significantly when customers can speak to the business [L1371, 2021-22].
- Plan staff capacity for phone sales before entering the high-ticket or bulk segment. Calls will reveal product questions that you can then answer on the website [L1277, 2020].

### Concierge-style selling

- High-ticket buyers are often over 50 and sometimes in their 60s or 70s. They value personal attention and clear answers to their questions. A concierge-style approach works best with this group [L1472, 2022].
- Concierge-style phone sale for an expensive product [L1472, 2022]:
  1. Answer every question the buyer has about the product, and pay attention to their specific needs [L1472, 2022].
  2. Create a draft order in your system and send it to the customer, so they can pay without going through the normal checkout for the first order [L1472, 2022].
  3. For repeat orders, keep the conversation going by email, text or voice memos, and send another draft order when they are ready [L1472, 2022].
  4. Keep the buyer updated on any back-order delays until the product arrives [L1472, 2022].
- A personal concierge approach can turn a single high-ticket buyer into a steady source of revenue over time. Draft orders avoid friction in checkout for buyers who prefer a personal sale [L1472, 2022].

#### Case: concierge sale with draft orders

- A store sold products priced between $2,000 and $9,000, with the most expensive variant at $9,000 [L1472, 2022].
- One buyer called with many questions about her purchase. The store answered each one attentively and sent a draft order, so she did not have to go through checkout for the first order [L1472, 2022].
- Two months later she placed a second order after only a few emails of prior contact, with no draft order needed. Over those two months she brought in about $13,000 in sales [L1472, 2022].
- A separate $9,000 order was back-ordered for three months. The store kept her updated on the status until it was delivered [L1472, 2022].

### Multi-person B2B buying

- High-ticket B2B purchases involve several people [L1315, 2023]:

  | Role | Description |
  |---|---|
  | Economic buyer | Controls the money |
  | Lead decision maker or product owner | Leads the decision on the product |
  | Functional buyers | For example, head of purchasing |

- Get in front of the key decision maker and build relationships with the others [L1315, 2023].
- Expect the buyer to need strong proof and security for the purchase [L1315, 2023].

### Long sales cycles

- High-ticket products can require around 20 touch points before purchase. Expect a long sales cycle and plan a sequence of follow-ups rather than expecting a sale after one contact [L0949, 2021-22].

### Direct mobile numbers and repeat business

- Giving a high-value buyer your direct mobile number can turn a one-time purchase into repeat business. The buyer may later text or send voice messages to request a new draft order [L1472, 2022].
- Accept the channel the buyer prefers, including voice memos, and respond reliably [L1472, 2022].

## Taking orders over the phone

### Staff authorisation and payment limits

- Staff are authorised to take customer orders over the phone and place them on the website for the customer. [L0404, undated]
- Phone orders are limited to credit card or bank deposit payment. [L0404, undated]

| Rule | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Who takes phone orders | Staff | [L0404, undated] |
| Payment methods allowed | Credit card or bank deposit | [L0404, undated] |

### When phone sales matter

- most sales come through the website rather than the phone. [L0834, 2021]
- phone calls are typically used to close bulk orders, for example 10 items for an office. A discount is offered, and the sale is then placed over the phone. [L0834, 2021]

### Taking the call

1. Answer the customer's product or stock question. [L1283, 2023]
2. If they want to buy, add the item to cart in the store. [L1283, 2023]
3. Collect the customer's name, shipping address, billing address and payment method. [L1283, 2023]
4. Enter the details as if the customer were checking out, using their email. [L1283, 2023]
5. Process and follow up the order like any other online order. [L1283, 2023]

The sources also give a fuller version of the call:

1. Ask the customer what they would like to buy. [L0404, undated]
2. Open the website, put those products in the cart, then go to checkout. [L0404, undated]
3. Collect the customer's contact details with them: name, email address, delivery address and phone number. [L0404, undated]
4. Move on to the payment page and ask which payment method they prefer. Phone orders can be settled by credit card or by bank deposit. [L0404, undated]
5. If the customer is paying by credit card, ask for the card numbers, type them into the checkout page, then click finish to complete the purchase. [L0404, undated]

### Routes for entering the order

The sources describe two routes. Phone quotes are sent as draft orders, and direct orders are entered through normal checkout. [L1000, 2017; L0470, 2023; L1283, 2023]

#### Normal checkout

- Take phone orders by entering them through the normal online checkout, asking the customer for the same information as on the site. No separate POS system is needed for this. [L0470, 2023]
- A phone-only order can be placed through the normal checkout in the back end, so the order record matches a customer-placed web order. [L1283, 2023]

#### Shopify draft orders

Creating an order for a customer by phone in Shopify:

1. Click "Create order" in the Orders section, which creates a draft order. [L1302, 2020-26]
2. Add the products the customer wants. [L1302, 2020-26]
3. Apply any discounts. [L1302, 2020-26]
4. Send the customer a link so they can complete checkout themselves. [L1302, 2020-26]

### Channel attribution

- Phone quotes are sent to customers as draft orders in Shopify. [L1000, 2017]
- When the customer buys from a draft order, the sale is attributed to the phone channel, not to the online store, Facebook or Pinterest. [L1000, 2017]
- Because of this attribution, the phone sales commission can be tracked by channel. [L1000, 2017]

| Sale placed via | Attributed channel | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Shopify draft order bought by the customer | Phone | [L1000, 2017] |
| Online store, Facebook, Pinterest | Their own channel | [L1000, 2017] |

### Referral questions

- Ask about referrals only if they are part of the sales workflow. [L1283, 2023]
- Referral status does not change the process. [L1283, 2023]

## Quotes and quote follow-up

### Handling quote requests

A call within three minutes of the form submission creates a strong impression, and the speed and personalisation set you apart from slower competitors. [L0284, 2020-26]

Use this opening script as an example:

```
Hi, this is [name] from [company]. I saw your request for [specific need-e.g., playground setup]. We're working on a quote and will follow up later today. Let me confirm your contact details.
```

[L0284, 2020-26]

For high-value custom orders, follow this call sequence:

1. Introduce yourself and your company source.
2. Acknowledge the specific need they requested, showing you reviewed their submission.
3. Confirm the action you are taking on their behalf.
4. Verify contact information for follow-up.
5. Express enthusiasm about working together.

This builds credibility, separates you from generic outreach, and increases conversion on B2B and custom product quotes. [L0284, 2020-26]

B2B prospects often submit requests to several vendors at once to compare pricing. Set up Zapier to send immediate Slack alerts when Wufoo or Typeform quote requests arrive, then call the prospect within minutes. The first vendor to respond with a qualified quote typically wins the order. [L0286, 2018]

### Product-specific questions before quoting

Before quoting or selling certain products, ask for the details that the product category requires, not a generic set. Examples include:

- whether the customer has space to install the item,
- a photo of that space,
- The customer's zip code,
- any regulation that applies to the product.

Keep a separate question list for each product category or brand so staff do not need to remember them. [L1396, 2021-22]

### Quote task cards

When a call or chat asks for a quote, handle the pending task as follows:

1. Create a card with the customer name and the item, both together, so cards are easy to find.
2. Record the contact information, the product, any shipping needs such as inside delivery or installation, and the shipping address.
3. Send the request to the manufacturer.
4. When the manufacturer replies, move the card to Quote Received and note the extra cost, for example that inside delivery costs $100.
5. Send the customer the quote, move the card to Quote Sent, and attach a screenshot or copy of the invoice or email.

[L1396, 2021-22]

Creating several cards for the same item made it hard to see which customer each card belonged to. Always record both the customer name and the item name in the card title. Without this, staff must open each card to find the right customer. [L1396, 2021-22]

### Follow-up sequence after every quote

Run this sequence after every quote, with a due date set on each step:

| When | Action | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Next day | Check that the customer received the quote email. | [L1396, 2021-22] |
| After 3 days | Send the warranty information for the product. | [L1396, 2021-22] |
| After 1 week | Send a message explaining why to buy from you, such as financing, price matching and other benefits. | [L1396, 2021-22] |
| After 1 month | Offer a free consultation with an expert to check whether the product fits the customer's needs. | [L1396, 2021-22] |

Give each follow-up message a real benefit, such as warranty details, reasons to buy, or a free expert consultation, instead of only asking whether the customer has decided. Many quotes stay open because the customer is missing information or budget options, so each new message should add value to the conversation. [L1396, 2021-22]

### Follow-up email templates

**Day after the quote.** Adjust the wording as needed:

```
Hi [customer's title and last name], this is [your name] from [company name]. I wanted to check whether you got the quote request you made for [item name]. If there is anything else we can help you with, just let us know. Best regards, [your name].
```

[L1396, 2021-22]

**Three days after the quote.** Send this so the customer receives useful information rather than just a request for a reply:

```
Hi [customer's title and last name], we are attaching the warranty details for your [product name]. You will find them [attached or listed below]. If there is anything further we can do for you, please tell us. Best regards, [your name].
```

Change the warranty details to match each product or brand. [L1396, 2021-22]

**One week after the quote.** Use this to outline why buying from your company makes sense:

```
Hello [customer name], this is [your name] from [company name]. Many customers pick us for [product name] for these reasons: [list your financing options, price match policy, support, and other advantages]. Let us know if there is anything else we can assist with. Warm regards, [your name].
```

[L1396, 2021-22]

**One month after the quote.** Use this to offer help rather than pressure:

```
Hi [customer name], this is [your name] from [company name]. Would you like a consultation with an expert to see whether [product name] fits your needs? If you prefer a lower-priced option that fits your budget, we can also look at alternatives with you. Kind regards, [your name].
```

[L1396, 2021-22]

### Recovering open quotes

Following up manually and sporadically left a large amount of quoted business unclosed, with about $70,000 in open quotes at one point. Quotes were sent once and then forgotten when staff did not remember to call back. A dated follow-up system fixes this, because each card shows its next due date. [L1396, 2021-22]

### Prospect sheets and short codes

Record every prospect who does not buy immediately in a prospect sheet, so that no lead slips through. In the notes, use short codes such as PH for phone and EM for email, plus the product sought and the reason for not buying. [L1000, 2017]
