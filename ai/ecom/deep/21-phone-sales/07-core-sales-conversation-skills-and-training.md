## Core sales conversation skills and training

### Conversation flow and qualification

#### Run the sales conversation in this order

1. Get the prospect's attention first. Nothing else works without it. [L0378, 2019+]
2. Find out about the prospect. Ask which provider or solution they use now, whether they like it, what is missing, and whether they care most about price, quality, or having the best option. [L0378, 2019+]
3. Present a custom offer that matches what you learned, walking through the options from highest to lowest. [L0378, 2019+]
4. Close by doing the math for them, starting with the highest figure that fits the argument. [L0378, 2019+]

#### Qualify prospects

- Start with people who are unhappy with their current solution. [L0378, 2019+]
- If they are satisfied, look for one missing feature that could matter to them. If nothing does, move on. [L0378, 2019+]

#### Build know, like and trust first

- Build know, like and trust before presenting an offer. Help people first, learn about them, and then move to the offer, because trust makes buyers far more likely to purchase. [L0378, 2019+]
- Moving a prospect to a second location during a conversation makes the interaction feel longer and builds trust faster. Use this when it fits the context. [L0378, 2019+]
- A sales call or customer chat should always end with a clear offer or next step. [L1439, 2024+]

### Price reveal order

#### Reveal prices top-down

- Reveal prices in a fixed order, from the top option down to the cheapest, one step at a time. Showing the cheapest option first removes the chance to anchor on the top price. [L0378, 2019+]
- Showing the highest price first creates a moment of concern that the buyer cannot afford it. Showing cheaper options afterward releases that tension and makes the buyer more willing to commit. [L0378, 2019+]

### Doing the math for the buyer

- Do the math for the buyer instead of expecting them to calculate. Lay out logically why the purchase makes sense, using figures that are believable and tied to their situation. [L0378, 2019+]
- Use the following closing template, filling in the placeholders with real figures:

```
So you pay [ONE-TIME COST] once and add [MONTHLY GAIN] to your bottom line every month. Is that worth it for you?

An extra [MONTHLY GAIN] a month adds up to [ANNUAL GAIN] over a year. If someone offered you [ONE-TIME COST] today in exchange for [ANNUAL GAIN] at the end of the year, what would you say?
```
[L0378, 2019+]

### Objections

#### Anticipate objections

- Top closers anticipate objections before they are raised and address them openly. [L0378, 2019+]
- Highlighting a product's real weaknesses, framed honestly, keeps trust and prevents later doubt. [L0378, 2019+]

#### Handle objections in person

1. List every objection the prospect raises on a pad before answering any. [L1370, undated]
2. Keep asking if there is anything else until the prospect has no more objections. [L1370, undated]
3. Answer each objection. [L1370, undated]
4. Ask a conditional closing question, such as whether the prospect will go ahead if all these concerns are resolved. [L1370, undated]
5. Once the prospect agrees, the close is in place. [L1370, undated]

Print copy cannot replicate this live feedback, so the letter must answer the full list in advance. [L1370, undated]

### Emotional buyers

- Some buyers, such as passionate sports fans, are much less sensitive to price for products tied to their team or interests. Identify the emotional attachment first and build the offer around it. [L0378, 2019+]
- Sales psychology matters. Understanding emotions, impulse and how people decide is the core of online selling, and studying human behaviour helps a store owner predict how a customer will respond. [L1440, 2021+]
- Customers, not payment processors or platforms, pay the business, so the owner must understand how people think and decide to buy. Understanding persuasion principles helps predict what people will spend money on. [L1513, 2019]

### Composure and not appearing desperate

- Sales conversations work best when the seller is not desperate for the deal, because the hardest deal is the one you visibly need. Taking a position of not needing the deal removes pressure and keeps buyers engaged. [L1370, undated]

### Training and role-play drills

#### Train sales skills in this order

1. Role-play different customer types, each with a different objection. [L0378, 2019+]
2. Drill the pricing sequence and numbers until they come out in the right order without thinking. [L0378, 2019+]
3. Keep a fixed close and offer block on printed cards for field use. [L0378, 2019+]
4. Improve the opening and the middle of each pitch, which is where creativity matters most. [L0378, 2019+]

#### Reference case: a commission-based field seller

- Starting point: a new salesperson sold TV subscriptions face to face, earning about $125 per installation, with the commission paid only when the service was installed. [L0378, 2019+]
- The first week was used to learn a four-step process. [L0378, 2019+]
- In week two, the salesperson moved from a busy discount warehouse to a quieter electronics store, where meeting buyers in aisles allowed building a relationship before pitching. [L0378, 2019+]
- He asked about current providers, identified what buyers wanted, presented options from the highest price down, and closed with a clear calculation. [L0378, 2019+]
- Results in one week:

| Salesperson | Units sold | Units installed |
|---|---|---|
| Colleague | 10 | 5 |
| Case salesperson | 7 | 7 |

- [L0378, 2019+]
- By week three he was making more sales and more installs than everyone else in the office. He kept customers from buyer's remorse by selling only what matched his promises. [L0378, 2019+]
- He later left after four weeks because the schedule was too demanding. [L0378, 2019+]

### Books and learning by doing

- Read recommended books on sales and persuasion. Dale Carnegie's *How to Win Friends and Influence People* is recommended for handling clients, and Alex Hormozi's *100 Million Offers* for building offers. [L0440, 2023+]
- Robert Cialdini's *Influence* is recommended for learning persuasion principles. [L1513, 2019]
- Time spent in calls and real-world conversations is the most effective way to learn sales, because the mistakes made in calls show what to do better next time. [L0440, 2023+]

### Written sales presentations

- Sell in print the way you sell in person. A sales letter is a sales presentation on paper, so write it as if speaking directly to one prospect at a kitchen table or living room floor. [L1370, undated]
- Invite an immediate phone call and tie it to a specific time the reader can act on before the deadline. A time-limited request removes the delay of a letter's slow reply. [L1369, undated]
- Formula: invite an immediate phone call tied to a specific time that the reader can act on before the deadline. [L1369, undated]
- Examples:
  - "Call me before noon today, and I can have your order confirmed and the first shipment booked for tomorrow." [L1369, undated]
  - "Call the office before 5 this afternoon, and we will hold the discounted rate for your team until Friday." [L1369, undated]

## Staffing, hiring and delegating phone coverage

### Decide when to answer the phone yourself and when to hire

- Handle calls yourself first so you learn them, then reinvest profits into hiring someone to take over. Do not outsource phone sales before the business is profitable. [L1109, 2018]
- Missed calls from customers mean lost sales. Whoever answers needs a set schedule and a quiet place to take calls. If that person is running errands, they carry a notebook to capture leads. [L0832, 2019]
- Customer service calls can interrupt the owner about every 10 minutes, which makes focused work almost impossible. [L1247, 2021]
- Whether you need coverage depends on whether the calls make money:

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Calls from customers turn into revenue | Have someone answer the phone while you are away. [L0808, 2026] |
| Calls do not generate revenue | You do not need to cover them. [L0808, 2026] |

### Timing the first hire

Sources give different triggers. Use the one that matches your situation:

| Trigger | Source | Applies when |
|---|---|---|
| The site has traffic | Older, 2019 [L0467, 2019] | You need someone to cover live chat and phone calls during hours you cannot. The cost should be recovered quickly from the sales they handle. [L0467, 2019] |
| The business is profitable | Older, 2018 [L1109, 2018]; older, 2020 [L0468, 2020] | You are handing phone sales over to someone else, with profits funding the hire. [L1109, 2018; L0468, 2020] |

- Phone support must still be answered while you travel. If you work from another time zone, hire a virtual assistant rather than leave calls unanswered, because sales drop when calls go unanswered. [L1002, 2019]
- The first hire is usually a customer service assistant. [L1000, 2017]

### Virtual assistants and remote coverage

- Once the business is profitable, a virtual assistant can handle customer phone calls so the owner is not tied to the phone while travelling. [L0468, 2020]
- For short absences, such as a week away, have a friend or family member answer by forwarding calls to them. [L0808, 2026]
- For ongoing phone coverage, hire a full-time person through onlinejobs.ph, a Filipino job board. [L0808, 2026]

### Who to hire for phone work

- Phone support staff should sound natural to the local market. When selling in the United States, for example, they should have no accent, because an accent can lower the conversion rate. Email and live chat do not carry this accent problem, so the person's location matters less for those channels. [L1430, 2020-26]
- Phone staff are usually more expensive than staff who handle email, live chat and order processing, especially at the start. That is why the two roles are often separated when you start out, although one person can do both. [L1430, 2020-26]

### Vetting and interview tests

Hiring and vetting a phone-support virtual assistant:

1. Post a job on onlinejobs.ph for someone with phone and email sales or support experience. [L1002, 2019]
2. Review resumes and prefer candidates with experience at large, well-run companies who are still employed there and looking for a better opportunity. [L1002, 2019]
3. Test the hire by calling your own business at least a couple of times per week, from a withheld number and at unusual hours. Confirm they answer and handle the call correctly. [L1002, 2019]

Interview checks for the first hire:

- Use a five-minute interview test. A call that ends in five minutes or less shows no rapport, and the candidate is unlikely to build rapport with customers. [L1000, 2017]
- Reject candidates who are hard to understand on a call. They will be the voice of the business to customers every day, so poor spoken English or unclear speech is a reason to remove them from the list. [L1000, 2017]

### Trial and pay structure

Pay structure for new phone or chat staff:

| Stage | Pay structure |
|---|---|
| Month 1 | One-month trial. [L0467, 2019] |
| After the trial | Add commission on sales. [L0467, 2019] |
| Once they prove reliable | Base salary plus incentives. [L0467, 2019] |

### Daily performance tracking

Tracking a phone and live chat team:

1. Log in each night and check how many calls came in and how many were answered. [L0467, 2019]
2. Check the average response time for live chats. [L0467, 2019]
3. Review the call logs in Mighty Call to confirm calls are answered and handled properly. [L0467, 2019]
4. Confirm the quality of the work matches the standard you expect from the hire. [L0467, 2019]

### Outsourcing outbound work

- For B2B businesses, outbound sales calls to other companies and LinkedIn lead generation can be outsourced. Delegate them once the business can afford it. [L1247, 2021]

## Measuring phone sales and ad attribution

### Measuring offline and phone sales

When half or more of sales happen over the phone, Google sees too few conversions and undervalues the campaigns. Either attribute offline sales back to the clicks that caused them, or lower the Google target ROAS to account for offline revenue. [L0454, 2019]

### Adjusting the target ROAS for offline sales

**Formula**

Google target ROAS = overall target ROAS × (1 − share of offline or phone sales) [L0454, 2019]

**Worked example**

- Share of sales made over the phone: 50%
- Overall target ROAS: 10x
- Google target ROAS: 10 × 0.5 = **5x** [L0454, 2019]

**Trade-off**

- This method avoids CRM work. [L0454, 2019]
- It requires a reasonable estimate of the offline share. [L0454, 2019]

### Diagnosing the funnel before blaming the creative

- Do not assume that a single poor result is caused only by the creative. Doing so leads to missed funnel problems. [L0361, 2024]
- Even with a weak creative, one add-to-cart from a large spend means something in the setup is also wrong. [L0361, 2024]

## Support resources and miscellaneous notes

### Testing products and offers

- Do not conclude that a product is dead just because a competitor is scaling it while your test fails. Before drawing a conclusion, check the competitor's pixel data, their earlier entry into the product, their targeting, the product page and the creatives. [L0349, 2024]
- Clothing brand and product tests should use cost-effective approaches first. A basic Facebook ad can start as low as five pounds a day to test quickly. [L0561, 2023+]

### Case: baby gyro ball

- **Starting point:** A dropshipper found a baby gyro ball through Facebook keyword research. The product solved a problem, sat in the evergreen baby niche, and sold for about 25 USD. [L1251, 2024+]
- **Action:** The product was marketed on Facebook, and sales began arriving in a steady stream once the ad worked. [L1251, 2024+]
- **Result:** The store generated 50,000 USD in just over a month. The operator learned that logistics, supplier quality and customer service were critical, after fragile items arrived broken and suppliers had to be changed. [L1251, 2024+]

### Native ad networks

- Native ad networks such as Taboola have few public tutorials, and agencies often claim the platform is very hard to master. Some brands do well with native ads, but beginners without a tested process should treat them as a supplement rather than a main channel. [L0349, 2024]

### Creative formats and attention metrics

- Meme-style video ads reached a 60 percent attention rate and a 4 percent click-through rate. They showed no direct sales but were kept running because Google Ads results improved after they went live, which suggested they fed other channels. [L0999, 2023]

### Presenter-led video

- A presenter-led product tutorial or course built from AI-generated videos can raise conversion because the buyer sees the product explained by a face and voice. [L0888, 2023+]
- The same approach works for a niche product such as pet health, where a how-to video shows the use of the product before purchase. [L0888, 2023+]

### Partner choice

- Do not partner with someone who has the same skills as you, lacks experience with failure, or wants a quick win. Each of these mismatches tends to end the partnership when sales fall short. [L0413, 2016]

## Other details

### Planning the sales process

- Describe the sales process as it should look in 10 years: who sells, how customers and prospects are contacted, and whether a sales team works on the phone. [L0439, 2021-22]
- For B2B niches, also define how prospecting and offline conversion are handled and by whom. [L0439, 2021-22]

### Checking outside advice against your store

- Advice from an external source should be checked against where your store actually stands. General courses distill information for everyone, while a personalized consultant tailors it to a specific store. [L0463, 2020-26]

### Matching the offer to the buyer

- Example: for parents selling exam-prep products, a low-cost ebook is more practical than an expensive class that requires travel, parking and time away from home. For this buyer, the ebook is more cost-effective. [L0513, undated]

### Negotiating with freight companies

- Use this email to ask a freight company for a better price. [L0855, 2023]

```
Hello, I am comparing quotes for this order. The current quote I have from [OTHER CARRIER/BROKER] is [AMOUNT], and the quote on your platform is [AMOUNT]. Is there any better pricing you can offer for this shipment? Thank you, [YOUR NAME].
```
