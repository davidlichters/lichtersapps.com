## Supplier follow-up cadence and record keeping

### Day-by-day contact plan

- Start with a phone call on day one whenever the supplier has a phone number. Calls were the quickest route to a yes compared with waiting for email replies. [L0936, 2020-26]
- The day one call has two goals: find out the supplier's application process, and identify the decision maker at the company. [L0936, 2020-26]
- Day three of the contact plan is another phone call. [L0936, 2020-26]
- The full sequence runs to day seven as a one-week follow-up plan that mixes calls and emails. [L0936, 2020-26]
- Keep following up using the plan until you get a clear yes or no. You can stop early if you reach the right person on the first call. [L0936, 2020-26]
- Do not stop outreach after one unanswered call or email. Suppliers often respond only after repeated follow-ups, so continue through the full sequence. [L0936, 2020-26]

### Call first, then email: sequencing

- Call the phone number listed for the supplier first. [L0929, 2021-22]
- Send a follow-up email confirming what you discussed. [L0235, 2021-22]
- When a supplier does not answer your emails, call instead, because a phone call is the most reliable way to be heard and to get a point of contact. [L1110, 2019]
- *Older advice (2019).*
- Procedure for an email that went unanswered:
  1. Call the supplier. [L1110, 2019]
  2. Mention the earlier email and roughly when you sent it. [L1110, 2019]
  3. Follow the call script from the course. [L1110, 2019]
- Keep following up by phone and leave voicemails, because many brands return calls later. [L0857, 2025]
- If a pitch script gets no results, make it specific by explaining how you will drive sales for the brand and how you will handle its customer service. [L0857, 2025]

### Repeated calls for unanswered suppliers

- Procedure for a supplier that has not replied:
  1. Keep calling if nobody answers, since a persistent caller usually reaches someone. [L0803, 2022]
  2. If there is no reply after a few days, send a follow-up in the same email thread so the messages stack. [L0803, 2022]
  3. If still no response, find supplier relations staff on LinkedIn and contact them there. [L0803, 2022]
- *Older advice (2022).*
- When suppliers do not answer calls, keep following up. Suppliers with limited stock may later accept new dealers, so approved products should be uploaded while contact continues. [L0963, 2022]
- *Older advice (2022).*
- Supplier approvals often need repeated calls because companies get busy. Keep following up by phone and log each conversation's notes in a tracking sheet. [L1279, 2021]
- *Older advice (2021).*

### Follow-up scripts

#### Follow-up call for a supplier that has not replied

```
Hi, this is [your name] from [your store name]. I sent an email yesterday to the dealer contact at [supplier domain] about becoming a retailer for [brand name], and I just wanted to make sure you received it. Would you have a few minutes to talk about how we could work together and what the next steps are for approval?
```

### Hesitant suppliers and callback dates

- Procedure for a supplier who is hesitant but has not said no:
  1. Do not push for an immediate decision. [L0929, 2021-22]
  2. Ask them to think it over for a few days. [L0929, 2021-22]
  3. Agree on a specific callback day and say you will call then. [L0929, 2021-22]
  4. Send a follow-up email confirming the conversation and including your contact details. [L0929, 2021-22]
  5. Note the callback date in your supplier sheet and in your calendar. [L0929, 2021-22]
  6. Call back on that date. If they ask you to end the call, end it respectfully and move on. [L0929, 2021-22]
- *Older advice (2021-22).*
- Persistence pays. A supplier who believes you will call back is more likely to decide, and a follow-up email keeps you in front of them. Always make the callback you promised. [L0929, 2021-22]
- *Older advice (2021-22).*

### Final offers and follow-up dates

- When a supplier declines, make a polite final offer and ask for a follow-up date. Recording that date in your notes gives you a reason to return with results. [L0801, 2021]
- *Older advice (2021).*

### Notes and tracking sheets

- Keep notes during or right after every supplier call. You will make many calls and cannot remember what each supplier said, and calling back to ask again makes you look unprofessional. [L0929, 2021-22]
- *Older advice (2021-22).*
- Procedure for each supplier call:
  1. Record the date of your request call. [L0929, 2021-22]
  2. Record what they said about supplying their products, their pricing and anything else specific to them, during or right after the call. [L0929, 2021-22]
  3. Record the name of the person you spoke to; this person becomes your contact. [L0929, 2021-22]
  4. Record any direct email, direct line or mobile number they give you for follow-up. [L0929, 2021-22]
  5. Mark the supplier as Approved (Yes) or not (No). [L0929, 2021-22]
  6. Move on to the next supplier. [L0929, 2021-22]
- *Older advice (2021-22).*
- Log each conversation's notes in a tracking sheet. [L1279, 2021]
- *Older advice (2021).*

## Other supplier channels: forms, email, events and in-person pitches

### Online dealer forms and follow-up

Older advice, 2021-2022 and 2022. Check against current supplier practice before use.

1. Submit the online form on the supplier's website. [L0235, 2021-22]
2. Wait 24 hours. [L0235, 2021-22]
3. If you receive no response, make a phone call. [L0235, 2021-22]

When following up by phone after a form, use this opening:

```
I'm John from Oz Off Grid. I submitted your online application form about a day ago and wanted to reach out to follow up and discuss it with your team.
```
[L0235, 2021-22]

Start supplier contact with the online dealer application form when it is available, and call during free time. The online form takes less time and can be completed in the evening. [L0665, 2022]

### Form or phone first: when each applies

These two sources give different first steps. Both are older (2021-2022). Use the phone when a number is available, and the form when calls are not possible.

| Situation | First step | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Online form available, you can call in free time | Form first, then call after 24 hours if no response | [L0665, 2022; L0235, 2021-22] |
| Phone number available | Call first, because direct conversation is more effective than email | [L0665, 2022] |
| Calls not possible | Fill out the online form | [L0665, 2022] |

Call a supplier rather than only submitting an online form. A call lets you explain your store, ask for the dealer application, and get the contact moving quickly. [L0801, 2021]

### Introduction emails

When first contacting a supplier by email, a short introduction is enough to get a referral to the right contact if it:
- names your store,
- says you want to sell their products, and
- asks for the person who opens accounts for internet retailers. [L0969, 2022]

### Pitching in person or on a call

Older source, 2018. Follow these steps:

1. Think about two things first: what the supplier cares about and how they can trust you. [L1085, 2018]
2. Be honest about where your store is. You can say you are learning from a training program and are just getting started. [L1085, 2018]
3. Explain the marketing you will use to bring them sales: Google Product Listing Ads, retargeting, exit-intent capture, a follow-up email sequence, YouTube and industry-specific platforms. [L1085, 2018]
4. Make clear that customer service is your first priority and that you are building a long-term business. [L1085, 2018]
5. Keep the focus on how you will bring the supplier more sales, not on the money you expect to make. [L1085, 2018]

Use this opening for a first meeting, adapted to your situation:

```
I appreciate you meeting with me. I am [your name] and I am starting an online store focused on [niche]. I am still early in the process and I want to be honest about that. I am learning e-commerce from [training program or mentor name], and I plan to bring you more sales by using [traffic channel 1] and [traffic channel 2] to reach new customers. Customer service is my first priority, and I want to build a business that customers come back to for years.
```
[L1085, 2018]

### Trade events

## Cold calling local businesses and agency clients

### Niche choice and target selection

- Choose a niche with healthy budgets and no shortage of money, such as dentists. [L1185, 2023]
- Dentists are an example of a local business type that is often not thinking about ads or growth in business terms, which affects how the first conversation must be led. [L0440, 2023+]

### Cold email first

- Send a cold email before calling, so the business has already seen the agency's name when the call arrives. [L0440, 2023+]
- The email should be short, open with one specific observation about the business's current online presence or ads, and ask for a 10-minute call. [L0440, 2023+]

### Call procedure

1. Send a cold email first to introduce the agency. [L0440, 2023+]
2. Call the business. If they are busy, remind them that the email was sent. [L0440, 2023+]
3. Ask for 5 to 10 minutes to discuss their ads or online presence. [L0440, 2023+]
4. Ask about their goals for the coming year, for example more clients, fewer low-value treatments, or more high-value services. [L0440, 2023+]
5. Present how running ads can help reach those goals. [L0440, 2023+]
6. Propose a meeting, ideally at the business's office, to close the sale. [L0440, 2023+]

### Opening the conversation

- Use a shared-interest opener. For example, note that the business owner and the agency both sell a product that solves a customer problem, and that the agency wants to help the business sell that product online. [L0440, 2023+]
- Local business owners are often not thinking about ads or growth in business terms, so the operator must lead the conversation toward growth and explain why ads and product offers matter. [L0440, 2023+]

### Leading the owner toward growth

- Ask the owner about their goals for the coming year, then link ads to those goals. [L0440, 2023+]
- When the business already has an online shop, show a demo store with an easier checkout, relevant apps and automated customer emails, so the gaps in their shop become visible. [L1017, undated]
- Quote a price based on what the client is able to pay. [L1017, undated]

### Payment plan offers for expensive treatments

- Offer local businesses a payment plan for expensive treatments, such as Invisalign, so that customers can pay monthly instead of a single large sum. [L0440, 2023+]
- Let a third-party installment provider take over collection of the payments to reduce the risk for the business. [L0440, 2023+]
- Example from a Swiss dentist's competitor: the offer was Invisalign on installments of about 60 francs per month, instead of a single payment of 3,000 to 5,000 francs. The installment provider paid the full amount to the dentist upfront and collected the monthly payments, charging late fees if payments were missed. [L0440, 2023+]

## High Probability prospecting calls

### Where prospecting fits in the approach

- The approach has three phases, in this order: High Probability Prospecting, High Probability Selling, and High Probability Closing. [L0770, undated]
  - Prospecting: make a short phone contact with a clearly stated offer, let prospects disqualify themselves, and set a meeting only with someone who wants the offer and intends to buy. [L0770, undated]
  - Selling: first build trust and respect in a relationship phase, then ask the discovery and disqualification questions in fixed order, letting the prospect end the process at any point. [L0770, undated]
  - Closing: define the prospect's Conditions of Satisfaction, obtain a commitment to buy if they are met, and only then present a proposal or demonstration. [L0770, undated]
- There are usually far more prospects than a sales team can visit, but at any given time only a small share are ready to buy. Contact as many prospects as possible, briefly, to find that small group, and spend meeting time only on them. [L0770, undated]

### Writing the opening offer

- Keep the offer under 45 words, including greeting and introduction. Write it in your own words so that it makes sense to you. [L0770, undated]
- Deliver it in a neutral, unemotional tone, because a convincing tone creates resistance even when the words are right. [L0770, undated]
- Benchmarks from practice:
  - One seller's offer went through three rounds of trial calls and settled at 34 words. It was later sharpened further based on prospect responses. [L0770, undated]
  - Another seller cut a 59-word offer to 42 words and saw better results. Shorter offers generally perform better. [L0770, undated]
- Opening offer template for a cold call, filled in and kept under 45 words:

```
Hello, this is [your name] from [company name]. We [concrete description of what you provide, with one specific benefit or measurable result, e.g. the product type and what it does for the buyer]. Is that something you want for [the prospect's product line, business or situation]?
```

  Deliver it in a neutral tone and take the prospect's answer as it comes. [L0770, undated]

- Formula for a cold-call offer:
  1. Name yourself and your company.
  2. State concretely what you provide.
  3. Add one specific benefit, coverage detail, or measurable result.
  4. Ask a yes-or-no question about whether the prospect wants it. [L0770, undated]
- The question keeps the prospect in control and makes a clear no easy. [L0770, undated]
- Own examples of the formula:
  - *This is Mark from Cleanline Office Services. We clean commercial kitchens after hours, which in our clients' experience cuts grease-related inspection failures roughly in half. Is that something you want for your kitchen?* [L0770, undated]
  - *This is Lena from Pressgrid Print. We produce custom labels in runs of 500 to 5,000 with a proof sent the same day. Is that something you want for your product line?* [L0770, undated]

### Running the call

- Follow this sequence on a High Probability prospecting call: [L0770, undated]
  1. State your offer clearly in no more than 45 words, counting the greeting and your introduction. [L0770, undated]
  2. Check whether the prospect wants what you are offering. [L0770, undated]
  3. If he says yes, ask whether he would like to set up a meeting aimed at doing business. [L0770, undated]
  4. If he is not ready at that point, book a new call for a time he chooses, and get his commitment to move forward then. [L0770, undated]
  5. End the call on any negative answer. Arrange a callback only if he states he will be a qualified prospect at a specific later time, and make clear that it is completely fine for him to say no whenever he hesitates. [L0770, undated]
  6. Disqualify anyone who does not want your offer or will not buy it from you. [L0770, undated]
- Two signs that you have slipped into selling on a prospecting call: you feel stress during the call, or the call runs longer than three to four minutes. When either happens, stop pitching and return to asking questions. [L0770, undated]

### Qualifying questions

- Collect the following from the prospect before any appointment, in this order: [L0770, undated]
  1. Does he want this product?
  2. Does he want to arrange an appointment?
  3. If not now, when?
  4. If not now, does he want you to check back later?
  5. Why does he want to meet at this time?
  6. What would he like to accomplish at the meeting?
- Add further disqualifying questions when the situation calls for them. [L0770, undated]

### Handling vague and negative answers

- If a prospect answers with "that sounds interesting", "maybe", "I don't know", "I don't think so" or "I'll consider it", reply in a flat tone: [L0770, undated]

```
Does that mean you want what I'm offering, or not?
```

- Treat a no as a clear answer and close the call politely. Do not argue, add benefits, or sell harder. [L0770, undated]
- If a prospect asks a question you cannot answer well yet, such as why he should switch when he is happy with his current supplier, use one of these replies in a flat tone: [L0770, undated]

```
I don't have a satisfactory answer to that yet. What answer would make sense to you?
```

```
It doesn't sound like this is a good fit for you. Is my offer something you want, or not?
```

  Choose the reply that fits the situation and let the prospect answer. [L0770, undated]

- A statement that does not answer your question, such as "I'm not interested", is noise rather than a real answer. Do not respond with a sales pitch. [L0770, undated]
  - Ask a clarifying question he can answer, such as whether he is the person who decides on this, and keep the conversation on your questions. [L0770, undated]
  - Disqualify him only when he clearly says he does not need, want, or can afford the offer. [L0770, undated]
- Interest has no sales value because it is not a commitment. Enthusiasm and direct, quick answers are better signs of a qualified prospect than polite interest. [L0770, undated]
- Ask yes-or-no questions that include the option of no, such as whether something is what he wants or not. A clear option to decline reduces resistance and makes the prospect responsible for his own choice. [L0770, undated]

### Information requests and materials

- If a prospect asks you to send information, reply in a neutral tone: [L0770, undated]

```
When someone asks for information, it often means the conversation is ending politely. If that is your intention, just tell me and that's fine. If you do want the details, tell me what you would need to decide so I can see whether it makes sense to send them.
```

- Send materials only when the prospect wants the offer, needs the information, and is likely to buy after reviewing it. [L0770, undated]
- Do not send unsolicited brochures or samples to cold prospects. Keep unsolicited mail for a mass mailing sent two or three times a year, and send individual material only to prospects who have confirmed interest. [L0770, undated]

### Finding the right contact

- When you need the right person at a company, first ask the front desk for whoever handles the product area, such as packaging design. [L0770, undated]
  - Many switchboards only release the marketing or sales manager's name. Ask for that person and let them point you to the correct contact. [L0770, undated]
  - If you still cannot reach the decision-maker, the sales department is a usable alternative route. [L0770, undated]
- In companies with several product lines, each line's product manager often owns packaging or purchasing decisions for that line. Identify the manager of each line separately instead of relying on one central contact. [L0770, undated]

### Scheduling the meeting

- Never try to sell the appointment. Ask whether he wants to meet to see if your offer fits his needs. Then let him propose the time, and ask him what he wants to accomplish, since a prospect who does not commit to a meeting has disqualified himself. [L0770, undated]
- Scheduling a sales meeting by phone: [L0770, undated]
  1. Set a tentative date for the meeting, usually a few weeks ahead if the prospect names that timing. [L0770, undated]
  2. Ask what the meeting should accomplish and whether the decision-maker should attend. [L0770, undated]
  3. Call the day before to confirm the time and the attendees. [L0770, undated]
  4. If the prospect does not confirm, treat the appointment as disqualified and do not chase it. [L0770, undated]

### Ending a call with a disqualified prospect

- Close politely and do not argue. Say you will only call again if new developments fit his stated needs, and do not try to convert him. [L0770, undated]
