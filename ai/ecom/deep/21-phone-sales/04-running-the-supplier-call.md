## Running the supplier call

### Before the call

- Prepare a short script before the first call. The conversation feels less daunting when you have one. [L0771, 2021-22]
- Keep the core call simple: pick up the phone, introduce yourself and your business, build a little rapport, and ask whether you can sell their products through your website. [L0771, 2021-22]
- Plan the framework, not the words. Know the purpose of each part (introduction, planned launch, reason for choosing them, request for the application process) and phrase it in your own words on each call. [L0928, 2020-26]
- Suppliers are busy and receive more requests as e-commerce grows, so the call must be ready before you dial. [L0929, 2021-22]

### Reaching the right person

- Call the supplier using the phone number on their website to start the approval conversation. [L1085, 2018]
- Use this first-contact procedure:
  1. Introduce yourself briefly and say you want the person who handles working with online retailers. [L0968, 2021]
  2. Wait for their reply. [L0968, 2021]
  3. If they are not that person, ask who you should speak to. In very small companies the owner often answers and is the right contact. [L0968, 2021]
- When calling a supplier for an account or referral, mention the existing supplier relationship and ask for the specific contact who handles approvals. [L0468, 2020]
- Use this procedure for account requests:
  1. Call the supplier's phone line and ask for the person who handles new retail or dealer accounts. [L0458, 2021-22]
  2. Introduce yourself and your business, and say that you have a website built for their products. [L0458, 2021-22]
  3. Ask whether you can sell their products through your website and whether they drop-ship to customers. [L0458, 2021-22]
  4. Ask for the online dealer application or product information, and note the name and direction given. [L0458, 2021-22]
  5. Send a follow-up email after the call to confirm the conversation, and record the date and outcome in your tracker. [L0458, 2021-22]
- When you get no decision maker, ask for a sales representative. Keep the call short and make sure your message is clear each time. [L0941, 2022]
- Handle a gatekeeper this way [L0929, 2021-22]:
  1. Briefly state that you want to talk about becoming a retailer or reseller for their products. [L0929, 2021-22]
  2. Let them direct you to the right person, such as the sales contact who handles new retailer requests. If that person is not available, ask for their mobile number. [L0929, 2021-22]
  3. If you reach the right person, let them tell you what they need next and respond to that. [L0929, 2021-22]
  4. Do not give a long pitch to a gatekeeper, since they cannot approve you. [L0929, 2021-22]
- Supplier approval calls should explain why the niche fits the supplier's brand, ask about their products, and ask who handles approvals. [L1111, 2020]

### Opening the call

Use this opening script, filling in the placeholders:

```
Hi, it's [Your name] calling from [Your company name]. How are you?
[Wait for their reply, then continue:] We are an online retailer of [your niche or product category]. We have looked at your products, we like them, and we would like to talk with you about becoming a retailer for them.
[Stop talking and let them respond. Do not fill the silence.]
```
[L0929, 2021-22]

### Explaining the model without the word dropshipping

- Avoid the word dropshipping on calls because some people have negative views of it. Describe the model in plain terms such as direct-to-customer or warehouse-direct-to-customer instead. [L0929, 2021-22]
- Some suppliers avoid working with dropshippers because of bad experiences. Do not use the term in the first contact. Present yourself as an additional sales channel that brings new customers. [L1085, 2018]
- Use this model explanation, filling in the placeholders:

```
Say: Look, we're an online-only retailer and we work on a direct-to-customer basis. If you are at your computer, you can have a quick look at [your website] while we talk. Here is how it works: we build and maintain our website, upload your products to it, handle all the marketing and customer service, and generate the sales. When we make a sale, we send you an order for that product, we pay for it upfront, and you ship it directly from your warehouse to the customer.
[Stop talking after this and let them respond.]
```
[L0929, 2021-22]

- Spelling out the model prevents confusion, shows you will not buy in bulk, and sets the basis for the request. [L0929, 2021-22]
- Mentioning upfront payment in that explanation lowers the supplier's risk immediately. A zero-risk offer is the easiest way to win agreement. [L0929, 2021-22]
- The same call approach works in any country; it is not specific to one market. [L0929, 2021-22]

### Stating the launch date

Sources give slightly different windows. Use the table to choose:

| Source | Recommended window | Upper limit | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| one source, pitching a new store [L0928, 2020-26] | Roughly two to four weeks away, or up to about one month | Never more than two months | A longer timeline makes the supplier treat the store as not relevant right now |
| one source, opening call [L0929, 2021-22] | About one month away | None stated | Launching a few days earlier or later is fine |
| one source, script guidance [L0928, 2020-26] | A few weeks to about one month ahead | Never more than two months | Applies to the window the supplier should expect |

- Use a real launch date, not a vague one. [L0928, 2020-26]
- Use the window in the table above; the shorter windows fit most calls, and the two-month ceiling is the hard limit. [L0928, 2020-26; L0929, 2021-22]
- If the store is almost ready, describe it as being in its final touches before launch, not in beta testing. [L0855, 2023]
- Present the store's future potential, not its current results. Do not present a store as having sales or achievements it does not yet have. [L1111, 2020]

### Positioning a store with no business history

- If this is the first store, say so honestly. Then explain the niche opportunity, the search demand, the brand quality, and why you want to sell that supplier's products. [L0855, 2023]
- Use the pronoun I when you run the business alone, instead of we. Use whatever you are comfortable with, but do not claim a team you do not have. [L0803, 2022]

### Checking US distribution

Use this script for a supplier with unknown US distribution. Replace the brackets and keep the question about shipping:

```
Hello, my name is [name] from [store name]. I noticed your products are listed on many US websites. Do you have distribution in the United States, a warehouse, or a fulfillment partner, and how does your shipping to US customers work?
```
[L0854, 2022]

### Building rapport

- Ask casual questions about the brand's history, goals, and best products. These questions build a relationship and give you data for your marketing. [L0801, 2021]
- Build a relationship rather than pitching only price. [L1111, 2020]

### Making the first request

- Your only request at this point is the product data: the price list, product images, and any useful technical specifications. Ask how soon they can send it to you. [L0929, 2021-22]
- Accept any delivery method for the product data, such as a shared cloud folder link, emailed files, a login to a dealer portal, or downloading images from their website. Confirm you will get access; do not insist on a format. [L0929, 2021-22]

### Tone

- Sound genuinely excited. A flat or disinterested tone makes the supplier less likely to approve you, but do not overdo it. [L0928, 2020-26]
- Stay calm, friendly, and respectful even if the supplier does not say what you hoped. Treat them with the same courtesy you would expect yourself. [L0929, 2021-22]

### Brevity

- Get to the point quickly and say as little as needed. Long rambling calls and long emails waste the supplier's time and weaken your chances. [L0929, 2021-22]
- Keep the sales call short, concise, and tight. Follow the framework by introducing yourself and your company, then stating which brand you want to carry. [L0936, 2020-26]
- When calling suppliers, keep the call short and make sure your message is clear each time. [L0941, 2022]

### Using scripts as guides

- Scripts are guidelines, not text to read aloud. Use the same key points on every call, phrased naturally. [L0928, 2020-26]
- Reading a supplier script word for word sounds unnatural and makes the call feel scripted. [L0928, 2020-26]
- Use the same framework in your own words instead of reading a call script word for word, which sounds unnatural and robotic to suppliers. [L0936, 2020-26]
- Use the script as a guide, not word for word, but cover the same key points concisely. Let the supplier do most of the talking, hear what they need, and then respond to it. [L0929, 2021-22]
- Use the supplier call script as a guide and speak naturally. Keep the questions consistent across calls. [L0963, 2022]

### After a yes

Follow this procedure:

1. Thank them and confirm when they will send the product data. [L0929, 2021-22]
2. Exchange contact details if not already recorded. [L0929, 2021-22]
3. Record the outcome in your supplier sheet. [L0929, 2021-22]
4. Follow up if the data does not arrive by the date they promised. [L0929, 2021-22]
5. Move on to the next call. [L0929, 2021-22]

## Handling supplier questions, objections and negotiation

### Opening supplier calls and pitching your store

- Lead with a benefit to the supplier, not a request. You bring traffic, exposure and sales; they only ship orders and answer emails. Keep every conversation pleasant and avoid negativity. [L0962, 2021]
- Differentiate with a specific reason. Suppliers choose between many callers, so a general claim about being reliable is weaker than a named product attribute. Use this formula:
  1. Name a specific attribute of the supplier's product that stands out from copies in the market.
  2. Show that you researched the brand.
  3. State how your store will serve their customers with care.
  - Furniture example: "Your outdoor sofas use weather-resistant frames that most competitors copy without the same build quality, and we want to show that quality to buyers who need durable outdoor furniture."
  - Skincare example: "Your formulas avoid the fragrance additives that most shelf brands use, and our store targets shoppers with sensitive skin who are looking for exactly that." [L0933, 2020-26]
- Show credibility and make the pitch easy to act on. Some suppliers hold back a large amount of their trust until an account is set up, so offer flexible shipping arrangements to reduce the barrier to working with you. [L1441, 2018]
- Prepare a short paragraph describing the marketing channels you will use to sell their products, and be ready to read it out when asked how you will market them. [L0929, 2021-22]

### Handling hesitation and refusals

- Never accept the first no. Politely ask why, because refusals are often quick exits, a busy moment or a need for more time, and the underlying objection can often be resolved. Hanging up without digging can cost you your best supplier. [L0929, 2021-22]
- Do not pressure for approval on the spot. Pushing damages a relationship you want to last for years. Many buyers want a day or so to think, and pressure makes them less likely to agree. Give them an easy way out of the call so they do not feel trapped. [L0929, 2021-22]
- Where a supplier is not taking new online retailers right now, follow this procedure:
  1. Politely ask why there is a limit, for example a supply shortage, limited product volume or a temporary issue.
  2. If the reason is temporary, offer to set up now but delay your first sales by about a month, so their current supply flow is not disturbed.
  3. If they still refuse, thank them, say you would like to work together later and leave your contact details.
  4. Ask them to contact you if a retailer spot opens up, and offer to start selling straight away.
  5. Record a callback date in your supplier sheet (for example three months later) and make that call. [L0929, 2021-22]
- Look for an arrangement that works for both sides rather than insisting on a rigid setup. Starting small and proving sales lets you ask for better terms later, while a hard line at the start can end the relationship before it begins. [L0929, 2021-22]

### Deposits, upfront payment and pre-purchase requests

- When a supplier asks for a deposit or pre-purchase, tell them you need to see sales first and propose a test. This reduces risk and keeps the deal open. [L0801, 2021]
- Upfront payment removes the supplier's financial risk. With credit terms they could be left chasing an unpaid invoice 30 days later, so zero risk is the most effective way to get a yes. [L0929, 2021-22]

### Answering "what's in it for us?"

When a supplier asks what is in it for them, offer these three benefits:

| Benefit for the supplier | What it gives them |
|---|---|
| Payment for each order upfront | Reliable cash flow and no late payments to chase |
| Access to new online customers | Their products reach new buyers and their market share grows |
| Fast route for excess stock | A quick way to move excess or discontinued stock |

- Suppliers often offer up to 40% off excess stock if you promote it actively. [L0929, 2021-22]

### Wholesale discounts, MAP and margins

- Do not ask for a discount in the first contact with a new supplier. Ask about pricing tiers and let the supplier name its own volume-based pricing before offering anything. [L1281, 2022]
- On a first call, after receiving the supplier's price list, follow this sequence:
  1. Thank them and say everything looks fine.
  2. Explain that you always look for the best prices so you can put the most money into advertising for every brand you sell.
  3. Ask whether they offer tiered pricing, such as lower rates above a certain dollar amount or a set number of units per month, and what the pricing would be at those levels.
  4. Stop and let the supplier answer.
  5. If the supplier has no tiers, add: most brands you work with have arrangements like that, so would they be open to discussing terms or incentives for selling more of their products? [L1281, 2022]
- When asked for a wholesale discount, explain the MAP margin reality and ask whether wholesale can be negotiated so that wholesale is half of MAP, rather than asking only for cheaper pricing. [L0665, 2022]
- If the gap between wholesale and MAP is too narrow to promote the brand heavily, use this email:

```
Hello [Supplier contact name], while going through all our price sheets, I found that the gap between wholesale and MAP is too narrow for us to promote your [brand name] line heavily. We'd genuinely like to do that, but we need a bigger margin to fund marketing for your products. Is there any kind of incentive pricing available? Do you have volume discounts or tiered pricing, such as a percentage off wholesale once we hit a set sales target?
Thanks,
[Your name]
[Company name]
[Phone number]
```
[L0835, 2023]

- For a phone version of the same request, use this script:

```
Hi, this is [your name] from [your brand]. We were looking over the price lists you sent us. We really want to put a real ad budget behind these products and become one of your top retailers, but the margins aren't where we need them to be. Do you have any incentive programs where, if retailers reach a certain number of units or a certain sales volume, wholesale prices get discounted? If not, please let me know so we can decide our next steps.
```
[L0970, 2023]

- When a supplier asks about private label, answer in terms of the products that can be relabeled, not as a unique product. [L0855, 2023]

### Shipping costs that erase the MAP margin

- If shipping costs wipe out the margin at MAP, use this call script:

```
Hi [supplier contact name], this is [your name] from [your store name]. We are interested in selling [product name] and we are ready to move forward. It costs us about $[shipping cost] to ship one [product name], and if we sell at MAP of $[MAP price] we lose about $[loss per sale] on every sale. I can see that [competitor 1], [competitor 2] and [competitor 3] are all selling at MAP. Do you know which carrier they ship with, or do they have better wholesale pricing? Can you help us find a shipping method or wholesale price that lets us make money at MAP?
```
[L1085, 2018]

- A supplier usually has all the information about how other sellers ship products. Ask them directly how competitors achieve profitable shipping instead of guessing. [L1085, 2018]

### Checking MAP compliance and pricing discrepancies

- If you see a product listed below the MAP, ask the supplier to confirm before assuming an error on your side:

```
Hi [Supplier contact name], I am not sure if I am misreading your price list. Your MAP for [product name] is [MAP price], but I see this product listed for [lower price] at [link]. Can you confirm whether I have the MAP wrong, and let me know what is going on?
```
[L1111, 2020]

### Promotions that conflict with MAP

- Before running a bonus promotion that may conflict with MAP, ask for written approval:

```
Hello [SUPPLIER CONTACT], I'd like to run a promotion on our store. When a customer buys [PRODUCT X], they also receive [BONUS PRODUCT Y] free. I know this may conflict with your MAP policy, so I'm asking for your approval before launching. Could you confirm in writing whether you approve this promotion? Thank you, [YOUR NAME] [STORE NAME].
```
[L0855, 2023]

- If the supplier declines, look for a similar product to use as the bonus instead. [L0855, 2023]

### Which other brands you work with

- Do not lie about other brands. If asked, name only the suppliers you are actually in an approval process with, meaning you have already submitted an application or were told one would be sent. [L1085, 2018]
