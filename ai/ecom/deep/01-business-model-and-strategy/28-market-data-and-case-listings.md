## Market data and case listings

#### Case listings (sold through a broker)

- Pets niche case: a Shopify store created in August 2014 sold a variety of pet products for several needs of pet owners. It sold for $367K, listed at 33x, and was supported by a Facebook group presence and an email list of about 5,000 emails. Financial data covered from the start of 2016 and earlier, showing a growing business with some dips. Traffic came from 55% paid, 30% organic, 10% direct and 5% other sources. The store worked with several US suppliers, and a customer support virtual assistant handled chat, phone calls, emails and Zendesk tickets, and was willing to continue with the new owner. The store also used paid search. [L0554, 2019]
- Beauty niche case: a dropshipping store focused on products for salons and massage businesses was listed at $167K at 30x. It had an email list of over 2,000 subscribers that was not monetized but received informational messages and links to blogs. The financial history was long with a strong general trend, though some monthly spikes were caused by when supplier bills were paid, and the accounting was cash-based. It had 18 suppliers, some with exclusive relationships, and about half of traffic came from paid search with some from organic sources. A full-time virtual assistant handled daily operations, and the store had full SOPs, training documents and documentation. [L0554, 2019]
- AliExpress/apparel case: a store with $8,700 monthly profit, Facebook-only traffic, Chinese shipping and decreasing monthly earnings was listed at $183,000 (21x), took 126 days to sell, and sold for $120,000 (13x). The store was 12 months old, and buyers had the same negative view of AliExpress models. [L0553, 2018]
- Dropshipping lifestyle case: $5,000 monthly net profit, 18 months old, Google Ads, organic traffic and Facebook retargeting, steady earnings. Listed at $142,000 (28x), sold for $133,000 (26x) in 10 days from listing. [L0553, 2018]
- Twitter-background website case: see the neglect example above. [L1468, 2014]
- Home lighting store case: a single home lighting store grew to multi-seven-figure annual revenue and was then sold for a six-figure sum, which freed capital for new stores. After the sale, the operator opened further sites and took partner stakes in other owners' stores. Selling a proven store and reinvesting the proceeds is a route to a portfolio of stores. [L1014, 2021-22]
- Early dropshipping store exit: a dropshipping store sold after about one year was priced at roughly one year's salary from a regular job. The first such exit was about 39,500 USD, showing that early store sales can be in the tens of thousands. [L1502, 2021-22]
- Building for exit: dropshipping stores can be built with the intent of selling them as an exit. Later stores reached exits in the high six figures, so building, scaling and selling a store is a legitimate business model in its own right. [L1502, 2021-22]
- Starting point case: the owner had about 1,000 USD left, no flight ticket home, and was building a store on a borrowed laptop while living in Thailand. The first sale came two months after starting. The store later reached over 1 million USD in total sales with close to 200,000 USD profit. Its web-only theme would have lost sales as shopping moved to phones, so the owner sold the store while it was still healthy and built another store using the same process. [L0826, 2016]
- The first brand the course group sold achieved a 37 times multiple on monthly net profit. [L1153, 2016]
- Early AdSense micro-site model: sites earned about 8 USD per month on average, and sites of this kind were sold at about 20 times their net profit. The money from each sale was reinvested into building the next, larger site. [L1359, 2014]
- Private sale or broker example: sale prices in examples of this kind ranged from about $44,000 to $60,000 per store. [L1126, 2018]
- Lesson sale of an early dropshipping site netted nearly $200,000 after two years of running it. [L0458, 2021-22]

#### Reinvesting the proceeds

- Treat the proceeds from selling a store as investment capital, not spending money. The lesson invested the full proceeds of each sale and kept living costs low so that no sale money was needed for daily life. [L1126, 2018]
- Selling a profitable store, investing the proceeds into income-producing assets and then starting another store can be a time-saving cycle. Brokers such as Empire Flippers handle buying and selling stores. [L1515, 2019]
- Selling a proven store and reinvesting the proceeds is a route to a portfolio of stores. After the sale, the operator opened further sites and took partner stakes in other owners' stores. [L1014, 2021-22]
- The early AdSense micro-site model reinvested the money from each sale into building the next, larger site. [L1359, 2014]
- Building a brand that can later be sold makes it easier to build several brands one after another. [L1439, 2024+; L1212, undated]
- Some buyers want a passive dividend; investors can place capital into a site run by a managing team. [L1468, 2014]
- A seller can sell only some of the stores to build a cash reserve and keep the rest. [L0468, 2020]

#### Market context

- Brokers' sales data shows dropshipping valuations rising over the two years before the talk. [L0554, 2019]
- Average store listing multiple in the last 12 months was 27x and the average sold multiple was 24x. [L0553, 2018]
- The market outlook for dropshipping is positive, and recessions may increase demand for models that need less upfront capital. [L0554, 2019]

#### Further details

- A store without consistent, established profit or revenue is valued mainly on its assets. Its price is then whatever a buyer is willing to pay. [L1092, 2022]

## Buying an existing store or business

### Build versus buy

- Building a store from scratch is generally preferred to buying one. A store with roughly 10,000 to 40,000 in profit can often be rebuilt within a few weeks, so buying it is rarely worth the price. [L0247, 2021]
- Buying a finished store is usually more expensive than building one, so building and growing the store until it produces income is the more common route. [L0967, 2020]
- Build online stores from scratch rather than buying existing ones. [L0852, 2020]
- Buying an existing online business is generally not recommended for most buyers, because e-commerce businesses are easily replicated. Starting a new store is usually a better use of several hundred thousand dollars than buying one (2024). [L0971, 2024]
- Buying a profitable store at a high multiple rarely makes sense, because a store that sells about one order per day is achievable with focused work and little money. The unit does not state the multiple itself. [L1002, 2019]
- A better route is to find which niches and stores are for sale, check their numbers, and rebuild a comparable model yourself. [L1002, 2019]

### When buying can make sense

- Buyers purchase an existing drop shipping store to:
  1. shorten their learning curve,
  2. trade money for time,
  3. use momentum the store has already built, or
  4. put a unique advantage of their own to work. [L0858, 2018]
- For high-ticket dropshipping, the choice depends on your resources. [L0949, 2021-22]

| Your position | Usually the better route | Source |
|---|---|---|
| More money than time | Buy an existing store. It saves setup time, but you inherit processes you did not design and must review them carefully. | [L0949, 2021-22] |
| More time than money | Build from scratch. | [L0949, 2021-22] |

- Only content sites with established traffic, such as a forum or blog running for around 15 years, are worth buying. Pure e-commerce stores are not. [L0852, 2020]

### Who should not buy yet

- Buyers without prior experience building stores from scratch often overpay and see sales fall soon after purchase, because they cannot judge the quality of the store. [L0949, 2021-22]
- Learning to build a store first makes it much easier to evaluate one that is for sale. [L0949, 2021-22]

### Verifying figures before paying

1. Verify the seller's figures, supplier relationships, supplier lead times and sales trend. Sellers can present numbers that hide problems, so check everything against the underlying data. [L0949, 2021-22]
2. Do your own check of revenue and margins. Many small online sellers keep poor financial records, so the figures they report are often unreliable. [L0949, 2021-22]
3. Check the length of the sales history before you agree on a multiple. See the valuation benchmarks below. [L1115, 2024]

### Valuation benchmarks

| Store or situation | Benchmark | Applies when | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Established profitable store | Around $500,000 or more | Store with a long, proven profit record | [L0852, 2020] |
| Small, unproven store | Roughly $5,000 to $20,000, risky for beginners | Little or no track record | [L0852, 2020] |
| Store with roughly 10,000 to 40,000 in profit | Often rebuildable within a few weeks, so buying is rarely worth the price | Comparing purchase price with build cost | [L0247, 2021] |
| Store with about one year of sales history | About 30 times monthly net profit is typical (2024) | Standard valuation for a store with roughly a year of sales | [L1115, 2024] |
| Store with less than one year of sales history | More risk for the buyer, usually a lower multiple (2024) | Short or unproven sales history | [L1115, 2024] |

- The 2020 price points are older and show only rough orders of magnitude. For a current valuation of a store with known monthly net profit, use the 2024 multiple. [L0852, 2020; L1115, 2024]

### Aged domains

- Buying aged domain names is generally not worth the money in drop shipping. Spending large amounts on several aged domains has not paid off. [L0853, 2021]
- An established store with an aged domain, existing backlinks and running traffic lets you pull growth levers quickly. [L0949, 2021-22]
- An aged domain with authority does not guarantee fast search rankings, so budget time for SEO even after buying. [L0949, 2021-22]
- Both statements apply to different things. An aged domain bought on its own has not been worth the money, while an aged domain that comes with a store that already has backlinks and traffic can speed up growth. [L0853, 2021; L0949, 2021-22]

### Shopify Exchange and broker listings

- Stores listed for sale on Shopify Exchange, as seen around its 2019 launch, were often overpriced, had little or no sales history and carried unrealistic valuations. Check the real revenue history and the asking price critically before buying a store there. [L0357, 2019]
- On a business broker listing for a dropshipping store, the sale includes the domain and all site contents and files. [L0458, 2021-22]
- The URL, Google Analytics access and profit-and-loss statement are visible only after you unlock the listing. [L0458, 2021-22]
- Review the listing carefully before buying. [L0458, 2021-22]

### Risks for buyers

- You inherit processes you did not design and must review them carefully. [L0949, 2021-22]
- You can overpay and watch sales fall, because you cannot judge quality without building stores yourself. [L0949, 2021-22]
- The seller's numbers can hide problems. [L0949, 2021-22]
- Reported revenue and margins are often unreliable when the seller kept poor records. [L0949, 2021-22]
- Small, unproven stores sold for roughly $5,000 to $20,000 are risky for beginners. [L0852, 2020]
- An aged domain does not guarantee fast rankings. [L0949, 2021-22]
- E-commerce businesses are easily replicated, which weakens the case for paying for one. [L0971, 2024]

### Learning due diligence

- The podcast Acquisitions Anonymous has its host vet two businesses a week as if buying them. It is a useful way to learn due diligence before purchasing an online store. [L0949, 2021-22]

## Other details

### Quick wins on an established store

- Apply these steps in order to a store that already trades. [L0575, 2014]
  1. Add abandoned cart emails. [L0575, 2014]
  2. Add "buy" and "online" to product title tags. [L0575, 2014]
  3. Make sure Google Shopping feed monitoring is set up daily, and split product types into separate campaigns. [L0575, 2014]
  4. Negotiate provider discounts (Shopify, payment processor, PayPal) by phone, and review annual billing. [L0575, 2014]
  5. Rewrite meta descriptions using the rules: capitals, unique per page, sales-letter style, rich snippets, and a phone number where relevant. [L0575, 2014]
  6. Disable pagination. [L0575, 2014]
  7. Set up uptime monitoring. [L0575, 2014]
  8. Validate Google Analytics e-commerce tracking, and remove PayPal Express buttons. [L0575, 2014]
- A store doing about $1 million in revenue applied three of these quick wins (titles, Google Shopping structure and meta descriptions among them) and added about $200,000 in revenue within three months. [L0575, 2014]
- A separate store cut Shopify costs by $100 per month with one phone call, saving over $1,000 per year. [L0575, 2014]

### Return fees

- Return fees spread first in the US, then in Australia, and more slowly into the UK and EU. Large fashion retailers such as ASOS and Zara now charge them. [L0955, 2025-26]
- Expect fee-based return policies to become the norm in each market, at a different speed in each. [L0955, 2025-26]

### Return on investment

- Example: a $2,000 retreat investment (flights, hotel and ticket combined) should generate $20,000 in annual profit through applied strategies, a 10x return. [L0286, 2018]

### Diagnosing a store that does not make money

- if a store is not making money, find the cause. It is either traffic quality and conversion, or product and supplier quality. [L1068, 2019]
- Spending more money on the same setup does not fix it. [L1068, 2019]
- Do not close a store before the root cause has been checked. [L1068, 2019]

### Competitive position

- The business is relationship-based, which is a real barrier against copycats. [L1086, 2019]
- Supplier relationships, optimized product pages, traffic setup and ad accounts all take work. That work is what separates the stores that win. [L1086, 2019]

### Applying dropshipping methods to other online businesses

- Human psychology and platform mechanics carry over from dropshipping to other online businesses. [L1251, 2024+]
- Only the objective changes. For example, a lead-generation campaign books sales calls instead of making a direct product sale. [L1251, 2024+]

### Direct mail

- Direct mail sells very different products with the same method, because buyer reactions stay the same across products. A seller who understands those reactions can apply one approach to any new offer. [L1369, undated]
- Direct mail reaches buyers quickly and at low cost in good and bad markets. It shows early whether a product sells, so a campaign can still run within the selling season. [L1369, undated]
- This only works for products with real merit and enough people who want them or can be made to want them. [L1369, undated]
- Management can check every claim before a direct mail piece goes out, which makes written promises easier to control than a salesman's spoken pitch. [L1369, undated]
- Small mailings let a seller test an idea cheaply before committing to a large budget. [L1369, undated]
- Direct mail can prepare a sales team's prospects before a visit, so salespeople meet buyers who already know the offer. [L1369, undated]
- The same letter technique can help a business sell to its own staff and partners. [L1369, undated]

### AI-first dropshipping

- An AI-first dropshipping system is designed for speed and leaner operations across product research, store build and ad creative. This is why its milestones are set aggressively. [L1452, 2026]
- The old approach of guessing, testing blindly, or spending weeks building a store is replaced by AI-supported work that speeds up each stage. The goal is faster decisions and less wasted effort. [L1459, 2026]
- AI dropshipping can be organized into five areas where AI tools add leverage: [L1459, 2026]
  - product research [L1459, 2026]
  - store creation [L1459, 2026]
  - creatives and content [L1459, 2026]
  - customer experience [L1459, 2026]
  - data-based decisions [L1459, 2026]
- Each area is a place where manual work or guesswork can be replaced by tools. [L1459, 2026]

### High-ticket dropshipping course: scope and audience

- The growth phase applies to an existing business. The build phase applies only to someone starting a new business, so a store already running should focus on growth content. [L1456, 2021-22]
- The course is aimed at people who already run a high-ticket dropshipping business, not at beginners. Learners still in the launch stage are told to start with the build-phase material. [L1495, 2021-22]
- The program targets stores trading for anywhere from a few months to several years. A store at either end of that range can use the grow-phase material. A store still being set up should begin with the build phase. [L1495, 2021-22]
- Sources differ on the entry point, and both apply in their own case. The course is framed for existing owners [L1495, 2021-22]. A beginner only needs to focus on the Build phase at the start [L1496, 2021-22]. Existing owners use the course directly, and beginners start with Build. [L1495, 2021-22; L1496, 2021-22]
- Training is meant to stay useful well after launch, including one or two years into a running business. A beginner can leave advanced material until the business has grown. [L1496, 2021-22]

### High-ticket dropshipping course: structure

- The course is split into a build phase, which covers setting up and launching the business, and a grow phase, which covers scaling traffic and conversion. An existing business owner can skip the build-phase modules. [L1495, 2021-22]
- The Build phase covers building and launching the business. The Grow phase covers marketing and outsourcing after launch. [L1496, 2021-22]
- The high-ticket dropshipping process is described as a sequence of five steps: build the business, then launch it, then grow it. The build phase is the first stage. The unit names only build, launch and grow, so the other two steps are not named in this source. [L1490, 2021-22]
- in the members area, the Build phase modules appear first, at the top of the page below the welcome video, under a heading called Build Phase. The course has about six Build phase modules, which must be completed in numbered order. [L1496, 2021-22]
- The growth stage consists of about ten modules, each tackling a single dimension of marketing for high-ticket dropshipping. That count keeps rising over time. Named modules: conversion rate optimization, backlink acquisition, content marketing, search engine optimization (SEO), pay-per-click advertising, email marketing and retargeting. [L1495, 2021-22]
- There is no fixed order for working through the grow-phase modules. Start with the weakest area of the business, or with a traffic channel not yet tested. [L1495, 2021-22]
