## Niche and market selection strategy

### Niche versus general store

- Build niche-specific stores rather than one general store that mixes unrelated categories. Mixed general stores convert much worse and make it hard to stand out from competitors. This applies as of 2020. [L1277, 2020]
- Combining unrelated categories is usually not worth it. [L1277, 2020]
- Sources differ on the stage. General stores are common for testing in the current landing page environment, and a single proven product can later move to its own store. Use a general store to test; move to a niche store once a product has proven itself. [L0950, 2023]
- Wayfair.com is an example of a general store that sells almost everything, with well over 90% of its products dropshipped. It grew into a billion-dollar, publicly traded company with hundreds of employees, while most dropshipping businesses build niche-specific stores instead. [L0766, 2020-26]
- Wayfair.com was previously known as CSN Stores and rebranded to Wayfair about three years before the lesson was recorded. Its general-store model differs from the niche-specific stores most dropshippers build. [L0766, 2020-26]
- A narrow niche builds authority with shoppers, which tends to raise conversion rates and make paid ads more profitable. A store that tries to sell almost everything loses that focus and the advantage that comes with it. [L0964, 2023]
- Single-niche stores that meet the niche criteria convert better and produce more profit per ad dollar than multi-niche stores. (2023) [L1283, 2023]
- Build a niche-specific store rather than combining unrelated niches, because focused stores convert better and make paid ads more profitable. [L0665, 2022]
- Add cross-sell accessories only when they are used together with the core product. Products that people do not buy together belong on separate stores. [L0665, 2022]
- Keep the store focused on one niche, with only products that belong to that niche. Add products from parallel niches only if the research numbers stop matching, which means niching up. [L1114, 2023]
- Choose one product type rather than one single product, and build the whole store around it. A focused niche lets the store establish credibility and authority in that category. [L1346, 2020-26]
- Do not build a store with only one product. A single-product store is not recommended. [L1277, 2020]
- A store that sells random, unrelated products, with no niche and no long-term plan, is a burner store. It looks built for quick sales, is unlikely to grow into a real business, struggles to earn trust, and is hard to sell later. [L1346, 2020-26]
- Narrow a new business to one niche category at the start instead of keeping many product ideas open, because a single focus makes it much easier to gain traction and sustain growth. (2025-26) [L1016, 2026]
- Serve one identifiable group of customers defined by a shared interest rather than by a single product, so you always have several profitable products to sell and do not depend on finding the next winning item. [L0458, 2021-22]
- Focusing on one winning product creates a turn-and-burn business that falls apart when the product's popularity fades. Defining the niche by customer interests makes marketing easier and keeps the business stable. [L0458, 2021-22]
- Large e-commerce companies such as Amazon cannot match the detailed service a niche business gives one customer group. You do not need to beat Amazon's size to earn a good income, only a small share of its market. [L0458, 2021-22]
- Most winning dropshipping products are not tied to a narrow niche but appeal to a broad audience, such as anyone who wants to look good or take care of their body. Narrow niches tend to cap volume, since a very deep niche may only reach around 50 to 100 orders per day. [L0780, 2024+]
- Sources differ on breadth. Broad-appeal products suit the product-winner search, while niche-specific stores suit a lasting business. Use the broad product to find a winner and the niche store to build on it. [L0780, 2024+; L0665, 2022]
- Plan the store for the long term from the start. A store focused on one product type is more valuable if you later want to sell it, and it is more likely to last for years. [L1346, 2020-26]

### Defining the audience first

- When a product idea looks too competitive, change the focus from the product to the customer: first define who buys, then decide which products they need. Niching down on products alone does not change what the site says to the buyer. [L0905, 2021-22]
- Case: a general e-bike store struggled for a long time with broad positioning. Its owner narrowed the whole site, content and design to one buyer group, hunters who use e-bikes built for hunting. The store reached about $5 million per year in revenue, while most general e-bike stores in the same market earned little. [L0905, 2021-22]
- A niche does not need to be a single-brand or single-category niche. If the products are sold on sites that also cover other niches, the niche is still fine, and a sub-niche can be split off and owned as its own niche. (2023) [L0835, 2023]
- Enthusiast or hobby niches are not a required filter. The highest-revenue businesses in this example were not hobby-driven, so niche choice should rest on other criteria such as search volume and supplier availability. (2023) [L0835, 2023]
- Shopping for a store's niche should first target products people already search for, which gives a store traffic and sales as soon as it launches. Once a store has traffic, an email list and social reach, new brands can be introduced to the store and promoted. [L1114, 2023]
- A market can be worth shortlisting even if you have no personal use for its products, when demand is clearly growing in your target country. Growth in a market is a valid reason to keep an idea in the running. [L1329, 2021-22]
- Do not niche into the example products used as course illustrations, such as chandeliers and stand-up paddle boards. Many beginners pick them for that reason alone and the market is crowded. (2023) [L0964, 2023]
- For a low-interaction business that can be automated as far as possible, the upper middle class is the preferred target demographic. Lower interaction means less customer service load, which lets the owner avoid being tied up in support work and build a lifestyle business. [L1518, 2020-26]

### Goal and time horizon

- Decide your goal before searching for products: a quick short-term profit, a dropshipping business, or scaling a product you already have. The goal determines which kind of product to look for. [L0706, 2020+]
- Choose products and niches for a horizon of three, five and ten years, not the next few months. Temporary events such as COVID-19 should not decide the niche on their own. [L0359, 2020]
- Choose evergreen niches whose products keep selling for years, so the store has no natural end date. You can sell the store later if you stop operating it. (2023) [L0934, 2023]

### Research sequence and first-niche pragmatism

- Niche research works as a fixed sequence in which each stage depends on the one before it. The brainstorm of at least 50 niche ideas must be finished before the validation tests are run: price points, margins, demand, seasonality, loyalty and competition. [L1057, 2020-26]
- A niche can be submitted for a verification review before any time is invested in it. Physical product niche ideas can be checked, and the review tells you whether to proceed, pivot or niche sideways. (2023) [L0835, 2023]
- The first niche does not need to be perfect. A viable but non-ideal niche will still generate profit and provide operational learning. While the first store runs profitably, build a second store with superior niche research. Continuous improvement and dual-store operation beats endless research with no launches. [L0268, 2020]
- When a niche meets the criteria but is slow, work with what you have before switching: onboard every supplier you can and make the most of those brands. A store netting about $2,000 profit per month is worth keeping and building on rather than starting over. [L1277, 2020]
- If a niche fails the criteria, add sub-niches. If it still fails, narrow to a higher-level niche. (2023) [L1283, 2023]
- Choose a niche where one customer can be sold several different products over time. Repeat sales should be built into the model from the start, not added later. [L1485, 2021-22]
- Build the business on a viable high-ticket product market. The marketing system only works on a viable market; if the niche or products are weak, marketing alone will not fix the business, so product validation must come first. [L0414, 2021-22]

### Recurring revenue and lifetime value

- Choose markets with recurring revenue potential, meaning customers buy from you again at least once after their first purchase. Recurring revenue is a core marketing concept for this model. [L0994, 2021-22]
- Avoid markets where a customer buys only once every five to ten years, because you must keep acquiring new customers to grow. That approach is more expensive and yields lower profit, even though it can still work. [L0994, 2021-22]
- A business that sells a single high-ticket product bought once every 5 to 10 years, or never again, must keep acquiring new customers and cannot market profitably to existing ones. Growth is capped by how many new buyers enter the market, so such a niche is a weaker choice than one with repeat purchases. [L1485, 2021-22]

### Seasonality and evergreen choices

Seasonality checks and rules:

| Situation | Guidance | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Google Trends, 5-year view, minor fluctuations, stays above 50% of peak year-round (kegerator example) | Acceptable | [L0268, 2020] |
| Search volume drops to near-zero in the off-season | Avoid | [L0268, 2020] |
| First store | Avoid seasonal niches | [L0468, 2020; L0469, 2021; L1281, 2022] |
| Existing seasonal store, low months | Use geo-targeted ads that reach only warmer states | [L0468, 2020] |
| Later store, off-season demand strong | Consider a seasonal niche | [L0469, 2021] |

- Use Google Trends' 5-year view of search volume to assess seasonality. Acceptable seasonality shows minor fluctuations without dropping significantly from peak; the kegerator example stays above 50% of peak year-round. Avoid a niche that drops to near-zero during the off-season. [L0268, 2020]
- Avoid seasonal niches for a first store, because revenue falls for part of the year regardless of execution. For an existing seasonal store, use geo-targeted ads that reach only warmer states during the low months. [L0468, 2020]
- Avoid seasonal niches for the first store, even when off-season demand is strong, because they add demand swings and many extra moving parts. Start with an evergreen niche and consider a seasonal niche for a later store. [L0469, 2021]
- Avoid seasonal products for a first store. Seasonal businesses take longer to reach a level that can replace a job, while a year-round product can stabilize income faster. [L1281, 2022]
- An evergreen brand can keep a stable core of products that sell all year and add event-based campaigns, such as Mother's Day, to capture busier periods. Event products are timed to the calendar, while evergreen products carry the business between events. [L0502, 2020+]

### Competition and saturation

- Competition test: if more than 50% of the products on the first page of organic Google Shopping results come from 10 or more different stores, competition for that product is considered too high. [L0969, 2022]
- A competitive niche is not suited to everyone. More competition usually means a bigger market, and returns can be higher, but it demands more time, attention, money and patience. [L0920, 2018]
- A competitive niche needs more time, attention, money and patience, and a bigger market usually means higher returns. It is a poor fit for a store you intend to flip or sell within one year. [L1357, 2018]
- Brand loyalty in a niche means only a few product types dominate, not a few stores selling many brands. A niche with many brands and few stores is acceptable. (2024) [L0856, 2024]
- Sources differ on competition. The test in [L0969, 2022] is a hard filter; a niche that passes it can still be competitive and demand more resources, as described in [L0920, 2018; L1357, 2018]. Apply the test first, then judge the resources you can commit.

### Mass desire and demand direction

- Advertising can only direct a desire that already exists among many people; it cannot create that desire. Treat the job as finding a shared, already-active desire and channelling it onto your product. [L0330, undated]
- Do not run against a rising public demand. A campaign that pushes a product in the opposite direction of a strong trend fails even with a large budget, so follow the trend and pick the product angle that matches it. [L0330, undated]
- Automotive examples show the rule:
  - In 1948 a manufacturer built a shorter, squatter car with more interior room while the public wanted longer, lower, wider cars; a multimillion-dollar campaign failed. [L0330, undated]
  - In the mid-1950s the market demanded horsepower, and brands that sold power sold well. A major brand that spent heavily on safety messaging saw no results and the following year released its most powerful engines. [L0330, undated]
  - A good, inexpensive compact car backed by heavy advertising also failed when demand shifted sharply to cheap, simple, economical compacts. [L0330, undated]
- Mass desire is a private want shared publicly by enough people to repay the cost of advertising, manufacturing, selling and profit. A market is born when such a shared desire becomes large enough to be profitable, and it can range from a few thousand people to tens of millions. [L0330, undated]
- Rate each candidate mass desire on three dimensions before choosing it: urgency and intensity (how strongly people need it), staying power (how often it repeats and never gets satisfied for good), and scope (how many people share it). Choose the desire that scores highest on all three together. [L0330, undated]
- A product usually satisfies two to four mass desires, but an ad can carry only one dominant desire at a time. Choosing that desire is the most important decision in writing the ad, because the headline depends on it and no later copy can rescue a wrong choice. [L0330, undated]
- Mass desires come from permanent forces, such as instincts (attractiveness, health, virility) and technological problems that remain unsolved, or from forces of change, such as trends, style shifts and mass education. Permanent forces require a fresh way to claim the same benefit. Changing forces require spotting the shift early and knowing when it becomes statistically significant. [L0330, undated]
- Channel an existing mass desire onto your product in three steps:
  1. Choose the single most powerful desire that can be applied to the product, scored on urgency, staying power and scope. [L0330, undated]
  2. Acknowledge that desire, reinforce it, or offer the means to satisfy it in one statement in the headline. Start the headline with the product if the prospect knows it, with the desire if the prospect knows only the desire, and with the problem if the prospect knows only the problem. [L0330, undated]
  3. Show, step by step in the body copy, how each product performance inevitably satisfies that desire. [L0330, undated]
- Supplements sell far more than gym services because a pill delivers the result with almost no time, effort or sacrifice. A rough claim puts the supplement market at about ten times the fitness industry, which is not verified. [L0549, undated]

### Price, margin and shipping viability

- Use the average price from Google Shopping results, not the median, to understand what products sell for across a price range. The average gives a realistic view of the price points you will list. [L0853, 2021]
- Niches with an average order value of about $300 to $500 are a practical option for a store with limited cash. Higher-ticket niches around $3,000 are also viable if you can cover the cost of one order. [L0853, 2021]
- The number of products in a niche and the price range matter more than the brands on marketplaces. A wide price range inside a niche is normal; for example, couches sell from about $150 to tens of thousands. [L0468, 2020]

Heavy and large products:

- Large and heavy products are viable if the price is high enough to cover shipping. For example, a futon ($300), a leather sofa ($1,500) and a sectional ($3,000) have similar shipping costs, so the cheapest one may leave no margin. (2024) [L0971, 2024]
- Large and heavy products only work when the selling price is high enough to cover freight, because shipping cost stays roughly the same whatever the item's price. A product selling for $200 leaves too little margin after wholesale cost, card fees, ads and free shipping, so cheap heavy items should not be sold or advertised. (2023) [L1114, 2023]
- Worked margin example (2023), [L1114, 2023]:

| Item | Selling price | Cost basis | Left for the rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Item with wholesale cost about 50% of selling price | $200 | about $100 wholesale | about $100 for card fees, ads and shipping |
| Three-seater fabric sofa | $1,500 | $750 | $750 for shipping and ads |
| Custom Italian leather sofa | $15,000 | not stated | same shipping cost range as the $1,500 sofa |

- Within a heavy-goods niche, pick a sub-niche with enough price range to make money, such as futons at $200 versus premium sofas at $1,500 or more. Cheaper items in that niche should be kept off paid traffic and may be kept only for organic visitors. (2023) [L1114, 2023]
- When shipping is expensive, as in Australia where a small envelope costs about $2, start with products small enough to fit in an envelope. [L1437, 2018]
- Products with complex installation can be sold, but the operator must plan installation support, which adds work. Hands-off products have less friction. (2022) [L0969, 2022]

### Returns and return-rate risk

- Returns on a low-margin dropship product are often cheaper to absorb than to build a returns system for, if the return rate stays around 5%. Early on, accepting the loss and moving on can be the rational choice. Stricter rules can be added later for customers who abuse returns, such as buyers ordering several clothing items and sending some back. [L0552, 2023]
- Case: a motorcycle gear brand sold products at around $120 with goods costing $70 to $80, and returns were costly, so the margin was too thin. A higher-ticket product needs a consultative sale and enough margin to absorb returns. [L0552, 2023]
- Apparel has a return rate well above the e-commerce average, mainly because customers misjudge size or material. Apparel stores should plan for higher returns and watch the return rate closely. [L0838, 2020-26]
- Large apparel retailers can absorb very high return rates because they own their brands and have a large database of repeat buyers. A dropshipping store without these advantages cannot absorb the same return rate, so high-return categories are risky for it. [L0769, 2020-26]
- If you run a lifestyle dropshipping business with limited customer service time, avoid niches with high return rates. Returns require constant customer service work that does not fit a lean operation. [L0769, 2020-26]

### Supplier availability and niche pivots

- If your chosen niche has fewer suppliers than expected, pivot within the niche before starting over. Expand, for example, from office chairs to office chairs plus desks, or office chairs plus ergonomic accessories. Niching sideways often provides enough suppliers without abandoning your research. (2023) [L0251, 2023]
- Use the supplier check together with the verification review and the competition test: a niche with few suppliers is not dropped automatically but first widened sideways. [L0251, 2023; L0835, 2023; L0969, 2022]

### Branded categories and B2B or B2C fit

- Manufacturers of branded products spend hundreds of millions of dollars on marketing to create customer loyalty. This spending makes it impossible for dropship stores to compete in those categories, even if the manufacturer approves dropshipping. [L0319, 2020-26]
- Manufacturer approval does not guarantee dropshipping viability. Even if Apple approved iPad dropshipping, you could not profitably compete, because major retailers like Best Buy and the Apple Store already own customer traffic and loyalty for those products. [L0319, 2020-26]
- Solo e-commerce operators cannot profitably sell branded products, because they lack the hundreds of millions of dollars in annual marketing budgets that manufacturers like Sony and Bose deploy. Branded categories require either manufacturer distribution partnerships or equivalent marketing spend, both unavailable to individual operators. [L0319, 2020-26]
- A drop-shipping store is not directly comparable to a brand that makes its own products, such as a luxury label. The goal is to be the best-known store for a niche's buyers, not to match a manufacturer's scale. [L0853, 2021]
- Renting products to end consumers online is a poor fit for a lifestyle business, because it creates repeated back-and-forth exchanges and support problems. Renting to businesses, such as party tents or inflatable bounce houses, is workable. [L0801, 2021]

### Operator fit and passion

- You do not need to be passionate about the niche you sell. Focus passion on building and growing the business. Profitability and business success matter more than personal interest in products. [L0270, 2021]
- The first store does not have to be the final answer; a profitable first store funds the learning for the second. [L0268, 2020]
