### Bing, Yahoo, X, Marketplaces and Other Channels

**Bing and Yahoo:**

- **Lenient policies:** Bing and Yahoo Gemini ad networks are often more lenient than Google about which products can be advertised. Check their policies when Google rejects a niche. They are worth testing as additional paid channels. [L0467, 2019]
- **Small share:** Bing and other search engines are much smaller than Google and should not form a significant part of the marketing effort. [L1486, 2021-22]
- **Taboola (case):** Taboola ads were tested at about $10K monthly revenue and did not work, because the seller lacked good information on how to run that channel. Focusing back on Facebook ads brought a large increase instead. [L1127, 2024]

**X (Twitter):**

- **Not recommended:** Twitter ads are not recommended, since they produced poor results in the presenter's own testing. [L0663, 2019]
- **Narrow fit:** X advertising is considered worthwhile mainly for fashion and jewelry in the US, and to a lesser extent the UK. It is not a good channel for large spend, where Facebook, Google and TikTok are the main platforms. [L1215, 2023+]
- **Bot traffic (2024):** Advertising on X did not work for the course author, and the traffic appeared to be mostly bots. Do not expect it to convert. [L1285, 2024]

**YouTube:**

- **Alone rarely works:** YouTube ads alone rarely work for drop shipping with a small budget. The platform is better suited to established brands with a strong budget and studio-quality creatives. [L0788, undated]
- **Journey role:** YouTube video ads can build brand awareness early in a multi-touch journey (see Multi-Channel Journeys). [L1142, 2020-26]
- **Expansion order:** YouTube ads are the first expansion step once a store is above $50,000 in monthly sales (see Expansion Triggers). [L1085, 2018]

**Marketplaces and classifieds:**

- **Amazon and eBay:** For a dropship store, Amazon and eBay are not recommended as sales channels. Pinterest and Instagram are considered worth selling on alongside the store. [L0960, 2019]
- **Free marketplace posts (2023):** Free posts in marketplaces such as Facebook and Craigslist can bring the first sales and cash before any paid advertising starts. Paid campaigns can follow once the store has a few sales and optimized product pages. [L0964, 2023]
- **Classifieds, zero-budget start:** A zero-budget start is possible with classified ads, but reinvest the first sales into paid Google Ads at about $1,000 per month once they come in. [L0663, 2019]
- **Classifieds, city choice:** Do not post the same classified ad in every city, because such ads get flagged. Post in the classified section of a few large, high-traffic cities such as New York (Manhattan) and Los Angeles. [L0663, 2019]

**Blocked niches and outreach:**

- **Direct placements:** When Google and Meta ads are blocked for a niche, buy placements directly on busy niche forums, Facebook groups that sell cover image space, or niche blogs, either through affiliate deals or paid ads. Link these placements to strong content pages, not only product pages, to build authority. [L0467, 2019]
- **Snapchat restriction (2025-2026):** Snapchat's Spotlight and Stories sharing was restricted for users under 16, who can no longer share outside mutual-friend groups. Brands targeting younger audiences on Snapchat need to account for this reduced sharing reach. [L1504, 2025-26]

**Local platforms:**

- **Later experiment:** Test local shopping platforms or comparison engines only after the store is profitable and the owner has built up cash reserves. Before that point, they are a later experiment, not a launch channel. [L0710, 2020-26]
- **Not ruled out:** Local shopping platforms are not ruled out for good. They can perform well in some markets, so they are a valid option once the core business generates profit. [L0710, 2020-26]

**New sources:**

- **Market research:** New and unique traffic sources are found through market research in your specific industry, not copied from others (see Platform Choice and Channel Fit). [L0413, 2016]

### Channel Expansion Triggers

- **Google first:** Expansion beyond Google only makes sense once the store is highly profitable and Google ads can no longer be scaled further. [L0586, 2020-26]
- **Squeeze Google:** Before adding new traffic channels, squeeze as much volume as possible out of Google, because splitting focus across channels too early dilutes results. [L0586, 2020-26]
- **Local platform trigger:** Test local shopping platforms or comparison engines only after the store is profitable and cash reserves exist. [L0710, 2020-26]
- **Profit trigger:** Local shopping platforms become a valid option once the core business generates profit. [L0710, 2020-26]
- **Mistake to avoid:** Jumping to a new local channel while the core system is not yet profitable or running splits attention and cash. [L0710, 2020-26]
- **Do not switch while it works:** Switching to unproven channels while the existing system works adds risk without a clear gain. [L0710, 2020-26]
- **Do not launch many at once:** Launching several traffic channels at once, such as Facebook ads and PLAs together, spreads a beginner too thin and usually causes trouble. Build a foundation with one channel at a time and expand step by step. [L1203, 2016]
- **Paid search first:** Establish profitable paid search first, then add SEO, social and content. [L0463, 2020-26]
- **Do not rely only on paid:** Do not rely entirely on paid ads for traffic, because paid advertising keeps getting more expensive. Add other channels so the business is less dependent on ad spend. [L1251, 2024+]
- **Defensive advantage:** Running several profitable traffic channels together, such as paid search, SEO, social, content and email, makes the business harder for new competitors to displace, because each channel supports the others. [L0463, 2020-26]
- **Mature business:** A mature business can run four or five profitable traffic streams. [L0439, 2021-22]
- **Expansion order, store above $50,000 per month:** Once a store is above $50,000 in monthly sales:
  1. Add YouTube ads to drive traffic (still under Google).
  2. Add Dream 100 outreach and influencer partnerships.
  3. Add ad platforms that fit your specific industry.

  [L1085, 2018]
- **Testing share:** At larger ad budgets, about 10% of the monthly ad budget can go to untested channels, while about 90% stays on proven campaigns (see Experimentation Budgets). [L0970, 2023]
- **Keep learning the basics first:** Learn each traffic channel in two passes, starting with a short platform overview before detailed material (see Experimentation Budgets). [L0806, 2024]

### Traffic Ownership Types

**Controlled (paid) traffic:**

- **Definition:** Controlled traffic is traffic you can direct anywhere, including solo ads, banner ads, native ads, pay-per-click on Facebook, Google and Yahoo, and affiliate or joint venture traffic. You pay again every time you want more of it. This makes paid traffic powerful but expensive to scale. [L0523, 2015+]
- **Paid is controlled and measurable:** If a business's website converts visitors into customers, paying to acquire those customers is a sound strategy, since paid traffic is controlled and measurable. [L1455, 2018+]

**Free and organic traffic:**

- **Organic and free listings:** The Google organic search and free product listing rows of the traffic table are free (see Channel Overview). [L0586, 2020-26]
- **Free-only stores:** A store running on free traffic alone is valid for products where ads do not convert. [L0855, 2023]
- **Free marketplace posts:** Free posts in marketplaces can bring first sales before paid advertising begins. [L0964, 2023]
- **Free Pinterest traffic:** Pinterest works best as free traffic for stores with an existing audience. [L1136, 2014]

**Owned audience and email:**

- **Email and audiences:** Building an audience from people who already acted on your site or social accounts lowers ad costs and raises conversion rates. [L0757, 2022]
- **Email capture:** Lead generation pages need an email capture form, and retargeting plus email nurture the visitor. [L0922, 2021-22]
- **Email in the journey:** Users sign up for an email list to get a coupon, which builds trust before the purchase. [L1142, 2020-26]

**Outreach and partnerships:**

- **Dream 100 and affiliates:** Traditional funnels include Dream 100 outreach (see Platform Choice and Channel Fit). [L0417, 2020-26]
- **Affiliate and own website:** Affiliate marketing, Amazon presence, organic search and the own website are named as examples of profitable streams. [L0439, 2021-22]
- **Influencers:** Influencer content often works best as the first exposure before retargeting and an after-visit funnel for unsearched products (see Funnel Stages). [L0510, 2018]

**Omnipresence:** Being present wherever the customer spends time online within the niche (see Platform Choice and Channel Fit). [L0414, 2021-22; L1117, 2021-22]

### Experimentation Budgets

- **Test each channel with real spend:** Not every traffic channel pays off. Some work well while others mostly waste ad budget. Test each channel with real spend and measure the results instead of assuming one works. [L0759, 2020-26]
- **Ten percent rule:** At larger ad budgets, about 10% of the monthly ad budget can go to untested channels such as TikTok ads, Performance Max, a new banner placement, or content-driven ads, while about 90% stays on proven campaigns. For example, a $1,000 monthly budget splits into $900 proven and $100 testing. [L0970, 2023]
- **Pinterest test budget:** A daily budget of about $5 for about seven days, roughly $35 in total (see Pinterest). [L0788, undated]
- **Pinterest budget comparison:** The most profitable products ran at $5 per day, while some tests at $100 per day did not perform better early on. [L1134, undated]
- **Audience testing:** Aim for at least 3 to 4 winning audiences per product, so each can be scaled separately. [L0648, undated]
- **Reinvest the first sales:** Reinvest first classified-ad sales into paid Google Ads at about $1,000 per month. [L0663, 2019]
- **Cash reserves:** Test local shopping platforms only after the store is profitable and cash reserves are built up. [L0710, 2020-26]
- **Keep the test running:** Do not stop paid ads when a niche product is only getting clicks without sales. Keep spending while optimizing keywords, bids and product pages. [L0856, 2024]
- **Time to learn on TikTok:** When switching an existing account to a new product, expect one to two weeks of slow results. [L1383, 2022]
- **Organic first:** Use organic content to find a winning product before spending on Spark Ads. [L1383, 2022]
- **Learning passes (2024):** Learn each channel in two passes: first a short overview covering Google Ads, Microsoft Ads, Meta Ads and SEO, then the detailed how-to material. Understanding the basics first makes the detailed lessons easier to apply. [L0806, 2024]
- **Scaling readiness:** Once the message resonates with the customer, reaching US$1k per day or even US$10k per day becomes a scaling problem rather than a mystery. [L1019, 2026]

### Multi-Channel Journeys

- **Channels influence each other:** Paid channels influence each other, so a campaign on one platform can lift total site traffic from other sources. Judge channels as a connected system, not as isolated silos. [L1142, 2020-26]
- **Typical journey with paid traffic:**
  1. Google automatically places a user who watches videos on a topic (e.g. health benefits of a product category) into an interest targeting category.
  2. Show that user a relevant YouTube video ad to build brand awareness.
  3. The user later searches for a buyer's guide, sees the brand in a Google search ad and clicks, which lifts CTR.
  4. The user signs up for the email list to get a coupon, which builds trust.
  5. The user searches for the product and clicks a Shopping ad, where the prior touchpoints make conversion more likely.

  [L1142, 2020-26]
- **Cross-platform lift:** Facebook ads can drive branded search on Google, so both platforms feed each other. [L0561, 2023+]
- **Flywheel:** Cold video ads prospect large audiences and build a warm pool as a byproduct (every 50% video viewer). Bottom-of-funnel ads harvest that warm pool with high ROAS. The longer cold video runs, the larger the warm pool grows, and the better bottom-of-funnel performs, all without changing bottom-of-funnel creative. This is why ROAS climbs passively over time. [L0316, 2026]
- **Shopper chain:** Shopping ads, then remarketing and trust-building emails, each setup feeding the next (see Meta). [L0757, 2022]
- **Multi-channel thinking:** The largest and most successful advertisers transfer learnings from Google Ads into content marketing, use Google Ads to amplify content, and use paid traffic to grow social media and SEO. Plan each paid channel with its effect on the other channels in mind. [L1142, 2020-26]
- **Four channels with four stages:** Free organic search, free Shopping listings, paid Shopping ads and remarketing each cover a different stage of the buyer journey. [L1393, 2020-26]
- **Measurement:** Use Google Analytics as the default measurement tool. [L0721, 2021-22]

### Advertising Principles

**Foundation before tactics:**

- **Strategy first:** Paid traffic results depend on a solid strategic foundation, and tactics only add value on top of it. Collecting tips without that foundation makes campaigns weaker, not stronger. [L1031, 2018]
- **Tip versus strategy:** A tip is a single action, while a strategy states the conditions under which that action works. For example, retargeting is a tip that performs well in e-commerce, but its strategic condition is that the first audience was targeted correctly. [L1031, 2018]
- **Tips on a broken base:** Relying on blog tips and tricks without a strong foundation leaves paid traffic performing worse, because the tips get applied to a broken base. [L1031, 2018]

**Cost and margin:**

- **Margins are thin:** High-ticket dropshipping has relatively modest margins, so the cost to acquire each customer must be controlled carefully. Each paid click has a direct cost, which is why selective targeting matters more in this model. Expect profitability to suffer if you buy every available click. [L1507, 2021-22]
- **Business model matters:** Paid clicks cost money and more spend usually brings more traffic, so business model matters as much as ad skill. A business that can spend more to acquire a customer, due to a higher cart value or a funnel with lead magnets, can outbid competitors and appear in better positions more often. [L1386, 2020-26]
- **Case, two surfing models:** Store A sells a single surfboard for $800 and pays $100 to acquire a customer, advertising commercial terms to people who are still asking whether they are strong enough to surf. Store B starts from the bottom of the funnel, sells surfboard packages with wax and wetsuit for $1,500, can afford $200 per customer acquisition, and offers a top-of-funnel ebook for $5 to collect leads who are later converted. Store B's higher cart value lets it outbid Store A and show more often in better positions, and the leads it collects feed more money into bottom-of-funnel ads. [L1386, 2020-26]
- **Reading high click costs:** If clicks look expensive in your niche, competitors may have found a better business model or funnel. Treat high click costs as a signal to examine how rivals fund acquisition, for example through higher cart values or cheap lead magnets. [L1386, 2020-26]
- **Scaling:** Once the message resonates, scaling is a question of execution rather than a mystery (see Experimentation Budgets). [L1019, 2026]

**Relevance and intent:**

- **Warm cheaper than cold:** Advertising to warm visitors is cheaper than cold prospecting. [L0757, 2022]
- **Traffic quality:** Traffic quality matters more than traffic volume (see Shopping and Search Intent). [L1223, 2024+]
- **Do not chase non-searchers:** Spending on people who are not looking for the product wastes budget (see Funnel Stages). [L1031, 2018]

**Investment logic:**

- **Acquiring customers is an investment:** If a business's website converts visitors into customers, paying to acquire those customers is a sound strategy, since paid traffic is controlled and measurable. [L1455, 2018+]

**Channel-level caution:**

- **Not every channel pays off:** Test each channel with real spend and measure results (see Experimentation Budgets). [L0759, 2020-26]
- **Do not overspend on Shopping for undercut products:** (see Shopping and Search Intent). [L0969, 2022]

### Lead Generation

- **Purpose:** Lead generation targets top-of-funnel searchers who are not ready to buy yet. The goal is to build brand familiarity and nurture them over time, so the brand is already trusted when they reach the buying stage, ideally before competitors do. [L0922, 2021-22]
- **Payback timing:** Lead generation is an investment that pays back over months, not on the first sale. Hot audiences who search product names, brand names or SKU numbers buy fastest, but lead generation targets people who are not ready to buy yet. [L1076, 2019]
- **Three prerequisites:** Lead generation with search ads depends on three elements: a content page with valuable content, retargeting set up for visitors of that page, and an email capture form on the page. Without follow-up retargeting and email, the visitor is not nurtured and the traffic produces no sales. [L0922, 2021-22]
- **Lead magnets in the model:** A top-of-funnel ebook for $5 collects leads who are later converted, and those leads feed more money into bottom-of-funnel ads (see Advertising Principles). [L1386, 2020-26]
- **Email in the journey:** A user who signs up for the email list to get a coupon builds trust before buying (see Multi-Channel Journeys). [L1142, 2020-26]
