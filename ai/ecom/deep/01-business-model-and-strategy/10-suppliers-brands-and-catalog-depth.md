## Suppliers, brands and catalog depth

### Demo store and readiness before outreach

- Create 10 to 20 demo products before contacting suppliers, because suppliers will not work with a store they cannot picture on the site. [L0036, 2020-26]
- Demo products are placeholder products with AI-generated images, names and descriptions. Suppliers see them to judge what the store will look like before you ask for authorization, which raises your chances of becoming an authorized retailer. [L0088, undated]
- Build the website first and then pursue supplier approvals, because approvals and supplier relationships depend on a working store. Plan the build for the next few months rather than waiting for a perfect launch. [L1287, 2025]

### Becoming an authorized retailer

- Authorization requires that you first present yourself as an official business entity with professional infrastructure: official business registration, a professional business email and a separate business phone. It also requires a proven supplier contact strategy before approval becomes possible. [L0109, undated]
- The aim of supplier outreach is approval with about five suppliers, so that you are an authorized retailer for five brands in your niche. The approval is the milestone that matters before any live product sales. [L1063, 2020-26]
- The goal is approval with every supplier in the industry, not celebrating the first approvals. Keep contacting unapproved brands every day and follow up with brands already contacted. [L0962, 2021]
- Non-native English speakers should not hesitate to contact suppliers. Several successful one source members are non-native speakers with seven-figure businesses, and most rejections are simply one supplier among many. [L0268, 2020]
- Age should not limit you. Representing yourself professionally, including having a phone number, establishes legitimacy regardless of how young you sound. For high-ticket products the customer's concern is business credibility, not age. [L0268, 2020]

### Finding and approaching suppliers

#### How many suppliers to list

- Find at least 20 dropship-friendly suppliers for a niche before committing to it, because only a few will approve in the first days. Even if only about 5 approve at the start, the remaining prospects are followed up later. [L0468, 2020]
- Treat supplier count as a numbers game early on. Many prospects decline or stay silent, so a long list keeps momentum going. Treat rejections as data and return to the other prospects later. [L0468, 2020]
- Pick one product type and source many units of it from many suppliers rather than relying on a single supplier. This spreads supply risk across the catalogue. [L0934, 2023]

#### Where to find prospects

1. Open the websites of your top competitors. [L1068, 2019]
2. Go to their bestseller pages. [L1068, 2019]
3. Note which suppliers make those bestselling products. [L1068, 2019]
4. Contact those suppliers first. [L1068, 2019]

- A business owner with an existing physical store can open an ecommerce store next to it and use existing supplier relationships as a competitive advantage. Online-only sellers usually cannot access those relationships. [L1068, 2019]
- If a niche has too few suppliers, broaden to a related category with more product types, for example from rocking chairs to outdoor furniture. This increases the number of brands you can get approved with. [L0468, 2020]
- Brands sold only through physical stores do not count as future competitors or suppliers for the drop-ship model. Look for online-only brands with no warehouse or showroom. [L0468, 2020]
- Selling a product type that is also sold in local retail stores is acceptable, as long as the brands you contact primarily sell online. Brands that depend on a showroom or physical stores are not suitable suppliers. [L0468, 2020]
- Local suppliers hold stock in the customer's own country, so shipping is typically 1 to 7 days, the products are already sold and tested in that market, and the brands are known to buyers. Stock in the customer's country is a fixed requirement: only work with suppliers who already sell in the same country as the customers. [L0771, 2021-22]

#### What to ask for

- Ask the supplier for a current product catalogue and review it before making an offer, because the best sourcing decisions depend on the full list. Many suppliers list products across unrelated niches, so filter those out before you start. [L0965, 2024]
- When a supplier asks for annual sales goals for a brand-new store, a reasonable answer is a figure between 2 and 500,000 (the currency was not stated). [L0934, 2023]

### Supplier fit: margins and pricing

- A supplier with a sliding-scale margin, where the margin depends on sales volume, is not automatically disqualified. Lower-margin products can still be listed, but they may be hard to run ads profitably on. [L0666, 2024]
- Pivot question: if a store has about 10 supplier approvals but low margins, keep the store live, upload some low-margin products without paid ads for credibility, and keep reaching out to higher-margin suppliers. Do not pivot completely. [L0856, 2024]
- Operating in a country with no MAP policy means price matching cannot be relied on, so pricing must be set from wholesale costs and competitor data. [L0967, 2020]
- Tariffs or supplier cost increases raise wholesale prices, which raises MAP and therefore customer prices. The effect applies to all competitors at the same time, so no single store loses sales to another. [L1109, 2018]

#### Case: a MAP-priced store with weak early results

A store owner spent a month building a store where items priced between $1,000 and $2,000 MAP had an average margin of about 20%, and the first week of ads produced one sale. The advice was not to switch niches. Instead, add more approved suppliers first, because about 80% of sales often come from about 20% of products. Until then, cut wasted ad spend using the optimization steps, and spend early money on clicks that bring quality traffic while testing keywords and price points. [L0835, 2023]

### Adding value and relationship building

- A supplier relationship depends on you adding value to their business beyond transactional order fulfilment. This is essential for building trust with silver and gold suppliers. [L0121, undated]
- Relationship-building tactics such as gifts, insider access and negotiated pricing belong to a later stage: roughly three to six months, or up to a year, after the business starts. They do not belong in the pre-launch phase or the first weeks after the store goes live. [L0939, 2020-26]

### Early traffic and brand choice

- Early traffic is easiest to get from brands and products people already search for on Google and Google Shopping. Smaller, unknown brands are best added later, when the store already has traffic, an email list and a social following to promote them. [L1114, 2023]

### Catalogue size and expansion

#### Why supplier count matters

- Adding more authorized suppliers and products is one of the two biggest levers for profit and revenue, the other being traffic growth. Stores with 20 to 40 brands have seen one additional supplier add 30 to 50% to revenue, and the impact of a single brand is hard to predict. [L0938, 2020-26]
- The two main drivers of store size are supplier approvals for strong brands with in-demand products, and sufficient traffic from ads and SEO. Market size sets the ceiling for how big a store can grow. [L1287, 2025]
- About 80% of revenue often comes from about 20% of products. This ratio may change over time, but it remains a consistent theme. [L0249, 2022]
- Low-selling products should not be removed. Instead, optimise their pages for conversion or reduce their advertising costs through lower CPC bids. [L0249, 2022]

#### How many brands and products

- Do not run a store with only three brands. Diversify to around 20 brands so that one supplier running out of stock does not leave the store empty. [L0468, 2020]
- A store that sells a wide range of products should start with a minimum of 100 products. Over time, it should add all the quality products from quality suppliers in the niche. [L0963, 2022]
- A new store can start with around 100 products and grow over months to 500, then 1,000 products, as more suppliers are approved. A niche can ultimately hold 5,000 to 10,000 products. [L0963, 2022]

#### Growing a single-product store

- Expand the product line only after one product has proven to be a winner. Then add small upsell products, free add-on items, complementary products or products from a similar niche. [L0435, undated]
- Once a product is winning, expand the product line by adding small upsell products, free items, complementary products or similar-niche products. These additions make the offer more attractive. [L0434, undated]
- A single-product store should gradually add complementary products from the same category, for example several beauty products instead of one brush. This enables better supplier volume discounts and creates a cohesive brand identity across the range. [L0324, 2025+]
- Adding complementary products or products from the same niche can attract a broader customer base. Different shoppers relate to different products, so each new product can bring in buyers the first product would miss. [L0434, undated; L0435, undated]
- Several related categories in one niche help a brand build authority in that niche. Each category is also a separate product that can be scaled with paid ads on its own, so a store gets more than one potential winner. [L0435, undated]

#### Case: Blissey (silk products)

Blissey started with a single product, silk pillowcases. It later added a silk sleep mask, silk hair accessories and further categories in the same silk-related messaging, and now sells several categories. The benefits shown were:

- complementary products encourage a second purchase;
- new categories are launched by email to the existing customer base with no ad spend;
- each category can become a separate winner to scale with paid ads;
- The brand builds authority in silk products;
- it attracts shoppers who were not interested in the pillowcases. [L0434, undated; L0435, undated]

#### Case: a UK two-store business

A UK dropshipping business with two Shopify stores reached its biggest month to date in April 2018, with 76,000 GBP in sales (about 100,000 USD), after adding more suppliers and uploading more products. The owners reached 1 million USD in total sales within roughly the first year across the two stores. [L1441, 2018]

### Sourcing scale and cost

- Competitors selling below your cost may be buying in bulk. Source products from Alibaba at larger quantities, for example 1,000 units, to check whether a cheaper cost of goods is possible. [L0640, 2023]
- Start lean: source from AliExpress or CJ Dropshipping first, and move to private suppliers only when a product is a clear winner. This keeps the time invested low until the ads prove the product. [L0642, undated]
- Focus first on consistent daily sales, such as around 20 to 50 sales per day, before switching suppliers to lower prices. Demand consistency matters more than a small cost saving. [L1426, undated]
- Dropshipping does not require you to hold stock, so inventory lives with the supplier. Only the top sellers need active stock monitoring. [L0671, 2021]

### Tier-two resellers

- Buying stock through a middleman who already holds a supplier account (a tier-two reseller arrangement) should be avoided, because the margin can disappear very quickly. [L0835, 2023]

### Sales channels and channel conflict

#### Wayfair

- Do not sell through Wayfair in this model, because Wayfair sells directly to consumers and therefore competes with the store. [L0247, 2021]
- Wayfair works directly with dropship brands, so it is not a retail channel for a dropshipper. Only private label products can be sold there. [L0963, 2022]

#### Amazon, eBay and other third-party platforms

These two units disagree. Both are kept here with the conditions under which each applies:

| Source | Advice | When it applies |
|---|---|---|
| one source [L0268, 2020] | Selling the same products on Wayfair, Amazon or other marketplaces alongside your own store creates no conflict; treat these as additional distribution channels. | Where no supplier agreement forbids third-party sales. |
| one source [L0272, 2022] | Do not sell on Amazon, eBay or any third-party platform. Suppliers typically forbid third-party sales in their agreements. Sell exclusively through your own Shopify store. | Where supplier agreements forbid third-party sales, which the source describes as typical. |

- Selling through eBay via a Shopify app is also not recommended. [L0247, 2021]
- Listing products on large third-party marketplaces such as Walmart.com is not a recommended main sales channel, because they host many outside sellers and are not niche-specific. If a marketplace is considered, it needs its own separate evaluation of fees, competition and margins. [L0359, 2020]
