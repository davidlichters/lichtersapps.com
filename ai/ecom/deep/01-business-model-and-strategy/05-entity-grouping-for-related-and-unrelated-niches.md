### Entity grouping for related and unrelated niches

#### Core rule

- For related niches (for example office chairs and desks), operate under a single LLC. For completely unrelated niches (for example office furniture and greenhouses), create separate business entities to compartmentalize finances and simplify future sale of individual stores. [L0251, 2023]
- Closely related businesses (for example modern office desks and modern office chairs) share one business entity, bank account, and credit card. Unrelated businesses receive their own business entity, bank account, and credit card. Determine relatedness by asking: Would the same customer buy from both? Would I cross-promote them? [L0253, 2024]
- Group related stores under one LLC and put unrelated niches into separate LLCs. This keeps money from being mixed, simplifies accounting, and makes it easier to sell related stores as a bundle later. [L0941, 2022]
- Each niche should stand as its own business line. Related niches that customers can cross-promote can sit under one parent company, while unrelated niches need a separate entity. This lets stores be sold together as a network. [L0667, 2025]

#### Related stores

- Stores in related niches are grouped under one LLC. This is the name used on supplier forms, the bank account and tax filings, while each store keeps its own brand and domain. [L0695, 2020-26]
- Related stores that are cross-promoted with each other can share one business entity and one EIN. [L0934, 2023]
- Example: a trampoline store and a bounce house store, which would be cross-promoted, share one corporation and one EIN. [L0934, 2023]
- Closely related stores sell better as a bundle or network, since buyers value a group of stores in one niche. Keep a separate legal entity for each group of related businesses so accounting stays clean and the group can be sold as one unit. ** [L0468, 2020]
- Stores that belong to the same network of related niches should sit under one company. A future buyer of one store usually wants a non-compete covering the whole network. ** [L1277, 2020]
- Sites in related niches that target the same customers can share one LLC. ** [L0969, 2022]
- Stores that are indirectly related, for example through cross-promotion, should be kept under one business entity so they can be sold together later. [L1114, 2023]
- One company entity can own several stores with different store names and domains. The company's assets are the domains, the store design, the customer lists and the supplier relationships, so a single entity can hold multiple stores. [L1114, 2023]
- One company can own several store domains. Form one company, such as a US LLC, and build each store under it. Each store can run as its own brand under the same legal entity. ** [L0469, 2021]
- One LLC can own several niche stores, but each store should keep its own identity and accounts. ** [L0358, 2020]

#### Unrelated stores

- Unrelated business areas, such as a store in a different niche, get a separate LLC. This keeps the books and accounting clean and makes it simpler to sell one business later without affecting the others. [L0695, 2020-26]
- A store in an unrelated niche should get its own LLC and EIN, so its accounting can be separated if it is sold later. [L0934, 2023]
- Example: a horse saddle store that is unrelated to the trampoline and bounce house stores gets a new LLC and a new EIN. [L0934, 2023]
- A store in an unrelated niche should be under a separate LLC, so it can be sold or audited without affecting others. ** [L0969, 2022]
- A new, unrelated niche should go under a separate entity, even if the same owner runs it with the same skills. ** [L1277, 2020]

#### Account separation

Sources differ on how far separation goes inside a shared entity. Apply the one that matches the relationship:

- Closely related stores share one bank account and one credit card. [L0253, 2024]
- Each store keeps its own identity and accounts, even within one LLC. ** [L0358, 2020]
- Each store keeps its own brand and domain under the same entity. [L0695, 2020-26]

### EINs and US registration

- A US EIN is recommended for doing business in the US. This applies to a Canadian business selling into the US. [L0360, 2024]
- A business can start as a sole proprietor with an EIN. [L0970, 2023]
- Related stores that are cross-promoted share one EIN. An unrelated store gets its own EIN. [L0934, 2023]

### Taxes and administration

- Setting up a company in a country with a favorable tax structure, such as Ireland, can be considered for selling to other markets. The tax benefit must be checked for the specific business before it is relied on. [L0359, 2020]
- Confirm the legal and tax requirements for your country and the US states you sell into before launch. ** [L0961, 2020]
- Dropshipping from a non-US country can be set up under a US company. Check the choice of entity with a forum or professional before setup. ** [L0357, 2019]
- Running the store from a non-US company can work with US suppliers. Check the legal side with an accountant or a community forum. ** [L1085, 2018]
- To check whether a home-based business needs a local license after moving to a new state, search for that state's business registration or business license page on a.gov website and call the phone number listed there. Ask your question directly, since the office can give the state-specific answer. [L0970, 2023]
- Spending dozens of hours researching where to register a business or how to minimize tax before the business earns meaningful profit wastes time that costs more than the savings. Register it where you already live while income is small. [L1515, 2019]

### Common mistakes

- Launching a cross-border high-ticket model without pricing in freight, customs duties and currency conversion can leave little or no margin. ** [L0781, 2021-22]
- Mixing personal and business funds removes the liability protection of a business entity. [L0970, 2023]
- Spending dozens of hours researching where to register a business or how to minimize tax before profits arrive wastes time. [L1515, 2019]
- Choosing a market by cheap traffic alone, instead of buying power, makes a poor test market. [L0361, 2024]
- Expanding abroad before the home market is proven. Start with one country and scale within it first. ** [L1277, 2020]
