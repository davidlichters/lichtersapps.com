### Print on demand

- Print on demand is very hard to make meaningful money with, because profit per sale is tiny. It is better treated as a brand-building add-on than as a profit source, especially since personal items such as clothing and bedding have high return rates when fit or feel does not match expectations. [L1097, 2025]
- A brand merchandise store that sells inexpensive print-on-demand items such as t-shirts, mugs and hats has very thin margins. Keep it for brand merchandise rather than treating it as a profit source. [L0852, 2020]
- Print on demand is a community-building add-on, not a primary revenue source. Add it only after a store has a loyal audience that wants branded products. [L0961, 2020]
- Print on demand is not recommended, because product prices are too low, margins are too thin, ads are hard to run profitably, and there is too little profit per sale for the work involved. [L0967, 2020]

**Note on print on demand:** the 2020 sources differ in strength. One treats it as merchandise only [L0852, 2020], one as a community add-on [L0961, 2020], and one as not recommended [L0967, 2020]. The 2025 source gives the most current position: a brand-building add-on, not a profit source [L1097, 2025]. [L0852, 2020; L0961, 2020; L0967, 2020; L1097, 2025]

### Marketplaces

#### Marketplace selling as a separate model

- Marketplace selling is a separate model from dropshipping. The seller lists products on large retail marketplaces without stocking them, and fulfills an order only once it sells. [L0663, 2019]
- Etsy suits small handmade or home businesses and is less suited for building a branded store than Shopify, which gives more control over brand presentation. [L0561, 2023+]
- Depending on a single marketplace is a single point of failure: an account suspension or restriction for a policy violation can shut the whole business down. A seller who runs their own website keeps the business alive regardless of what any one marketplace decides. [L0760, undated]
- Marketplaces such as Amazon can remove a seller's account and destroy the business overnight. An own website gives control over the brand, the customer data and the option to sell the business later. [L0771, 2021-22]

### Is dropshipping still viable?

- Dropshipping is not dead. Claims that it has died come up every year, while e-commerce keeps growing. It would only die if people stopped buying online. [L1480, 2021-22]
- Dropshipping is a real business that has existed for at least 15 years and is still viable. It is cheaper to start than many other models. [L1480, 2021-22]
- Dropshipping is a short-term model only when done poorly. Done correctly, it builds a brand and customer knowledge that can be used later to sell your own products. [L1481, 2021-22]
- Dropshipping stores often fade after two or three months. Brands that last test consistently after a winning product and invest in customer experience. [L1213, 2023]
- Platforms such as Facebook do not ban dropshipping as a model. They judge account quality, customer ratings and copied content. [L1213, 2023]
- After the pandemic, e-commerce adoption normalized to the historical 20-year growth trend. The spike during lockdown created excess capacity. Shopify laid off 1,000 people (10% of its workforce) on July 26, 2022, because growth returned to normal rather than to a sustained high trajectory. [L0272, 2022]
- Shopify's public data reportedly showed about a 10% revenue increase for the average store in Q1 2025 compared with Q1 2024. Use this only as a general market signal. [L0972, 2025]
- AI dropshipping keeps the same business model as regular dropshipping: selling products online without holding inventory. The difference is that AI tools are applied at every step of the process, so the seller can move faster, smarter and leaner. [L1459, 2026]
- Between 2016 and 2018, vendors flooded AliExpress listings with lengthy delivery times. The approach paid off because Facebook advertising was inexpensive, yet the buyer experience suffered. [L1459, 2026]
- Branded dropshipping took off from 2019 through 2021. Quicker delivery, stronger ad creatives and bespoke storefronts distinguished the successful operators from the rest, but most workflows stayed hands-on and experimentation moved slowly. [L1459, 2026]
- From 2023 onward, the AI era allows a business to run with fewer people, lower costs and data-driven decisions instead of intuition alone. [L1459, 2026]
- **Note on viability:** the sources do not conflict on whether the model works. "Not dead" [L1480, 2021-22] applies to dropshipping as a whole, while "fades after two or three months" [L1213, 2023] applies to stores run without a brand plan. The short-term outcome depends on how the store is built. [L1480, 2021-22; L1213, 2023; L1481, 2021-22]

### Choosing a business model

- When choosing an online business model, compare the options on three criteria: whether it can be run remotely, how much startup capital it needs, and whether it can be built part-time next to a job. Common options to compare are affiliate marketing, selling a service, selling online education and selling physical products through e-commerce. Physical-product dropshipping can meet all three criteria for someone who still has a job and fixed financial obligations. [L1014, 2021-22]
- Dropshipping with approved suppliers and low-priced products suits a first step with low capital. Choose the model that matches your remote working needs, your starting capital and the time you can give alongside other obligations. [L1014, 2021-22; L0345, 2018; L1283, 2023]
