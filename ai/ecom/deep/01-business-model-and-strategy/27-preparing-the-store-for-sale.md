## Preparing the store for sale

#### Six months before the sale

1. Make your final changes to content, link profile and SEO, including any change of monetization model, because Google needs a couple of months to recognize them. [L1468, 2014]
2. Start tracking revenue, costs and owner time separately for each store. [L1468, 2014]
3. Order or run a basic due diligence report to find problems buyers would flag, and fix what you can. [L1468, 2014]
4. Cut exploratory paid traffic that has no ROI and focus on what produces net profit. [L1468, 2014]
5. Speed up the site with quick code fixes rather than an expensive hosting upgrade. [L1468, 2014]
6. Audit the backlink profile, disavow spam links, and reduce over-optimized anchor text by adding more varied anchors. [L1468, 2014]
7. Replace duplicate product descriptions with unique ones. [L1468, 2014]
8. Install Google Analytics (or an accepted alternative such as Clicky) and make sure you control or can grant admin access. [L1468, 2014]

#### Three months before the sale

1. Compare current traffic trend (stable, growing, declining or seasonal) with the changes you made three months earlier and check whether they are still helping. [L1468, 2014]
2. If growth is still climbing steeply, consider delaying the listing until it levels off. [L1468, 2014]
3. Identify strategic buyers, get to know competitors and check whether they would be open to acquiring your business. [L1468, 2014]
4. Check that supplier, affiliate and special pricing terms are clean and can be transferred to a new owner. [L1468, 2014]
5. Verify that you are visibly a real person online, such as with real social profiles and connections. [L1468, 2014]

Start looking for strategic buyers and talking to competitors about three months before the sale. Strategic buyers are the route most likely to produce the highest multiple. [L1468, 2014]

#### One month before the sale

1. Document all processes as standard operating procedures (SOPs). [L1468, 2014]
2. Replace yourself in day-to-day work with VAs, so the business runs without you. [L1468, 2014]
3. Review the financials, cut useless expenses, and confirm revenue and profit are clean and tracked per store. [L1468, 2014]
4. Set up a training and site-turnover plan for the new owner. [L1468, 2014]
5. Add at least two alternate suppliers, even if you do not use them. [L1468, 2014]
6. Shop for a broker or platform that fits your site and price target. [L1468, 2014]
7. Turn off WHOIS privacy so your name shows as the domain owner. [L1468, 2014]
8. Prepare the listing materials: earnings records, traffic data, and any video or screencast evidence. [L1468, 2014]
9. Make sure all financial figures in the listing match each other exactly and that date ranges and metric definitions (sessions or page views) are stated. [L1468, 2014]

#### Sale timeline

- Prepare a sale plan about six months before the sale, because the site needs time to recover from changes. [L1359, 2014; L1468, 2014]
- Vetting usually takes 2 to 4 weeks. [L0554, 2019]
- Typical time on market is about 2 to 3 months. Average time on market in the last 12 months was 66 days. [L0553, 2018]
- Store listed with a broker: the full timeline is in the broker process below. [L0554, 2019]

#### Documentation and handover preparation

- When selling an online store, do not leave the handover to the end. Assemble the store's documentation, supplier and account details, and process notes before listing it, because reconstructing them after the sale is slow and frustrating. [L0826, 2016]
- Keep documentation as a separate asset. A brand built around proven sales with clean records can be transferred to another owner. [L1439, 2024+]
- A transition can be handled by informing each supplier that a new owner is taking over, asking them to remove your card from future payments, and putting the new owner's card on file. The same step applies when closing a store to stop future charges. (2023) [L0970, 2023]

## Sales channels

#### Overview of channels

| Channel | Advantages | Disadvantages | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| DIY, direct to strategic buyers (competitors) | Usually the highest price; no listing or broker fee | You must open your books (numbers, keywords, paid traffic methods) to competitors who may copy the site; heavy outreach and buyer qualification needed | Start by approaching competitors in your niche |
| Auctions and aggregators (Flippa, BizBuySell, Centurica feed) | Built-in buyer audience; Flippa approves about 98% of listings; Centurica pulls broker listings into one feed | Lower price, listings sit next to scam or low-quality sites, low multiples; domain, traffic and earnings are public and invite copycats | BizBuySell mainly lists offline businesses |
| Brokers (Empire Flippers, FE International, formerly Flippa Enterprises; Quiet Light Brokerage) | Usually the second-highest price after strategic buyers; done-for-you transfer, buyer vetting and escrow-style fund holding | Fee of 10 to 20% of the sale price; Empire Flippers rejects 30 to 40% of submitted sites | Quiet Light usually handles deals of $500,000 or more |

- Doing the sale yourself means no listing or broker fees, but finding and qualifying interested buyers takes a lot of legwork. Sellers without deal experience may find this process difficult. [L1468, 2014]
- A private sale lets the seller keep 100% of the price, but the seller must find buyers alone. Competitors often request due diligence only to copy the business, so vet any competitor carefully before sharing your books. [L0554, 2019]
- Brokers help with negotiations involving financing, caveats and time wasters, and they add migration protections for both sides. Brokers also tend to achieve higher prices than sellers on their own, even after their fee. [L0554, 2019]
- Auction platforms like Flippa offer a hidden-listing option, but using it removes most of the exposure that attracts bids. Sellers who hide their domain and traffic usually get far fewer buyers, so the trade-off between privacy and reach must be weighed deliberately. [L1468, 2014]
- Online businesses such as Amazon stores or Shopify stores can be sold through EmpireFlippers.com, which handles sales of sites from tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands of dollars. To start, use the sell-your-site button in the top-right corner of the website. [L0963, 2022]
- Empire Flippers is an online business brokerage marketplace where owners list online businesses for sale and buyers evaluate them. Listings there require accurate financial records. [L0449, 2021-22]
- Empire Flippers is an online business brokerage that buys and sells websites and can help value a store. It was cited as having sold over $560 million in websites. (2025) [L1097, 2025]
- Empire Flippers listings and brokers such as Empire Flippers handle buying and selling stores. Selling a profitable store, investing the proceeds and starting another store can be a time-saving cycle. [L1515, 2019]
- Business brokerages list drop shipping stores for sale and usually show revenue and profit figures for each one. [L0803, 2022]
- A dropshipping store that earns steady monthly revenue and profit can itself be sold as a whole business on marketplaces such as Flippa. Buyers list the domain together with verified revenue and profit figures, so a profitable store also works as an exit option. [L0549, undated]
- Members can sell their online stores to other members. A store for sale is posted in the For Sale section at the top of the channel list, and members browse that section to see available stores. [L0517, 2020-26]
- Online stores can be sold through brokers that specialize in internet business sales, such as Empire Flippers. Sale prices in examples of this kind ranged from about $44,000 to $60,000 per store, and the proceeds can then be invested. [L1126, 2018]
- Doing it yourself: start by approaching competitors in your niche. [L1468, 2014]

#### Broker process

Steps for selling a dropshipping store through a broker, in order:

1. Get an initial online valuation on the broker website. [L0554, 2019]
2. Submit the business for vetting, which usually takes 2 to 4 weeks and requires a large amount of information, including checks of the Shopify numbers. [L0554, 2019]
3. The broker markets the business to its buyer list and may run seller interviews for YouTube. [L0554, 2019]
4. The seller answers buyer questions and enters negotiations. [L0554, 2019]
5. The business is transferred to the buyer and the seller receives payout. [L0554, 2019]

4. Market your business and negotiate terms. [L0231, 2019]

#### Broker fees and incentives

- Brokers charge 10 to 20% of the sale price as their fee. They reject 30 to 40% of the sites submitted, and rejected sellers get their listing fee refunded. [L1468, 2014]
- Broker fees are a percentage of the final sale price, so the broker earns more only when the seller earns more. This aligns incentives, but the seller should still check the broker's listing standards and fee terms before signing. [L1468, 2014]
- The platform's brokers represent the seller and owe the seller a fiduciary duty. They earn fees on the sale price, so sellers must decide their own minimum acceptable price rather than rely on brokers to push for the highest possible offer. [L0553, 2018]
- Broker fees create a weaker incentive than the seller's own interest in price. On a store that sells for $270,000 instead of a $300,000 list price, the seller loses $30,000 while the broker's fee difference is only about $4,500, roughly 15 percent of that gap. Sellers should therefore decide their own minimum acceptable price before reviewing offers. [L0553, 2018]
- Brokers that vet listings typically have staff collect the site's information and then have a principal approve or deny each listing. Expect a curated marketplace with rejections rather than automatic acceptance. [L1468, 2014]

#### Channel eligibility

- Only online businesses are eligible for sale on this platform. Physical-location, retail, offline and real estate-related businesses are not accepted. [L0553, 2018]
- The sale platform accepts almost any online business model, including non-dropshipping types such as Kindle e-book businesses. It excludes offline businesses, businesses with a physical or local component, and real estate-related businesses. [L0553, 2018]
- A Shopify store is hard to sell unless it has at least one year of revenue history and roughly 500,000 USD in total revenue. Buyers for such stores were mostly US-based companies, according to the speaker's experience. [L1469, 2023+]
- A store with no real revenue has almost no resale value, and buyers typically want about 12 months of consistent revenue. If a store has no sales, it is usually better to drop it and focus on a new product or store than to try to sell it. [L0660, 2019+]

## Vetting and due diligence

- Brokers' vetting takes 2 to 4 weeks and requires a large amount of information, including checks of the Shopify numbers. [L0554, 2019]
- Buyers ask for the full financial history, commonly four years of financials. A business without accurate, complete numbers cannot be sold. [L0599, 2021-22]
- Basic due diligence reports from third-party services such as Centurica tend to be conservative. Fixing the issues they flag before listing reduces the chance that a buyer uses the report to negotiate the price down. [L1468, 2014]
- A business with a sustained sharp decline should be considered for sale while it still has value. A buyer pays for something, while a business at zero yields nothing. [L0449, 2021-22]
- If a business is declining toward zero, selling it earlier can still recover some value, which is better than selling nothing later. [L0599, 2021-22]
- The critical question buyers ask is whether the business will thrive and operate successfully without your direct involvement and daily management. This assessment determines whether they acquire an independent, scalable asset or commit themselves to a full-time job. [L0231, 2019]
- Buyers reject a store if it lacks a defensible moat, has poor customer experience from China shipping, depends entirely on finding winning Facebook ads, or has no established brand. [L0231, 2019]
- Store with unexplained dips: each dip should be explained, as stated above. [L0554, 2019]
- Use the broker's industry report and buyers' numbers to test the store's trend before vetting. [L0554, 2019]

## Buyer profiles

- Dropshipping store buyers comprise several profiles: cash-rich, time-poor investors seeking passive income; operators who prefer hands-on management; creators focused on building and innovation; cash flow investors prioritizing profitability; and portfolio or fund buyers acquiring multiple businesses for diversification. [L0231, 2019]
- Typical buyer profiles include a salaried person seeking a lifestyle change with $200,000 to spend on a store earning $8,000 to $9,000 monthly profit; a hands-on buyer looking for a dropshipping store around $40,000 to tweak; and an experienced e-commerce operator who adds stores to existing staff. [L0553, 2018]
- Buyers of online stores are often people with money but no time, people who want to skip the trial-and-error stage, and institutional or fund buyers. Online assets are compared with index funds and real estate as investments, with higher risk but potentially higher returns. [L0554, 2019]
- Strategic buyers, usually competitors in the same niche, generally pay the highest price. [L1468, 2014]
- Some buyers want a passive dividend and do not want to run the site. A broker can place investor capital into a site and have a managing team run it, though this was described as an early test. [L1468, 2014]
- Private equity typically buys deals of $10 million or more, and the platform handled deals up to about $5 million. Newer micro-funds pooling $10 million to $100 million for small online assets started appearing in the last 12 months. [L0553, 2018]
- Buyers want to see a business that will thrive without the owner. A store that depends on the owner is a job, not an asset. [L0231, 2019]
- Dropshipping stores remain attractive to buyers and retain strong valuations despite market concerns that dropshipping is declining. Buyers actively seek dropshipping businesses, particularly those seeking small business capital, and valuations have not fallen drastically. [L0231, 2019]
- The market outlook for dropshipping is positive, with valuations rising over the previous two years. Recessions may push buyers toward models that need less upfront capital, which can increase demand. [L0554, 2019]
- Buyers pay for proven monthly net profit. [L0553, 2018]

## Deal terms and payment

- Buyers may pay part of the price at closing and hold back the rest until a contractual obligation is met, such as finishing staff training after the handover. For example, a $300,000 sale could be split into $200,000 paid at closing and $100,000 paid once training is complete. Sellers who train their team before listing avoid this deferred-payment risk. [L0553, 2018]
- Buyers may hold back part of the payment, for example 50% upfront and 50% over six months, or 70% upfront and 30% after training and handover. This happens when the business depends on the owner's knowledge. [L1468, 2014]
- The post-sale support a seller offers should match the buyer's experience. Buyers of a small store around $40,000 are often newcomers who need more help, while buyers of stores in the $400,000 to $500,000 range usually have more experience and need less. The support scope is agreed and written down before the deal closes, and extra support can be used as a negotiating point. [L0553, 2018]
- A non-compete usually applies in the same niche for a set period after the sale. (2024) [L0836, 2024]
- Check that supplier, affiliate and special pricing terms are clean and can be transferred to a new owner. [L1468, 2014]
- Sell only once the business is clean: confirm the financials, supplier terms and documentation before terms are negotiated. [L1468, 2014; L0970, 2023]

## Handover and SOPs

- Document all processes as standard operating procedures (SOPs). [L1468, 2014]
- Replace yourself in day-to-day work with VAs, so the business runs without you. [L1468, 2014]
- A pets store used a customer support virtual assistant for chat, phone calls, emails and Zendesk tickets, and the assistant was willing to continue with the new owner. [L0554, 2019]
- A beauty store had a full-time virtual assistant handling daily operations, and it had full SOPs, training documents and documentation. [L0554, 2019]
- Set up a training and site-turnover plan for the new owner. [L1468, 2014]
- Add at least two alternate suppliers, even if you do not use them. [L1468, 2014]
- Turn off WHOIS privacy so your name shows as the domain owner. [L1468, 2014]
- Make sure you control or can grant admin access to analytics. [L1468, 2014]
- When selling a store, a transition can be handled by informing each supplier that a new owner is taking over, asking them to remove your card from future payments, and putting the new owner's card on file. (2023) [L0970, 2023]
- A business with documented, transferable systems is worth substantially more than one that depends on the owner. [L1473, 2019]
- Brands with systems and records are easier to hand over. [L1439, 2024+]
- When a business with several sites is sold, the usual method is an asset sale: the sites, customer lists, historical data and SOPs are sold. The entity itself is then dissolved rather than sold. [L1092, 2022]
- Sites in the same niche with overlapping suppliers are valued together, and a buyer will often purchase related sites as a bundle. [L1092, 2022]
