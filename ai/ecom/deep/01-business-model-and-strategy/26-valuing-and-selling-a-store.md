## Valuing and selling a store

### Deciding whether and when to sell

- Stores are usually sold for a few recurring reasons: the owner has better uses for the cash, a life event happens, the owner has lost interest, or the business has peaked and stopped growing. [L0858, 2018]
- Common concrete reasons include wanting cash for a second store or another business; life events such as marriage, divorce, bereavement or adoption costs; boredom with the niche; and a growth plateau that a buyer with a different skill set may still push higher. [L0553, 2018]
- Owners sell for concrete personal reasons such as paying off a mortgage, affording a baby, moving capital into rental property, or funding a larger business. Finding the real reason helps the seller negotiate the terms they actually need. [L1468, 2014]
- Deciding whether to sell is personal. One option is to sell only some of the stores to build a cash reserve and keep the rest. [L0468, 2020]
- Sell when the business has growing or steady sales and profits. Two years of historical data strengthens buyer confidence, and the market trend does not need to be perfect. [L0231, 2019]
- A flat business is also acceptable to sell. A declining business sells at the low end of the multiple range, and buyers are very nervous about it. [L0554, 2019]
- Sell while revenue and traffic are steady, slightly declining, or rising, since buyers prefer those profiles. Letting a site decline through neglect makes it much harder to sell and lowers its price. [L1468, 2014]
- Sell a store while its revenue and profit are rising every month, because buyers pay more for a store on an upward trend. If a store has a weak month, improve it before considering a sale. [L0826, 2016]
- Do not sell just because a store reached a revenue milestone. Keep feeding a store that is still growing, and consider selling once it flatlines, because spending another 18 months growing it can add a large amount to the eventual sale price. [L1277, 2020]
- If growth is still climbing steeply three months before listing, consider delaying the listing until it levels off. [L1468, 2014]
- Avoid listing at a peak you believe is final. Sellers often close on the expectation that a buyer will keep growing the business, and owners often do not know when they have reached a peak, so buyers may acquire at the top profit level to grow it further. [L0553, 2018]
- The two positions above differ in emphasis: the 2020 advice says to sell once growth flattens, while the peak warning says not to list when you believe the peak is final. In both, do not wait for the decline. [L1277, 2020; L0553, 2018; L1468, 2014]
- Do not sell right after a peak season. [L0553, 2018]
- A store is worth closing mainly when its customer service workload outweighs its sales, which is common with low-ticket products. A store with running ads but little traffic should be optimized first, because its conversion rate cannot be judged yet. (2025) [L0837, 2025]
- Do not turn off ads or stop running the store once it is listed for sale. Buyers want running and growing businesses, so the business must keep performing during the sale process. [L0554, 2019]
- Do not wait until you are disengaged from the business to sell. An actively managed, profitable operation commands a higher valuation than one run by a burned-out owner. [L0231, 2019]
- A business run at 20 or more hours per week sells for the lowest multiplier on net profit or annual sales. A business run at five hours per week or less gets a significantly higher multiplier and attracts a larger pool of buyers who want a portfolio asset. [L1122, 2016]
- A dropshipping site earning 1,000 USD per month that needs about 40 hours of personal work per month is hard to sell, because buyers see it as a job. Preparing a site for sale should start about six months before the sale. [L1359, 2014]

#### Holding period and minimum ownership

Sources give different holding periods. Each applies to a different situation.

| Holding period | When it applies | Source |
|---|---|---|
| At least 6 months before relisting a store bought from another owner | General guideline for resale of a bought store | [L0553, 2018] |
| 4 months | Done in special cases only; not a sound business model, because buyers ask why the store was resold so fast and the 12-month profit average would show only the owner's short period of added value | [L0553, 2018] |
| About 6 months | Store can be sold if revenue is stable or rising | [L0470, 2023] |
| 12 months | Store at least one year old sells for the highest valuation multiples; holding 12 months instead of six allows more history and growth, which can raise the multiple; a store sold after 12 months gets a higher multiple than the same store sold after 6 months, even with identical profit | [L0966, 2025; L0836, 2024; L0855, 2023; L1277, 2020] |
| 12 to 18 months | Proven track record, consistent results and growing sales; described as the way to maximum value | [L0852, 2020] |
| At least 2 years | Maximum valuation, because buyers trust longer profit history; one year can still be listed, and older businesses are worth more | [L0554, 2019] |

- Hold a store for about 12 to 18 months before selling to show a proven track record, consistent results and growing sales. [L0852, 2020]
- A store sold after 12 months gets a higher multiple on net profit than the same store sold after 6 months, even when profit is identical. (2023) [L0855, 2023]
- A store that is at least one year old sells for the highest valuation multiples. Younger stores can still be sold, but at lower multiples. (2025) [L0966, 2025]
- A store with a weak launch month followed by several strong months still has value. A longer record of stable or growing months lifts the multiple a buyer will pay. (2025) [L0966, 2025]

## Valuation

#### Formula and pricing basis

- Store valuation = average monthly net profit (revenue minus expenses) × multiple. Online dropshipping stores sold at 21x to 35x monthly net profit, which is roughly 2x to 3x annual profit. [L0554, 2019]
- Example: a dropshipping store with $5,000 monthly net profit values at $105,000 using a 21x multiple or $175,000 using a 35x multiple. [L0231, 2019]
- Use the 12-month average of net profit as the pricing basis, because buyers trust longer periods more. A 3- to 6-month average can fit a fast-growing business, but a steady $5,000 per month for 12 months is worth more than $5,000 in only the last month. [L0554, 2019]
- Buyers who pay high multiples value an online store on its trailing 12 months of revenue and net profit, with the averages of the most recent three months weighted most heavily. (2025) [L0966, 2025]
- Store value is not created by hours worked, money spent or future potential. Buyers pay only for the profit the store earns now, so owners should first increase profit and then sell. [L0858, 2018; L0553, 2018]
- Sellers should focus on maximizing a store's value before listing it, because buyers pay for proven monthly net profit rather than for hours worked, invested money, or future potential. [L0553, 2018]
- Buyers pay for a fast learning curve, for trading money for time, for established market demand visible in existing sales, and for applying their own unique advantage, such as paid social ad skills, to a store that lacks them. [L0553, 2018]

Sources differ. Use the figure that matches the source's date and the store's profile.

| Source and period | Multiple | Notes | Source |

| 2020 to 2022 | About 30x monthly net profit | Example: $5,000 per month = $150,000; also quoted for websites, networks and stores | [L0967, 2020; L1092, 2022; L1277, 2020; L0270, 2021] |
| 2021 | About 30x; lower if sales are trending down or the store has limited history | Example: $5,000 per month = $150,000 asking price | [L0270, 2021] |
| 2022 | 23x to 40x, per listings cited in the lesson | See listing table below | [L0458, 2021-22] |
| Current range | 21x to 35x monthly net profit (roughly 2x to 3x annual profit) | Low end for declining stores; top end for steady, diversified stores | [L0554, 2019] |

- The store valuation multiple of about 30x was the general figure for dropship stores in the older guidance, and it fell when sales were trending down or the store had limited history. [L0270, 2021]
- The 2019 and 2020 to 2022 guidance put the general multiple at about 30x, and the current range is 21x to 35x. [L1002, 2019; L0967, 2020; L0554, 2019]

#### Valuation of 2014 marketplace sales

- In September 2014, the average sale price on the Empire Flippers marketplace was about $258,000 per site, a figure from the broker's own reporting. Buyers outnumbered good sellers at that time, so finding profitable established sites was the main bottleneck. [L1468, 2014]
- A broker valued a website with $1,000 monthly net profit at about $20,000, roughly 20x monthly net profit. A strategic buyer could reasonably pay $25,000 to $30,000 for the same site. [L1468, 2014]

#### Brokers' sales data by year

|---|---|---|---|
| 2017 | 12 (one per month) | About $70,000 | About 2.5 months |
| 2018 | 15 | $138,000 (almost double 2017) | About 2 months |

- The broker rejected about two-thirds of submitted businesses and listed about one-third. Listed dropshipping businesses sold for multiples from 21x to 35x monthly profit over the two years before this talk. [L0554, 2019]
- In the last 12 months, 26 dropshipping stores sold for a total of $3.03 million, with prices from $30,000 to $533,000 and an average of $116,000. Smaller stores around $30,000 produced about $1,000 monthly profit, and the largest, around $533,000, produced about $20,000 monthly profit. [L0553, 2018]
- Average time on market in the last 12 months was 66 days, and about 60 percent of the 26 stores sold in under 60 days. Two to three months is a reasonable timeframe. [L0553, 2018]

#### Profit-based examples

- A store with an AliExpress/apparel model, $8,700 monthly profit, Facebook-only traffic, Chinese shipping and decreasing monthly earnings was listed at $183,000 (21x). It took 126 days to sell and sold for $120,000 (13x). The store was 12 months old, and buyers had the same negative view of AliExpress models. [L0553, 2018]
- A dropshipping lifestyle store with $5,000 monthly net profit, 18 months old, using Google Ads, organic traffic and Facebook retargeting, with steady earnings, was listed at $142,000 (28x). It sold for $133,000 (26x) in 10 days from listing. [L0553, 2018]
- Pets niche case, sold through a broker: see the case listings section. [L0554, 2019]

#### Revenue-based and brand valuation

- The valuation multiple depends on revenue, not profit, so an exit at a revenue multiple can be much higher than a profit multiple. [L1153, 2016]
- A brand with its own store, email list, Facebook group and subscription service is valued much higher than a pure Amazon seller. An Amazon-only business sold for about 30 to 35 times monthly profit, while a true brand with those assets can reach about 2.5 to 3 times annual revenue. [L1153, 2016]
- The first brand the course group sold achieved a 37 times multiple on monthly net profit. The sellers note that a revenue multiple of 2.5 times would have produced about double the sale price, which is why they built a brand. [L1153, 2016]
- A brand with consistent revenue, returning customers and several traffic channels can be sold for about 3x to 5x its profit, even when profit margin is only 10 to 15 percent. Building the brand is therefore an asset, not only a profit source. [L0871, 2021+]
- Sales from a store sold on a verified marketplace are typically valued at about 5 to 7 times annual profit. Empire Flippers was mentioned as a marketplace to check, and the Shopify Exchange marketplace had closed. [L0642, undated]
- The 5 to 7 times annual profit figure is higher than the current 2x to 3x annual profit range for dropshipping stores [L0554, 2019]. Apply it to sales on verified marketplaces only, as stated for that source. [L0642, undated; L0554, 2019]
- A profitable high-ticket dropshipping store may be sold as an asset for anywhere from tens of thousands to around $1 million, typically a couple of years after it is built. Treat these as anecdotal ranges, not a valuation standard. [L0912, 2021-22]
- A store or network is typically valued at about 30 times its monthly net profit. A network making 25,000 USD net profit per month would sell for roughly 750,000 USD. [L1277, 2020]
- A dropshipping store that sells about 100,000 USD per year is treated as a successful benchmark, and the first sale is an early milestone on the way to it. Stores at that level can be valued well above 100,000 USD when sold. [L0826, 2016]

## Factors that raise or lower the price

#### Factors that raise the price

- Net profit alone does not set the value of a store; quality matters. A store with diversified traffic, several products and steady profit sells for more than a store with higher profit that depends on one channel and shows declining trends. [L0553, 2018]
- Higher quality, meaning steady earnings, several traffic sources and a longer track record, produces a higher sale price and a faster sale. Quality matters more for the price than the owner's effort. [L0858, 2018]
- Buyers want traffic from several sources, because a single source such as Google Ads creates risk if it underperforms. Organic and social traffic alongside paid traffic makes buyers more comfortable. [L0554, 2019]
- Specialist stores are worth more than broad general stores. Specialist stores can build email lists, Facebook groups and push notification lists that buyers value. [L0554, 2019]
- A specific niche with a recognizable brand protects valuation, because it differentiates the store from large retailers and creates assets competitors cannot replicate overnight. [L0231, 2019]
- Store niches with broad consumer demand, such as dropshipping lifestyle niches, sell more quickly and at higher multiples than AliExpress-sourced stores that rely on Chinese shipping, poor product quality and Facebook-only traffic. [L0553, 2018]
- Dropshipping lifestyle stores sell quickly at high multiples; member stores typically sell quickly at high multiples. [L0270, 2021]
- A business with documented, transferable systems is worth substantially more than one that depends on the owner. The lesson claims an increase of several times and sometimes up to about 10x. Systems also make the business easier to hand over. [L1473, 2019]
- If 30% of your traffic comes from one forum account, buyers will expect that account to be included in the sale. Be ready to explain every major referral source to a buyer. [L1468, 2014]
- A store with a strong general trend and a long financial history is easier to value, and the seller can explain irregular months. [L0553, 2018; L0554, 2019] [L0554, 2019]
- Sites in the same niche with overlapping suppliers are valued together, and a buyer will often purchase related sites as a bundle. Keeping them under one LLC makes that bundled sale simpler. [L1092, 2022]
- Strategic buyers, usually competitors in the same niche, generally pay the highest price. They can cross-sell to your existing customers, so they value the customer base more than the current profit alone. [L1468, 2014]
- A brand built around proven sales can later be sold to another owner, so it is worth structuring the store and its records so the business can be transferred. This makes it easier to build several brands one after another. [L1439, 2024+]
- Build a brand that can later be sold: grow revenue with a brand, build infrastructure, connections and branding partnerships, and then sell the whole brand to a larger company. [L1212, undated]
- Stores with a full set of SOPs, training documents and documentation are easier to sell. A business with a virtual assistant running daily operations and documented SOPs is a clear example. [L0554, 2019]

#### Factors that lower the price

- Buyers reject AliExpress-based stores because they lack a defensible moat (easily copied), offer poor customer experience from China shipping, depend entirely on finding winning Facebook ads, and have no established brand. [L0231, 2019]
- The first question a broker asks when vetting a dropshipping store is whether it is a one source store or an AliExpress-style store. Brokers stopped listing AliExpress-style stores because they were hard to sell. [L0554, 2019]
- A store that depends on a single traffic source with falling earnings is hard to sell and sells at a much lower multiple. Diversifying traffic and keeping earnings steady protects the sale price. [L0858, 2018]
- Buyers pay a lower price for stores with one channel because a single source creates risk. Diversifying traffic and suppliers reduces perceived risk for buyers because the business does not depend on a single channel or vendor. [L0231, 2019; L0858, 2018]
- Avoid building stores around short-term fad niches. Buyers prefer sustainable markets with long-term product demand. [L0231, 2019]
- Monthly dips are normal and do not hurt the sale if the general trend is steady or positive. Each dip should have an explanation, such as a supplier problem, because unexplained, sporadic earnings worry buyers. [L0554, 2019]
- A heavily seasonal business such as Halloween supplies may receive a lower multiple when sold far outside its season, because buyers must estimate performance. Seasonal stores can still be sold. [L0553, 2018]
- A store with no profit, weak assets and no email list or social following is worth roughly 1,000 to 3,000 dollars. A store with built-up assets such as an email list and social pages may reach about 4,000 to 5,000 dollars. Profitable stores are valued as a multiple of monthly net profit, not on a design-only basis. [L0941, 2022]
- Non-English dropshipping or affiliate sites are avoided by buyers, unless the foreign-language portion is small. English-speaking countries pose no problem. [L0553, 2018]
- Local services alongside ecommerce are not included in the valuation. Establish the local service as a separate legal entity. [L0231, 2019]
- Only 100% online businesses can be sold through the marketplace. A local service run by the owner is usually not counted in earnings, although a local team that can be managed online may be considered. [L0554, 2019]
- Letting a site decline through neglect makes it much harder to sell and lowers its price. [L1468, 2014]
- A broker valued a site and a strategic buyer could pay more; the difference between a broker's valuation and a strategic price is a reason to approach competitors. [L1468, 2014]

#### Store examples that show the effect of neglect

- A seller had a Twitter-background website earning a couple of thousand dollars of profit per month. The owner stopped working on it, and over a few years profit fell to a couple of hundred dollars per month. At its peak the site could have sold for about $40,000 to $50,000, but it was given away for free instead. [L1468, 2014]
