## Product testing and winner discovery models

### Test-many-products approach

- Before building a branded store, test many markets, products and niches through dropshipping, because dropshipping carries no stock risk and shows whether demand exists before money goes into inventory. [L0492, 2023+]
- The aim of product testing is to find winning products fast and build them to a higher sales level by September. Testing many products quickly beats spending lots of effort on a few. [L1171, 2024+]
- There are two strategies: invest time in one product researched deeply to build a brand, or test many products quickly to find a winner and then build a brand around it. Both have trade-offs, and quick testing usually reveals winners sooner. [L1382, undated]
- Test-and-iterate works because a product that is already trending can carry an average marketer. Keep launching products and creatives until one catches a wave of demand, and use ad spy tools to find such trending products. [L1348, 2023]
- Store choice is not decisive. The product matters most, while domain name and store structure play a small role, so optimise the funnel and the speed of testing new products instead. [L1410, 2023]
- Do not rush a product to market and abandon it after weak early results. Test offers and ad angles over time and invest enough effort to learn whether the product can work. [L1469, 2023+]
- Finding the first winning product is the hardest part of e-commerce, and it usually takes until about 50,000 to 100,000 USD in revenue is made. After that point there is money to reinvest in learning and growth. [L0693, 2024]
- Product research is the most important skill, because a good product is easier to market and a weak product cannot be marketed successfully. Improving research quality makes every other step easier. [L0693, 2024]
- Products generating about 30,000 USD in revenue per month are common, while products at 1,000,000 USD per month are rare. Aim for products with steady demand rather than extreme volume. [L0693, 2024]
- Dropshipping success often takes longer than beginners expect. One profitable product after about six months, found after testing roughly four to five products, is presented as a reasonable pace. Plan for testing several products rather than expecting the first one to win, and aim for up to about 20 products in the worst case. [L0690, 2022+]
- Testing products before the first major winner took between 18 and 24 products in the cases described. One product that finally scaled was the 24th tested, after the mentoring phase. [L0819, 2020+]
- Product selection should be driven by quality, ethical sourcing and meeting all defined selection criteria. Personal preference should not override these requirements. [L0924, 2020-26]
- Choose low-risk products that are cheap to buy and need little storage, so a home-based seller can test without losing much money. [L1437, 2018]
- Before building a full ebook or other product, validate demand in the earliest stage with a simple landing page offering pre-orders or a waitlist, and test the concept on social channels before investing in inventory or custom production. [L1016, 2026]
- Keep experimental spending limited to one or two tests at a time, because most growth projects have no large recurring cost and money should not be spread across many new ideas. [L0970, 2023]

### Pump-and-dump short-term products

**Definition and purpose**

- A pump-and-dump product sells very well for a short window, usually about 2 to 4 months and sometimes 4 to 5 months. It cannot be relied on as steady year-round income, but it can produce useful cash flow. [L0347, 2026]
- A pump-and-dump product is a short-term testing vehicle, meant to generate cash flow and momentum that is then reinvested in a long-term brand. Its purpose is cash flow and learning, not longevity. [L0347, 2026]
- This model is a short-term, cash-focused strategy built to generate fast capital, not a lasting brand. The operator uses a product's temporary moment and does not try to build loyalty or a community around it. [L1211, 2026]
- The cash from pump-and-dump stores is meant to fund a real, long-term brand with actual equity later. The model is valuable mainly as a source of startup capital. [L1211, 2026]
- Because the operator has low emotional attachment to any single product, they can enter, scale and exit without the pressure of a legacy brand. [L1211, 2026]

**Procedure**

Procedure [L1211, 2026]:

1. Find a trending, attention-grabbing product, usually a gadget, gimmick or viral item.
2. Push aggressive ads into it and scale quickly.
3. Collect the cash it generates.
4. Move on to the next product once momentum fades. [L1211, 2026]

**Exit and lifespan**

- Exit a product when its momentum dies down, its margins shrink or market saturation sets in. Exit before the product's decline makes the ad spend unprofitable. [L1211, 2026]
- Pump-and-dump stores usually burn out within one to three months. Some decline even faster, so a store should not be planned around a long lifespan. [L1211, 2026]

**Who it suits and what it costs**

- This model suits beginners and anyone who wants to build capital quickly without the long wait of building a brand. It is not suited to someone who wants to build equity. [L1211, 2026]
- The model creates no brand equity, so nothing lasting is built and the store cannot really be sold as a brand. The money comes from selling the product and then leaving. [L1211, 2026]
- The model is one of the fastest ways to generate short-term income, especially in 2026, when attention spans are shorter and virality matters more. It fits operators whose goal is quick, scalable cash flow and who accept moving from product to product. [L1211, 2026]
- Stores running this model can generate thousands of dollars within days. In the first week, a store can sometimes reach about 10,000 USD per day. [L1211, 2026]

**Learning value and progression**

- The model works as a fast testing ground where an operator quickly learns marketing, product testing and paid ads. These are skills needed later when running a real brand. [L1211, 2026]
- Operators who want to build a lasting business will need to move beyond this model eventually. Knowing which stage of the journey you are in is what makes the model useful rather than harmful. [L1211, 2026]

Procedure [L1211, 2026]:

1. Generate fast cash with pump-and-dump products.
2. Reinvest that cash into building a real, sustainable brand.
3. Build the brand toward community-driven growth, loyal customers and repeat sales as the long-term goal. [L1211, 2026]

**Books as pump-and-dump products**

- Book products can be used as a short-term capital builder for a beginner, and a book trend can be a pump-and-dump rather than a long-term business. Treat each book as a test with its own lifecycle. [L1175, 2026]
- Some books are fast, short-lived products that cannot be turned into a lasting brand, because they fade quickly and the content cannot be owned. Such products are not a foundation for brand building. [L1175, 2026]

### Viral and TikTok-driven testing

**Checking viral products**

Procedure for checking a viral product's real sales [L0655, undated]:

1. Find the viral video and note its view count and the creator's follower count over time.
2. Review the creator's other content for context and timing (when the video was posted).
3. Place a real test purchase on the product page from a phone or desktop.
4. Wait for the order confirmation email and check the spam folder if it does not arrive.
5. Note the order number and confirmation number that the store issues.
6. Estimate the store's order count from the visible order-number sequence, the number of reviews and the time since launch.
7. Multiply estimated orders by the sale price to estimate revenue. [L0655, undated]

- Judge a viral product by sales signals rather than by view counts alone. A video with 700,000 views from a small follower account may still have converted well, and a seller who keeps uploading is likely still earning something. [L0656, undated]
- A low follower count can be a warning sign when a seller keeps an account running, because continued posting usually means the account is still generating sales. [L0656, undated]
- A profile image that does not show the product is a weak choice, because viewers may take the account for a bot and scroll past it. [L0656, undated]

**Case: blender from a TikTok account**

- A blender sold through a TikTok account that started on March 31 reportedly generated over 600,000 USD in sales from that one product by the time of the review. The hosts estimated about 20 USD profit per unit and roughly 50% margin, but they stated these were guesses. [L0657, undated]
- The account grew fast through a follow-for-giveaway hook, the product was fairly priced, and sales slowed in the last one to two months, after uploads had been consistent for months before. [L0657, undated]

**Amazon spillover**

- When a product goes viral on TikTok, it can spread to Amazon, where listings and reviews may appear. Amazon reviews from viewers who bought elsewhere can be negative, so product quality matters even on platforms outside your store. [L0657, undated]

**Store and organic sequence**

- Build the store before going viral with a new account. Viral success can come fast, and viewers who see no link cannot come back, causing lost sales and lost returning customers. Coins can send traffic to the store. [L0501, 2022]
- Once a TikTok account reaches around 10,000 followers, it is considered credible as a business. With over 50 to 100 videos in the library, older videos keep producing views for weeks and months, and some resurface after many months. [L0501, 2022]
- The same TikTok testing method works for personal brands or own products, not only dropshipping. Accounts with about 10,000 followers or more can be sold or turned into personal accounts if the product does not sell. [L0501, 2022]
- Start with organic content to gain experience before paying for Spark Ads or TikTok ads. Organic results show which products and angles work before you spend on ads. [L0706, 2020+]
- Organic TikTok selling keeps ad spend low in the early phase, so the cost of testing is time rather than money. Paid ads can burn budget quickly during product testing, and a budget of more than 5,000 USD was reported as spent on ads before the first major winner. [L0819, 2020+]

Procedure [L1380, 2022]:

1. Find a product (on TikTok, AliExpress or Facebook ads) and order a sample, starting with Amazon for speed.
2. Create and post content on the product about 3 times per day.
3. Study competitors and track what performs.
4. Build the Shopify store after content gets traction (a few thousand views), rather than before.
5. Add the website link to the bio once the account reaches 1,000 followers, and test the link. [L1380, 2022]

**Organic proof of concept**

- A TikTok organic operator built an account to about 20,000 followers, which reportedly generated around 200 USD in sales, mostly from paid ads rather than organic reach. Organic content acted as a proof of concept for products that then ran as paid campaigns. [L0819, 2020+]
- The same operator reported a first 230,000 USD revenue month in Q4 2020, with a best single day of about 15,000 USD, and later preferred lower revenue with higher profit. [L0819, 2020+]

**Competition and timing**

- Competition for organically proven products tends to rise toward Q4, as more sellers copy the same content. Products that show early traction are worth acting on before the crowd arrives. [L1380, 2022]
- Timing a launch with peak demand, such as the work-from-home surge during COVID, can make a product sell far more easily. Check trend tools for upcoming trends when choosing a problem or niche. [L0871, 2021+]

### Accounts as split tests

- A proven viral video can be reproduced on a new account with the same product, which can give a second viral run. Running two accounts that both link to one store also protects sales if one account or store is restricted. [L0352, 2022]
- Two accounts on one product are a split test and a safety net. Once you know which account performs better, you can merge both into one store. Consolidation is often the final step after the test. [L0352, 2022]
- Spark Ads and paid promotion of a viral organic video may end its organic reach. One practice was to keep separate accounts: one strictly organic, another used for paid ads, so paid activity does not damage the algorithmic value of the organic account. [L0819, 2020+]
- A new account often outperforms a long-established account with a large following. Follower count is weakly tied to reach on TikTok, so a product test can be moved to a new account when the existing one stops performing. Accounts with tens of thousands of followers were sometimes abandoned for this reason. [L0819, 2020+]
- Multiple stores or accounts for one product can be used to expand reach, to run ads through a secondary store or to escape a restricted account. The reasons behind them are not always clear. [L0656, undated]
- Reusing one set of clips across two accounts is a common mistake among sellers who open a second account, and it often explains why the second account grows slowly. [L0656, undated]

### Testing budgets and stop-loss

**Budget discipline**

- Start each new product with a testing budget of 10 to 30 USD per day, and increase the budget only while the 10x ROAS target is being met. [L1115, 2024]
- Ad budgets for Google and Meta should be spent on the store that already has product detail and authorised suppliers. Products with no evidence of sales should not take a large share of the budget. [L0360, 2024]
- Budget for product testing so that each product gets at least about 250 USD of spend before judging whether it works. A product is cut at roughly 300 USD of loss. Total pre-profit ad spend in this case was about 2,000 to 3,000 USD across four failed products and the fifth winner. [L0690, 2022+]
- Cut a product once its losses reach about 300 USD without profitability, and move on to the next product. Setting this stop-loss in advance keeps testing disciplined and limits the amount lost per test. [L0690, 2022+]
- Test a product fast, kill losers early and move to the next product when the first one does not show signs. Ad spend on an unproven product can be stopped around 50 to 100 USD, with 200 to 250 USD as the upper limit for a product without signs. [L1382, undated]
- Testing a product with strong comment demand but high pricing: test it with paid ads at small budgets to see if it actually sells before scaling. [L0530, 2022]
- A product test that ran about four months with dozens of products and no winner, with old ad accounts showing cost per click of 5 to 8 USD and CPM of 20 to 50 USD: a new ad account with a new pixel and 10 new ads was launched. After 6.60 USD in spend, CPM and CPC were already clearly better, though CTR was still below the 1% target. [L1194, undated]
- In the same case, the ad setup was changed to 10 ad sets, each with a unique video. The product name was changed to a single sleep benefit. A launch price of 49 to 59 USD was considered to gather pixel data, against cost of goods of about 20 USD and break-even of about 52 USD. [L1194, undated]
- In the same case, the countdown and the GIF were to be reviewed, and the product page was to be rebuilt with benefits, how it works, FAQ, testimonials and guarantee. The agreed plan was to perfect one product page, check its add-to-cart ratio, and then duplicate it for other products. The lesson did not report a final result. [L1194, undated]
- Two founders, self-funded, first tried Facebook ads, Google Ads and influencer marketing in August 2020 after finding dropshipping on YouTube. Four Facebook ad accounts were banned almost at once, and about 7,000 to 8,000 euros were lost across ads, influencers and tools with no return. They then invested in a mentorship and spent about 2,500 to 3,000 euros in a second test phase until the next winner. [L1440, 2021+]

**Tools**

- Do not pay for spy tools such as AdSpy before you have a first winning product and some sales. Reinvest early profit into such tools once you want to find trends for your next store. [L0388, 2024+]
- Tools cited as worth comparing for product research: Peakster for ad spying and store tracking, and Shop Hunter for product research. Compare each one by the specific job you need. [L0561, 2023+]

### Product stage and fads

**Kill rules and product-market fit**

- If the product itself has poor quality or weak market fit, no improvement to ad creative will fix the fundamental problem. Remove the product from testing and focus resources on better-performing products instead. [L0008, 2023]
- A Facebook Ads testing campaign exists to answer one question: is there product-market fit between your product and the Facebook audience you are targeting? Success means the product can be sold profitably to that audience, not merely that it generates clicks or website traffic. [L0229, undated]

**Moving from test to brand**

- Launch a new product at a lower price to get early traction and learn whether the market wants it. Only after that validation should the product be developed into a brand. [L1194, undated]
- At the testing stage the goal is not to build a brand but to find out quickly whether a product sells profitably. Brand work comes only after that is confirmed. [L1252, 2026]
- When a niche has many winning products, there is no need to research every product in it. Once one product passes all tests, a store can be built on it and the remaining winners kept for later stores. [L1114, 2023]
- Many sellers run several near-identical simple stores and Facebook pages, one per product, to test quickly. When one of them performs, they build a brand around it. Seeing many similar stores is a sign of this testing method, not proof that a product is dead. [L1176, 2023]

**Fads and short windows**

- The model is most useful when the operator knows the product's stage. A pump-and-dump product is a fad in the short window and must be exited before its decline. [L1211, 2026] [L1211, 2026]

### General-store testing

- When starting out, run a general store with a generic name and several products from different niches, rather than buying a domain and branding for a single product. This keeps the budget lean, because you only invest in full branding after a product proves it sells. [L0562, undated]
- A general store with a generic name is a valid testing ground for product proofs of concept, because it lets you test many products quickly. Later, a store with a generic name can be turned into a one-product store if one product proves itself. [L0950, 2023]
- Some sellers make money on test stores but struggle when moving to a one-product store, so do not assume a switch to a single-product store will automatically keep results. [L0950, 2023]
- Niche-only stores can lock you out of other products worth testing. In the testing phase, a general store lets you test more products faster. [L0950, 2023]
- Test new products on a general store first. Once a winning product is found, a store can move from general to single-product, and a sharper brand build can come later. [L1212, undated]

Procedure for general-store testing [L0637, 2023+]:

1. Launch a general store under a brandable, random domain name and test products one by one.
2. Focus effort on strong landing pages with a clear offer and trust elements.
3. When one product clearly performs, move it to a single-product store after about one week.
4. Add upsell and closely related products to build a niche around the winner.
5. Sell related products through email marketing or launch a second hero product for ads. [L0637, 2023+]

- When one product wins in a general store, keep related products in the same store, place the winning product at the top of the collection and use its image in the creatives. Create a separate one-product store (new domain, new logo, new homepage) only when the products are unrelated. [L0642, undated]

### EU and US testing

**EU general-store model**

- A general-store model in EU markets runs one native-language store per market (for example Dutch, French and German), tests many products across them, and scales only the products that sell. Store-level scaling happens after a product proves itself, not before. [L0660, 2019+]
- In EU general-store testing, the target is 5 to 10 new product tests per day across all stores. [L0660, 2019+]

Procedure for the EU product test cycle [L0660, 2019+]:

1. Launch the new product in the relevant native-language store with a Facebook campaign.
2. Spend about EUR 50 on the test.
3. If the product has no sale once EUR 50 is spent (the speaker equated this to about USD 25), kill the product immediately.
4. Keep products that sell and scale them across the stores.
5. Move on to the next product test. [L0660, 2019+]

**US testing**

- US product testing requires about 400 to 500 USD in ad spend per product. During that test, run A/B tests of headlines on the sales page and new creatives. [L0660, 2019+]
