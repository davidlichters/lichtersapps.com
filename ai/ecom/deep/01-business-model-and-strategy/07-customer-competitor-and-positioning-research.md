## Customer, competitor and positioning research

### Customer avatars and target audience

- The target audience definition has two goals. First, understand not only what people do but why they do it and what they need. Second, communicate in a way they understand and connect with, so they take the action you want. A precisely defined audience makes the message more relevant, selects better products, and improves user experience, conversion rates and retention. [L0497, undated]
- Trying to serve everybody dilutes the message. A broad audience makes marketing fail, so narrow the audience to a specific segment before writing any messaging. [L0497, undated]
- Customer avatar work is a foundational marketing task. If you already have current profiles, review them only for accuracy. If you have never done it, complete the full sheet. [L0907, 2021-22]
- There is no fixed number of customer avatars that applies to every business. The right count depends on the market, the niche and the product category, so decide it separately for each one. [L0907, 2021-22]
- Answer these four questions in order before building any funnel: [L0523, 2015+]
  1. Who is my dream client: the specific person I most want to work with, including their traits, goals, passions and income level?
  2. Where do they spend time online: which sites, groups, newsletters, blogs and platforms do they use?
  3. What bait will attract them and repel everyone else?
  4. What result do I want to deliver to them? A business sells results, not products.
- Market and design a product where the customer journey is understood. Sell a product for the real reason people buy it, such as the feeling of control or the desire for a hobby. [L1426, undated]

### Customer awareness pyramid

**Structure and stages**

- The customer awareness pyramid shows the five stages a buyer passes through before purchasing: completely unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, and ninja aware. It represents the whole market for a product. Each higher stage contains fewer people, so the bottom stage is the largest and the top stage is the smallest. [L0481, 2021-22]
- Customer awareness follows a five-stage pyramid: completely unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware and ninja aware. Each buyer passes through these stages before purchasing, usually without conscious awareness of the steps. [L1117, 2021-22]
- The five stages in practice: [L1117, 2021-22]
  - **Completely unaware:** the person does not know they have a problem. They experience the problem a product solves without knowing it, and they form the largest part of the potential market. [L0481, 2021-22; L1117, 2021-22]
  - **Problem aware:** the person knows the problem but not the solution. They begin searching for a fix. The segment is still large but smaller than the completely unaware stage. [L0481, 2021-22; L1117, 2021-22]
  - **Solution aware:** the person knows which type of solution fits but has not chosen a product type. [L0481, 2021-22; L1117, 2021-22]
  - **Product aware:** the person has narrowed down to a product type and compares variants. [L0481, 2021-22; L1117, 2021-22]
  - **Ninja aware:** the person knows the exact product they want and only decides where to buy it. [L0481, 2021-22; L1117, 2021-22]
- Ninja aware buyers make up roughly 10% or less of the total market at any given time. Each stage is a smaller slice of the pyramid than the one below it. [L0481, 2021-22]
- If the best product is sold by only one seller, the ninja aware buyer has no choice of retailer. If several sellers offer it, as with reselling, the buyer still chooses a retailer at the final stage. [L0481, 2021-22]

**Problem first**

- E-commerce sells solutions to problems or desires, not products alone. The buyer first has a problem, then turns it into a want or need, and only then looks for the product. Marketing must therefore start from the problem the product solves. [L0481, 2021-22]
- Example of a beginner surfer: first the person wants to surf, then realises a surfboard is required (problem aware). Next they weigh buying against renting and decide to buy because they plan to surf for years (solution aware). They then compare board types for beginners, choose one based on budget and reviews (product aware), and finally pick a seller and pay (ninja aware). [L1117, 2021-22]

**Passing through the stages**

- Every buyer passes through all awareness stages, mostly subconsciously, as a natural process of education before buying. Ignoring this process lowers the conversion rate. [L0481, 2021-22]
- The time a customer takes from first noticing a need to buying varies widely, from a few days to several months. Low-cost, simple products usually have short journeys. Expensive or complex products, such as a $3,000 kitchen appliance, usually need longer consideration. [L1117, 2021-22]

**Mapping the pyramid to the funnel**

- The traditional sales funnel corresponds to the awareness pyramid turned upside down. Top of funnel means completely unaware or problem aware. Middle of funnel means solution aware or product aware. Bottom of funnel means ninja aware, ready to buy. [L0481, 2021-22]
- The awareness pyramid is an inverted sales funnel. Completely unaware is the top of the funnel, solution aware sits mostly in the middle, and product aware and ninja aware form the bottom, where buyers are closest to purchasing. [L1117, 2021-22]
- The two sources above place product aware buyers differently. [L0481, 2021-22] puts them in the middle of the funnel, and [L1117, 2021-22] puts them at the bottom with ninja aware. Use the middle placement for a three-part top, middle and bottom view. Use the bottom placement when the question is which buyers are closest to purchasing. [L0481, 2021-22; L1117, 2021-22]
- The awareness pyramid is a framework for the customer journey that also applies beyond copywriting. It can guide email marketing, retargeting and the type of content published for the business. [L0904, 2021-22]

**Where growth comes from**

- Many e-commerce businesses focus only on the bottom of the funnel (product aware and ninja aware). That segment is small at any time and cannot grow. The business stops scaling once it has saturated the available traffic there. [L1117, 2021-22]
- The market is far larger at the completely unaware, problem aware and solution aware stages than at the product aware and ninja aware stages. Scaling therefore requires reaching people earlier in the journey. [L1117, 2021-22]
- Be present at every stage of the pyramid, not only at the bottom of the funnel, where competitors already hold the buyer's attention. [L0414, 2021-22]

**Order of work**

- Before building traffic, work through the Know component first. Understand the ideal customer, the competitors, the financial numbers and the business goals. Then move on to Convert, Educate, Promote, Develop and Consolidate, in that order. [L0414, 2021-22]

### Market segmentation and niches

- Market segmentation groups an audience into segments that share common needs and characteristics and react similarly to a given marketing message, so each segment can receive a tailored message. [L0497, undated]
- Market segmentation means grouping people who share characteristics so you can serve them with a message fitted to them. In a crowded market, narrowing the audience can shrink competition, so a product already on the market can still win by targeting a new segment. [L0999, 2023]
- Segmenting the audience is a way to find a less competitive position, even for a product that already exists elsewhere. Testing one specific audience can be worth doing before a broad approach when platforms push toward broad targeting. [L0999, 2023]
- Any product can sell when the audience is narrowly defined and the ad copy, creative, landing page and emails all address the same pain point. Choosing a specific audience and angle matters more than the product itself. [L1194, undated]
- About 10% of a broad audience may recognise dropshipping. A large audience of 100 million therefore still leaves about 90 million potential buyers. Focus on service quality and product quality to win these buyers. [L1213, 2023]
- A market niche is a group of prospects with well-defined, shared requirements, such as delivery to a job site, measured quantities or delivery within hours. Find niches by listing the specific needs of a group and checking whether your strengths meet them. Use your previous industry experience to spot niches others have missed. [L0770, undated]

### Identifying competitors

- If you resell other brands, your closest competitors sell the exact same brands and products. Second-level competitors sell the same type of product, even under other brands, and should still be listed. [L0893, 2021-22]
- If you sell your own product, competitors are those selling a similar competing product, including popular look-alike products sold only on Amazon. Resellers should leave out Amazon, eBay and similar marketplaces and focus on standalone websites that appear in the same search results as you. [L0893, 2021-22]
- A competitor is a store that sells for several brands without its own manufacturing and without a physical location (no warehouse, retail store or showroom). Two to three such online-only competitors with over 20 brands each is enough to call a niche drop-ship friendly. They do not all have to be in the exact niche. [L0834, 2021]
- Competitor sites that describe their products as factory direct are most likely drop-shipping from warehouses. Treat that wording as a strong sign of drop-shipping. [L0469, 2021]
- Large retailers such as Wayfair mostly drop ship the products they sell and also private label many of them, while holding little stock themselves. Treat such a retailer as a future competitor, not as a company to sell to or a model to copy directly. [L1224, 2025+]

### Competitor analysis sheet

- A competitor analysis sheet uses one row per competitor, with these columns in this order. [L0893, 2021-22]

| Competitor Name | Website URL(s) | Same or Similar Products? | Traffic Sources | Email Marketing? | USP | Proof Points | Bonuses/Premiums | Risk Reversal | Price & Terms | Hook | Primary Marketing Promise | Features/Deliverables | Do they have content? | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (one row per competitor) | | | | | | | | | | | | | | |

- The competitor analysis sheet is a living document. Review it at least once a year, ideally every six months, and whenever a new competitor appears or something new is learned. [L0893, 2021-22]
- The competitor sheet can be shared with a marketing team or employees so they can quickly see what competitors are doing. Keeping the sheet current is cheap and useful for the whole team. [L0893, 2021-22]
- Competitor analysis is an ongoing task, not a one-time check. Review the top three competitors regularly to see what they add, change or drop. [L0894, 2020-26]
- Store-level competitor analysis: compare the store's product page with the top two to three competitor stores found by searching the product in Google. Look at what the customer would see as a reason to buy elsewhere. [L0856, 2024]
- Shoppers often compare your store with other sellers of the same products. The purpose of a competitor review is to find what the top competitors skip or do poorly. Make those gaps the focus of your own store so buyers have a clear reason to pick you. [L0894, 2020-26]
- Once traffic sources are mapped across all competitors, channels that no competitor uses can reveal an open opportunity. Record gaps in the market as carefully as the channels competitors already use. [L0893, 2021-22]
- If a competitor's unique selling proposition or email marketing cannot be found, customers probably cannot find it either. Treat it as effectively absent from the market. Use this to judge which gaps in competitor offerings could be an opportunity. [L0441, 2021-22]

### Competing against large players

- Entering a market with a big competitor selling the same product carries risk, because the competitor already has trust, resources and scale. Look for differences in the country, the marketing angle or the platform that the competitor does not use. For example, check traffic in a specific country with SimilarWeb. [L1382, undated]
- When a big brand already sells the same product, do not compete head-on with the same market and angle. Look for an unused country (check traffic via Similarweb), a different marketing angle, or a platform the brand does not use, such as Pinterest. [L1436, undated]
- A niche-specialist store can compete with large generalists such as Wayfair, which sells over a million products. Build the store for one specific buyer type, study competitor weaknesses, and position yourself as the authority in that niche. [L0469, 2021]
- Choose a product category where the market has known problems, such as trust or quality issues, and solve them publicly. Being open about formulas, sourcing and testing is a differentiator in such categories, because most competitors avoid these topics. [L0334, 2017]
- Competing with Amazon works through specialist expertise, niche focus, buying guides, product bundles, free bonus gifts and direct relationships with brands. Amazon sells everything but offers less specific advice, fewer special bundles and less industry-specific content. [L0358, 2020]
- Do not try to beat Amazon on price. Its algorithms track the lowest price and drop their own price further. Once the price falls, Amazon rarely raises it again. [L1293, 2014]
- Before entering a niche, check whether the top competitors run strong sites with guides, videos and reviews. If they do, plan content of the same quality or better from day one instead of launching a basic store. [L1274, 2018]
- A store that copies the default supplier descriptions and a few basic images will rarely beat a competitor with richer content. Google will not rank the bare minimum above established high-quality sites. [L1274, 2018]

### Value, USP and differentiation

- Many conversion problems come from a weak understanding of the market rather than from the store's design. A business must know where it fits in its market and why customers should pick it over rivals before most marketing decisions get easier. [L0893, 2021-22]
- A business is fundamentally about solving real problems for other people, and products are the means of doing that. Growing a large e-commerce business requires communicating clearly how the business and its products solve problems that real people actually experience. [L0872, 2021-22]
- Answers about the market and the competition feed copywriting, on-site conversion work, ad copy, email content and retargeting ads. A clear picture of competitors makes all of these tasks easier. [L0893, 2021-22]
- Before choosing marketing actions, a store owner should be able to explain in a few lines why customers would buy from them rather than from competitors and what makes the business distinct. Understand the ideal customer first, then the competitors, and only then the point of difference. [L0893, 2021-22]
- The competitor analysis is a starting point for every later decision. Turn its findings into action items such as a new USP, a new email sequence or a new content plan. The purpose is to find where your business can differ from the others. [L0893, 2021-22]
- Define your business differentiator before scaling: what sets you apart from competitors, including your products and the business mission. This determines where you fit in the market, so it is the most important input for later planning. [L1268, 2026]
- Frame the business around the result you give customers rather than the product you sell. When the result is clear and valuable enough, price stops being the main barrier to buying. Identify the highest-level result you could deliver, even if most customers first buy smaller pieces. [L0523, 2015+]
- Alex Homosi's value equation:

  ```
  value = (increase perceived likelihood of achieving the dream outcome, or getting away from pain) combined with (reduce time, effort and sacrifice)
  ```

  Dream outcome and perceived success probability go up. Time, effort and sacrifice go down, because those are scarce resources people try to conserve. [L0497, undated]

### Price, quality and service strengths

- Every business has three areas of market strength: price, quality and service. In a competitive market a company generally cannot be the best in all three at once, so decide which combination you truly offer before choosing prospects. [L0770, undated]
- Match your strengths to the segments that value those strengths most, and avoid segments whose priorities you cannot meet. A good offer aimed at buyers who rank a different factor first still wastes effort. [L0770, undated]
- Classify a feature such as on-time delivery by what the business sells. For a packaging supplier it is part of service. For an overnight carrier, on-time delivery is the product's quality, and service covers tracking, billing and courtesy. The category decides which buyers will value it. [L0770, undated]

### Qualifying prospects

- Sort every prospect into one of four groups. Spend most of your time only on the first group. [L0770, undated]
  1. Needs, wants and can afford the product: ready to buy.
  2. Needs and can afford it but does not want it.
  3. Needs and wants it but cannot afford it.
  4. Needs, wants and can afford it but prefers another supplier.
- Because time and resources are limited, every call to a low-probability prospect costs the chance to reach a likely buyer. Qualifying out most of the market early is the normal way to work, not a missed sale. [L0770, undated]
- Select customers who purchase online independently, accept standard delivery timelines without requesting constant updates, and are satisfied with the completed transaction. Avoid haggling customers, chronic complainers and those seeking continuous contact, as they demand excessive personal attention that conflicts with a self-managing business operation. [L0019, undated]

### From segment to message

- Before touching any ad platform, understand your market, your audience and your customers. Ads run without that groundwork waste money and time on any platform, including Facebook, Google, YouTube, Amazon and Pinterest. [L0643, 2015]
- Paid ads should not show a buy-now offer to everyone. Most people in a market are not ready to purchase today. The rough benchmark is that under 10% of searchers are ready to buy now. Segment the audience by readiness and treat each segment with a different message. [L1386, 2020-26]
- The sales funnel runs Awareness, Interest, Learn, Shop, Buy, and each stage needs its own kind of message. First make people aware of the solution, then give them interest, then let them learn and compare, then let them shop and finally buy. Digital marketing and sales funnel models vary, but this five-stage sequence is the common baseline. [L1386, 2020-26]
- Process for each client, packaged as a repeatable product so it can be rolled out for every client and scaled: [L0643, 2015]
  1. Know the market.
  2. Choose a segment.
  3. Craft the message.
  4. Prepare the creative.
  5. Prepare the structure, which means the store or the product-specific landing page.
