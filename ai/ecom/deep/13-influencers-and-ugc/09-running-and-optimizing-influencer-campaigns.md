## Running and Optimizing Influencer Campaigns

### Campaign Setup and Objectives

**Campaign procedure.** The two one source versions of this procedure are merged here. Each step lists the ids it draws on.

1. Define one specific objective for the campaign: a new product launch, an upcoming sale, brand awareness, or a new product promotion. [L0787, undated; L0868, undated]
2. Tell the influencer the objective so both sides understand the goal. [L0787, undated; L0868, undated]
3. Request a draft of the influencer's content before anything is published. [L0787, undated; L0868, undated]
4. Review the draft and confirm it matches the campaign objective before publication. [L0787, undated; L0868, undated]
5. Create the content together with the influencer, as a joint post published alongside their own posts. Agree in advance on what will be posted and which results it should generate, and confirm both sides are aligned on the message and intended result before the campaign goes live. [L0787, undated; L0868, undated]
6. Publish across your own social media and the influencer's social media at the same time. [L0787, undated; L0868, undated]
7. Analyze the results, document what worked, and use the insights to choose future influencer collaborations that resonate with your audience. [L0787, undated; L0868, undated]

**One clear objective per campaign.** Every influencer campaign needs one clear, specific objective before it starts. Typical objectives are a new product launch, an upcoming sale, or promoting new products. Without a defined objective, the influencer's content has no direction and the campaign cannot be measured properly. [L0868, undated]

**State the objective explicitly.** The objective must be communicated to the influencer so they understand what the brand is trying to achieve and which product or sale is involved. The influencer should know the specific objective before content is created. [L0868, undated]

**Review every draft against the objective.** Never let influencer content go live without first reviewing a draft. Check the draft against the campaign objective, not only for general quality, and confirm its message serves the chosen goal (brand awareness, sales, or a specific product) before it goes live. [L0787, undated; L0868, undated]

**Co-create the post.** Creating the post together with the influencer, and agreeing on the message and intended result before launch, keeps the content aligned with the campaign goal. This shared understanding reduces the risk of off-message posts. A joint post also gives the brand control over the campaign message. [L0787, undated; L0868, undated]

**Respond quickly during the campaign.** Answer influencers quickly and handle their questions and scheduling issues in time. Slow or unavailable brand contacts make the campaign feel badly run to the creator, which can hurt both the result and the working relationship. [L0782, undated]

### Coordinated Posting and Timing

**Post on both channels at once.** Publishing the same campaign simultaneously on your own channels and the influencer's channels maximizes reach and impact. Coordinated posting across both audiences works better than posting on only one side, and running both channels together is more successful than either alone. [L0787, undated; L0868, undated]

**Protect the measurement day of a major post.** For a major influencer post:
- Do not run other promotions on the same day.
- Do not let other influencers post on that date.
- Ask the influencer to post within their own two-hour window so traffic can be measured clearly. [L0790, 2017]

**Approve the first post with a new influencer.**
- Approve the photo and caption before publication.
- Require your company name in the first eight words of the caption.
- Approve all photos and captions for high-priced products. [L0790, 2017]

**Seasonal window.** The period between Black Friday and Valentine's Day is a strong season for influencer marketing. The influencer receives a product, a script and a coupon code to drive sales. [L1194, undated]

**Scale with micro-influencers in bulk.** The goal was about 1,000 micro-influencers available to post at any time. Micro-influencers work best in bulk because each one reaches a small, targeted audience. [L0790, 2017]

### Promo Scheduling and Story Frameworks

**Five-story Instagram promotion framework.** Each campaign runs over five stories, in this order:
1. Introduce the product, its benefits and the problem it solves, framed as a personal story, such as answering questions about where the product came from. [L0789, 2022]
2. Demonstrate the product in use, such as showing how to apply a cream or use a hair tool. [L0789, 2022]
3. Share a coupon code for a limited number of buyers, such as 50 discount codes, plus any free-shipping offer, and mention scarcity. [L0789, 2022]
4. Ask the influencer to show how to apply the coupon code on the website, by screen recording or on a smartphone. [L0789, 2022]
5. Repeat the coupon code and remind followers of the deadline. [L0789, 2022]

Timing of the follow-ups:
- 24 hours later: post a story saying the coupon has sold out and promise another batch later. [L0789, 2022]
- 24 hours after that: post a story announcing an extra batch of 30 codes, a last-chance message, and extra value such as a bonus. [L0789, 2022]

**Story script template.** Adapt the wording to the product.

```
Story 1: 'Hey guys, I got a lot of questions about this. People kept asking where I got this. I got it from this brand. It does [benefit] and it helped me with [problem]. This is the product, here is what it does and which issues it solves.'
Story 2: The influencer demonstrates the product in use, for example applying a cream or using the hair tool, on camera.
Story 3: 'Hey, a lot of you have been asking, and I have good news. I reached out to the brand and got a discount code for the next 50 buyers. You get 50 percent off with my code, plus free shipping on orders above [amount]. Enjoy.'
Story 4: Screen recording or smartphone video showing how to go to the website and enter the coupon code.
Story 5: 'Just a reminder, here is the coupon code again.'
Follow-up after 24 hours: 'A lot of you have asked for this coupon. Sorry, it has expired. We will have to wait for the next batch, but I will keep you posted if I can get another one.'
Another follow-up after 24 hours: 'Good news, I got 30 extra codes and I cannot get any more. If you missed it last time, this is your last chance, plus extra value from the brand.'
```
[L0789, 2022]

**Serial progress videos.** A creator posting a daily check-in on a product's results brings viewers back to follow the next update. This format works best for products where results can be shown over several days. [L0784, 2025-26]

### Discount Codes and Exclusive Offers

**Follower-only codes.** Exclusive coupon codes or special offers available only to an influencer's followers create a sense of privilege and special access. This increases redemption rates and strengthens follower loyalty. [L0226, 2017]

**Limited batches.** The five-story framework uses small, capped batches (50 codes, then 30 extra) and announces sellouts and deadlines to create scarcity. [L0789, 2022]

### Organic-to-Paid Engagement Posts

**Growing a Facebook or Instagram page with a promoted post.** This is the one source 2024 approach.

1. Find trending memes or content seen on Facebook, Twitter or Instagram and adapt it to the target customer's audience. [L0666, 2024]
2. Publish it as a post on the Facebook business page. [L0666, 2024]
3. In Meta Ads Manager, create a new campaign with the goal set to Engagement. [L0666, 2024]
4. Choose "use existing post" in the ad builder and select the published post. [L0666, 2024]
5. Set a daily budget of about 3 to 5 dollars and narrow targeting to the ideal customer (age range, gender, location, interests). [L0666, 2024]
6. Let it run for a few days, then set the budget to zero and turn the campaign off, since engagement can continue for months. [L0666, 2024]

### Partnership Ads, Spark Ads and Whitelisting

**Spark Ads boost content designed to convert.** A Spark Ad can give an early boost to a video designed to convert, and organic reach may then take over. Videos can be posted organically first and promoted later, or created from the start for promotion. [L0352, 2022]

**Spark Ads procedure (TikTok).**
1. Have an influencer create and post content featuring your product on their own TikTok page. [L1374, undated]
2. Ask the influencer to whitelist that specific post. This requires only the authorization code for that one post, not their account login. [L1374, undated]
3. Run the influencer's post as a Spark Ad from your ad account. [L1374, undated]
4. Keep your own organic posts separate from the Spark Ad to avoid damaging their organic reach. [L1374, undated]

**Do not Spark-boost your own winning organic posts.** Spark Ads run on the same brand page can kill organic reach. Run Spark Ads from an influencer's post or from a separate page, not from your own successful organic posts. [L1374, undated]

**Spark Ads are TikTok-only.** Spark Ads can only run on TikTok placements. If a campaign also uses placements such as Pangle or Audience Network, Spark Ads require a separate campaign limited to TikTok. [L1374, undated]

**Partnership ads.** Partnership ads run a collaborator's post as a paid ad. They need the other account's permission before they are set up, and the ad runs through the partner's post under that account's identity. [L0865, 2025-26]

**Your own character page versus creator whitelisting.** Running ads from your own character page avoids paying a creator revenue share and removes dependence on a page owner who can withdraw or renegotiate. Creator whitelisting runs ads from a real creator's account, where you have no control over that account. The trade-off of a character page is that you carry all page and account risk yourself. [L1475, 2026]

**Whitelist pages for video ads.** Whitelist pages also work for video ads, especially UGC. A video that looks like a real person testing the product feels native and gets watched rather than scrolled past. [L1476, 2026]

**Matching faces.**
- When a UGC video shows a face, pair it with a whitelist character of similar look, such as the same gender and similar hair.
- With several UGC videos featuring different people, use different whitelist pages and match each page to its UGC face.
- A similar look is acceptable. A copy of a real creator's face is not. [L1476, 2026]

### Handling Audience Saturation

**Move to paid and UGC when an audience is saturated.** Influencer partnerships depend on the audience. When one influencer's audience is saturated, shift to paid ads and UGC to reach new people. [L0789, 2022]

### Repurposing Creator Content

**Why repurpose.** Reusing creator content reduces production cost, since the video already exists. It also increases reach and authenticity, because the audience sees different people with different experiences instead of the same brand voice every time. [L0784, 2025-26]

**Organic videos from unpaid creators.** When a product becomes a trend, creators often post organic videos about it without compensation. If people spontaneously talk about the product, the brand can ask to reuse those videos, as long as the rights are agreed in writing first. [L0784, 2025-26]

**Case: a viral candida cleanse product on TikTok.** The brand that drove the movement worked with several creators, each taking a different angle. The lesson does not give the brand's figures or say which creators were paid, but many creators posted without compensation. Five example videos:
1. A creator tried the viral cleanse and posted a daily check-in, with day 4 as one update. [L0784, 2025-26]
2. A creator made a comedic video with a funny trending audio about sugar cravings going away. [L0784, 2025-26]
2b. The second creator's video was trend-based with no voiceover, using a trending audio. [L0784, 2025-26]
3. A creator unboxed the product and walked through her expectations. [L0784, 2025-26]
4. Another daily check-in on day 4, which viewers returned to follow. [L0784, 2025-26]
5. An instructional video about what to eat and which practices to follow. [L0784, 2025-26]

Lesson: when customers organically join a conversation, the brand can repurpose those videos on its own social channels after getting rights. [L0784, 2025-26]

**Choose candidates by engagement.** Check engagement metrics, both among followers and among non-followers, to see which content performs best with the broader audience. [L0784, 2025-26]

**Prefer evergreen content.** Choose video that still makes sense when shown years later. Avoid renewing rights to trend-based videos, because they lose relevance once the trend ends. [L0784, 2025-26]

**Check brand fit before reuse.** Confirm each creator video matches the brand's values and aesthetic before reusing it. The question is whether the video represents how the brand wants to be seen. [L0784, 2025-26]

**Re-publish on other platforms.** A UGC video can be reused on another platform by changing the title, description or thumbnail, since these affect click-through rate. The video itself can stay the same. [L1214, 2023+]

### Long-Term Partnerships and Scaling

**One creator over many one-offs.** A long-term partnership with one creator usually delivers more value than many short one-off collaborations with different creators. The relationship produces more connected and authentic content. [L0783, 2025-26]

**Ambassador deals and the fade-out.** Long-term ambassador deals can keep producing sales while a creator stays popular, because their audience keeps trying to copy them. Expect the partnership to lose value when the creator's personal brand fades, and plan the budget for that window. [L1214, 2023+]
