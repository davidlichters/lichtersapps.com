## Producing Your Own UGC and AI Content

### Planning and Principles

- Own UGC is the starting point when no budget exists. Film it yourself first, then move to paid creators once the product shows consistent profit. [L0308, 2023; L1219, undated; L1030, 2026]
- Phone footage is usually good enough when testing a product early. Shoot the product from several angles: held up, a slow slide across a surface, and slow zooms, so the editor has enough material for the narrative. [L0344, 2023]
- Customisation costs time and money because each custom UGC video needs creator credits or production. Spend on it only after the original product shows consistent profit. [L1030, 2026]
- Custom content performs better than existing content on TikTok, so prioritise your own videos over reused viral clips. [L1377, 2022+]
- Custom content recorded by the owner on a phone, with simple scripts and no studio, performs better than stock drop-shipping footage, because viewers relate to a normal person explaining the benefit. [L0871, 2021+]
- Authentic creator footage filmed on a phone outperforms polished company production, because viewers trust a regular person more than a brand. [L0707, 2026]
- Strong video ads combine three things: showing how the product works, using UGC as social proof, and a first 5 seconds that grab attention. The opening sets the base for the rest of the video. [L0777, undated]

### Filming Yourself and Solo Shoots

- Shoot solo when no helper is available: hold the product in one hand, operate the phone with the other, and prop the phone on a towel or flat surface for static shots. Ask a partner to hold the product or the phone only for shots that need two hands. [L1403, undated]
- A UGC video can be shot by one person in an ordinary room such as a bedroom or bathroom when no studio is available. A plain surface is acceptable, because the goal is to show the product clearly, not to produce a clean product-photo backdrop. [L1403, undated]
- If there is no budget for paid UGC, the creator can film the product personally, add a voice-over and short text overlays. Reusing other people's content is possible but not advised. [L0561, 2023+]
- Warehouse or packaging videos with your own logo add a layer of trust for customers. A freelancer can create this kind of corporate video. [L0950, 2023]
- Customer or staff-filmed product videos can serve as user-generated content. Film real use, explain the product's feel and fit, and post them on the store and on YouTube. [L0801, 2021]
- Point-of-view (POV) style content works well on Instagram for product marketing. Learn this format before running paid creator campaigns. [L1367, undated]
- Clients often find it useful to be shown a real video of a procedure from the business itself, such as a dentist's own footage of a treatment, because it adds credibility to ads. [L0440, 2023+]

### Outsourced Filmmakers, Photographers and Creators

- Own creatives can be produced quickly with photographers and videographers, for example sourced from China or Fiverr, who shoot product photos and videos. Show the product in hand or in use rather than only the face. [L1345, 2021+]
- A product bought from Amazon can be used for sample shots if it is cheap enough. Express shipping can deliver a product to the UK within two to three days for filming. [L1345, 2021+]
- A product shown being used by a creator in a reaction or try-on video usually converts better than a brand-made ad, because buyers respond to the face and reaction, not only the product. [L1379, 2020+]
- Creator reaction or try-on workflow:
  1. Send the product to the creator first. [L1379, 2020+]
  2. Let the creator film a reaction or try-on video. [L1379, 2020+]
  3. Ask for a private draft and review the video before it is published. [L1379, 2020+]
  4. Add any missing product details in the review before posting. [L1379, 2020+]
- If the person on camera does not match the target audience in age or style, outsource the on-camera part or the voiceover to a script writer or creator instead of filming it yourself. Record the script anyway, because the voiceover is still the timing reference for the edit. [L1403, undated]

### Partnering and Exchanging Shoots

- Partner with friends in your network who match your target audience demographic, and exchange UGC shoots for free. [L0308, 2023]

### Scripting and Shot Structure

- Ask for a UGC piece about the product built on the problem-agitate-solution framework, then refine the output through follow-up prompts before passing it to creators or clients. [L0353, 2023]
- Create short, exaggerated reaction clips of the presenter using the product in different settings, such as at a desk, in the bath, or at a friend's place. Use the funniest or most surprising clip as the ad hook and keep the others as alternative openings for testing. [L1403, undated]
- The answer to "how would you describe the brand to a friend" contains the sales language buyers use without prompting and the order in which they rank benefits. Use these exact phrases and benefit order in UGC scripts, testimonial copy and word-of-mouth ads. [L1139, 2026]
- Hooks and benefit scenes: the creator performs an action without speaking on camera, and the voiceover is added in editing. Speaking on camera is needed only for scenes such as a testimonial or a call to action. [L0638, 2023]
- Speaking to camera: videos where the seller speaks to the camera received fewer hate comments and gave better like-to-view ratios, and viewers trusted the seller more. This made talking-to-camera content useful when the video was clean and good quality. [L0657, undated]
- Speaker profile: the speaker should sound like someone who was skeptical before and only changed their mind after trying the product. [L0707, 2026]
- Naming a personal insecurity: testimonials that describe a personal insecurity remind the viewer of their own insecurity and how it makes them feel. Selling works by solving a problem or insecurity the buyer already feels, so the speaker should name it openly. [L0707, 2026]

#### Modular 60-Second Script Workflow

| Step | Action | Ids |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Divide a 60-second script into scenes: for example five hooks of five to six seconds, several benefit scenes, and two to three call-to-action scenes. | [L0638, 2023] |
| 2 | Write a creative brief for each scene with the exact action the creator should perform. | [L0638, 2023] |
| 3 | Have the creator record the full 60 seconds once. | [L0638, 2023] |
| 4 | Record voiceover and captions as separate files. | [L0638, 2023] |

| 6 | Test the combinations in the market and keep the best performers. | [L0638, 2023] |

### Voiceovers, AI Voices and Audio

- If you are uncomfortable with your own voice, use AI voice generators such as 11 Labs, with voices such as Adam or Bella. [L0308, 2023]
- 11 Labs: AI voice generator for UGC videos. It mimics UGC cheaply by combining found footage with natural-sounding voiceovers, and is an alternative to hiring creators. [L0308, 2023]
- Voiceovers should match the audience's accent. For an Australian audience, an Australian voiceover tested better than an American one, and an American voiceover did not beat a self-recorded voice. [L1427, 2023+]

### Sourcing Footage and B-Roll

- Source footage from TikTok, Instagram, or AliExpress product videos where available. [L0308, 2023]
- Use footage of the product, such as supplier or AliExpress clips, for product introductions. For emotional scenes, such as someone looking angry or exhausted, search a stock library by emotion. [L1427, 2023+]
- Reusing other people's content is possible but not advised. Prefer your own footage. [L0561, 2023+; L1377, 2022+]
- Re-shooting a proven competitor video: with the same hook, message, vibe and caption, change only the setting, and optionally the music. [L1377, 2022+]

### Testimonials, Personas and Storytelling Patterns

#### Testimonial Sources and Credibility

- A named, credible person speaking for the product, such as a respected user or expert, carries more weight than an anonymous company voice. Their personal authority can make a claim believable. [L1261, undated]
- Let a third party such as a doctor or expert recommend the product in the story instead of the brand claiming its own superiority. This keeps the tone editorial and makes the recommendation more credible. [L0578, 2023+]
- Use testimonials from named, real customers who live in the same town or area as the prospect, quoting a specific outcome they experienced. Local recognition of the customer name gives the claim more weight than the seller's own words. [L1369, undated]
- Testimonial clips of real people describing a visible result can be reused as the proof part of a new creative, including clips from ads for other products in the same category. Choose a speaker the buyer can identify with, because that is what the buyer wants to see. [L0901, 2026]

#### Celebrity, Expert and Everyday Voices

- Bring in other people to see the product through their eyes. Use a celebrity testimonial for a famous endorsement, or an ordinary person's case history or narrative for identification. Their actions and reactions make the performance feel new. [L0330, undated]
- Show experts reacting with surprise. Professional or sophisticated people who are impressed by the product add surprise, competition and discovery to the image, and their astonishment is more convincing than the ad's own claim. [L0330, undated]
- Use both celebrity testimonials and ordinary people's case histories to reaffirm the benefits. Celebrities give status, while everyday people give the reader an easy identity to share; both show the product from a new angle. [L0330, undated]
- Showing envied or admired people in ads can increase desire, because readers want the status or outcome the person represents. Use the buyer's aspirations to select who appears. [L1261, undated]

#### Chained Persona Testimonials

- A strong testimonial ad can chain several short creator testimonials, each speaking from a different persona such as a parent, a relative of someone with a specific problem, or a professional. Each persona names a different problem or angle, so one ad covers several viewer segments while the product stays the same. Keep each speaker's story short and tied to one concrete result. [L0707, 2026]

#### Contrast Testimonial Pattern

Pattern: a relatable person, such as a parent or partner, describes a contrast between age or a condition and how they look or feel, and credits the product as the cause. It works because a third person's visible result feels like proof rather than a sales claim. [L0901, 2026]

- Example 1: "My husband is fifty, but he keeps up with the kids on weekends since he started this protein mix." [L0901, 2026]
- Example 2: "My grandma was always cold until she began taking this iron supplement." [L0901, 2026]

### Faceless and AI-Generated Content

- Faceless UGC-style videos and short viral content can be produced with AI tools, so no creator has to appear on camera. [L0914, 2026]
- TopView, or similar AI platforms, creates realistic product mockups showing a person holding or using the product. This removes the need to send physical samples to influencers for every visual. [L1459, 2026]

### AI-Generated UGC Photos

Prompt, paste as-is [L0794, 2026]:

```
Write 10 image prompts for a UGC-style photo of one person holding my book. Give each prompt a different person (age, gender, natural and varied skin tone), a different camera angle and a different setting, and have the person hold the book up toward the camera. Keep the whole book cover visible and facing the camera, and do not let the person or the frame cut the cover in half. Give every prompt an authentic phone-camera look with soft focus, slight grain and natural colour, without studio gloss. Output one prompt per line, ready to paste.
```

#### UGC Photo Batch Procedure

1. Ask Claude for 10 scene prompts with different people, angles and settings. [L0794, 2026]
2. Read each prompt and remove any that do not make sense. [L0794, 2026]
3. Paste 2 or 3 scenes into Book Scene Studio as a test. [L0794, 2026]
4. Upload the book cover, 1 to 3 angles. [L0794, 2026]
5. Set the aspect to 4:5 portrait. [L0794, 2026]
6. Run the test and check that the whole book is visible in each output. [L0794, 2026]
7. Once the test passes, shoot the full list. [L0794, 2026]
8. Upload the chosen images to the review platform manually. [L0794, 2026]

#### Phone-Camera Style Rule

- Stating a phone-camera style in the prompt, such as an older phone model look with slight softness, grain and unedited colour, makes the image read more like a real customer photo than studio gloss. [L0794, 2026]
- Name the camera style in every shot prompt, and vary the person, angle and setting across at least three UGC variations. [L0794, 2026]

### Creative Hooks and Attention

- Product-in-use is the key. Show how the product works in the first moments, then support it with UGC as social proof. [L0777, undated]
