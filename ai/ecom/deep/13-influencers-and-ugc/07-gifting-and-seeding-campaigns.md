## Gifting and Seeding Campaigns

### Gifting campaign phases and funnel benchmarks

1. Gift products to a broad list of creators and record their details in a spreadsheet (Google Sheets), starting with 30 to 50 creators. [L0915, 2025-26]
2. Expect attrition. From 50 outreach DMs, roughly 30 reply, 20 want the product, and 10 post on Instagram. [L0915, 2025-26]
3. Track which creators engage with the brand on their own. These smaller creators are the ones to move into paid partnerships. [L0915, 2025-26]

- Funnel benchmark for creator outreach: of 50 DMs sent, about 30 reply, about 20 want the product, and about 10 post on Instagram. [L0915, 2025-26]
- Plan for roughly a 60 percent loss from reply to posting, so start with more creators than you need. [L0915, 2025-26]
- Arithmetic from the same benchmark: reply to post is a loss of about 67 percent (30 to 10), product request to post about 50 percent (20 to 10), and DM to post about 80 percent (50 to 10). The stated 60 percent does not match the 30-to-10 figure exactly, so keep both and use the stage figures to see where creators drop out. [L0915, 2025-26]

Numbered campaign procedure:

1. Gift the product to a broad list of 30 to 50 creators and record each creator's details in a Google Sheets list. [L0915, 2025-26]
2. Send outreach DMs and track replies. From 50 DMs, expect roughly 30 replies, 20 who want the product, and 10 who post on Instagram. [L0915, 2025-26]
3. Watch which creators engage with the brand on their own, without prompting. These are the genuinely interested ones. [L0915, 2025-26]
4. Invite the genuinely engaged, usually smaller, creators into paid partnerships. [L0915, 2025-26]
5. For a challenge or campaign, choose the paid creators from the pool of people already gifted and engaged with the brand. [L0915, 2025-26]
6. Secure usage rights before running any creator content through paid media such as Facebook ads. [L0915, 2025-26]

### Gifting outreach messages and offers

- Common mistake: a custom cushion brand sent a short email offering a free custom-designed cushion in exchange for showing it on Instagram. The email had only a website link and gave no details about what the brand wanted, the product options, or the deliverables. Creators receive many such emails every day and will rarely do gifted campaigns for an unknown brand, so generic gifting outreach like this is ignored. [L0783, 2025-26]
- Template for a gifting offer:

```
Hi [CREATOR NAME],

I saw your [SPECIFIC SERIES OR VIDEO] and loved [SPECIFIC DETAIL]. My name is [YOUR NAME] and I work on creator partnerships at [BRAND].

We would like to send you [GIFT PRODUCT] for your next [PROJECT]. In return, we are looking for one Instagram story and one TikTok post showing how you use it. Please let me know whether you are interested, and feel free to ask any questions before deciding.

Best regards,
[YOUR NAME]
```
[L0783, 2025-26]

- Formula for a gifting offer: the creator's name, a specific reference to their work, the gift and what it will be used for in their project, and the exact return asked (for example one Instagram story and one TikTok post). Stating the gift and the expectation together lets the creator judge the offer in one reading. [L0783, 2025-26]
  - Example 1: Hi Lena, your upcycling series on small bathrooms is great, and we would like to send you an oak wall panel for your next project. In return we would love one Instagram story and one TikTok post. [L0783, 2025-26]
  - Example 2: Hi Sam, your cookware reviews are exactly the style of our customers, so we would like to send you our new cast-iron pan. We are looking for one Instagram reel and one story in return. [L0783, 2025-26]
- Gifting procedure for influencer outreach:
  1. Find the creator's PO box address in their Instagram bio or YouTube description. [L1214, 2023+]
  2. Send a product personalized for them, such as a version with their name or a cutout of their photo. [L1214, 2023+]
  3. Include a short note saying the gift is from you, with your social media handle. [L1214, 2023+]
  4. Do not ask for promotion. Smaller creators often promote the gift for free as a goodwill gesture. [L1214, 2023+]

### Seeding relationships before any request

- Seed your product to major influencers in your niche, including websites, YouTube creators, Instagram and TikTok creators, larger businesses, magazines, blogs and forums. Send the product and ask for honest feedback. [L0768, 2021-22]
- Older advice: when seeding influencers, do not ask for anything in return and do not request a post. Build a real relationship first, because transactional requests for posts rarely lead to results. This applies to seeding established influencers, and it conflicts with the gifting offer format above, which states the return asked. Use the seeding rule when there is no relationship yet and the aim is trust. Use the offer format when the deliverables are defined and the creator is being asked to produce content. [L0768, 2021-22; L0783, 2025-26; L1214, 2023+]

### Non-posting losses

- About 20 percent of influencers receiving product did not post. Plan for this loss and test enough influencers to find reliable ones. [L0790, 2017]
- Read this figure as the loss among influencers who already received product. The funnel figures are higher for cold outreach: about 50 percent of creators who asked for product did not post, and about 80 percent of all DMs did not lead to a post. [L0790, 2017; L0915, 2025-26]

### Selecting creators for paid work

- Gifting cannot guarantee coverage, so use it to find genuinely interested creators. Those who engage organically are the best candidates for paid collaboration. [L0915, 2025-26]
- For a challenge or campaign, choose creators from the pool of people already gifted and engaged with the brand. They are more authentic and need less briefing on brand image and voice. [L0915, 2025-26]
- Do not pick creators only because they are viral at the moment. Choose creators who genuinely invest in the brand. [L0915, 2025-26]
- Content produced by campaign creators can be pushed through paid media such as Facebook ads, but only if you have the usage rights to that content. [L0915, 2025-26]

### Selling at cost instead of free product

- If free products are not feasible, offer an influencer the product at cost in exchange for shout-outs and links. For example, a $1,000 paddle board can be offered at $500. This is older advice from one source (2021). [L0671, 2021]
- Influencer marketing is rarely done with free products. Instead, sell the product to influencers at cost, so you lose no money and still get exposure. This is older advice from one source (2020). The 2020 source treats at-cost sales as the usual approach. The 2021 source treats it as a fallback when free product is not feasible. [L1277, 2020; L0671, 2021]

### Shipping and packaging

- Influencer marketing works best when the product ships quickly, since slow shipping damages the influencer's reputation. [L1184, 2023+]
- A custom box can be designed for the product by a freelancer on Fiverr for about $5 to $10, then quoted by a supplier at about $0.50 per box for around 100 units, which costs about $50. Creators showing the custom box in their videos build a stronger brand impression than plain shipping. [L1382, undated]

### Launch seeding with multiple creators and markets

- Launch sequence with untapped-market creators:
  1. Pick three or four influencers in an untapped market, such as certain European countries, Australia or New Zealand. [L1213, 2023]
  2. Send each influencer a brief specifying the posting day, time and the points to mention. [L1213, 2023]
  3. First video: the influencer uses the product without introducing it, which creates a rumor and vibe around it. [L1213, 2023]
  4. Second video: announce the launch date and offer a discount, such as 20%, to people who sign up to the email list before launch. [L1213, 2023]
  5. Use a signup form on the Shopify store to collect email, name and optionally phone number in exchange for an incentive of 10% to 20% off. [L1213, 2023]
  6. Let the influencer announce a time-limited sign-up discount, for example valid one to two hours. [L1213, 2023]
  7. Warm up the new email list with benefit-driven emails before the launch date. [L1213, 2023]
  8. Sell at launch while stock lasts, then plan the next hype once stock and customization are ready, often 10 to 30 days later. [L1213, 2023]
- Case: a pre-launch influencer campaign for a product not yet released built a large email list through a signup offer, then sold 20K to 60K per day on launch day. Sales were stopped once stock ran out so existing orders could be fulfilled. [L1213, 2023]
- Launching with several mid-size creators in the same region at the same time makes a product look widely discussed. Pick three to four creators with roughly 10K to 200K followers each, because their audiences overlap, with about 10 percent of one creator's followers also following another. Their combined posts reach people that no single creator would reach alone. [L1213, 2023]
- Test a product that works in the US with US influencers, then launch it in a European country such as France with a local influencer. This approach was used in 2020 and produced sales. This is older advice. [L1214, 2023+]
- Case, Mary Roost (multivitamin supplement brand): the brand gained a large TikTok push for its multivitamin hair growth products through a gifting campaign. It sent out large amounts of product and offered affiliate commissions to people who posted videos, without paying flat fees to creators. The campaign targeted several audience groups with separate creators: a health-focused mom, a fitness and supplements enthusiast, a health-field expert giving a scientific explanation, and a younger woman reporting fast hair growth. Once the topic trended and TikTok Shop offered affiliate links, buyers and more creators joined in, creating a domino effect. The campaign showed that it does not cost much: sending product and contacting creators can be enough to start. [L0505, 2025-26]
- Principle: a gifting campaign can target several audience segments at once by selecting one creator per segment, each speaking to that segment's specific interests or concerns. Pairing product gifting with affiliate commissions motivates creators to post without flat-rate fees. [L0505, 2025-26]

## Pricing, Budgeting and Negotiation

### Fee drivers

#### Audience size and engagement

- Follower count alone does not set an influencer's fee. A larger audience usually commands a higher price, but that link is not reliable, so audience size must be weighed against the other factors. [L0785, undated; L0793, undated]
- Engagement rate often matters more than follower count. Example: an account with about 500,000 followers and a 20% engagement rate can ask for more than an account with 1 million followers and a 5-6% engagement rate, because its audience is more active and more likely to buy. Engagement rate is a strong signal of what a creator can charge. [L0785, undated; L0793, undated]
- Price the whole package, not audience size alone. Niche fit, content quality, engagement rate and the campaign's brand objective together determine what is fair and justified. [L0785, undated; L0793, undated]
- For UGC that the creator posts on their own account, follower count and engagement rate significantly raise the price. The engagement rate on the creator's Instagram or TikTok account should be above 4-5%. If the brand only uses the video itself, follower count and engagement matter little. [L1402, undated]

#### Niche and expertise

- Creators who serve a narrow niche or are true specialists charge more, because few creators cover that niche and the market is less saturated. Confirm that the creator's niche and expertise level match what you are budgeting for. [L0785, undated; L0793, undated]
- Influencers who are one of only a few covering a specific task usually charge more. True specialists in their domain charge even higher rates. [L0793, undated]

#### Content quality and track record

- Content quality can justify a higher fee. Relevant factors are production value, brand alignment, how long the creator has worked as an influencer, how much content they have produced, and how well they produce it. [L0785, undated; L0793, undated]
- A creator with a proven track record, high engagement and a recognised name in the industry, who works in content full time, will usually ask for a higher fee. Leaders in their niche, such as a well-known tech creator, can command a premium because their expertise adds to the collaboration's success. [L0785, undated; L0793, undated]
- A fair fee is a judgment across audience size, engagement, niche expertise, content quality and the brand's goal. Weigh all of these before deciding what is justified. [L0785, undated; L0793, undated]

#### Usage rights

- Define the usage term clearly: state the exclusivity and the usage rights you need for the content. Exclusive use, or the right to repurpose content across channels and ads, raises the fee. [L0793, undated]
- Price exclusive usage rights, and the right to reuse content in paid ads or across several channels, separately. Charge a higher fee than for organic use on one channel. Write the granted rights into the contract. [L0793, undated]
- Document in writing which rights are granted to whom. [L0793, undated]
- Royalty alternative: a content creator who makes ad material for a brand can be paid about 3% of the revenue the creative generates in paid ads, instead of a flat fee. This works only when the brand can attribute ad revenue to that content. [L0789, 2022]

#### Deliverables and campaign complexity

- Define deliverables in detail: the exact scope of posts, stories, costs and creative work the creator must deliver. Each additional element raises the compensation. [L0785, undated; L0793, undated]
- Assess campaign complexity when pricing. Low-effort campaigns leave room to negotiate a lower fee. Campaigns that need photographers, videographers or specific locations justify higher compensation. [L0785, undated; L0793, undated]
- Price UGC by use case. Videos for paid ads need higher production quality and justify a higher budget. Organic TikTok content can use lower production quality if it is engaging and attention-grabbing. [L1402, undated]
- The platform or company used to source the creator is a major price factor, alongside the use case and the creator's reach. [L1402, undated]

#### Pricing procedure

1. Define the usage term clearly: state the exclusivity and the usage rights you need for the content. Exclusive use or the right to repurpose content across channels and ads will raise the fee. [L0793, undated]
2. Negotiate the terms openly with the influencer so both sides are satisfied, and document in writing which rights are granted to whom. [L0793, undated]
3. Assess the campaign's complexity: low-effort work leaves room to negotiate the price, while high effort such as photographers, videographers or specific locations requires higher compensation. [L0793, undated]
4. Define the deliverables precisely (posts, stories, creative work). Each additional element should increase the compensation. [L0793, undated]
5. Evaluate the track record: well-known leaders in their niche who make their living from this work charge more, because their expertise contributes to the success of the collaboration. [L0793, undated]
6. Negotiate the final compensation with all of these points factored in, and check that the price fits your budget. [L0793, undated]

### Market benchmarks

- Influencers charge anywhere from about $50 to about $1 million per collaboration. Before reaching out, determine where your budget sits within that range. [L0793, undated]
- Know the market benchmarks for audience size, engagement rate, content quality and your niche before negotiating. Without them you risk overpaying or underpaying. [L0793, undated]
- Adjust the market rate for campaign-specific factors such as the influencer's expertise, the complexity of the work and any exclusive rights. Reflect these adjustments in the contract. [L0793, undated]

### Budget sizing

#### Budget procedure

1. Assess the overall marketing budget and set a reasonable portion for influencer marketing without compromising the strategy or the business. [L0793, undated]
2. Prioritise influencers within that budget. If an influencer is out of reach, choose another who fits both budget and goals. [L0793, undated]
3. Research industry standards for audience size, engagement rate, content quality and your niche so you know the usual rates. [L0793, undated]
4. Adjust the market rate for expertise, campaign complexity and exclusivity, and reflect this in the contract. [L0793, undated]
5. Communicate openly about the budget and any constraints during negotiation. [L0793, undated]
6. Show flexibility through long-term collaboration, mutual promotion or affiliate options. [L0793, undated]
7. Emphasise the mutual benefit, especially the unique value your brand brings to the influencer's audience. [L0793, undated]
8. Discuss a long-term partnership, in which the rate can be lowered for an extended period. [L0793, undated]

#### Sizing rules

- The influencer budget should be only a reasonable portion of the total marketing budget. Size it so that it does not compromise the overall marketing strategy or the business. [L0793, undated]
- Never choose an influencer whose price is outside your budget, even if you strongly want them. Look for an influencer who fits both your budget and your campaign goals. [L0793, undated]
- Test with smaller creators before committing large budgets. A high fee for a large influencer can return far less than expected: one reported $15,000 post returned about $2,000 in sales. [L1214, 2023+]

### Flat versus performance pay

- Flat fee: pays a fixed amount per deliverable. Use it when the deliverables and usage rights are fixed in advance and the brand cannot attribute sales to the content. [L0793, undated]
- Royalty: about 3% of the revenue the creative generates in paid ads. Use it only when the brand can attribute ad revenue to that content. [L0789, 2022]
- Small fixed payment plus affiliate commission: works when the budget is limited. The creator earns a base amount for the post and more for each sale, which aligns their incentive with yours. [L1379, 2020+]
- Affiliate commission for micro-influencers: the offered range is 6-7%. [L0226, 2017]
- Worked example: a kayak seller offers a local kayaker with a blog or Instagram account 3-5 free kayaks plus a 6% affiliate commission per referral. The creator profits from promoting to their niche audience without large upfront costs. [L0226, 2017]
- Flat rate for all creators: if a brand pays one flat rate to all creators, for example $500 per creator, state in the brief or negotiation that there is no room to negotiate. [L0579, 2025-26]

### Story versus feed pricing

- Story placements are usually much cheaper than feed posts for the same creator. For accounts with roughly 30,000-50,000 followers, a story post is often the better buy for predictable reach. [L1379, 2020+]
- Story posts give predictable reach near the creator's average story views. A feed or TikTok post can go viral but depends on video quality. [L1379, 2020+]
- Choose story posts when consistency matters. Choose feed posts when you want high variance. You can also negotiate both. [L1379, 2020+]

### Seasonal rates

- Creators charge more during the fourth quarter and around holidays. Negotiate and book posts before the season. [L1379, 2020+]

### Negotiation tactics

#### Opening and concessions

- Start with a low offer and increase only if the creator pushes back. Creators who work with you once often accept lower rates the next time. [L1379, 2020+]
- Open communication about your budget and any constraints often leads influencers to come down in price, especially when they want to work with the brand. [L0793, undated]
- Negotiate the terms openly so that both sides are satisfied. [L0793, undated]
- Ask for the creator's rate directly. For a paid Instagram post, contact the account by direct message and ask for the creator's rate for a post that shouts out your store. [L0528, 2025+]
- For industry event sponsorships, ask for the costs first, because some are expensive and others are affordable. [L0528, 2025+]

#### Non-cash and incentive offers

- Non-cash offers such as long-term collaboration, mutual promotion or affiliate marketing show flexibility and can bridge a gap in the fee. [L0793, undated]
- Micro-influencers can be offered a combination of incentives:
  - free products;
  - affiliate commissions of 6-7%;
  - exclusive coupon codes for their followers;
  - free content creation help (guides or product reviews);
  - interviews featuring their expertise. [L0226, 2017]

#### Mutual value and partnership

- If your brand gives the influencer's audience unique value, the influencer may be more willing to work with you and adjust their rates. Emphasise this mutual benefit in the negotiation. [L0793, undated]
- Long-term partnerships lower the cost per collaboration. Influencers value regular income, and the brand gains a reliable partner who keeps working with them over time. [L0793, undated]
