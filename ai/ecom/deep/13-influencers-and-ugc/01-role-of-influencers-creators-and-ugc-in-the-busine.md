## Role of Influencers, Creators and UGC in the Business Model

### Role of Influencers, Creators and UGC in the Business Model

#### Where Influencer Marketing Fits

- Influencer marketing is an advanced lever for later stages of a business, not a starting tactic for a new store. [L0544, 2026+]
- Influencers are one of the core traffic sources in this model and are used alongside SEO and paid search, not instead of them. [L0759, 2020-26]
- Consistent revenue comes from paid ads, while influencers add brand and social proof. [L0661, undated]
- Influencer marketing is a long-term channel that requires patience, consistency and admin work. Over time it tends to be more profitable, consistent and reliable than relying on paid traffic alone. [L1251, 2024+]
- Affiliates, influencers and high-authority blogs rarely send meaningful sales early on. Focus first on Google Ads and Meta remarketing. Once the business has momentum, use paid affiliate deals and a referral program. [L0804, 2023]

#### When to Start (Stage-of-Business Timing)

- Validate the product with Facebook ads before investing in influencers. Then choose influencers with an engaged audience. Influencer work is best used to build social proof and brand awareness once sales exist. [L0661, undated]
- Start commissioning UGC after a product is validated, meaning ads on Facebook or TikTok already bring real buyers and the goal is to improve conversion rate and prepare for scaling. [L1402, undated]
- When building a brand with purchased stock instead of dropshipping, hire creators from the start to make authentic videos that connect people with the brand faster. [L1402, undated]
- Early growth was built by finding the most ideal customer, growing an Instagram page organically, and getting people and influencers to promote the brand. This worked well only in the earlier social media environment. [L1438, 2023+]
- Influencer marketing and referral marketing suit later stages, after validation, when sales already exist. [L0544, 2026+; L0661, undated; L0804, 2023]

#### Why Influencers Work

- Influencer marketing transfers the trust an influencer already has with followers to a brand. Recommendations often feel more genuine than traditional advertising. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- Influencer marketing is a useful way to reach a new audience and bridge the gap to consumers, especially for new brands, because the creator already has an established audience and the content feels authentic. [L0630, undated]
- Influencer marketing suits new brands because influencers already have an audience, which helps bridge the gap between a new brand and its customers. Its authentic, relatable content tends to build brand awareness and visibility, though its effect must still be measured. [L0786, undated]
- Influencer partnerships drive brand growth mainly because they put a recognizable face behind the brand, which raises visibility and credibility. [L0505, 2025-26]
- Shoppers often check a brand's social profiles to confirm it is legitimate and to see real people who have used the product. [L0505, 2025-26]
- Content posted on the creator's own page carries more trust than content they only deliver for paid media, because it uses the audience relationship the creator has built. [L0505, 2025-26]
- An influencer's large reach can expand brand awareness and visibility on social platforms, bringing the product in front of people who would not otherwise see it. [L0480, undated]

#### Creative Reuse

- Influencer collaborations give brands access to creative content that already resonates with an audience. That content can be reused as shareable material to amplify the brand's message. [L0479, undated]
- Influencers are skilled at making content that resonates with their audience. Brands can reuse that content as shareable, compelling material to amplify their message. [L0480, undated]

#### Expected Returns and Volatility

- Influencer marketing has high ROI potential if successful. When it works, the return can exceed any other paid platform. Success is not guaranteed and requires strong research skills. [L0296, 2021+]
- Influencer marketing gets traction more cheaply and quickly than paid ads. [L0561, 2023+]
- Influencer sales come in spikes and are hard to predict, so they are a gamble for direct sales. [L0661, undated]
- Influencer-driven sales are volatile and come in spikes. Do not expect steady daily revenue from influencers alone. [L1440, 2021+]
- Example: an influencer-led store sold about 16,000 euros in one day with a 50% profit margin on a one-day spike. The sales came from influencer marketing only, with no other ads running. This is one case and a spike, not a steady baseline. [L1440, 2021+]
- A survey figure cited for influencer marketing states that 86% of people who use it effectively report an immediate or long-term effect on their brand. No survey source is named. Use it only as a rough indication and verify it before relying on it in a plan or report. [L0630, undated]
- About 50% of consumers look for recommendations from others before they buy, so influencer content works best high in the sales funnel to bring in qualified traffic. [L0790, 2017]

#### Trends by Period

- Influencer marketing was described as regaining strength in 2023 after a weaker period in 2021 and 2022, so it was treated as a channel to test again. Check the current market before relying on older assumptions about any channel. [L1214, 2023+]
- Older advice from 2019 to 2022 on influencer effectiveness should be treated as dated; the 2023 recovery means it must be re-tested rather than assumed. [L1214, 2023+]

#### Brand-Image Tradeoffs

- Influencer marketing gets traction more cheaply and quickly than paid ads, but may devalue the brand image. Paid ads give more control over how the brand is perceived. [L0561, 2023+]
- Celebrity endorsements paid for across many unrelated products have damaged the credibility of testimonials. Prefer ordinary customers describing their own experience over famous names praising products they have not truly used. [L1369, undated]

| Channel | Speed to traction | Cost profile | Brand control | Revenue pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Influencer marketing | Faster, cheaper traction | Lower entry cost | Lower; may devalue brand image | Spiky, hard to predict [L0561, 2023+; L0661, undated] |
| Paid ads | Slower to start | Ongoing spend | Higher control over perception | Consistent revenue [L0561, 2023+; L0661, undated] |

#### Affiliates and Referral Partnerships

- Affiliate partnerships with blogs and creators can bring traffic and sales. They are a useful growth channel once a product is validated. [L0661, undated]
- Once the business has momentum, use paid affiliate deals and a referral program. [L0804, 2023]

#### Testing Influencer Traffic

- When paid ads produce few add-to-carts, influencer content can be tested as an alternative source of traffic. In the discussed case it generated more add-to-carts and at least one purchase. [L0361, 2024]

#### Seeding and Budget Split

- In a first year with almost no money, one brand split its marketing budget 75% to influencer seeding and 25% to remarketing. The reasoning was that traffic it paid for had to be captured again. This is one brand's choice in a low-budget situation, not a general rule. [L0790, 2017]

#### Choosing Influencers by Niche

- Influencers are useful for a print-on-demand niche only when the niche is open to influencer marketing and the influencers are not too large (not macro). Choose influencers whose audience fits the niche. [L1438, 2023+]

#### Digital Products

- Digital products such as eBooks can be promoted through influencers instead of paid ads. This approach was planned but not yet tested. [L1431, 2020+]

#### UGC Practice

- User-generated content builds trust because prospects believe other buyers' experiences more than polished brand ads. In dropshipping, raw clips from real people often move a viewer to the next step faster than traditional advertising. [L1402, undated]
- For UGC after validation: give the creator the product and let them name the store in the video to build more trust. [L1402, undated]

#### Common Mistake

- Celebrity endorsements paid for across many unrelated products have damaged the credibility of testimonials. [L1369, undated]

#### Further details

- For holiday campaigns, influencer marketing on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube and Twitter was described as the most profitable approach, ahead of paid ads. Twitter ads and influencers were described as especially strong. [L1214, 2023+]

## Setting Objectives and Choosing Platforms

### Setting One Campaign Objective

- Define one specific objective before starting an influencer campaign, such as launching a new product, promoting an upcoming sale or building brand awareness. Tell the influencer this objective so both sides work toward the same goal. [L0787, undated]
- Set the campaign objective before choosing an influencer, because brand awareness, engagement and conversions each require a different kind of influencer. [L0479, undated]

| Objective | Influencer to look for | Reason | Source ids |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand awareness | Broad reach and a large, diverse audience | A high-profile influencer can introduce the brand to many new potential customers. | [L0479, undated] |
| Engagement | An active audience whose followers regularly comment, like, share and re-post | This audience may be smaller than a broad-reach audience but is more involved. | [L0479, undated] |
| Sales and conversions | A track record of shaping purchase decisions, such as in-depth product reviews, product usage demonstrations or exclusive discounts for followers | Purchase decisions are the target. | [L0479, undated] |
| New product launch | An influencer who has previously introduced new products to their audience | Consider a promotion or giveaway with them. | [L0479, undated] |

- For awareness, a high-level influencer may still be worth it when you need impressions, brand recognition and press. Press features and backlinks from these collaborations can also drive traffic and improve search visibility. [L0790, 2017]
- Creators match different objectives. A creator with a highly engaged community who starts conversations on most posts suits community engagement. A creator who dedicates videos to product reviews suits driving sales conversions. [L0505, 2025-26]

### Defining the Target Audience First

- Define the target audience's demographics, interests and behaviour before searching for influencers, because these determine which influencer will resonate most with your customers. [L0479, undated]
- Examples of audience-first matching:
  - A fitness brand targeting health-minded young adults should look for a fitness, nutrition or wellness influencer whose followers already share that interest, not only an influencer with a large following. The influencer then aligns with the brand's values, and their followers are more likely to share the buyer's interests. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
  - A tech brand should look for an influencer who does gadget reviews, tech tips and gaming content, or who regularly publishes product reviews and covers the latest gadgets. That audience is tech-savvy and more likely to be interested in technology products. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
  - A fashion brand with bold, edgy designs aimed at young buyers should look for a streetwear or experimental fashion influencer whose aesthetic matches the brand. That style fits the brand's look and can attract new customers who match its designs. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- The right influencer depends on the brand's niche and region. A kitchenware brand may work with food bloggers, while a wellness brand may work with fitness coaches or lifestyle creators. There is no universal list of influencers for every brand. [L0505, 2025-26]
- Start with micro-influencers, and test at least five different influencer categories before choosing a focus. Categories can include fashion, beauty, fitness, pets, mommy bloggers and photographers. [L0790, 2017]

### Influencer Selection Procedure

Follow these five steps in this order:

1. Define the target audience by demographics, interests and behaviour. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
2. Set a clear campaign goal: brand awareness, engagement, conversions or another specific objective. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
3. Use an influencer marketing platform to search for candidates that fit the audience and goal. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
4. Check each candidate's social media channels on every platform they use, and check whether their content style suits your audience. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
5. Analyze the influencer's engagement metrics before collaborating. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]

- The influencer marketing platforms in step 3 are search tools for finding creators. They are a different thing from the social platforms covered in the next sections. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]

### Platform First or Influencer First

- Audiences differ significantly by platform, so a creator's audience on TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat or YouTube should be evaluated separately. Choose the platform for the campaign first, then find creators present on it. [L0505, 2025-26]
- Start influencer work by choosing the social platforms your brand will use, and complete your research on those platforms first. Only after that, identify potential influencers who are active on them. Then evaluate each candidate on content, engagement and past collaborations. [L0782, undated]
- These two rules agree on the order of platform and creator. The selection procedure above uses "platform" for influencer search tools, so keep the two meanings apart when briefing your team. [L0505, 2025-26; L0782, undated; L0479, undated]

### Instagram

- Instagram skews toward millennials and young adults aged 18 to 34 and is strongly visual. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- Instagram works well for photos, very short videos, Stories and links in Stories. It is a strong choice when you want high engagement. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- Instagram has a shop feature. A shop can be attached to the account so products can be sold directly from the platform, and influencer content can drive direct product sales. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- Instagram's "Your Algorithm" control accepts plain-language requests about the content a user wants to see. A baby-product brand could ask for parenting advice, baby care and children's DIY videos, which surfaces creators whose audiences match its target customers. [L1504, 2025-26]
- Instagram remains the largest audience for most influencers, even when they also post on TikTok. [L0789, 2022]

### YouTube

- YouTube has a broader audience and favours long-form content such as tutorials, reviews and entertainment. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- Viewers who watch a full video are more likely to buy, so YouTube has a high conversion rate. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- YouTube viewers often arrive with search intent, looking for a specific product or solution. This high purchase intent gives YouTube influencer content strong conversion potential, which is why YouTube suits product-focused influencer content. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- YouTube is sometimes used as an SEO platform, and YouTube videos can support SEO. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- A YouTube creator with large view counts can still underperform for a physical product if the audience is young and often lacks its own payment method. Check the buyer age before paying for a YouTube collaboration. [L0790, 2017]

### TikTok

- TikTok's core audience is Gen Z. Its short-form content spreads trends quickly and favours very short, fast content. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- TikTok's perceived authenticity is high, and users often buy directly from authentic creators. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- TikTok Shop allows a shop to be attached to a TikTok account. It is a newer function, and it has shown strong sales results and growth. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- TikTok influencers can deliver about three times the results of an Instagram influencer at about half the price. Once they have a following, they start receiving sponsorship offers themselves. [L1440, 2021+]
- TikTok creators can be cheaper than Instagram creators, so a TikTok-focused campaign can be proposed as an alternative to Instagram. [L0789, 2022]
- The two figures above come from different sources and make different claims. The first is a results and price claim. The second is a cost and reach comparison. The units do not state a measurement basis for either, so test with your own data before relying on them. [L1440, 2021+; L0789, 2022]

### Snapchat and Pinterest

- Snapchat and Pinterest also exist but were left out of the main comparison. Instagram, YouTube and TikTok were chosen for their engagement, sales and conversion results. [L0480, undated]
- Snapchat and Pinterest are additional social channels. For influencer vetting, check Instagram, YouTube and TikTok first, and add other channels only when the target audience clearly uses them. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- For a fashion sunglasses brand, Pinterest was used only as a repost channel and Snapchat was hard to penetrate, so the paid focus went to Facebook and Instagram. [L0790, 2017]
- Pinterest group boards have more than one contributor and are marked by a multi-person icon in the corner. Some have thousands to over 100,000 followers, so getting pins onto them can reach a much larger audience than your own profile. [L1136, 2014]
- To move existing audiences onto Pinterest, post a message on your other social accounts, such as Twitter or Facebook, inviting followers to follow your Pinterest profile or a new board. [L1136, 2014]

### Matching Content Style to Platform

- Match the platform to the content style and the audience. TikTok suits quick, short-form, entertaining content for a younger, trend-driven audience. Instagram suits visually appealing images, videos and Stories. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- Let the chosen platform shape the influencer brief, so the format delivered fits how the audience on that platform consumes content. [L0479, undated]
- For influencer marketing of clothing, try-on hauls perform best, followed by unboxing content, which can be used as reels and Stories. [L0779, 2023+]

### Brand's Own Channels

- The platforms used to vet influencers, Instagram, YouTube and TikTok, can also serve the brand's own presence. Plan the brand's own channels alongside influencer partnerships. Each platform's format and audience should guide what the brand posts there. [L0479, undated]

## The Dream 100 Partner Strategy

### The Dream 100 Concept

- The Dream 100 strategy identifies the top players already established in your niche, such as celebrities, websites, blogs, YouTubers, course creators, events, Instagram accounts, podcasters and associations. You form relationships with them so they send their traffic to your store, instead of competing with them for the same audience. This traffic source is relationship-driven and requires constant nurturing and tweaking, but it can generate consistent revenue as a gold mine channel. [L0183, undated]
- The approach means identifying people, businesses or organizations that already have an engaged audience of your target customers, then building relationships with them. This lets you gain some of the reach and trust of their audience while growing your own. [L0412, 2021-22]
- Partnerships with influencers work because target customers trust, respect and listen to that person. The "influencer" label describes this narrowly. Think of it as borrowing an existing audience's trust. [L0412, 2021-22]
- The concept has been described in sales and marketing literature for a long time and is a common way many businesses grow through relationships with others who serve the same target customer. It is used less in e-commerce than it should be, but it is a well-tested process. [L0412, 2021-22]

### The Four-Step Process [L0412, 2021-22]

1. **Research:** Identify the people, businesses or organizations that already have an engaged audience of your target customers.
2. **Follow and contact:** Follow them and make contact with them.
3. **Build a relationship:** Build a relationship with them.
4. **Explore collaboration:** Explore collaboration options with them.

### Building the Dream 100 List

- A Dream 100 list is the top 100 people and entities in your industry that already reach your ideal customers. It works like a master supplier list, but for influencers and referral partners. Blogs, companies and communities count as well as individuals. [L0528, 2025+]
- The method builds a fixed list of about 100 high-reach partners in a niche, such as influencers, community administrators or companies. You then approach them for partnerships, instead of sending individual cold emails or posting ads directly in their communities. [L0837, 2025]
- Any online place where your target audience already spends time belongs on the list. [L0528, 2025+]

### Partner Types

- Dream 100 partners can be celebrities, websites, blogs, YouTubers, course creators, industry events, Instagram accounts, podcasts, online stores that are not direct competitors, and associations or online communities centered on your niche. [L0528, 2025+; L0183, undated]

| Partner type | How to find or approach it | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Course creators | Teachers of your niche on learning platforms such as Udemy. Search the platform for niche courses. [L0528, 2025+] | [L0528, 2025+] |
| Niche competitions and industry events | Valid partner types for your list. [L0528, 2025+] | [L0528, 2025+] |
| Communities (Facebook groups, Reddit communities) | Where a whole segment already gathers, such as interior designers. Approach the group administrators, not the group. [L0837, 2025] | [L0837, 2025] |
| Blogs and YouTube channels | Approach the owners of the channel or blog about a partnership. [L0837, 2025] | [L0837, 2025] |
| Instagram pages | Approach the owner of the page about a partnership. [L0837, 2025] | [L0837, 2025] |
| Associations | Find them by searching Google for your niche name plus "association." [L1274, 2018] | [L1274, 2018] |

- To reach a whole segment at once, such as interior designers, find the communities where they already gather: Facebook groups, Reddit communities, blogs and YouTube channels. [L0837, 2025]

### Partnership Forms

- Free relationships require no upfront payment. They include guest podcast appearances, guest blog posts, speaking at industry events while representing your store, and affiliate deals where the partner earns a commission. [L0528, 2025+; L0183, undated]
- Paid relationships mean putting money or product in upfront. They include sponsored YouTube videos, Instagram posts, event sponsorships, celebrity video collaborations, and paying a creator for a recorded video. [L0528, 2025+; L0183, undated]

### Association Partnership Procedure [L1274, 2018]

1. Search Google for your niche name plus the word "association."
2. Contact each association by email or phone. Introduce yourself and say where you are calling from.
3. Offer the association's members a bonus gift or a discount on your products.
4. Ask the partner to add a link to your store, with your promo code, on their website.

The link brings customers from the partner and gives you a backlink for SEO. This works whatever you sell. [L1274, 2018]

### Why Relationships Beat Cold Outreach

- Partner credibility transfers into purchase decisions because customers recognize the name and face of a partner they trust. The partner's endorsement can move buyers who would not find the store through search alone. [L0528, 2025+]
- One-to-one cold emails to individual companies are manual and require long back-and-forth conversations. Partnership-based one-to-many outreach is usually more efficient, because many inbound cold emails get ignored. [L0837, 2025]
- Posting ads directly in groups usually gets them removed. Approach the group administrators and the owners of channels or Instagram pages about a partnership instead. [L0837, 2025]

### Approaches to Avoid

- Do not reach out to media or as-seen-on companies proactively, because the cost is high and the benefit is small. [L1281, 2022]
- If a company asks for free products in exchange for a credit, offer the products at wholesale cost instead of giving them away. [L1281, 2022]
