## Finding Influencers and Creators

### Choosing a Discovery Method

- Start with manual search on native platforms. It is the primary method because it lets you review each creator's engagement and the conversation around them directly on the platform. [L0505, 2025-26]
- Use third-party tools as a secondary option, mainly when time is short. [L0505, 2025-26]
- Once you find one suitable influencer, find more through the creators they collaborate with and the accounts the platform suggests on their profile. This builds a chain of relevant creators. [L0505, 2025-26]
- AI-generated lists are only one source. Also search Google, YouTube and Instagram for your niche terms and check which sites, channels and accounts already publish for your audience. This shows real web presence and quality that an AI list cannot reveal. [L0528, 2025+]
- Start with your own network. Use your local contacts to reach creators in the target market and ask them to promote the product there. [L0549, undated]

### Building the Long List

one source's outreach procedure (2021 material, older; the approach still applies):

1. Search keywords around your niche, for example "best kayak accessories", "kayak reviews" or "how to choose a kayak". [L0226, 2017]
2. Identify bloggers, YouTubers, Instagram creators and Twitter users who discuss your products. [L0226, 2017]
3. Create a large list of potential influencers. Expect rejection from most of them. [L0226, 2017]
4. Contact 20 to 30 influencers with personalized outreach by email, Twitter or Instagram. [L0226, 2017]
5. Assess each influencer's content quality, follower engagement, posting frequency and audience relevance, using Alexa.com or similar tools. [L0226, 2017]

If a few channels dominate the results for a keyword, rerun the YouTube search with variations. For example, add "advanced" to a beginner term. This removes generic tutorial videos and surfaces more creators in the niche.  [L0527, 2021-22]

Only contact influencers in a niche you understand. You must be able to judge the product and provide real value, so avoid chasing unrelated niches. [L0789, 2022]

### Native Platform Search

**Instagram hashtag search (free)**

1. On Instagram, search for a specific hashtag in your niche, such as hair, nails or acne. [L1213, 2023]
2. Choose hashtags with about 50K to 100K posts. [L1213, 2023]
3. Open the Top posts tab, not Recent posts, to see the most engaged creators. [L1213, 2023]
4. Check each creator's profile and avoid those who promote many products. [L1213, 2023]
5. Check the engagement rate and the comments before shortlisting. [L1213, 2023]

**Instagram hashtag research without paid tools**

1. Search hashtags related to your product. [L0788, undated]
2. Open the top results and note the creators or posts that appear. [L0788, undated]
3. Check each creator's profile and calculate their engagement rate with free online tools. [L0788, undated]
4. Keep only creators with an engagement rate of at least 3%. [L0788, undated]
5. Read the comment section to spot bought likes and generic or fake comments. [L0788, undated]

**TikTok**

- Searching a product name on TikTok shows which brands are driving trends. Other examples cited include chlorophyll drops added to water in health and wellness, and a candida cleanse product that prompted many creators to post about lifestyle changes. [L0505, 2025-26]
- The product detail page in the TikTok Shop explorer lists the creators who promote a product, with their revenue, sales and video output over the last 30 days. It also shows the highest-revenue videos for that product. [L0892, 2026]

**Instagram Explore, TikTok Discover and YouTube Search** are the native tools for the manual method. [L0505, 2025-26]

### YouTube Research

**Keyword-based channel research**

1. Compile 40 to 50 keywords covering your niche, the problem your product solves and what your target audience searches for. [L0788, undated]
2. Search each keyword and filter the results to show channels only, not videos. [L0788, undated]
3. Keep channels with between 10,000 and 100,000 subscribers. [L0788, undated]
4. Check upload frequency. A channel should post at least two videos per month, and weekly or daily posting is better. [L0788, undated]
5. Check the view rate. Recent videos should reach at least 15% to 20% of the subscriber count, and 50% or more is a strong sign. [L0788, undated]
6. Check the comments. A video with around 50,000 views should have at least 2% comment rate, roughly 100 to 200 comments, and they should be genuine. [L0788, undated]
7. Find the contact in the About section, where an email or a linked Instagram or TikTok account is often listed. [L0788, undated]

Note on the comment figures: at 50,000 views, a 2% comment rate would be about 1,000 comments, not 100 to 200. The source gives both values without reconciling them. Use the 2% rate as the stricter check and treat 100 to 200 as the floor that the source states for that view count. [L0788, undated]

**Channel size.** Sources give different thresholds:

- Step 3 above uses 10,000 to 100,000 subscribers. [L0788, undated]
- Another source considers YouTube creators to be up to roughly 100,000 subscribers, and sometimes above that. [L0788, undated]

**VidIQ** is a free tool to check YouTube channel engagement and trending videos. Use it during YouTube research. [L0788, undated]

### Paid Influencer Platforms and Tools

- **Modash.io** is a paid tool for finding Instagram influencers. It gives detailed analytics and engagement data for each creator and is considered accurate. [L0788, undated]
- **Modash** is a paid influencer search tool with good results. [L1213, 2023]
- **Influencity, AspireIQ and Tracker** are influencer marketing platforms. They connect brands with influencers and show performance data, audience demographics and engagement rates. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- These platforms list hundreds of thousands of influencers, so searching with defined criteria is much faster than finding candidates manually. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- These platforms are most useful at the start of an influencer search, because they allow data-driven filtering and comparison of many influencers instead of manual searching. They are especially useful for brands that are just starting with influencer marketing. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
- **Influencer databases** with filters: Upfluence, Aspire and Traackr. They are useful when time is short, but they only show creators who have signed up to the tool. [L0505, 2025-26]
- **Social listening tools**: Hootsuite, Sprout Social or Brandwatch. Use them to monitor conversations about relevant topics and identify creators already engaging with your topics or with your brand. [L0505, 2025-26]
- **Fiverr** can be used to hire someone who sources influencer lists to a specific brief covering country, niche, follower range and minimum engagement rate. Check any list from any source against a second analytics tool. [L0789, 2022]

**Working with a platform, in order**

1. Sign up and create an account. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
2. Define your search criteria. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
3. Search for influencers that match the target audience. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
4. Review the platform profile of each candidate. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
5. Reach out and connect with the influencer. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
6. Negotiate the deal. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]
7. Track the campaign performance. [L0479, undated; L0480, undated]

### Influencer Directories

- **Collabstar** is an influencer directory. Use it to find people, then contact and pay creators directly instead of through the directory. [L1214, 2023+]
- **InfluSpine** is a French influencer database. It requires a paid plan, reported at about €40 per month, and works best with a translation tool for non-English profiles. [L1214, 2023+]
- **InfoSpy** is a French influencer-discovery tool. It shows placement history, follower growth, engagement rate and audience demographics. Its paid version unlocks country, follower and language filters, audience breakdowns and contact email addresses. [L0789, 2022]

### Existing Customers With an Audience

Karo's influencer side can scan your existing customers. The following steps are from one source's 2021-22 material:

1. Sign up for the influencer partnership side. This is the minimum recommended setup. [L0869, 2021-22]
2. Click "Get Started" and follow the setup wizard. [L0869, 2021-22]
3. Connect your Shopify account. [L0869, 2021-22]
4. Let Karo scan your existing customers and, optionally, feed in your email list through an email tool such as Klaviyo. [L0869, 2021-22]
5. Review the flagged customers who have an audience and check whether they are relevant for your Dream 100 list. [L0869, 2021-22]
6. Contact them directly, because they already know and bought your product. [L0869, 2021-22]

Why this works:

- Customers who have an audience are a good starting point, since they have already had a good experience and own the product. [L0869, 2021-22]
- Influencers already in your customer base or email list are a shortcut in a Dream 100 process, because they already have your product and are easy to approach. Sort them into the Dream 100 list after you discover them. [L0869, 2021-22]
- Karo's influencer side also covers creators and press or media who like your brand, not only social media influencers. A customer who runs a blog, podcast or publication can be approached through the same process. [L0869, 2021-22]
- Karo's Shopify integration can read email lists from tools such as Klaviyo, so both your customer base and your email subscribers can be screened. [L0869, 2021-22]

Outreach pattern for existing customers . Approach them as a warm lead: thank them as a customer, point out that you noticed their audience, and propose exploring a way to promote your products to it.

```text
Thanks for being one of our customers. We noticed you have a following that fits our brand. Would you be open to talking about a partnership where you share our products with your audience?
```
[L0869, 2021-22]

Limits of this method:

- For a young store with few customers, Karo's discovery may find nothing. It is still worth setting up early, because the next sale could come from an influencer. [L0869, 2021-22]
- A store with many sales can still have no customers with an audience, so the free test is the only way to know. [L0869, 2021-22]
- Karo's network figures were recorded in 2021 to 2022 and are a rough scale reference only, not current figures: about 33,598 brands, 1,098,986 products, 6,853,585 influencers and 170 countries. [L0869, 2021-22]

### Agencies and Management

- **Avoid agencies that represent influencers for a fee.** Agencies often place many brands in front of the same audience, which causes burnout. Find hidden opportunities manually instead.  [L0671, 2021]
- High-level influencers usually go through their management or an agency, so you may never speak to the person directly. Mid-level and micro-influencers are usually reached directly or through an assistant, which allows a closer one-to-one relationship. [L0790, 2017]

The two rules are compatible: the avoidance rule concerns fee-based agencies representing creators, while the second rule describes how to reach top-tier creators who sit behind management. [L0671, 2021; L0790, 2017]

### Creator Size

The sources disagree on the right size. Use each figure for the situation it describes.

- **Under 50,000 followers, one country or language.** These creators are easier to work with and usually have an audience but no own product or paid-placement income yet. Avoid influencers who already sell their own course, product or brand. [L0789, 2022]
- **Micro-influencers near the target market.** When the market is a specific country, find a micro-influencer near it and have them film the video, so the accent and context fit the audience. A creator from a different country can reduce trust among local customers. [L1439, 2024+]
- **Big influencers outside the US.** For influencer marketing, target big influencers in countries other than the US, such as Germany, France, the Netherlands and Sweden. [L1440, 2021+]
- **Example range.** A specific niche, for example beauty influencers in Germany with 50,000 to 200,000 followers, makes the search more precise. [L0789, 2022]

The sources do not state a rule for choosing between small and big creators beyond the market context: small creators fit one country or language, while the big-creator guidance applies to markets outside the US. [L0789, 2022; L1439, 2024+; L1440, 2021+]

### Regional and Non-English Markets

**Choosing the market**

- Focus on one country or language and one niche when searching. Non-English markets such as Spanish, German or Dutch face less competition than English. [L0789, 2022]
- Untapped markets, such as certain European countries, Australia and New Zealand, are often better for influencer launches than the US, where beauty products are already widely seen. [L1213, 2023]
- Strong influencer markets were listed as the UK, France, the Netherlands, Germany, Canada and Australia. In Europe, the Netherlands, Germany and France were ranked strongest, with Belgium acceptable. [L1214, 2023+]

**Working in a non-English market**

- For creators in non-English countries, the influencer's posts and your website must both be in their native language. Use a human translator for the website. [L1214, 2023+]
- Message the influencer on the platform where they are most active. [L1440, 2021+]
- If TikTok is blocked or restricted in your own country, hire creators in other countries to shoot content and manage your TikTok accounts. The course operator used this approach for brands that could not access TikTok locally. [L1377, 2022+]

**Tools for non-English discovery:** InfoSpy for French creators and InfluSpine for French profiles, with a translation tool for non-English profiles. [L0789, 2022; L1214, 2023+]

### Creator Outreach Case: Thomason

Thomason is a platform connecting sellers with lifestyle bloggers who write reviews and promote products across their blog, Facebook, Instagram and Twitter.

- Offering free products, or charging $197 for a $40 product, drew backlash from the community. [L0228, 2015]
- Even charging $2 per blogger produced 50% non-completion rates. A real review needed $100 to $300 per quality blogger. [L0228, 2015]
- ROI was poor and the time investment was not worth it. The seller abandoned the platform in favor of Feedback Genius email automation and organic, sales-driven reviews. [L0228, 2015]

### Product-Specific Case: Teddy Bear Product

A seller testing a teddy bear product found it hard to crack with ads and turned to influencers.

1. Target accounts that post videos with kids, and check the audiences of those accounts. [L1379, 2020+]
2. Reach out to moms through those accounts. [L1379, 2020+]
3. Share a demo video of the product with a child reacting. Moms who saw it were more willing to promote it. [L1379, 2020+]
4. Comparison videos against a known competitor also performed reasonably well. [L1379, 2020+]
