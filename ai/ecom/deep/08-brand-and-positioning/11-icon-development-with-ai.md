### Icon development with AI

- Run the icon or logo brainstorm in a chat that already holds the brand details, such as the name, target customer and style words. The AI then has the brand context and returns concepts that fit the brand instead of generic symbols. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Suggest icon ideas with this prompt: [L1047, 2025-26]

```text
Suggest several icon ideas that could stand for [BRAND NAME]. For each one, explain its shape, the feeling it conveys, and where it would appear, such as hang tags, waistbands, packaging, an app icon, or embroidered details. Keep the look consistent with [BRAND STYLE WORDS, e.g. soft, minimal, luxe, neutral, sporty, playful].
```

[L1047, 2025-26]

- If you want a full logo rather than a separate symbol, ask for logo concepts instead of icon concepts in the brainstorm prompt. This produces a cohesive logo design that already includes the brand name. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Choose the concept whose shape and mood match the target customer and the brand direction. A refined, luxury-leaning silhouette can fit a women's brand better than a more generic symbol, even if the generic one looks more striking at first. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Pick icons that match brand maturity. A sophisticated, techy or luxury brand fits abstract or geometric marks, while cutesy, cartoon-like graphics suit a playful, girly brand. Compare each candidate with competitor logos in the same niche to see where it lands. [L1047, 2025-26]
- An abstract symbol built from three flowing lines can carry several meanings at once, such as breathability, movement and luxury. This gives the brand a story it can reference in marketing and product messaging. [L1047, 2025-26]
- The icon must fit the existing brand elements, such as the font and existing icons. A geometric symbol pairs well with a squared-off typeface, while a playful rounded font suits a more cartoonish mark. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Judge an icon by how it looks at small sizes, such as a profile picture circle or an app icon, not only at full size. These are the places where the symbol appears alone, so it must stay recognizable there. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Manual icon method: [L1047, 2025-26]
  1. Decide on the exact icon idea you have in mind before you open the chat.
  2. Draw the logo on a sheet of paper. You do not need artistic skill, but expect several tries.
  3. Take a clear photo of the sketch.
  4. Drag and drop the photo into the ChatGPT chat.
  5. Ask ChatGPT to turn the sketch into the icon.
  6. Compare the AI result with the sketch and refine it if needed.
- Sketch-to-icon prompt: [L1047, 2025-26]

```text
Can you turn this sketch into the icon? Keep the exact outline, proportions and key details of my drawing, and turn it into a clean, simple shape that works as a logo.
```

[L1047, 2025-26]

- AI image generation can ignore details described in the text, such as a leg silhouette, and return a plain letter shape instead. For a specific shape, upload a sketch so the AI follows your drawing rather than a written description. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Smoothing prompt for a sketch-based icon: [L1047, 2025-26]

```text
Smooth this icon so the edges are clean and even. Then make it even smoother and more minimalistic, keeping the core shape and proportions unchanged.
```

[L1047, 2025-26]

- Logo pattern in use, from a case: [L1047, 2025-26]
  - **Starting point:** a women's luxury-leaning fleece and activewear brand had an original stylized logo that was judged not versatile enough for everyday brand use. A mockup on an Instagram profile showed the wordmark fit poorly where a compact icon would fit well.
  - **Step 1:** ChatGPT brainstormed icon concepts, including a curved C with a leg silhouette, a spiral C, a flame C, a soft mountain, a crescent moon, a monogram and a wave C. The curved C with leg silhouette was the first favorite, and it was generated as concept 1.
  - **Step 2:** Concept 1 ignored the leg shape and came out as a plain C. The team drew the leg by hand, photographed the sketch, and uploaded it so ChatGPT could turn it into an icon. The sketched leg icon was more recognizable but its first AI version was rough.
  - **Step 3:** The team went back to an earlier message and branched the conversation to generate the mountain concept as concept 6. The mountain concept was judged fine but without much personality. The crescent moon concept 9 was rejected, and the monogram concept 8 looked like a copyright symbol.
  - **Step 4:** The leg sketch was refined by asking for smoothing and a more minimalistic look, which made it cleaner. The wave C concept 4 was judged abstract, luxurious and suitable as a profile icon, with three lines that could suggest breathability, movement and luxury.
  - **Step 5:** The wave C was chosen over the leg icon. The leg icon was seen as more cutesy and playful, while the brand was judged more mature and techy, and the geometric wave C paired well with the existing geometric icon and squared-off font.
  - **Step 6:** ChatGPT regenerated the chosen icon as a transparent PNG, which was then vectorized in Kittl and tested by scaling up.
  - **Step 7:** Variations were built in a grid of icon, wordmark, stacked and left-aligned logos, each in black, white and color, plus black and white text over a color icon. The stacked logo used center alignment and a larger text than symbol.
  - **Result:** all variations were exported at 3x size as PNG and SVG files, and screenshots were collected on a FigJam board. The next planned step was a branding deck.

### Icon shape and style patterns

- A curved C icon: centered and left-aligned versions look strongest, though other alignments such as right-aligned are possible. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Choosing the alignment that suits the icon shape is a principle to follow. [L1047, 2025-26]

### Variations

- Build a logo variation grid in Kittl. Rows are layouts and columns are colour versions. [L1047, 2025-26]

| Layout | Black | White | Color | Black/Color | White/Color |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Icon | black icon | white icon | color icon | not used | not used |
| Wordmark (text only) | black text | white text | color text | not used | not used |
| Stacked (center aligned) | black | white | color | black text, color icon | white text, color icon |
| Left aligned | black | white | color | black text, color icon | white text, color icon |

Black/Color means black text with a color icon, and White/Color means white text with a color icon. [L1047, 2025-26]

- Plan colour versions from the start. A colour icon with black text or with white text is very common and is needed for many backgrounds. You will not realise you need these combinations until you try to use the logo. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Choose a logo colour version for each background type: a light version for light backgrounds and a dark version for dark backgrounds. A colour that works on white often needs to be lightened to work on black. [L0952, undated]
- A minimum set of logo versions to launch a brand is one light version, one dark version and one social media profile version. [L0952, undated]
- In a stacked logo, make the text larger than the symbol for a more balanced look. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Align the stacked and left-aligned logo versions by eye, judging by how they look visually, rather than following strict alignment hacks. This optical approach usually produces the best result. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Test versatility by checking the logo on each background and in each context before finalizing. [L0952, undated]

### Formats and files

- A transparent PNG is an image with no background, so it can be placed on any colour or photo. A normal image with a white box cannot be used on top of other designs or surfaces. [L1047, 2025-26]
- A raster file such as PNG becomes pixelated and blurry when enlarged, so it fails on large uses such as building signs, vehicle graphics and billboards. A vector version is required for any logo that must be used at large sizes. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Use PNG files with a transparent background for logos and favicons. JPG files do not support transparency and show a white box around logos placed on a dark background. Only PNG and JPG files are generally needed for a Shopify site. ** [L1371, 2021-22]
- Export the logo variations: [L1047, 2025-26]
  1. Click each variation in the group.
  2. Choose Quick Export.
  3. Pick the format: PNG for the transparent raster version, SVG for the vector version.
  4. Set the size to 3x.
  5. Click Export.
  6. Repeat for every variation in every group.
  7. Screenshot the finished variations and add them to a FigJam board for review.
- Name the downloaded logo files as [brandname]_wordmark_[A-D].png for the wordmarks, [brandname]_favicon_[A-D].png for the favicons and [brandname]_palette_swatch.png for the palette, all in lowercase. On a Mac these files land in the Downloads folder, where they can be checked in Finder before uploading. [L0954, 2025-26]
- Prepare the store logo for Shopify: [L1371, 2021-22]
  1. Open the transparent PNG logo in PicMonkey.
  2. Use Crop canvas to remove the extra white space around the logo.
  3. Use Resize to set the width to about 500 px, keeping the logo rectangular.
  4. Download as PNG.
  5. Upload it under the header logo and checkout logo in Shopify.
  **
- In the Shopify admin, the logo and the wide homepage header image can be downloaded from Content > Files by opening each file and clicking its download button. The wide header image is a separate file from a square image, so pick the wide version for the Facebook cover photo. [L0654, 2020-26]

### Favicons

- Create one favicon per wordmark option: a PNG showing the first letter of the brand name. The favicon is what appears in the browser tab, and a store without one looks unfinished. [L0954, 2025-26]
- A favicon is the small icon shown in the browser tab, and a site without one shows a default blank globe icon. Use an element of the logo so the browser tab looks polished and recognizable. ** [L1371, 2021-22]
- Favicon image size: the Shopify Favicon field asks for a 32 x 32 px PNG, while the lesson resized the favicon to 40 x 40 px. Use 32 x 32 px where the Shopify field specifies it. The favicon should be a square PNG with a transparent background that represents the brand. ** [L1371, 2021-22]
- Create a favicon in Shopify with PicMonkey: ** [L1371, 2021-22]
  1. Open the logo PNG with a transparent background.
  2. Use Crop canvas to make a square around one element of the logo, such as an icon, and click Apply.
  3. Click Resize, enter 40 in both width and height in pixels, and click Apply.
  4. Click Download, keep the PNG file type, and save the file.
  5. In Shopify Theme settings under Favicon, click Select Image, upload the file, and select it.
- Theme settings > Favicon provides several colour variants of the favicon image in the Files list. Pick the variant that matches the logo colours so the browser tab icon stays consistent with the brand. [L1309, 2020-26]

### Social profile versions

- For social media profile pictures, the logo should be square and have a lot of empty space around it so it fits the circular or square crop without being cut off. A simpler, centred version is usually better for this use. [L0952, undated]
- A square profile version and a wide header version serve different places. The profile uses the square version, and the Facebook cover photo uses the wide header version from Content > Files. [L0654, 2020-26; L0952, undated]
- Mock up the logo on the real profile before deciding. A wordmark can fit poorly in a small profile circle while a compact icon fits well. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Judge the profile version at small sizes such as a profile picture circle, because the symbol appears alone there and must stay recognizable. [L1047, 2025-26]

### Logo don'ts

- Avoid busy, cartoonish or sports-team style logos, because they look childish and unprofessional. A logo that looks amateur undermines trust in the store from the first impression. [L0953, 2026]
- Do not use a busy or heavily decorated template, even if it catches the eye first. [L0953, 2026]
- Do not over-engineer the logo. When in doubt, choose the simpler option. [L0952, undated]
- Do not spend much time on logo design at the start. [L0361, 2024]
- Do not write the logo in lowercase where the brand expects capital letters. A logo written in lowercase can look careless. *(Lowercase is a valid option where it is the brand's chosen style, such as option A in the wordmark prompt.)* [L0656, undated; L0954, 2025-26]
- Do not repeat the same element inside a logo, because it is distracting and weakens the mark. A monogram-style letter mark can also look like an existing copyright sign, so check how it reads before using it. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Do not make the symbol larger than the text in a stacked logo, because it often looks wonky. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Do not leave colour versions until later. Plan them from the start. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Do not expect AI to follow a written description of a specific shape. Upload a sketch instead. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Do not include the domain extension such as.com in the wordmark text. [L0954, 2025-26]
- Do not use a circular logo in a header-bound layout, because it is difficult to size and display properly. ** [L0323, 2021-22]
- Do not add a tagline unless it genuinely resonates with the target market's values or positioning. ** [L0323, 2021-22]
- Do not use a raster PNG at large sizes such as signs, vehicle graphics or billboards. Use a vector version. [L1047, 2025-26]
- Do not place a normal image with a white box on top of other designs or surfaces. Use a transparent PNG. [L1047, 2025-26]

#### Further details

- Premium brands often use a single clean mark or wordmark with no extra decoration, which signals luxury. To apply this, pick one clear element, such as a monogram or a single elegant serif wordmark, and leave out ornaments. Example 1: a black serif wordmark with one small letter-based symbol on a plain background. Example 2: a white minimal monogram of the brand's initials in a thin circle. [L0953, 2026]
