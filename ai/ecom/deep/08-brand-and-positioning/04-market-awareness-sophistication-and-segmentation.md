## Market awareness, sophistication and segmentation

### Two dimensions to check before writing any ad

- Establish two dimensions before creating ads: customer product awareness (how well the target knows the problem and that a solution exists) and market sophistication (how exposed the market is to the problem and to existing products). Read the Breakthrough Advertising copywriting framework to see how these shape the whole marketing message. [L0258, undated]
- Place prospects on the awareness scale before you look at Facebook or build ads. Most advertisers target only product-aware people because it is easy, but speaking to the other levels can make sales far easier and more relevant. Spend two to five hours on this mapping. [L0643, 2015]
- Audiences who already know the product well and fully unaware audiences are both among the hardest to sell to. Unaware buyers offer the highest revenue ceiling but need the most effort to move down the funnel. [L0374, 2026]
- Map the audience through awareness stages (problem aware, solution aware, product aware) and match each stage to a marketing angle built on the pain point. This is what lets the audience convert. [L1030, 2026]
- Position the product by where it sits in the market and how exposed that market is to similar items before writing copy. In a saturated market the product must stand out with unique features; in a less crowded market a clear problem-and-solution message can work. [L0552, 2023]

### Product awareness: the five stages

- **Most Aware:** knows the product and wants it, so it only needs an offer and a call to action. [L0497, undated]
- **Product Aware:** knows the problem and some products, so reinforce what your product does differently. [L0497, undated]
- **Solution Aware:** knows the problem and that solutions exist, but not your product, so show how it solves the problem. [L0497, undated]
- **Problem Aware:** knows the problem but no solutions, so introduce the solution. [L0497, undated]
- **Completely Unaware:** does not recognise the problem or need, so show the problem, for example through a surprising fact. [L0497, undated]
- The same five levels appear in reverse order in the Schwartz scale: unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware. Each level needs a different message, and the same logic applies to ad copy, homepage, sales page and product page. [L0643, 2015]
- Example from a dry-scalp journey: the person first becomes problem aware, then solution aware (several ways to treat a dry scalp), then product aware (narrowing to certain shampoos), and finally most aware, having chosen a brand and waiting for a discount. For the completely unaware stage, one can market a hidden problem, for example the fact that over-washing hair causes a skin condition. [L0497, undated]
- Buyers sit at different stages of product awareness. When they know the problem but not the solution, lead with the problem. When they already know many solutions, they need the product's distinct benefit to stand out. [L0644, undated]
- Product positioning depends on market awareness. A product in a crowded market needs a new mechanism or functionality to stand out, while a product that solves a new problem can be introduced directly. Each stage (problem unaware, problem aware with unknown solution, solution aware with an alternative) needs different copy. [L1426, undated]

### Reading awareness from ads and sales data

- In the Awareness Stages view, many Unaware ads suggest a new brand buying cold, top-of-funnel traffic. Many Problem Aware or Product Aware ads suggest an established brand running brand marketing or launching offers. [L0892, 2026]
- Check awareness and sophistication against sales data. When volume falls even as the ad budget rises, and a direct statement of the product's function or the desire it satisfies no longer sells, the product needs to be reborn. Treat it as an unaware-market problem and restart the analysis at the identification stage. [L0330, undated]
- Products that lose public acceptance are often reborn by identification rather than by a new claim. The reader is brought into the ad through a situation or self-image he recognises, not through a desire he has already rejected. [L0330, undated]
- A declining product was revived when its headline changed to an identification one. The approach addressed a fear that daily habits were damaging the reader's health, then offered a healthy alternative with no direct comparison to the old product. The brand recovered its market, while the old direct claims no longer worked. [L0330, undated]

### Market sophistication: the five stages

- **First to Market:** the claim is new, so just launch the product. [L0497, undated]
- **Second to Market:** copy the successful claim and amplify it. [L0497, undated]
- **Third:** the market knows the claim, so introduce a new feature or mechanism that makes the claim fresh and believable. [L0497, undated]
- **Fourth:** elaborate on a secondary claim, since the main claim has already been made. [L0497, undated]
- **Fifth:** all features are known, so attach the product to the customer's identity, as Nike does with "we represent athletes like you." [L0497, undated]
- A market with many competitors sits at the fifth or fourth stage, and its products need new mechanisms or features to stand out. [L0497, undated]

### Writing for a first-to-market product

- Write for a market where you are first, with no prior sophisticated competitors, in four steps:
  1. Keep it simple and direct; do not be fancy.
  2. Use either the need or the claim as the headline, nothing more.
  3. Dramatise the claim in the copy so it is as powerful as possible.
  4. Bring in the product and prove that it works.

  The prospects have no previous information, so once interested they become more enthusiastic, believe more and buy more readily. [L0330, undated]
- Being first can come from a technology breakthrough that makes a new product, a radically better product, or a familiar product at a very low price. It can also come from applying an established product to a market it never served, reaching an unused medium, or discovering a hidden performance that opens a bigger market. [L0330, undated]

### Second stage: enlarge the claim

- When competitors already make a direct claim that still works, you are in the second stage. Copy the successful claim and enlarge it, pushing it to the outer edge of what is legal and believable, and outbid the competition on size. [L0330, undated]
- The second stage eventually breaks down. Headlines grow longer and larger, words lose meaning, readers discount claims by about half, regulators start to investigate, and sales curves turn down. The sign to change approach is when enlargement stops working and piles on promise instead of proof. [L0330, undated]

### Later stages: identification and mechanism

- At the fifth stage the field looks exhausted: the market no longer believes the advertising, and competitors leave en masse. Revival comes through identification, not promise or mechanism. The copy brings the prospect into the ad through his own self-image and the roles he wants to play. [L0330, undated]
- When the market no longer believes product claims, the copy has to sell an identity instead of features. This is the hardest form of marketing. A brand that ties the product to who the buyer wants to be can break out of pure claim-based competition. [L0516, 2023]
- The cigarette industry passed through all five stages, which is a classic case:
  - **Stage 1:** ads focused on pleasure and taste with direct claims.
  - **Stage 2:** they grew by comparisons the market could not measure.
  - **Stage 3:** they introduced mechanisms, such as toasting processes or longer filters.
  - **Stage 4:** competitors copied these mechanisms and elaborated on them.
  - **Stage 5 (early 1950s):** with the government restricting health claims, motivation research showed brands could sell with visual identification alone, such as rugged, virile men in the ads.

  The same industry faced two side challenges. First, it opened a market to women by linking a man and a woman in an identification scene with a four-word headline. Second, a cancer scare in the late 1950s caused four reactions: holders and pipes failed, the industry funded its own research, sales dipped, and a new market for filter cigarettes opened. That market went through the same five stages in turn, returning to flavour in the fifth stage. [L0330, undated]
- Sources differ on the fourth-stage response. one source says to elaborate on a secondary claim, since the main claim has already been made [L0497, undated]. The Schwartz table says to elaborate on the mechanism and prove it works differently [L0613, 2026]. The cigarette case shows competitors copying and elaborating mechanisms at this stage [L0330, undated].

### Positioning response per sophistication level

Schwartz scale: market sophistication levels and the response each calls for.

| Level | Market state | Positioning response |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | New product with little competition | Be first, be direct, state the claim simply |
| 2 | Competitors claim similar benefits | Enlarge and extend the claim |
| 3 | Market saturated with similar claims | Introduce a new mechanism |
| 4 | Market disillusioned with previous claims | Elaborate on the mechanism and prove it works differently |
| 5 | Market highly educated and skeptical | Use identity marketing: connect to who they are |

Rate the market's level 1-5 with explicit evidence, and rate customer awareness level 1-5 with a detailed analysis. [L0613, 2026]

### Mapping the message: formulas and patterns

- **TMO message formula:** describe what the prospect has at the moment, framed neutrally and not as a negative; then what they want to achieve; then present your product as the way to get there. Example: *You travel a lot and have no time for the gym? Keep your strength training in the hotel room with these resistance bands for $39.95.* [L0643, 2015]
- **Outcome formula:** state the result the customer wants, then name the life outcome it unlocks, such as freedom, security, time, status or care for family. Speaking to the unlocked outcome is more powerful than describing the result alone.
  - Example: *A five-minute morning routine that clears your inbox means you leave work on time for your kids' school pickup.*
  - Second example: *A bookkeeping tool that cuts monthly admin to one hour means you stop dreading tax season and sleep better.* [L0993, undated]
- **Fear-of-falling-behind formula:** promise the look or result the buyer already wants, then attach the fear of falling behind a current trend or technology. It names both the desire and the threat to that desire, so the viewer feels the message is about their own tension.
  - Example: *Keep your home office looking polished without falling behind on the latest smart-desk setups.*
  - Second example: *Look timeless at the gym without missing the newest fitness-tracking features everyone else is using.* [L0993, undated]
- **Ask-the-customer prompt:**

```
Before you came across (BRAND), which methods were you relying on to deal with (PROBLEM), and what made them fall short? Goal: map the solutions the customer already knows, how aware and advanced they are, where rival products let them down, and the precise language they use to describe that gap. Apply it to the angle that other options have already let them down.
```
[L0906, 2026]

- **Marketing angle pattern:** a marketing angle is the same product presented through a different buyer motivation. Build it as: target audience, their specific situation, and images, description and reviews that speak to that situation.
  - Example 1: wireless earbuds for runners, with sweat-proof visuals.
  - Example 2: the same earbuds for commuters, with visuals of train travel and noise reduction. [L1219, undated]
- The same product can perform very differently by angle, because the wrong audience can hide a good product. Test several angles before dropping a product. [L1219, undated]
- The marketing angle and the offer decide profitability. A product can fail with one offer and work after the offer and the interest targeting are changed. [L1176, 2023]
- Case: a necklace product spent about 1,000 USD on ads and returned about 600 USD in sales, and it was not profitable at first. The offer was weak and the targeting used broad celebrity interests with a very diverse audience. After changing the offer and switching to niche interests tied to a specific community in the US, the product became profitable, though the break-even ROAS remained a constraint. [L1176, 2023]
- Test niche interest targeting before broad celebrity interests, since narrow, specific audiences can outperform wide ones. [L1176, 2023]
- A broad hook such as "stay warm this winter" often fails. Narrow the message to a specific audience, for example cyclists or football fans, using the same product, and test several niches to find the one that converts. [L1316, 2024+]
- Use a holiday gifting angle for gift products, and show what buyers gift it for in ads and on the storefront. Keep gifting messaging running through the holiday period. [L1225, 2025]

### Choosing a segment

- Market segmentation means splitting a broad market into smaller groups of people who share characteristics or needs, so each group can receive a focused message it responds to more strongly. Segmenting still helps in a saturated market, because a narrower angle can stand out among many competing offers. [L0993, undated]
- Targeting a huge group, such as every snowboarder or every motorcycle rider, dilutes the audience and makes the message generic. Narrow the group by context, problem or need instead. [L0993, undated]
- Example: a pet hair removal brush sold to everyone competes with countless similar brushes. Positioning it for German Shepherd owners, whose dogs shed heavily and lie on the bed, gives one specific problem to speak to: a dirty bed and flaring allergies. The ad can carry this most specific message even if the whole store is not built around it. [L0993, undated]
- Example: a generic ad for a watch case that says it keeps all your watches in a box speaks to anyone. A message aimed at frequent travellers who must match a watch to each outfit on a trip, for example to Dubai, is more potent because it names a context and a need. [L0993, undated]
- A company with a small budget should not fight larger rivals for the whole market. It should choose a smaller segment defined by social profile rather than geography. That choice then sets the headline, the copy, the proof and the media, such as specialist magazines and recommendations from doctors instead of drugstore shelves. [L0330, undated]
- When one desire splits into sub-groups with different reasons for wanting it, keep the core appeal but change the mechanism for each group. For example, weight loss for appearance favours speed and ease of results, while weight loss for health favours safety and lasting results. [L0330, undated]
- Once a campaign to a narrow segment succeeds, widen it to the larger market and use the segment's results as the point of difference. Concrete results from the first group, such as how much more often the group relied on the product than on alternatives, give the wider campaign a reason to be believed. [L0330, undated]
- A persona for a motorcycle brand targeted carefree, somewhat rebellious riders who reject conventional gear styles. Speaking to that attitude gave the message more pull than a general message aimed at every motorcyclist. [L0450, 2022+]
- Choosing a market based on products alone, or focusing research on product lists, leads to weak markets. Start from the customer and the interest that brings them together. ** [L0771, 2021-22]
- Store-level segment rules **:
  - A store that tries to appeal to every segment often appeals to none. Choosing one segment and serving its interests better than any competitor works, and the segment must be large enough, which depends on the market size. [L0905, 2021-22]
  - Segmentation can be based on product style instead of how the buyer uses the product. The principle is the same: one defined group of buyers is served directly. [L0905, 2021-22]
  - Personal familiarity with the target customer's hobby or lifestyle helps but is not required. The owner of a successful focused store had no personal interest in the product's use. [L0905, 2021-22]
  - A segment-focused store still sells to buyers outside that segment. The focus means it captures most of that segment's traffic and sales compared with other stores. [L0905, 2021-22]
  - When a store is built around one buyer segment, the content, design and social posts all need to address that segment's specific use, so a visitor recognises the site as made for them. Competitors that speak to everyone are then easier to stand out from each time that buyer finds the store. [L0905, 2021-22]
- A reseller selling another brand's product faces competitors selling the same product. If a competitor captures a buyer's attention at the Problem Aware stage and guides them through the journey, that buyer is unlikely to switch to another seller later. ** [L0904, 2021-22]

### Niching down

- Niche down and build a brand instead of running a general store, because specialist stores compete on research and expertise rather than price. Buyers in specialist niches often buy from the store where they have researched, instead of comparing prices on Amazon or Walmart. [L0554, 2019]
- Procedure for turning a general store into a niche brand:
  1. Run ads for several products in the general store and track which one sells.
  2. Once one product is clearly winning, focus the store on it.
  3. Buy a new domain that fits that product.
  4. Create a new logo for the product's niche.
  5. Build a niche store around the winner and its variants. [L0562, undated]
- Pick a niche and build the store around a single product category, because a specialised store makes the core products clearer and easier to market than a general store. [L1147, 2020-26]
- Specialise each store around a single niche to become the recognised expert, which large generalist marketplaces cannot match. Niche specialisation also raises conversion rates. ** [L1111, 2020]
- A niche store focused on one customer type can serve that customer's needs better than a generalist such as Amazon can. Competing on this focus is realistic even against much larger companies. ** [L0771, 2021-22]
- Specialise the store on one product area and the buying experience around it. A tightly focused store with a clear identity converts better than a general store with mixed categories. ** [L0801, 2021]
- If a single product type meets all research criteria, build a dedicated store for that product type to become the authority for it. Broaden to a wider niche store only if the narrow product type fails the criteria. [L0934, 2023]
- When a niche is sold across several niches on competitor sites, a sub-niche can be split out and owned as its own niche, which lets you become the recognised authority in that specific niche. [L0835, 2023]
- Combining two niches, such as personalised jewellery with a religious identity, can create stronger customer identification and a more distinctive offer. This works when the two niches appeal to the same buyer. [L1172, 2023+]

### Brand-dominated niches

- A seller usually cannot compete in brand-dominated niches. Big manufacturers are unlikely to grant authorised retailer status to a small store. Even with authorisation, large retailers such as Best Buy, Target and Costco sell these products at a scale a small store cannot match. [L0988, 2020-26]
- Leading manufacturers in brand-dominated niches spend hundreds of millions or billions of dollars on distribution and marketing to win shoppers. A new seller cannot outspend them, so this is a reason to avoid those niches. [L0988, 2020-26]
- Brand preference is acceptable; brand loyalty is the problem. A shopper who simply prefers one brand in a niche with many options is fine. A niche is a problem when most buyers think of the same few brands first for that product type. [L0988, 2020-26]

### Identity-driven and lifestyle marketing

- When a product is a commodity, such as shoes or a period belt, marketing must be identity-driven rather than feature-driven. [L0497, undated]
- Case: a heated period belt that vibrates. The target audience is women under 50 with severe period pain, who may take time off work; further segmentation is possible. The belt entered a market with many belts already on sale, so it sits at the fourth stage of market sophistication. The brand must create new features or mechanisms and enlarge its claims to show it solves problems earlier solutions could not. [L0497, undated]
- Male grooming is a narrower market than female beauty, which is larger and more crowded. Marketing to men needs a tone that fits masculine identity, not generic beauty language. ** [L0524, 2023]
- Fashion and passion products can be sold on aspirational needs such as self-expression, status and looking good, rather than on a problem to solve. Show how the product looks and what style it gives the buyer. [L1315, 2023]
- A brand can sell the lifestyle and identity around a product instead of the product itself, as energy drink brands do when they feature motocross and surfing. Viewers buy the feeling the brand connects to, so ads should show that world, not only the item's features. [L1426, undated]
- A brand's jewellery or fashion products can be positioned as a form of self-expression, which connects strongly with a defined audience. Content about the meaning of the piece adds value and helps the brand stand out. [L1439, 2024+]
