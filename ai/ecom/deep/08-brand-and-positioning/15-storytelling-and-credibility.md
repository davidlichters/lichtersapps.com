### Storytelling and credibility

- Credibility comes from three storylines:
  1. *I've been in your shoes*: the strongest, where the author faced the same struggle and found the mechanism.
  2. The Robin Hood storyline: the author reveals a secret usually kept from ordinary people.
  3. The expert storyline: credentials such as books, TV appearances, years of experience and accomplishments.

  Use the first option if possible, fall back to the others when it is not, and mix them when that makes the story stronger. [L1365, 2019+]
- Story formula: describe a struggle that mirrors the reader's current pain, then the moment of discovering your mechanism, then the result the reader wants, achieved. Include relatable background details such as a middle-class upbringing or limited starting money. The reader sees their own life in the author and imagines the same outcome without the seller saying it directly. [L1365, 2019+]
  - Example: *I was paying for three ad tools and still seeing no sales, until I simplified my funnel to one page; within a quarter the store was profitable on half the budget.* [L1365, 2019+]
  - Example: *I worked two jobs and still had no savings, until one change to my weekly budget routine let me pay off my card in eight months.* [L1365, 2019+]
- Insider-secret formula: describe an insider secret that the powerful use, state that you are revealing it, and promise the reader the same edge. It works best when the secret is something they do not want the reader to know. It positions the seller as an ally who levels the playing field against insiders. [L1365, 2019+]
  - Example: *I spent years on the buying side of this industry and watched how big suppliers quietly steer everyone toward the most expensive tier; here is the pricing trick I kept to myself.* [L1365, 2019+]
  - Example: *Before I left the agency world, I saw which accounts got the cheapest clicks; the method they never taught clients is what I'm sharing here.* [L1365, 2019+]
- For a product that needs education, lead with the problem and story rather than the product box. Well-known brands can lead with the product because the audience already knows them. [L1225, 2025]
- Believability matters more than credibility. Facts such as years in business or number of clients build trust, but customer stories, benefits achieved and doubts resolved persuade more strongly. Facts only support persuasion and do not replace it. [L1370, undated]

### Email and community messaging

- Choose the community word that fits the niche: *community* or *family* for jewellery, *club* for passionate niches such as fitness, and *family* for pet niches. Use it in the welcome email so new subscribers feel they are joining a group. [L0605, undated]
- Add a short brand story to the footer of welcome emails, such as an owner story or any story about the store, if you do not want to share a personal name. Customers relate to it and trust the store more. [L0605, undated]
- Welcome email subject lines should combine a benefit of joining with an offer, and use curiosity-driven lines that speak to the use case. [L0605, undated]

### Restraint and tone limits

- Keep to the niche's tone rules: emojis only for playful brands, no slang in site copy, no dropshipping in consumer copy, and a quiet premium tone instead of the word luxury. [L0853, 2021; L1467, undated; L0989, 2025-26; L1016, 2026]

#### Further details

- Choose benefits based on what the target customer believes, such as concern for the environment, rather than on what the seller personally believes. A customer avatar built at the start of the process shows which beliefs and problems matter to the buyer. [L0396, 2021-22]

- A marketing message has five parts: a primary promise, a unique mechanism, dimensionalized benefits, a call to action and proof points. Each product's description should contain all five, because together they explain the promise, why it works, what the customer gains, what to do next and why to believe it. [L0399, 2021-22]

- Check whether existing product descriptions include all five marketing message parts. Missing parts are a gap to fix in the next step, not a reason to stop. [L0399, 2021-22]

If an external writer creates the product descriptions and marketing messages:
1. Share the unique mechanism with the writer.
2. Give the writer the marketing message builder information for each product they are writing about.
This helps them write accurate descriptions.
[L0399, 2021-22]
