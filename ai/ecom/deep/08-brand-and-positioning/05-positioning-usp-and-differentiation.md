## Positioning, USP and differentiation

### Work order: define the business before the product

- Start with the business differentiator. It is the most important input for finding your place in the market, so define it before choosing brand perception, creatives or messaging. [L1267, 2026]
- Know your products well enough to name two or three facts every buyer should understand. These points feed directly into your messaging and ad angles. [L1268, 2026]
- Before any creative is made, define the USP (unique selling point) and the ICP (ideal customer profile), because every creative decision depends on them. [L1019, 2026]
- Before building any ad, write one sentence that states who the product is for and which pain point it removes. [L1019, 2026]
- Judge each competitor from the buyer's perspective. Imagine a shopper with your site and two competitor sites open, and ask which concrete advantages would make that shopper choose you. [L0894, 2020-26]
- A new opportunity is only worth testing if you can name a unique selling proposition for it. If you cannot, the idea is not unique. Generic claims such as eating healthy and exercising, or investing in the stock market, do not qualify. [L1365, 2019+]
- Choosing a positioning approach: pick one of five options. For each, give concrete implementation examples, the risks, examples from comparable markets, and the proof it needs. [L0613, 2026]
  1. A new mechanism: explain in depth what makes it distinct.
  2. A superior product: name the differentiators and proof points.
  3. Identity marketing aimed at a specific group: spell out the identity markers and emotional connection points.
  4. Depositioning competitors: pinpoint their weaknesses to target.
  5. Repositioning toward a different desire: name the alternative desires to satisfy.

### Defining the USP

- The USP is the set of reasons a business gives customers to buy from it. Not every business needs one. It may be a single statement or a multi-point list. [L0893, 2021-22]
- The USP should state why a customer should buy from this business instead of a competitor. It should focus on the benefits of buying from the business, in addition to the product's own benefits, such as shipping, returns or price guarantees. [L1310, 2021-22]
- USPs are the unique selling propositions a brand repeats in its ads. In one example they were a 90-day money-back guarantee, a microcrystal technology as the main mechanism, bundle pricing under $40, two formulations for different skin needs, and bundle savings. [L0374, 2026]
- Make the USP obvious across the whole store, including the About Us page, product pages, images, graphics and all marketing. Visitors should understand within seconds what makes the store different. [L0413, 2016]
- Make the USP visible everywhere in the store, not on the About page alone. [L0413, 2016]
- The USP is the first element to dissect when analysing competitors, followed by their other marketing elements. Your own USP should be defined against what those competitors already claim, so that it stands apart. [L0898, 2021-22]
- A USP that matches competitors' USPs is not a USP, because the U stands for unique. Compare your USP against every competitor's and change it until it is distinct. [L0893, 2021-22]
- A unique selling angle is the main reason a customer buys from you instead of a competitor. Options include:
  - a lower price;
  - higher quality for buyers with more purchasing power;
  - more colours and sizes;
  - a different focus, for example sustainability instead of comfort;
  - above all, a strong offer such as BOGO (buy one, get one), buy two get one free, or bundles. [L1183, undated]
- Be specific: a clearly stated, honest difference is what makes an offer stand out. [L1183, undated]
- Define yourself by the outcomes you deliver, not by a single service category. A label such as one service category sets expectations about price and scope, while outcomes let you set your own pricing. [L0378, 2019+]
- Being different is often easier and more effective than being better. Use a distinctive trait that sets you apart, as long as it is honest and relevant to buyers. [L0378, 2019+]
- Find a seeming advantage even when products are similar. Discover a specific feature, process or fact that competitors have not yet told the market, and lead with that point. [L1261, undated]
- Brand differentiation comes from good customer support, fast shipping, a clear niche and a visible story. Together these let a store charge more than competitors selling the same product. [L0871, 2021+]
- If you copy a competitor, still beat them with better branding, a better landing page, a better offer and better ads. The copied product alone does not guarantee sales. [L1177, 2023]
- Differentiate beyond the base product when possible. Methods include:
  - a custom formula or unique composition;
  - premium packaging instead of generic plastic;
  - bonus items included;
  - superior quality in a specific area.
  Even small differentiators justify premium pricing. [L0227, 2014]

#### USP formulas and patterns

- USP formula: "[Brand] is the [category] that [specific differentiator], focused on [result the customer gets]." It names one clear reason to choose this brand over alternatives. [L1071, undated]
  - Example: "Lumen Shoes is the running shoe brand that uses recycled foam, focused on lighter, longer-lasting training."
  - Example: "Kettle Coffee is the morning coffee brand that roasts in small batches, focused on fresher taste every day."
- Multi-point USP formula: a headline claim followed by three to five short points. Each point proves a specific advantage such as speed, range, price or support. Each point should be specific and checkable so it is clearly different from competitors. [L0893, 2021-22]
  - Example: "Same-day dispatch, free sample kits, and a 60-day fit guarantee" (footwear brand).
  - Example: "Vegan ingredients, refillable packs, and carbon-neutral shipping" (skincare store).
- Single-idea headlines and multi-point USPs apply to different places. A headline has room for one main idea, and each further thought must hold the reader to pull them into the next sentence. [L0330, undated]
- A multi-point USP block lists several checkable advantages. Use it where the USP is shown as a list, and keep the headline to one idea. [L0330, undated; L0893, 2021-22]

### Primary promise, hook and proof points

- The primary promise is a headline that directly reflects the number one problem the customer wants solved when considering the product. It usually leads to the product's number one benefit, because buyers pay for the improvement in their life rather than for features. [L0416, 2021-22]
- The primary marketing promise is the main thing a business says it will deliver to customers. It is similar to the hook, so record it separately from the USP and the hook. [L0893, 2021-22]
- The hook is a one-line phrase, often a tagline, that is memorable but is not the USP. If a business has none, record no hook rather than inventing one. [L0893, 2021-22]
- Hook and promise formula: a short phrase that names the customer benefit in one or two plain words, such as ease or speed. The short wording is easy to repeat and remember across the whole site. [L0893, 2021-22]
  - Example: "Fast fixes" (repair service).
  - Example: "Simple sizing" (clothing store).
- First-line USP pattern: state the single most valuable selling point as a short, punchy headline. Choose between tangible value (free or fast shipping, a 30-day guarantee) and ownership claims (local or family owned). The shopper reads the value before the brand. [L0567, 2025-26]
  - Example: "Free delivery on every order over $80."
  - Example: "Made by a family studio, dispatched within 24 hours."
- Positioning formula: name a specific customer and the frustration they already feel. Show that your product removes it safely, and tie the benefit to an emotional need such as control, competence or relationship. Buyers respond to a message that mirrors their own situation and fears more than to a feature list. [L0395, undated]
  - Example A: "Remote workers with cluttered desks: a compact cable organizer that ends the daily search for chargers, so they feel in control of their workspace."
  - Example B: "First-time home bakers who keep burning bread: a digital proofing timer that tells them when dough is ready, so they bake with confidence."
- The brand message should answer why the product is the preferred choice over every other option the buyer could pick, including doing nothing. Prepare a short summary sentence that states that reason in one line. [L0395, undated]
- Proof points are evidence that backs up a business's claims. Examples include customer review counts and average ratings, the number of items shipped, or years in business. Big, bold proof points carry more weight than small ones. Record them with how they are displayed. [L0893, 2021-22]
- Treat every proof point as a claim that needs checking. A high rating with a large review count is strong evidence only if the reviews look genuine. [L0893, 2021-22]
- Founding year and trading history are proof of longevity and trust. A store may use its founding year, years of trading and family ownership as proof. Record the founding year and how prominently the history is told, because established stores often rely on this kind of proof. [L0893, 2021-22]
- Catalogue size is a proof point of range. A store can show the number of products, for example in the search box. Record such counts next to the other proof points so the range claim can be compared across competitors. [L0893, 2021-22]
- Statistical data works well as a USP on landing pages and ads, because people respond to figures. For example, a survey result showing a percentage of people losing productivity from a problem. [L1158, 2023]
- Build credibility with a verifiable figure about the product's content or quality, such as a percentage of actual ingredient or measured output. Buyers trust a measurable comparison more than a general claim of quality. [L1369, undated]
- Pairing a customer count with a question, such as why thousands of people choose the product, adds social proof and sets up the comparison with competitors. [L1207, undated]
- Compare your product with competitors on the points where you win. When you frame yourself as better than the alternatives, your brand looks stronger to the shopper. [L1207, undated]
- Once a store has more reviews than its competitors, the review count and rating become part of its unique selling point. For example, claim to be the highest-rated seller of a product. A large review base also raises the valuation of the business when it is sold. [L1012, 2021-22]
- Example: a large cosmetics brand was reported to have about 16,500 reviews, most of them five-star. It built that volume into its core positioning. A very large, mostly positive review base can become a headline selling point. [L1012, 2021-22]

### Unique mechanism and product-level USP

- The unique mechanism acts as a product-level USP and complements the USP of the business as a whole. Each product should have its own small USP, and the mechanism is a large part of it. [L0465, 2021-22]
- In a crowded market, a product needs a new mechanism or feature to stand out. For example, a dish brush with a built-in soap reservoir offers a different mechanism than a standard brush for the same job. [L0644, undated]
- A new mechanism explains how the new opportunity is delivered and why only your solution works. The reader must believe only your method can meet their need. Prospects have usually heard every claim, so a mechanism that is new is what makes a familiar promise feel possible again. [L1365, 2019+]
- Typical mechanisms by niche:
  - financial offers: a proprietary algorithm behind a trading system;
  - business-opportunity offers: a mentorship programme teaching a new kind of online business;
  - health: a specific ingredient combination in a single pill. [L1365, 2019+]
- Example: a dog hair removal brush enters a crowded pet grooming market. Its new mechanism is a static pick-up that lifts hair into a holder for easy disposal, which sets it apart from older spiky brushes. [L1426, undated]
- Naming formula: [memorable branded name that describes the payoff] for the new mechanism, used in the headline, proof and offer. A short label gives the reader something specific to remember and repeat, and ties the mechanism to the benefit. [L1365, 2019+]
  - Example 1: "Morning Margin Tracker", a daily dashboard that shows profit per product before the store opens.
  - Example 2: "Idle Cash Routine", a weekly transfer method that moves unused balances into interest-earning accounts.
- Work-out procedure for product mapping (one row per product, columns in this order): [L0416, 2021-22]

| Column | What to write |
|---|---|
| Product name | The exact product name |
| What is the Primary Problem this product solves? | The single main problem buyers want solved, which becomes the basis of the primary promise |
| What are the Secondary Problems this product solves? | Other problems the product solves, used as fallback headline angles |
| What is/are the Unique Mechanism(s) of the product? | The feature or technology that makes the product different |
| How does the Unique Mechanism work? | A plain explanation of how the mechanism works, with any numbers that have evidence |
| What are the product's features? | List of the product's features |
| What are the benefits of each feature? | For each feature, the benefit it gives the buyer and why it matters |

- Analysing a product's performances: [L0330, undated]
  1. List every performance the product delivers to the buyer, such as transport, dependability, economy, power, recognition, value and novelty.
  2. Group each performance under the mass desire it satisfies.
  3. Feature the single performance that will bring the most sales power at that time. Use the others only to reinforce it.
- A dominant performance must be squeezed for all its power in the ad. The reader must be convinced that only this product delivers that satisfaction. [L0330, undated]
- Worked example: an infrared sauna for two people. Its mapped primary problems were health benefits such as better sleep, relaxation, circulation, relief from joint and muscle pain, and better skin. The secondary problem was using a sauna at home rather than paying to travel to one. Its unique mechanism was a full-spectrum infrared heating system producing 1000 W, which the manufacturer claimed is 25 times the output of the closest competitor. [L0416, 2021-22]
- Product facts used in the infrared sauna copy, all as the manufacturer states them: [L0416, 2021-22]
  - full-spectrum heater output of 1000 W;
  - electromagnetic field of 1 mg against a recommended maximum of 3 mg;
  - 360-degree heating;
  - non-toxic materials;
  - lifetime warranty;
  - chromotherapy lighting;
  - assembly by two people in about one hour;
  - reservation mode that preset start up to 36 hours in advance;
  - Bluetooth and AUX audio;
  - app control via Android tablet or smartphone;
  - made from Eco-certified grade A cedar or basswood.
- When selling your own product, list its features and deliverables and compare them with competitors' lists. This matters less for resellers, but features mentioned beyond the USP are still worth recording. [L0893, 2021-22]
- If the attribute has no name yet, create one. A self-coined name that no competitor uses makes even a similar attribute look unique to the customer. [L0465, 2021-22]
- Perception is decisive. A customer who sees a named technology that competitors do not name will believe the product is different, even if the underlying attribute is similar. Name the mechanism clearly and present it consistently across the product page and packaging. [L0465, 2021-22]
- Sell a concept (positioning, big idea, unique selling proposition, gimmick), not the bare product. The concept is what makes the product stand out, and the product becomes the vehicle for it. [L0366, 2025-26; L1366, undated]
- Exception: a genuinely new product can be its own concept. Early ads can focus on its features. [L1366, undated]
- Once the category gets crowded, each ad needs a distinct angle that differentiates the product through a concept. Examples are the thinnest version, the most premium version, or a new manufacturing method. [L1366, undated]
- A technical production detail that explains why a product is special can become the headline concept. Ask the engineers how a feature is made and why competitors cannot copy it, because that answer often contains the strongest angle. [L1366, undated]
- Two products that sell well can be combined under a single concept headline. One ad then promotes both and draws attention to the catalogue. The shared concept should describe what both products achieve for the buyer. [L1366, undated]
- The concept a buyer perceives depends on price. Identical copy can read as serious electronics at a high price, a sophisticated gadget at a middle price, and a toy at a low price. Set the price with the intended concept in mind. [L1366, undated]
- Example: a pocket CB radio sold at 39.95 read as a serious electronic product similar to a full-size CB radio. At 29.95 it read as a sophisticated walkie-talkie, and at 19.95 as a toy, with almost the same copy. Use these price bands as a check on which concept a price communicates. [L1366, undated]
- Adding a recognisable ingredient or variant to a basic product can give it a distinct angle against plain versions. Examples are honey, lemon, avocado or berries in ice cubes. Test the variant against the plain version rather than assuming the twist will sell. [L0657, undated]
- Licensing a known franchise or an established brand differentiates a product from copycats. It borrows that brand's audience and authority. The value depends on how large a stake the licensing partner takes. [L0524, 2023]
- A made-in-USA positioning should be repeated across the logo, product pages, about page, mission statement, exit popups and social media banners. A single mention on the about page is not enough to convert. [L0359, 2020]

### Competitor analysis

#### Research procedures

- Competitor research for offer optimisation: [L0898, 2021-22]
  1. Identify the direct competitors in your market and the offers your target customers already buy from them.
  2. Document each competitor's offer and marketing elements in a competition research worksheet.
  3. Dissect each competitor's USP (unique selling proposition) and the other marketing elements they use.
  4. Identify the additional value each competitor gives customers beyond the core product.
  5. Compare these findings with your own offer to find where yours is unique or can become unique.
  6. Keep the completed worksheet at hand and use it when you plan conversion rate optimisation changes.
- The competitor analysis worksheet is filled with data from your main competitors, gathered from their websites and from traffic tools. Include the questions about their USP and email marketing, even though some competitors will have no answer for them. [L0441, 2021-22]
- Competitive analysis for a unique value proposition: [L0243, 2019]
  1. Identify the top three direct competitor websites selling the same products.
  2. Review each competitor's product pages, about page, shipping policy and return policy comprehensively.
  3. Document what all competitors are doing (e.g., all charge $1,000 with free shipping).
  4. Identify gaps competitors are not filling or mentioning (e.g., estimated ship date, delivery timeframe).
  5. Identify what competitors offer but do not highlight (e.g., a one-year warranty that competitors never mention).
  6. Build unique value by addressing competitor gaps: provide estimated ship and arrival dates, offer an extended warranty, include bonus gifts with orders.
- Competitor research for a product in Google Shopping: [L1508, 2022+]
  1. Search the product term in Google Shopping and note which sellers appear.
  2. Open the websites of the top sellers and review their offers, bundles, design and pop-ups.
  3. Look for a gap, such as no unique offer, and build a stronger offer on your own store.
  4. Differentiate with your own photos, since identical photos make a product look identical to shoppers.
- Competitor check via Google Shopping and the competitor's store: [L1112, 2021]
  1. Open the competitor's store and their Google Shopping listing for the product.
  2. Note what they do well, such as photos, price, promotions and product page content.
  3. Note what they do poorly or leave out.
  4. Write down what you would change on your own product page to beat them.
- Brand Tracker lists, per brand, the Ad Copies, Ad Headlines, Ad Hooks, Landing Pages, Personas, Concepts, Angles, Desires, Emotions, Awareness Stages, USPs and Ad Rank. Use these tabs to see how an advertiser frames offers, proof and urgency. [L1164, 2026]
- Top e-commerce brands with strong creative execution to study include Gymshark, Snocks, Obvi, Spacegoods, Lion's, TRX and Angles90. Reverse-engineer their ad frameworks and angles across platforms for patterns applicable to your niche. [L0258, undated]
- The top data of a tracker gives four shortcuts: use top USPs to skip angle testing, top landing pages to copy proven funnel structures, top personas to find new audiences for the same product, and top desires to build a product around a need that already sells. [L0374, 2026]
- When studying a direct-to-consumer competitor, list what it does well and what it does poorly, such as an outdated website or no ads. Build your store to beat it on the weak points. Shoppers often care more about a stronger offer than about how long a competitor has been in business. [L0857, 2025]
- Example procedure for a downloadable sewing pattern with 919 total ads: it led with Instant PDF Download, Beginner Friendly and Step-By-Step Instructions as main USPs, with Promotion / Download Pattern as its top concept. Digital product sellers can position on instant access and skill level in the same way. [L0892, 2026]

#### Reading competitors

- Judge each competitor by matching the basics first. Match the basic features that the best competitor already offers, such as high-quality photos, displayed free shipping, estimated delivery, live chat, a price match policy and financing. Then add differentiators that the competitor lacks. [L0894, 2020-26]
- Recurring complaints about a competitor's failure point are an opening. Position your brand on a better product, better service or a clear fix for that exact problem. [L1164, 2026]
- Turn recurring cons from competitor reviews into your own selling points. If many listings share one complaint, address it openly on your page as a fix, which shows you listened to customers. [L1158, 2023]
- Use Amazon review pros as copy for landing pages and ads, and use cons to identify what customers are not getting. If cons dominate, the product is a poor choice, so consider switching products. [L1183, undated]
- Identify common complaints in competitor reviews. Research all competitor reviews in the product category, identify the common complaints in negative reviews, and design your product to overcome these specific complaints. [L0227, 2014]
  - Example: if competitors' spatulas fall apart in the dishwasher, make yours dishwasher-safe and market this feature.
  - Highlight these improvements in marketing and listing copy. [L0227, 2014]
- Limitations that buyers mention about competing products reveal gaps you can fill. For example, a brush that is not suitable for knit fabrics means a product that is safe on upholstery and furniture can make that exact point its unique selling point. [L0450, 2022+]
- Look at the parts of a competitor's offer. A competitor's product is not your only lever. [L0502, 2020+]
- If a competitor sells the same product, differentiate through the parts of the offer: better reviews, better photos, ebooks, detailed descriptions, video tutorials, guarantees, a better checkout and better local payment providers. Each of these raises the perceived likelihood of achievement, lowers the perceived time delay or effort in the buyer's mind. [L0502, 2020+]
- Compare each competitor's product pages and policies, not just their prices, to find where a gap exists. [L0243, 2019; L0898, 2021-22]
