## Color palette

### Why a tight palette builds trust

- Global theme colors control buttons, text, backgrounds, and UI across the entire site, so a tight palette of 2 to 4 brand colors with consistent application makes the store look professionally designed and trustworthy to suppliers [L0101, undated].
- Inconsistent color use makes a store look untrustworthy [L0387, 2022].
- Exceeding the color limits creates visual confusion and reduces perceived trustworthiness [L0229, undated].
- Many colors and fonts quickly make a store look messy, so reuse the same 2 to 4 brand colors everywhere [L1309, 2020-26].
- a multicolor website that looks like a rainbow appears unprofessional and confuses visitors who cannot focus on key information. Restrict yourself to three carefully chosen colors plus white [L0323, 2021-22].
- for startup brands, design uniqueness matters more than design quality. Your logo and colors must visually differentiate you from competitors rather than copy their approach [L0323, 2021-22].
- Before creating a brand, research the niche's branding. Check the colors, hex codes, fonts, and font sizes used by large brands in the same niche, since they have already tested these [L1158, 2023].

### Choosing the number of colors

Sources give different counts. Use the count that matches your situation:

| Situation | Color count | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Quick product test | Black, white, and one brand color, such as the product color | [L0361, 2024] |
| Standard store | Maximum of three to four colors: black, white, and one brand color (recommended palette) | [L0229, undated; L0916, 2022+] |
| Standard store, strict limit | At most four colors: white, black, and one brand color or brand color palette | [L1345, 2021+] |
| Brand identity, main design | Three colors plus white | [L0323, 2021-22] |
| Main colors plus supporting colors | Three main colors, with two or three supporting colors used sparingly | [L0387, 2022] |
| Brand palette reused across the store | Two to four brand colors | [L0101, undated; L0775, 2020-26; L1309, 2020-26] |
| Brand colors with contrast button | Three to four colors: one primary, one contrasting button color, and one minor accent | [L1371, 2021-22] |

- Keep the total to no more than four colors when you ask for a palette [L1259, undated].
- Use one dominant neutral color with occasional bright accent colors to highlight products [L1071, undated].
- Use only one bright accent color, reserved for highlighting products or calls to action. Keep the base mostly neutral, such as black and white [L1044, undated].
- Keep one clearly separate brand color so the brand is recognized immediately. Do not use many accent colors across the site, because the brand color stops standing out [L0916, 2022+].
- Colors matter less before the store sells. The goal at this stage is a reasonable-looking site rather than perfection [L1371, 2021-22].

### Choosing colors from emotion and market

- Choose colors by considering what they mean to the brand, the customer, and the niche, since color carries emotion and signals. Colour choices should match the mood the brand wants to create [L1071, undated].
- In color psychology, purple represents luxury, royalty, rest, and sleep. It reads as feminine but more mature than pink. For brands emphasizing comfort and trust, pair purple with warm, grounded, calm accent colors rather than high-contrast or vibrant colors [L0317, 2025-26].
- Choose the three brand colors to align with the target market's expectations and emotions: water-based products benefit from blues and greens, while luxury goods work with blacks, golds, and silvers [L0323, 2021-22].
- Color selection should reflect the market context. For water sports, use oceanic blues and greens with a contrasting button color like red. For luxury goods, use sophisticated blacks, golds, and silvers. This creates emotional alignment between your visual identity and customer expectations [L0323, 2021-22].
- Older example (2021-22): Oz Off Grid uses three colors, green (primary), burnt orange (accent), and yellow. They were selected because they represent nature, survival, and off-grid living. The tagline Prepare. Survive. Prosper reinforces the target market of survivalists and environmentally conscious customers [L0323, 2021-22].
- Muting a strong red by shifting it slightly toward pink or blending it into a softer tone makes it less aggressive on a page. A gradient that moves into a softer shade of the same red has the same effect [L0659, undated].
- Take the accent color from the product photo to keep the store palette consistent. A very saturated accent often reads better on web pages when you reduce its intensity and move it slightly toward gray [L1462, undated].

### Hex, RGB, and exact codes

- Hex codes are six-character color codes, such as #540863 or #FFD3D5. They ensure exact color consistency across all brand materials: website, print, video, social media, and design work [L0317, 2025-26].
- The same hex code always produces the same color in Shopify, Kittl, web browsers, and print services [L0317, 2025-26].
- Hex codes are the universal format for specifying online colors: a hashtag symbol followed by six letters or numbers that produce the exact same color in any design software or website builder. The example #0DJ34K contains letters outside the hex range. Valid hex digits are only 0 to 9 and A to F [L0323, 2021-22; L1309, 2020-26].
- Color codes are six characters long, a mix of digits and letters in hex format. Enter the exact code from the brand file so the theme matches the logo and images [L1309, 2020-26].
- Hex codes and RGB values describe the same color in two notations: a six-digit hex code and three RGB values from 0 to 255. List both for each palette color so the color can be set in the store and reused consistently in creatives [L0954, 2025-26].
- Store the brand's hex color codes in a shared document so that anyone creating social media graphics uses the correct colors [L1000, 2017].
- Use the exact hex codes from the design tool that created your logo and images, so the store colors match the brand [L0775, 2020-26].

### Roles of colors

- your brand identity uses a primary color for main design elements, an accent color for subtle highlights, and a contrasting button color reserved for clickable elements that must stand out from the rest of the page [L0323, 2021-22].
- choose one primary color used most often, one contrasting color for buttons, and a third color for minor accents [L1371, 2021-22].
- The palette needs a dark color and a light color that work with white, so the whole store does not end up in one color. The darker colors are used for buttons and contrast [L0954, 2025-26].
- keep most of the website background white. Use color only on small elements such as the menu, footer background, buttons, and link hover states, not on the whole page [L1371, 2021-22].
- Set the color scheme before designing the site. Brand colors are part of the palette you apply to headings, backgrounds, and buttons [L1260, undated].

### Contrast and button colors

- Complementary colors sit opposite each other on a color wheel and create strong visual contrast. Use this principle to select your button color: blue contrasts with orange, purple with green [L0323, 2021-22].
- Use a darker, bolder shade of the brand color for the call-to-action button so it stands out [L1345, 2021+].
- Use a contrasting color, such as orange, for buttons so the call to action stands out [L1283, 2023].
- Keep the main text dark, such as black, so it stays easy to read [L1371, 2021-22].
- On a dark section, build text hierarchy with tint. The title should have the strongest contrast, the subtitle slightly less, and the body text the least prominence while staying readable. This keeps the title as the first thing the eye reads [L0659, undated].
- Dark backgrounds with light text can look jarring in a layout. Check colors together in a realistic website layout to avoid this problem [L1044, undated].
- Test the palette on a sample website layout before finalizing it [L1044, undated].
- A very dark brand color, such as deep purple, can look almost identical to black. Before finalizing, place the color version next to the black version and check that the difference is visible. If it is not, the color variant adds little [L1047, 2025-26].

### White space

- white space is your most important color on a website because it allows the human eye to process information clearly. Solid color blocks everywhere make pages hard to read and cause visitors to disengage and look elsewhere [L0323, 2021-22].
- Keep most of the website background white to create white space and make the site easy to read [L1371, 2021-22].

### Applying exact codes in the theme

- Global theme colors control buttons, text, backgrounds, and UI across the entire site [L0101, undated].
- Use the exact hex codes from the brand file, and copy the codes from the design tool used for the logo, favicon variants, color-matched images, and banner visuals into the theme colors [L1309, 2020-26].
- Theme settings > Colors: solid button label white. Accent 1 is the primary brand color (pink in the example). Accent 2 is the secondary color (teal in the example). Gradients are not used [L1309, 2020-26].
- Theme settings > Colors > Secondary: text black, outline button in the brand blue, background a light gray slightly lighter than the default. The price for you save red stays at default and appears on product pages later [L1309, 2020-26].
- Header navigation menu colors in the example: background black, first level link white, sub menu background white, sub menu link the brand blue, sub menu border the brand pink, and active link color in main menu white. The same brand colors are reused across these fields rather than new ones [L0775, 2020-26].

### Applying exact codes in email, chat, and apps

- To set the button fill color in Shopify Messaging template branding, copy your store's hex color code from Shopify or a browser color picker and paste it into the button fill color field [L0147, undated].
- The chat button brand color can be changed in the Shopify Inbox settings to match the store's color scheme. Take the exact hex code from the store's button color, for example via a free Chrome color picker extension, and paste it into the brand color field [L0948, 2020-26].

### Applying exact codes in design tools

- Set the color scheme before designing. In Canva, go to the home page, open Brand kit, and scroll to Brand colors. The free plan allows three brand colors, and black and white can be used alongside them [L1045, 2020-26].
- Brand colors saved in Canva's Brand kit appear as swatches in the text and element color pickers, so later designs can reuse the same palette without re-entering codes. The free plan allows three brand colors, and black and white can be used alongside them [L1045, 2020-26].
- In Adobe Illustrator, a palette saved as a color scheme in Adobe Color can be opened from the Libraries panel and applied directly to shapes and text. Save the palette there first so it is ready for the layout [L0659, undated].
- Use the same hex codes in Kittl, Shopify, and all other design tools [L0317, 2025-26].

### Ebooks and documents

- An ebook palette example uses deep forest-ink, sage green, warm coral, and soft gold, with Fraunces for headings, Inter for body text, and Space Mono for small labels. Pick one palette and two or three typefaces that match the store brand, then use the same colors for headings, highlights, and dividers across all pages [L0861, 2026].
- A calm health or wellness ebook can use the same deep forest-ink, sage green, warm coral, and soft gold palette, with Fraunces for headings, Inter for body text, and Space Mono for small labels and eyebrows. This pairing looks editorial and slightly apothecary, and avoids a generic wellness cream look [L0862, 2026].
- In an ebook that reframes a myth into a fact, give the myth and the fix two different colors, for example coral for the myth and sage for the fix. The reader sees the reframe structure at a glance before reading the text [L0862, 2026].

### Common mistakes

- Using one color for the whole store makes it look flat and gives buttons no contrast. Always add darker colors for buttons and text on top of the light tones [L0954, 2025-26].
- Too many bright colors weaken the brand's identity [L1044, undated].
- Do not use many accent colors across the site, because the brand color stops standing out [L0916, 2022+].
