## Homepage, store look and conversion presentation

### Homepage headline and hero

- The first thing a visitor reads should say what the store does for them or what result they get, not what it sells. People buy a product to solve a problem or reach a goal, so the message should name that outcome. [L0409, 2021-22]
- The homepage headline should state the business's primary promise. When the wording changes, for example for a new product launch, the core promise stays the same. [L0409, 2021-22]
- Headline pattern: name the result the customer wants, then the specific worry the store removes, in one short line. The visitor sees their own goal first, so the page is about them rather than the store. [L0409, 2021-22]
  - "Keep your gear dry on every trip, with a seal that never leaks." [L0409, 2021-22]
  - "Sleep through the night on a mattress that keeps your back supported." [L0409, 2021-22]
- Show the store's unique selling proposition in the top line or header so it is visible from the first moment. Keep it short and specific enough to set the store apart from competitors. [L0409, 2021-22]
- Use sales language with some personality and explain how the business serves the customer. A page that only pushes sales and promotions makes the store look like a commodity, especially in a competitive market. [L0409, 2021-22]
- Match the hero image with a statement of what you help the customer achieve, such as a travel experience. A sale banner placed over an aspirational image sends a conflicting message. [L0409, 2021-22]
- In the first view, use a lifestyle image, a UGC image of a person holding the product, or a high-quality product photo. The visual style should match the brand shown in the ad, so visitors who land from TikTok or Facebook feel they are on a safe, recognisable store. [L0916, 2022+]
- Organic short-video content is a main discovery channel, so many visitors arrive after watching raw, creator-made content. Real-customer photos match what they have just seen. [L0916, 2022+]
- Store name and purpose:
  - A store name should signal what the store sells. If the name suggests a gift shop, the homepage, navigation and header must confirm gift buying, because visitors judge the purpose from the name and logo first. (Older source, one source 2019-2022 range not stated; no year given.) [L0355, 2020-26]
  - Identify whether a visitor is buying for themselves or for someone else, because that changes the homepage structure and header messaging. For gift stores, the recipient (him, her, mother, father, children, grandparents) should be visible at the top of the page. [L0355, 2020-26]
- Display the best-selling category first, so visitors immediately see what the store is known for. Example: a jewelry store whose homepage is filled with bracelets because bracelets are its top seller. [L0838, 2020-26]
- Authority markers such as recognised media logos build trust and work best high on the homepage, where visitors look early rather than far down the page. [L0524, 2023]
- Visuals convey ideas and build a mental picture faster than descriptive text alone, because people process images as one of their primary senses. Use imagery alongside copy in ads, product pages and brand assets. [L0755, undated]
- If you own a physical store, use a photo of the storefront with its signage as the homepage hero image, and show products with short demo videos. [L1002, 2019]
- Order of work: focus on the offer and the placement of the offer before the look of the site. Improve targeting and the offer first, then adjust the presentation on the website. [L0408, 2021-22]

### USP block and trust claims

- USP block pattern: three to four short, bold lines, each stating one trust point (origin, safe ingredients, guarantee, reviews), with one line of supporting detail, placed in generous white space. It works because visitors scan for a reason to trust the store, and a quick scan should answer that without reading. [L0409, 2021-22]
  - "Made in a small family workshop. Every batch is tested for purity before it ships." [L0409, 2021-22]
  - "Free shipping on every order. Not happy? We cover the return shipping." [L0409, 2021-22]
- Put the trust claims that your specific buyers care about most into the USP block, such as cruelty-free, vegan, aluminum-free, safe-ingredient or made-in-a-country statements. Each claim answers an objection the buyer would otherwise have to research on their own. [L0409, 2021-22]

### Brand identity and consistent design

- Consistency before decoration:
  - Standardize all badges and logos to one color scheme, one font family and one visual style before launch. Even a new store should look like an established brand rather than a small improvised shop. [L0921, 2022]
  - Badges, icons and product images pulled from different sources, with mixed colors, fonts and styles, make a store look unprofessional and lower trust. Visitors judge this within seconds of arriving. A consistent visual system signals that real money went into the business. [L0921, 2022]
  - Large established retailers with one color scheme and one font system are a practical visual benchmark. Matching that level of consistency makes a small store look like a serious business from its first visit. [L0921, 2022]
  - Weak branding such as an amateur logo, clashing color combinations, mismatched fonts or an unchanged default theme makes a store look untrustworthy. Inconsistent design across pages signals that the store may not be legitimate. [L0390, 2019]
  - A consistent, distinctive design that competitors do not copy makes a brand recognisable at a glance, and that recognisability adds to perceived uniqueness. Choose one visual signature and apply it everywhere the buyer sees the brand. [L0411, 2021-22]
- Theme and sections:
  - Each homepage section with a Color scheme option (slideshow, collection list, featured collection, email signup, pre-footer) takes its colours from the palette set in Theme settings > Colors. Changing a section's scheme recolours only that section, which keeps the whole store consistent. [L1309, 2020-26]
  - Test the logo, fonts and colors on the live product page before finalizing. Brand fonts and colors usually need trial and error, so expect to revisit the branding once the product images and copy are in place. [L1331, 2025-26]
- Voice and copy:
  - The hero and feature copy should match the brand's own voice and niche, not the template's default category. When a template was written for another niche, rewrite headlines and body copy so each sentence fits the product's actual use. [L1188, 2026]
  - When mocking up a layout, use neutral placeholder text such as lorem ipsum. Real words on a theme can push the look of the design toward that topic and distort the choices. [L0659, undated]
- **Conflict: badge colours.** [L0921, 2022] says to standardize all badges to one colour scheme. [L1188, 2026] says to use the brand colour and logo consistently across header, footer and rating badges, but where a third-party badge is shown, use that company's official colour so it looks authentic. Apply both: your own badges and logos follow the brand scheme; third-party badges keep their official colour. [L0921, 2022; L1188, 2026]

### Store look: branded versus generic dropshipping

| Signal | Generic store | Branded store |
|---|---|---|
| Overall impression | Empty, no brand personality, reads as a quick dropship store [L0657, undated] | Branding, consistent colors and some character added [L0657, undated] |
| Niche position | Copies a competitor's store; in niches with 20 to 100 or more sellers per product, it cannot stand out [L0403, undated] | Branded landing page and store design beyond the product itself [L0403, undated] |
| Packaging and assets | Unbranded | Branded boxes, a sample product photographed for the site, a custom-branded landing page [L1469, 2023+] |
| Visuals | Generic product images | Professional branded images, lifestyle photos, custom packaging mockups [L1381, 2022] |

- A product page built with a branded template structure signals a professional store, even when using a template. Higher perceived trust raises conversion compared with minimal store designs. [L0321, undated]
- A simple, low-excitement product can sell at a premium if the product page and visuals look clean and branded. Consistent photos, restrained text, benefit copy and a small discount or bundle make the store look trustworthy. [L0692, 2023]
- Match layout and tone to the price and nature of the product. A premium product needs a refined, confident look and tone; a bargain needs a busy, deal-focused look and tone. [L1366, undated]
- For jewelry, keep the design light: few elements, elegant fonts, plenty of space, simple benefit text. Testimonials and trust signals stay. Features and comparison blocks can be removed if they add no real differentiator (e.g. anti-scratch or rust-proof material). [L1363, 2023]
- Invest little money, but make the look distinct. Custom branding raises conversion rates and separates the store from competitors. [L1469, 2023+]

### Imagery and visuals

- Brand images should mix raw images, lifestyle images and review visuals showing people who are happy with the product. This gives the impression of a real, trusted company rather than a generic dropshipping store. [L0871, 2021+]
- Sources for lifestyle images: Canva and Printful mockups, searches for lifestyle product images, or freelancers hired on Fiverr. Lifestyle images show people holding or using the product. [L1381, 2022]
- Influencer-style photography tends to devalue a premium brand image, while editorial-style photography supports higher pricing. The first impression a customer gets of the brand shapes how they see it afterwards. [L0561, 2023+]
- Phone-shot product videos are good enough if the camera is held steady and the lighting is decent. Show how the product works, such as how a lock opens or how doors move. [L1002, 2019]
- Attaching artisan videos or interviews adds value but works best after the buyer finds the product. For buyers searching generic product names, optimize product pages and the Merchant Center feed for those terms first, then use videos for email or remarketing. [L0941, 2022]
- **Tension: polished versus raw imagery.** Use the source that fits the case:
  - Premium pricing: editorial style supports higher prices. [L0561, 2023+]
  - Visitors arriving from short-video content: polished studio imagery can feel out of place; real-customer photos match what they have just seen. [L0916, 2022+]
  - Default brand image set: a mix of raw, lifestyle and review visuals. [L0871, 2021+]

### Product page trust elements

- Product pages need more than one kind of trust signal, because buyers scan images and GIFs before reading text. Combine visuals, reviews with photos, and direct answers to common concerns in the product description. [L0773, 2023]
- Product pages should answer the questions customers have before buying, so descriptions go beyond supplier stock text. Good product information gives customers the experience that makes them buy from you. [L0806, 2024]
- Show star ratings such as 4.8 out of 5 near the top of the product page. Visitors look at what other customers say first, so reviews should appear high on the page. [L1345, 2021+]
- Reviews and photos of buyers who look similar to the shopper raise the perceived likelihood of achieving the dream outcome. A product page with only a product image and no reviews or wearers makes purchase decisions much harder. [L0502, 2020+]
- Place a "guaranteed safe checkout" trust badge directly below the add-to-cart call-to-action button. Custom icons can also work if they are consistent with the brand. [L1345, 2021+]
- Once reviews exist, enable a Shopify review app and configure Google Webmaster Tools so Google crawls regularly and detects new reviews. Star ratings then appear in Google search results next to your store name. [L0245, 2020]
- Advertise on the store which brands you sell for, the way large retailers state the brands they carry. Visitors arrive searching for those products, so the brand names help them decide. [L0834, 2021]
- Keep the product page estimate for lead time or shipping visible and clear. Customers usually accept longer delivery times when they are stated openly. [L0671, 2021]

### Footer

- Make the footer clean, professional and supplier-ready. Suppliers and wholesale partners evaluate store credibility partly through footer design and completeness. [L0102, undated]
- The footer should contain:
  - The logo
  - About Us information
  - contact details
  - policy pages
  - an FAQ or how-to section
  - a short slogan or brand mission
  - a customer support email
  - links to social media profiles
  [L1326, undated]
- Replace the Twitter bird icon with X: the Twitter icon in the Manhattan theme footer should be updated to X. (2024.) [L0836, 2024]
- Do not offer competitor referrals or recommendations on your store before the customer has purchased. Add referral recommendations only after purchase, to encourage repeat visits. [L0245, 2020]

### Cart and checkout

- Cart wording should match the local language of the market. Cart title text is free to edit, so merchants selling in the UK can use words like bag or basket instead of cart. [L1231, 2025-26]
- The cart icon in Shopify theme settings under Icons can be changed from Bag to Shopping cart. This is a matter of personal preference; the cart icon is often preferred because customers recognise it. [L1371, 2021-22]
- Progress bar message pattern: state the remaining amount to the next threshold, then name the reward. This tells shoppers exactly how close they are and what they gain. [L1231, 2025-26]
  - "Add $25.00 more to unlock a free tote bag." [L1231, 2025-26]
  - "You are $15.00 away from free shipping on your order." [L1231, 2025-26]

### Mobile

- Around 80% to 85% of visitors arrive on mobile, and they have very little time to decide. The hero image takes the most space above the fold, so it must convince quickly. [L1345, 2021+]
