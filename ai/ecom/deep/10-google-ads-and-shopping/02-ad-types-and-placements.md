### Ad Types and Placements

#### Overview of Types

- Four main Google Ads types matter for a dropshipping store: Search Text Ads, Display Ads, YouTube Ads and Shopping Ads. Other ad types exist but are rarely used in this approach. [L0714, 2020-26]
- Google Ads shows ads in several placements: search results (SERP), Shopping (including on mobile), the Display Network on individual websites, Gmail and YouTube. For e-commerce, search and Shopping placements usually deserve the most attention first. [L0720, 2020-26]
- Google Ads and Bing Ads are paid search channels that show ads to people actively searching for products. Use them as main paid traffic sources next to Facebook Ads. [L0597, 2020-26]

#### Search Text Ads

- Search Text Ads appear at the top of Google search results for a keyword, marked as paid, and are won through a keyword auction where the highest bidder ranks first. Bid amount alone does not decide position because Quality Score also counts. [L0714, 2020-26]
- Google search text ads are the sponsored ads that appear above the organic results. [L0676, 2020-26]
- Search text ads can outperform Shopping in some cases, especially outside the US, so test both formats before deciding. [L0739, 2021-22]
- On a results page, Shopping ads come first, followed by the first block of search text ads, then organic results, then a second block of text ads at the bottom. Shopping results are shown above all other ads when Google has relevant products. [L0741, 2021-22]
- When relevant Shopping ads exist for a search, they are the first results a shopper sees, above search text ads and organic results. On desktop they sit at the top of the page above the fold. Google shows no further Shopping ads lower on the page, only search text ads. [L0739, 2021-22]

#### Shopping Ads

- Shopping Ads show a product image, product name, price, store URL and a short description for matching searches. They work very well for dropshipping stores and are a core part of the approach. [L0714, 2020-26]
- Google Shopping ads are paid placements that show the product image, store name and price on the front of Google and at the top of the Google Shopping section, making them the most controllable traffic source in this method set. [L0586, 2020-26]
- Google Shopping ads appear in a carousel at the top of the regular Google results page for product searches and in the Shopping tab. They are labeled "Sponsored products" and are charged per click. [L0743, 2026+]
- Shopping ads display the product image, price and product name directly in the ad, so the user sees exactly what they would be clicking. This makes these clicks purchase-ready, which is why they tend to convert better than generic ads. [L0746, 2020-26]
- Shopping ads were reported to convert about 30% better than standard text ads and to produce about 40% higher average order value. In the same source, Shopping ads made up about 60% of Google ad clicks and about 82% of non-branded clicks. [L0510, 2018]
- For high-ticket dropshipping, Google Shopping produces most of the sales that come from Google Ads traffic for about 90% of businesses, usually at the best profitability. Some stores, especially outside the US, get better results from search text ads, so test both formats before deciding. [L0739, 2021-22]
- Google Shopping is the main Google Ads format for high-ticket dropshipping. For most such businesses it generates the majority of ad-driven sales at the best profitability. Search text ads can outperform it in some cases, especially outside the US, and the Display Network is a secondary format. [L0741, 2021-22]
- Shopping ads appear at the top of the results page, where most clicks and sales come from. Free product listings appear lower and require submission to Google Merchant Center. [L0671, 2021]
- Google Shopping ads are the first traffic source for a new store, because they bring in shoppers with clear purchase intent. Facebook ads should follow to re-engage visitors who leave the site. [L1069, 2019]
- Google Shopping is the highest converting traffic source for these stores. Keep it as the core of paid traffic and focus optimization effort there. [L0664, 2020]
- Start conversion work with Google Shopping campaigns, since they usually bring most of a store's traffic, and only then move to remarketing. Judge those campaigns by their own conversion rate, not the store-wide average. [L0626, 2025+]
- Google Shopping product listing ads are a main traffic source that sends shoppers directly to a product page on the Shopify store. [L0676, 2020-26]
- Shopping ads appear in Google's Shopping tab and in regular search results when a user searches for a brand name, product name or SKU that exists in the product feed. Merchant Center must hold the products and be linked to the Google Ads account before any of this works. [L0746, 2020-26]

#### Product Listing Ads (PLA)

- Product listing ads (PLAs) are image-based product ads shown in Google search results. They display a product image, title, price, store name and star ratings, which gives shoppers a lot of buying information before they click. [L0762, 2020-26]
- Google Product Listing Ads show products with images and prices directly in Google search results and are one of the main traffic sources in this model. [L0759, 2020-26]
- PLAs reach people who are already searching for a product, unlike Facebook ads, which interrupt users who are browsing their feed without buying intent. This makes PLAs a stronger fit for buyers than interruption-based placements. [L0762, 2020-26]
- Until a store makes more than $50,000 in sales per month, keep focusing the paid budget on Google Product Listing Ads and do not start other paid channels. Google PLAs stay in use even after that point, but they are no longer the only focus. [L1085, 2018]
- PLAs only appear for commercial search queries, meaning searches where the user is looking to buy. Informational how-to queries show few or no product ads, which filters out clicks from people who are not interested in buying. [L0762, 2020-26]
- With Shopping campaigns you do not select keywords yourself. Google decides which searches trigger your product ads, mainly by matching the search against your product data, especially product titles. [L0762, 2020-26]
- Google automatically classifies each search as commercial or non-commercial and only shows product ads for the commercial ones. This classification happens without any keyword setup from the advertiser. [L0762, 2020-26]
- A commercial search such as "infrared sauna" showed many product listing ads, while the informational search "how to install cabinet doors" showed only one product ad next to a knowledge panel. The difference shows how Google separates buying intent from research intent. [L0762, 2020-26]

#### Display Network

- The Google Display Network places image banners of various sizes on partner websites across the internet. For a new store with a small budget, Display Ads usually have a weak return on ad spend and are not recommended as a starting point. They become more useful as the business grows. [L0714, 2020-26]
- Google Ads serves on the Google Display Network, which includes YouTube, Gmail and many other sites. [L0020, undated]

#### YouTube

- YouTube Ads appear before or during videos on YouTube, and viewers can skip after 5 seconds. They need extra production work to set up and usually give a weaker return on ad spend than other Google ad types, so they are not recommended for beginners. [L0714, 2020-26]
- YouTube works across the whole funnel, so it is useful at every stage when combined with other channels. [L0414, 2021-22]

#### Performance Max and Other Types

- Besides brand search, other Google campaign types include dynamic ads and Smart Shopping campaigns. Performance Max is treated as the second priority after a brand search campaign. [L1213, 2023]
- Google Ads campaign types include Shopping Campaigns, Search Campaigns, Video Campaigns and Display Network campaigns. [L0020, undated]

#### Placement and Layout

- Google search results show Shopping ads in the right-hand column and text search ads on the left. Shopping also appears on mobile, where Shopping Spotlight ads present products in a richer format. For e-commerce, Shopping placements often need the most attention. [L0720, 2020-26]
- Google changes how Shopping results are displayed over time, for example as a horizontal row at the top or a vertical block on the side. A different layout on a later check is normal and not a reason to change the campaign. [L0739, 2021-22]
- Shopping ads appear mainly on the Google search results page, but they can also show on other Google properties such as YouTube video overlays and Gmail inboxes. Placement settings control where they run. [L0739, 2021-22]
- Shopping ads appear mainly on the Google search results page and can also show as overlays on YouTube videos and in Gmail inboxes. On desktop they sit above the fold, and Google may change their layout at any time. [L0741, 2021-22]
- Shoppers can click the Shopping tab on the results page to see a larger grid of related products. Clicking a product ad sends the shopper directly to the retailer's product page. [L0739, 2021-22]
- Shopping ads always send traffic to the product page of the promoted item, not to collection pages. This is the default behavior and cannot be changed. [L0801, 2021]

### How Google Shopping Works

#### Comparison Engine

- Google Shopping works as a comparison shopping engine. Shoppers compare the same or similar products across retailers by price, title, retailer name, reviews and delivery without opening each site. Your product page is therefore judged directly against competitors shown beside it. [L0739, 2021-22]
- Customers compare the same product across several retailers on price, brand, delivery, special offers, reviews and seller. As a reseller you are therefore always compared directly with other sellers of the same product. [L0741, 2021-22]
- Google Shopping is a comparison engine by design, and avoiding comparison with other sellers of the same product is not possible within it. If you cannot accept competing with identical listings, this channel is not a good fit. [L1170, 2021-22]
- Shoppers decide mainly on price, product title, retailer name and reviews. When five or more shops sell the same product, the listing also shows delivery and special offers. [L0739, 2021-22]
- Shoppers decide on price, product title, retailer name and reviews, and may also see delivery and special offers when several shops are listed. [L0741, 2021-22]
- Shopping ads place your product alongside other sellers' near-identical listings, so price competition is a race to the bottom. Differentiate with better offers, better content and better images, not with price, and do not compete against the AliExpress listing itself. [L0577, 2023]
- Google Shopping only shows "compare prices at X stores" when multiple sellers list the same product. If only one listing exists, only one price appears. (2024) [L0836, 2024]

#### Shared Product Pages

- When several retailers sell the same product, Google groups their listings together, and the price range can be wide. A listing marked as refurbished can show the lowest price, so check the condition label when benchmarking competitors. [L0739, 2021-22]
- Google groups listings with matching product data, such as the same GTIN and brand, onto one shared product page where each seller shows its own price, delivery and offers. Resellers of the same item therefore compete on that page, and price, delivery and offer details decide which seller gets the click. [L0741, 2021-22]
- A seller who lists a refurbished unit under the same identifiers can appear with the lowest price on the shared product page, and the condition is only visible in the details. A retailer that sells only new products must expect such lower prices and compete on other factors such as delivery, offers and page clarity. [L0741, 2021-22]

#### Query Matching and Auction Entry

- For each search, Google checks whether its product feeds contain a matching product. If so, it runs an auction among the retailers who submitted that product. Entry depends on the bid, whether the advertiser has enough budget to win a click, and any negative keywords the advertiser has set. [L0741, 2021-22]
- Google Shopping entry procedure, as described in the source: [L0739, 2021-22]

1. A customer enters a search on Google. [L0739, 2021-22]
2. Google checks whether a product in the merchant's product feed can match that search. [L0739, 2021-22]
3. If there is a match, Google runs an auction among eligible retailers, considering each one's bid, budget and negative keywords. [L0739, 2021-22]
4. If the bid and budget win the auction and no negative keyword blocks the search, the Shopping ad is generated and shown to the customer. [L0739, 2021-22]

- Google's incentive is to show ads for many searches that carry some chance of a sale, not to filter by intent. The advertiser must build the filtering through bids, budgets and negative keywords, and avoid overexposure to low-value traffic that burns budget. [L0741, 2021-22]
- Google keeps matching your products to a broad range of terms because most of its revenue comes from ads, and it accepts loosely related searches as long as some sale chance exists. The advertiser therefore has to manage intent-based bidding and exclusions. [L0739, 2021-22]
- Search results and ad details depend on the searcher's location, so check how your ads look from the target country before judging them. Local tax and delivery display are part of that check. [L0739, 2021-22]

#### Taxes and Location

- In the US, Google can show a sales tax column per state, based on the shopper's IP address location. In Australia, the single national GST of 10% is included in the displayed price and is not shown as a separate column. [L0739, 2021-22]
- Some markets show sales tax as a separate column based on the shopper's IP location, while in Australia tax is included in the price and not shown separately because the national rate is a single 10%. [L0741, 2021-22]

### When Google Suits a Product or Store

#### Product Fit

- Google Ads works best for products where people already search for solutions or have pre-existing intent, such as furniture, health and fitness. [L0296, 2021+]
- Furniture and home decor are good categories for Google Ads because they are high-ticket products with good search volume. Allow one to two weeks to warm up the account so the algorithm understands the audience. [L0788, undated]
- For Google ads for clothing, collection and catalogue ads perform best. [L0779, 2023+]
- For high-ticket products (roughly 1,000 USD and up), Google Search and Google Shopping are the main paid channel to start with. Buyers of expensive items look for solutions via search engines rather than on social platforms like Facebook, Instagram or Pinterest. [L1065, 2021-22]
- For high-ticket e-commerce products, Google Ads (search and Shopping) and organic SEO are the primary marketing channels. Buyers who have a question or a problem to solve type their query into a search engine, so search is the place to meet them rather than social feeds where they are not actively looking for such products. [L1065, 2021-22]
- Google Shopping campaigns for expensive products require a different structure than campaigns for low-priced items such as deodorant, toothbrushes or 30 USD t-shirts. Follow the high-ticket approach step by step rather than applying generic low-ticket setups. [L1065, 2021-22]
- Google Shopping campaigns for high-ticket dropshipping need a more advanced setup than basic campaigns. Expect the setup and ongoing management to take more time to learn. [L0713, 2021-22]
- A good traffic source combines traffic volume, affordable cost per visitor and a good chance of conversion. Google Ads is a suitable starting point because it offers all three together. [L0713, 2021-22]
- Google Shopping is a sensible first traffic source for a new high-ticket store, and it reaches people who are already searching for the product. (see next subsection for conflicting views) [L0713, 2021-22]

#### Store Readiness and Timing

- For a new high-ticket dropshipping store, paid Google Ads and Google Shopping are a sensible first traffic source because they deliver traffic almost immediately. Organic search traffic takes much longer to build. [L0713, 2021-22]
- Paid search ads can be switched on immediately and bring traffic within a day, whereas SEO typically needs several months before measurable results appear. Paid ads therefore suit the early launch phase of a new store, but they should not be the only traffic source over time. [L1065, 2021-22]
- Early paid traffic lets a new store collect performance data, customer feedback and customer service experience. These show what buyers want, what they reject, and whether the website fits the market. [L0713, 2021-22]
- Start paid traffic with Google Ads before other paid channels, because the Google Ads supply of qualified shoppers is large enough to justify mastering it first. [L1109, 2018]
- Google Ads works best for a store that already gets roughly 1,000 to 5,000 monthly site visitors, with Google Analytics and the pixel connected. Without existing traffic and tracking, Google campaigns have little data to learn from. [L1213, 2023]
- Google Ads for drop shipping does not make sense as a first test, since Google is expensive at the top of the funnel. Use it only once the product is proven and you have a branded store, and then it is a good source of conversions. [L0577, 2023]
- Google Ads is suitable for beginners only if the product is already generating sales (proof of concept) from another platform, or if the product has inherent search intent. [L0296, 2021+]
- Revenue from Google Ads differs strongly by market. Some high-ticket stores generate multiple millions of dollars per year from Google Ads alone, while in other markets the same channel may generate only about $500,000 per year. [L0713, 2021-22]

#### Starting Point: Conflicting Views

The sources disagree on whether Google should be the first paid channel. Each applies under different conditions:

- Google Shopping as the first traffic source for a new store, with Facebook following to re-engage visitors. Source from 2019, so older. Applies to a new store with clear purchase intent. [L1069, 2019]
- Keep the paid budget on Google PLAs and do not start other paid channels until the store makes more than $50,000 in sales per month. Source from 2018, so older. Applies below that sales level. [L1085, 2018]
- Google Ads and Google Shopping as a sensible first source for a new high-ticket store, because traffic arrives almost immediately. Source from 2021-22, so older. Applies to high-ticket products with search demand. [L0713, 2021-22]
- Google as a later step, not a first test, because it is expensive at the top of the funnel. Applies to drop-shipping products that have not been proven and have no branded store. [L0577, 2023]
- Google as a beginner channel only when the product already sells elsewhere or has inherent search intent. Applies to beginners. [L0296, 2021+]
- Test top-of-funnel on Facebook and TikTok first, and test on Google once the product works with branded content. Applies to products that need awareness testing. [L0577, 2023]

#### Scaling and Competition

- A single product on Google Ads has a scaling ceiling based on search volume and user intent. Scaling depends on how much search demand exists, unlike Meta or TikTok, where account capacity allows higher scaling. [L0296, 2021+]
- An established competitor's edge in paid Shopping comes from campaigns that are better optimized with more data, and usually from higher bids, not from store age. A new store can match or outrank them quickly in paid results by optimizing its campaigns and bidding more. [L0963, 2022]
- Competition in Google Shopping is measured per product, not per store. Count the stores that sell the same specific product, not the total number of stores in the niche. (2024) [L0856, 2024]
- If more than 10 suppliers advertise the most popular product in Google Shopping, do not bid on that product with Shopping ads. Rely on free organic listings and traffic instead. [L0963, 2022]
- Google Ads is well suited to this model because spend per sale is small relative to the product price. [L1111, 2020]
- Google Ads has been the most profitable traffic source across niches for over a decade, so do not abandon it after weak early results. Refine campaigns before moving to other channels. [L0803, 2022]

#### Competitor Check Before Launching Google Shopping

Procedure for a target country: [L0788, undated]

1. Connect a VPN with an IP address from the target country. [L0788, undated]
2. Search the product name on Google. [L0788, undated]
3. Open the Shopping listings of competitors. [L0788, undated]
4. Compare their images, pricing, free shipping tags and whether they look unique or high quality. [L0788, undated]
5. Create a stronger first image if competitors look generic. [L0788, undated]

### Control and Automation

- The main advantage of paid traffic is control over who sees an ad, based on demographics, age, household income, search behavior and other signals. A billboard reaches a broad crowd and makes it hard to tell which exposures led to a sale, while targeted paid ads can be measured and adjusted. [L0720, 2020-26]
- Google Ads gives you control over where and when your ads appear. When evaluating new features or options, be skeptical of anything that removes your control, because this trades away the ability to target the right audiences precisely and prioritizes visibility over conversions. [L0020, undated]
- Avoid Google Ads features that take away control over bidding, targeting or ad creation, because they tend to optimize for visibility rather than direct conversions. Google's former AdWords Express bundled automated bidding, automated targeting and automated ad creation and is the example of this kind of setup to avoid. [L0720, 2020-26]
- Creating a campaign without goals guidance gives more manual control over settings. More control over campaigns tends to produce a higher return on ad spend than letting Google automate by fitting into its boxes. [L0734, 2020-26]
- Google's Shopping campaign setup has changed little in about a decade. Google does not publish how ad rank or Quality Score work, so be skeptical of anyone claiming hidden shortcuts. Once the known levers are handled, further account optimization usually brings diminishing returns. [L0879, 2021-22]
