### Clicks without sales (conversion)

**Benchmarks and checks**
- The working conversion target is 2.5 sales per 100 qualified clicks. [L1254, 2020-26; L0626, 2025+]
- Case: 52,000 impressions, 280 clicks, $100 spend over 4 days, 0 sales. At a 2.5% conversion rate, 300 clicks should yield 6-7 sales minimum. It is too early to judge; wait the 1-5 day conversion window before optimizing. [L0274, 2023]
- A product that gets over 100 unique visitors per month from Google Ads and does not convert is quality traffic that should be converting. Its product page is the first place to look for problems. [L1092, 2022]
- A store had 2,444 visits and one sale of $599 after 30 days, a conversion rate of 0.04%, far below the 2.5% target for Google Shopping. Ads were above 1% CTR, and six suppliers had been reduced to 12 advertised products from two of them, because the other four did not offer MAP pricing. The advice was to check the Shopify sales funnel, separate bot and organic traffic, measure the Google Shopping conversion rate on its own, then review the keyword report and compare the top products with competitors. [L0626, 2025+]
- A store with about 180 visitors per day, $1,200 per month on Google product listing ads and $50 to $100 per month on Facebook retargeting made one or two sales per day, but mostly low-priced add-on items instead of the high-ticket products the niche was built on. The fix was to split the Google Shopping campaigns by price band, then clean up keywords and improve product pages. [L1086, 2019]
- If Shopping traffic is not converting, first check the keyword triggers and the product pages, not the visitor count. About 180 targeted visitors per day should produce one or two sales per day, and unique visitors without purchases point to conversion problems. [L1086, 2019]
- A Google Shopping campaign produced 233,000 impressions over 45 days, a 0.69 percent click-through rate, and only two sales. A follow-up count showed about 1,300 unique visits, which lowered the click-through rate further. The advice was to raise click-through rate first with negative keywords and campaign segmentation, then check product and cart pages for errors by running a test purchase. [L1088, 2020]
- A store that had launched a month earlier ran Google ads for four weeks with no sales, 124 impressions and one click. (See the account-health section for the identifier fix.) [L1088, 2020]
- Spending thousands of dollars on Google Ads without a single conversion typically results from opening with budgets that are too large or leaving campaigns running without optimization. [L0666, 2024]
- Price changes can briefly lower click-through rate, especially when a price moves above the price most competitors charge. Make pricing changes when necessary, not constantly, because each change makes Google learn again. [L0856, 2024]
- Do not raise prices just because conversion is low. First analyze the campaign data, such as impressions, clicks and sales per search term and product, to find the real cause. [L0671, 2021]
- A sudden drop in conversions with steady traffic: the likely causes are new keywords bringing lower-quality traffic, or traffic going to product pages that convert poorly. Comparing product-level clicks and sales across two date ranges usually shows which one it is. (2025.) [L1097, 2025]

**Procedures for clicks without sales**

Troubleshooting campaigns with no conversions (2024):
1. Set the date range to your first 3 weeks when sales occurred. [L0276, 2024]
2. Check alpha and beta campaigns to see which products generated sales. [L0276, 2024]
3. Review ad spend distribution, impressions, clicks, and CTR in those first 3 weeks. [L0276, 2024]
4. Compare these metrics to the most recent 5 weeks with no sales. [L0276, 2024]
5. Identify which products changed in performance. [L0276, 2024]
6. Check which keywords drove impressions in the successful period vs now. [L0276, 2024]
7. Look at top-performing competitor product pages to spot positioning or messaging changes. [L0276, 2024]
8. Add non-converting search terms and competitor terms as negative keywords. [L0276, 2024]
9. Lower cost-per-click bids on products showing no conversions. [L0276, 2024]

Troubleshooting a product with clicks but no sales (2024):
1. In Google Ads reporting, check which search terms/keywords triggered the clicks for that product. [L0666, 2024]
2. If the triggering keywords are irrelevant, add them as negative keywords; if they are relevant, leave them as is. [L0666, 2024]
3. Search the product name in Google Shopping as a customer and note the listing and the 1-3 competitor listings. [L0666, 2024]
4. Compare competitor product pages for features that could drive buying and improve your own product page accordingly. [L0666, 2024]

If a Shopping product has no profitable sales yet, optimize before removing it (2024):
1. Check which search terms and keywords produced the clicks and add irrelevant ones as negative keywords. [L0856, 2024]
2. Compare the product page against the top two to three competing stores for that product and close gaps in content and presentation. [L0856, 2024]
3. Optimize the product page against the on-page setup of the store. [L0856, 2024]
4. Let about 100 more clicks run. [L0856, 2024]
5. If there are still no sales after about 200 to 300 clicks on fully optimized campaigns and pages, lower the CPC sharply or remove the product. The first roughly 100 clicks are a learning phase. [L0856, 2024]

Investigating weak conversions from ads (2021):
1. Confirm the site works and that customers can complete a purchase. [L0853, 2021]
2. Break down Google Ads performance by keyword and see which searches generate impressions and clicks. [L0853, 2021]
3. Add non-buying keywords as negative keywords. [L0853, 2021]
4. Lower the default cost per click for the least profitable products, so budget shifts toward products that earn more. [L0853, 2021]
5. Use the cleaned-up data as the benchmark, not a generic industry number. [L0853, 2021]

Auditing an account with high traffic but few sales (2022):
1. Check which keywords and ads produced the sales. [L0854, 2022]
2. Find which keywords produced many clicks and few conversions. [L0854, 2022]
3. Turn off the wasteful campaigns and reduce spend on the low-quality ones. [L0854, 2022]
4. Focus the budget on the campaigns and keywords that produced the sales. [L0854, 2022]
- If a site receives many visitors from a small ad budget, check whether the spend reached the right keywords. Many visitors with few sales point to low-quality traffic. [L0854, 2022]

Optimizing a Google Shopping campaign that is not converting after about three weeks (2021):
1. Review the analytics for keywords generating impressions and clicks, and the product pages those clicks land on. [L0834, 2021]
2. Add negative keywords for the terms that do not work. [L0834, 2021]
3. For products with many impressions but no clicks, search Google Shopping for those products and compare your competitor's price, product name, and default image. [L0834, 2021]
4. Optimize those product pages. [L0834, 2021]
5. For clicks that come from buyer-intent keywords (product name, brand name, SKU), optimize the landing product pages. [L0834, 2021]
6. Do not change the budget yet; move spend to the best-performing places. [L0834, 2021]

Auditing a Google Shopping campaign that spends without sales (2021):
1. Review the search terms that triggered your ads over the past month. [L0968, 2021]
2. Add negative keywords for anything too broad or irrelevant. [L0968, 2021]
3. Check the product groups for impressions and clicks. [L0968, 2021]
4. For products with many impressions but few clicks, compare images and prices with competitors. [L0968, 2021]
5. For products with clicks but no sales, compare the product page with competitors in split screen and improve it. [L0968, 2021]
6. Check for MAP violations. [L0968, 2021]
7. Lower the budget on products that make no sense and optimize product pages for those that do. [L0968, 2021]

Side-by-side competitor analysis for a non-converting product (2022):
1. Open your product page in a small window on the desktop. [L1092, 2022]
2. Open the same product page on the top two competitor sites. [L1092, 2022]
3. Compare them from a customer's point of view and ask why they would buy from the competitor instead of you. [L1092, 2022]
4. Change your page to make people more likely to choose you. [L1092, 2022]

Non-converting clicks and search terms:
- If clicks come in but there are no sales, look at the search terms triggering the clicks and judge their buying intent. Add irrelevant broad terms as negative keywords and increase the cost per click on terms that convert. [L1112, 2021]
- Very low conversion with clicks on buyer-intent terms: for clicks from buyer-intent keywords (product name, brand name, SKU), optimize the landing product pages before changing bids. [L0834, 2021]
- A product with 100 clicks, 80% search impression share and no conversions should first have its store page checked for conversion elements. If it still does not convert, its max CPC should be lowered so it stops taking budget. (2024.) [L0750, 2020-26]
- Raising the max CPC on a product that gets clicks but no conversions wastes budget. Check the store page and conversion elements first, then reduce the CPC if it still does not sell. [L0750, 2020-26]
- Spend on a product should be reduced if it gets clicks but no conversions, unless the store page and conversion elements are fixed first. [L0750, 2020-26]
- Check abandoned carts in Shopify and call those customers to learn what stopped them. Then audit the ad account to make sure money goes to products that convert. [L0801, 2021]
- Traffic bouncing with an average session of 0 seconds on Google Ads is most likely bot activity from Google checking the store against the Merchant Center feed. Verify by checking which keywords triggered the clicks in Google Ads campaigns, and add negative keywords if the clicks come from irrelevant search terms. (2024.) [L0971, 2024]

**Product page checks (conversion)**
- Watch visitor recordings in Lucky Orange to see where people click, whether they bounce and whether anything looks broken. Check the bounce rate in Google Analytics. Browse your own store as a buyer, add an item to the cart and check every step for problems. [L1085, 2018]
- To review a stalled Alpha campaign for conversions, the store page must be checked first. [L0837, 2025]

### Sudden drops and change history

**Procedures**

Diagnosing sudden drops in Google Ads performance (older advice, 2021; two versions of the same procedure, merged here):
1. Go to your campaign within Google Ads. [L0270, 2021]
2. Click "More" in the left navigation, then "Change History". [L0270, 2021]
3. Review all changes made to that campaign in the date range before performance dropped. [L0270, 2021]
4. Identify which change caused the drop. [L0270, 2021]
5. Revert that change directly through Change History without rebuilding the campaign. [L0270, 2021]

Checking change history after an unexplained traffic jump (2024):
1. Open a campaign and set the date range to start at the change window (for example from April or May until today). [L0856, 2024]
2. Click Change history at the bottom left. [L0856, 2024]
3. Review every change made in that range. [L0856, 2024]
4. Revert changes that should not have been applied. [L0856, 2024]

Checking a sudden traffic drop in Google Ads (2023):
1. Open Google Ads and go to Change history. [L0934, 2023]
2. Choose All campaigns. [L0934, 2023]
3. Set the date range from when traffic was normal until today. [L0934, 2023]
4. Compare the performance graph with the logged changes. [L0934, 2023]
5. If a change matches the drop, click Undo on it. [L0934, 2023]

Troubleshooting a drop in Google Ads conversions (2025):
1. In Google Ads, open the alpha and beta Shopping campaigns. [L1097, 2025]
2. Set the date range in the top right to a period when sales were strong. [L1097, 2025]
3. Note which products received clicks and which products sold. [L1097, 2025]
4. Change the date range to the recent period when traffic is still coming in but sales have dropped. [L1097, 2025]
5. Compare which products received impressions and clicks then versus now. [L1097, 2025]
6. Adjust bids so traffic returns to the product pages that converted before. [L1097, 2025]

Diagnosing a performance drop in an underperforming alpha campaign (older advice, 2021, from the same source as the review procedure below):
1. Review the change history for every modification made during the period before performance dropped. [L0270, 2021]
2. Identify negative changes. [L0270, 2021]
3. Revert problematic changes directly through Change History without rebuilding campaigns. [L0270, 2021]

**Rules and principles**

- Fix the root setting first. When an account changes sharply, a rebuild or advanced optimization is less useful than finding the change that caused it. [L0837, 2025]
- Pause the existing campaigns while investigating a suspected settings problem, so they stop spending on unqualified traffic. (2025.) [L0837, 2025]
- Recreate the Alpha and Beta campaigns step by step, following the Google Shopping setup lesson and checking each setting as you go. Confirm that each setting matches the reference, including location targeting. (2025.) [L0837, 2025]
- If the same pattern of many clicks and little return appears in the first four to five days after launch, do not wait out the full learning period. Look for the root cause instead. (2025.) [L0837, 2025]
- A traffic jump caused by a change: Google's Invalid Click Analysis and third-party click fraud tools differ in accuracy. A monthly invalid click share of about 10 percent after a sudden traffic jump calls for an account-change audit first. Avoid auto-applied recommendations from Google's AI suggestion panel and calls from Google "ad specialists", as they tend to increase spend. (2024.) [L0856, 2024]
- If a campaign's ROAS falls while spend rises, it does not prove the country is maxed out. First fix negative keywords, segment campaigns so best sellers get their own budget, and improve conversion before concluding the budget is saturated. [L0968, 2021]

### Budget not spending and bid levels

**Why the budget does not spend**

- Two budget factors must be evaluated together: the daily budget amount and the cost-per-click bid. A large daily budget with a very low CPC bid spends far less than the budget allows, because available traffic at that price point is limited. [L0243, 2019]
- Example: if the daily budget is $1 million USD but the CPC bid is $0.001, the campaign only spends approximately $100 per day, because sufficient search traffic does not exist at that price point, regardless of budget size. [L0243, 2019]
- A low CPC with a high daily budget will not spend because the bid is below market competition. Increasing CPC lets the full daily budget spend. For example, a $10 daily budget with $0.01 CPC spends $0; raising CPC to $0.15 lets the full $10 spend. [L0249, 2022]
- Cut ad spend for products that are typically backordered for about three weeks or more, and move the budget to products that can ship. [L0803, 2022]

**Procedure for underspending**
1. Verify the daily budget amount is an insufficient reason. [L0243, 2019]
2. Increase the cost-per-click bid incrementally (the "inch up" method). [L0243, 2019]
3. Monitor spend to confirm the budget now depletes. [L0243, 2019]
4. Simultaneously implement negative keywords to improve relevance and CTR of remaining spend. [L0243, 2019]

**Procedure for a budget that is not fully spent**
1. Check whether the campaign is spending its full daily budget. [L1092, 2022]
2. If not, raise the cost per click for the product or campaign in steps, for example from 25 cents to 40 cents, then to 60 cents, until traffic rises. [L1092, 2022]
3. If the full budget is spent but clicks are too few, either increase the daily budget or lower the target cost per click. [L1092, 2022]

**Procedure for a campaign that spends about one cent**
- Raise the default CPC by 5 cents, wait a few days, and check the result. If spend still does not change, raise it another 5 cents and repeat. [L1112, 2021]

**Optimization order for an underperforming Shopping campaign (undated, a $10 daily budget case)**
1. Exclude all products under $200 from campaigns, either with the feed app's product inclusions and exclusions or by setting their max CPC to $0 or $0.01 in the product groups. [L0748, 2020-26]
2. Optimize the product page title and meta tags with better keywords, which is the second priority and takes less urgency than step 1. [L0748, 2020-26]
3. Keep the ads running for two weeks with the optimized products and the cheap products excluded, and do not change the max CPC during this time. [L0748, 2020-26]
4. If the $10 daily budget still does not spend after two weeks, raise max CPC from $0.50 to $0.55 and let it run for one week. [L0748, 2020-26]
5. If the budget still does not spend, raise max CPC to $0.60 and let it run for one week. [L0748, 2020-26]
6. If the budget still does not spend, raise max CPC to $0.65, and keep increasing slowly until the budget spends at a profitable level. [L0748, 2020-26]

- Raising max CPC is a later lever. When the product mix sends budget to low-price items, a higher bid only buys more unprofitable clicks. Fix which products are being advertised before increasing bids. [L0748, 2020-26]
- Raising max CPC early, while cheap products are still in the campaign, spends more money on sales that cannot reach the target ROAS. This is the common error when a campaign seems to underspend and the store owner assumes the bid is the problem. [L0748, 2020-26]
- Case: a store ran Google Shopping campaigns for three months, named alpha and beta, with a $10 daily budget that never spent in full. Results: 3 sales in total, 2 from alpha and 1 from beta. Spend: alpha $152, beta $54, total $206, about $69 per month. Cost per sale was $74 for alpha and $52 for beta. The store owner had stopped raising max CPC at $0.50 and was adding weekly negative keywords. The sold products were priced at $67, $197 and $247, average about $170, far below the $740 needed for alpha's cost per sale and the $520 needed for beta's cost per sale to reach a 10x ROAS. Recommended actions: exclude all products under $200, optimize titles and meta tags second, run for two weeks with cheap products excluded, then raise max CPC gradually from $0.50 to $0.55, $0.60 and $0.65, one week each, until the budget spends. The lesson does not report the final outcome. [L0748, 2020-26]
- Raising the budget alone does not fix low impression share; raise the CPC and optimize the product page. [L0856, 2024]
- Do not change the budget yet while many campaigns are still learning; move spend to the best-performing places. [L0834, 2021]
- Spend stop on a specific supplier: go to the Alpha and Beta campaigns, find all products from that supplier, and set their Cost per click (CPC) to 0 cents. Google then does not spend money on them, so they stop showing. (Undated.) [L1015, 2020-26]

### Irrelevant traffic and search terms

- If unwanted search terms appear in the medium or low priority campaigns, the cause is one of two things: the high priority campaign is not working correctly, or its budget is "Limited by budget". Fix the budget status first, then recheck the setup for negative keywords that were not applied across all campaigns. [L0744, 2021-22]
- When the funnel is set up correctly, the negative keyword strategy is close to foolproof, and failures almost always trace back to setup errors. Recheck the list and the setup before blaming the system. [L0744, 2021-22]
- Check the search terms that trigger your ads and add irrelevant or overly broad ones as negative keywords. [L0357, 2019]
- To optimize Shopping campaigns, open the Google Ads account and find the keywords that triggered impressions or clicks. Then check which product pages those searches landed on. Optimize those product pages first, because they already receive traffic. (2025.) [L0667, 2025]
- Check your ad location settings: if traffic from unwanted countries appears, select only the United States (or only the intended country) in campaign settings to stop paid traffic from there. [L0665, 2022]
- Add broad or irrelevant terms that take most of the spend and bring visitors who do not buy as negative keywords. Find the keywords and products that generate sales, move them into a separate campaign with its own higher budget, and build a layered campaign structure. [L1086, 2019]
- Do not spend on products that are sold below MAP by others. Monitor the Google product listings as well, because they overlap heavily with the ad auction. (2024.) [L0806, 2024]
- A campaign that gets clicks from a non-English search term when you serve only the US market: check Locations and set it to "United States Only" rather than "All territories and countries." [L0251, 2023]

**Negative keyword rules**
- Negative keywords for irrelevant searches: a shopper searching for a brown leather chair when you sell grey fabric sectionals is wasted spend, so add such terms as negative keywords. [L0551, 2020]
- Add non-converting search terms and competitor terms as negative keywords. [L0276, 2024]
- If you see many clicks from non-buying keywords, check the negative keyword list for over-blocking and correct broad match negatives. [L0966, 2025]
