### Over-Blocking Risks

- Double-check the negative keyword list early on, because over-blocking can cut off buyers who search for specific product names. [L0966, 2025]
- Do not add a broad negative that blocks a model, product or buying term. Blocking a broad use-case term can also block buyers who search for the same product type. [L0665, 2022]
- Add a use-case search term as a negative keyword only when it clearly signals no purchase intent, such as a treadmill search for weight loss. Never block model numbers, brand names or clear buying terms. [L0665, 2022]
- Adding a generic keyword as a broad or phrase negative can block the specific product-name searches that convert best. [L0626, 2025+]
- Ingredient and feature terms that describe your selling points must not be negated, even when an AI lists them as irrelevant. Check the negative list against your full product range and selling propositions. [L0876, 2025-26]
- Some terms that look irrelevant can be real top-of-funnel or B2B demand, such as "how to" or recipe queries, or "wholesale" searches when the brand sells wholesale. Put these in a review list rather than negating them automatically. [L0876, 2025-26]
- Never negate terms that could come from real buyers. When uncertain, place the term in a separate "Review before adding" list rather than the main list. [L0876, 2025-26]
- A negative keyword added by mistake will quietly block real buyers. Read every term in an AI-generated list before adding it to the account, and treat AI suggestions as a head start rather than a final list. [L0876, 2025-26]
- Uploading a large list of negative keywords generated by ChatGPT can block searches you want, such as relevant customer queries. Check each proposed negative keyword against your target customers before adding it. [L0471, 2024]
- Negative keywords reduce spend on low-intent searches, but a list built too quickly can limit reach. Build negatives gradually. [L0972, 2025; L0471, 2024]
- Do not block a search term only because it has no clicks yet. Block it when it is not relevant to what you sell, whether or not it is getting clicks. [L0748, 2020-26]
- Do not block a term only because it has no clicks yet, but do not let it wait when it is clearly irrelevant. Waiting before cutting them wastes impressions on unrelated searches. [L0551, 2020]

#### Testing for Over-Blocking

Test whether broad negatives are blocking profitable traffic:

1. Copy all universal negative keywords to a Google Doc. [L0274, 2023]
2. Remove most negatives from the campaign (keep only your core niche keyword). [L0274, 2023]
3. Run the campaign for one week. [L0274, 2023]
4. If results improve, add back only the obviously irrelevant negatives. [L0274, 2023]
5. If results worsen, restore all negatives. [L0274, 2023]

This determines whether negatives are too restrictive. [L0274, 2023]

### Competitor Names

- Do not block competitor names as negative keywords by default. Let search term data show which terms lose money, and add those as negatives. [L0247, 2021]
- Start with exact match on competitor brand names rather than blocking them fully. [L0804, 2023]
- Long competitor-style product titles that trigger the ad can be added as negative keywords, entered as phrase match negatives in quotation marks ("term") so that exact searches are blocked and pushed into the other campaign. one source, Beta campaign. [L0805, 2024]
- If search terms show a competitor that sells direct to consumers and whose products you do not carry, add that brand as a broad negative keyword so you stop appearing for it. [L0837, 2025]
- Competitor brand names belong in the negative list only for prospecting campaigns that should not target competitors. Flag them for review because they depend on the category. [L0876, 2025-26]

### Own Brand and Product Names

- In the beta campaign, add your own brand names, product names and SKU numbers as negative keywords, so that this traffic is pushed to the alpha campaign. Alpha is meant to show for those brand, product and SKU terms, so never add them as negatives there. [L0750, 2020-26]
- Add branded product names and specific SKUs as negative phrase or exact match keywords in your beta campaign so they automatically funnel to your alpha campaign. For example, if "Lifestyles" is your brand, add negative phrase "lifestyles" to beta to block all related searches. [L0225, 2015+]
- For Beta campaigns, add brand names you sell, product names you sell, and SKU numbers as negative keywords to prevent your ads from appearing for your own products. [L0193, undated]
- Beta campaign negative list: add all brand names you are authorized to sell (e.g. the five authorized brands), all product names from the store, and all SKU numbers, entered as broad match. Searches for these terms are blocked in Beta and handled by Alpha. Update the list over time as new brands are added. [L0743, 2026+]
- Brand names for the Beta list are entered in the Brand Names list, using just the brand name, one per line. [L1074, 2021-22]
- Block brand names in the beta campaign, vague short-tail keywords and universal keywords; set up match types (broad, phrase, exact) later based on data. [L0969, 2022]

Sources disagree on own-brand negatives in Alpha:

| Source | Advice | Applies when |
|---|---|---|
| one source | Add your brand name as a phrase-match negative in the alpha campaign, together with closely related but different product variants. [L0961, 2020] | Read as the older view; not the current rule. |
| one source | Never add brand, product or SKU terms as negatives in alpha, because alpha has to be able to show for them. [L0750, 2020-26; L0746, 2020-26] | Current rule for Alpha. |

Own-brand negatives in a campaign that carries the brand:

- In a campaign with a brand term, do not add your own brand to the negative keyword list if you carry that brand. Negative keywords should block only terms for product types you do not sell, such as commercial, which can be added as a universal negative. [L0429, 2022]
- Use this rule for the brand campaign; the Beta rules above apply to the Beta structure. [L0429, 2022; L0743, 2026+]

### Suppliers and Other Brands

- Do not add search terms for suppliers you don't carry as negative keywords from day one. Add them only after search term reports show consistent spend without sales, using exact match, e.g. a supplier's brand name only. [L0854, 2022]
- If a search term shows another brand you do not carry, add it as a universal negative keyword. [L0276, 2024]
- Universal negative keywords are terms for other brands you do not sell. [L0750, 2020-26]

### Building Negatives from the Search Terms Report

#### Where to Find the Report

- To find search terms triggering your ads in Google Ads: navigate to Campaigns → Select your campaign → Insights & Reports → Search Terms. [L0193, undated]
- The Search terms report is found inside a campaign under Insights and reports > Search terms. It shows the actual phrases people typed that triggered the ads, with metrics for each. Use Add filter on the Search term column to narrow the list. [L0876, 2025-26]
- To see which searches triggered your ads, open the campaign in Google Ads, expand "Insights and reports", and select the "Search terms" tab. Review each term for relevance and add irrelevant ones as negative keywords. [L0837, 2025]
- To see which search terms triggered Google Shopping ads: open the Shopping campaign, click Insights and reports in the left navigation and then click Search terms. It shows the terms you appeared for and what you spent on each. [L0857, 2025]
- To see which searches triggered your Google Ads, open the campaign in Google Ads and click Keywords. Review the listed search terms and mark the irrelevant or overly broad ones as negative keywords. [L0551, 2020]
- To see which search terms converted: open Google Ads, select the campaign, then click Keywords or Search terms in the left sidebar. Choose the date range; the view shows the terms that triggered ads and the conversions they produced. [L0963, 2022]
- To see which search terms trigger a Beta campaign: open Google Ads, go to the Beta campaign, click Keywords, and review the triggering search terms. [L0805, 2024]
- Do not use the Keywords section for this. It only shows negative keywords already added, not the search terms the ads actually appeared for. Use the Search terms report under Insights and reports. [L0750, 2020-26]
- Find which search terms a Shopping product got impressions for: 1) Open Google Ads. 2) Go to your alpha and beta campaigns. 3) Open the keywords view and click the search terms your product appeared for. 4) Look for keywords with many impressions that go to that product. [L0806, 2024]

#### Reading the Report

- The Search terms view shows the columns Search term, Match type, Added/Excluded, Ad group, Clicks, Impr., CTR, Avg. CPC, Cost and Conversions. Use these to spot irrelevant terms, low click-through terms and clicks without sales. [L0744, 2021-22]
- The Search Terms view can list thousands of terms over 30 days, for example 6,971 terms in an established account. The view can show up to 500 rows per page, and a single ad group view can show 1 to 500 of 2,673 terms. [L0744, 2021-22]
- The Google Ads search terms view paginates its rows, so only a fraction of a large report is visible on screen at once. Export the complete search terms report to a file before analysis instead of reading the on-screen list. [L0876, 2025-26]
- Use the search terms report within each ad group to view exact searches triggered by your ads. Add irrelevant searches as negative keywords at the ad group level to prevent wasted spend. [L0262, 2021-22]
- Shopping campaign search terms: Open the campaign, then the ad group. Look at Keywords and then Search terms. Expand the date range if nothing shows. If a product group reads not set, click it and expand it. [L0247, 2021]
- Search terms optimization in Shopping campaigns: sort by clicks to see where most traffic came from. Check that branded terms show up in the campaign that should contain them, since branded terms normally convert very well and should not run in the beta campaign. [L0745, 2020-26]
- Compare reported impressions with the impressions the ad group actually received. The difference (example: 1,500 shown vs 3,800 received, about 2,000 hidden) is the search traffic Google does not report. [L0745, 2020-26]
- Compare the compare-period columns (Clicks, Impr., CTR, Conversions, Conv. rate, Cost / conv., Cost) against the previous period to spot new waste. [L0876, 2025-26]
- Use a negative keyword list built from the search terms report. Add a term as a negative only after checking whether it produced clicks or conversions. [L0963, 2022]
- Build negative keyword lists from the search terms report: add terms that get impressions or clicks but make no sense for your product, and terms that spend money without producing sales. [L0857, 2025]
- Negative keywords come from the search terms report: any term that triggers your ads but does not match what you sell should be added as a negative. Example: if you sell only glass dining tables, the word wood should be a negative keyword when it triggers searches. [L0855, 2023]
- Negative keywords come in three match types: exact, phrase and broad. Every irrelevant search term found in the Search terms report becomes a negative, so the report determines what to cut. [L0876, 2025-26]
- Negative keyword decisions require judgement, not a fixed rule. Combine click-through rate, relevance and conversions. In a profitable account exclude only a few terms, while in an unprofitable account cut more aggressively. [L0744, 2021-22]
- A search term with 13,000 impressions, 19 clicks, a CTR of 0.14% and no tracked conversions is a clear negative keyword candidate. Either the term is not relevant to the product, or shoppers are not looking for it. [L0744, 2021-22]
- When low CTR is the problem, exclude search terms with a CTR below 0.5%, unless conversion data shows that these terms still produce sales. [L0879, 2021-22]
- About three months of search term data is usually enough for a useful search term report. Use a shorter or longer period depending on how much data the account has. [L0880, 2021-22]

#### Weekly Review Procedure

1. Open the high priority campaign, click into it to see its ad groups, then open Keywords and choose Search Terms. [L0744, 2021-22]
2. Set the date range from the day after your last review to today, for example 2 November to 7 November, and apply it. [L0744, 2021-22]
3. Work through the list from top to bottom, marking search terms the business does not sell, clearly irrelevant terms, terms with very low click-through rate, and terms with clicks but no conversions. [L0744, 2021-22]
4. Select the chosen terms, choose Add as negative keyword, select the master generic negative keyword list and save. [L0744, 2021-22]
5. Before going to the next page, save the selected negatives, because selections are not kept when you change pages. [L0744, 2021-22]
6. Repeat for each page of up to 500 rows until the end of the list. [L0744, 2021-22]
7. Note the review date so the next date range starts the day after it. [L0744, 2021-22]

Remove search terms for products or services the business does not sell, such as outdoor saunas or Roman tubs. Plural forms and variants of the same term may need their own negative entries. [L0744, 2021-22]

Remove searches for used, rental or repair items when you sell only new products: if you sell only new products, searches containing words such as used, second-hand, rental or hire do not match your offer, so add them as negative keywords. Image-only or repair-related searches for products you sell are also poor matches and should be excluded when they waste clicks. [L0739, 2021-22]

Broad category searches often come from people who want product images, repair or servicing of a product they already own, used or secondhand items, or rental for an event, and a retailer of new goods cannot sell to any of these. Clicks from such terms cost money without sales, so limit them with negative keywords or bid less. [L0741, 2021-22]

Example: if a search term shows an irrelevant category, such as a chandelier store that shows for tanning beds, block it in both campaigns. [L0746, 2020-26]

#### Search Term Routine by Campaign Type

Shopping cleanup for a campaign with no sales:

1. Export the search term report and review terms that received impressions, not only terms that got clicks. [L0807, 2025]
2. Mark irrelevant or researching terms as negatives. [L0807, 2025]
3. Add negatives at campaign level or as universal negatives, using Merchant Center for universal ones. [L0807, 2025]
4. Leave bids and budgets unchanged while the new negatives take effect. [L0807, 2025]
5. Set up Meta remarketing shortly after the negatives are in place. [L0807, 2025]

Finding negative candidates in the alpha campaign:

1. Open the Google Ads account. [L0970, 2023]
2. Click into the alpha campaign. [L0970, 2023]
3. Open the Keywords view. [L0970, 2023]
4. Review the keywords that received impressions, the clicks each one produced, and the sales that came from them. [L0970, 2023]
5. Add the irrelevant or vague terms as negative keywords. [L0970, 2023]

Reviewing and refining negatives in the alpha and beta Shopping campaigns:

1. Open the alpha and beta Shopping campaigns. [L0972, 2025]
2. Review the search terms that triggered clicks. [L0972, 2025]
3. Check whether each search term is relevant to buying your products. [L0972, 2025]
4. Add irrelevant search terms as negative keywords, and keep the list growing over time. [L0972, 2025]

Daily routine for Google Shopping campaigns:

1. Each morning or evening, open the search terms report for the day. [L0357, 2019]
2. Mark search terms with no buying intent, such as generic terms, video searches or unrelated topics (for example standup paddle board yoga), as negative keywords. [L0357, 2019]
3. Keep brand names, product names and SKU numbers, because these show purchase intent. [L0357, 2019]
4. Compare impressions with clicks for each search term and investigate keywords with many impressions but few clicks. [L0357, 2019]

Steps to find negative keywords:

1. Let the ads run for a while. [L0961, 2020]
2. Review the search terms that trigger your ads. [L0961, 2020]
3. Mark the terms that are irrelevant to your products, for example free, retail or a related but different product type. [L0961, 2020]
4. Add those terms as negative keywords with the right match type. [L0961, 2020]

Daily checks for irrelevant keywords:

1. Check Google Shopping ads daily for impressions on irrelevant keywords. [L0251, 2023]
2. Screenshot or note keywords that do not match your product category or brand. [L0251, 2023]
3. Add these as negative keywords in your campaign settings before they generate clicks and cost. [L0251, 2023]
4. Repeat this process every day to prevent budget waste on unqualified traffic. [L0251, 2023]

Search terms optimization in Shopping campaigns: use the same five-step routine above, then add terms you never want to appear for to the universal negative list. [L0745, 2020-26]

Example of a search term to block in a chandelier store: a term that shows the ads for tanning beds, blocked in both campaigns. [L0746, 2020-26]

#### Find the Right Scope Before Changing Anything Else

- If Google Shopping spend goes to search terms without buying intent, fix keyword targeting and add negative keywords before changing the niche or widening the product range. Buying intent means the searcher is ready to purchase a specific product now. [L0665, 2022]
- If search impression share for broad category keywords stays low in a beta campaign set up for brand plus SKU searches, raise the default cost per click so those keywords get impressions. Add the brand name as a negative keyword to keep category searches out. [L0470, 2023]
- When a broad niche keyword takes most of the Google Shopping budget, add that keyword as an exact match negative keyword so spend shifts toward more purchase-ready searches. [L0857, 2025]
- Negative keywords reduce wasted spend, lift click-through rate and bring more relevant impressions. If search impression share is low, the ads are often showing for irrelevant search terms. [L0806, 2024]
- If visitors click but do not convert, check which keywords generate those clicks and add negatives for irrelevant searches, because the buying intent of the traffic drives conversion rate. Negative keywords reduce spend on low-intent searches and raise the share of buyers in the traffic. [L0471, 2024]
- Adding a negative keyword as a fix for a product that is shown too often: add negative keywords or lower its CPC at the product level. [L0803, 2022]
