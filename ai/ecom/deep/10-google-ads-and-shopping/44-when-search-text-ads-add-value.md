### When Search Text Ads Add Value

- A search campaign can bring sales before the Shopping side is ready. A new store with search text ads made its first sale 45 minutes after the first ad went live, while the product feed was still pending [L1437, 2018].
- For a new store, running a search campaign alongside Shopping builds early visibility, because search does not depend on the store's existing footprint. Google then also starts recognizing the store on the Shopping side [L1508, 2022+].
- Search text ads built on product listing ad (PLA) terms show below or beside the product listing ads, which lets the store take up more of the results page. In one example, text and PLA ads together filled most of the page and no organic results were visible [L1203, 2016].
- To launch search text ads on profitable terms from PLA data:
  1. Open the search terms report of your product listing ads to see which terms trigger them.
  2. Pick the best-performing terms.
  3. Create a search text campaign built around those terms.

  No extensive keyword research is needed because the best keywords already appear in the PLA search terms [L1203, 2016].
- Stacking means bidding on the same keywords with text ads that already win in Shopping, so the text ads sit on top of the product listing ads for those keywords:
  1. Identify the keywords that already convert for you through Google Shopping campaigns.
  2. Bid on the exact same keywords with Google Search text ads.
  3. Expect the combined presence to take up more of the search results page, which generally brings more traffic for those proven terms [L1350, 2020-26].
- For choosing keywords, Google has already classified some terms as having commercial intent. Bidding on these lets you take up more space on the search results page [L1350, 2020-26].
- A search ad for a keyword that already wins in Google Shopping should use the same exact keywords, so the text ad and the product listing appear together. Do not add keywords that have not yet converted in Shopping just because they look similar [L1350, 2020-26].
- **** Run product search text ads only for your top roughly 1 percent of products that sell repeatedly and already max out your Google Shopping budget. Avoid generic keywords for these campaigns [L1275, 2019].

### Why Relevance Matters More Than in the Past

- Many e-commerce stores fail when they add Google Search text ads to an existing Shopping setup because they run them with the mindset of the early 2000s, when search clicks were cheap and competition was low. Search clicks today are expensive, so the same approach wastes budget [L1350, 2020-26].
- Around the year 2000, nearly all Google search clicks cost less than 50 cents, which let advertisers tolerate very low conversion rates on loosely related queries. This no longer holds, so search campaigns need tight targeting [L1350, 2020-26].
- When clicks are cheap, a broad or semi-relevant keyword can still pay off with a few conversions. When clicks are expensive, irrelevant keywords drain the budget quickly. Keyword relevance therefore matters far more for search text ads today than in the past [L1350, 2020-26].
- A loosely related keyword can bring around 700 clicks and only one or two sales. When clicks cost pennies, that return looked acceptable, but at today's click prices the same pattern drains the budget quickly. Check the search terms report for such queries and exclude them [L1350, 2020-26].

### Keyword Sources from Shopping Data

- A keyword is the term you bid on. A search query (also called search term) is the text a shopper actually typed and that triggered your ad. The two are often confused, but they are the same thing in reports only when the match is exact [L1352, 2020-26].
- To find converting search terms, open the campaign, go to Keywords and then Search terms, add the Conversions column, and download the report. Then add the product column in the downloaded data and filter for search terms with more than one conversion [L1352, 2020-26].
- Google Ads does not show the product and the search term that triggered it together in the standard interface. This makes it hard to know which product a converting query belongs to. Workarounds exist, such as a separate ad group per product, but they are time-consuming [L1352, 2020-26].

### Building a First Search Campaign from Converting Shopping Terms

1. Open the Shopping campaign, go to Keywords > Search terms and add the Conversions column.
2. Download the search terms report and add the product column to see which product received the conversions.
3. Filter the data to search terms with more than one conversion.
4. Confirm that all conversions point to the same product and note its URL as the landing page.
5. In Google Ads, start a new campaign and choose Search as the campaign type.
6. Select Website visits as the goal and enter the website URL.
7. Enter a descriptive campaign name.
8. Set the location to the United States and exclude Hawaii, Alaska and Puerto Rico.
9. Set the location presence option to target people in the targeted locations and exclude people in the excluded locations.
10. Set the language to English and choose the daily budget.
11. Keep delivery method on Standard.
12. Set bidding to manual and keep Enhanced CPC off.
13. Skip dynamic search ads, audiences, sitelinks and call extensions for now.
14. Save and continue.
15. Create the ad group with a descriptive name that states the theme and source of the terms.
16. Set the default bid to about $2 CPC, taken from the average CPC of the Shopping campaign.
17. Enter the converting terms as keywords, typing exact match keywords inside square brackets.
18. Write the ad: final URL, headline and display path. Skip tracking templates unless a third-party tool needs them, and add a mobile URL only if one is used.
19. Save, continue to the campaign and launch it.
20. In the campaign, change every keyword from broad match to exact match in one step using Change match types [L1352, 2020-26].

The purpose of this campaign is that it competes on the same results page as the winning Shopping ads and sends traffic to the same product URL that already converted. Because the terms already converted in Shopping, they have a high chance of converting in Search as well [L1352, 2020-26].

### Campaign Settings and Starting Bids

- Settings for a new Search campaign:
  - Campaign type: Search network only
  - Goal: Website visits
  - Location: United States, with Hawaii, Alaska and Puerto Rico excluded
  - Location option: target the chosen locations while excluding the excluded ones
  - Language: English
  - Delivery: Standard
  - Bidding: manual, Enhanced CPC off [L1352, 2020-26]
- The procedure names Search as the campaign type [L1352, 2020-26], while the settings list narrows it to Search network only [L1352, 2020-26]. Use the settings list as the stated setting.
- Bidding should be set manually at the start, with Enhanced CPC switched off, so the seller controls every bid while the campaign collects data. Dynamic search ads, audiences, sitelinks and call extensions can be added later once the basic campaign performs [L1352, 2020-26].
- Use the average CPC from the Shopping campaign for the same product as the starting Search bid, which in the example was about $2 CPC. Adjust it later based on the search terms data [L1352, 2020-26].
- When creating a new Search campaign, Google Ads asks whether the campaign should focus on conversions or on conversion value. Choose the option that fits how the campaign will be measured, and keep Enhanced CPC off while the campaign is still being started [L1352, 2020-26].
- A descriptive campaign name can combine the product, the channel and the target return on ad spend, for example "infrared sauna search text 6x return on ad spend". Naming it this way makes the goal visible in every report [L1352, 2020-26].

### Keyword Research and Checks

- Ahrefs and SEMrush are keyword research tools used to measure monthly search volume for categories. Both offer free trials that can be used for a basic keyword check [L1389, 2025-26].
- Relevance check before bidding on a keyword:
  1. Type the target keyword into Google in a private window and note which results show ads.
  2. Read the top ads and check whether the ad text mentions the specific use case or the informational need in the query.
  3. Compare the ads with the organic results below them.
  4. If the organic results match the query more closely than the ads, the ad is not relevant enough. Rewrite the ad and the landing page so they address the query directly, or drop the keyword.
  5. Repeat the check for each keyword before you start bidding on it [L1350, 2020-26].

### Match Types

| Match type | Notation | What it matches |
|---|---|---|
| Exact match | [keyword] in square brackets | Only the exact term and very close variants (plurals, misspellings, same meaning in word order) |
| Phrase match | "keyword" in quotation marks | Searches that contain the keyword phrase in that order, with other words possibly before or after it |
| Modified broad match | +word1 +word2 with a plus sign before each word | Close variants of each marked word individually (e.g. +red also matches reddish), but not unrelated words such as pink for +red |
| Broad match | keyword without brackets, quotes or plus signs | Synonyms, related searches, word stems and many unrelated but loosely connected queries |

[L1352, 2020-26]

- Broad match can show ads for a very wide range of queries, so the same keyword can trigger on surprising searches such as a different quantity or a different product type. Exact match is the most controlled option, phrase match is in between, and modified broad gives more control than broad because each word only expands to its own close variants [L1352, 2020-26].
- Roughly 25 percent of Google searches each month are completely new and cannot be predicted by a seller. Match types exist so that the ad can still appear for these unknown queries [L1352, 2020-26].
- Phrase match on the keyword "jump rope" lets a Search ad appear for long-tail searches such as a jump rope that survives hot summer sun, which a seller could never list in advance. This is why phrase match captures traffic that exact match misses [L1352, 2020-26].
- Broad match on "car" can trigger on synonyms and related searches such as automobile, Ferrari paint color, truck tires and skyline roadster. This shows how far broad match can drift from the seller's product [L1352, 2020-26].
- Modified broad match with +hike can also match its word stem hiking, and +CA can match the abbreviation California. Each marked word expands only to its own close variants, which keeps the reach narrower than broad match [L1352, 2020-26].
- Keywords entered without brackets, quotes or plus signs are broad match, so a new Search ad group can start with broad keywords even when the intent was exact. Check every match type before the campaign goes live and convert the keywords to exact match in one step if needed [L1352, 2020-26].

### Launch Keyword Mix and Bidding by Match Type

- Start a new Search campaign with exact match keywords only, using terms that already converted in Shopping. Add phrase and modified broad keywords later at lower bids, and add broad match last at the lowest bids [L1352, 2020-26].
- Launching a new Search campaign with broad match keywords lets the ads appear for many irrelevant queries, which wastes budget before any data is available. Use only proven converting terms at launch [L1352, 2020-26].
- A long, specific term that already converted in Shopping, such as "infrared sauna for three people", should become the first exact match keyword in a new Search campaign. If it were broad match, the same ad could show for unrelated queries such as "infrared sauna for 20 people" [L1352, 2020-26].
- Bid by match type in this order:
  - Most for exact match
  - Somewhat less for phrase match
  - Less again for modified broad match
  - Much less for broad match

  This keeps the budget on the most reliable queries [L1352, 2020-26].
- Long term, the keyword mix of a Search campaign should be mostly exact match, with some phrase, some modified broad and only a small share of broad match. This is a long-term target and not the setup for a new launch [L1352, 2020-26].

### Ad Group Structure

- Keep an ad group to about 10 keywords maximum. With more keywords the ad group mixes too many themes, and the ad can no longer match what a searcher is looking for, which lowers relevance [L1352, 2020-26].
- An ad group that mixes several themes, such as cabinet doors with other product types, forces the ad copy to be generic. A generic ad does not speak to a specific query, which lowers relevance and clicks [L1352, 2020-26].

### Product-Specific Campaigns

- Build each product's search campaign around that product's own converting terms and its single landing URL, so every conversion points to the product that earned it [L1352, 2020-26].
- A separate ad group per product is the workaround for seeing which product a converting search term belongs to, although it takes more time [L1352, 2020-26].
- **** Product search text ads are for the top roughly 1 percent of products that already max out the Shopping budget [L1275, 2019].

### Search Intent and Funnel Stage

- Meet the searcher at their stage in the funnel, whether they are early in research or ready to buy. Each keyword's landing page should reflect that stage [L1350, 2020-26].
- Match the landing page to the search intent. A top-of-funnel informational query should lead to an informational page, while a bottom-of-funnel commercial query can lead to a product page. Never send top-of-funnel traffic to a bottom-of-funnel landing page [L1350, 2020-26].
- Sending people who search for information, such as how-to or comparison questions, straight to a product page is a costly mistake because they are not ready to buy. Running traffic this way is essentially paying for awareness and rarely converts [L1350, 2020-26].
- Classify search keywords by commercial intent to decide which funnel stage each keyword belongs to [L1386, 2020-26]:

| Group | Examples from the source |
|---|---|
| Commercial | "buy product", "product free shipping", product-type terms |
| Midway | "product comparison", "product A versus B" |
| Non-commercial | Keywords that describe a problem or a question |

- The sources place comparisons in the midway group [L1386, 2020-26], while the list of information-seeking queries that are not ready to buy includes comparison questions [L1350, 2020-26]. Treat comparison keywords as midway and do not send them straight to a product page [L1386, 2020-26; L1350, 2020-26].
- Build your ad messages from the searcher's exact question or problem, and match the landing page to that stage, because a sale depends on the ad, the landing page and the user's readiness working together [L1386, 2020-26].

### Informational and Lead-Generation Searches

- Organic results for informational searches often outperform paid ads. Turning a strong informational organic listing into an ad would likely bring even more traffic. Paid informational ads should therefore point to genuinely helpful content, not a sales page [L1350, 2020-26].
- For an informational query such as asking what makes a good trail running shoe, ads that push free shipping, free returns, or a shop-now message miss the searcher's intent. The searcher wants information, so a sales-driven ad gets fewer clicks than an informational result [L1350, 2020-26].
- An informational landing page lets you capture a lead through an email opt-in, set a tracking cookie, and retarget the visitor later. These benefits make top-of-funnel traffic valuable even when the visitor does not buy immediately [L1350, 2020-26].
- Keywords that do not fit the simple top, middle and bottom split can still be handled:
  - For someone who knows the problem but not the solution, such as "manage business finances", offer a free software trial as a lead generation step and sell the paid sign-up later [L1386, 2020-26].
  - For a problem such as "improving focus at work", offer a low-commitment product such as a supplement with the first month free [L1386, 2020-26].

### Ad Copy Relevance

- Ads for a specific use case, such as running shoes for hills, fail when neither the ad text nor the landing page mentions that use case. Without this relevance, the ad performs worse than the organic results for the same query [L1350, 2020-26].
- Showing ads for search queries that differ from what the buyer intended pushes the click-through rate very low. Keep keyword targeting and ad text tightly aligned with the actual search intent [L1401, 2020-26].
- The display URL path is free text that can contain a sale word or the target keyword, which signals to the searcher where they will land. It does not have to be the real page address [L1401, 2020-26].
- Putting the price in the ad copy pre-qualifies buyers and stops people who cannot afford the product from clicking. This may lower CTR, but it improves the cost-to-revenue ratio, which matters more than CTR. Shopping campaigns use the same logic by showing prices [L1401, 2020-26].
- Adding buying-intent keywords (such as "buy" or "price") keeps non-buyers from clicking at all. Limiting unlikely buyers before the click usually saves more money than raising CTR [L1401, 2020-26].
- **** The copywriting principles for search text ads apply equally to Bing or any other search engine where the ad is shown. Google is the platform noted for automatically bolding the searched keyword in the ad [L1351, 2021-22].

### Ad Copy Testing

- Routine ad copy testing:
  1. Run at least two ads at the same time.
  2. Track which headlines, descriptions and calls to action get clicks and which get conversions.
  3. Keep written notes on what works and what does not, so new tests build on past results.
  4. Compare CTR and conversion rate together before deciding a winner [L1401, 2020-26].
- Ad test steps for finding the strongest versions:
  1. Split test your ads to find the versions with the highest click-through rate.
  2. Use emotion in the ad copy to make people want to click.
  3. Give a clear call to action.
  4. Give people a good reason to click on the ad [L1226, 2020-26].
- Optimize Google text ads for click-through rate, but always check conversion rate alongside it. Run at least two ads in the same ad group at the same time so results can be compared [L1401, 2020-26].
- CTR and cost-to-revenue can pull in different directions. Price in the copy may lower CTR, and the cost-to-revenue ratio then takes priority [L1401, 2020-26]. Where that trade-off applies, judge the ad by revenue per cost, not by CTR alone [L1401, 2020-26].
- **** Saving changes to a live ad creates a new version that is sent back for review. Metrics from earlier versions remain visible, so you can compare the old and new ad versions [L1391, 2021-22].
