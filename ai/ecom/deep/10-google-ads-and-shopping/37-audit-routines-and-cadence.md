### Audit Routines and Cadence

#### Cadence Reference

| Phase or account type | Cadence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| General account audit | Every two weeks against KPIs; change only if data is within plan or clearly broken, for example traffic falling from 100 to 20 daily visitors. ** | [L0249, 2022] |
| Ongoing, indefinitely | Every one to two weeks, a few hours a week for an established account. ** | [L0834, 2021] |
| Ongoing, Shopping and Google Ads | Every two weeks; each analysis takes 15 to 30 minutes. Make significant bid and budget changes on this cycle. ** | [L0470, 2023] |
| Established store with long history | Every two weeks. | [L0715, 2020-26] |
| New store, first weeks | Weekly on a fixed weekday and time. | [L0715, 2020-26] |
| New store, review cycle | Every two weeks, editing campaigns based on results of the previous two weeks. [L0471, 2024] | [L0471, 2024] |
| Early days | Daily audit in the first week and daily until the right budget and keyword set is found. ** | [L1002, 2019] |
| Several times a week | For larger budgets, roughly every two weeks with smaller budgets, or several times a week with larger budgets. | [L0805, 2024] |

- Audit Google Ads accounts every two weeks to review performance against KPIs. ** [L0249, 2022]
- After a Google Ads account is running, audit it about every two weeks to review keywords, click-through rate and spend. In the first weeks, audit daily until you find the right budget and keyword set. ** [L1002, 2019]
- Review Google Ads at least every two weeks, check what is working, and shift budget to profitable ads while cutting losers. Market conditions change, so this self-audit is needed regularly. ** [L0467, 2019]
- Audit Google Ads and Facebook Ads accounts every two weeks once the campaigns are stable. Check whether the numbers still look right, and if not, find the cause and fix it with negative keywords or budget changes. ** [L0551, 2020]
- Google Ads cannot be left on autopilot, even for long-running accounts, so review and adjust campaigns about every couple of weeks. Weekly audits should check spend profitability and shift budget toward products that earn well. ** [L0961, 2020]
- Google Ads should be checked and optimised every one to two weeks indefinitely. A few hours a week is enough for an established account. ** [L0834, 2021]
- Audit Google Ads about every two weeks and make significant bid and budget changes on that cycle, so Google has time to learn. Spend 15 to 30 minutes on each analysis, and make smaller changes only when there is enough data. ** [L0470, 2023]
- Make campaign changes based on the data that exists, not on a fixed schedule. ** [L0470, 2023]
- For a new store, review the ad account every two weeks and edit campaigns based on the results of the previous two weeks. ** [L0471, 2024]
- Review Google Ads reports every week or every other week and track whether impressions, clicks and conversions are trending up, down or flat. ** [L0968, 2021]
- Google Ads is low maintenance once set up correctly. Optimisation can happen every 7 to 15 days, or the account can run with minimal intervention. [L0296, 2021+]
- The purpose of the weekly audit is to improve return on ad spend by cutting weak spots and putting more budget behind what already brings results, which is how ads are scaled profitably. [L0715, 2020-26]
- Keep the same weekly audit schedule at the start. For established stores with a long history, audit the account every two weeks instead. [L0715, 2020-26]
- The first full audit of a new Google Ads account should happen about one week after the first data comes in, then on the same weekday and at the same time every week. Setting a recurring alarm helps keep this routine. [L0715, 2020-26]
- Regular optimisation shows which keywords perform and lets you remove weak ones faster, which raises both results and efficiency in Google Ads. The gains compound the longer the account is actively managed. [L1078, 2020-26]

#### Weekly Routine for a New Store

Procedure: weekly Google Ads optimisation routine for a new store. [L0715, 2020-26]

1. Set a fixed weekday and time for the account review, for example with a recurring alarm. [L0715, 2020-26]
2. Audit both the alpha and the beta campaign and note what is working and what is not. [L0715, 2020-26]
3. Add new negative keywords to both alpha and beta. [L0715, 2020-26]
4. Adjust bids for products that get too few impressions or clicks. [L0715, 2020-26]
5. Once the account has 50 or more sales, switch the campaigns to Enhanced CPC. [L0715, 2020-26]
6. When one brand clearly wins, move it into its own alpha campaign. [L0715, 2020-26]
7. Exclude products or brands that are losing money or disappointing customers from all campaigns. [L0715, 2020-26]
8. Products that get clicks but no sales go to conversion optimisation of the store. [L0715, 2020-26]

#### Daily Audit in the First Week

Procedure: daily Google Ads audit in the first week. ** [L1002, 2019]

1. Day one: check where your ads appear and which search terms triggered them, then add irrelevant terms as negative keywords. [L1002, 2019]
2. From day two: check click-through rate by comparing clicks with impressions. An impression is one view of your ad. [L1002, 2019]
3. If click-through rate is low, compare your prices with competitors' Google Shopping listings and check whether they honour MAP. [L1002, 2019]
4. Compare your product images with competitors' images and improve yours. [L1002, 2019]
5. Repeat the audit every day, aiming to have the main optimisations done by about day four or five. [L1002, 2019]
6. Around halfway, expect visitors to add items to the cart and to use live chat or send inquiries. By the end, aim for the first sale. [L1002, 2019]

- Do not wait for perfect data before optimising, especially if the store must be profitable soon. Even a small budget like $20 per day gives enough information to start. ** [L1002, 2019]

#### Early Shopping Campaign Optimisation

Procedure: early Google Shopping campaign optimisation in the first days and week. ** [L0801, 2021]

1. Check which keywords produce impressions and which produce clicks in the Google Ads account. [L0801, 2021]
2. Identify search terms and products you do not want to appear for. [L0801, 2021]
3. Add negative keywords for unwanted searches in the Shopping campaigns. [L0801, 2021]
4. Adjust cost per click by product, lowering it for products that get impressions but that you do not need to sell. [L0801, 2021]
5. Let the data accumulate before making more changes. [L0801, 2021]

- Once the account is running, a new Shopping campaign should be judged with a few hundred clicks, not a few. The early phase is about launching, collecting data and optimising from it, not about judging profitability. [L0856, 2024]
- Shopping campaigns should be checked for impressions and clicks early, so that drastic changes are avoided and small adjustments can be made. [L0971, 2024]

#### Spend Audit

Procedure: to audit ad spend. ** [L0803, 2022]

1. Look at where money goes and identify spend that is not producing sales. [L0803, 2022]
2. For products or keywords that get consistent spend, first optimise the product pages so more visitors buy. [L0803, 2022]
3. If the pages are already optimised and visitors still do not buy, cut the ad spend sooner rather than later. [L0803, 2022]
4. Move the freed budget into the products that sell. [L0803, 2022]

- Regularly review search terms, product-level performance and bids, pausing or reducing spend on products and queries that do not produce profitable sales. ** [L0430, 2023]

#### Keyword Review in Alpha and Beta Campaigns

Procedure: to analyse Google Ads keyword data in alpha and beta campaigns. ** [L0803, 2022]

1. Open the campaign and select Keywords. [L0803, 2022]
2. Review impressions, clicks and the search terms that triggered each ad. [L0803, 2022]
3. Add negative keywords for terms that will not lead to sales or are too broad. [L0803, 2022]
4. Optimise the product pages that get clicks, to convert those visitors. [L0803, 2022]

#### Ongoing Optimisation Routine

Procedure: optimisation routine for Google Ads data. [L1078, 2020-26]

1. Review the performance data and ask how deeply it can be broken down (by keyword, placement, audience, time, device or other segments). [L1078, 2020-26]
2. Stop or cut what is not working, such as keywords, placements or ads that spend without producing results. [L1078, 2020-26]
3. Improve and expand what is working, such as scaling budgets or adding similar keywords and audiences. [L1078, 2020-26]
4. Repeat this review continuously. [L1078, 2020-26]

#### High-Ticket Management

- For high-ticket dropshipping Shopping campaigns, only two recurring management tasks are needed: search term control through negative keywords, and bid and budget adjustment. ** [L0744, 2021-22]
- Shopping campaigns start producing sales once switched on, but ongoing management is where profitability improves. Each optimisation round tends to lower ad spend per sale and raise profit. ** [L0744, 2021-22]
- Optimising a store with about 60 active products after a month is a sound basis for making decisions. [L0666, 2024]
- Shopping campaign benchmark for performance checks: the advertiser's threshold for investigating a problem is a return on ad spend below 10x. Check click-through rate, cost per click, clicks and conversion rate in Google Ads first. Only then look at Google Shopping for MAP violations. Do this roughly every two weeks with smaller budgets, or several times a week with larger budgets. ** [L0805, 2024]

#### Holiday Campaign Preparation

Procedure: to prepare holiday Google campaigns. ** [L1113, 2022]

1. Schedule each promotion campaign weeks in advance with a start date and an end date. [L1113, 2022]
2. Leave at least a couple of days of buffer so you can make edits if a campaign is not approved. [L1113, 2022]

### Change History

- Google Ads Change History shows all changes made to accounts with timestamps. Use it to understand what changed and when, and to roll back problematic changes if needed. ** [L0249, 2022]

### Benchmarks

#### Benchmark Principles

- There is no reliable average click-through rate or click share benchmark for Google Ads, because results depend on the spend and how the campaigns are segmented. Judge performance by profit per campaign. ** [L0356, 2019]
- No universal impression or click benchmark exists, because results depend on the product, the brand and the niche. Judge performance against your own products and goals. ** [L0469, 2021]
- Judge a new Google Ads account over a sample of hundreds to thousands of visits per week, not over a few clicks. [L1088, 2020]

#### Click-Through Rate

Sources give different values. Each value applies to the context stated.

| Value | Context | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2% or lower on relevant keywords | Signals a problem. ** | [L0243, 2019] |
| About 5% target, 1% minimum | Below 1%, look for negative keywords to add. ** | [L0551, 2020] |
| Just under 1% | Average Google Ads CTR. Below 1% needs optimisation; aim above 1% as a baseline and ideally above 1.5%. ** | [L0665, 2022] |
| At least 1% | Minimum for a Google Shopping campaign; below that there is room for optimisation. | [L0966, 2025] |
| At least 3% | Google Shopping aim. A rate from 0.32 to 2.71% indicates a problem with keywords, pricing or bids. ** | [L1110, 2019] |
| 2% | A good result in Google Shopping for high-ticket products. ** | [L0744, 2021-22] |
| About 0.14% | Very low in Google Shopping for high-ticket products; worth investigating. ** | [L0744, 2021-22] |

- Click-through rate equals clicks divided by impressions. Example: 7 clicks from 800 impressions is about 0.9%, which is a signal to compare your listing with competitors' listings before raising the maximum bid. ** [L0663, 2019]
- A campaign with 35 clicks from almost 3,000 impressions has a click-through rate of about 1.2%. That is very low and should be investigated. ** [L1002, 2019]
- Google does not show a Quality Score for Product Listing Ads, so CTR becomes the main metric to optimise for those campaigns. Compare your CTR with the benchmark CTR of competitors and aim to beat it. [L1226, 2020-26]
- If competitors have higher benchmark CTRs in Product Listing Ads, they are winning the Quality Score battle and paying less per click. Check your prices and product titles first when your CTR is below theirs. A healthy Google Ads account is one where your CTR beats the benchmark CTR of your competitors. [L1226, 2020-26]

#### Conversion Rate

- Google Shopping and Product Listing Ads had an average conversion rate of about 2.3%, while about 97.7% of visitors did not buy on their first visit. These figures were cited in the talk and should be checked against your own account data. [L0510, 2018]
- Use Google Shopping and Remarketing as the primary qualified traffic sources for conversion benchmarks, and treat their conversion rate target of about 2.5% as the reference. ** [L0836, 2024]
- Google campaigns should aim for an add-to-cart ratio around 7%, higher than Meta or TikTok, because search is intent-based. The landing page should match the keywords the buyer searched. [L1194, undated]

#### Impression Share

- Use the search impression share metric as a baseline to compare what the budget captures with the full market share needed to be seen by most eligible shoppers. ** [L0357, 2019]
- Impression share shows what portion of possible impressions your ads actually received. A 30 percent share means 70 percent of potential impressions are missed. ** [L0968, 2021]
- A share of 2% means 98% of eligible searches are missed. ** [L1109, 2018]
- Example: in a campaign generating about $69,000 monthly revenue, Impression Share sat at 32% instead of 66%, so the open question was whether better campaign work alone could double revenue. [L0463, 2020-26]
- A Search Impression Share near 100% means an ad group already shows for nearly every relevant query its negative keyword list allows, so that targeting has no room to grow. More volume would then require relaxing the negative keyword list, which usually brings back irrelevant searches. ** [L0744, 2021-22]
- A reasonable early sample for Google Shopping is around 200 impressions. If impressions remain far below this with a normal bid and budget, check the campaign setup. ** [L0834, 2021]
- Impression share in Google Shopping depends on product quality and on which dashboard view is checked. Adding more products does not automatically increase impression share, but better-performing products improve it. [L0666, 2024]
- To increase a low search impression share, add the keywords your products already show for and keep adding negative keywords. Better click-through rate then raises quality score, which brings more impressions at a lower cost, while raising the bid alone may not get more impressions. ** [L0470, 2023]
- Check the search impression share column once the account is set up. A share of 2% means 98% of eligible searches are missed; if impression share is very low, see the inch-up method below. ** [L1109, 2018]

### Diagnosing High Impressions and Low Clicks

- When a Google Ads campaign shows high impressions but very few clicks, the most likely cause is that it appears for irrelevant or overly broad search terms. Review the search terms report and add the off-target terms as negative keywords. [L0856, 2024]
- Shoppers who see the ad as less relevant than competing ads, or a search term that is not relevant, will not click. Protecting click-through rate protects quality score and lowers cost per click. ** [L0744, 2021-22]

Procedure: handling high impressions but low clicks. ** [L0469, 2021]

1. Check the search terms triggering the product ad. [L0469, 2021]
2. Add a negative keyword for every search term that is unrelated to the product. [L0469, 2021]
3. If the keywords are relevant but clicks are still low, search the product on Google Shopping. [L0469, 2021]
4. Compare the competing listings and identify what they do differently that makes shoppers click. [L0469, 2021]

Procedure: diagnosing high impressions with uneven distribution. ** [L1109, 2018]

1. In your Google Ads account, look at which products and search terms generate impressions. Look for uneven distribution, for example 600 of 1,000 daily impressions coming from one keyword or product. [L1109, 2018]
2. Compare the click-through rate of the concentrated impressions with the rest. If the concentrated part is irrelevant and has a low CTR, that is the problem. [L1109, 2018]
3. Add the irrelevant search terms as negative keywords. CTR rises because Google then shows the product only for better matches. [L1109, 2018]
4. Search for your own products in an incognito Chrome window in Google Shopping to see your position against competitors. [L1109, 2018]
5. If competitors above you have the same price, images and shipping, raise your bids and differentiate your offer to win higher positions. [L1109, 2018]
6. Check the search impression share column. A share of 2% means 98% of eligible searches are missed. [L1109, 2018]
7. If impression share is very low, raise bids across the board using the inch-up method: increase bids gradually, not budget, until daily spend reaches the ideal level, then keep optimising. [L1109, 2018]

- Google Shopping does not show every search term that triggers your ads, so search term data cannot fully explain performance. Use it to find mismatches, not as a complete picture. ** [L0879, 2021-22]

Case: established high-ticket Shopping funnel. ** [L0744, 2021-22]

- Starting point: a shared budget of $300 per day across three campaigns, high priority at a $0.30 bid and an average CPC of $0.26. [L0744, 2021-22]
- Over 30 days the search terms report listed 6,971 terms, many of them generic and low-value. [L0744, 2021-22]
- One campaign showed the Limited by budget status. Google's budget explorer recommended $370, a rise of about $70 that was judged acceptable for a profitable account. [L0744, 2021-22]
- The alternatives were lowering the high priority bid from $0.30 to $0.20 to bring CPC to $0.20 or below, or adding negatives. [L0744, 2021-22]
- Each review then covered only the date range since the previous check, and negatives were added to the master list so all three campaigns received them. [L0744, 2021-22]

- Daily check for high impressions and clicks: look at the search terms, then add negatives for irrelevant ones, and check competitor listings. ** [L1002, 2019]
- Break down the Google Shopping campaign daily by keywords with impressions and keywords that spent money, add irrelevant keywords to the negative list every day, and raise the budget only once the account is profitable. ** [L1068, 2019]

Procedure: daily improvement of the Google Shopping campaign. ** [L1068, 2019]

1. Open the keywords inside the Shopping campaign. [L1068, 2019]
2. Review which keywords received impressions. [L1068, 2019]
3. Review which keywords spent money. [L1068, 2019]
4. Add irrelevant keywords to the negative keyword list every day. [L1068, 2019]
5. Raise the budget only once the account is profitable. [L1068, 2019]

### Diagnosing Low CTR

- A low CTR affects the performance of the whole campaign, not the whole account. A few low-CTR products among many have little effect, while low CTR across most products in a campaign has a significant effect, though the exact size is unknown. ** [L0879, 2021-22]
- A Google Shopping click-through rate in the 0.3 to 2.7 percent range usually comes from three causes: very broad keywords that attract non-buyers, competitors or suppliers pricing below your MAP, or a bid so low that your listing sits at the bottom of results. Remove non-buying keywords and check pricing before raising bids. ** [L1110, 2019]
- A rate of 0.32 to 2.71 percent indicates a problem with keywords, pricing or bids. Aim for at least 3 percent. ** [L1110, 2019]

Procedure: troubleshooting a low Google Shopping click-through rate. ** [L1110, 2019]

1. In Google Ads, open the keyword view of the campaign and find keywords with many impressions but few or no clicks. [L1110, 2019]
2. Add non-buyer, broad keywords as negative keywords. [L1110, 2019]
3. Search those keywords on Google yourself and check the shopping results. [L1110, 2019]
4. If competitors sell the same product for less, check for pricing discrepancies or MAP violations. [L1110, 2019]
5. If you rank near the bottom, raise your bid by about 20 percent, wait a few days, then check your position and click-through rate. [L1110, 2019]
6. If the position is still low, raise the bid again. [L1110, 2019]

Procedure: diagnosing a low CTR on Shopping ads. ** [L0879, 2021-22]

1. Compare your ad with competitors' ads for the same product, looking at image, title, price, review stars and any promotion. [L0879, 2021-22]
2. Check whether competitors are selling at a lower price across the board, which can explain why people click on them instead. [L0879, 2021-22]
3. Check your position in the auction. With 10 or more advertisers, ads at the bottom get fewer clicks, so a small bid increase may move the ad into the middle of the pack and raise CTR. [L0879, 2021-22]
4. Check the search terms your ad appears for to find mismatches. [L0879, 2021-22]

Procedure: diagnosing low click-through rate in a Shopping campaign. ** [L0745, 2020-26]

1. Check whether prices are competitive. [L0745, 2020-26]
2. Check whether the campaign shows for irrelevant keywords and add negative keywords after doing negative keyword research. [L0745, 2020-26]
3. Run the target searches yourself and judge whether your product images are engaging enough. [L0745, 2020-26]

Procedure: diagnosing low CTR on relevant keywords. ** [L0243, 2019]

1. Log into Google Ads account. [L0243, 2019]
2. Navigate to keywords section. [L0243, 2019]
3. For keywords showing many impressions but few clicks (low CTR on relevant terms), check if competitors are violating MAP pricing, if your bid is too low, or if your product image is inferior to competitors' images. [L0243, 2019]
4. Make targeted improvements to high-quality keywords before optimising or removing weak keywords. [L0243, 2019]

- CTR of 2% or lower on relevant keywords signals one or more problems. Keywords may match irrelevant search queries, your product price may be higher than competitors (violating MAP pricing), your bid may be too low to place you high in search results, or your product image and description may be weaker than competitors. ** [L0243, 2019]
