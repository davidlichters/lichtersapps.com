## Learning Phase, Reporting and Weekly Optimization Routine

### Learning Phase Durations

The length of the learning phase depends on what changed. Use the row that matches the trigger.

| Trigger | Duration | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Significant change to a Google or Meta campaign | 3 to 5 days, sometimes up to a week, depending on traffic volume. Performance may decline noticeably during this time. [L0224, 2024+] | [L0224, 2024+] |
| Change to CPC | Let it run one to two weeks before judging. [L0360, 2024] | [L0360, 2024] |
| Campaign spending about $44 per day | One to two weeks to finish the learning phase. [L0837, 2025] | [L0837, 2025] |
| General Google learning phase | About two weeks. [L0276, 2024] Typically two to three weeks without many changes. [L1094, 2023] | [L0276, 2024; L1094, 2023] |
| Google Ads minimum to optimise | 7 to 10 days. Results are not to be expected within 3 to 5 days, unlike Meta and TikTok. [L0296, 2021+] | [L0296, 2021+] |
| Brand-new Google Ads account with no conversion data | About 2 to 4 weeks while Google tests the ads. [L0857, 2025] | [L0857, 2025] |
| New Shopping campaign, before judging its structure | 2 to 4 weeks. [L0750, 2020-26] | [L0750, 2020-26] |
| New store, before major bid or budget changes | 2 to 4 weeks. [L0193, undated] | [L0193, undated] |
| Budget increase | A new learning phase follows. Judge the new return on ad spend only after it has settled. [L0963, 2022] | [L0963, 2022] |

- Every new campaign goes through the learning phase again, even when the advertiser has historical data from an older campaign. Past data helps a little, so it is better than nothing, but it does not remove the learning phase. [L0965, 2024]
- Major setting changes or new campaigns restart the learning phase. Relearning is acceptable when the current data is mostly wasted traffic, because it is better for the campaign to learn from a correct setup than from lots of traffic with few sales. [L0837, 2025]
- Multiple significant changes in quick succession restart the learning phase repeatedly, so avoid multiple changes during this window. [L0224, 2024+]
- After a large budget increase, or a restart, Google needs time to relearn. A large budget at restart can prevent efficient spending, so grow the budget gradually after each stable step. ** [L1112, 2021]
- If a campaign spends its full daily budget during the learning phase, the bids are sufficient. If the budget is not spent, raise bids to start getting traffic. [L1094, 2023]
- After large bid increases, Google re-tests keywords, which can cause a short dip in sales. Judge the change only after enough new data has come in. ** [L0470, 2023]
- There is no fixed number of weeks a campaign must run unchanged. The time needed depends on how fast the budget is spent, so judge a campaign by its impressions rather than by the calendar. ** [L0470, 2023]
- Google Ads itself is low maintenance once set up correctly. Optimisation can happen every 7 to 15 days, or the account can run with minimal intervention, because the platform optimises itself. [L0296, 2021+]

### Time from Launch to First Data

- New Google Ads campaigns can take up to one week to get approved, so give the account a few days before expecting data. [L0715, 2020-26]
- After launch, ads may need up to five business days before they start getting impressions, while clicks and sales come later. [L0715, 2020-26]
- Products approved in Google Merchant Center take 1 to 3 days to enter the auction and start showing impressions. Do not adjust campaigns immediately; give 1 to 3 days for data to appear. ** [L0274, 2023]
- New Shopping campaigns usually need about 2 to 3 days after approval before they start getting impressions and clicks. [L0750, 2020-26]
- Once campaigns are approved, impressions usually start right away and clicks follow. For a new store, expect roughly one to three days from approval for traffic to start showing. ** [L1068, 2019]
- Once Google Shopping ads are approved, they show impressions and clicks within a few days, which shows which product pages to optimise first. Use this data before optimising the whole catalogue. ** [L0968, 2021]
- Give a new Google Shopping campaign a few days to level out before changing bids or budgets. ** [L0852, 2020]
- Alpha and beta campaigns that are structured correctly and have sizeable traffic should produce the first sales within about 2 to 5 days. ** [L0854, 2022]
- Launch with alpha and beta campaigns, let them collect data, then adjust keywords, bids and product pages based on that data. Do not judge profitability from day one. [L0856, 2024]

### Conversion Timing

- The conversion window is typically 1 to 5 days after the click. Users see the original ads and remarketing ads during this period before purchasing. [L0274, 2023]
- The typical buying window for Google Shopping traffic is 1 to 3 days after the click. A campaign with about 100 targeted visitors a day should therefore produce at least two sales a day. ** [L0834, 2021]
- Once Shopping campaigns are optimised, conversions typically appear within about three days after the click. ** [L0855, 2023]
- Conversions may take up to about 24 to 48 hours to appear in Google Ads. Check the attribution model before judging a campaign's conversions. [L0510, 2018]

### When Not to Change

#### Inside the Learning Window

- During the learning phase, make changes to negative keywords rather than to budgets or bids, so Google does not restart learning. Negative keywords can be added from day one. ** [L1094, 2023]
- Do not start scaling Google Shopping ads immediately after launching campaigns. Allow 2 to 4 weeks for the learning phase and enough data collection before making major changes to bids or budgets. [L0193, undated]
- Do not change the campaign structure before at least 2 weeks have passed since the ads started getting impressions and clicks. [L0750, 2020-26]
- Avoid drastic changes to bids, budgets or structure while a new Shopping campaign is still collecting data. About 71 clicks over roughly two and a half weeks is too little to judge conversion. [L0971, 2024]
- New Shopping campaigns need time to learn, because quality score improves when visitors stay and browse several pages. Avoid drastic bid or budget changes early on. Raise CPCs only on the specific products that need more traffic. [L0971, 2024]
- Test Shopping campaign changes about once a week and give each change a full week of data. Allow a two- to three-day learning curve after each change, so a change made on Monday and changed again on Tuesday resets the learning. Friday is an example change day for a store with low traffic. [L0856, 2024]
- For a brand-new account, do not make structural changes during the first 2 to 4 weeks of the learning period. If the account spends money with no sales, lower the budget while you investigate. [L0857, 2025]
- Do not make major changes before the brand-new account's learning period of about 2 to 4 weeks has passed. [L0857, 2025]
- The first seven days of a new Google Ads account and store are not representative, because campaigns and product pages are not yet optimised. Judge conversion rate and spend only after the store has been optimised and enough visitors have arrived. ** [L1092, 2022]
- Do not judge a new account over a small sample. Four weeks with 124 impressions and one click is too small to judge conversion; judge over hundreds to thousands of visits per week. ** [L1088, 2020]
- Do not draw conclusions from data right after the holiday shopping season, because buying slows in the weeks that follow. Wait about two weeks into January before making decisions. ** [L0551, 2020]

#### Sample Size and Decisions

- Small click counts are not enough to judge a campaign. For example, 161 clicks or even 300 clicks is a small sample, and the next batch can change results completely, so avoid drastic changes until more volume arrives. ** [L0801, 2021]
- Do not make big decisions on a product until it has about 100 clicks. Before 100 clicks, only make small adjustments such as CPC changes. [L0941, 2022]
- Wait until a campaign has at least a few hundred clicks or visitors, typically over about a week, before drawing conclusions. ** [L0803, 2022]
- Judge a Smart Shopping change by comparing a recent period with an earlier period of the same length, not by individual days. Daily results can swing widely while the overall trend still improves. [L0454, 2019]
- Changes take a day or two to show in the data. Check after changes every few days, then weekly or every other week once the campaign is stable. ** [L0469, 2021]
- Google Ads data is only trustworthy after many more visits. With a small sample, wins or losses can be random. ** [L0855, 2023]
- For a new store with few sales, some sales may come from random causes that do not repeat. Look for a repeating ad source or keyword across more data before drawing conclusions. ** [L0429, 2022]

#### Product Exclusion

- Do not exclude a new Shopping product just because it has no sales after a couple of weeks. Decide based on click volume: about 100 clicks is the lower end, and 300 or more clicks gives a more reliable data basis, since conversion rates can still level out between 200 and 300 clicks. [L0666, 2024]
- Conversion rates for a single product are highly variable in small samples. Early zero-sale results are often randomness rather than a broken product. [L0666, 2024]
- A store with about 60 active products that has run Google Ads for around a month usually has enough data for significant optimisations, so waiting for more data is rarely necessary. Start with the troubleshooting steps for clicks without sales. [L0666, 2024]

#### Budget, Bids and Live Changes

- For high-ticket stores, bids do not need daily changes because there is little benefit in it. With steady performance, bids can stay unchanged for months. ** [L0744, 2021-22]
- If a campaign spends an account's budget without sales, do not change bids on impulse; first confirm the campaign is set to spend the budget. A $20 daily budget that produces only one click in the first days is too little data to optimise from. ** [L0834, 2021]
- Changes can be made directly in a live Google Ads campaign. There is no need to pause the campaign first. Pausing only makes sense if you intend to stop spending. [L0972, 2025]
- Changes should be made based on the data that exists, not on a fixed schedule. This may mean every 2 to 3 days or every two weeks. Changing dozens of campaigns from a daily export is far too extreme, though negative keywords are the exception and can be added at any time. ** [L0470, 2023]
- Google Ads optimisation score is not a reliable decision tool. Stores with low scores can be very profitable, so do not change the campaign because of that number alone. ** [L1090, 2021]
- Do not run new ad platforms during the low-competition window. Keep what works and raise budgets there instead. ** [L0468, 2020]
- Do not let a profitable campaign that is already making money sit untouched, because a campaign that looks stable slowly loses performance over weeks and months unless someone keeps actively improving it. [L0463, 2020-26]
- Do not leave a Shopping campaign unchanged for weeks; review the data early and optimise sooner rather than later. ** [L0855, 2023]

### Columns, Dashboards and Reports

#### Core Metrics to Monitor

- Monitor these key metrics to evaluate Google Ads performance: Impressions, Clicks, Cost, Cost per all conversions, Average CPC, Benchmark maximum CPC, Click share, Search impression share, Search absolute top impression share, Search lost impression share, and Benchmark CTR. [L0059, undated]
- Add these columns to Google Ads campaign reports: Cost, Conversion Value, Conversion Value ÷ Cost (ROAS), and Search Impression Share. These metrics guide all bid and budget decisions. [L0193, undated]
- Review Google Ads reports every week or every other week, and track whether impressions, clicks and conversions are trending up, down or flat. ** [L0968, 2021]

#### Revenue Columns

- In Google Ads, configure your columns to display revenue metrics. Navigate to `Columns > Conversions` and select both `Conversion value over cost` (your ROAS) and `Conversion value per click` (your revenue per click). These two columns are essential for bid optimisation. [L0299, 2020-26]
- Conversion value and revenue are identical terms in Google Ads reporting. Your conversion value comes from your e-commerce platform's total order value tracking. [L0299, 2020-26]

#### Comparison Table for Ads

** Columns to include in a Google Ads report for comparing ads: [L0968, 2021]

| Column | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Impressions | How many times your ad was shown |
| Clicks | How many people clicked your ad |
| Click-through rate (CTR) | Clicks divided by impressions |
| Cost per click (CPC) | Average spend per click |
| Conversions | Conversions per ad |
| Average impressions | Average impression count per ad |
| Impression share (add later) | Share of possible impressions your ads received |

[L0968, 2021]

- Impression share shows what portion of possible impressions your ads actually received. Check it once negative keywords and campaign segmentation are tight, because a 30 percent share means 70 percent of potential impressions are missed. ** [L0968, 2021]

#### Impression Share and Competitive Metrics

- To add Search Impression Share to a report, open `Columns`, then `Modify columns for ad groups`, go to `Competitive metrics`, select `Search impr. share` and `Search lost IS (rank)`, then `Apply`. `Search lost IS (rank)` shows the share lost because of ad rank. ** [L0744, 2021-22]
- In the `Modify columns` panel under `Competitive metrics`, `Search abs. top IS` and `Click share` can be added next to `Search impr. share` and `Search lost IS (rank)`. `Search abs. top IS` shows how often ads appear in the top position, which helps compare placement with share of impressions. ** [L0744, 2021-22]
- To check impression share and similar metrics, click `Columns`, then `Customize columns`, and add the column you need, such as impression share, to the campaign view. [L0805, 2024]
- `Customize columns` lets you add metrics such as impression share to the campaign table to see how often an ad appears for search terms. [L0805, 2024]
- Google Ads reports can break down performance by day and hour (`Reports > Breakdown > Time and day`) to see clicks and sales for each hour of each weekday. Campaigns can be scheduled for those hours. [L0971, 2024]
- To see which search terms triggered clicks in Google Ads, go to `Insights and reports` and open `Search terms`. [L0471, 2024]
- Keyword-level breakdowns show where impressions and clicks come from. Add irrelevant search terms as negative keywords, and lower the default cost per click for less profitable product groups instead of removing them. ** [L0853, 2021]
- In Google Shopping, setting up each product as its own product group lets impression share be measured per product, not only for the whole campaign. Adding more products does not automatically raise impression share. [L0666, 2024]

#### Product Identification

- In Google Ads reporting, click `Columns` at the top right and add a product information column so product IDs show product details. The product ID in Google Ads matches the Shopify product ID, so the number can be searched in the Products list of the Shopify admin. [L0836, 2024]

#### Dashboards

- Google Ads has a native Dashboards feature for free. Create a new dashboard (it starts blank), add widgets such as conversions by device, conversion rate by device, geographic performance by state or a campaign view, resize them, and click Save. Saved dashboards can be reopened at any time to check metrics and bid adjustments without navigating through campaigns. [L1236, 2015+]

### Analytics Custom Reports and Attribution

#### Building the Custom Report

- When building a custom Google Analytics report for Google Ads, use the metric "Transactions" instead of "Conversions", because conversions may not report correctly in custom reports. ** [L0256, 2021-22]
- The Google Analytics dimension for the level below ad group is `Keyword` for search text campaigns. For Google Shopping campaigns the equivalent is `Product group`, because Shopping ads target products rather than keywords. The report template dates from when search text ads dominated. ** [L0721, 2021-22]

Procedure: create a custom Google Analytics report to track Google Ads performance at the search-term level. ** [L0256, 2021-22]

1. Go to Customisation > Custom Reports and click Create Custom Report. [L0256, 2021-22]
2. Enter Title: Google Ads Performance Report. [L0256, 2021-22]
3. Set Report Type to Explorer. [L0256, 2021-22]
4. Add Metrics by searching for and selecting each: Advertising Clicks, Advertising Impressions, Advertising CTR, Advertising CPC, Advertising Cost, Transactions, Advertising Cost per Transaction, E-commerce Conversion Rate, E-commerce Revenue, Advertising ROAS. [L0256, 2021-22]
5. Add Dimension Drilldowns in order: Campaign, Google Ads Ad Group, Keyword, Search Query. [L0256, 2021-22]
6. Add Filters: (Traffic Type = paid) AND (Source/Medium = google / cpc). [L0256, 2021-22]
7. Click Save to populate the report. [L0256, 2021-22]
8. Drill down from campaign through ad group and keyword to individual search terms to compare clicks, cost, transactions, conversion rate, revenue and ROAS. [L0256, 2021-22]

- The custom Google Analytics report for Google Ads shows revenue and ROAS attributed to individual campaigns, ad groups, keywords and search terms. This is visibility the Google Ads interface itself does not provide, enabling granular performance analysis and optimisation at the search-term level. ** [L0256, 2021-22]

#### Attribution Models

- Last-click attribution is the standard tracking model. The most recent traffic source click within the same session (approximately five minutes of on-site activity) receives 100 percent of the conversion credit. Example: if a customer clicks an ad on Monday, watches YouTube on Monday, but converts via email on Wednesday, email receives full credit. [L0257, 2020-26]
- When 100 percent of traffic originates from AdWords, the tracking system shows only approximately 60 percent of conversions as AdWords and 40 percent as Direct/Shopify. Attribution becomes increasingly fragmented as you diversify traffic sources. [L0257, 2020-26]
- Assisted conversions in Google Ads show how many multi-touch conversions a campaign influenced, not just the final click. This gives a more accurate view than last-click attribution alone of a campaign's true contribution in multi-click customer journeys. [L0257, 2020-26]
- Use Assisted Revenue in Google Ads to see the full attribution picture beyond last-click conversions. It reveals how multiple touchpoints across sessions contribute to each sale. [L0033, undated]
- Google Ads and Google Analytics attribution models: First click gives the conversion to the first interaction. Last click gives it to the last interaction. Linear splits one conversion across all clicks, producing fractional conversions such as 0.5 for a Shopping click and 0.5 for a text ad click. [L0510, 2018]
- Check the attribution model before judging a campaign's conversions, because different models assign the same sale to different ads. [L0510, 2018]
- Keyword attribution: to see which keyword produced a sale in Google Ads, open the keyword breakdown inside the campaign that brought the sale. In Google Analytics, conversion tracking shows which campaign sold the product, while organic sales often appear as non-set or not-set keywords. ** [L0429, 2022]

### Tracking Discrepancies

- Shopping ads and Facebook ads are not the only traffic sources to track. Google Ads, Shopping and Facebook all over-claim conversions, so compare them against store analytics. ** [L0247, 2021]
- Compare Google Ads conversions with real orders. Sales can be attributed elsewhere if customers visit from an ad, bookmark the page and buy later, or buy from an organic search or email link. [L0359, 2020]
- The search term report in Google Ads is not updated in real time, and its dates use the account's time zone (GMT-05:00, Eastern Time). Compare date ranges with that in mind, and do not judge a search term from the last day or two of data. ** [L0880, 2021-22]
- Google Shopping does not show every search term that triggers your ads. A campaign with 1,000 impressions may list only a few search terms, so the search term data is incomplete and cannot fully explain performance. ** [L0879, 2021-22]
- When checking whether an excluded Shopping product group still gets clicks, use a date range that starts after the exclusion. A longer range such as 30 days still shows older data. [L0836, 2024]
- Check whether a cost-per-click increase has been applied to all product groups, because some products can be excluded from bid changes and stop getting traffic. Audit the account regularly, since errors can appear without warning. ** [L0357, 2019]
