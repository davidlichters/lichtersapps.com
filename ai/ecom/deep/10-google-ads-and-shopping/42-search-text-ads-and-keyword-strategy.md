## Search Text Ads and Keyword Strategy

### Funnel Position and Intent

- Align search text ads to the prospect's funnel position: awareness and research at the top of the funnel, purchase intent at the bottom, because search keywords and buyer intent vary significantly by stage. [L0074, undated]
- Search text ads perform best on search terms with mid-to-bottom funnel intent, where the intent points to a specific collection page or product page. Judge these keywords as low, medium or high value, the same way as for Shopping ads. [L0751, 2021-22] **
- Targeting broad, top-of-funnel search terms with search text ads wastes budget when those visitors convert poorly or slowly. The account can then run at a loss for a long time without any return within the planned timeframe. [L0751, 2021-22] **
- Low-value or top-of-funnel search terms can be targeted with search text ads only as an advanced technique, and only once the account is already profitable. [L0880, 2021-22] **
- More specific keywords produce more specific clicks and a higher conversion rate. Broad or generic keywords bring visitors who are not yet ready to buy the product. [L1085, 2018] **
- Search targeting means bidding on core commercial keywords, often called money keywords, which are the terms that generate sales. It is the first targeting method to prioritize. [L0720, 2020-26]
- Search ads aimed at informational queries such as "buyer's guide" plus a product category can put the brand in front of shoppers who are still researching before they buy. Brand visibility in these searches can raise the click-through rate of the same brand's later ads, so include such queries in the search plan. [L1142, 2020-26]
- Buyer-intent keywords can be found in the search terms of a live campaign. Start a new store with one alpha and one beta campaign, review which keywords bring impressions, clicks and sales, and push more budget to those. [L0470, 2023]

### Role and Placement Alongside Shopping

- In a search campaign the advertiser types in the keywords and chooses broad, phrase or exact match, which makes it easy to get a lot of traffic that rarely converts. Shopping campaigns therefore remain the primary recommendation, with search campaigns used as a secondary source. [L0373, 2024+]
- Search campaigns can reach shoppers who never open the Shopping tab, so they add reach. Add them only once the Shopping campaigns are working and the store generates real traffic. [L0373, 2024+]
- A store can receive traffic from three sources at once: organic Google results, Shopping campaigns, and small search campaigns for best-selling products. A branded search campaign is added on top once the store is profitable. [L0373, 2024+]
- Search text ads appear on the Google results page for a keyword, usually as up to three ads and sometimes four, above the organic listings. A small block can also appear below the organic results. The top of the page is the most valuable placement. [L0751, 2021-22] **
- Running Shopping ads, search text ads and organic listings together can give a store up to three visible spots on the same results page. Seeing the brand several times raises the chance that a shopper clicks one of its listings. [L0751, 2021-22] **
- Many shoppers avoid clicking ads, but plenty of others do click them. Search text ads therefore still capture a real share of clicks, even though some users will ignore them. [L0751, 2021-22] **
- For high-ticket dropshipping, search text ads are rarely the main ad format because Shopping ads usually deliver better returns. Search text ads still have a place alongside Shopping ads. [L0751, 2021-22] **
- In markets with lower Shopping ad usage, such as Australia and the UK, search text ads can outperform Shopping ads for some stores. Even there, Shopping ads tend to be the stronger performer overall. [L0751, 2021-22] **
- Ad stacking, meaning multiple ads for the same product, on your best-selling items gives maximum exposure on the search results page. [L0074, undated]
- Once one product sells consistently, show it in as many places as possible, including an exact-match search text ad with the product name so it can dominate the first page for that name. [L0356, 2019] **
- A strong-performing keyword can also be pulled into its own search text campaign as an exact match, sending traffic to the same product page that already converts. This is an extra channel on top of the Shopping campaign, not a replacement for the Shopping setup. [L0856, 2024]

### When to Start Search Text Ads

- Google Shopping is the first campaign type to start with and should take most of the daily ad budget at the beginning. Search text ads come later, after the Shopping campaigns are profitable. [L1086, 2019] **
- Do not start with Google search text ads. They become worth considering only once products are proven and the store is highly profitable. [L0247, 2021] **
- Traditional Google search text ads should not be started until Shopping campaigns are already highly successful. [L1069, 2019] **
- Search text ads should not be started until Shopping is already profitable. [L0855, 2023] *(2023)*
- Do not turn off campaigns that are profitable. Search text ads should not be used until all other campaigns are already profitable. [L0969, 2022] **
- Shopping ads must be profitable before you add search text ads. If Shopping ads do not work yet, search text ads are unlikely to succeed, with only a small number of stores as exceptions. [L0880, 2021-22] **
- Start search text ads only after your Shopping ads have revealed medium- and high-value search terms. These are terms that show buying intent for your products: product IDs, brand names combined with product types, or very specific long-tail terms for one product or a small group. [L0880, 2021-22] **
- Search text ads take longer to set up than Shopping ads, and many products are never searched by name or product code. Build search text ads only for search terms that people actually use for your products or brands. [L0880, 2021-22] **
- The search term reports of your Shopping campaigns are the data source for finding keywords that should become search text ads. Shopping ads act as cheap research before you spend setup time on search text ads. [L0880, 2021-22] **
- Do not start product-specific search campaigns until Shopping campaigns have shown which keywords sell. Shopping campaigns are expected to keep a better return on ad spend than search campaigns, so the search campaign adds only a small extra source of traffic. [L0373, 2024+]
- Do not launch Google search text ads until the Shopping campaigns are already profitable and you know your best sellers. Until then, check the Google Ads account at least once a day and add irrelevant search terms to the negative keyword list. [L1086, 2019] **
- Before running search text ads for a high-ticket dropshipping store, answer these questions in order: [L0751, 2021-22] **
  1. When is the right time to start running search text ads?
  2. Which search terms should you target? Start with terms that have high purchase intent and are likely to convert relatively quickly.
  3. What is the best way to write the ad copy, including the headline, the ad text and the extension details?
- Search text ads that build on a proven product follow this sequence: [L0806, 2024] *(2024)*
  1. Identify the best-selling product.
  2. Create a search text ad for it with the product name as a phrase match keyword.
  3. Optimize the product page for SEO.
  4. The result is three spots on page one: the Shopping ad, the search text ad above organic results, and an organic listing.

### Keyword Sources from Shopping Data

**Procedure: starting search text ads from Shopping data** [L0880, 2021-22] **
1. Confirm that your Shopping ads are profitable.
2. Open your Shopping campaigns.
3. Find search terms that already get traffic and are specific to one product, one model, one brand or a small product group.
4. Build search text ads around those terms and test them with a small budget.

**Procedure: search term mining in Google Ads** [L0880, 2021-22] **
1. Open the Google Ads account and go to Campaigns.
2. Skip the high-priority Shopping campaign, which holds low-value search terms.
3. Open the low-priority Shopping campaign, which holds the high-value search terms.
4. Optionally open one ad group if its products have better margins and you want to test only those products.
5. Open the Keywords tab in the ad group and select Search Terms.
6. Set the date range to about three months if the account has run that long.
7. Look for very specific terms such as model codes, model names, brand plus model combinations and product-specific wording.
8. Note close variations of each strong term, since they are needed to build exact match or phrase match targets.
9. Download the search term report as Excel, Google Sheets or PDF.
10. Copy the terms you want to target into a central keyword spreadsheet and repeat for the other ad groups, and for the medium-priority campaign where brand-level terms can be found.

**Setting: Google Ads path for search term analysis** [L0880, 2021-22] **
Campaigns > open the Shopping campaign > open the ad group > Keywords tab > Search Terms. Set the date range (the course uses a custom range of about three months) and use the Download button to export the list.

**Procedure: keywords from the Shopping keyword report** [L0855, 2023] *(2023)*
1. Open Google Ads and go to the Shopping campaigns.
2. Pull the keyword report for the Shopping campaigns.
3. Filter the report to your best-selling product.
4. Note the keywords that have already led to sales of that product.
5. Add those keywords to a search text campaign built around that product's page.

**Procedure: extracting keywords for a new Search campaign** [L0210, 2022]
1. In Google Ads, open your Shopping campaign.
2. Navigate to the Keywords section or the report showing where your ads appeared.
3. Identify all keywords that generated clicks and sales.
4. Copy the keywords that drove sales for your target product.
5. Paste them into your new Search campaign ad group as phrase match keywords (enclosed in quotation marks).

**Rules for choosing keywords**
- Keyword selection for search text ads is based on what already sells, not on search volume or competition scores. Keyword volume and competition figures from Google are not used for choosing these keywords. [L0855, 2023] *(2023)*
- Shopping data for individual product pages supports the decision of which products to put on search text campaigns. [L0855, 2023] *(2023)*
- Keywords that already drove sales of a product, and that show a high click-through rate on Shopping ads, are strong candidates for a search text ad test. Start with an exact match on the core term, then add the close variations found in the report. [L0880, 2021-22] **
- Model names and model codes are high-intent search terms because the searcher already knows which product they want. Keep every variation of a model name or code in your target list. [L0880, 2021-22] **
- A brand-level search term has a lower conversion rate than model-level terms, but in some niches a low enough cost per click makes it viable. [L0880, 2021-22] **
- Keep every target keyword in one central spreadsheet, recording the search term, its match type and its performance figures in the same file, so you can reuse the terms without searching the reports again. [L0880, 2021-22] **
- Optimize a Google Search campaign that starts with many keywords and search terms, since some pay off and others do not. [L1077, undated] **
  1. Review which keywords and search terms generated sales or add-to-carts.
  2. Keep and scale the terms that convert.
  3. Remove or refine the terms that spend without paying off.

**Case: model-level starting point** [L0880, 2021-22] **
Starting point: a single-brand ad group in the low-priority Shopping campaign with a max CPC of US$1.20 (enhanced) and a three-month search term report. The report showed many searches for specific models and model codes such as HL400D, HL200SN and HL300SN, which became the candidates for search text ads. The ad group totals for the period were 289 clicks, 4,187 impressions, a cost of US$266.20, a cost per conversion of US$91.36 and a conversion rate of 1.01%.

**Case: brand-level benchmark** [L0880, 2021-22] **
Brand-level test: a Shopping ad group for a brand in another campaign had a max CPC of US$0.80 (enhanced) and an analysis window of about three months. It produced 112 clicks, 3,404 impressions, a cost of US$130.92, a cost per conversion of US$65.46 and a conversion rate of 1.79%. This was the benchmark for judging whether brand-level search text ads could pay off.

**Brand-level terms** [L0880, 2021-22] **
Brand-level search terms can be tested in the medium-priority campaign. Their conversion rate is usually lower than for model-level terms, but in some niches a low enough cost per click makes them viable.

### Keyword Research and Validation

**Setting: Keyword Planner access** [L1120, 2024+]
Google Keyword Planner is reached via Google Ads > Tools > Keyword Planner. Inside it, select the option Discover new keywords.

**Procedure: keyword research in Keyword Planner** [L1120, 2024+]
1. Open your Google Ads account.
2. Click Tools.
3. Click Keyword Planner.
4. Click Discover new keywords.
5. Enter your niche (for example standing desk), and also enter your own URL or a competitor's URL to find related keywords. Doing both gives more and different keyword ideas.
6. Click Get results.
7. Close the refined keywords section, since it is not needed for tracking.
8. Record the average monthly searches, the competition rank and the top-of-page bid range (low and high).
9. Use the high range of the top-of-page bid for tracking.

**Setting: where to find volumes** [L0876, 2025-26]
Keyword Planner is found at Tools > Planning > Keyword Planner. Set the plan location to the target country (for example United States), set languages to All languages, and use the last 12 months as the date range before reading volumes.

**Keyword Planner validation sheet** [L0876, 2025-26]

| Keyword | Avg. monthly searches | Three month change | YoY change | Competition | Ad impression share | Top of page bid (low range) | Top of page bid (high range) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Read each AI-generated keyword in the group against these columns. The Keyword ideas, Forecast, Saved keywords and Negative keywords tabs sit in the same plan.

- Search volume differs widely between closely related terms, so each keyword needs its own volume check. Keep high-volume terms and be careful with niche terms that have very low monthly searches. Apply your own judgement to every term that has real volume. [L0876, 2025-26]
- A validated keyword set shapes everything downstream, including ad groups, ad copy and campaign structure. Build the set carefully before creating campaigns, because it sets the direction for the rest of the account. [L0876, 2025-26]
- Keep ad groups tight so the ad copy matches the keywords in the group. Relevant copy protects Quality Score over the long term. [L0876, 2025-26]
- Google Ads shows keyword search volumes in large ranges (for example 10,000-100,000) until the account is established and has spent more money. Exact numbers appear only with account maturity and spending history. [L0278, 2025] *(2025)*
- Third-party keyword research tools give exact monthly search volumes with country-specific filtering. Google Ads shows broad ranges until the account is established and has spent money. [L0278, 2025] *(2025)*
- Keywords Everywhere is a keyword research tool used to compare search volume and competition for product keywords. [L0788, undated] **
- For a new store, look for keywords with 1,000 to 10,000 searches per month and a cost per click below $1 with low competition. [L0788, undated] **
- Golden nugget keywords have moderate search volume but low competition. They often deliver better returns for Google Ads than high-volume, highly competitive terms. [L0788, undated] **
- Keyword Planner cost-per-click estimates are usually higher than what you will actually pay. Google adjusts the real cost based on click quality and how users behave on the landing page. [L1090, 2021] **
- A low top-of-page bid range does not mean zero impressions. Better click-through rate and longer time on site can earn cheaper clicks, so a keyword with a high range can still be worth starting. [L0855, 2023] *(2023)*
- **Sources differ on AI-generated and outside keywords.** One source says keywords for search text ads come from the Shopping keyword report, not from outside keyword tools or AI brainstorming. [L0855, 2023] Another builds an AI-generated keyword set and validates every term in Keyword Planner before creating campaigns. [L0876, 2025-26] Both apply to the same decision: the Shopping report sets the list for proven products; a validated set is needed when you build a broader list.

### Match Types

**Table: match types for search ads** [L0080, undated]

| Match Type | Syntax | Behavior | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exact match | [keyword] | Appears only for the keyword and close variants such as plurals | [car] triggers for 'car' and 'cars' |
| Phrase match | "keyword" | Appears when the phrase is present with other words before or after it | "car" triggers for 'fast car', 'car parts', 'amphibious car' |
| Broad match | keyword | Appears for any search related to the keyword, including synonyms and variations | car triggers for 'automobile', 'ferrari paint color', 'car parts', 'truck tires' |
| Modified broad | +keyword +modifier | Appears for searches containing the modified terms or close variations | +red car triggers for 'reddish car', 'stemming', 'CA' |

**Table: positive targeting match types** [L0880, 2021-22] **

| Match type | Syntax | Ad shows for | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exact match | [keyword] | Only the same words in the same order; close variants such as plurals and misspellings are also accepted; no extra words around it | Tight control, needs many variations listed |
| Phrase match | 'keyword' in quotation marks | The phrase in the same order, with other words allowed before or after it | Catches longer searches without predicting every extra word |
| Broad match modifier | +word1 +word2 | All words marked with + must appear, in any order, with any other words around them | Middle ground between precise and wider reach; recommended by the course |
| Broad match | keyword with no punctuation | Any search containing at least one of the words | Not useful for e-commerce conversions; only for brand awareness |

- A keyword is what you bid on in Google Ads. A search query is the actual search term that triggers your ad. Your chosen match type determines which search queries will display your ad. [L0080, undated]
- Google Ads keyword match types (broad, phrase and exact) determine how closely a user's search must match a keyword before an ad can show for it. Choosing the match type controls how much irrelevant traffic a campaign receives. [L0844, 2025]
- Use exact match with square brackets, for example [trail bike X200]. It triggers only that wording in the same order, while plurals and small misspellings still trigger it. [L0880, 2021-22] **
- Use phrase match with quotation marks, for example "trail bike X200". It also triggers "buy trail bike X200 online", but not "X200 trail bike" because the word order changes. [L0880, 2021-22] **
- Use broad match modifier with a plus sign before each required word, for example +trail +bike +X200. It triggers any search that contains all three words in any order, so "X200 bike trail shop" triggers it, while "trail hiking boots" does not. [L0880, 2021-22] **
- Broad match without punctuation can show ads for unrelated searches that share only one word with the target, such as a generic word that appears in many searches. Forgetting the punctuation when you set keywords is a common cause of ad spend running out before anyone notices. [L0880, 2021-22] **
- Prefer exact match, phrase match and broad match modifier for search text ads, and avoid broad match. Broad match modifier gives extra reach over exact match while keeping the core product term fixed, which protects profitability. [L0880, 2021-22] **
- Broad match modifier is the recommended match type for specific product searches, because it captures variations without manual updates. [L0262, 2021-22] **
- For product-specific searches, target using exact match on the complete product phrase in square brackets, for example [Sunray Cayenne Sauna HL400D]. Exact match typically generates fewer impressions than broad match modifier but may deliver a higher conversion rate due to stronger relevance. [L0262, 2021-22] **
- Broad match modifier keywords can include more than two required terms. Start with the most critical product identifiers marked with plus signs (for example '+cayenne +hl400d'). You may optionally add terms like '+sauna' for higher specificity. Add extra terms only when the additional precision is needed to exclude irrelevant searches. [L0262, 2021-22] **
- In a phrase-match search campaign, ads can also trigger when the searcher adds words before or after the keyword phrase. Limit the keyword list to phrases that have already sold well in Shopping campaigns so the campaign stays narrow. [L0373, 2024+]
- Product-level keywords combine the brand with the model number and size, rather than using the broad category name. A TV seller would bid on a brand plus a size and model, not on the word TV, which keeps the cost per click much lower. [L0551, 2020] **
- Three keyword types for a high-ticket store should be targeted with search text ads: product ID keywords, brand plus product type, and product type plus specific descriptors that narrow results to one product or a very small number of products. Broader non-branded terms are the last option. [L0880, 2021-22] **
- Example for a store selling outdoor saunas: a model code plus brand (for example a brand with model code HX400) points straight to one product page; a brand plus product type (for example a brand plus "infrared sauna") targets a larger group; and a non-branded term with size and type (for example "four person outdoor sauna") needs a well-built collection page with content. [L0880, 2021-22] **
