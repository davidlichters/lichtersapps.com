### Interface-Change Mindset

- Google Ads implements significant changes almost monthly, so it requires regular monitoring of the official Google Ads Blog, Google Ads Updates and Google Ads Support to maintain campaign performance. [L0017, undated]
- The foundational principle for Google Ads campaigns, right message, right person, right time, remains valid regardless of interface or feature changes. [L0017, undated]
- Google Ads changes its features almost every month, and many changes affect core functionality, not just colors or layout. Check the account and its settings regularly, because a setup that worked a few months ago may behave differently now. [L0489, 2020-26]
- AdWords was renamed Google Ads and its interface was fully redesigned. Features may have moved to other menus or may work differently, so an older tutorial can send you to settings that no longer exist. [L0489, 2020-26]
- Google has posted over 2,000 changes to its official AdWords blog in recent years. Follow the official Google Ads announcements instead of relying only on older course material or forum answers. [L0489, 2020-26]
- Build large libraries of campaigns, keywords, audiences and email lists so you can rebuild quickly when Google changes its tools. A large stock of tested assets is a hedge against platform changes. [L0489, 2020-26]
- Older Google Ads course instructions for conversion tracking setup often no longer match the current interface and menu names. Before following a guide step by step, check the actual menus and settings in the live account and compare them with Google's current official documentation. [L0489, 2020-26]
- Platform changes in Google Ads were mainly visible in YouTube campaigns, so they had limited impact on a standard Shopping setup. Adjust only when you actually see a change in your own account. [L1090, 2021]
- Google Ads changes are not a reason to change a working campaign by themselves. Shopping layouts shift over time, and a different display on a later check is normal. [L0739, 2021-22]

### Skepticism Toward Google Recommendations, Reps and Agencies

#### Google's Recommendations

- The Google Ads optimization score should not be treated as actionable advice. Following Google's recommendations usually means raising budgets and opening up targeting, which can distort the numbers. [L0429, 2022]
- Google will call and push budget increases and wide targeting. Its recommendations generally mean spending more money, so they should be weighed against your own data. [L0429, 2022]
- Google's advice is shaped by its goal of increasing advertiser spend, which may differ from your goal of profit. Evaluate every recommendation against your own margins and results before acting on it. [L0489, 2020-26]
- If a single keyword wastes about $100 per week without producing sales, the usual fix is to pause that keyword. Google may instead suggest moving it into a Display campaign, which would keep spend flowing, so judge that suggestion against your own results. [L0489, 2020-26]
- Applying Google Ads recommendations automatically is risky, because they are generic, auto-generated and not specific to your business. Only apply a recommendation when you can see a business reason for it. [L0879, 2021-22]
- Do not follow Google's automatic recommendations (such as the one-click auto-apply suggestions) by default. Apply them only when the account data supports them. [L0969, 2022]
- Do not use automatically applied Google Ads recommendations or follow advice from Google account representatives who call or email the account, because these often add automated spend-raising settings. (2024) [L0856, 2024]
- Avoid following Google's advice without checking it. Changes that raise spend are the default direction of its suggestions. [L0429, 2022]

#### Google Reps

- Google reps often push higher spend, so treat their recommendation as a sales pitch. Test changes before committing a large budget. [L0801, 2021]
- Google account reps tend to recommend higher budgets, broad targeting and their own suggested campaigns. These increase spend without improving sales, so treat their recommendations with caution. (2023) [L0804, 2023]

#### Agencies

- Reject any agency proposal that departs radically from a proven high-ticket Google Ads approach, such as running broad top-of-funnel targeted search text ads, because such strategies will not work for high-ticket dropshipping. [L1053, 2021-22]

### Core Principles Summary

- Google is a comparison engine and a cost-per-click platform, so your product page is judged against competitors on price, title, reviews and delivery. [L0739, 2021-22; L0741, 2021-22]
- Relevance and CTR drive Ad Rank and cost per click, so clickable ads come before higher bids. [L1226, 2020-26; L1031, 2018]
- Google is strong at the bottom and middle of the funnel and weak at the top, so treat it as a demand-capture channel. [L0296, 2021+; L0741, 2021-22]

### What Google Ads Is

- Google Ads is a self-serve pay-per-click platform. Anyone can open an account, set up ads, and change or delete anything without contacting Google. It was called AdWords until July 2018, when it was renamed. [L1455, 2018+]
- With paid search you pay only when someone clicks, and you control bids, the search terms you appear for, and how your ad looks. This makes results easier to track and control than organic or unpaid content. [L1455, 2018+]
- Paid search reaches people who are actively looking for a product or solving a problem, unlike passive browsing of social feeds. Search intent is therefore the key reason to buy clicks for purchase-ready keywords. [L1455, 2018+]
- Google paid traffic is generally higher quality and more consistent than social media traffic, because shoppers actively search for products. Once an account is set up and working, its performance tends to be more predictable and reliable. [L1251, 2024+]
- Google works more like a pull channel than Meta, Instagram or TikTok. Those platforms mostly push products to people who did not ask for them, which favors impulse buys. On Google, shoppers already look for the product, so results depend on matching existing demand. [L1508, 2022+]
- Google Ads and Google Shopping are the paid channels used to attract buyers who search for the specific high-ticket products being sold. [L1481, 2021-22]
- Target customers who are actively searching for the exact product type sold, because they are ready to buy when they reach the site. [L1481, 2021-22]

#### Scale of the Platform

- In the 2018 lesson, the Google Display Network reached about 80% of US internet users. Google's ad revenue for 2017 was close to $100 billion, showing that pay-per-click advertising is its main revenue source. [L1455, 2018+]
- In 2018, on most search result pages for e-commerce keywords, ads took over more than 50% of the visible screen, often with only one organic placement shown. This indicates Google prioritizes shopping and ad placements for product-related searches. [L1455, 2018+]

### Google Ads Versus Other Paid Channels

- Google Ads is generally cheaper over the long term than Facebook and TikTok, which face frequent regulation, suspensions and account issues. This makes Google a stronger choice for a steady paid channel. [L1251, 2024+]
- Google Ads has a higher knowledge requirement at the start than Facebook Ads. Beginners should give themselves time to learn it, because rushing in without that learning often leads to frustration. [L1251, 2024+]

### The Auction: Price and Position

- Your cost per click usually lands slightly below your own bid and above the bid of the advertiser directly below you. Some advertisers in neighboring positions pay less than their own bid, so the result can vary. [L1226, 2020-26]
- Ads are shown in positions one to eight on the results page. Bidders whose Ad Rank is too low to reach that range do not appear on the page at all. Ads shown off the page receive no clicks, so a low Ad Rank means lost traffic even when the budget is available. [L1226, 2020-26]

**Worked auction example** [L1226, 2020-26]:

1. Bidder A has a bid of $4.00 and Quality Score 8, giving Ad Rank 32.
2. The bidder directly below has a bid of $3.45 and Quality Score 7, giving Ad Rank 24.15. This bidder sets the price.
3. Divide 24.15 by 8 and add one cent: about $3.03 per click, close to the stated $3.02.

**Low-bid example** [L1226, 2020-26]: A bidder at $0.50 with a very high Quality Score in a low slot paid $0.19 per click, because the bidder just below was bidding $0.20. A high Quality Score can keep costs low even at a small bid.

### Quality Score and CTR

- CTR is the dominant component of Quality Score, so it is the first thing to improve. Landing page relevance is a pass-or-fail check: it only needs to be relevant enough to pass. [L1226, 2020-26]
- Keyword relevance means the keyword must match the ad, and the ad must match the other keywords in the same ad group. All keywords and the ad should relate closely to each other. [L1226, 2020-26]
- In an account, Quality Scores below 6 are a major problem that should be fixed first. Such scores usually mean the ads are paying penalties on every click. [L1226, 2020-26]
- Higher CTR lowers your cost per click, because Google treats more-clicked ads as more relevant. That raises return on ad spend. [L1274, 2018]

**Case: client account, Google Ads, June 29 to July 19** [L1226, 2020-26]. The team did not change the landing page or anything else. It only worked on raising CTR.

| Metric | June 29 | July 19 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quality Score | 4.7 | 7.5 | up 60% |
| CTR | 5.6% | 11.65% | roughly doubled |
| Cost per click | $1.70 | $1.29 | down 24.6% |

The same traffic cost much less. The source states the 24.6% drop. The rounded prices ($1.70 and $1.29) give about 24.1%, so the stated figure probably comes from unrounded values. [L1226, 2020-26]

### Funnel Position: Bottom, Middle and Top

- Pay-per-click ads should send the right traffic, at the right time, at the right price to your website. This applies to every paid traffic source, including Google Ads, Facebook ads, banner ads and Pinterest ads. Ads do not have to produce the conversion themselves. [L1507, 2021-22]
- The right traffic is a person who is in the market for your product at some point and matches your ideal customer, even if they are not ready to buy today. In Google Ads, this means a search with relevant intent toward the products you sell. Not every searcher is the right traffic. [L1507, 2021-22]
- Right time refers to the customer's position in the sales funnel, not your own schedule. Top-of-funnel users do not yet know what they need, so buy-focused ads aimed at bottom-of-funnel users will not make sense to them. Match the ad message to where the person is in the funnel: top, middle or bottom. [L1507, 2021-22]
- Google Shopping works well for prospects who are most aware of their need or already know the product. It is poorly suited to prospects who are only aware of their problem, because it usually sends visitors to a product page, which often does not move a problem-aware visitor forward. [L1392, 2021-22]
- Paid search text ads can become too expensive to generate meaningful volume from completely unaware or problem-aware audiences. They are better suited to prospects who are actively searching for a solution. [L1392, 2021-22]
- If targeting is weak, the offer and site presentation are much less likely to convert, even if they are strong. Targeting does not have to hold the highest single share of conversion chances to be critical. Getting the audience right is necessary for the rest of the funnel to succeed. [L1507, 2021-22]

### Campaign Build Order

Campaign build order for a paid search funnel, from highest to lowest priority [L1386, 2020-26]:

1. Audience-based search campaigns, starting with bottom-of-funnel priorities.
2. Shopping ads with stacked targeting, including retargeting, serving shopping ads to people who previously visited the website.
3. Search text ads with stacked targeting on the winning keywords learned from shopping campaigns, so shopping results inform the search text ad keywords.
4. Display retargeting: dynamic retargeting and general retargeting.
5. Search campaigns without audiences (cold traffic), including shopping campaigns to cold traffic.
6. Middle-of-funnel audience search text campaigns to cold traffic, targeting people looking for information related to the products, such as buyer's guides or comparison guides.
7. Top-of-funnel cold traffic on display, YouTube and Gmail, targeting in-market users and demographics that resemble website visitors and customers, sent to a non-commercial page.

### Search Campaigns: Conflicting Advice

- one source advises against running standard Google search campaigns for a product store, because cost per click is very high even though search generates a lot of traffic. Use shopping-based campaigns instead. This applies to the early setup of a product store. [L1251, 2024+]
- one source says search campaigns have a place once a store is scaling, but not in the first stage. Start with Shopping and Performance Max only after the data supports it. [L1281, 2022]
- one source's build order places search after shopping and retargeting, and cold search without audiences comes at step 5. [L1386, 2020-26]

Both sources agree that search is not the first step. one source's warning applies to standard search in a product store's early setup. one source's sequence applies once shopping and retargeting data exist.

### When Google Suits a Product or Store

- Before adopting a Google Ads strategy, check which business type it was built for. A tactic that works for one model, such as a law firm or a high-margin service, may not work for a low-margin high-ticket dropshipping store. Always weigh the price of the product against what each click costs. [L1228, 2021-22]
- Some Google Shopping tactics work for low-ticket products because buyers face little risk and purchase quickly. The same tactics often fail for high-ticket products. Using the wrong tactic for your business type wastes both time and ad budget. [L1228, 2021-22]
- For a high-ticket dropshipping store, Google Ads is usually the first paid traffic source to build, ahead of other channels, and it remains a core part of the overall marketing system afterward. [L1494, 2021-22]
- A typical Google Ads setup for a high-ticket dropshipping store combines Google Shopping campaigns, search text ad campaigns and display network retargeting campaigns. [L1494, 2021-22]
- Business buyers search Google the same way consumers do, so target them with the same Google Ads setup. Also place banner ads on trade and industry websites that business buyers visit. [L1279, 2021]
- Shoppers compare Google Shopping listings that show near-identical photos and descriptions, so price is often the only factor for the first click. Matching the lowest price is needed to earn that click. Undercutting causes a price war where everyone loses. [L1293, 2014]
- Competing with Amazon works best with non-US stores. Amazon has about 80% market share in the US, and local markets where Amazon is weaker offer more room. A non-US store requires more effort to find suppliers, often about two extra weeks. [L1293, 2014]
- Before choosing a Google campaign type, check whether you are building your own brand or running a dropshipping-style store. That decides how much differentiation the product needs. Brand-building stores should move to their own designs and photos once a tested product shows demand. [L1508, 2022+]

### Mindset: The Ad Is Not the Sales Tool

- Thinking that a good ad alone causes sales is a common error. It leads to lower profitability and wasted spend. Evaluate ads by the clicks and visitor quality they produce, and by whether the site converts that traffic profitably. [L1507, 2021-22]

### Skepticism Toward Google's Recommendations

- Google account reps push more spend and services, such as search campaigns, that are not needed at the start. Follow the account data and the blueprint instead of their upsell advice. [L1281, 2022]

### Agencies, Reps and Outsourcing

- When judging a PPC agency or freelancer, assess three areas: activity (current account work and recent results), communication (response speed and clear, regular output), and technical expertise (depth of paid search knowledge). Use these as a screening checklist, not as a guarantee of results. [L1262, 2015+]
- Google ranks agencies on performance and customer care, so a Google Partner status with a top-tier standing is one signal of an agency's recent activity. Verify the status directly in Google's partner directory before relying on it. [L1262, 2015+]
- A WordStream study ranks advertisers on performance, customer care and account activity. A top 5% placement is cited as a benchmark for a strong account. The figure is a vendor claim, so confirm it against the study itself before using it as a hiring criterion. [L1262, 2015+]
- A PPC-only agency tends to have deeper technical expertise than a generalist agency, because the team builds its strategies from years of paid search work. When vetting one, ask how long they have run PPC and whether paid search is their only service. [L1262, 2015+]
- When screening a PPC agency for a store, ask whether they have managed PLA campaigns specifically, not just general AdWords accounts. [L1262, 2015+]
- Ad agencies that cold-email newly created Shopify stores usually sign up many clients without improving results. Do not pay an outside agency until the owner has made ads profitable. [L1281, 2022]
- Set up Google Ads yourself first and get them to at least profitable before considering an outside service. Outsourcing comes after the owner knows what works. [L1281, 2022]
- A Google Ads ROAS of 3.26 was reported at one point. The account was outsourced to an agency after the speaker had set it up. [L1440, 2021+]

#### Further details

- Product-focused Google ads that send buyers straight to product pages should be used first because those people already want to buy. Lead generation ads need more money, content and time to pay off, so they are a later addition. [L0471, 2024]

- 1. Learn the theory of pay-per-click advertising and the buyer psychology behind search campaigns. 2. Set up and launch the Google Ads and Google Shopping campaigns so they start generating traffic. 3. Move into a management phase, where ongoing monitoring and adjustment of campaigns is the main source of learning. [L0713, 2021-22]
