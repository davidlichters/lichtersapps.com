### Starting Daily Budgets (incl. weekly click target: about 100 clicks, 2 to 3 sales a week)

- Weekly click target for the starting budget (January 2019 coaching call): start at 20 to 30 USD a day, then raise the budget with the inch-up method (see bidding page 28) until the store gets about 100 targeted clicks per week, counted across all campaigns combined; that should bring roughly 2 to 3 sales a week. [L0356, 2019, 42:51 to 43:34; older advice, newer courses may differ] Same line on https://lichtersapps.com/ai/ecom/deep/10-google-ads-and-shopping/04-unit-economics-before-spending-break-even-target-r.md (section "Expected Clicks and Conversions").

**Maximum starting budgets**

- A maximum starting daily budget of about $30 is suggested for Google campaigns, and smaller starts are acceptable. (2024) [L0360, 2024]
- For a new Google Shopping account, cap the daily budget at about 30 per day; 50 per day is too high early on. [L0247, 2021]
- In the 2021 Google Shopping setup described, a new account should start with a maximum shared daily budget of about 30 per day, and 50 per day is considered too high for a store with a small uploaded catalog. The catalog should first be expanded so Google has more products to optimize across. [L0247, 2021]
- Start Google Shopping with a $30 per day budget, with the option to start at $10 per day when money is tight. Budgets for Google Shopping and Meta remarketing together came to about $30 to $35 per day in 2023. The minimum target ROAS stayed at 10X. (2023) [L0804, 2023]
- Do not set a Google Ads daily budget far below $10 per day, because campaigns need enough budget to spend and collect data. The usual starting point is about $30 per day, and $10 per day is the lowest reasonable start. (2023) [L0804, 2023]
- Ignore Google's suggested budget increases, such as a recommended $90 per day, because those suggestions aim to increase your spend. Set budgets based on your own sales data. (2026) [L0808, 2026]

**Starting formula**

- Starting daily ad budget = $15 (bestseller shared budget for Google alpha and beta) + $5 (everything-else shared budget for Google alpha and beta) + $5 (Meta remarketing) = $25 per day. Microsoft and Bing are excluded from the starting budget. (2024) [L0360, 2024]

**Beginner launch sequence (2019 example)**

- Starting budget of $200 consists of the $100 credit plus $100 of the store owner's own money. The 2019 credit for new Shopify stores was $100. [L1002, 2019]

| Channel | Budget | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Google Shopping (product listing ads) | $180 | $20 per day, running about 9 days |
| Facebook remarketing (dynamic product ads) | $20 | $5 per day, retargeting visitors who came from Google |

The call estimated total spend at about $215, and the budget is spread over several days so Google has time to collect data. [L1002, 2019]

**Procedure (2019)**

1. Finish setting up and optimizing the store before spending on ads.
2. Set the Google Shopping daily budget to $20 instead of $30 to $50, so the budget lasts about nine days.
3. Do not spend the whole budget on day one; allow several days of data.
4. Set a Facebook remarketing campaign with a simple dynamic product ad group at $5 per day, retargeting people who came from Google.
5. Start the daily audit process from day one. [L1002, 2019]

**Procedure**

1. Start a Google Shopping campaign.
2. Set a daily budget of $25 to $50 when working with a starting budget of $500 to $1,500.
3. Set a target ROAS of 3x to 4x, for example aiming for $200 in sales from $50 of spend.
4. Let the products appear in Shopping; no extra campaign structure is needed at the start. [L1508, 2022+]

Source conflict: the one source procedure above uses $25 to $50 per day with a $500 to $1,500 starting budget, while the one source sources use $5 to $30 per day for new stores. Both are given because they describe different starting budgets.

### Monthly Caps and Test Budgets

- A starting Google Ads budget of about $1,000 per month is typical. This equals approximately $33 per day. [L0059, undated]
- A recommended starting budget for a new Shopping (PPC) account is at least $1,000 per month, which equals $33 per day. [L1341, 2020-26]
- A first Google Ads budget of about $500 to $1,000 per month is reasonable. Sales should show within a few days if the campaign is set up correctly. [L0967, 2020]
- A new store can start with about $500 to $1,000 per month in ads, which works out to roughly $15 to $35 per day. Spending above that level should come from reinvested store profit. [L0468, 2020]
- A $1,000 first month is based on a $30 per day Google Shopping budget. Starting at $10 per day is fine but gives slower data. (2023) [L0804, 2023]
- A first month test budget of about 1,000 dollars can be spent as 30 dollars per day on Google Shopping shared between alpha and beta, 3 to 5 dollars per day on Google remarketing and 3 to 5 dollars per day on Facebook remarketing, then scaled once conversions appear. [L1113, 2022]
- With a $3,000 monthly budget, $2,700 goes to Google Shopping. The remaining $300 goes to remarketing, split into $200 per month for Facebook dynamic product ads and $100 per month for Google remarketing. [L0854, 2022]
- With a total ad budget of 100 USD, put about 90 USD into Google Ads and about 10 USD into Facebook dynamic remarketing. [L0961, 2020]
- Budget split between channels: about 90% of advertising budget goes to Google Ads, and about 10% goes to Facebook remarketing to bring people back to the store. [L1090, 2021]
- Alpha and Beta test split (2019 example): Alpha campaign (high-priority, high-bid, converting keywords) set to $30 daily budget. Beta campaign (high-priority, low-bid, weak keywords) set to $17 daily budget. [L0243, 2019]
- A $25 Google budget can be split so that each of the alpha and beta campaigns gets $12.50. (2024) [L0360, 2024]
- Daily Google Ads budget is roughly the monthly budget divided by 30. A starting budget of $1,000 per month equals about $33 per day. [L0663, 2019]
- Adjust the starting budget to the cost per click; see the cost-per-click rule under Expected Clicks and Conversions. [L1341, 2020-26]

### Scaling Rules

- Start Google Ads with small budgets and optimize every two weeks. Starting with large budgets and letting campaigns run without tweaks is what causes thousands of dollars to be spent with zero conversions. (2024) [L0666, 2024]
- Start Google Shopping campaigns with small initial budgets. Scale spending upward only as performance data proves profitability. [L0163, undated]
- Buy data first: the starting budget is for collecting data on which products get clicks and sales, not for scaling. [L1090, 2021]
- ** Starting with a small budget is for collecting data: impressions, clicks and purchases show which products people find and buy. Use that data to add negatives and adjust cost per click per product, then increase budget once return on ad spend improves a few days after optimizing. [L1090, 2021]
- Scale the budget gradually with ROAS above 10x, and only as profitable campaigns prove the ratio. [L0270, 2021]
- Products with a strong MAP-to-wholesale spread and good conversion are the only ones that should receive increased spend. [L0966, 2025]
- The scaling procedure for Google Ads budgets is:
  1. Set a target ROAS for the current budget, for example 15x on $2,000 spend.
  2. Improve efficiency until that target holds.
  3. Only then scale the budget, expecting ROAS to fall somewhat as spend grows. [L1111, 2020]
- Increase the budget as long as ROAS holds; the ratio is the signal to raise spend. [L1090, 2021]
- Add money in steps: after the first 2 to 4 weeks of learning, judge the campaign on the data and do not scale on day one. [L0808, 2026]
- The learning phase for the ROAS target is the first two to three weeks of a campaign. [L0472, 2025]
- Use the 2019 approach: scale up the campaigns that are profitable while cutting the ones that are not. [L0356, 2019]
- Keep the test budget unchanged until it is profitable, apply the Google Ads optimizations, and only then scale the budget by reinvesting profits from existing sales. (2025) [L0667, 2025]
- Scale spending for a product only after it proves profitable and reinvest store profits into ads once the free credits are used. [L0468, 2020]
- The budget for the second half of the first month is raised only after conversions appear, not before. [L1113, 2022]
- Budget can increase with a profitable ROAS, with no upward limit set in the 2023 source. [L0274, 2023]

### Ad Credits and Promotional Vouchers

**Credit and voucher offers**

- Google Ads and Microsoft Ads offer discount codes for new advertiser accounts. When first setting up advertising, ask about or search for promotional codes to reduce your initial ad spend. (2024) [L0253, 2024]
- Use Google Ads vouchers, which provide free credit to start. [L0854, 2022]
- When creating a Google Ads account, select Offer A to spend $500 and receive a $500 credit. No money is lost if spending does not reach $500. [L0184, undated]
- Google Ads promotional offers come as coupon codes, either a fixed dollar credit or a matching offer. Matching offers change over time, so check the current terms before signing up. [L0717, 2020-26]
- A current matching offer in the US pays back spend up to $150, but only if the spend happens within the first 30 days after the Google Ads account is created. Verify the exact amount and window in the offer terms shown at the bottom of the coupon page. [L0717, 2020-26]
- The matching offer window of 30 days starts when the Google Ads account is created, not when the coupon code is received. Create the account only when the spend for the next 30 days is ready. [L0717, 2020-26]
- Google's sign-up promotion for new Google Ads accounts (as shown in 2020s lessons, may change): spend $500 within the first 60 days to receive $500 in free advertising credit. Higher tiers of $1,000 and $1,500 spend are unrealistic for a beginner budget, so the lowest tier is the sensible choice. [L0718, 2020-26]
- The 2024 offers are: a Google Ads credit of $1,000 after $1,500 in ad spend, and a Microsoft Ads credit of $500 after $500 in spend, with both requiring spending by November 30. [L0360, 2024]
- The Google Ads credit offer ($500 credit for $500 spend in 60 days) and the Bing offer ($500 credit for $250 spend) together can add up to $750 in free ad credit. This justifies spending at least the required amount during the launch window. (2024) [L0971, 2024]
- Source conflict on the Microsoft or Bing credit: one 2024 source gives $500 after $500 in spend ([L0360, 2024]), and another gives $500 after $250 in spend ([L0971, 2024]). Use the terms shown in the offer itself. [L0360, 2024; L0971, 2024]
- Source conflict on the Google credit window: the same $500 for $500 offer is described with a 60-day window ([L0718, 2020-26; L0971, 2024]), while the matching offer described in [L0717, 2020-26] has a 30-day window. Check which offer type is shown. [L0718, 2020-26; L0971, 2024; L0717, 2020-26]
- ** Google, Bing and Facebook ad credits offered at signup were worth about $200 to $300 in total for a new store. These are 2020 offers, so check current offers before relying on them. [L0468, 2020]
- ** After the free ad credits are used, set aside a couple hundred dollars for ads and then reinvest store profits into ads. [L0468, 2020]

### Bonus Offers and Conversion Cost

- A bonus gift can lower advertising cost, not only raise sales. When the bonus removes the reason to compare prices, buyers purchase on their first visit instead of leaving, searching again and clicking a paid ad a second time, so the cost per sale falls. [L1293, 2014]
- You pay for every click in Google Ads, including clicks from visitors who never buy. Irrelevant or poorly matched traffic therefore costs money with no sales in return. [L1228, 2021-22]
- Irrelevant and wasted spend is reduced by excluding unprofitable products and by adding negatives after data is collected. [L0748, 2020-26; L1090, 2021]

#### Further details

- In Google Ads, the ad for a niche with a higher ad spend should show roughly 1,100 sessions for about 300 dollars per day; the question to ask is whether the traffic converts into sales, not just whether the clicks are high. [L0964, 2023]
