## Writing shipping, returns, refund and warranty policies

### Before you start: supplier terms and policy basis

- Base the store's return policy on each supplier's return policy. If a supplier allows 30-day free returns, the store can offer the same. If a supplier allows no returns, mark those products on the site as not eligible for returns. [L0551, 2020]
- Read the signed supplier approval documents. They state each supplier's return terms, and the store's return policy is set around them. [L1113, 2022]
- Your return window must match what the supplier allows. Read the supplier's agreement documents before you set the policy, and show any longer supplier window on the product pages. [L0855, 2023]
- Return responsibility for heavy items depends on each supplier's policy, which you learn when the supplier approves you. [L0663, 2019]
- If suppliers charge return shipping or restocking fees, state those fees in the shipping and returns policy and base the policy on the supplier's terms. [L1275, 2019]
- Return policies differ between brands. The store's returns policy should summarise all supplier policies, and each supplier's policy should be noted in the retailer paperwork. [L1111, 2020]
- Before listing a brand, verify its return process. Brands with supplier-level support and clear policies reduce return and warranty problems. [L1111, 2020]
- Expect higher return rates in some niches, and avoid those niches when choosing products. [L1283, 2023]
- Compare your return policy with the top two or three competitors in the niche. A policy with refunds within 30 days without a reason, a restocking fee (for example 15% to 20%) and customer-paid return shipping is acceptable if competitors do the same. You can set the fee lower to stand out. [L0805, 2024]
- To set return shipping fees, look at the return policies of your top future competitors and model yours on them. Choose whether to charge actual cost or a fixed amount. [L1285, 2024]

### Shipping policy content

- The shipping policy must be specific enough for a reviewer to understand within a minute. It should state when orders ship after purchase (a range is acceptable), the average transit time, and the shipping cost, including whether shipping is free. [L0377, 2020-26]
- Vague shipping policies are a known cause of compliance problems. Avoid any wording that could be read as misleading. [L0377, 2020-26]
- Use this template for the shipping policy text:

```
Once your purchase goes through, you'll get an order confirmation email. We dispatch every order within [X] days of placement and send you an email with the tracking number as soon as it leaves us. Across the United States, every order ships free through [carrier(s) you typically use]. Choose free shipping and transit takes [X] to [Y] business days. If the store offers faster options, add this line: customers who need quicker delivery can pick [upgraded delivery option] at checkout, and those expedited rates are calculated live at checkout. If the store ships internationally at no charge, add this line: every international order ships free, and with free international shipping transit runs [X] to [Y] weeks.
```
[L0377, 2020-26]

- Reference figures from the lesson's US example: dispatch within 3 days, US transit of 3 to 5 business days, international transit of 1 to 3 weeks, carrier USPS. [L0377, 2020-26]
- If actual average shipping times are not yet known, check competitors' shipping pages to estimate transit times. The policy can be updated later. It only needs to exist and be accurate from the start. [L0377, 2020-26]
- A high-ticket dropshipping store usually does not ship internationally. Publish the international shipping paragraph only if the store actually ships abroad. A store that ships worldwide should state the international transit time in weeks. [L0377, 2020-26]
- The shipping page should list the expected shipping time for each supplier or brand, because suppliers ship at different speeds. Update these times as they become clear, for example after a few weeks of order data. [L0910, 2015]
- Shipping time should be stated in the store's policies, along with contact methods, the return policy and product descriptions, because missing details are a common reason for a store review. (2023) [L0855, 2023]
- Keep store policies short and clear, not long legal texts. The lesson's policies are only a few sentences each. [L0377, 2020-26]
- Many shoppers do not know a new store. Write the shipping policy (cost, delivery time, carrier) and the returns policy in plain English, and make them better than competitors' policies. [L0894, 2020-26]
- The contact page must offer at least two different ways to reach the business, such as a phone number and a business email. A contact form may be added, but it does not replace the two methods. [L0377, 2020-26]
- Until dedicated support mailboxes exist, use support@ followed by your domain (for example support@yourdomain.com) in the policy templates. Replace it with the real mailbox once it exists. [L1048, 2020-26]

### Shipping and returns page placement

- Shoppers read the shipping policy before they spend a lot of money, so it must be easy to find and complete. Place shipping information where buyers look before ordering. [L1130, 2020-26]
- The returns and exchange page answers what happens if the buyer is unhappy, which buyers ask before purchasing. Keep it visible before checkout to reduce hesitation. [L1130, 2020-26]
- The shipping page can link to the returns policy so customers find return terms next to delivery information. A short shipping statement with a link to the returns page reduces questions. [L1264, 2021-22]
- Leaving the return policy off the page leaves shoppers worried about damaged or unwanted items. State easy returns clearly on the page. [L1207, undated]
- Most merchandise sold by mail ships subject to return. Return rights are a standard part of direct response selling, and a clear return policy removes the buyer's risk. [L1261, undated]
- Do not open the returns and exchanges policy by default, especially a harsh one. Place it in an expandable section for curious buyers, because a long multi-step return process can scare off undecided visitors. [L1016, 2026]
- Pre-footer trust column: use a clear header such as "Easy returns" and link it to the returns policy page once that page exists. Until then, leave the link empty. [L1309, 2020-26]
- Policy pages such as shipping or terms are needed before they are linked in the pre-footer. In the example, a temporary link to terms of service was used and should be replaced with the correct page later. [L1309, 2020-26]
- Publish the shipping policy and the returns policy on the store, and make sure everyone handling orders has read both before working on them. [L1291, undated]

### Free shipping, add-ons and accessories

- A free standard shipping offer on all products is a common store policy. Expedited shipping or white glove delivery can be offered as paid add-ons. [L1283, 2023]
- Conditional free shipping is set up by creating separate shipping profiles on the Shopify shipping page, each with the criteria that must be met for it to apply. [L1283, 2023]
- Accessories with flat-rate shipping should be sold only as add-ons to a main product. Do not list accessories as standalone products or include them in ads and product feeds. [L1283, 2023]
- When offering free shipping, a customer return means the shipping cost already paid may not be recovered. Design the return policy around each supplier's return rules, not a single store-wide policy. [L0837, 2025]
- Advertising free shipping while charging customers for return shipping is acceptable if the return costs are clearly disclosed in the policy. Clarity in advance prevents complaints about shipping. [L1275, 2019]

### Return window

- Return windows should always have an end date, because unlimited free returns make it impossible to tell when a product is profitable. Match the store's return window to the supplier's policy, for example 30 days when the supplier allows 30 days. [L1069, 2019]
- US average return window for physical products is about 30 days. Supplier windows can be 14, 30, 60 or up to 180 days. (2023) [L0855, 2023]
- No general US legal return period exists for ordinary products that the store must offer. This is general information, not legal advice. (2023) [L0855, 2023]
- A UK retailer may be required to offer a 14-day return policy for consumer goods, so check local rules before setting the policy. The US has no equivalent blanket rule for most products. [L0963, 2022]

- Return windows differ by brand. A general 30-day window applies to most items in new condition, but a brand can have its own window. [L0556, 2021-22]
- For a 30-day window, the one source template reads "You have [number of days] days from the purchase date to initiate a return." Choose the start point (purchase date or delivery) and state it in the policy. [L1250, 2026; L1327, 2026]
- Match the store's return window to the supplier. When suppliers differ, use the window in the store's general policy and show the exception on product pages. [L0855, 2023; L0470, 2023]
- Customer can return within 30 days on the store-wide default, with a 10% restocking fee and customer pays return shipping (example brand structure). [L0972, 2025]

### Return conditions and eligibility

- Returned items must go back in original packaging with all parts. [L0556, 2021-22]
- A returned item must be unused, kept in the state it arrived in, and still in its original packaging. Any return needs a receipt or other proof of purchase. [L1250, 2026]
- Change-of-mind returns can be accepted even when special packaging has been opened, as long as the product itself is in original condition. Record this condition because it determines whether a return is accepted. [L0893, 2021-22]
- For change-of-mind returns, the store's policy should copy the supplier's policy for gently used products that customers simply change their mind about, because the store cannot send the item back to the supplier under different terms. (2023) [L0855, 2023]
- Return conditions to state in the policy: [L0539, undated] "New, unused, in original packaging, all accessories included" is the example condition set. [L0539, undated]
- Return wording for a dropshipping store reads: "Items must be returned in their original condition." [L0855, 2023]
- Record the return conditions as one clear rule set per supplier when they differ. [L0893, 2021-22]

### Non-returnable items and excluded products

- Typical exceptions to change-of-mind returns include sanitation products that were opened or installed, electronics unless in sealed original packaging, items sold by the metre, specially ordered items, and installed, modified or used items. Free freight included in the price can be deducted from the refund. [L0893, 2021-22]
- Bidets and toilets (personal hygiene products) cannot be returned once opened or installed. [L0556, 2021-22]
- Food and flowers (perishables), gift cards, and health and personal-care products cannot be sent back. This is the one source sample exclusion. [L1250, 2026]
- If a product is an ingestible, a hygiene item, a pre-owned good or a final-sale item, the return policy must state that. These categories are often excluded from standard refunds, so the exclusion must appear on the policy page and not be left for the buyer to discover. [L1188, 2026]
- Refunds apply only to full-price items. Discounted goods are not refundable in the sample policy. [L1250, 2026]
- A supplier with a no-return policy should be matched on your store. Add a note to each product page from that supplier stating it is not eligible for returns unless the item arrives defective or damaged, using the supplier's exact wording. (2023) [L0835, 2023]
- Products that the supplier does not take back must be marked as non-returnable on the policy page and on each product page. This prevents disputes when a customer later asks for a return. [L1085, 2018]
- Suppliers without a return policy can still be sold, but the product pages and the return policy page must state that those products are not eligible for return. [L1113, 2022]
- If a supplier refuses returns for a product, either mark the product as not eligible for returns on its page, or ship the product to a fulfilment centre you control if you want to sell it. [L0801, 2021]
- If a supplier refuses returns for damaged items, do not accept returns for those products either. State the exclusion in the return policy and also on that supplier's product pages. (2025) [L0667, 2025]
- When some suppliers accept no returns, state that clearly on their product pages where customers see it before buying. [L0968, 2021]
- Products from suppliers that do not accept returns should be excluded from the return policy. Add a note on those product pages and in the shipping and returns tab that the items are not eligible for returns. [L0962, 2021]
- Products from suppliers that accept no returns must be named as not eligible for return on the shipping and returns page and on their product pages. [L0963, 2022]
- Changing their mind on a no-return product: "This item is not eligible for returns unless it arrives defective or damaged. [Use the supplier's exact return wording]" [L0835, 2023]

Exclusion wording template:

```
This item is not eligible for returns unless it arrives defective or damaged. [Use the supplier's exact return wording]
```
[L0835, 2023]

```
Exclusion: products from [Supplier name] are eligible for return only if they arrive defective or damaged.
```
[L0835, 2023]

- Procedure for adding a no-return policy for a supplier that does not accept returns:
  1. Update the shipping and returns policy page with a note listing the affected supplier or products.
  2. Add the same note to every product page from that supplier, in the shipping and returns tab.
  3. Use wording such as "This product is not eligible for returns; all sales are final."
  [L1085, 2018]
- Procedure for a non-returnable brand (2025):
  1. Keep your general return policy for all brands.
  2. Add an exception at the bottom of the policy stating that products from the non-returnable brand are not eligible for return.
  3. On each product page of that brand, replace the standard return text with a statement that the product is not eligible for return.
  [L0837, 2025]
- Follow the procedure in [L0837, 2025] when a brand has no returns. [L0837, 2025]
- Do not use a general no-return rule for a supplier whose terms are otherwise general. Put the exclusion only on that supplier's pages. [L0968, 2021]

### Restocking fees

- Restocking fees and return shipping costs depend on each supplier's return policy. Customers usually pay return shipping, but this varies by supplier. [L0247, 2021]
- A restocking fee should match the supplier's restocking fee, not be added on top of it. The purpose is to avoid losing money on returns, not to make profit from them. (2025) [L0972, 2025]
- Restocking fees and return terms can differ per brand. Show them on the general return policy page, and optionally in expandable sections on each product page. (2025) [L0972, 2025]
- Use this structure for a brand-specific return policy (2025):

```
Our return policies vary by brand. Please see the list below for the return policy of each brand.

Brand A products can be returned within 30 days. A 10% restocking fee applies, and you are responsible for return shipping costs.

Brand B products can be returned within 60 days. A 20% restocking fee applies, and you are responsible for return shipping costs.

Replace the brand names, return windows and fees with the exact terms from each supplier.
```
[L0972, 2025]

| 15% to 20% | Acceptable if competitors do the same (2024). | [L0805, 2024] |
| 15% | Large items. | [L0556, 2021-22] |
| 15% | Steam shower or bathtub that fails local inspection, unit still in original packaging. | [L0556, 2021-22] |

- Restocking fees can also apply to damaged packaging, to inspection and testing of appliances, and to items with multiple components. Expect fees beyond the standard percentage in these cases. [L0893, 2021-22]
- If a store charges no restocking fee, state this clearly and make it prominent in the refund policy. Google views a zero-dollar restocking fee favourably. [L0724, 2020-26]
- If a customer asks for a return fee receipt, a restocking fee can be charged and documented in the return policy so that customers can see it before buying. (2023) [L1094, 2023]

### Return shipping and who pays

- For a non-defective return, the customer normally pays the return shipping fee. Damaged or defective items are replaced at no cost to the customer. Returns should stay a very small share of total sales. (2021) [L1279, 2021]
- Return shipping costs are always charged to the customer. (2024) [L0253, 2024]
- Customers usually pay return shipping. For a damaged item, the customer does not pay for the return. [L0663, 2019]
- Free returns are the default for stores of this kind, and customers pay for return shipping on almost all stores. Restocking fees are the alternative. (2023) [L0964, 2023]
- If a supplier offers free return shipping, absorb the cost and do not charge the customer. Otherwise, the customer pays. (2023) [L0251, 2023]
- For damaged or defective returns, the supplier should pay the return shipping. Supplier applications may ask who pays, and the answer is the supplier. (2023) [L0855, 2023]
- If a store-caused problem (wrong or defective item) occurs, the store can cover all return shipping and refund the original shipping cost. Keep the store-caused case separate from buyer's remorse. [L0539, undated]
- Store covers all shipping for defective or wrong items. [L1048, 2020-26]
- Heavy products: the refund policy must state that a customer who requests a refund pays the shipping cost to send the item out and the return shipping, even if the original order had free shipping. This can be about 35 dollars each way for heavy products. State this on the shipping page, in the order confirmation and in the terms. [L0910, 2015]
- Shipping cost for the return: "Customer is responsible" for buyer's remorse; "Store covers all shipping" for defective or wrong items (example input). [L0539, undated]
- For a buyer's remorse return, the customer can be made responsible for return shipping, and the restocking fee terms must be stated. [L0539, undated]
- For warranty claims, the customer returned the faulty item to the seller at their own cost. The seller paid to send the repaired or replacement item back once a manufacturing fault was confirmed. If no manufacturing fault was found, the customer paid the freight for the item's return. [L0893, 2021-22]
- Free return postage in the one source example was limited to 3 months for spare parts and 12 months for other items. If no manufacturing fault was found, the customer paid the freight back. [L0893, 2021-22]
- one source sample: the customer pays return postage, and that postage is not reimbursed. [L1250, 2026]
- Shopify return rules: return shipping cost is set to "Customer provides return shipping" or "Flat rate return shipping". [L1250, 2026]
- Shopify default: the customer pays for return shipping. [L1327, 2026]

### Prepaid return labels

- If prepaid return labels are used, the policy should state whether the label cost is deducted from the refund. [L0539, undated]
- Example input: "Yes, cost deducted from refund." [L0539, undated]
- Exchanges use prepaid shipping labels in the default settings for a new dropship store. [L1048, 2020-26]
