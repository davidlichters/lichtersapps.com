### Instagram Trial Reels

- Find Trial Reel insights at Profile > Reels tab > 'Drafts and trial reels' > 'Trial reels', where all uploaded Trial Reels are listed. Open a reel and tap 'View more insights' to see the full data. [L1397, 2025-26]
- The Trial Reel insights screen shows the banner 'This trial reel is being shown to non-followers' and a 'Share to everyone' button. Use the button only after the insights show the reel is performing well. [L1397, 2025-26]
- Example Trial Reel insights, for comparison with a typical reel:

| Metric | Example Trial Reel | Typical reel |
|---|---|---|
| Views | 7,973 | n/a |
| Share of viewers who are non-followers | 100% | n/a |
| Accounts reached | 6,730 (about 6,700) | n/a |
| Total watch time | 14h 41m 0s | n/a |
| Average watch time | 7 seconds | n/a |
| Watch-through rate | 74.1% | 68.6% |

[L1397, 2025-26]

- Track watch time, interactions relative to views, the like ratio and the view rate past the first 3 seconds. Compare them against your typical reel, not against absolute numbers. [L1397, 2025-26]
- In the example, 8,000 views was high performance for a Trial Reel, and 3,000 views still meant reaching 3,000 people who had never seen the account. Viral reach is unlikely on Trial Reels, because they reach only a small group at first. [L1397, 2025-26]
- Share a Trial Reel to the main feed only when its watch-through rate and interactions beat your typical reel. In the example, a 74.1% watch-through rate against a 68.6% typical rate and a 7-second average watch time signalled that the reel would likely do well on the feed. [L1397, 2025-26]
- Trial Reel insights take time to build, because the reel reaches a limited, non-follower group. Do not judge a reel on its first hours of data. [L1397, 2025-26]
- In Instagram's reel insights, the view rate past the first 3 seconds can be filtered by All, Followers or Non-followers. For a Trial Reel, read the Non-followers value, since that is the audience it reaches. [L1397, 2025-26]
- For a Trial Reel, use the 'Accounts reached' figure to see how many distinct non-follower accounts it reached, and compare that with total views. Judge the reel by how many new accounts it reached, not by view count alone. [L1397, 2025-26]

### Revenue per million views and product decisions

- Revenue per million views (dollars per million views) = total sales divided by the number of millions of views. Example: 10 million views and 7,000 USD in sales gives 700 USD per million. Use it to compare products and angles, not just raw views. [L0501, 2022]
- Example: 5,000 USD in sales on 8 million views gives 625 USD per million views, below the 1,000 USD benchmark. [L1444, 2022]

#### Benchmarks

Sources give different thresholds. Each applies to a different market or purpose, as shown below.

| Context | Benchmark | Ids |
|---|---|---|
| USA, as seen by the author | About 1,000-3,000 USD per million views; sometimes 3,000-4,000 with strong angles; the low end can go under 100 USD | [L0501, 2022] |
| High-income countries such as the UK | Similar to the USA | [L0501, 2022] |
| Other, lower-income countries | Roughly 100-300 USD per million. The author aims at an average of at least 1,000 USD per million, with 500 USD per million as the minimum acceptable | [L0501, 2022] |
| Viable product (general) | About 1,000 USD in sales per million views. Products earning around 100 USD per million are not worth continuing and should be dropped | [L1444, 2022] |
| Rough observed example of a viral product | About 2,000 USD revenue per million views | [L0655, undated] |
| TikTok product videos, rough benchmark for testing | About 500 USD in sales per million views | [L0706, 2020+] |
| Same product, different videos | Roughly 100 USD per million on one video and a few thousand USD per million on another | [L0657, undated] |

- A 10 million view video should produce at least some sales. In a higher-income country it should produce more than 1,000 USD. Zero sales from a large view count is a red flag. Check the geographic split of views, since a viral video mostly seen in lower-converting countries will not sell well even with many views. [L0501, 2022]
- Check where views come from before judging a product. A large share from high-income countries (USA, UK, Canada) converts much better than views from other countries. Read the sales-per-view benchmark per geographic market. [L0501, 2022]
- Track views and sales together and check whether they match. Strong views with no sales point to a problem with the product, store, price or offer rather than with traffic. [L1444, 2022]
- Comments asking to buy do not equal purchases. Many visitors and commenters who say they want to buy never complete a purchase, so judge the product by actual sales and store sessions. [L1444, 2022]
- Views do not equal sales. The same product on the same account can convert at roughly 100 USD per million views on one video and at a few thousand USD per million on another, depending on how well the video shows the product. Track revenue per view for each video, not view count alone. [L0657, undated]
- Organic views on a viral product are not a conversion result. Judge a product by orders per thousand views and by whether it is a real purchase, not by follower growth or comment volume alone. [L0655, undated]
- A product that loses views late in week 2, for example from several thousand views in week 1 down to a few hundred, is a bad sign. Start a new account if you want to keep the product, otherwise drop it. [L0501, 2022]
- Read a low sales-per-view result carefully. If revenue per view is poor but still steady after 24 hours, the videos may have died down. If old views converted well but have stopped, the product can still be improved rather than dropped. [L0501, 2022]
- Views do not reliably predict sales. A video with tens of millions of views can sell very little, while a product with a good offer can convert well from far fewer views. Judge a product by its orders and conversion, not by its viral reach. [L0372, 2022]
- Price check before testing: compare the average unit price with the ad's stated price, because a price mismatch in creative lowers conversion and makes results hard to read. Track views, revenue and the number of creators or ad variations to see which parts of the structure matter. [L0707, 2026]
- Revenue per 1,000 views = ad revenue divided by views, multiplied by 1,000. For three variants of the same winning creative:
  - 165.67k revenue on 8.38m views gives about 19.8 per 1,000 views
  - 106.90k on 4.78m gives about 22.4
  - 64.66k on 2.99m gives about 21.6

  Rank variants by this number, not by views alone. The variant with the most views had the lowest revenue per 1,000 views. [L0707, 2026]

#### Cases

- Viral case: a store uploaded 12 TikTok videos and went viral on day four. The video reached about 1.5 million views in the first 12 hours and about 600 sales that day. The video itself converted poorly because it was a joke with a negative angle, and sales were about 500 the next day. The account was then shadowbanned and sales slowed sharply. [L0372, 2022]
- Race Car case: an honesty-angle video whose message was that the product does not even work had about 1.8 million views and gained about 1,500 followers in 24 hours. The store price was 30 USD and only 2 orders were confirmed, against an expected 10 to 15 orders. Lesson: the marketing angle drew attention but did not convert, and the product itself was a weak buy. [L0655, undated]
- Facebook ad case: a Facebook ad for a simple household product reached about 2.3 million views and drew strong comments and buyer interest. The creator saved the video and planned to test it both as a TikTok ad and as an organic post. The video was about 16 seconds long, used background music and a call to action, and showed the product solving a small everyday problem. The product's European AliExpress listing had over 5,000 orders, and another listing of the same product showed about 213 orders. [L0706, 2020+]
- Low-converter case: in one reviewed case, a product video reached about 10 million views and produced around 3 sales, while another account with about 12 million views produced 10 orders. Ratios of only a few sales per million views were the signal that the product was a weak converter, despite the huge view counts. [L1385, undated]

### Store funnel after traffic spikes

- Store performance example for one week of a viral TikTok campaign: about 1,220 sessions, 57 add-to-cart events and 47 checkouts, with zero sales. Viral views (22 million in 36 hours on one video) did not produce purchases because the traffic was not relevant. Compare these funnel numbers against the expected conversion rate before concluding the product is the issue. [L1077, undated]
- Organic traffic is less targeted than paid traffic. Viewers of a viral video may visit out of curiosity. Track the funnel from sessions to add-to-cart to checkout to sales, and compare those rates to paid benchmarks when judging a product. [L1077, undated]
- In Shopify's product admin, the Insights panel stays empty until the product has had recent sales. It cannot judge a newly embedded product video. Use the LyveCom story numbers (views, click-through rate, add to cart, checkout revenue) to judge video performance in the meantime. [L0959, 2025-26]
- LyveCom's analytics suite reports revenue attribution, engagement rate for all video content, and the effect of video on conversion, average order value and session time. Use it to see which content has the highest impact on the store. [L0951, 2025-26]

### Organic content, SEO and content ROI

- Monitor traffic sources and performance:
  1. Use Google Analytics to track organic, referral, social and direct traffic.
  2. Set goals for traffic composition (target: 80% organic).
  3. Review monthly which keywords and pages drive conversions.
  4. Identify underperforming content and improve or remove it.
  5. Test changes incrementally and measure impact. [L0226, 2017]
- Content ROI takes time. Blog articles and SEO improvements do not generate immediate traffic. Plan for 2-6 months before seeing significant organic traffic increases. Long-term payoff is free, recurring traffic from Google, unlike paid ads. [L0226, 2017]
- Compare the cost of content with paid traffic. A blog post of about 500 USD can cost as much as a Google Ads budget that is spent within a month or even a single day, whereas a well-ranked post can drive traffic for years. [L0379, 2021-22]
- Direct sales are hard to attribute to a single article, so measure content by the number of email signups it produces. Compare signups per article to decide which topics to repeat. [L0382, 2017]
- In Google Analytics, create a goal for visits to the newsletter confirmation page. Use it to see which pages send the most visitors to that page. [L0382, 2017]
- Monthly content review:
  1. List all articles.
  2. Sort them by email signups.
  3. Use the top performers to choose keywords in the keyword tools and create similar articles. [L0382, 2017]
- Use the Google Search Console and Google Analytics data together to audit SEO. Set measurable goals first, then pick the actions that match how competitive those goals are. [L0962, 2021]
- For SEO opportunity analysis, use a Search Console window of about 30 to 95 days, with 90 days as a practical default. Windows of 7 to 28 days are too slow or too small to be reliable. Periods much longer than 90 days blend performance across a year and hide recent changes. [L1269, 2025-26]
- Search Console > Performance shows by default the past three months of organic impressions and clicks. A new store will not show meaningful data for the first weeks. [L1271, 2020-26]
- Google Analytics must be installed on the store to track organic traffic behaviour, such as bounce rate and time on page. On Shopify it is very easy to install. [L1272, 2021-22]

### Link-building tracking

- Outreach for link building produces a growing volume of data over time. Record each contact, date, subject and result from the start, and keep it retrievable for future campaigns. [L0422, 2021-22]
- Store outreach tracking files both online in Google Drive and as a physical copy on an external or portable hard drive, because data kept in only one place can be lost. [L0422, 2021-22]
- Repeat outreach to the same contact is acceptable over time, but only with enough spacing between messages. [L0422, 2021-22]
- Four Google Drive template sheets are used for outreach tracking [L0422, 2021-22]:
  - Master Database
  - Outreach Batch folder
  - No Email Database
  - Success Database
- Keep a very large master spreadsheet stored online in Google Drive, but edit it offline in Excel. Big online spreadsheets in Google Drive process slowly, while Excel handles very large files quickly. [L0422, 2021-22]
- Ahrefs crawls showed that many links are uncrawled or blocked by robots.txt, so read crawl totals with that in mind. The Ahrefs data explorer can export URL lists with columns such as Page Rating, traffic and HTTP code. [L1372, 2021-22]

#### Further details

- Organic sales are hard to attribute to a single video. Use split tests with separate stores or accounts to learn which angles and accounts make the most money per million views. [L0352, 2022]
