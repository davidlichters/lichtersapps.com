### Profit apps

#### B Profit

The sources spell the app as B Profit, B-Profit and BeProfit. The sources do not say whether these are the same product, so the B Profit and BeProfit walkthroughs are kept separate.

- B Profit is a Shopify app that automatically tracks profit, KPIs and profit margins, and shows whether the seller is at loss or profitable. Automatic cost fetching is possible for CJ Dropshipping, AliExpress and Auto DS. It can be used for free with a start plan. [L875.023]
- B-Profit is a Shopify app that automates profit tracking. Install it from the Shopify App Store, connect ad accounts such as Facebook Ads, and enter the cost per item, handling cost and shipping price. It also picks up Shopify and external gateway fees, and shows gross profit and costs in one dashboard. A free plan (start free) is available. [L445.021]
- The B-Profit dashboard shows gross profit, all costs and profit margin, so you can see whether you are at a loss or profitable and whether to scale right now. [L445.023]
- B-Profit setup for dropshipping: choose niche, select channel (e.g. Facebook Ads), set inventory type to dropshipping, pick supplier (CJ Dropshipping, AliExpress, AutoDS or "Other"), then enter product cost per item, handling cost (0 in the example) and shipping prices. Finally connect external payment gateways such as Stripe or PayPal. [L445.022]
- Setting up B Profit in Shopify for profit tracking: [L875.022]
  1. Open the Shopify app store via the recommended apps section and install B Profit. [L875.022]
  2. Create an account and open the B Profit dashboard. [L875.022]
  3. Select the niche (for example electronics) and the sales channels, for example Facebook Ads, and connect the ad account. [L875.022]
  4. Add the inventory source: choose the dropshipping provider (CJ Dropshipping, AliExpress or Auto DS can be connected automatically to fetch cost prices), or select Other for manual entry. [L875.022]
  5. Select the fulfillment provider, or choose Other. [L875.022]
  6. Enter the shipping prices and product cost per item (example: cost per item $5, handling cost $0). [L875.022]
  7. Click Next and connect any external payment gateway such as Stripe or PayPal. [L875.022]
  8. Shopify plan fees are fetched automatically. Click Finish and start with the free plan to see gross profit and costs on the dashboard. [L875.022]
- Cost: B Profit costs about $7,200 per month and is aimed at large, established stores rather than beginners. [L1213, 2023] The free start plan described above is what the sources give for beginners. [L875.023, L445.021]
- Profit app setup as a procedure: [L1213, 2023]
  1. Connect Shopify Payments, Stripe and/or PayPal to the profit app. [L1213, 2023]
  2. Enter the transaction fees and any other margin fees. [L1213, 2023]
  3. For each product and each variant, enter the cost of goods sold (COGS), shipping cost and any extra charges. [L1213, 2023]
  4. Add extra charges such as Shopify's transaction fee, which the speaker noted as 0.5%, 1% or 2% depending on the plan. [L1213, 2023]
  5. Let the app calculate profit and loss for every order. After one to two days you will have a reliable picture of your current numbers. [L1213, 2023]

#### BeProfit reports, cost of goods and examples

- BeProfit tracks profit and loss by combining revenue, product costs, payment fees and expenses. The Financials section gives the profit and loss report. Settings > cost of goods lets you update costs when supplier prices change. [L0477, undated]
- In BeProfit, the Financials section shows the profit and loss report, and Settings > cost of goods is where you change product costs. If variants are missing, fill in their costs there so profit is calculated correctly. [L1297, 2023]
- Entering the wrong cost of goods in BeProfit distorts profit figures. Enter the actual total dropshipping price for every variant you sell, as quoted by the supplier. [L477.032]
- Example: after entering only the $29 Shopify plan as an expense and no orders yet, the BeProfit dashboard showed a loss of about $27. Costs are counted from the first day, so a store with zero sales shows a loss equal to its fixed expenses. A negative profit before the first sale is therefore expected when only fixed costs are entered. [L1297, 2023]

#### Why apps and what they miss

- Profit apps typically capture about 80 to 90% of order costs automatically from connected payment accounts. The remaining share, such as extra returns, disputes, chargebacks and payment-provider charges, must be added manually to get a true margin. [L1213, 2023]
- Spreadsheets are hard to keep accurate because every payment and cost source must be connected. Profit apps automate this by pulling data from payment providers. [L1213, 2023]

### Shopify reports and billing

- Shopify Settings > Billing lists every paid app subscription, what each costs, and when each bill is due. Check it regularly to track recurring app costs. [L1301, 2020-26]
- Paid apps that are no longer used keep billing every cycle. Review the Billing page and uninstall or cancel any app that is no longer needed. [L1301, 2020-26]
- Shopify analytics shows all store reports and activity. With Shopify Payments, payments and finances are also visible in the same admin. [L934.082]

### Fixed costs and expenses

- Shopify monthly subscription costs and app subscriptions are fixed costs and are not included in the per-order profit sheet. The sheet measures the actual profit per sale, so fixed costs must be reviewed separately. [L1202, 2021+]
- List every recurring cost as an expense with its status and payment frequency, for example the Shopify plan, other paid apps, a VA and fixed company costs. [L1297, 2023]
- The monthly budget sheet holds fixed costs on their own lines, so the monthly result reflects them. [L550.001, L550.002]
- A personal-finance style spreadsheet can also show which recurring subscriptions are unused, marked in red as they are entered, so they can be cancelled. [L678.004]

### Review cadence

- Track profit daily at first, then switch to weekly reviews to see the average weekly profit. Weekly data smooths out daily noise and gives a steadier basis for scaling decisions. [L875.024]
- Weekly tracking gives a clearer view of average profit than single-day numbers. [L445.020]
- Compare cost per IC and cost per ATC day over day to locate the funnel step where money is lost. [L1123, 2026]
- Judge a product on whether its daily profit holds above the threshold across consecutive days, not on the running total. [L1030, 2026]
- Periodically review overall business profit. Take distributions based on monthly or weekly profit, not on a per-order basis. [L268.041]
- Spot check periodically to confirm that a product line, brand or best-selling product is still profitable. [L1246, 2021-22]
- Return to the tracker after supplier price changes or your own price changes to see how things have developed. [L1246, 2021-22]

### Sheet-building rules

- Enter amounts as plain numbers and keep the currency in the header or a note. [L550.007]
- Check every date after entry, and make sure the cell is formatted as a date rather than text. [L550.008]
- Every expense and income row needs a category that matches the sheet. [L550.009]
- Customer Paid must be the amount the customer actually paid, as shown in Shopify. [L1202, 2021+]
- Keep the shipping cost in its own separate cell as a reference note. [L1201, 2021-22]
- Keep the shipping column for what you paid out only. Customer-paid shipping belongs in Customer Paid. [L1202, 2021+]
- The Date column stores a spreadsheet date serial, so format it as a date before reading it. [L0394, undated]
- Use one row per order, with the supplier, product, quantity and promotion. [L0394, undated]
- Keep the blank template tab unchanged and copy it for each new period or store. [L0394, undated]
- Keep a duplicate backup tab with the formulas, and do all entry in the working tab. [L1202, 2021+]
- Only enter data in the input cells. Dark purple (auto-calculated) cells must not be typed into. [L1123, 2026]
- Keep the IF pass-through formulas (`=IF(source="","",source)`) intact so the cells stay linked to their inputs. [L1123, 2026]
- Fill only the white input cells in the one source order tracker. [L1201, 2021-22]
- Use the fill handle to copy a full formula row for each new order. [L1202, 2021+]
- Enter a fee in the Setup tab for each payment method. Each processor name must match the payment method shown in Shopify. [L1201, 2021-22]
- Keep the order-level tracker separate from fixed costs; add fixed costs to the expense list or budget sheet. [L1202, 2021+]
- Enter the planned test budget per product in the planned field so the summary shows the difference. [L678.001]
- Keep the sheet's expense list and total in sync: the daily cost is derived from the entered total. [L900.021]
- The 30-row daily grid covers one 30-day period. [L1123, 2026]

### Template mistakes

- Typing a fixed number over a calculated cell, such as gross profit, contribution margin or break-even ROAS, breaks the formula chain and makes every later figure wrong. Change the input cells instead and let the calculator recalculate. [L0433, 2025-26]
- Typing values into the colored, automatically calculated cells breaks all calculations in the tracker. Enter data only in the white cells. [L1201, 2021-22]
- Entering the product's list price in Customer Paid instead of the amount the customer actually paid. Discounts and paid shipping then distort the profit result and make the per-order numbers unreliable. [L1202, 2021+]
- Entering shipping that the customer paid in the shipping column. The shipping column should hold only what you paid out, so customer-paid shipping belongs in Customer Paid. [L1202, 2021+]
- Leaving Tax Collected empty or treating collected sales tax as revenue. It must be entered and deducted, otherwise net profit is overstated. [L1202, 2021+]
- Working in the original template tab without keeping a duplicate backup. If the formulas or automations break, a clean duplicate is needed to restore the calculation. [L1202, 2021+]
- Amounts entered with a currency symbol, such as "$250", are stored as text and are not counted in the totals. [L550.007]
- A date typed with a wrong year, such as 0203 instead of 2023, places the entry outside the month and breaks any date-based filtering. [L550.008]
- A log row without a category, like the 100 Facebook Ads entry without a category, will not appear in the category totals of the monthly sheet. [L550.009]
- The Shopify 1% Fee column header does not match the sample value, which equals 0.5% of revenue. Check the fee rate before relying on the column. [L900.019]
- A PayPal figure sits next to the profit column but is not part of the profit formula. Confirm whether PayPal fees are already in the payment processor fee column. [L900.020]
- The total cost of running the business is entered as 400 USD, but the listed monthly expenses add up to 606 USD (605 without the designer value of 1). The expense list and the total must be kept in sync. [L900.021]
- Entering the wrong cost of goods in BeProfit distorts profit figures. [L477.032]
- Using an order profit tracker to calculate profit on every single order is not recommended for dropshipping, because supplier invoices change and are hard to keep accurate. [L484.022]

### When per-order tracking stops paying off

Sources disagree on per-order tracking. Each position applies to a different stage and case.

- Per-order tracking applies while the store is still learning which products make money. A simple spreadsheet is enough while volume is low, for example a few orders per day. [L1202, 2021+]
- Per-order tracking applies during the early build phase. Calculate the profit of every single order, because margins only become clear once real supplier costs, shipping, processor fees and fraud fees are included. Older source (2021-22). [L1201, 2021-22]
- Once the store is established, tracking each order matters less. Older source (2021-22). [L1201, 2021-22]
- Before handing the task to someone else, enter and check the profit of at least the first 5 to 10 orders yourself. Older source (2021-22). [L1201, 2021-22]
- Per-order tracking has diminishing returns in the growth phase, because the profit per additional sale of the same item barely changes while the time to collect invoices and costs is large. Older source (2021-22). [L1246, 2021-22]
- Per-order COGS accrual is effectively impossible for dropshipping, and the return on investment is negative. Older source (2021-22). [L484.009]
- Per-order tracking for dropshipping is not recommended, because supplier invoices change. Older source (2021-22). [L484.022]
- When volume grows, assign the per-order tracking to a virtual assistant, and review overall profit periodically. Older source (2020). [L268.041]
- Automate profit tracking once the store runs, so margin is measured per order rather than estimated from totals. [L532.023]
- Per-sale tracking is the rule in the 2023 source: track the net profit of every sale in a spreadsheet. [L970.149]
- Adding a new supplier or product: track it for a while until you know how it performs. Older source (2021-22). [L1246, 2021-22]
- If you are still not confident about profit per order, use the tracker for a week at a time instead of continuously. Older source (2021-22). [L1246, 2021-22]
- Working rule that reconciles the positions: while product profitability is still unknown or changing, track per order; once products and supplier costs are stable, switch to periodic spot checks and weekly or monthly reviews. [L1202, 2021+; L1246, 2021-22]

### Daily D2C funnel tracker

The daily D2C funnel tracker has five sections: unit economics, Meta ads, website, and the inputs and calculations behind them. Each row lists what the column means and the benchmark stated for it [L1354, 2025-26].

| Section | Column | Meaning | Benchmark stated for this tracker |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit economics | Store listing price | Input, price per unit | $150 in the example |
| Unit economics | COGS + shipping | Input, cost per unit | $35 in the example |
| Unit economics | Product margin | Calculated | 76.67% in the example |
| Unit economics | Target CPP | Calculated, max cost per sale | $115 in the example |
| Unit economics | Break-even ROAS | Calculated | 1.30 in the example |
| Meta ads | Ad spend | Daily budget | $100 per day in the example |
| Meta ads | CPM | Cost per 1,000 impressions | About $20 healthy; reevaluate near $60 |
| Meta ads | CTR | Click-through rate | Above 1% good |
| Meta ads | CPC | Cost per click | $0 to $3 healthy; about $1 very good |
| Meta ads | CPP | Cost per purchase | Must stay below target CPP |
| Meta ads | ROAS | Revenue / ad spend | At or above break-even ROAS |
| Website | ATC | Add to carts | Count per day |
| Website | Purchases | Sales count | Count per day |
| Website | CVR | Conversion rate | 2% in the example; 0.5% in a weak example |
| Website | AOV | Average order value | Should be good |
| Website | Store revenue | Total daily revenue | Input to ROAS |

Each cell is coloured with one of three labels, Good, Improve or Bad, judged against the benchmark in the last column [L1354, 2025-26].
