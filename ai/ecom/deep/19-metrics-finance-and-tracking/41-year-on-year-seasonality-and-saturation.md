### Year-on-year, seasonality and saturation

**Seasonality first**

- Before reacting to a sales drop, check whether it is seasonal by comparing at least two years of data. Most markets have predictable seasonal patterns. [L0949, 2021-22]
- In 2021, sales fell in July and August across roughly 100 Google Analytics and ads accounts in the US. This pointed to market-wide forces rather than store-level problems. The speaker linked it to broader economic conditions and stock delays. [L0949, 2021-22]
- Read dips in a monthly creative report against the ad account's seasonality. A drop in January and February can reflect an off-season rather than a problem. Compare each region or account with its own past seasons. [L0885, 2025-26]
- A store with consistent prior sales should not expect a drop in February. If sales fall, check the optimization lessons in module six for Meta and Google Ads to find what changed. [L0964, 2023]
- Launch timing matters little for non-seasonal niches, so any month is a good month to launch. Tax refund season can bring a small sales spike. Supplier closures from Black Friday through Christmas matter more when securing stock. [L0807, 2025]
- Black Friday example: revenue doubled compared with the previous month, while January and February were the best months for the store because competitors were out of stock. [L1363, 2023]
- Q1 2025 (January through March) sales were up approximately 10% compared with Q1 2024, indicating healthy market conditions despite economic concerns. [L0255, 2025]

**Holiday auctions**

- When CPM rises during Black Friday and Cyber Monday, ad costs go up because more competitors bid in the same auctions. Check CPM, CPC and clicks year on year to see which part changed. [L0472, 2025]
- Use the Google Ads date range selector to compare the same range (e.g. Thanksgiving week through Cyber Monday) across two years when measuring seasonal performance. [L0472, 2025]
- CPM is the cost per 1,000 impressions, meaning what it costs to reach 1,000 people with an ad. CPC is the cost per click. If CPM and CPC both rise during a holiday period, competitors are often bidding for the same auctions, so compare year on year before changing bids. [L0472, 2025]

**Growth trend and saturation**

- Compare revenue month over month and use the trend to set the next goal. Example: $30,000 in one month and an average of $35,000 in the following month shows growth of about 17%, which becomes the basis for planning the next quarter. Ask the same question every month so growth opportunities are not missed. [L0463, 2020-26]
- Without proper forecasts and inventory tracking, early-stage patchy sales hide whether a trend is fatiguing or just seasonal. Track weekly to spot real trend signals and adjust investment accordingly. [L0041, 2025-26]
- Year-on-year tracking, excluding seasonality, reveals market saturation. A 20% year-on-year revenue drop in March, followed by a 20-25% drop two months later, signals that the market is fatiguing and the trend is plateauing. [L0041, 2025-26]
- When year-on-year drops repeat at similar levels (e.g. 20-25% down in consecutive periods), the trend is fatiguing. Adjust investment toward product innovation, new markets or channel diversification. [L0041, 2025-26]
