## Bookkeeping, P&L, balance sheet and cash flow

### Bookkeeping foundations and when to start

- Bookkeeping means setting up accounting software, importing bank transactions, classifying them and reconciling them. It is complex and time-consuming, so many owners hand it to a monthly bookkeeping service. [L0632, 2021-22]
- Set up sound accounting practices from the first sale, either yourself or with an accountant or bookkeeper. Basic bookkeeping is enough at the start to track profit per sale and where costs go. [L0702, 2021-22]
- Leaving accounting until sales are high usually results in messy books. Without knowing where money comes from and where it goes, the business is harder to grow. [L0702, 2021-22]
- Solid accounting shows the profit on each individual sale and where marketing spend is too high. This clarity supports sound business decisions and reduces financial stress. [L1405, 2021-22]
- The general ledger is the foundation of business finances. KPIs, budgets, cash management, financing and loans should only be built on top of it, because without that foundation advanced financial analysis is not reliable. [L1246, 2021-22]
- The general ledger does not answer every financial question by itself. It answers the basic ones, and more advanced needs are addressed with additional layers built on it. [L1246, 2021-22]
- The general ledger can be maintained by the owner in accounting software or handed to an outside bookkeeping firm. Either way, the point is to have the reports it produces available for decisions. [L1246, 2021-22]

### Accounting software and bookkeepers

#### Choosing a bookkeeping approach

- When hiring a bookkeeping service, choose one that specializes in ecommerce operations. [L0107, 2021-22]
- Professional ecommerce bookkeeping services cost a few hundred dollars per month. [L0107, 2021-22]
- To handle bookkeeping, choose one of three approaches: [L0107, 2021-22]
  1. Hire a bookkeeping service specializing in ecommerce for complete done-for-you management.
  2. Enroll in a self-paced bookkeeping course with group coaching. the source members receive free access.
  3. Recruit and train a virtual assistant from Upwork or Online Jobs using bookkeeping course materials.
- Do not hire cheap or unvetted bookkeepers because you lack expertise to audit their work. Problems remain undetected for months, and fixing poorly maintained books costs far more than hiring professionals initially. [L0107, 2021-22]
- The monthly P&L, balance sheet and cash flow statement are generated by general ledger bookkeeping software if the owner does the books, or delivered by a bookkeeper if one is hired. The owner should ask the bookkeeper to walk through these reports each month. [L0484, 2021-22]
- If the owner cannot read the monthly reports, or the reports do not explain where the cash went, the bookkeeping is weak. Replace or improve the bookkeeper rather than relying on unclear numbers. [L0484, 2021-22]

#### Spreadsheets versus accounting software

- Google Sheets can handle accounting for a small business early on, so switching to Xero is optional at the start. [L0247, 2021]
- Xero: bookkeeping software that is not necessary when starting. Google Sheets is sufficient until revenue grows enough to justify a bookkeeper. [L0268, 2020]
- Excel or Google Sheets also works for tracking earlier in the business. Zero is accounting software used by some members for multiple businesses. [L0804, 2023]
- Xero can be used as accounting software once volume grows, but a free profit tracking spreadsheet is enough when starting. [L0855, 2023]
- Xero is used for accounting and bookkeeping with Shopify integration, typically with the help of an accountant. A simple profit-tracking spreadsheet can replace it while the business is very new. [L1281, 2022]
- Google Sheets is suitable for day-to-day bookkeeping and monitoring a small business. Send the records your accountant needs to Xero, which they use to manage the books and taxes. [L1274, 2018]
- Switch from the spreadsheet template to a real accounting platform such as Xero once the store grows, because the bookkeeping approach changes as the business gets more professional. [L0763, 2021-22]
- An accounting platform such as Xero or QuickBooks, also called a general ledger, records all transactions and provides a P&L, a balance sheet and a cash flow statement. It gives a more complete and balanced view of the business than a P&L spreadsheet. [L1246, 2021-22]

#### QuickBooks, Shopify sync and connectors

- QuickBooks Online is used for accounting. A Shopify-to-QuickBooks sync app (transcribed in the source as Sinder, check the exact name) pushes Shopify transactions into QuickBooks, so each order shows its cost of goods, sales, shipping and profit after setup. [L0337, 2022]
- QuickBooks Online works for accounting, and a connector such as A2X links Shopify to it. [L1281, 2022]

#### Owner-run bookkeeping in practice

- The owner uses the Zero accounting app for bookkeeping but still enters supplier bills by hand and categorizes bank transactions the software does not match automatically. The owner reviews the resulting margins personally, so the bookkeeping is not fully automated. [L1473, 2019]

### Spreadsheet P&L setup (build phase)

#### Template and structure

- The "Financial statements" template by Xero is available in the Google Drive template gallery and runs in Google Sheets. It is a free basic P&L template for a drop shipping store, with tabs for Instructions, Transactions, Dashboard, Profit & Loss, Categories and Balance Sheet. [L0763, 2021-22]
- The spreadsheet template is a build-phase tool. [L0763, 2021-22]
- The Transactions sheet has the columns Date, Description, Amount and Category. Category is a drop-down that must be set for every row, and the Profit & Loss sheet only reads transactions that have a category. [L0763, 2021-22]
- Track business expenses on dedicated cards so that month-end reconciliation between bank records, card statements and the spreadsheet is quick and reliable. [L0903, 2021-22]
- Ignore the balance sheet tab during the build phase. It needs manual entries in shaded boxes and does not help with tracking income and expenses. [L0763, 2021-22]
- Review the P&L month by month, because the monthly layout shows how revenue, cost of goods sold and expenses change from one month to the next. The growth rate projection and Dashboard charts are optional extras. [L0763, 2021-22]

#### Setup steps

Setting up the P&L template in Google Sheets: [L0763, 2021-22]
1. Open the Transactions sheet and delete the sample transactions.
2. Enter all business transactions (date, description, amount) in the Transactions sheet.
3. Select the matching category for each transaction from the drop-down menu.
4. The Dashboard and Profit & Loss sheets update automatically from the transactions.
5. To rename or change a category, edit it on the Categories sheet.
6. To add a new category, first insert a new row below the last row of that category type on the Categories sheet, type the category name and select the category type from the drop-down. Then go to the Profit & Loss sheet, select the row showing #REF! and insert a new row below it. The new category then appears on the Profit & Loss sheet and in the Transactions sheet drop-down.
7. The balance sheet step (enter values in the shaded boxes) is not needed in the build phase.

In the Google Sheets financial statements template, open the Profit & Loss sheet and select cell F4 to choose the first month of the financial year. The other month columns update from that choice. Revenue, cost of sales and expenses are summarized automatically from the Transactions sheet into a Total YTD column. An optional Growth Rate field then creates a Projected column for a projected P&L. [L1246, 2021-22]

On the Profit & Loss sheet, select cell F4 to choose the first month of your financial year, and the month columns update from there. Negative amounts are displayed in parentheses, and the Total YTD column sums the year so far. [L0763, 2021-22]

#### Sign convention and checks

- Enter money you spend, including cost of goods sold, as a negative amount. Enter money you receive, such as sales, as a positive amount. [L0763, 2021-22]
- Entering expenses and supplier payments as positive numbers gives impossible results, such as a gross profit larger than sales minus supplier costs and a wrong total profit. Check the signs of all amounts before reading the P&L. [L0763, 2021-22]
- A small orange warning triangle that appears in the template after an entry signals an input problem. In the lesson's example it flagged amounts entered with the wrong sign. Check that all costs are negative and all incoming money is positive before trusting any profit figure. [L0763, 2021-22]
- Sign error example in a build-phase drop shipping P&L: a Shopify payout of 2,000 dollars is recorded as positive revenue, but the 1,200 dollar supplier charge and the 500 dollar Google Ads charge are entered as positive numbers too. The sheet then shows a gross profit of 3,200 dollars and a total profit of 3,700 dollars, which cannot be right for these transactions. After both costs are changed to negative amounts, gross profit correctly becomes 800 dollars and the total profit after the 500 dollar advertising expense is 300 dollars. [L0763, 2021-22]
- Starting point: a new drop shipping store with no sheet data yet. On March 1, a Shopify payout of 2,000 dollars was recorded as a Sales transaction (positive). On March 15, Supplier A charged 1,200 dollars, recorded as a negative Cost of Goods Sold. On March 17, Google Ads charged 500 dollars, recorded as a negative Advertising expense. The result is a Gross Profit of 800 dollars (2,000 minus 1,200) and a total profit of 300 dollars after the 500 dollar ad expense. [L0763, 2021-22]

Categories in the template, grouped by P&L section: [L0763, 2021-22]

| Cost of sales | Cost of Goods Sold, Custom COGS 1, Custom COGS 2 |
| Expenses | Advertising, Office Supplies, Car/Truck, Insurance, Rent-Car/Equipment, Repairs, Legal Expenses, Contractors, Travel, Meals & Entertainment, Utilities, Rent, Loan Expenses, Taxes and Licenses, Other Expenses, Custom Expense 1, Custom Expense 2, Custom Expense 3 |

- Keep changes to the default categories to a minimum. Categories such as Car/Truck, Rent-Car/Equipment and Repairs rarely apply to a drop shipping store, so rename one of them, or use a Custom Expense slot, only when you need a category that is missing. [L0763, 2021-22]
- Example: the unused Repairs category was renamed Website & Hosting on the Categories sheet, and a 32 dollar Shopify charge was then entered under it. The new name appears automatically in the Transactions drop-down and the P&L after you insert the row as described in the category setup steps. [L0763, 2021-22]

#### Projection

- The Profit & Loss sheet has a Growth Rate field next to the Total YTD column. Entering a percentage there, for example 20%, adds a Projected column based on the year-to-date total. In the example, a Total YTD of 3,100 with 20% growth gives a projected 3,720. This projection is optional and not needed during the build phase. [L0763, 2021-22]
- The projected figure equals the Total YTD value multiplied by (1 + growth rate). For example, 2,000 with a 20% growth rate projects to 2,400. [L1246, 2021-22]

### What belongs on a P&L

- A profit and loss statement (P&L, also called an income statement) lists revenue, costs and expenses for a period and ends with the profit or loss. In the build phase, when a store is preparing to launch or has just made first sales, a basic P&L is enough to track money in and out. [L0763, 2021-22]
- A P&L (profit and loss sheet, also called an income statement) lists income, expenses and the resulting profit after all expenses are subtracted. It is the basic financial overview a store owner needs from the start. [L1148, 2021-22]
- The profit and loss statement (income statement) is the first report to review each month and for the full year. It lists total income, cost of goods sold, gross profit, operating expenses, operating income, other income and net income for the period. [L1046, 2021-22]
- A P&L shows income, expenses and profit, where profit equals income minus expenses. It shows overall profitability and where money is spent, but it is not sufficient on its own to judge the financial health of a business. [L1246, 2021-22]
- Looking only at the P&L gives a skewed view of the business, especially when a business is large enough to be scrutinized by a buyer. [L1246, 2021-22]
- The following transactions do not belong on a P&L: owner draws, owner contributions, transfers between own accounts, credit card payments, inventory purchases, loans and COVID stimulus payments. Recording inventory as an expense can have a serious impact on taxes. [L1246, 2021-22]
- The P&L does not show cash flow, owner withdrawals or profit distributions, loans or credit card payoffs, investments into the business, or inventory purchases. Its bottom line will not match the bank account balance. [L0484, 2021-22]
- In a drop shipping business, income means sales and expenses mean every outgoing payment, such as supplier costs, advertising, hosting and co-working spaces. Keep only income and expenses in the sheet during the build phase and leave out internal transfers, unless they go to a supplier. [L0763, 2021-22]
- Cost of goods sold is what you pay the supplier for the products, including the shipping they handle for you. Large supplier payments made by ACH or wire transfer are recorded in the same way as card charges, as cost of goods sold. [L0763, 2021-22]
- Money moved between your own accounts is neither income nor an expense, so do not record it. The exception is a transfer sent to a supplier, which is an expense and goes into the sheet as cost of goods sold. [L0763, 2021-22]
- The P&L follows bank activity, meaning the date money arrives or leaves, not the date an order was placed. A sale may therefore appear in the following month if the payout or supplier charge comes later. Do not expect the monthly totals in the order profit tracker to match the monthly P&L, because the order profit tracker shows when orders were taken while the P&L shows when money came in or went out. [L0763, 2021-22]
  - Example: a sale made on February 28 with a Shopify payout arriving about three days later lands in the bank in March, so the P&L shows it under March. Monthly differences like this are normal because the P&L follows bank activity, not order dates. [L0763, 2021-22]
- Formula, Total Cost of Sales = Cost of Goods Sold + Custom COGS 1 + Custom COGS 2. Gross Profit (Gross Margin) = Total Sales minus Total Cost of Sales. Total Expenses = sum of all expense categories. Total Profit or Loss = Gross Profit minus Total Expenses. Negative results are shown in parentheses. [L0763, 2021-22]
- Gross margin on the P&L equals total sales minus total cost of sales. For example, sales of 2,000 with cost of sales of 1,200 give a gross margin of 800. [L1246, 2021-22]
- Track each advertising cost to the specific product or department it sells, and require every unit of spend to justify itself within a short cycle. Unattributed budget lines are where money is most often wasted. [L1261, undated]

#### Annual P&L structure

- Use a profit and loss sheet that deducts Shopify fees as its own line, because a P&L without platform fees overstates your real profit. Replace older versions of the sheet with the updated one. [L1323, 2026]
- Using a P&L tracker template: [L1323, 2026]
  1. Open the template and choose File > Make a copy so you can edit your own version.
  2. Fill in the light purple input cells with your figures.
  3. Leave the other cells alone, since they calculate automatically.
- Break out all variable expenses on the left side, separate from COGS, so you can quickly model the impact of cutting or changing each line on net profit. P&L design must support decision-making. [L0218, 2025-26]
- Most operating expenses stay roughly flat from month to month, while product cost, outbound shipping and merchant fees move with sales. Review advertising spend and freelancer costs separately, because they are the largest operating items that can be cut or redirected. [L1046, 2021-22]
- Card rewards and other non-operating income should be shown separately from sales and product margin. In the example they added 23,514 and were about 17 percent of the 138,142 net income, so they should not be mistaken for operating performance. [L1046, 2021-22]

### Reading the P&L

- Reading a P&L from top to bottom: [L0484, 2021-22]
  1. Total income (sales).
  2. Cost of goods sold (COGS).
  3. Gross profit (total income minus COGS).
  4. Operating expenses, listed by category.
  5. Net ordinary income (gross profit minus operating expenses).
  6. Other income and expense, such as interest income, miscellaneous income and credit card rewards.
  7. Net income, the bottom line, which is the profit.
- Monthly net results in a seasonal dropshipping business can swing negative because supplier payments follow earlier orders. Judge profitability by the full year and expect it to rise or at least hold steady year over year. [L1046, 2021-22]

### Sales reports and margins

- In Shopify's sales breakdown, total sales equals gross sales minus returns. In the example month, $915,682.28 across all channels minus $47,519.91 in returns gave $868,162.37, and the online store alone showed $763,095.30 minus $42,630.12 = $720,465.18. [L0400, 2021-22]
- Formulas: net sales = total sales minus returns (for example, $251,671.35 minus $23,721.80 equals $227,949.55). Net profit percentage = net profit divided by net sales, where net profit is what remains after product, fulfillment, shipping and marketing costs (for example, $50,148.90 divided by $227,949.55 is about 22%). Average order value = net sales divided by orders, and conversion rate = orders divided by sessions. [L0458, 2021-22]
- Read the store's sales report in this order: total sales, returns, net sales, orders, sessions and visitors, and profit after all costs. Returns must be deducted before calculating the profit percentage, otherwise profit looks higher than it is. [L0458, 2021-22]
- Gross margin = gross profit divided by total income. The course example shows 14.14 percent by dividing by gross sales before discounts and returns (258,322 divided by 1,826,410). On the same 258,322 gross profit and 1,554,399 total income, the margin is about 16.6 percent. Pick one denominator and use it consistently when comparing months and years. [L1046, 2021-22]
- Net margin = net income divided by total income. The example gives 138,142 divided by 1,554,399, which is about 8.9 percent (about 9 percent). [L1046, 2021-22]

### Accrual and cash accounting

#### Definitions and examples

- Under accrual accounting, an order is recorded as income on the day it is placed, regardless of when payment arrives. Under cash accounting, income and expenses are recorded only when money actually reaches or leaves the bank account. For example, an order placed on March 31 with a Shopify payout three days later would appear in March under accrual but in April under cash. [L0484, 2021-22]
- Cash-based accounting records income and expenses when money moves in or out of the bank account. Accrual-based accounting records income when it is earned and the matching product cost in the same month, which evens out dips. The broker prefers accrual accounting for online businesses, though both work for dropshipping. [L0554, 2019]
- Cash-based example: in month 1, 10 units sell at $100 each with a $50 cost per unit paid on 30-day supplier terms, so cash profit is $1,000. In month 2, 3 units sell for $300 and the bill of $500 for the month 1 units is paid, so cash shows a $200 loss. [L0554, 2019]
- Accrual example: the same 10 units in month 1 show $1,000 income and $500 cost, so profit is $500. In month 2, the 3 units show $300 income and $150 cost, so profit is $150. [L0554, 2019]
- Cash-based accounting becomes a serious problem for businesses with inventory. For example, buying 1,000 units for $20,000 can show a very large negative month, so accrual accounting makes more sense in that case. [L0554, 2019]

#### Which method applies when

- For dropshipping, record sales income on the order date but record cost of goods (supplier cost) only when money leaves the bank or credit card to pay the supplier. This cash-basis approach to COGS is the practical standard, as opposed to tracking profit per order. [L0484, 2021-22]
- Most small dropshipping stores record product cost when the supplier charges the card rather than on the order date. This is a hybrid of accrual and cash bookkeeping. Full accrual bookkeeping for every invoice is usually too time-consuming and expensive at scale. [L1046, 2021-22]
- Where the sources differ: the broker recommends accrual for online businesses, while the one source course treats cash-basis COGS as the practical standard for small stores and hybrid as the norm. Use accrual where inventory is large and lumpy, as in the 1,000-unit example, and the hybrid or cash-basis COGS approach for small dropshipping stores where full accrual is too costly. [L0554, 2019; L0484, 2021-22; L1046, 2021-22]
