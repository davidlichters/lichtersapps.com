### Benchmarks: Meta

#### Meta CTR

- **Minimum and test targets:**
  - Test target for new ads: at least 1%. [L1194, undated]
  - Facebook CTR target: at least 1.5%. A CTR above 0.7% is acceptable, but 1.5% is the target value. [L1220, undated]
  - A CTR of about 1.5% is a decent guideline for Meta ads. Anything lower is generally considered weaker. Treat this as a guideline, not a hard cut-off. [L0644, undated]
  - A CTR of at least 1.5% is the guideline for Meta ads. Lower CTRs need attention to the middle of the video and the offer. Ads with a CTR around 0.7% to 1.0% still produced sales for some stores. [L1426, undated]
  - A CTR around 1% was considered weak in one example, while 1.83% and 1.24% were seen on other ad sets. A CTR of 3% or above is a strong result in the examples given. [L0650, undated]
- **Good readings:**
  - A good CTR on Facebook is above 1.5%. Winning products usually sit between 2% and 2.5%. Below 1.5% combined with no sales is a warning sign. [L0773, 2023]
  - On Facebook ad sets, a CTR above 1.5% signals that ads are performing. CTRs of 3% to 5% show strong relevance but still need sales to be meaningful. Winning products in the lesson hit roughly 2% or more. [L0773, 2023]
  - A CTR above 1.5% on Meta ad sets shows the ad is attracting clicks. [L0774, 2023]
  - Good Facebook CTR range: 1.5% to 2.0%. [L0229, undated]
  - Industry-standard CTR on Facebook is about 2%, meaning roughly 20 clicks per 1,000 people reached. [L0639, 2023]
  - A CTR above 2% is considered good, and the goal is to push it higher over time. [L0652, 2023]
  - Facebook CTR target: aim for above 2%. [L0640, 2023]
  - Good performance on Meta ads means a CTR above 2% and a CPC below $1. The audience is interested and clicks through to the store. [L1172, 2023+]
  - With strong creative, a CTR over 2% and CPC around $1 to $1.40 are the cited benchmarks. [L1315, 2023]
  - For T4 and T5 markets, a healthy Meta ad has a CTR around 2% or above. [L1260, undated]
  - Unique link CTR above 1.5% is considered good. [L0502, 2020+]
- **Higher readings:**
  - CTRs of 3% to 5% are good on Meta, as rough reference points that depend on product and market. [L0361, 2024]
  - The ideal Meta ad CTR is 3% to 7%. Unlike CPC and CPM, a higher CTR is better. [L0874, 2026]
  - A CTR of 2.2% to 2.4% is acceptable if you let the ad run another day or add a bit more spend. [L0874, 2026]
  - In one real campaign, a CTR of 2.12% per impression was below the ideal range. [L0874, 2026]

**Reconciling the CTR figures:** The units give 1.5% as the floor for link CTR, about 2% as the good line, and 3% to 7% as the ideal. The 3% to 7% range is the one source reading, and the 1.5% and 2% lines appear across one source and the other units. Always confirm which CTR variant you are reading (link CTR, unique outbound CTR or CTR all) before comparing. [L0874, 2026; L0639, 2023; L0502, 2020+]

#### Meta CPM

- Average CPM on Facebook is about $10. At that CPM, a $10 daily ad set budget reaches up to about 1,000 people per day, usually fewer. [L0639, 2023]
- Target: under $10, ideally near $5. Old ad account showed $20 to $50. [L1194, undated]
- Healthy setup: CPM below 25 USD. [L0773, 2023; L0774, 2023]
- For T4 and T5 markets: a Meta ad is healthy when CPM is under 30 dollars. [L1260, undated]
- A CPM above $30, sometimes $35 or more, points to a competitive audience or weak creative that forces views. [L0398, 2023+]
- Reference point for a low CPM in a real campaign: $31.52 per 1,000 impressions. Compare your CPM against this kind of figure, keeping in mind that a lower CPM means cheaper reach. [L0874, 2026]
- one source reading for 2026: a CPM of about $50 is decent, and anything from about $70 upward is poor. A lower CPM is always better because reaching 1,000 people costs less. Benchmarks may rise as competition increases. [L0874, 2026]
- **Reconciling the CPM figures:** The sources range from about $10 (average), under $25 and under $30 (healthy limits) to about $50 (decent) and $70 (poor). The one source 2026 figures are its own reading of current competition. The one source limits (under $25) and the T4/T5 rule (under $30) are the stricter checks. Apply the one that matches your market and period, and state it. [L0639, 2023; L0773, 2023; L1260, undated; L0874, 2026]

### Benchmarks: TikTok

- **CPC:** target below $1 per click. [L1375, undated]
  - For a US audience, a CPC of about $0.50 to $0.62 is good. [L1382, undated]
  - Any CBO campaign or ad group with a CPC above $1 should be cut. [L1382, undated]
- **CPM:** target below $10 per 1,000 impressions. [L1375, undated]
  - TikTok ads have much lower CPM than Facebook. The main difference between platforms is CPM, not the ideal CTR. [L0773, 2023]
- **CTR (conflicting readings):**
  - Target: above 1.5%, meaning the share of viewers who click the ad. [L1375, undated]
  - In TikTok product tests, a CTR above 1% is strong. A like rate of 1% or more signals engagement. [L1382, undated]
  - A CTR around 0.8% can be acceptable on TikTok if traffic, conversion rate and profit are good. [L0773, 2023]
  - Apply the 1.5% target as the default. Use the 1% and 0.8% readings only when traffic, conversion and profit support them. [L1375, undated; L1382, undated; L0773, 2023]
- **Formula:** CTR is clicks per impression, so CPC = CPM ÷ (1,000 × CTR) and CTR = CPM ÷ (1,000 × CPC). Example: at a CPM of $10 and a CTR of 1.5%, the CPC is about $0.67. A CPC below $1 alone does not guarantee the 1.5% CTR target. [L1375, undated]
- **Add-to-cart ratio:** minimum 3%, read in the Shopify backend. [L1194, undated]
- TikTok ads usually cost more per impression than Pinterest for similar targeting. [L0788, undated]

### Setting up custom metrics in the ad platforms

- **Hook Rate and Hold Rate in Ads Manager** (procedure):
  - Open the column or custom metric option and choose create custom metric.
  - Name the first "Hook Rate", set the format to percentage, and use 3-second video plays divided by impressions.
  - Name the second "Hold Rate", set it to percentage, and use ThruPlays divided by impressions.
  - Add both columns to the dashboard to compare creatives side by side. [L0398, 2023+]
- **Funnel analysis metrics:** In Facebook Ads Manager, go to Columns > Customize columns > Custom metrics to create the custom metrics used for funnel analysis. Set the Hook rate metric as a percentage of impressions where viewers watched the first three seconds. [L0950, 2023]
- **Hook rate definition as a custom metric:** people who watch the first 3 seconds of a video divided by all impressions, as a percentage. [L0950, 2023]
- **Video attention metric:** a custom metric calculated as 3-second video plays divided by impressions, as a percentage. In Meta Ads Manager, create it via Customize columns > Create custom metric, name it "Video attention", set the format to percentage, and enter the 3-second video plays field divided by the impressions field. [L1426, undated]
- **Video attention, step by step:** open Columns > Customize columns, click Create custom metric, name it "Video attention", set it as a percentage, and use 3-second video plays divided by impressions. Save it and add it next to click-through rate in the reporting view. [L1426, undated]
- **Frequency:** found in the Facebook Ads columns. [L0950, 2023]
- **Video attention benchmark for the first three seconds:** about 25% is acceptable, 30% and above is good, and 40% and above is excellent. A value around 20% is considered low, and the hook should be improved. [L1426, undated]

### Checking numbers by hand

Ad set metrics can be checked from the raw totals:

- Frequency = impressions ÷ reach. Example: 2,017,463 ÷ 862,845 = 2.34. [L0797, 2025-26]
- CPM = amount spent ÷ impressions × 1,000. Example: 10,832.32 ÷ 2,017,463 × 1,000 = $5.37. [L0797, 2025-26]
- CPC = amount spent ÷ link clicks. Example: 10,832.32 ÷ 27,761 = $0.39. [L0797, 2025-26]
- CTR = link clicks ÷ impressions. Example: 27,761 ÷ 2,017,463 = 1.38%. [L0797, 2025-26]
- Cost per result (purchase objective) = amount spent ÷ website purchases. Example: 10,832.32 ÷ 306 = $35.40. [L0797, 2025-26]
- Platform revenue = website purchases × AOV. Example: 306 × $129.20 = $39,535.20. [L0797, 2025-26]

If you estimate impressions from spend, use impressions ≈ spend ÷ CPM × 1,000. Example: at a CPM of 10 USD, 50 USD of spend gives about 5,000 impressions, so 25 link clicks is a CTR of 0.5%, below the 1.5% target, and a CPC of 2 USD, above the 1 USD limit. [L1220, undated]

### Common mistakes

- Spending on Facebook ads without knowing what CTR and CPM mean makes results impossible to read and wastes budget. Learn these metrics before testing products. [L0519, 2020+; L1176, 2023]
- Meta Ads Manager metrics such as CTR, CPC and CPM describe ad delivery and click efficiency, not profitability. Decide on scaling only by comparing ROAS and cost per purchase against the breakeven and desired values. [L0445, undated]
- A high CTR does not prove a good ad when no purchases follow. [L1426, undated]
- Judging hook benchmarks from two different sets without naming which one you used. Pick one set, state it, and apply it consistently. [L0613, 2026]
- Reading the CTR (all) figure as link CTR when comparing against benchmarks. Confirm the variant. [L0874, 2026]
- Treating a low CPM as proof of a low CPC. CPC also depends on CTR. [L0774, 2023]
- Chasing the lowest possible bid rather than better ad quality. [L1031, 2018]
- Using an arithmetic error from a source as a benchmark. In one lesson the figure of about $33,000 for 500,000 impressions at a $15 CPM is wrong; the correct cost is $7,500. [L0871, 2021+]

#### Further details

- Click-through rate = clicks / impressions. An impression is counted each time the ad is shown, so a low result signals that the ad, image or price is not attracting clicks. [L1002, 2019]

- In the book niche, sustainable CPC and CPM sit around $0.80 to about $2. Costs in this range keep early tests affordable. [L1175, 2026]
