## Seasonality, Market Conditions and Special Cases

### Seasonal Planning and Q4 Cost Pressure

- Historical ROAS of 5-7 is the normal baseline. During peak seasons, when 70-80% of market budgets concentrate on holidays and seasonal events, a drop to 3.5-4.5 is normal, and sustained competition keeps ad costs high. [L0298, 2023]
- Q4 and Black Friday raise CPMs because large brands spend heavily, so customer acquisition gets more expensive late in the year. Plan budgets and expected costs with that increase in mind. [L0692, 2023]
- High CPMs on new products and new creative during peak periods, such as after Halloween, are normal because big brands spend millions at the same time. CPM falls as competitive spend normalizes. [L0298, 2023]
- Ad costs rise in Q4 because retailers and profitable businesses increase spending before year-end to reduce taxable profit. Black Friday through Christmas traffic is therefore more expensive. [L1110, 2019]
- To prepare for higher Q4 costs:
  1. Move part of the normal November and December ad budget into earlier months, when costs are lower. [L1110, 2019]
  2. Use that budget to build an audience of website visitors. [L1110, 2019]
  3. Remarket to those visitors through Black Friday, Cyber Monday and Christmas, with both ads and emails, which is the lowest-cost advertising available. [L1110, 2019]
- For cold-weather products, raise ad spend as the cold season starts in the target market, not after demand has peaked. Demand for these products rises as temperatures fall, so the budget should follow that rise. [L1316, 2024+]
- Increasing ad budgets is appropriate in a slow January when ROAS looks healthy, because sales typically pick up the following week after the post-Black Friday lull. [L0349, 2024]
- Dropshippers without warehouse stock cannot advertise during a supplier closure. An operator with stock can gain share from competitors that cannot run ads. [L0349, 2024]

### Ad Budget Formulas

- Monthly ad budget = target monthly revenue ÷ 10, because the target return on ad spend is 10x. [L1277, 2020]
- Daily budget = monthly ad budget ÷ 30. [L1277, 2020]
- Example: a 10,000 USD revenue target gives a 1,000 USD ad budget, about 33 USD per day. [L1277, 2020]
- Ad spend should not produce a return below 10x. Anything lower means the ads are losing money. [L1277, 2020]
- Example daily split for a 10,000 USD monthly revenue target:

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Monthly revenue target | 10,000 USD |
| Monthly ad budget at 10x return | 1,000 USD |
| Daily ad budget | about 33 USD |
| Google Shopping (product listing ads) | about 28 USD per day |
| Facebook remarketing | 5 USD per day |

Audit the ads every few days. [L1277, 2020]

- Conflict on ROAS targets: the 2020 source sets a 10x floor and says anything lower loses money. The one source source accepts a drop to 3.5-4.5 during peak seasons as normal. The sources do not say which governs a given campaign. [L1277, 2020; L0298, 2023]
- Planning CPM: use about 15 USD CPM when estimating ad cost per 1,000 impressions for the product's price point. [L1176, 2023]

### CPM Thresholds and Market Selection

- Alarm threshold for a digital product: a Facebook CPM above roughly 20-25 is expensive. Above 30, interactive ad formats are recommended to lower it. [L0678, 2023]
- Q4 testing rule: avoid testing in countries where CPM is above 35 USD, unless the product is already profitable there. Prefer countries with lower CPM for testing. [L1219, undated]
- The two thresholds apply to different decisions. The 20-25 and 30 figures are general alarm and action levels. The 35 USD figure is a cutoff for Q4 test markets. [L0678, 2023; L1219, undated]
- Interactive ads that get comments, likes and shares from the audience tend to lower CPM. [L0678, 2023]
- Targeting a different market is the most basic way to reduce CPM, because some countries deliver much cheaper impressions. [L0678, 2023]
- Procedure: find the country with the highest CPM and check whether it delivers results. [L0678, 2023]
  1. Open the ad account in Meta Ads Manager. [L0678, 2023]
  2. On the right side, click Breakdown. [L0678, 2023]
  3. Choose By delivery. [L0678, 2023]
  4. Review age, gender and country to see which combination gives the best results. [L0678, 2023]
  5. Stop spending on countries that show high CPM without good results. [L0678, 2023]
- Case: a digital product priced at 97 USD was tested with a Facebook ad set covering many countries, with CPM of about 50-80 USD. After switching to a single ad set with several English-speaking and European countries, CPM dropped to about 18. The seller had three sales at that point. [L0678, 2023]
- Do not judge a campaign on CPM alone. The case above shows CPM changing with the country mix, but the seller's sales count was still very small. [L0678, 2023]

### Advertiser Pullouts and Falling CPMs

- Meta ad prices work like an auction. The more advertisers bid for the same audience, the higher the price of each impression. When a large group stops bidding, prices fall for everyone, including those who did not depend on that group. [L1407, 2023+]
- Case, April 2025 (the lesson was recorded on Wednesday, April 30): Temu stopped running Meta ads on April 16. The speaker estimated that a large share of US Meta ad spend came from Chinese sellers and US dropshippers sourcing from China, and that this spend disappeared at once. A CPM drop across Meta followed within about two weeks. This is the speaker's estimate, not a verified figure. [L1407, 2023+]
- Temu was named as one of the large low-price sellers that spent heavily on Meta before pulling its ads in April. The speaker said Shein and similar sellers were also major spenders and that their pullback mattered too. These are the speaker's unverified estimates. [L1407, 2023+]
- Unverified estimates cited in the lesson:
  - An analyst estimated that about 7 billion USD of Meta's 132 billion USD in 2023 ad revenue came from China. The speaker assumed the real figure was higher in 2024 and 2025. [L1407, 2023+]
  - The speaker estimated that more than 10% of the US Meta ad budget came from Chinese companies or US dropshippers sourcing from China before tariffs and the ad pullback. [L1407, 2023+]
- When ad costs fall because large advertisers pause, the same budget reaches more people. This is temporary. Prices are expected to return to normal if the tariffs or market shift that caused the pullback end. [L1407, 2023+]
- Ad costs also fell during COVID, when many physical businesses such as retail stores and restaurants stopped advertising. Any large group of advertisers pulling out lowers prices, whatever the reason. [L1407, 2023+]
- Use falling CPMs to test ad goals that cost more when prices are high:
  - scaling spend on winning ads
  - lead generation ads that send visitors to an email opt-in page
  - ads that promote blog posts
  - campaigns that build social followers

  Do this only for stores that already have sales and profit, not for brand-new stores. [L1407, 2023+]
- If you already run Meta ads, compare your current CPM with your CPM one, two and three months ago. A lower CPM means the same budget reaches more people. [L1407, 2023+]

### Holiday and Event Campaigns

- Use the same ad format throughout the campaign: dark imagery with a simple sales banner overlay. Stage discounts: 50% off in pre-sale, then 60% off on Black Friday itself. The extra margin is small, only 10 percentage points, but it drives urgency. [L0308, 2023]
- Run the same holiday deal creative in both the Instagram and Facebook news feeds, with the same image and copy adapted to each placement. [L0799, 2021-22]
- State the exact end time in the body copy, such as midnight on the last day of the sale. A fixed, real end point makes the urgency believable. [L0799, 2021-22]
- Send holiday deal ads to a dedicated sale landing page for that event, such as a /black-friday URL, not the homepage. This keeps the click focused on the one offer the ad promises. [L0799, 2021-22]
- For a promotion lasting only a few days, rotate several creatives in bright, different colors plus one video variant, so the same audience does not see one ad for the whole sale window. Keep the same offer in every version and change only the visuals. [L0649, 2019]
- Run promotions on Reach and Frequency, not Conversions, and show them to warm audiences: website visitors from the past 180 days, plus Facebook and Instagram engagers. Do not exclude past customers, and never show promotions to cold audiences. [L0649, 2019]
- Meta remarketing for holiday promotions:
  - Create one new campaign per holiday.
  - Use static image ads, not dynamic remarketing ads.
  - Audience: all website visitors from the past 180 days.
  - Exclusion: all customers who purchased in the past 30 days.

  [L1223, 2024+]
- Retargeting worked well for large events such as Black Friday, Cyber Monday or Christmas, when site traffic is high. Outside such events it may not work consistently, especially after iOS 14 changed tracking. [L0646, 2023]
- Black Friday and Cyber Monday CPMs rise because competitors raise budgets, which makes targeting more expensive. A useful response is an extended Black Friday or Cyber Monday discount starting the day after the event. [L1185, 2023]
- A Meta ad account that had spent steadily for about 1.5 years was allowed to scale to very large daily budgets when spend jumped. A new account or newly set-up business manager may face restrictions when spend increases quickly. Build a steady spend history before planning a large sale. [L1363, 2023]
- For a holiday-themed campaign, restrict ads to the countries where the holiday is celebrated instead of running them broadly. For Cinco de Mayo, the example pointed to Mexico and the US. [L0577, 2023]

### Geographic and Gifting Niches

- GCC markets such as Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE showed a Facebook CPM of about 1 USD per 1,000 impressions. This is cheap, but these markets still required cash-on-delivery to convert. [L1212, undated]
- For gift-type products in the holiday season, test a broad audience in one ad set, alongside audiences such as grandparents or people with a friend who has a child, since they may buy as gifts. Avoid overly narrow interests with this approach. [L0692, 2023]

### Digital Products

- Validate the product with paid traffic before investing time in creating it. Keep early costs low by using existing market videos. [L0513, undated]
- Facebook is a strong platform for digital products, together with Pinterest and Etsy. [L0577, 2023]
- Pinterest and organic social media can also drive traffic to digital products, alongside paid Facebook and TikTok ads. [L0513, undated]
- Run Facebook and TikTok ads for a digital product as you would for a physical product:
  - Start with interest-based targeting that matches the problem and audience profile, such as age, gender and related hobbies.
  - Use the cost per customer from these ads to decide the price and how much room the margin allows.

  [L0513, undated]
- Facebook ads are a recommended paid traffic source for affiliate funnels. Free alternatives, if you want the first sale without spending on ads, are guest posts on other blogs, forum participation and active community engagement. [L0514, 2015]

### High-Ticket Products

- For high-ticket items, a lead campaign that drives people into a funnel with email capture, followed by a drip sequence, can work better than sending cold traffic straight to a sale. Higher-priced products need more patience and more spend to gather enough data. [L0678, 2023]
- Touchpoints before purchase:
  - Products around 50-60 USD may need about 7-10 touchpoints.
  - Products around 200 USD may need 20-30 touchpoints.

  Expect a higher-priced product to take more creative exposure and more time before results. [L1225, 2025]
- Example for a 200 USD product: one creative may need to be seen three or four times, and another about five times, before the buyer converts. Plan the testing budget around that exposure. [L1225, 2025]
- About 300 USD of spend was cited as a rough reference for a campaign to start learning. Higher-priced products typically need more spend and more time in market. Do not judge a higher-ticket campaign on a few days of data. [L1225, 2025]
- Do not start a high-ticket product with a small test budget such as 10 EUR per day. Collecting useful data at a higher cost per purchase takes too long. [L0693, 2024]
- For high-ticket products, organic social posts rarely convert directly. Post one or two times per week to show activity, and focus budget on proven paid ads.  [L0806, 2024]

### Print-on-Demand Products

- For print-on-demand products, Facebook carousel ads and shopping ads were reported to work best. [L1214, 2023+]

## Ad Operations, Delegation and Platform Maintenance

### Daily Tracking Routine

- Track these Meta ad metrics every day in the tracker: ad spend, CPM, CTR, cost per click, cost per purchase (compared with target cost per purchase), and ROAS. The tracker colours each metric green, amber or red against its benchmark. [L1354, 2025-26]
- Do not refresh the ad account every 20 minutes. Refreshing interrupts the algorithm's learning and drives emotional reactions. Step away from the dashboard so the campaign runs long enough to produce usable data. [L1445, 2026]
- Compare the set budget with actual spend in Ads Manager instead of trusting the displayed budget. A campaign can keep showing a daily budget that the account no longer spends after a failed payment. One account went from a 50 USD daily budget rising to about 100 USD back to a displayed 50 USD, while delivery stayed near 10 USD in 24 hours. [L1432, undated]

### Ad Account Limits and Ad Housekeeping

- Meta limits active or in-review ads to around 250 per ad account. Turn off non-performing ads to free space, rather than creating new copies of the same creative. [L0637, 2023+]
- To reuse an existing post and avoid creating new ads: [L0637, 2023+]
  1. Open the ad set and duplicate it. [L0637, 2023+]
  2. At the ad level, choose "Use existing post" instead of creating a new ad. [L0637, 2023+]
  3. Select the ad or paste its Post ID from the Page post column. [L0637, 2023+]
  4. Keep the same creative, so the engagement from the original ad is combined into one post. [L0637, 2023+]
- To apply a new offer to all ads: [L0650, undated]
  1. Copy the offer sentence from the store. [L0650, undated]
  2. Go to the ads and select all of them. [L0650, undated]
  3. Click edit on the ad and paste the offer into the primary text. [L0650, undated]
- If many advertisers report the same platform-side inconsistency, pause the affected campaigns and relaunch them after one or two days, since the problem is often solved within that time. [L0692, 2023]

### Batch Launching

- Batch processing applies the same targeting, budget and placement settings to an entire group of creatives and launches them all simultaneously, instead of creating each ad individually in Meta Ads Manager. This eliminates repetitive configuration steps and drastically reduces launch time. [L0207, 2025-26]
- The built-in Meta Ad Manager integration in Ad Manage.ai loads campaign data and analytics in 2.9 seconds. [L0207, 2025-26]

### Account Structure and Spend Control

- A new ad account may start with a daily spend limit of about $50. Split that daily budget across several interest audiences so each one gets enough spend to show usable metrics. [L0640, 2023]
- After about 100-200 sales, switch to a dedicated ad account with a simple structure of two to three main campaigns. Use one pixel dedicated only to that store. [L1217, 2023+]
- Products from the same niche can be tested in parallel in the same ad account and pixel. Separate ad accounts when a winning product belongs to a different niche. [L1217, 2023+]
- ** Facebook supports multiple ad accounts within one Business Manager. Keep one credit card per business network and separate ad accounts for each store. [L1277, 2020]

### Billing Charges and Payment Methods

- A Facebook ad account can start spending and bill the card on file even when no campaigns are switched on, with one case reported at about £10,000. Remove unused payment cards from ad accounts you no longer use. [L1214, 2023+]
- Threshold charges on new accounts:

  | Case | Amount | Assessment |
  |---|---|---|
  | Typical threshold charge on a new ad account | About 10 USD | Signals that the account will pay in the future. [L1347, undated] |
  | Threshold charge on a new account | 200 EUR | Not normal and should be disputed. [L1347, undated] |

- Example of an unexplained charge: a seller bought a Business Manager, created a new ad account and started spending. When the spend reached the threshold, Facebook charged 200 EUR that had not been spent, and the money was not held as a budget. Support forwarded the case to an internal team and no quick fix followed. The ad kept running and Facebook then tried to charge another 150 EUR for money not spent. The seller created a second ad account, and the third account was charged 16 EUR. The seller asked for a screenshot of the billing page to investigate, and the issue was described as rare and unusual. [L1347, undated]
- If Facebook charges an unexplained amount on an ad account: [L1347, undated]
  1. Take a screenshot of the billing page to document the charge. [L1347, undated]
  2. Contact Facebook support and ask for the charge to be reviewed. [L1347, undated]
  3. Do not assume the issue is resolved while ads are still running. Monitor the billing page for further charges. [L1347, undated]

### Facebook Shop Refunds

- Orders can come in through Facebook Shop without the buyer visiting the Shopify store, so refunds and returns for these orders must still be handled. Facebook can side with the buyer in disputes, so keep the order records in Shopify ready. [L1128, 2023]
- Case: a seller issued a refund for a Facebook Shop order to avoid trouble with Facebook. The buyer then appealed with Facebook and received a second refund, pulling money back from the seller's account. The seller appealed with Facebook by sending the Shopify order information and records, and Facebook reversed the refund and returned the money to the seller. [L1128, 2023]

### Delegating Ad Setup

- Delegate traffic and ads only after you know which Facebook or TikTok ad strategy works for your store. Otherwise a contractor who leaves or suddenly raises rates leaves you with no way to keep the ads running. [L1358, undated]
- To delegate ad campaign setup: [L1358, undated; L1360, undated]
  1. Have the team member prepare the creatives first, including videos and pictures for the ads. [L1358, undated; L1360, undated]
  2. Give the team member access to your Business Manager so they can build the campaign structure. [L1358, undated; L1360, undated]
  3. Tell them exactly how many ad sets you want, the budget, and how many creatives per ad set have been successful for you in the past. [L1358, undated; L1360, undated]
  4. Let them run the campaigns for a couple of days, while you monitor them closely. [L1358, undated; L1360, undated]
  5. Give feedback. [L1358, undated; L1360, undated]
  6. Once you have a proven, experienced team member and results are stable, let them manage the campaigns independently. [L1358, undated; L1360, undated]
