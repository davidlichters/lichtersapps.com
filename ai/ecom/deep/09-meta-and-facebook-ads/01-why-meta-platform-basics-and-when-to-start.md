## Why Meta, Platform Basics and When to Start

### Why Meta is the main paid channel for beginners

- Meta is the parent company of Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp and Oculus, so Facebook advertising now runs under the Meta name [L1020, 2020-26].
- Meta Ads Manager creates and runs ads on Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp and third-party websites and apps in the Audience Network [L1020, 2020-26].
- Facebook Ads is a paid social traffic source and one of the main paid channels for a Shopify store [L0597, 2020-26].
- Meta's algorithm is the oldest and most consistent at finding target customers. Other platforms' algorithms are built on Meta's data and approach, which makes Meta the most reliable choice for scaling [L0296, 2021+].
- Meta is the best platform for beginners among all paid traffic options because of consistent results, scalability and ease of use, despite ban risks [L0296, 2021+].
- Facebook still has the strongest algorithm compared with TikTok, Pinterest and Snapchat. A beginner should concentrate on Facebook and use at most two platforms in total [L0349, 2024].
- Facebook is the main channel when the core audience is active on it. Target women over 40 on Facebook, and test Google Ads and TikTok ads later [L0492, 2023+].
- Customer acquisition is built on profitable ads on Meta (Facebook and Instagram), so ad performance decides whether the business can grow [L0914, 2026].
- Paid traffic compared with organic: results within days instead of weeks, precise audience targeting, control over when to spend (day parting), and fast scaling from small to large budgets [L0296, 2021+].
- Facebook suits higher-ticket items if the product can justify the price [L0363, 2022+].
- Products priced above roughly 40 USD and aimed at an audience over 35 are better tested on Facebook and Instagram. They can succeed there even when they fail on TikTok [L1212, undated].
- Facebook was the main platform and fastest route to scaling in one operator's experience, with TikTok second. Google Ads were only tested for the brand at that time [L0519, 2020+].
- Facebook was the main source of traffic and sales for one store. Keeping Facebook as the core channel and testing a second platform reduces risk [L1439, 2024+].
- In one lesson's test for a female audience, Facebook produced better results than Pinterest, so Facebook was the main channel. Pinterest was not tested and could be checked as a second channel [L0388, 2024+].

### Platform basics: how Meta ads reach people

- Meta delivers an ad much like organic content. Its algorithm finds viewers based on how people respond to the creative. The advertiser's only levers are audience restrictions (targeting) and budget [L0639, 2023].
- Engagement drives delivery. Meta favours ads with strong engagement, and higher engagement usually brings lower CPM and lower cost per click. Creative quality should create engagement naturally; manipulating comments is an untested tactic [L0353, 2023].
- Meta punishes content that viewers skip or ignore. This raises CPMs and lowers CTR and engagement, so a poorly matched creative or audience drives costs up [L0639, 2023].
- Meta targets across three funnel stages [L0296, 2021+]:
  - Top funnel: cold prospects with a relevant problem.
  - Middle funnel: people who engaged (viewed, commented).
  - Bottom funnel: high-intent actions (add to cart, initiated checkout, purchase).
- Facebook has fewer targeting options than Google because it restricts some audience data, such as members of certain groups. Targeting is less precise than on YouTube, and a Facebook view can register when someone scrolls past a video [L0510, 2018].
- Facebook's algorithm tends to deliver to people with stronger purchase intent [L0363, 2022+].

### How the auction works

- Ads are sold through an auction. Advertisers compete for the chance to show an ad to one particular person, and the top-ranking bid claims the spot. A bid is the sum an advertiser will spend to reach that audience [L0977, undated].
- Facebook runs an auction for each ad impression, so the price depends on competition for the same audience. Bidding 5 cents above your maximum bid gives you priority in the auction, but you do not pay that maximum. You pay the auction price [L0643, 2015].
- Example of a two-advertiser auction: each side raises its bid to win more impressions. A bid of 0.50 dollars is outbid by 1.00 dollar, then by 1.10 dollars, then by 1.25 dollars. Every raised bid means a higher price for the same audience, so an advertiser leaving the auction lowers the price for everyone who stays [L1407, 2023+].

### How delivery and testing work

- A new video ad is first shown to a small test group, for example about 1,000 people. Meta optimises delivery toward the people who engage [L0398, 2023+].
- A strong creative earns more engagement and watch time from that group, so Meta finds similar people and reach grows. A weak creative gets fewer engaged viewers and reach shrinks [L0398, 2023+].
- Thresholds for pushing an ad to a larger audience [L0639, 2023]:
  - About 3% CTR or about 5% engagement, where engagement includes likes, comments and watching the video for more than 10 seconds.
  - At later stages, the examples given were 3 to 5% CTR and 6 to 10% engagement.
- Widening stages. The sources differ on the sizes [L0639, 2023; L0646, 2023]:

| Stage | Size per [L0639, 2023] | Size per [L0646, 2023] | Ids |
|---|---|---|---|
| Test | About 500 to 1,000 people | About 1,000 people | [L0639, 2023; L0646, 2023] |
| Next | About 1,000 to 5,000 people | About 10,000 people | [L0639, 2023; L0646, 2023] |
| Next | About 10,000 people | Keeps widening while CTR or engagement stays around 3 to 10% | [L0639, 2023; L0646, 2023] |
| Next | About 100,000 people | Keeps widening while CTR or engagement stays around 3 to 10% | [L0639, 2023; L0646, 2023] |

- This organic-style spread is why an ad with strong early engagement can keep growing [L0646, 2023].
- Cold traffic from the Meta traffic objective tends to be low quality and should not be scaled up. If it is already running and performing acceptably, leave it unchanged [L0360, 2024].

### Automation features

- Modern Facebook advertising uses five core automation features, known as Power5 [L0238, 2019]:
  1. Auto advanced matching
  2. Dynamic ads
  3. Automatic ad placement
  4. Account simplification
  5. Campaign budget optimization
- The strategy is to set your objective and let Facebook's system find profitable customers and distribute ads across all placements [L0238, 2019].

### Facebook vs TikTok

- TikTok ads are hard for beginners. Creatives fatigue after a few weeks, and constant video production is required, while Facebook offers the more powerful algorithm [L0349, 2024].
- Facebook is preferred over TikTok in this lesson because its algorithm is older and more stable, and older audiences (40+, 50+) are easy to target. TikTok suits younger-demographic products such as beauty, jewelry, fashion, health and wellness, fitness and toys, and its CPMs fluctuate more [L1177, 2023].
- Facebook tends to deliver stronger purchase intent, while TikTok gives broader reach where audience quality depends more on the product [L0363, 2022+].
- Testing counts: Facebook needed about four to five tested products to find a clear winner, while TikTok needed about seven to eight. Overlap between TikTok audiences is also lower because the audience is very large [L1436, undated].
- Facebook gives more chances per product test than TikTok. The risk on Facebook is account bans, which can be reduced by following a correct account setup process [L1382, undated].
- Case: in the lesson's Apple Watch strap test (summer), Facebook CPA was too high while TikTok CPA was good [L0577, 2023].
- Platform choice should be tested. Facebook also serves Instagram, so it reaches younger users than assumed. TikTok may serve a younger audience better. If ads do not work on Facebook, testing them on TikTok is a reasonable next step [L0999, 2023].
- Once a product works for one or two months on one channel, test it on TikTok, Google, Pinterest and organic social. A product that sells to one audience usually sells to others too [L1213, 2023].
- Native ads, Pinterest, Snapchat and Twitter ads are better treated as supplements to Facebook, not primary channels [L0349, 2024].

### Facebook and Instagram

- Facebook is the strongest platform for audiences over 30 and for reaching people outside major cities. Instagram works better for younger audiences in major cities [L0790, 2017].
- Instagram ads are created in the same ad platform as Facebook, with the same terms and targeting options, so audiences built for Facebook can be reused. Instagram is best for visual products that look attractive in an image or short video [L0643, 2015].
- Facebook owns Instagram, so running both together is a two-in-one approach for paid social [L0852, 2020].
- Instagram placements are slightly easier to get approved, but the return was lower in one case [L1440, 2021+].
- Set up an Instagram account and post a few times per week from the beginning, but do not use it for paid expansion until sales exceed about 50,000 dollars per month from Google Shopping and Facebook dynamic remarketing [L1279, 2021].

### Pinterest

- Pinterest is not beginner-friendly. Its setup requires pixel tag implementation and substantial effort to understand its algorithm, which deters most beginning operators [L0296, 2021+].
- Pinterest is a discovery and search platform where users come for inspiration and to buy. In the US its CPM typically runs around $3 to $5, which is cheaper than most ad platforms. Niches that fit well include home decor, fashion, beauty and health, and about 60% of users are women [L1133, undated].
- Pinterest has rare bans and accepts traditional dropshipping products that other platforms often reject. More than 25% of time spent on Pinterest is shopping-related [L1133, undated].
- Pinterest suits products for home decor, beauty, home improvement, inspiration, DIY and art. Results are less consistent than on Facebook or TikTok, but the platform is cheap to test [L1218, undated].
- First Pinterest campaign structure [L1133, undated]:
  - 1 campaign
  - 1 interest (stacked interests allowed)
  - 3 creatives
  - Campaign budget of $5 per day
  - Launch at 00:00 UTC
- Pinterest has no ad set budget option (no ABO). The only structure is a campaign budget optimization (CBO) campaign [L1134, undated].
- Testing a product on Pinterest ads can start at about $5 per day, but run at least 5 products at a time, about $25 per day in total. Finding winners usually takes more than a week [L1218, undated].
- Older test budget: a Pinterest test budget can start at about 5 to 10 dollars per day [L0962, 2021].
- Pinterest can be connected to a store, and small-budget Pinterest ads can be tested [L0671, 2021].
- Pinterest ads are not a first channel. Use them only after Google organic, Google Shopping and remarketing are dialled in. They can work for some stores [L0969, 2022].
- Do not spend on Pinterest ads until Google Shopping and Meta ads are scaled as high as they can go while keeping ROAS at 10x or above. Pinterest organic posting can be done earlier (2025) [L0972, 2025].

### Twitter (X)

- Twitter (X) has lower CPM than Meta, TikTok or Google because few e-commerce advertisers use it, but it is not beginner-friendly. The algorithm is unproven, Shopify integration is unavailable, pixel tracking is not yet implemented, tracking tags must be added manually, and only certain product types succeed [L0296, 2021+].
- X/Twitter ads were reported to have cheap CPMs and good click-through rates, mainly for specific countries such as the US or Gulf states. This was not tested yet and should be validated before budget is committed [L1108, 2023].

### YouTube, Audience Network and LinkedIn

- YouTube ads are not a core early channel. Most stores should not start them in the first six months to a year, because other channels offer more opportunity [L0671, 2021].
- The Audience Network shows ads on third-party news sites and partner websites that Meta controls. A shopper who left a store can later see that store's ads there. This placement reaches past visitors again; it does not find new buyers [L1020, 2020-26].
- LinkedIn ads are very expensive and have rarely been profitable for e-commerce, so they are not recommended for beginners. Relationships with LinkedIn groups and their sponsors are a better way to reach a B2B audience [L1069, 2019].

### Platform choice by product and audience

- Paid social is a separate channel group that includes Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest and Twitter. It is grouped apart from search traffic in the overall funnel plan [L0676, 2020-26].
- Search platforms such as Google, Amazon and YouTube search reach only about 5 to 10 percent of the market, while about 90 percent sits on social platforms such as Facebook, Instagram and Pinterest. Each platform needs its own conversation and an offer adjusted to it. Facebook works best as a conversation-starting channel [L0643, 2015].
- Social media is effective at reaching unaware prospects and turning them into problem-aware prospects. It is a weak way to find the small group of buyers ready to purchase now, apart from retargeting [L1392, 2021-22].
- Facebook can work across about 20 industries. Service businesses such as plumbing or bricklaying are harder to sell through ads because the need is urgent, not social. First decide whether your customers search for the product or discover it socially, then choose the platform [L0643, 2015].
- Organic Instagram growth was realistic around 2019, but algorithms are more complex now, so a modern brand typically needs paid advertising to reach buyers. Plan the marketing budget with paid media in mind from the start [L1438, 2023+].
- Paid social is not required for success. Dynamic remarketing is the main recommended use, and flashy video ads are not necessary to start [L1111, 2020].
- Sending Facebook and Pinterest traffic directly to an Amazon product listing does not convert well. These platforms work best for content marketing, email list building and retargeting, not cold traffic to a marketplace listing where the customer cannot see the brand story or benefit from the community [L0228, 2015].
- Facebook works well for product launches with heavy discounts and hyper-targeted audiences. Facebook users are in social mode, not buying mode, so ads need to build awareness and desire before purchase. This makes Facebook better suited to cold traffic acquisition than Amazon's buying-mindset audience [L0227, 2014].
- Facebook is currently difficult for beginners to test products on, because it is expensive and bans are common. In this case, Instagram placements were slightly easier to get approved, but the return was lower [L1440, 2021+].

### Channel order: Google first, Meta remarketing early

- Google has buying intent because people search for what they want. Facebook targets broad audiences who are not actively searching. This is why Google is the first platform to prioritise [L1090, 2021].
- Facebook ads are useful for cold traffic, but for buyers who actively search for a product, Google ads deliver more profitable traffic. Facebook is not dropped, but it is used with less priority [L0967, 2020].
- Facebook ads are best used for remarketing to people who already visited the store, since broad audience targeting is weaker for finding new buyers. Keep Facebook at about 10% of the total advertising budget [L1090, 2021].
- Starting Facebook campaigns before Google Shopping is a poor order, because Facebook is better used to capture visitors who left the store [L1069, 2019].
- Meta remarketing is worth setting up early even with a small budget, because return on ad spend is usually high when the budget is small and each sale is worth a lot. Set up Meta remarketing once the Google negatives are in place (2025) [L0807, 2025].
- Start Meta with remarketing ads only, and do not test cold Meta ads at the beginning. Scale Meta after Google Shopping has reached its practical budget limit (2025) [L0972, 2025].
- Facebook ads should start early with very small budgets and be used for dynamic remarketing. Start them when your Google ads go live [L0671, 2021].
- Remarketing should run from day one, since it usually produces the most profitable ads, but it cannot work without Google traffic feeding the audience [L1086, 2019].
- Paid promotion of Facebook page posts, such as boosting them to remarketing audiences of past visitors, should not start from day one. Google Ads offers easier early wins for a new store, so paid Meta posts come after the organic page is set up and active [L0653, 2020-26].
- Do not run Facebook awareness ads while the store is still being built or before suppliers are uploaded. Start them once the store has products and the pages are ready [L0468, 2020].
- Do not set up Facebook or Google ad accounts until supplier products are live in the store, because ads need real products to send traffic to [L0962, 2021].
- Do not launch Meta ads before the store has been reviewed and approved. Launching early is a common rookie mistake that wastes ad spend. Launch only after store approval [L0491, 2026].

### When to hold off on cold traffic

- Do not send cold Facebook traffic to the store until it generates at least 50,000 dollars in sales per month from Google Shopping and Facebook dynamic remarketing combined. Those channels are easier wins first [L1279, 2021].
- Sources differ on the gate. The 2021 source uses the $50,000 monthly sales gate above. The 2025 source says not to test cold Meta at the beginning and to scale Meta after Google Shopping reaches its practical budget limit [L1279, 2021; L0972, 2025].
- Cold Meta traffic from the traffic objective tends to be low quality and should not be scaled up [L0360, 2024].
- Pinterest scaling gate: do not spend until Google Shopping and Meta are scaled with ROAS at 10x or above [L0972, 2025].

### When to hold off on social commerce

- Use Facebook dynamic remarketing as one of the core paid channels before trying social commerce such as Instagram Shopping. Instagram social commerce is not a requirement and can distract early on [L0665, 2022].
- A store should reach about 50,000 dollars per month in revenue before testing social commerce [L0665, 2022].
- As of August 2019, the Facebook Shop channel was not a proven extra revenue source, so ads that send traffic to the store site remain the priority [L1069, 2019].
- Selling directly on Facebook is not recommended because the platform is not built for it. Send traffic to your own store or sales page instead. Facebook was rolling out seller listings, but at the time of the lesson they were not yet reliable [L0643, 2015].
- Selling directly inside a social platform was not recommended for Facebook at the time, because its seller listings were still unproven. Pinterest had run on-platform selling for longer and was described as comparatively good for it [L0643, 2015].

### Operator sequence for starting Meta

1. Store reviewed and approved; supplier products live in the store [L0491, 2026; L0962, 2021].
2. Google ads live: Google Shopping first for cold traffic [L1090, 2021; L1069, 2019].
3. Meta set up with remarketing ads only, at a small budget, once Google negatives are in place [L0972, 2025; L0807, 2025; L0671, 2021].
4. Instagram account created and posted to a few times per week from the beginning, without paid expansion yet [L1279, 2021].
5. Cold Meta traffic only after the revenue gate and Google Shopping limit are reached; check against the sources above [L1279, 2021; L0972, 2025].
6. Social commerce only after remarketing is strong and revenue is around 50,000 dollars per month [L0665, 2022].
7. Pinterest only after Google Shopping and Meta are scaled with ROAS of 10x or above; Pinterest organic posting can start earlier [L0972, 2025].
