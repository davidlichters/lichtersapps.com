### Kill and keep rules

#### When to kill an ad set or interest

- Cut an ad set that has not produced a sale after spending its break-even amount. If the ad set shows promising add-to-carts or initiate checkouts, or a good CTR, let it run one more day before deciding. [L0645, 2023]
- Use this procedure to decide when to turn off an ad set: 1. Let the ad set spend an amount equal to the cost of goods of the product (for example, $20 for a product costing $20). 2. Analyse the CTR of the ad set. 3. Check whether it brought a sale and whether the return on ad spend (ROAS) is good. 4. If the sale exists and the ROAS is good, keep the ad set running. 5. If not, turn it off and replace it with another interest or audience. [L0562, undated]
- After a test in which ABO has lost money, test a new interest in CBO with a $100 budget. Cut it if it has no add to cart after about $15 to $20 spent (for a $40 product with a $25 margin), or if it has no sale after $40 spent. [L0645, 2023]
- Judge an ad set after roughly $20 to $30 of spend. Pausing it earlier is premature impatience. Once it has passed that spend and still performs poorly, pause it and duplicate the best performing ad set instead. [L0650, undated]
- Spend roughly $20 to $30 on an ad set before judging it, because that amount shows whether the cost per acquisition is affordable against the break-even point. Pausing an ad set before it reaches that spend usually means judging it too early. [L0650, undated]
- Another operator used a stricter cut-off of about $10 to $15 of spend for a new ad that had not converted, and paused it at that point. This is a faster test than the $20 to $30 guideline and fits a tight starting budget. [L0650, undated]
- Wait about four days after publishing, so each ad set spends around $20, then analyse the results. Keep ad sets running only if they meet the thresholds for CTR and cost. [L0642, undated]
- Close an ad set in a test if it has spent $10 and its CTR is below 1.5%. If an ad has spent only around $0.30, leave it running because there is too little data. [L0642, undated]
- An ad with a CTR around 1% after more than $10 of spend is a weak signal and can be paused. An ad with a clearly higher CTR and low spend (around $4) can be kept running because the spend is still too small for a verdict. [L0650, undated]
- Ads with spend of $20 to $30 and no sales can be closed. If all ads produce sales, keep them running together in the same ad set. [L0773, 2023]
- Remove a creative only when it performs badly in a consistent way across all ad sets. If results are mixed, keep it and retest it before deleting it. [L0692, 2023]
- Ads that show sales stay in place. [L0773, 2023]
- Keep ad sets that are profitable, close ad sets that are not, and test a different interest. [L0440, 2023+]
- Profitability cannot be expected immediately. Finding the right audience, the right ad and the right offer on the website usually takes time before an ad set becomes profitable. [L0440, 2023+]
- If CTR stays at or below 0.3 to 0.5 after about $100 in spend, replace the creative. If CTR stays very low after replacing it, stop the product earlier. [L1184, 2023+]
- A winning product shows initial traction within the first week of running Facebook ads. Early positive signals include audience engagement with the creative, website visits, and user actions such as adding to cart or saving to wishlist. These indicators that the audience resonates with the product justify continuing to test that product-audience combination. [L0229, undated]
- Dropshipping is about testing rather than overthinking: launch the product, gather data and decide based on the results. If the data shows good CTR and low CPC, the product is worth more investment. If not, move on. [L1172, 2023+]

#### Kill when no add to cart or no sale (combined with rules 1 to 4)

- Kill an ad set if it has made no sale after testing, but apply Rules 1 to 4 first. Details are in the Rules section below. [L0229, undated]
- Test procedure step: after spending another BECPP, check whether ROAS is above BE-ROAS. If yes, let it run. If no, kill it. [L0375, undated; L0376, undated]

#### Kill and reset rules for ads and products

- Judging a new product's ads after a day or two is premature and wastes money on losing ads. Cutting an ad that has spent beyond its profit margin without a sale is the usual decision, but the margin must be checked first. [L0999, 2023]
- Judging an ad as a failure after $10 to $15 spend with no add-to-carts is premature. First check whether the offer and product page are attractive enough, and test different offers and bundles before turning ads off. [L1220, undated]
- Judging a new ad after a few hours on a $5 daily budget is premature, since small budgets need a day or more before the data shows what works. Facebook normally spends well, so give it time before cutting. [L0645, 2023]
- Do not judge a Facebook ad or ad set before it has spent roughly $20 to $30. [L0650, undated]
- Remove an ad set in testing when it spends beyond break-even without a sale, unless it shows add-to-carts or CTR signals. [L0645, 2023]
- Pushing a product at audiences who do not respond to it leads ad platforms to penalise the advertiser's content. Founders who rely on their own intuition that a product is great, instead of letting the market react, often run into this. [L0760, undated]
- Running ads in a market with strong competition is normal: some advertisers scale a product in a market while others run only a single ad for it. A single ad in a market does not prove the product fails there. [L0691, 2024]
- Cutting ad creatives and age groups too quickly after a few good sales can cause CTR and sales to drop sharply, so changes to a working test need careful evaluation rather than sudden large cuts. [L0690, 2022+]
- Test a product for at least the spend set out in its budget before deciding, and do not replace a product after one bad day. [L1124, 2026]

### Rules 1 to 4 and safety rule

one source decision rules for an ad set after launch. Apply them in order.

1. **Rule 1:** Allow each ad set to spend its break-even cost per purchase before making optimisation decisions, OR let it run for three days, whichever comes later. For products with margins under $20, prioritise the three-day minimum to accumulate sufficient data. [L0229, undated]
2. **Rule 2:** After Rule 1 is complete, check whether the ad set generated at least one sale.
   - If yes, let it continue spending another full break-even cost per purchase amount.
   - If no sale occurred, check the CPC. If CPC is below $1.50, extend the ad set for one additional day. If CPC is $1.50 or above, deactivate the ad set and replace it with a new interest. [L0229, undated]
3. **Replacement procedure:** To replace an underperforming ad set while testing new interests:
   1. Identify the ad set that achieved at least one sale (the winning ad set).
   2. Duplicate that winning ad set.
   3. Open the Audience section and click the "Suggested" button to see interest recommendations based on the current audience.
   4. Select a new suggested interest (e.g., if current is "Carpet," try "Living Room" or related suggestions).
   5. Verify the audience size is reasonable (e.g., 100+ million for broad targeting).
   6. Click Publish to launch the new ad set.
   7. Deactivate the old underperforming ad set.
   8. Apply all rules starting from Rule 1 to this newly created ad set. [L0229, undated]
4. **Rule 3:** If an ad set survived Rule 2 by receiving a one-day extension but made no sale within that 24-hour period, deactivate it and replace it using the same duplication and interest-suggestion method described in Rule 2, starting the new ad set through Rule 1 again. [L0229, undated]
5. **Rule 4:** Once an ad set clears Rules 2 and 3, allow it to spend an additional amount equal to double the break-even cost per purchase (not double the total, but double the initial threshold). By the time this additional amount is spent, the ad set must achieve a return on ad spend (ROAS) equal to or higher than its break-even ROAS. If it falls below break-even ROAS at that point, deactivate and replace it. [L0229, undated]

**Safety rule:** If after running ads for three to four days your entire campaign shows zero sales across all ad sets, stop the campaign immediately. Do not continue spending in hopes of organic improvement. Proceed to troubleshooting instead. [L0229, undated]

**Safety rule for a campaign with no sales:** Pause a campaign immediately if no sale occurs within the first $50 of spend. [L0298, 2023]

**Reduce, do not kill outright:** Reduce budget if results are consistently poor for the last 4 days. Test a reduced version before completely pausing. [L0298, 2023]

**First-day rule for high-budget campaigns:** Pause the campaign for the day if it spends twice the break-even cost per purchase without producing a sale. For example, with a break-even CPA of $30 and a $100 launch budget, turn it off once it has spent $60 without a purchase. [L1335, 2023+]

**Sources disagree on the first-day stop.** The one source 2026 course says not to cut a product after a single day or after only $50 without sales, because Meta needs time to test segments. [L0037, 2026] The one source safety rule stops a campaign with no sale within $50 of spend. [L0298, 2023] The first applies to deciding whether to cut a product. The second applies to a campaign running with no sales. For a high-budget campaign, the 2x break-even stop at [L1335, 2023+] applies. The one source case in which day one looked like failure and became profitable on day three shows why a day-one cut can be wrong. [L1124, 2026]

**Patience against day-one panic.** A new Meta test with a high cost per purchase on day one should not be switched off right away, because one day of spend and a single sale is too little data to judge a product. [L1124, 2026] Cutting a test after the first day with a high CPA and moving on to the next product, a habit known as paper handing, often kills products that would have become profitable. Judging a product on the first day alone is the main reason good tests get lost. [L1124, 2026]

### Scaling thresholds

- Scale an ad set once it has produced around four purchases. [L0524, 2023]
- Scale an interest only after it reaches the sales threshold set in advance (for example six to seven sales) and is profitable. [L0361, 2024]
- If any running ad set brings 4 or more profitable sales, scale it. [L0375, undated; L0376, undated]
- Once a sale comes in, spend up to the break-even cost per purchase again. [L0502, 2020+]
- Example of a scaling outcome: a new product runs five ad sets at about $12 each. Three are switched off for weak performance, and the other two keep running until they reach seven profitable sales, which can take about one to one and a half weeks. [L1107, 2023]
- When scaling into CBO, look for at least three purchases on the ad set before trusting the result. [L0645, 2023]
- Test a new product with interest targeting first. Once it reaches roughly 10 to 20 to 30 sales, add an Advantage+ broad campaign alongside it. [L1161, 2024+]
- Once a sale starts, the ads work: find the winning interest, country and offer. [L1161, 2024+]
- Keep ad sets with sales running, and put the budget behind the country that performs best rather than spreading it evenly. [L1220, undated]
- Scale only the creative that performs, and stop the rest early. [L0561, 2023+]

#### Day-of-week and schedule

- Product-testing campaigns are commonly run from Thursday to Sunday. Running them on Monday to Wednesday is acceptable too, as long as the data is broken down by country, age group and gender (if several genders are tested) afterwards. [L1220, undated]
- Do not assume that weekends or Thursday to Sunday are always the best days to test audiences. Day-of-week patterns differed strongly between months and products, so check your own data over a longer period before using them. [L0692, 2023]
- Spread a test budget across every day of the week rather than a short burst. A three-day run misses buyers who are paid on a particular day, such as Thursday, and gives weaker data. [L1225, 2025]
- Schedule the start for the next day at 12:00 AM in the ad account time zone. [L0648, undated]

#### Editing during the test window

- Once a test campaign is running and the store, creatives and product page are ready, do not edit the campaign during the test window. In the example, leaving every setting untouched for two days brought CPA down sharply on its own. [L1124, 2026]
- one source day checks (procedure): 1. Launch a CBO test with three ad sets at a $100 daily budget, with all creatives placed in each ad set. 2. Make no edits to the campaign for three days. 3. Compare each day's CPA against the break-even CPA for the product. 4. Once the campaign produces purchases below break-even, scale it (the scaling method was not covered in the lesson). [L1124, 2026]
- CBO test rhythm: let the test run for two days, check the metrics and P&L on days 1 and 2, and on day 3 scale only if the product is green and the margins are good. Keep the same CBO and duplicate it with new creatives on day 3 instead of restarting. [L0348, 2026]
- Changing audience, placement or other ad set settings during a product test muddles the results. Keep default settings unless a legal or practical constraint requires a change. [L1193, 2026]
- Keep ad sets under review by checking daily data on creative, audience and trend when a CBO runs at $100 or more per day. [L1345, 2021+]

#### Product rhythm

- Run product tests in a repeating loop: test a product, give it enough time and budget to read the result, and only then move on to the next product. Do not replace a product after one bad day. [L1124, 2026]
- Testing a product: products are not given a fixed budget in advance. If a product is not profitable, switch to the next product. [L0440, 2023+]
- If no ad set brings any sale after testing at least 10 interests, switch to the next product. [L0375, undated; L0376, undated]
