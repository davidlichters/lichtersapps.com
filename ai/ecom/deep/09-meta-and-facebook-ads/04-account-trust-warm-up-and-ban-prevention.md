## Account Trust, Warm-up and Ban Prevention

### Ban Rates and What They Mean for Planning

- Older sources put the ban rate for new advertisers very high. One source said Facebook bans beginners about 90% of the time, because bots are placed to protect user experience [L0296, 2021+]. Another claimed 90-95% of beginners are banned, but this is an unverified estimate [L0637, 2023+]. A speaker estimated 80-90% at that time [L1440, 2021+]. These figures are older and unverified.
- A newer source says bans for new advertisers have become less common and are no longer a universal problem. Recovery solutions exist for properly set-up accounts that do face suspension [L0296, 2021+]. Plan from the newer position, but do not treat any percentage as a guarantee [L0296, 2021+; L0637, 2023+].
- Meta's ban systems are largely automated. New advertisers are screened by algorithms before any human looks at the account [L0637, 2023+].
- Prevention steps lower the risk. No step guarantees that a ban will not happen [L0637, 2023+; L1440, 2021+].
- Meta reacts to new accounts that show unstable or suspicious behaviour, such as changing devices, IP addresses, payment details, or sudden spend jumps. Keeping device, IP, proxy, payment and spend patterns consistent makes the account look like one stable, legitimate user [L1477, 2026].

### Profile Age and Identity

- Use an older personal profile, at least 2 years old, not a new dummy profile [L0637, 2023+].
- A brand-new dummy profile usually gets the account banned right away, because Meta has no activity history to trust [L0637, 2023+].
- Set up a new business account using an existing personal profile that already has history, not a brand-new account. A new account with no data and sudden trading can look suspicious [L0950, 2023].
- Do not run ads from a bare personal account. Set up a business profile in Meta Business [L0341, 2023].
- Running ads from a personal profile without a business account and business details raises the risk of a flag. Meta reviews new advertisers for legitimacy, so set up the business and provide as much verifiable information as possible [L0343, 2023].
- Confirm the profile identity early, before any problem. When Meta asks for ID verification after a ban, it checks that a real, legitimate person runs the ads, so the platform should already know who you are [L0637, 2023+].
- Profile identity confirmation is reached from the personal profile via Settings & privacy > Settings > identity confirmation. Upload a government or national ID document there. The menu path is as stated in the source; check the current Meta UI, since menu names change [L0637, 2023+].
- Meta can suspend a new ad account, sometimes instantly, if identity verification is not completed in time. Submit the requested ID promptly [L0341, 2023].
- Meta may suspend a new ad account right after creation and may ask for identity confirmation within about 48 hours. Respond in time, because missing the deadline can lead to a ban [L0343, 2023].
- Meta can grant account verification on Facebook and Instagram after substantial ad spend, usually through an account representative who requests the verification [L0466, 2018].

### Two-Factor Authentication

- Enable two-factor authentication on the personal profile via profile > Settings & privacy > Settings > Security and login > Two-factor authentication. The purpose is to show Meta that the same person who verified the identity is the one running the ads [L0637, 2023+].
- Set up two-factor authentication for every admin on a Meta business account, especially admin accounts. One account was reportedly banned because a non-admin user had no two-factor authentication set up [L0343, 2023; L0341, 2023].
- Under Business settings > Business options, the Two-factor authentication setting can require a login code to access the business portfolio from unknown browsers. The option was set to No one in the example. The Access recommendations setting suggests extra assets to people who manage permissions [L0651, 2026].

### Business Manager and Business Verification

- Keep the Business Manager separate from the personal profile. A ban on the ad account or Business Manager should not take down the personal profile. In the worst case only the Business Manager is shut down and a new one can be created. A personal profile ban can block Facebook use entirely [L0637, 2023+].
- Create the Business Manager at business.facebook.com/create, not as a personal ad account [L0637, 2023+]. Create it with business details rather than the default personal one [L0650, undated].
- Create the Business Manager and verify it with a legitimate business. A business account without a real business can cause verification problems and more ad account bans [L0950, 2023].
- Business verification is optional but strongly increases trust with Meta. Verify in Business settings by submitting one business document, such as an LLC, Ltd or similar. The review took about 4-5 days in the source. Verification does not restrict which countries you can target [L0637, 2023+].
- Verified businesses get more flexibility and access to live chat support. Verification also helps Facebook see the business as legitimate rather than as a scam risk [L0709, 2017+].
- One account went through a 13-step verification process and had no restrictions or bans for about 1.5 years afterwards [L1440, 2021+].
- Add 1-2 trusted people to the Business Manager with page and pixel rights [L0637, 2023+]. Extra people add trust and protect the assets. If the main personal profile gets banned or loses access, they keep access to pages, pixels and ad accounts [L0637, 2023+].
- Keep the same ad account and Business Manager for a store, rather than creating new ones for each product or test. A stable, consistently used account is less likely to be blocked [L0650, undated].
- Separating Business Managers by product risk is a containment practice. Use a separate Business Manager for products that carry ban risk, such as products with health claims, revealing skin or controversial topics. Keep the main Business Manager for safe products and for other stores [L1347, undated]. Do not use a separate Business Manager to run a product that the account is already restricted for.
- Do not link your personal profile to a new Business Manager, and do not send invitations between the old and new accounts. In one reported case this led to both accounts being blocked [L1347, undated].
- Browser for Business Managers: use Brave for new Business Managers and keep Google Chrome for existing ones. Microsoft Edge and Safari (on a Mac) are also options for separating accounts [L1347, undated].
- Do not create a brand-new personal profile to get around limits. Profiles with no history are often banned. The approach is a trusted friend or family member with an established profile who creates the Business Manager and adds you as admin or partner. This avoids the need for their profile login [L1213, 2023; L1218, undated]. Do not use this arrangement to get around a restriction on an account, because that is circumvention of Meta's enforcement.
- Creating a second Meta Business Manager soon after the first can trigger a security restriction or suspension. If the account is restricted, request help through Get support on the Meta business help page and complete the identity verification Meta asks for. Do not open more accounts [L0806, 2024].

### Page Warm-Up

- A warmed-up Facebook Page and profile help, because Meta and visitors trust an established account more than a new one. Skipping the warm-up makes it harder for ads to run and convert [L1021, 2026].
- Fill the page with business information: phone number, business address, description and website link [L0650, undated].
- Fill the About section in detail, including the website. Before running conversion campaigns, a new page should have around 10-20 likes. Avoid running ads right after the page is created [L0637, 2023+].
- Warm up the page with 2-3 simple posts over 1-2 days before starting conversion campaigns. Good posts are happy or product-related content, or a screenshot or banner from the store, without hard sales messages or scammy claims such as weight-loss promises [L0637, 2023+].
- Engagement posts can be a product photo with a positive quote and no traffic link [L0650, undated].
- Keep warm-up creatives simple: product-only images or a positive quote with no people in them, because an image that shows a person can cause problems during warm-up [L0650, undated].
- Make the page look legitimate and branded, so it does not look scammy to Meta or to users. A bare or generic page raises the risk of problems [L1477, 2026].
- The page is the asset that gets restricted first when ads are banned. One profile can run several products under one page, but each product needs a clear business identity behind it. Build the page and Business Manager as the trusted front of the store [L0637, 2023+].
- Paid traffic networks are more likely to trust a store that shows a physical postal address in the footer. An address supports ad platform review, even though it may not be kept long term [L0681, 2021-22].
- Meta tends to prioritise conversion campaigns that send traffic off the platform, so new pages, Instagram accounts and Business Managers attract extra scrutiny. Every new asset linked to the ads adds risk of a temporary block [L0650, undated].
- Launching a conversion campaign with purchases on a brand-new Facebook page and account, without any warm-up, was linked to a temporary block. A new page, a new Instagram account or a new Business Manager each adds risk, because Facebook does not trust new assets yet [L0650, undated].
- A newly created Facebook page and Instagram account can be blocked from running ads, including remarketing, until the accounts show some activity. Post regularly on both before planning paid campaigns. Look in community groups for others who had the same block (2023) [L0804, 2023].

#### Instagram

- Creating a new Instagram account or connecting Instagram to the ads adds work and risk. A connected account must be posted to regularly and needs a real follower base [L0650, undated].
- Running traffic-only ads without connecting Instagram sends visitors straight to the website [L0650, undated].
- Messenger requires only the Facebook Page. Choosing Instagram as a message destination requires connecting an Instagram account first through the Connect account option [L0614, 2026].

#### Page Warm-Up Procedure

Warm up new Facebook assets before running conversion campaigns:

1. Secure the personal profile with two-factor authentication [L0650, undated].
2. Fill the Facebook page with business information such as phone number, business address, description, and website link [L0650, undated].
3. Create a separate business manager with business details rather than using the default personal one [L0650, undated].
4. Complete business verification [L0650, undated].
5. Run an engagement campaign of about $1 to $5 for one to two days with simple, positive posts (for example a product photo with a positive quote) and no traffic link [L0650, undated].
6. Then move to conversion campaigns [L0650, undated].

### Ad Account Warm-Up: Engagement Campaigns

Sources disagree on budget, duration and audience. Both approaches are recorded below. Use the one source 2026 setup when you want a fixed Messenger-based warm-up with a defined stop point, and the one source setup when you want the smallest possible spend.

| Setting | one source 2026 approach | one source approach |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | $10 daily for the simple setup [L0348, 2026]; $20.00 daily under Ad set budget, with Ads Manager showing a maximum of $35 per day and $140 per week [L0614, 2026] | $1-5 per day [L0637, 2023+; L0650, undated]; $5-10 per day in another source [L1213, 2023] |
| Stop point | Switch off once it has spent about $8 to $10 [L0614, 2026]; roughly $10 is spent on the warm-up in the simple setup [L0348, 2026] | Run for at least 1-2 days [L0637, 2023+]; run for one to two days at most, because longer runs add low-quality audiences to the pixel data [L1213, 2023]; off after 2 days when published without the timeline [L0637, 2023+] |
| Audience | Four countries (United States, Canada, Australia, United Kingdom) with the custom audience blank and Advantage+ audience on [L0348, 2026]; age 18-65+ [L0348, 2026]; or United States with Broad audience, estimated 73,700,000 to 86,700,000 [L0614, 2026] | Worldwide or United States, Canada and the top four countries, with broad targeting giving about 100 million to 500 million [L0637, 2023+] |
| Creative | Image ad with one image [L0614, 2026]; animal picture such as a dog [L0614, 2026]; or a simple non-explicit image such as a company logo or neutral photo [L0348, 2026] | Store banner, smiley faces or a smiling dog, with a short positive quote as copy [L0637, 2023+] |
| Message destination | Manual, Messenger [L0614, 2026] | Not specified |

#### one source 2026 Engagement Warm-Up

- A small engagement campaign with a low budget can warm up a new or inactive Meta ad account before running campaigns that matter. The goal is account activity, not sales [L0614, 2026].
- Simple setting: after creating the pixel, open the ad account, press Create, then start an Engagement campaign with recommended settings and a budget of about $10. Later ads from the account should go active much faster [L0348, 2026].
- Ad set: target four countries (United States, Canada, Australia, United Kingdom), leave the custom audience field blank, keep the Advantage+ audience setting on, and use a daily budget of $10 [L0348, 2026].
- Age 18-65+ with Advantage+ audience switched on lets Meta show ads beyond the suggested audience when that is likely to improve results [L0348, 2026].
- Warm-up ad: untick the Advertiser Ads option, remove the media, upload any non-explicit simple image such as a company logo or a random neutral photo, publish, and wait for it to finish processing. This spends roughly $10 [L0348, 2026].
- Campaign objective: Engagement. Create a Manual engagement campaign instead of the recommended setup for finer control. The campaign name can stay at its default [L0614, 2026].
- Budget: set the Daily budget under Ad set budget to $20.00 USD. Ads Manager then shows a maximum daily spend of $35 and a maximum weekly spend of $140 for that setting [L0614, 2026].
- Budget strategy: Campaign budget (Advantage+ campaign budget, which spreads spend automatically across the campaign) or Ad set budget (set per ad set, with its own bid strategy and budget schedule options). For a single-ad warm-up, Ad set budget gives direct control over the Daily budget [L0614, 2026].
- Message destinations: choose Manual destination, not Automatic (recommended), then select Messenger. Automatic lets Meta send people to whichever messaging app they use most, so Messenger is not guaranteed [L0614, 2026]. Automatic can send people to Instagram instead of Messenger. Set Manual destination when Messenger must be the target [L0614, 2026]. Automatic sends people to whichever messaging app Meta expects will lower ad cost, while Manual limits the ad to the apps you tick [L0614, 2026].
- Audience definition: keep the setting on Broad rather than Narrow. Under Locations, add United States, which the tutorial recommends, but any audience works for a warm-up. Ads Manager showed an estimated audience of 73,700,000 to 86,700,000 with the broad setting [L0614, 2026]. Broad audiences can improve performance and reach more people likely to respond. Meta notes that its estimates do not include Advantage+ audience options and can change over time [L0614, 2026].
- Audience controls can be set for all campaigns in the ad account to reflect legal or practical constraints only, such as restricted locations or age ranges. They are not needed for a standard warm-up unless a constraint applies to the account [L0614, 2026].
- Ad format: image ad with one image. Ads Manager accepts one image or video per ad, and the image may be cropped automatically for Square, Vertical and Horizontal placements [L0614, 2026].
- Call to action: Send message. People who see the ad can message the Page directly, which matches the goal of an engagement ad that starts conversations in Messenger [L0614, 2026].
- Identity: the field sets which profile appears in the ad, usually the Facebook Page that represents the business. Messages started from the ad are routed to the chosen destinations, so the Page must be the one that will answer the customer [L0614, 2026].
- Partnership ads and Multi-advertiser ads are separate toggles that default to Off. Keep both Off for a plain warm-up ad. Partnership ads use signals from both accounts, and Multi-advertiser ads can appear beside other advertisers and may crop or resize the creative [L0614, 2026].
- Switch the warm-up engagement ad off once it has spent about $8 to $10 in total. Letting it keep running after that spends budget without any further purpose [L0614, 2026].

Warm-up engagement campaign in Meta Ads Manager:

1. Go to Campaigns, click + Create, and choose the engagement objective [L0614, 2026].
2. Select Manual engagement campaign instead of Recommended settings [L0614, 2026].
3. Leave the campaign name at its default [L0614, 2026].
4. Set the Daily budget under Ad set budget to $20.00 USD [L0614, 2026].
5. Under Message destinations, select Manual destination and tick Messenger [L0614, 2026].
6. Under Locations, add United States and keep Audience definition on Broad [L0614, 2026].
7. Under Ad creative, choose Create image ad, select one picture of an animal (for example a dog), and upload it [L0614, 2026].
8. Enter a short headline (see the pattern unit for the headline style) [L0614, 2026].
9. Click Publish and accept Meta's terms [L0614, 2026].
10. Turn the ad off once it has spent about $8 to $10 [L0614, 2026].

#### one source Optional Warm-Up

Ad account warm-up, as an optional safety step:

1. Create a single campaign with one ad set and one image ad [L0637, 2023+].
2. Set the budget at $1-5 per day for an engagement campaign [L0637, 2023+].
3. Target either worldwide or the United States, Canada and the top four countries, keeping the targeting broad, which gives an audience of about 100 million to 500 million [L0637, 2023+].
4. Use a simple image such as a store banner, smiley faces or a smiling dog, with a short positive quote as copy [L0637, 2023+].
5. Run it for at least 1-2 days [L0637, 2023+].
6. Then start conversion campaigns [L0637, 2023+].

- Post engagement campaigns used only to warm up a new ad account or page cost about $5 to $10 per day and are optional. Run them for one to two days at most, because longer runs add low-quality audiences to the pixel data [L1213, 2023].
- To run a warm-up engagement ad without publishing it on the main page, use the normal ad creation flow, select the page as the advertising identity, and upload the image directly. The ad is not published to the page's timeline and can be switched off after 2 days [L0637, 2023+].
- Running conversion-objective campaigns directly, without a warm-up engagement campaign, can still produce good results and engagement. Engagement campaigns can help in pet, kids and some fashion niches but are not required [L1213, 2023].
- Large advertisers spending roughly $100k to $1M or more can skip warm-up and launch campaigns directly. Smaller accounts should still warm up for 1-2 days before conversion campaigns [L0637, 2023+].
- Large, established accounts with a long payment history can skip warm-up, but new accounts should not [L0637, 2023+].
