## Lead Generation, Engagement and Audience Building

### Audience Building and Engagement Foundations

- Audience building runs in a cycle: create a seed audience from initially engaged followers, promote new content to the seed audience and an engagement audience to build awareness, build look-alike audiences from the engaged followers (look-alikes engage with the content), and repeat until everyone in the target market knows the brand name. [L0237, 2018]
- Engagement compounds: when people like, comment or share, their friends see the post in their own feeds, so reach grows without paying for those extra viewers. Well-targeted content can create a snowball effect and very low cost per engagement. [L0487, 2018]
- Facebook shows the size of the audiences you have reached, so you can see how much of the interested market you have covered. Keep reaching new people until the audience of interested people is largely exhausted. [L0487, 2018]
- Early audience building can run on about 5 dollars per day of ad spend, and spend can be increased whenever you are ready. Spreading spend out is sensible while no product is for sale yet. [L0487, 2018]
- Engagement-objective campaigns for audience building have no link or call to action to a store. The ad text asks people to like and share, so the only goal is engagement. [L0487, 2018]
- A test of interest for a product without its own page: run an engagement ad (likes, shares) to compare which product attracts the most attention, instead of a conversion ad. This is a test of interest, not a sales test. [L0552, 2023]

### Organic Reach Limits and Page Strategy

- Facebook announced organic reach cuts to about 6% of the people who see a page's posts, and as low as 2% for business pages (figures as announced at the time). Paid ads are therefore needed to reach a target audience. Because few businesses pay to play, competition for paid placements stays lower. [L0641, 2014]
- A Facebook page is required to run ads, but a page has very low organic reach. Use a Facebook group for most engagement and traffic, and let retargeting ads invite engagers to join the group. [L0487, 2018]
- Relying on the organic reach of a Facebook page is a mistake: a post on a page with about 2,000 likes reached only around 100 people. Paid ads or a group are needed to get posts in front of the audience. [L0487, 2018]
- Facebook organic reach is very low, so use Facebook and Instagram pages mainly to show that the business is active and real when visitors search for it. [L0962, 2021]
- Regular posts on a Facebook page, even on old pages, barely reach anyone, so reach usually has to be paid for with a small boost. Organic follower growth alone does little until paid campaigns already perform well. (Advice from 2024.) [L0666, 2024]
- Organic follower growth is easy, but converting followers into sales is hard, so organic page growth is usually a lower priority than paid traffic. It makes sense mainly after monthly paid spend has reached a satisfactory return on ad spend. (Advice from 2024.) [L0666, 2024]
- Regular posting to Facebook is a positive signal for a fashion store, so posting frequency and content types should be kept up. [L0838, 2020-26]
- Post on social media a couple of times per week mainly so buyers can see the business is active and real before purchasing. This is for conversion trust, not for reaching new people. [L0941, 2022]
- A Facebook community about the product topic can be built first and used to get ideas for content, share it and promote offers. Content is then validated through the audience. [L0941, 2022]

### Algorithm Signals and Zapier Engagement Alerts

- Facebook and Instagram algorithms prioritize fast initial engagement (likes, comments, shares) on new posts. Automating team alerts to engage within minutes of publishing improves organic reach. [L0286, 2018]
- Create a dedicated Slack "Engagement" channel where Zapier automatically alerts team members (@channel) whenever new Instagram or Facebook posts publish, requesting likes and comments within their earliest convenience. This leverages team assets to improve algorithmic reach at no cost. [L0286, 2018]

### Facebook Groups for Engagement and Sales

- Use active niche Facebook groups to build sales:
  1. Identify groups in your niche that are active.
  2. Build authority by adding value to discussions and staying active.
  3. Promote your sales inside the group.

  This approach requires a personal account. [L0920, 2018]
- When looking for niche Facebook groups, choose groups with new posts at least a few times per week. Less active groups produced few prospective customers in this approach. [L1357, 2018]
- Participate actively in niche Facebook groups and add value on a regular basis before promoting sales. This makes sales posts look like contributions rather than spam, and you can do it yourself or hire a social media manager. [L1357, 2018]
- Joining these Facebook groups generally requires a personal Facebook account. If you do not want your personal account linked to the store, or you hand the work to someone else, create a separate personal account for this purpose. [L1357, 2018]

### Engagement Campaign Tests and Their Limits

- Engagement campaigns to build a warm audience can give cheaper clicks but lower buyer intent, and they can disturb the ad account and pixel data. Use them only for a short creative test (3 to 5 days at about $10 to $20), not as a permanent part of the funnel. [L0645, 2023]
- Engagement campaigns can show a CTR near 3 percent, but the same creatives in purchase campaigns often drop to about 1.2 to 2 percent. Engagement audiences have lower buying intent and cheaper clicks, so use an engagement test only to rank creatives by CTR, not to forecast purchase results, and keep it to a few days at about $10 to $20. [L0645, 2023]
- Page-like and engagement campaigns can quickly build follower counts, but followers and likes rarely turn into sales. Targeting English-speaking tier-three countries with about $20 over a week produces numbers, not revenue. (Advice from 2023.) [L0934, 2023]
- A Facebook page likes campaign with a few dollars per day for about a week can add followers and social proof. Those users rarely become customers, so do not expect sales from them. (Advice from 2024.) [L0965, 2024]
- A boosted post does not train the pixel or ad account to find buyers well. Keep the budget for proper conversion campaigns so the data is not wasted. [L1225, 2025]
- Example of a boosted reaction reel: a $55 spend produced 147 link clicks at $0.37 each, about 3,000 views, about 2,300 viewers and roughly 950 three-second video plays. These are good engagement stats, but none of the clicks converted to a sale. [L1225, 2025]

| Test type | Budget | Duration | Use for | Do not use for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Engagement creative test | about $10 to $20 | 3 to 5 days | Ranking creatives by CTR | Forecasting purchases [L0645, 2023] |
| Research engagement post | $5 to $10 per day | Until about 200 comments | Collecting objections and wording [L0906, 2026] | Driving sales [L0906, 2026] |
| Engagement on existing post | $3 to $5 per day | Short test | Boosting a post to engage [L0666, 2024] | Conversion data [L1225, 2025] |

### Engagement Campaign Setup

- To run an engagement campaign on an existing Facebook post (Advice from 2024):
  1. Choose Engagement as the campaign goal.
  2. In the ad builder, click "use existing post" instead of creating a new ad.
  3. Select the page post.
  4. Set the budget to 3 to 5 dollars per day. [L0666, 2024]
- For a research engagement campaign on Meta:
  1. Create a new campaign for the research test.
  2. Set the optimization goal to engagement optimization instead of sales and purchases.
  3. Post one simple open question from a whitelisting page, not from the brand page, written like a real person asking for advice.
  4. Set the budget to about $5 to $10 per day.
  5. Reply in the comments as the original poster, guiding the conversation with follow-up questions.
  6. Read every comment and aim for 200 or more comments.
  7. Note the main objections, prior experiences with other brands and the wording people use.

  [L0906, 2026]
- For a research engagement campaign, set the optimization goal to engagement optimization. Do not use sales and purchases, because the aim is to collect feedback from commenters rather than to drive purchases. [L0906, 2026]

### Lead Campaign Foundations and Measurement

- Meta lead objectives fill the account with cheap form fills and low-quality traffic. The algorithm cannot optimize those leads toward purchases or return on ad spend. [L0797, 2025-26]
- Sending Meta a purchase conversion event lets the algorithm optimize ad sets toward buyers. For giveaway funnels, a purchase objective therefore works better than a lead objective. [L0797, 2025-26]
- A Meta traffic campaign can send cold visitors to a content page that collects email opt-ins, but the traffic objective usually brings low-quality cold visitors. If such a campaign already performs acceptably it can be left unchanged, but its budget should not be scaled up. (Advice from 2024.) [L0360, 2024]
- Cold traffic lead generation campaigns typically run 6 to 12 months (half a year to a year) before campaign performance requires moving to Sandbox split-testing. [L0238, 2019]
- The minimum starting daily budget for lead generation campaigns is $40 per day. [L0238, 2019]
- A lead ad can also bring in Facebook page likes, because viewers can like the advertising page while responding to the offer. Treat this as a side benefit that grows the page audience, not as the main goal of the campaign. [L0641, 2014]

### Building a Lead Campaign in Power Editor

- Building a lead campaign in Power Editor:
  1. Open Power Editor and go to Ads.
  2. Click the plus icon to create a new campaign, ad set and ad.
  3. Name the campaign after the industry, and create a separate ad set for each precise interest so you can split test them.
  4. In the ad, select the Facebook page to advertise from.
  5. Enter the website URL of the squeeze page or offer page.
  6. Upload the image and fill in the text above the image, then the headline, link description and display link below the image.
  7. Check the preview, which shows how the ad will appear in the news feed.
  8. Create the conversion tracking pixel with the Leads event and place it in the head section of the thank-you page.
  9. Set the audience location to the market where your store ships, and select only one precise interest per ad set.
  10. Set optimization to optimized CPM with the default bid, and select the tracking pixel as the conversion.
  11. Set the ad set budget and upload the changes to Facebook.
  12. If the ad is denied, open a support ticket and ask which change would get it approved. [L0641, 2014]
- Lead ad set audience: select the location where your online store operates, such as the United States for a US store, and select one precise interest per ad set. The right-hand panel shows the potential reach, which for the example niche was about 560,000 people. [L0641, 2014]
- Lead ad set optimization: use optimized CPM with the default bid, and select the tracking pixel in the bottom section. Manual or custom bidding can be tested later once the basics work. [L0641, 2014]
- Ad set budgets can start as low as 5 dollars per day. Keep budgets low at the start because many ad sets are split tested and you want to see early whether the site converts before spending heavily. [L0641, 2014]
- The Facebook lead ad form has a call-to-action selector at the bottom of the ad settings. A preview on the right side shows how the finished ad will appear in the news feed, so check it before publishing. [L0641, 2014]
- Cold lead generation campaign settings: buying type Auction, objective Conversions for leads, and campaign budget optimization turned on. The budget is set at campaign level with an amount that suits the business. [L0649, 2019]
- Example for a yoga mat seller: cold lead generation can use one campaign with separate interest ad sets for yoga, pilates and a related athleisure brand, plus a lookalike built from past customers. Under campaign budget optimization, Meta shifts spend toward whichever interest converts best after about a week. [L0649, 2019]

### Email List Building with Facebook Lead Ads

- Facebook lead ad campaigns for building an email list: set the campaign objective to Conversions and the conversion event to Lead, so people submit their details inside Facebook without visiting the website. (Advice from 2024.) [L0836, 2024]
- Setting up a Facebook lead ad to grow an email list (Advice from 2024):
  1. Create a campaign with the objective set to Conversions.
  2. Set the conversion event to Lead.
  3. Use a cost cap; start low, around 5 USD per lead, and never exceed the value a lead is worth to the business (for example 10 USD).
  4. Offer a valuable lead magnet, such as a buyer's guide that already converts on the store.
  5. Leads opt in on a Facebook form, and their data flows into Shopify, triggering the email flow.
  6. Run for a few hundred leads, then compare the sales they generate against the lead cost.

  [L0836, 2024]
- Before paying for leads through Facebook lead ads, calculate what an email subscriber is worth in revenue (for example from exit-intent overlay sign-ups) over 30 to 60 days, and set the cost cap below that value. (Advice from 2024.) [L0836, 2024]
- Facebook lead ads for growing an email list are an advanced tactic. Use them only when a store already has a proven opt-in offer and a working email flow, not as a beginner step. (Advice from 2024.) [L0836, 2024]
- For a list-building campaign, run Conversion campaigns (not Lead Ads) with "lead" as the conversion event, sending traffic to a custom landing page where users enter their email. This delivers higher-quality leads than Facebook's built-in lead forms. [L0307, 2023+]
- To grow an email list, use a retargeting-led sequence:
  1. Run Facebook and Instagram retargeting ads to people who already visited the store.
  2. Run cold Facebook and Instagram ads that send new people to a top-of-funnel social opt-in.
  3. Email the people who opted in.
  4. Send the email subscribers to a product page.

  [L0454, 2019]
- Run Facebook ads to collect emails for a free offer, such as a three-page giveaway. Then:
  1. Use the email list to push a low-cost digital subscription.
  2. Aim for the tripwire offer to at least pay for the Facebook ads spent to collect the emails.
  3. Count physical sales and physical subscriptions as upside on top of that.

  [L1153, 2016]

### Lead Magnets and Targeting for Lead Ads

- For a lead magnet ad on Facebook, target people by interests tied to the niche, its events and the brands or companies in it, not by buying intent. Because these people are not searching to buy yet, the ad should offer a free resource rather than a discount. [L1076, 2019]

### B2B and Local Lead Examples

- For B2B or commercial products that are not aimed at consumers, such as restaurant equipment, use a branding campaign rather than a conversion campaign. Facebook's interest and demographic targeting can find business owners, which lets you build brand recognition over time. [L1243, 2017]
- Local dentist lead campaign, audience and ad settings: target the business's city with a radius of about 20 kilometers, ages 18 to 65 plus, and interests such as tooth beauty, salons and fitness. The ad uses a real procedure video provided by the dentist. [L0440, 2023+]
- The dentist's ad call-to-action uses a Facebook lead ad form that collects the prospect's name, email and phone number, along with consent to use the data. The lead then gets an option to book a free consultation at the dental practice. [L0440, 2023+]
- Example from one test: a Facebook ad campaign for a dentist's Invisalign offer generated 6 leads in its first days. The ad cost about 8 to 9 Swiss francs or dollars per lead, and each lead had a potential value of 3,000 to 5,000 dollars. [L0440, 2023+]

### Retargeting and Paid Traffic Priority

- Paid ads are needed to reach a target audience, and the paid-traffic priority applies across the funnel: organic page growth ranks below paid traffic until monthly paid spend reaches a satisfactory return on ad spend. [L0641, 2014; L0666, 2024]
