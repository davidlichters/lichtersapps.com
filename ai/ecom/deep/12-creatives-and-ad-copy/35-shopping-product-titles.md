### Shopping product titles

**Length and visibility**

- **Know the character zones.** Shopping product titles have a 150-character limit, but weight is not evenly distributed. The first 25-35 characters are nearly all visible in mobile previews and carry the most impact. The zone between characters 35 and 80 has secondary impact. Characters beyond 80 are rarely seen and carry minimal weight. [L0220, 2025-26]
- **Conflict: which length carries the most weight.** Sources differ. one source 2025-26 identifies the first 25-35 characters as the preview-visible zone [L0220, 2025-26]. one source states that Google weighs the first 70 characters most heavily [L1328, 2015]. Apply both: the first 25-35 characters decide what a mobile shopper sees, and the first 70 characters is the zone that carries the most weight. Put the core keyword in the first 25-35 characters and the remaining core terms before character 70. [L0220, 2025-26; L1328, 2015]
- **Keep high-value terms out of the back half.** Do not place high-value keywords or unique selling points beyond character 80 in shopping titles. The back half has minimal visibility and weight; reserve it only for secondary details. [L0220, 2025-26]

**Structure**

- **Lead with type, keyword and differentiator.** Lead shopping product titles with product type + strongest keyword + one differentiating attribute, all within the first 25-35 characters. This section determines preview visibility, especially on mobile devices. [L0220, 2025-26]
- **Order the second zone by search volume.** The second keyword zone extends to approximately 70-80 characters. After the lead, order remaining content by search volume: key specifications and materials first, then use cases, then secondary details. [L0220, 2025-26]
- **Include the attributes customers search for.** A product title should include, in roughly this order: brand or manufacturer, gender, color, size, product type or category, and any other attribute customers search for. Each attribute you include lets the product show up for more specific searches. [L1328, 2015]
- **Use the title formula.** Brand + Gender + Specific product name + Color + Size. Each element matches a separate search phrase, so one title covers many relevant queries. Examples: "Northwind Women's Insulated Rain Jacket Navy Medium" and "Acme Men's Trail Running Shoe Black Size 10". [L1328, 2015]
- **Use plain color names and no SKU codes.** Leave supplier SKU codes out of product titles unless keyword research shows that people search for them; check this in Google Keyword Planner. Use plain English color names, such as green instead of a supplier's technical color name, because they match what shoppers search for. [L1328, 2015]
- **Put variants in the description.** Put variants, long-tail phrases and synonyms in the product description rather than cramming them into the title. The description is where you can add many search terms without weakening the title. [L1328, 2015]

**Campaign type**

Sources differ on the first word of the title. Apply each by campaign type:

| Campaign type | Lead with | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Prospecting shopping campaigns | Descriptive, searchable product terms customers actually type; brand name at the end after a separator, or omitted if it consumes critical keyword space | [L0220, 2025-26] |
| Branded shopping campaigns | Brand name first, followed by product descriptors and keywords | [L0220, 2025-26] |
| General title order (brand-first formula) | Brand or manufacturer first, then gender, color, size, product type | [L1328, 2015] |

- **Prospecting campaigns.** In prospecting shopping campaigns, lead with descriptive, searchable product terms customers actually type. Position the brand name at the end after a separator, or omit it if it consumes critical keyword space. [L0220, 2025-26]
- **Branded campaigns.** In branded shopping campaigns, reverse the structure: begin with the brand name, then follow with product descriptors and keywords. [L0220, 2025-26]

**Bundles, promotions and sizes**

- **Multi-pack quantity first.** For multi-pack and bundle products, place the quantity at the title start (for example, "Four Pack" or "Twelve Pack"). This makes the count visible in previews and justifies the higher price before clicks. [L0220, 2025-26]
- **Promotional language after keywords.** Google's official policy discourages purely promotional titles, but well-placed promotional language in the 35-80 character zone, after core keywords, can improve conversion because it reads as a direct response to the search. Test while monitoring policy compliance. [L0220, 2025-26]
- **Keep size out of the title.** Avoid including size variants in shopping product titles. Shoppers reject listings whose size does not match their needs, clicking away before reviewing the product. Add size information to Google Merchant Center size fields instead. [L0220, 2025-26]

**Feed and store titles**

- **Do not copy store titles.** Do not copy titles directly from the Shopify store into the Google shopping feed. Shopify titles target website visitors; shopping feed titles target Google's search algorithm and customer intent. They require fundamentally different structures. [L0220, 2025-26]
- **Feed text must match search terms.** Product titles, descriptions and SKU numbers on the product page all feed the Google Shopping results, so write them to match the terms customers search for. [L0468, 2020]
- **Do not copy the supplier name or SKU.** Copying the supplier's product name or SKU as the title limits the searches a product can appear for and lowers click-through and conversion. Such titles often also miss the gender or the correct color. [L1328, 2015]

**Example: five title variations for one product**

Product: a 15-calorie Southwest Ranch sauce from Taste Flavor Co. Develop five title variations where each emphasizes a different product strength.

- **Version 1:** Leads with the low-calorie advantage and creamy texture, adds sugar-free and avocado oil details, and suggests wings and fries as applications.
- **Version 2:** Balances the calorie count with gluten-free positioning and lists meal contexts: tacos, salads and grain bowls.
- **Version 3:** Targets keto shoppers explicitly, highlights avocado oil and pairs with roasted vegetables.
- **Version 4:** Combines multiple dietary benefits (low calories, gluten-free, sugar-free) within a meal-prep frame using chicken and rice.
- **Version 5:** Emphasizes sugar-free and seed oil-free claims while identifying multiple uses: salad dressing and wings.

Rotating which benefit appears first lets each title capture different search patterns, so the brand can maintain separate product listings that each address a specific buyer priority. [L0220, 2025-26]

### Shopping product images

- **Leave margin around the product.** Google automatically crops and centers Shopping images, so the product should leave spacing around it instead of filling the frame edge to edge. Check generated images for this margin before upload. [L0882, 2025-26]
- **Edge-to-edge images get cropped.** A product that fills the whole frame edge to edge will be cropped by Google's automatic cropping of Shopping images. Leave margin around the product in every generated image. [L0882, 2025-26]
- **Show the product up close in lifestyle images.** A lifestyle image where the product is clearly visible up close helps the shopper recognize the product before clicking. The claimed benefits are higher CTR and fewer bounces from the site, which should be verified through the split test. [L0882, 2025-26]

### Dynamic product ads and carousels (Meta)

**Dynamic ad text**

- **Question, product name and offer.** Dynamic ad text follows one pattern: a question that names the product, followed by a specific offer and the live price. It works because people recognize the product and see the price before clicking, which reduces wasted clicks. Two examples in the same pattern: "Still thinking about {product name}? Save 10% on {product price} this week." and "Ready for {product name}? Get free shipping on {product price} today." [L0510, 2018]
- **Headline template.** Full template text:

```
Do you already adore {product name}? Today you can take 15% off its usual retail price of {product price} on our website. Place this as the dynamic headline at the top of a Facebook dynamic product ad, where the {product name} and {product price} values are pulled in automatically from the product feed. Keep the wording brief so the item name and the discount are noticed right away, and choose the discount level based on your profit margin.
```

[L0510, 2018]
- **Show the price in the image.** Put the price inside the product image as an overlay for dynamic product ads. Shoppers then see the price before clicking, which avoids clicks from people who only leave after seeing the price. [L0510, 2018]
- **Dynamic text works with the feed.** Dynamic ad text is one lever for DPA CTR. It works together with product feed optimization. [L1031, 2018]

**Carousel settings and copy**

- **Carousel primary text formula.** Name the store, state that it offers the newest items, and point to products the person already viewed. This works because the reminder of the store and the recent items match the visitor's intent. Examples: "Still thinking it over? Your recently viewed picks are waiting at Store Name." and "Found what you were looking for? Grab the latest arrivals from Store Name before they sell out." [L1029, 2020-26]
- **Keep the product name field.** Headline in a dynamic product carousel: keep the dynamic product name field so each card shows the product name pulled from the product feed. Product description: leave it empty or insert a feed field such as price. [L1029, 2020-26]

**Creative source**

- **Pull creative from product pages.** Creative for remarketing ads should be pulled from the store's own product pages instead of custom videos. This keeps the ads cheap to produce and consistent with the product listings. [L1111, 2020]

### Dynamic remarketing (Google)

**Copy patterns**

- **Three-part remarketing pattern.** A short headline with the store name and a call to action, a long headline that prompts the visitor to return and finish the purchase, and a description that states who the store is. The formula works because it names the brand, reminds the visitor of their unfinished intent, and reinforces trust. Examples:
  - Example 1: short headline "shop [brand name]", long headline "pick up where you left off and checkout today", description "the official [brand] store for [product category]".
  - Example 2: short headline "get [brand] now", long headline "your cart is still waiting for you", description "the trusted home of [product type] with fast shipping".

  [L0733, 2020-26]
- **Headline formula with the store name.** Use a short headline with the store name and a verb such as "Shop" (about 2 to 3 words), a long headline that tells the visitor to finish the order they started, and a description that states it is the official store. The visitor already knows the product, so the copy should only bring them back to finish, not introduce the brand. New examples in this pattern:
  - "Shop [Brand] Essentials" with long headline "Your cart is still waiting, finish checkout" and description "Shop the official [Brand] store today."
  - "Shop [Brand] Now" with long headline "Pick up where you left off at [Brand]" and description "Order from the full [Brand] range on our official site."

  [L0734, 2020-26]
- **Enter several variations.** Enter multiple headline and description variations. Google tests them automatically and shows more of the variations that get clicked most. [L0734, 2020-26]
- **Variations inside one ad.** Multiple headline and description variations inside one ad let Google test them against each other. Google then shows the best performing combinations to more people. For a first remarketing ad, one ad with one set of text is a reasonable starting point. [L0733, 2020-26]
- **Only viewed products appear.** Dynamic remarketing ads show only the products each visitor actually viewed, so a visitor who looked at just a few products sees only those few in the ad and preview. The ad therefore adapts to each person's browsing without manual changes. [L0733, 2020-26]

**Ad builder settings**

- **Turn off auto video.** For a dynamic remarketing ad, leave the automatic video option off. Enter headlines, long headlines and descriptions, which appear under the product images. Add several variations in the same ad so Google can test them and show the best performers to more people. Headlines and descriptions are also required for previews on YouTube and Gmail. [L0733, 2020-26]
- **Use the feed for product display.** In the ad builder, the final URL is the website homepage. The business name field gets the business name, and the ad preview shows products automatically pulled from the Merchant Center feed. [L0734, 2020-26]
- **Use headline ideas.** The headline field offers a generate ideas option. Enter a few words about the business and Google suggests headline ideas, which can be used or ignored. [L0734, 2020-26]
- **Turn off auto video and native formats.** Under additional formatting options, turn off "Use auto-generated video" and turn off "Use native formats". This keeps ads in the dynamic format shown in the preview. [L0734, 2020-26]
- **No video in remarketing.** Do not use video in remarketing campaigns. Their purpose is to show visitors the products they already viewed, and auto-generated video does not look good for this. [L0734, 2020-26]
- **Add a logo and two images.** Add a logo in the ad builder. The campaign also needs two images, one landscape and one square, which Google can suggest from the site. [L0734, 2020-26; L0189, undated]
- **Fix errors on Next.** If the ad builder shows errors on Next, the usual cause is missing images. Adding one landscape and one square image and clicking apply changes fixes it. [L0734, 2020-26]
- **Preview with sample products.** Google's ad preview shows how the dynamic product ad looks with sample products. The preview updates as headlines and descriptions are entered. Previews for YouTube and Gmail need more assets, so they only appear after headlines and descriptions are added. [L0733, 2020-26]

**Image formats and template**

- **Exactly two formats.** Google Display remarketing ads require exactly 2 image formats: 1 landscape image and 1 square image. [L0189, undated]
- **Conflict: image counts by campaign type.** Google Display remarketing ads use exactly two images (one landscape, one square) [L0189, undated; L0734, 2020-26]. Standard Google Display static campaigns use about five images that cover the target sizes, with the 300 x 250 pixel size included [L1342, 2021-22]. Apply the two-image rule to remarketing and the five-image rule to standard display campaigns.
- **Browse-selection template.** Template text:

```
Browse [Store Name]'s selection
```

[L0189, undated]

### Retargeting messages

- **Restate the benefit with a time-limited discount.** A retargeting ad for people who already saw the advertorial restates the benefit and adds a time-limited discount, either a percentage or a fixed amount off, to make the second visit worth it. The ad then sends the visitor straight to the offer page. Examples: "Still thinking about a calmer night for your dog? Come back today and get 15 percent off your first order." and "You looked at this before. Take 8 euros off your bundle if you order in the next 24 hours." [L1131, 2018+]
- **Email-based retargeting copy (prompt).** Full prompt text:

```
Thanks. I'm also going to run retargeting ads on Meta and the Google Display Network. Based on the email copy above, write ad copy for both. Make a separate version for Meta and one for Google Display Network. Use the same discount code, discount percentage, and delivery deadline as in the emails, and give each ad a short headline and body text.
```

[L0620, 2025+]
- **Follow-up retargeting copy (prompt).** Full prompt text:

```
Once that's done, I'll also run retargeting ads on Meta and the Google Display Network. Write the ad copy for those placements, using the email copy from above as your source.
```

[L1224, 2025+]

### Display banners and static display ads

**Banner copy and placement**

- **Banner copy: brand and tagline.** Banner ad copy should include your branding and a brief tagline describing what you do, the problem you solve, or your key strength. Keep text minimal; it is not a sales pitch, just enough to grab attention. Visitors learn full details after clicking through to your landing page or homepage. [L0288, 2021-22]
- **Example banner tagline.** "award-winning Shopify themes, legendary support." This works because it highlights the product advantage while addressing a known customer pain point: poor support from competing theme makers. [L0288, 2021-22]
- **Partner sites.** For banner ad placements on a partner's site, you usually supply the creative (the banner file) yourself and the site owner places it. Partners rarely design banners for you. [L0525, 2021-22]
- **Clickable image with a visible call to action.** On Google Display Network static ads, the whole image is clickable, but the call to action still has to look clickable. It does not need a fancy button, but it must not look like plain decoration. [L1169, 2021-22]
- **Show business identity.** Static display ads must show the business identity, for example the plain-text brand name, the base URL or a logo. In some countries this is a legal requirement so that users do not mistake an ad for editorial content. [L1169, 2021-22]
- **Copy length by placement.** Display placements offer less text room than Facebook placements. Use more copy and more product detail wherever the layout has more space, and keep the copy short where space is tight. The message stays the same across placements. [L1169, 2021-22]
- **Small formats need short copy.** On small display formats such as 300 x 250, there is little room for body copy. Shrinking the image can free some space, but long text will not fit. Keep the copy to a short line about the problem the product solves. [L1169, 2021-22]

**Product reminder ad elements**

Key attributes of a product reminder ad [L1169, 2021-22]:

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Image | The best-quality image of the product that is available, ideally showing the product in use or in its intended setting. Resize it to fit each placement. If the product photos are weak, use the best available and accept the limit. |
| Headline | Highlight the primary benefit of the product, not the product name. |
| Body copy | At a minimum, a short description of the problem the product solves. Where there is more space (Facebook), add benefits and the USP. Much of this can be taken from the product description. |
| Call to action | Test "Shop Now", "Buy Now", "Add To Cart" and "Learn More". Learn More sometimes performs well. |

- **Benefit headline pattern.** State the single benefit that proves the product performs where cheaper alternatives fail, written in plain words, instead of repeating the product name. Shoppers may recognize the name but buy the outcome, and a benefit that competitors lack separates the product from others in the same category. Examples: "Keep your garden shed dry through a wet winter." and "A tent that stays standing in a storm, not just on a sunny day." [L1169, 2021-22]
- **Do not headline the product name.** Do not use the product name as the headline of a reminder ad. Shoppers need a reason to click, and the name alone does not give one. [L1169, 2021-22]

**Image formats, sizes and file rules**

- **Use JPEG under 150 KB.** Upload Google display ads as JPEG rather than PNG, and keep each file under 150 KB. A 299 KB PNG was rejected by Google with an error. [L1342, 2021-22]
- **Upload around five standard images.** Upload around five standard display images that cover the target sizes, with the 300 x 250 pixel size included, so that they all show in the campaign. Google accepts image ads as GIF, JPG and PNG, and HTML5 or AMPHTML ads as ZIP. [L1342, 2021-22]
- **Performance Max assets.** Setting up a Performance Max campaign requires creating assets: square and rectangle image banners, five text descriptions and the business name. The setup guides you through these assets step by step. [L1251, 2024+]
