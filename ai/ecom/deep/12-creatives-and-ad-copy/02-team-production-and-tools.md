### Team, production and tools

**Team**

- Prioritise which products get custom AI images, because generation takes time per product. Good candidates are new launches, best sellers that need a higher CTR, and underperformers that should be pushed harder. [L0882, 2025-26]
- Different people react to different creatives, so a founder needs to produce many creatives and master customisation. Test many variations of the same winning concept. [L1195, 2026]
- Authentic production starts with the founder's own skills. The founder-style example in the workhorse section applies here: only ads the current team can speak and film now belong in production. [L0485, 2025-26]

**Tools and costs**

- Photoshop costs about £10 per month and is used for product photo editing and background replacement. [L0561, 2023+] *(Source lists the price in pounds; verify current pricing before budgeting.)*
- Canva offers free graphic design and video editing with a small course. CapCut is free for video editing. [L0561, 2023+]

**Organising production files**

- Give each product its own dated folder, named with the date and product name, such as 2023-04 plus the product name. [L1427, 2023+]
- Inside each product folder, keep Assets (static images, emojis, logos), Audio (music, voiceovers, sound effects) and Video. Split the video folder by theme, such as testimonials, TikTok winners, paid influencer or UGC footage and dated shoots. [L1427, 2023+]

**Producing your own creatives**

- Once a product reaches about $500 to $600 in revenue and shows potential, create your own ads. [L1173, undated] *(Sources differ on the threshold; see the proof-of-concept figures below.)*
- Create your own ads by editing the footage yourself, ordering new footage, or briefing a UGC creator to make a video. [L1173, undated]
- To make a product stand out from competitors after a few days of consistent sales, produce custom content: order the product, film your own videos or hire creators and actors, then give the product a unique custom name. Custom content makes it harder for copycats to reuse the same videos. [L1382, undated]
- Own creatives and unique marketing angles differentiate a store from competitors who use the same ads. Without them, growth stays slower even when the product sells. [L1171, 2024+]
- When launching a product, use existing drop-shipping video to test quickly, then switch to custom content for products that show promise. Speed and resource use matter early, but custom content gives a stronger return later. [L0871, 2021+]
- A store can build an organic video library first, then run ads only on the one video that performs well. This keeps paid spend focused on proven creative. [L0706, 2020+]

### Lean starts and when to go custom

**Starting a test with competitor material**

1. Copy the competitor's active ads exactly to see how they perform for your store. [L1173, undated]
2. Once the product reaches about $500 to $600 in revenue and shows potential, create your own ads. [L1173, undated]
3. Create your own ads by editing the footage yourself, ordering new footage, or briefing a UGC creator to make a video. [L1173, undated]

- Start a product test by copying competitor ads to see whether the product works. Once the product reaches roughly $1,000 to $2,000 in sales, invest in your own creatives to stand out. [L1171, 2024+]

**Proof-of-concept gates (sources differ)**

| Source | Trigger | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| one source 2026 | About $75-100 per day held consistently for 3-5 days on the testing campaign | Starting custom ads such as VSLs and deep-research ads [L1195, 2026] |
| one source | About $500-600 in revenue, with potential shown | Creating your own ads from competitor testing [L1173, undated] |
| one source | About $1,000-2,000 in sales | Investing in your own creatives to stand out [L1171, 2024+] |

- The three thresholds measure different things (daily spend, revenue, total sales) and come from different sources. Use the one that matches the metric your store tracks, and treat the proof of concept as a signal that the offer works rather than a fixed rule. [L1195, 2026; L1173, undated; L1171, 2024+]
- Before reaching the proof-of-concept gate, keep it simple: run static image ads and test the offer structure, discount levels and page layout. [L1195, 2026]
- VSL first, statics later: VSLs convert better and force you to articulate the full message. Extract the winning angle from the VSL, then iterate it into static ads. If you cannot produce VSLs in-house, hire an agency, since outsourcing a high volume of VSLs is expensive. [L1195, 2026]
- Creative quality usually matters more than website quality for a new store. Strong creatives can make an average store convert, while the website alone rarely fixes weak ads. [L1454, 2026]
- After a product has passed validation, creative quality is the main lever for results. A decent product can still perform well with strong video ads. Build the test around finding proven creatives rather than trying to rescue a weak product with heavy editing. [L0704, 2026]
- Before focusing on the store, improve existing footage and images, add the AI tools where useful and communicate the offer clearly. [L1219, undated]

### Creative as the main lever

**Ranking the levers (sources differ)**

- Creative is the second biggest lever in a paid ad account after product and offer. Fresh creative supply matters more than most campaign tactics or bidding tricks. Without a steady stream of newly tested creatives, cost caps, bid caps and structure changes do little. [L0902, undated]
- Creative is described as the main lever in current Meta and TikTok advertising. Invest in better creators and in understanding how hooks and hold rates work. [L1213, 2023]
- Creative is the main lever in Meta ads now. A business that stops testing new creative usually sees its campaigns decline within one to two months, so constant creative testing is a core process, not an occasional task. [L0398, 2023+]
- The creative is the main lever in Facebook ads. When the creative is strong, Facebook learns from the people who engage with it, so creative quality matters more than the interests chosen. [L1469, 2023+]
- Creatives are the main driver of Meta campaign performance. With weak creatives, even good ad sets fail, while strong creatives let broad targeting without interests perform because the algorithm finds buyers. [L1335, 2023+]
- Creative is the main driver of performance on TikTok, and roughly 90 percent of the outcome depends on it. When CTR is poor, change the creative rather than the audience, because the audience is rarely the cause. [L1377, 2022+]
- On TikTok, creatives matter more than targeting. The seller should test many creatives, varying the hook, length, caption style and trends rather than only the product shot. [L1177, 2023]
- Creatives drive targeting results more than CBO or ABO settings, because Facebook increasingly targets through creatives when targeting is broad. The main priority is avoiding overspending during product tests. [L0349, 2024]
- With the same audience and budget, the creative determines how many viewers engage, click the call-to-action and reach the website. It is therefore the main variable to optimise. Other factors such as CPM matter less over the long run. [L0639, 2023]
- Creative merit is a main driver of results because strong engagement lets Facebook push the ad to more people. Creatives with strong engagement receive a higher engagement ranking, which tends to lower CPM and CPC compared with competitors. [L0646, 2023]

**Reconciling the rankings:** the sources agree that creative is the decisive lever once the product and offer are validated, and they differ on where it ranks before that point. Validate the product and offer first, then treat creative as the main lever for scaling. [L0902, undated; L0704, 2026; L0398, 2023+]

**Video and platform differences in the ranking**

- Creative drives results on Facebook and Instagram when targeting is broad, so keep producing and testing new creatives. [L1426, undated; L1335, 2023+]
- On TikTok, creative drives most of the outcome. [L1377, 2022+; L1177, 2023]

### Testing cadence and refresh

**Creative testing volume**

- Creative testing is a numbers game. Brands can test 100 to 200 creatives per week, and three to ten winners can cover the testing cost. Plan for many variants rather than expecting one video to win. [L0638, 2023]
- In the early phase, one operator launched at least five new creatives per week and tested about 20 offers, while the landing page converted at about 3.5%. Testing volume came before optimising any single ad. [L1315, 2023]
- On TikTok, test at least three new creatives per week while a product is running, since TikTok is almost entirely creative-driven. [L1177, 2023]
- Upload at least 20 videos within the first month, or in one to two weeks for fast learning, before judging your creative skills and expecting results. [L0820, undated; L0821, undated]
- Test new creatives every week. The mentor said this is the rule even though his own schedule often prevents it. Consistent weekly creative testing is the target. [L1107, 2023]

**Refresh rhythm (sources differ)**

- Plan to test new creative concepts roughly every two weeks, because a single creative typically delivers good results for about one month before performance declines steadily. [L0902, undated]
- Refresh the testing cycle with new creatives weekly when a fresh set is available, and at least every two weeks otherwise. Each creative typically performs well for about one month before results decline, so the testing pipeline must keep producing new concepts. [L0902, undated]

**Expected rotation size**

- Creatives in rotation = concepts × creatives per concept. Example: 10 concepts × 5 creatives each = 50 creatives in rotation, and the count grows as you iterate. [L1019, 2026; L1195, 2026]

**Using the testing pipeline**

- Not every creative will convert. Many creatives work as cheap touchpoints that help other creatives convert, so test a large amount of variation. [L1225, 2025]
- Creative testing should use a pipeline of stages. A typical example is seven creatives in the pipeline, with five in preparation and two in production. [L0646, 2023]

**Testing a new market with creatives**

1. Create four creatives for four audiences, with different benefits and call-outs. [L0646, 2023]
2. Go deeper into the one that responds best. [L0646, 2023]
3. Aim for a range of concepts, such as seven creatives in the pipeline with five in preparation and two in production. [L0646, 2023]

**Not dropping a product too early**

- When a product's first batch of ad creatives fails, do not drop the product right away. Produce several more creative batches and test them before deciding the product does not work. [L1469, 2023+]
- Start the test with the widest range of approaches before judging a creative as failed, because merit depends on angle and narrative as well as quality. [L0644, undated]

**Volume versus perfection**

- When producing ad creatives, volume beats perfection. Launching more variations quickly and gathering test data outperforms spending time perfecting a small number before launch. [L0199, 2026]
- The creative agency goal is to produce volume of creatives that each generate positive contribution margin, not to find one rare breakout ad matching your highest-performing creative from the past. Many creatives will underperform. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Creative success should be measured by the batting average, the percentage of creatives generating positive contribution margin, not by finding one breakout ad that beats all previous records. [L0217, 2025-26]
- The goal of a creative agency is to produce volume of creatives that each generate positive contribution margin, similar to how professional baseball players succeed only 20 to 30 percent of the time at the plate. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Success is measured on the percentage of ads hitting positive contribution margin, similar to a professional batting average. [L0217, 2025-26]

**Output limits**

- More creative volume is not automatically better. The real question is how much is too much for a given brand. For larger brands, plotting revenue against the number of creative assets shows whether more output correlates with more revenue. [L0885, 2025-26]
- Mapping creative launches per product exposes mismatches, such as heavy spend on one product while only three or four new creatives launch for it each month. This approach is especially suited to brands with three or four products. [L0885, 2025-26]

**Expected rotation for a set of concepts**

- Expected volume is roughly 10 concepts × 5 creatives, about 50 creatives in rotation. [L1019, 2026]

### Benchmark output tracking

- Track net new concepts and iteration concepts separately to see how much output is truly new versus variations of existing ads. Both numbers together show whether creative work is actually broadening the account. [L0885, 2025-26]
- Iteration versus net-new tracking is not in the naming convention at the time of the lesson. Options are to add a flag for iteration or new concept to the names, or to let Meta classify each ad as iteration or new concept, since briefs already exist. [L0885, 2025-26]

### Weekly cadence and creative routine

- Plan to test new creative concepts roughly every two weeks, and refresh weekly when a fresh set is available. [L0902, undated]
- Consistent weekly creative testing is the target. [L1107, 2023]
- On an unprofitable day, customer research and sharpening the next creative are a better use of time than monitoring metrics. Research improves the next ad rather than reacting to yesterday's numbers. [L1445, 2026]
- Testing creatives is one of the most important activities in e-commerce, so new creative ideas should flow continuously through a pipeline. [L1181, 2023]
- Build the test around finding proven creatives. [L0704, 2026]
