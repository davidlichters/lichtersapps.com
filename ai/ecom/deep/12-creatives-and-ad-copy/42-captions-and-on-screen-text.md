### Captions and on-screen text

**Safe zones and placement**

- Canva safe zone for a 4:5 video ad: set up the 4:5 ratio at 1080 × 1350 px. Place critical text such as the offer in the safe zone where Facebook will not crop it. Stories show text at the top and bottom; the main feed may cut off bottom text. Design accordingly. [L0308, 2023; L0777, undated]
- Source conflict: the Canva note gives 1350 × 1920 px for 4:5. That frame is not 4:5 (1350 ÷ 1920 ≈ 0.70, while 4:5 = 0.80). Use 1080 px wide × 1350 px tall for 4:5, the vertical size that matches the ratio. [L0308, 2023; L0777, undated]
- Use 4:5 (1080 × 1350 px, vertical) because the feed is viewed mainly on mobile and 4:5 fills more of the screen. [L0777, undated]
- Mobile viewing drives most sales for social video ads, so design creatives for the phone screen first. The source claims about 90% of sales come from mobile. [L0777, undated]
- Create two versions of each video: one format for the Facebook and Instagram feed, and a separate one for Reels and Stories, because the two placements display content differently. [L1347, undated]
- Test video ad dimensions and aspect ratios. Some videos may appear differently sized than expected, which affects ad performance and reach. [L0209, 2023]
- Place the on-screen call-to-action text high enough in the video so it stays visible and is not hidden by platform UI elements. A low position was noted as a weakness in the example. [L0655, undated]

**Why captions matter**

- Feed viewers often watch without sound, so the caption carries much of the message. Put a benefit-driven line of 4-5 words on screen for each clip, with an emotional trigger. [L0398, 2023+]
- Viewers are often distracted, for example waiting in line or in a crowded place, so messages must be understood quickly. Add subtitles, because many viewers cannot hear the sound. [L0998, undated]
- Consider how people browse the ad. A person scrolling while queuing in a store has split attention and may have no sound on. Design the creative to work silently, for example with subtitles on videos. [L0993, undated]

**Caption tools and steps**

- Auto captions in CapCut (desktop and mobile) create a text layer from the spoken words, which can then be edited or deleted. Generate captions only after the voiceover is final, otherwise you will have to regenerate them. [L0344, 2023]
- Auto captions in CapCut:
  1. Select all clips with the voice track. [L1423, undated]
  2. Go to Text and click Auto captions at the top. [L1423, undated]
  3. Check the caption language setting, since the auto-detected language can be wrong. [L1423, undated]
  4. Click Create. [L1423, undated]
  5. Review the captions track above the timeline and correct any misheard words in the captions list. [L1423, undated]
  6. Use the captions list to split or merge caption lines and adjust their length. [L1423, undated]
- Vide.io: upload the video, open the subtitles function and let it generate the text automatically. Then choose the subtitle style (plain or in a box), set the size and select the font. [L0888, 2023+]
- On CapCut mobile, the auto-caption step asks which caption animation to apply. The Pro list includes an animated caption background that changes colour as each word is spoken. This was popular for TikTok-style captions at the time, but it may not suit every ad, and the desktop version did not offer it during the demo. [L0344, 2023]

**Caption styling**

- Style captions in the text panel: add strokes, add a background, and align text left, middle or right. For a TikTok-style look, use a rounded rectangle background and increase its height and width for padding. [L0344, 2023]
- Caption styling in CapCut, under Text > Basic:
  - Use a readable font. The system font is recommended for UGC.
  - Set the text colour.
  - Either add a stroke for a simple TikTok or Instagram look, or switch on the background, set it to a rounded rectangle with white fill and black text, and increase the height and width to round the corners.
  - Tick Apply to all, then increase the line height if it looks cramped. [L1423, undated]
- Every text layer on video needs a background, a stroke or a drop shadow, because light and dark areas of the footage can make plain white text disappear. Glow and drop shadow are other options in the text settings. [L1423, undated]
- Text over video needs readability treatment. On a video with white text, place a gradient behind it or add a black stroke. On TikTok, the native text style uses a black stroke over white text. [L1348, 2023]
- Avoid CapCut's template text styles for UGC. They look overdone and not like user-generated content. [L1423, undated]
- To style one caption differently, untick Apply to all in the caption settings, then move, resize or restyle that caption independently. [L1423, undated]
- Text animation duration is set under animation at the bottom of the timer. A value of 0.4 seconds gives a quick entrance for short-form ads. Use the same short duration across lines so the rhythm stays consistent. [L0344, 2023]
- Split a long claim into several short text lines, stacked or staggered, so each benefit appears on its own. For TikTok keep text short and punchy; on Facebook you can use more varied title styles. [L0344, 2023]
- When text layers are stacked and move together, untick the Apply to all checkbox so each layer can be moved independently. Once unlinked, each line can be positioned and timed on its own. [L0344, 2023]
- To give different lines of one multi-line text layer different colours in CapCut, select the specific line in the text layer and change its colour in the text section of the control panel. Each line can be styled separately without creating a new layer. [L0344, 2023]
- Duplicate a styled text layer with Ctrl/Cmd+C and Ctrl/Cmd+V to keep the same font, stroke and size, then edit only the words. This is faster than styling each bullet from scratch. [L1423, undated]
- Blinking text effect: a blinking text effect can lift CTR, especially for seasonal promotions such as Black Friday. It is a small change, but creative is a major factor in results. [L1194, undated]

**Benefit overlays and bullet graphics**

- Benefit list overlay: while the speaker names a product's benefits, short text lines appear on top of B-roll one after another, in sync with the words. Formula: spoken claim, then one benefit per line, timed to the moment each benefit is said.
  - Example 1: "Calms the mind and helps you unwind" appears while the speaker says "calm evenings".
  - Example 2: "Lasts up to 60 hours" appears while the speaker says "long-lasting burn time". [L1423, undated]
- Bullet-point graphic:
  1. Create a text layer for each benefit with the same style. [L1423, undated]
  2. Pick a checkmark from the Stickers tab or download a free check icon, and place it beside the text. [L1423, undated]
  3. Position all items so they fit together on screen. [L1423, undated]
  4. Select the group and drag each line onto the timeline at the moment the speaker mentions that benefit. [L1423, undated]
  5. Resize the items so they are legible on a phone. [L1423, undated]
- Add icons, ticks, arrows and emojis next to claims to emphasise them, and make them appear at the same time as the text they support. Keep these graphics smaller than the main text so they do not compete with it. [L0344, 2023]

### Transitions, zooms and attention cuts

**Transitions**

- Transitions are built into CapCut and can be added between two cuts. A zoom or pull-in suits an introduction of the product, and a duration around 1.2 seconds was used as a starting point. Drag the transition onto the cut point and adjust the duration in the control panel. [L0344, 2023]
- Adding a transition between two clips in CapCut:
  1. Transitions can only be placed between two clips, so duplicate the clip and place the copy on a track above. [L1423, undated]
  2. Trim the top copy so the cut lines up with the transition point. [L1423, undated]
  3. Open the Transitions tab and drag a transition, such as Slide down, between the two clips. [L1423, undated]
  4. Preview the transition to confirm it works. [L1423, undated]
- Use a transition every time the scene or subject changes, so the viewer stays engaged. Effects such as colour overlays, graphics and filters are for style and should be used sparingly. [L0344, 2023]
- Use transitions sparingly and match them to camera movement: pan or slide when the camera moves sideways, and zoom when the shot pushes in. Panning and zooming transitions are the most useful. Overusing transitions makes the ad look chaotic. [L1423, undated]
- Reading [L0344, 2023] and [L1423, undated] together: a transition belongs at each scene or subject change, with the type matched to the camera movement. Do not add transitions where nothing changes, and keep colour overlays and filters to a minimum. [L0344, 2023; L1423, undated]
- Clip animation in the video controls can animate a clip in, out or both, using options such as swing or slide. Avoid slide animations that bring in a black background, because they look unpolished on a product ad. [L0344, 2023]

**Attention cuts and zooms**

- Zoom into the subject with a keyframed scale:
  1. Select the clip you want to zoom into. [L0344, 2023]
  2. Move the playhead to the frame where the zoom starts and click the keyframe button next to Scale in the video controls at normal size. [L0344, 2023]
  3. Move the playhead to the frame where the zoom ends. [L0344, 2023]
  4. Increase Scale to the target size, which creates a second keyframe. [L0344, 2023]
  5. Play the section to check that the zoom moves smoothly between the two keyframes. [L0344, 2023]
- Cut-in zoom: split the clip with the blade at the moment of the action (for example a wink), select the second part, go to Video > Scale and increase the scale so the shot zooms in at that moment. Pairing the zoom with a short sound effect like a ding adds emphasis. [L1423, undated]
- Keyframe zoom in CapCut:
  1. Select the clip and open the scale property in the Tools panel. [L1423, undated]
  2. Click the keyframe diamond to set a keyframe at the start. [L1423, undated]
  3. Move the playhead to a later point. [L1423, undated]
  4. Change the scale, which creates a keyframe automatically. [L1423, undated]
  5. Drag keyframes to adjust timing. [L1423, undated]
  - A slow zoom over the whole clip builds engagement, and a fast zoom in the first seconds grabs attention. [L1423, undated]
- Jump cut: blade just before a small action such as a wink, then scale the new clip in so the eye line stays where it was. Pair jump cuts with a short sound effect at the moment of the cut to grab attention. [L0344, 2023]
- The two main attention cuts are a slow zoom into the subject and a quick jump cut. Both can be layered with sound effects. Use them across the ad, not only at the start. [L0344, 2023]
- Cuts, zooms and motion keep attention and are used throughout the ad. Zoom in during speech to add emphasis, and zoom out to make a cut out. Both help retention. [L1423, undated]
- Cut types:
  - A straight cut joins two shots back to back and is the main cut for basic ads.
  - A jump cut skips time or zooms in between shots.
  - A J-cut starts the next shot's audio before its picture.
  - An L-cut keeps the previous shot's audio playing after the cut. [L1427, 2023+]

**Motion, easing and blur**

- Things in motion accelerate and decelerate, so a linear keyframe move looks mechanical. Apply Ease Out to the first keyframe and Ease In to the second, or use a custom speed graph, to make motion look natural. [L0343, 2023]
- Motion blur on a fast zoom: increase the shutter angle in the motion blur settings. A high shutter angle creates visible blur during quick movement, which makes the motion look more natural. [L0343, 2023]
- Motion blur and easing make movement look natural. Without motion blur, fast-moving objects look harsh. At the time of this demo the desktop version did not offer motion blur, so check whether your version has it before planning moving text. [L0344, 2023]
- Any property with a keyframe diamond can be animated over time, including scale, position and colour. Position keyframes can move a product or hand in from off screen, and text can be animated the same way. [L1423, undated]

### Overlays, cut-outs and compositing

- Before placing an emoji or graphic over footage, check that its PNG has a transparent background, because a solid background covers the video underneath. If it does not, find another PNG or remove the background before placing it. [L0343, 2023]
- Arrows and graphics are found in the Stickers tab and dragged onto the timeline. Move, resize and rotate them with the rotation handle in the Tools panel, and add position keyframes to slide them in from off screen. [L1423, undated]
- Chroma key: use the chroma key option in the Cut out panel to pick a colour and remove it from a clip, which is how green-screen footage is cleared. Auto cut out can isolate a person from the background so the person can be placed in front of an overlay. [L0344, 2023]
- Removing the background with auto cut-out:
  1. Duplicate the clip with the person on the timeline. [L1423, undated]
  2. Open the Tools panel, go to Cut out and choose Auto cut out. [L1423, undated]
  3. Wait for processing to finish; the person is isolated from the background. [L1423, undated]
  4. Place graphics such as wings behind the cut-out layer so they appear behind the person. [L1423, undated]
- Chroma key on green-screen footage:
  1. Place the green-screen clip on the timeline above the background. [L1423, undated]
  2. Open Cut out and choose Chroma key. [L1423, undated]
  3. Use the colour picker to select the green background. [L1423, undated]
  4. Raise the strength of the chroma key until all green is gone. [L1423, undated]
  5. Turn off any shadow so the keyed element blends cleanly. [L1423, undated]
- Blend mode overlays:
  - Multiply keeps the dark parts of the overlay and removes the light parts.
  - Screen or brighten keeps the light parts and removes the dark parts.
  - Soft light is a gentle overlay that blends an effect into the footage without overpowering it.
  - Example 1: a soft light glow over a candle video.
  - Example 2: a multiply texture over a dark background. [L1423, undated]
- Animated social proof workflow (pop-up comments or testimonials):
  1. Design the static graphics, such as comment cards or testimonial frames, in Canva. [L1348, 2023]
  2. Export them as image files. [L1348, 2023]
  3. Import the images into a video editor and animate them, adding pop sound effects and motion. [L1348, 2023]
- CapCut is a quick editor for animated social proof because it includes built-in pop sound effects. [L1348, 2023]

### Sound design and music

**Background music**

- Set the music volume low enough that the voiceover is clearly audible. A starting point of 12 to 16 in the volume control of the panel worked in the example, then adjust by ear. Music should sit in the background and create atmosphere. [L0344, 2023]
- Background music setup in CapCut: go to Audio, pick a track by searching a mood keyword such as "lo-fi", drag it onto the timeline, trim it to start at the beginning, then lower its volume in the Tools panel so the voice is clearly audible. Pick music that fits the product's mood, for example calm lo-fi for home decor. [L1423, undated]
- Pick the track that fits the mood of the ad, not the most popular one. Envato Elements provides stock video, music and sound effects searchable by keyword, for example "home decor music". [L0344, 2023]
- Fading music: select the music clip and drag the small circle at the clip's start or end to fade the music in or out. Fade it out at the end of the ad. [L1423, undated]
- Auto fade out: use the auto fade out switch on the audio track so the music fades slowly at the end instead of stopping abruptly. The fade belongs at the end of the ad, after the closing call to action. [L0344, 2023]
- Polished background music in a short product video can make a simple, low-cost product feel more premium. A minimal video with a refined soundtrack can present a premium look better than a busy, text-heavy ad. [L0692, 2023]

**Sound effects and cues**

- Sound effects for transitions and emphasis: open Audio > Sound effects, pick a sound such as a whoosh for a transition or a ding for a wink, drag it onto an audio track aligned with the action, and lower the volume so it is subtle. [L1423, undated]
- Use sound effects like whooshes to support transitions, aligned with the cut, and turn them down so they stay in the background. A sound effect should never be the main element of the piece. [L0344, 2023]
- Sound design has two purposes: ambient sounds that match what is on screen (for example train noise for a train), and stylistic cues tied to a visual moment (for example a ding on a wink or a whoosh on a transition). Stylistic cues are in CapCut's sound library at no extra cost. [L1423, undated]
- Audio cues that fall on the same beat as a visual cut make the edit feel intentional. Layer cues sparingly so they guide attention rather than distract. [L0344, 2023]
- Shocking visuals, animations or sounds can hold attention, but use them only when they fit the brand and the audience. Test them against a plain version before committing. [L0524, 2023]
- Ads should avoid silence. Continuous sound and visual movement hold attention, unless a pause is used deliberately for effect. [L0344, 2023]

**Trending and recreated sounds**

- Use trending sounds and seasonal trends to attract viewers, but only if they fit the product. Examples mentioned: a major sports event that was still running, and the US holiday travel trend around Christmas and New Year. [L0657, undated]
- Viewers must be watching or listening at every moment. Text-to-speech over a strong satisfying sound distracts from the sound, so use plain on-screen text there instead. [L1383, 2022]
- For a satisfying product sound, use plain text instead of text-to-speech so viewers can hear the sound. For an ordinary sound, text-to-speech and on-screen text can run together. [L1383, 2022]
- Keep the original trending sound at a very low level, around 5, so viewers hear the recreated sound and the environment rather than the trending track. [L1383, 2022]
- Recreated sounds usually sound better than originals, because you control the recording. Re-record a sound and layer it in place of the original. [L1383, 2022]
- Find a trending sound on the For You page:
  1. When a sound plays on the For You page, tap the sound circle in the bottom right corner. [L1383, 2022]
  2. Note how many videos already use the sound. [L1383, 2022]
  3. Open the original video. If there is no original, open the most popular video using the sound. [L1383, 2022]
  4. Check when the sound was first used. [L1383, 2022]
  5. Look at view counts of other recent videos that use the sound. [L1383, 2022]
  6. Use the sound only if recent videos using it still get large views. Skip sounds that are about six months old or whose videos are old. [L1383, 2022]
- Recreate a product sound and raise its volume in Splice:
  1. Record the real product sound on a phone with the Voice Memos app, using a second cup and water if needed. [L1383, 2022]
  2. End the recording, then share the audio to Splice. [L1383, 2022]
  3. In Splice, tap Audio, then Music, then import the recording. [L1383, 2022]
  4. Detach the audio from the video at the point where the sound should play. [L1383, 2022]
  5. Split the audio where it starts and where it ends. [L1383, 2022]
  6. Open Volume and raise the level. Volume starts at 100; set it to about 180 to 200 so it plays louder without distorting. [L1383, 2022]
  7. Review the result and keep the recreated sound on the cue point. [L1383, 2022]

### Editing tools

**CapCut**

- CapCut is a video editing app good enough for beginners to make their own ad videos. Learning to produce creatives yourself also lets you brief outside creators more precisely. [L1107, 2023]
- CapCut is the editor used to cut TikTok Shop mentions, Orange Cart mentions and non-matching offers out of a copied video. [L0704, 2026]
- Edit simple problem-and-solution ads in CapCut using existing footage from AliExpress or YouTube, arranged as a short narrative: problem first, then solution. [L0950, 2023]
- Editing for UGC-style content can be done with free tools such as CapCut, which allows text overlays and short-form edits. Voice-over can be added to filmed product footage. [L0561, 2023+]

**Other tools**

- Splice is used for the traditional TikTok ad workflow above and for recreating product sounds. [L1381, 2022; L1383, 2022]
- Final Cut Pro, to make a TikTok version:
  1. Open the project settings and change the format from YouTube to TikTok. [L0888, 2023+]
  2. Move the character to the left so the speaker stays visible in the vertical frame before exporting. [L0888, 2023+]
- Opus Clip can cut one long customer video into several shorter clips and add an AI voiceover. Use it to turn existing UGC into many ad variations for split testing. [L1225, 2025]
- Basic editing skills, such as cropping out unwanted text and adding simple transitions, are enough to repurpose footage for ads. Advanced production is not required to start testing. [L0644, undated]
- Freelance editing: if editing is needed, send the footage and shot list to a freelance video editor on Fiverr or Upwork. A story and footage must exist before outsourcing the edit. [L0561, 2023+]
