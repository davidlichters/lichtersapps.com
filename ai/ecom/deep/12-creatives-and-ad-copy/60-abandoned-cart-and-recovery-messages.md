### Abandoned cart and recovery messages

- Second abandoned checkout email: write the draft directly in Shopify, copy the complete text, paste it into ChatGPT or another AI tool, request improved clarity and tone, then replace the original draft in Shopify with the AI-improved version. [L0149, undated]
- Proof belongs on the product page, not in the cart email. See the proof rule above. [L0261, 2025-26]
- Cart-recovery email prompt, Gary Bencivenga style. Use with the brand name filled in:

```
Write a cart-recovery email for [brand] that reads like a Gary Bencivenga piece. It should bring a shopper back to checkout by speaking to the strongest want they have. Keep the voice chatty, as if one person is talking to another, and take tone, values and word choice from the brand profile supplied earlier in this chat. This is the first message this shopper receives from us, so leave out the subject line and preview text, and offer no discount. Sell only what finishing the order gives them. Structure: (1) a brief, punchy headline naming our single most valuable selling point, preferably one that also answers a common hesitation, such as fast delivery, a 30-day guarantee, or Australian-owned. (2) A sub-headline built on their desire. (3) Wording in the target market's own language that says 'you left this behind'. (4) A picture of the items they abandoned. (5) A short call to action reading either 'Complete Your Order' or 'Return to Cart'. (6) A scarcity or urgency line suited to the audience, invented fresh rather than borrowed from any example, such as one about low stock after a viral moment. (7) A short opening centred on their desire that invites them back to the cart. (8) Persuasive answers to the three most common customer reservations, never using the word 'objection'. (9) A line saying help is available at [email]. (10) A strong closing call to action. (11) A sign-off. Aim for high burstiness and perplexity: original phrasing, varied sentence length, emotional pull. Adapt language to the target market. Keep it short enough to scan, put spacing between every block, and never repeat the same claim in two blocks.
```

### Pre-sale and offer-based outreach

- Make-an-offer email for a time-limited offer window. Use with the placeholders filled in:

```
Subject: Make us an offer, [promotion dates]

Hi [first name],

For the next [number] days, you can make an offer on [product or category] at [store name]. Reply to this email with the price you would pay, plus anything we should know, such as quantity or delivery date.

We review every offer personally. We may accept it, counter it with a different price, or decline it, and we will reply within [timeframe].

[store name] team
```

### Lead magnet ebooks

- Lead magnet offer formula: '[Product category benefit] solved with [number] proven [format] in [time]. [Social proof or scarcity]. Normally [original price], get it free. [Scarcity limiter, e.g. only 100 copies available].' This frames free content as premium while creating urgency. [L0307, 2023+]
  - Example: 'Seven best relaxation techniques to relax at home in less than 5 minutes. Usually $19.99, download free. Only 100 copies available.' [L0307, 2023+]
  - Example: 'Five energy-boosting rituals for busy entrepreneurs in under 10 minutes. Usually $29, free today only. Limited to first 50 subscribers.' [L0307, 2023+]
- Ebook cover line pattern: a named brand or expert presenting a solution, followed by the author's name, for example 'Your mentor presents [solution], written by [author name]'. This makes the cover read as a credible guide to a specific problem. [L0513, undated]
- Ebook title formula: a number or timeframe, a concrete promised result, and the topic or audience. The title should promise a clear, specific outcome that matches the product. (2026) [L0860, 2026]
  - Example 1: '5 Desk Stretches To Ease Stiff Shoulders' [L0860, 2026]
  - Example 2: '30-Day Starter Plan For Beginner Runners' [L0860, 2026]
- AI chat tools can contain factual or wording mistakes, so review the full ebook text before publishing it to customers. Health-related guides should carry a short educational disclaimer and point to a qualified professional. (2026) [L0861, 2026]
- An ebook used as a free gift should be 15 to 25 pages with 4 to 6 chapters. The product is mentioned only in the closing section. The ebook gives real value first and supports the product second. (2026) [L0861, 2026]
- Creating a free ebook gift with AI (2026):
  1. Use the ebook prompt, replacing the target audience, the product name and the problem with your own. [L0862, 2026]
  2. Check the generated text for placeholder text and for any leftover call-to-action placeholders. [L0862, 2026]
  3. Ask the AI to turn the text into a stylish, complete HTML file with the product name, and ignore the CTA link. [L0862, 2026]
  4. Review the HTML and the design notes. [L0862, 2026]
  5. Ask the AI to convert the HTML into a PDF. [L0862, 2026]
  6. Download the PDF and upload it to the Digital Downloads app as an asset. [L0862, 2026]
  7. Attach the e-book product to the gift position in the bundle. [L0862, 2026]
- Structure of a free-gift ebook, in order (2026):
  1. Title and subtitle. [L0862, 2026]
  2. Welcome note or short author intro that explains what the reader will get. [L0862, 2026]
  3. Four to six chapters, each with real, practical content, subheadings, short paragraphs and bullet points. [L0862, 2026]
  4. An actionable exercise in the chapter where it fits. [L0862, 2026]
  5. A closing section with a single soft next step. [L0862, 2026]
  6. An educational disclaimer at the end. [L0862, 2026]
- Example of an actionable exercise: a 30-second self-check at night with a flashlight and a mirror, followed by a single simple observation. It gives readers a concrete task without asking them to change their routine. (2026) [L0862, 2026]
- Choosing a lead magnet topic:
  1. Pick the sites, communities or influencers where you want to advertise. [L1076, 2019]
  2. Look at their homepage, feature articles and what is trending or popular there. [L1076, 2019]
  3. Check which books or resources on the topic sell best. [L1076, 2019]
  4. Create your own related version of the most popular idea and offer it as the free item. [L1076, 2019]
- Design the lead magnet PDF as a brochure. The first page introduces the company and why it is in business. Every page footer carries an offer and a link to the store. The conclusion explains why to buy. Save it as a PDF with clickable links so readers can act from their device. [L1076, 2019]
- Outsource lead magnet ebook design on Fiverr or Upwork for about 20 to 50 dollars, after you have written the content yourself in a Google Doc. Only the footers, logos and backgrounds need a designer. [L1076, 2019]

### Blog and SEO content

- Create one custom image per blog article that contains the target keyword and matches the brand. Most competitors skip this step, so it helps you stand out. [L0380, 2014]
- Google Docs article template, kept in one document so there are no multiple file versions:
  1. The keyword at the top. [L0382, 2017]
  2. The article draft. [L0382, 2017]
  3. The edited version, in the same document. [L0382, 2017]
  4. Meta descriptions and headlines. [L0382, 2017]
  5. Images and anything else related to the article. [L0382, 2017]
  6. A content suggestion section, filled by the data entry assistant with what the top 10 results contain. [L0382, 2017]
- Optimise existing articles first. Use a sales copywriter to improve headlines and meta descriptions for articles that already exist, because this is the highest-leverage step for traffic that already comes in. Old-article optimisation can be tested before new articles are written. [L0382, 2017]
- Create custom images for articles to keep a consistent brand look, and put the target keyword in the image alt tag so search engines understand the image. [L0382, 2017]
- Content is a versatile asset. It supports organic search, Google search text and display ads, YouTube ads and organic traffic, LinkedIn for B2B, Google remarketing, Facebook and Instagram retargeting, and cold social traffic on Pinterest, Facebook and Instagram. It also improves conversion rate and makes email marketing easier. [L0414, 2021-22]
- Four-step content marketing process:
  1. Plan: write 12 content ideas that answer the customer's most common problems and pain points in as much detail as possible. Start with easy, low-hanging topics and move to research-based topics later. Twelve pieces give roughly three to six months of content. [L0414, 2021-22]
  2. Produce: write it yourself or outsource it to a freelance writer, which makes consistent output more likely. A freelance job can be posted on ProBlogger (problogger.com) via its jobs board. [L0414, 2021-22]
  3. Post: publish two new pieces of quality content per month for 12 months. [L0414, 2021-22]
  4. Promote: actively promote each piece across traffic channels, since posting alone does not grow traffic. [L0414, 2021-22]
- Affiliate blog post for an information product:
  1. Find the information product on ClickBank or in your niche's resources, communities or forums. [L0923, 2015]
  2. Watch reviews or unboxing videos of the product so you understand what it is, without buying it. [L0923, 2015]
  3. Write a blog post on your Shopify blog that gives useful information about the product and explains why you recommend it. [L0923, 2015]
  4. Close the post with a call to action and your affiliate link, for example a link to join the program. [L0923, 2015]
  5. Retarget visitors of the blog post with Facebook ads. [L0923, 2015]

### Organic social: cadence and content

- Post one to two times per week on both Facebook and Instagram, without exception. Inactive pages whose most recent post is months or years old signal to customers that the business may no longer be operating. [L0157, undated]
- Basic organic posts, about a couple per week, add credibility but do not drive early revenue. Posts can be scheduled weeks or months ahead, and AI can help draft them. (2025) [L0972, 2025]
- For a brand that posts organic content, publishing a higher volume of posts showing the product in use supports reach and virality. Choose posts that show the product in use rather than giveaways, because they attract people more likely to buy. (2025-26) [L1016, 2026]
- Test organic posts first, then move the best performers into paid ads. Running organic posts as ads without adjustment produced no sales in one brand's test over three months. (2025-26) [L1225, 2025]
- Share the product images and pins you create for Pinterest on Facebook and other social networks too. Visual content performs well there, so one set of images can serve both platforms. [L1136, 2014]
- Keep most reels evergreen, meaning still relevant months later. A common split is about 70 percent evergreen content and about 30 percent trend-based content. (2025-26) [L0796, 2025-26]
- Keep posts professional and straightforward rather than chasing viral content. Announce new authorised supplier relationships, highlight specific products you recommend, or create product spotlights that explain one to three key reasons why customers should benefit from each item. [L0157, undated]
- Use AI to generate 30 weeks of weekly content ideas if you need guidance on post themes. Keep the focus on posts that build business credibility rather than chase viral engagement. [L0157, undated]
- Start with easy wins in your niche. Example: home grilling for a grilling brand. [L0237, 2018]
  1. Create a Facebook Business Page.
  2. Create an Instagram account.
  3. Post memes related to your niche.
  - Steps 1 to 3 in the order given. [L0237, 2018]
- Share diverse content types that resonate with your audience: blogs, recipes, stories, photos, videos and memes. [L0237, 2018]
- Start with content that is funny or educational, because humour is the easiest way to earn engagement early. Test formats until one earns shares, and keep making content people want to share. [L0487, 2018]
- Post content people are proud to share, because sharing should raise their social standing with friends by making them look funny or informed. Ask whether a friend who shares the post will look good for doing so. [L0487, 2018]
- Meme-style audience-building pattern: set two contrasting options that the niche cares about, such as a premium version against a mass-produced one. End with a short request to like and share if the reader agrees. People who identify with one side engage quickly and invite friends to join. [L0487, 2018]
  - Example: 'Hand-ground coffee versus the instant stuff: which side are you on? Like and share if you agree.' [L0487, 2018]
  - Example: 'Hand-rolled pasta versus the boxed kind: tag the friend who still buys boxed.' [L0487, 2018]
- Blanket statements that could describe any brand get no engagement, because people see many similar posts every day. Content has to be specific to the niche and stand out from competing posts. [L0487, 2018]
- Product post captions for organic social use three parts: a short everyday moment or season the product fits, the product photo, and one light call to action such as a new arrival, a restock or a link to browse. The mood line makes the post relatable without pushing a price. [L0653, 2020-26]
  - Example 1: 'Mornings start easier with our new travel bottle. Back in stock, link in bio.' [L0653, 2020-26]
  - Example 2: 'Rain outside, warm drinks inside. Our insulated mugs now come in three new colours, see the collection.' [L0653, 2020-26]
- News-based posts: ask the AI for recent news in a niche and for post ideas that tie the brand into it. Web search in the tool may return only a few results, so verify the news before posting. [L0353, 2023]
  - Template:

```
Ask for the latest news in [niche], explain that you sell [product], and request news items that could serve as material for social posts linking your brand to them.
```

  [L0353, 2023]
- Organic videos should be entertaining and show the product in use, not list ingredients or specifications, because informational talk reduces watch time. [L0820, undated]
- Overly polished, brand-heavy content lowers performance on short video. Aim for authentic, watchable content with a clean background and good camera quality rather than heavy branding. [L0820, undated]
- Organic videos should avoid explicit buy or shop calls to action. Paid ads should include a clear call to action and a scarcity element. [L0820, undated]
- Mistake to avoid: blanket content is ignored; see the niche-specific rule above. [L0487, 2018]

### Organic social: captions, polls and interaction

- Write the way you would text a friend, in captions and replies. Humour, voice notes and voiceover make content feel real, and the more casual the tone, the better it resonates. (2025-26) [L0576, 2025-26]
- Questions in captions invite comments and act as free market research, showing what the audience thinks and wants. They also make followers feel more connected to the brand. (2025-26) [L0576, 2025-26]
- Question-and-reply procedure:
  1. End the post caption with an open question that invites an opinion or a verdict. [L0576, 2025-26]
  2. Check the comments on the post. [L0576, 2025-26]
  3. Reply to each answer, ideally with a follow-up question about what the commenter meant. [L0576, 2025-26]
  4. Keep the dialogue open for as long as people keep responding. [L0576, 2025-26]
- Caption question pattern: end with a short, open question that asks for a verdict, an opinion or a shared experience. It gives people a reason to respond and turns the post into a conversation. [L0576, 2025-26]
  - Example 1 for a kitchen gadget: 'Did this actually save you time, or is it just a gimmick?' [L0576, 2025-26]
  - Example 2 for a skincare brand: 'Have you ever had a breakout before a big event? What helped you?' [L0576, 2025-26]
- Asking a question and then not replying to the answers wastes the engagement the question created. Reply to comments so people see the brand is listening. [L0576, 2025-26]
- Instagram's polls-in-captions feature is newer and actively promoted by the platform, and creators use it to boost engagement. Newer features a platform pushes are generally worth adopting early. (2025-26) [L0576, 2025-26]
- Polls in captions: write the caption first, then add a poll below it. Viewers can vote while they watch the reel, without leaving the caption or writing a comment. (2025-26) [L0576, 2025-26]
- Two-option poll pattern: pose a light, fun question about what the audience prefers next, such as behind-the-scenes content against a tutorial. Keep it quick to answer, so people vote in seconds. A single tap is an easy ask, and the result shows which content the audience wants more of. [L0576, 2025-26]
  - Example 1: 'Coffee at home or at the café?' [L0576, 2025-26]
  - Example 2: 'Morning routine or packing hacks next?' [L0576, 2025-26]
- Polls can also use emoji voting, where each choice is represented by an emoji. Low-effort formats like polls and emoji votes are the easiest way to start audience engagement. (2025-26) [L0576, 2025-26]
- Instagram Stories interactive stickers include polls, question boxes and quizzes. Use them repeatedly in a recurring series so the audience expects them and returns to the same format. (2025-26) [L0576, 2025-26]
- Emoji sticker on Instagram Stories: place a heart (or another emoji) sticker with a short prompt, for example asking viewers to tap it if they have been following for a while. Tapping sends an immediate reaction back to the story, a one-tap way to take part. [L0576, 2025-26]
- Offer low-barrier ways to take part, such as tapping a sticker, double tapping or dropping one emoji, instead of asking for a full comment or direct message. Lower effort brings more participation. (2025-26) [L0576, 2025-26]
- Add new interactive elements to content followers already know and love, such as the regular weekly stories, so engagement becomes part of an existing habit. [L0576, 2025-26]
- Polls in Instagram captions help the algorithm and give the brand insight into what its audience wants. A poll below the caption is a quick way to get engagement and feedback from the same post. (2025-26) [L0576, 2025-26]
- Pair a quick poll with an open question. Use the poll for a one-tap yes-or-no or either-or choice. Ask a more in-depth open question in the comments for people who want to write more. Low-effort and high-effort participants then have separate ways to respond to the same post. (2025-26) [L0576, 2025-26]

### Viral follow-up and organic-to-paid

- Organic video conversion rules differ by stage. Two values apply to two situations:
  - Default organic videos: no explicit buy or shop call to action [L0820, undated].
  - After a viral video: the next video can be more product-focused, with a clear call to action and a direct offer, because converting viewers often needs an offer more than entertainment [L0706, 2020+]. Follow-up videos can be more toned down and professional, with more calls to action and some traditional ad tactics [L1077, undated].
- Once one organic video goes viral, the next should be more toned down and professional. Viewers who come from the viral video then see a calmer follow-up, which builds credibility and encourages them to visit the store and buy. [L1077, undated]
- Extend a viral video's effect and turn views into sales:
  1. Upload four to five good-quality videos while the viral effect is still active, spaced about two hours apart. [L0706, 2020+]
  2. Once the viral effect slows, boost the video with TikTok coins. [L0706, 2020+]
  3. Alternatively, run Spark Ads on the video to reach more viewers. [L0706, 2020+]
  4. Use an urgency call to action at the end of new videos to convert viewers. [L0706, 2020+]
- Case: a slip-resistant slipper product went viral with a video of about 44 million views, posted September 14, with no paid ads. The video quality and audio were poor, but the clip opened with a slipping risk, showed the slippers solving that problem, and repeated the demonstration. The account had uploaded several times a day, and this one video drove most of the result. The store was later no longer available. [L0657, undated]
- Organic-only success can be judged by the absence of repeated ad-style videos or paid-boost patterns on the account. The hosts believed the slipper account was fully organic because no other video showed signs of paid promotion. [L0657, undated]

### Images and alt text

- Image alt text describes what an image shows. It helps visually impaired users and lets Google understand the images, including their appearance in Google Images. Editing alt text in bulk saves time when a store has many product images. [L1337, 2020-26]
