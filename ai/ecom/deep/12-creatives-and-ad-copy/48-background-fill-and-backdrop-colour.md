### Background fill and backdrop colour

- Standard product backdrop: a neutral light-grey, hex E3E3E3 (RGB 227, 227, 227). In Photoshop, add a new layer, choose Edit > Fill, set Contents to Color, enter the value, and place the filled layer below the product photo. Place it below the photo, not above. [L1132, undated; L1178, undated]
- Use one consistent light-grey background (E3E3E3 in the lesson) across all product images so the set looks uniform. Apply the same fill to every cutout and check it visually after placing the subject. [L1178, undated]
- Off-white looks more attractive than pure white for product photography. Add a new layer under the product, use Edit > Fill, and set the colour to off-white. [L1199, undated]
- An off-white or slightly grey backdrop hides cut-out edges better than pure white. Choose the final background colour before cutting out the product, so the edges are planned for it. [L1199, undated]
- Pure white is still the standard for product listing ads. Keep standard white-background photos for them. [L1094, 2023]
- A light-grey background matching the website's colour scheme is a practical default, so cutouts fit the store's look. [L1258, undated]
- Product photos for a store with a light-grey colour scheme can use a plain light-grey background that matches the theme. Other colours are possible in Canva, but light grey suits that palette. [L1257, undated]

### Shadows

- Add a simple shadow in Photoshop: [L1199, undated]
  1. Draw an ellipse with the Ellipse tool, set fill to black and stroke to none.
  2. Convert the layer to a smart object.
  3. Apply Filter > Blur > Gaussian Blur.
  4. Position it under the product.
  - A thick, tight blur gives a shadow close to the floor. Blur more and move the shape further back for a shadow that looks farther away.
  - Side shadows: copy the layer, use Alt or Option to clip it to the image layer, and transform it with Command+T.
- Refine a shadow with a layer mask: add a mask to the shadow layer, then paint away parts with a black brush, or use the Gradient tool to fade the shadow along the left edge. Tune it until it looks convincing. [L1199, undated]
- Canva background removal also removes the shadow. Add shadows back using Elements > Shadows. [L1199, undated]
- PhotoRoom's AI Instant Backgrounds places the product on a pre-made background with shadows. [L1199, undated]

### Generative Fill and AI-based background work

- Remove a background with Generative Fill in Photoshop: [L1199, undated]
  1. Use the Lasso tool to draw around the product. Keep the shadow inside the selection if you want to keep it.
  2. Press Ctrl+Shift (Command+Shift on Mac) to invert the selection to the background.
  3. Right-click and choose Generative Fill, then click Generate to create a clean background.
  4. Pick one of the generated options.
- Canva's Magic Media tool handles most product image creation and editing needs. [L0276, 2024]
- Canva's AI erase tool is a basic option. [L1199, undated]
- Use AI-generated background images for social media posts and email campaigns, and occasionally for product pages. Keep standard white-background photos for product listing ads. [L1094, 2023]
- Before a Shopping image is generated with AI, check the source photo. Replace a weak source first. [L0882, 2025-26]

### Canva background removal and edits

- Canva background removal: open the uploaded image, choose Edit photo, then select Background remover. Canva's Magic Grab lifts the object off the page. [L1199, undated; L1093, 2023]
- Canva one-click removal, with refinement: [L1257, undated]
  1. Open the image and click Edit photo.
  2. Click Remove background and let Canva process it.
  3. Check the result; for a clean look, choose a plain light-grey background, which matches a website colour scheme.
  4. If parts were cut out wrongly, refine after the fact: click Adjust, then Configure, then use Remove and Restore.
  5. Use the brush-size control and zoom in to erase leftover lumps with Erase, or restore cut-out areas with Restore.
  5. Check edges for thin black lines and remove them manually.
- Refinement tools are found under Adjust > Configure, which offers Remove and Restore. Brush size can be changed and the area zoomed in for finer work. [L1257, undated]
- Canva's background removal may leave thin dark lines around the edges of a cutout. Erase them manually with the Erase tool at high zoom, or the image looks unpolished on the product page. [L1257, undated]
- Canva's auto cutout does not always get edges right. Check every edge and remove leftover fragments before exporting. Hair, small gaps and white fringes are common problem areas. [L0754, undated]
- Canva's one-click removal is fast and works well when speed matters more than precision. It gives less control than Photoshop: blemishes are hard to remove, shadows are harder to handle, and edges may not be clean enough for a premium product page. [L1257, undated]
- Canva's refinement is rougher ("a ball park") than Photoshop's precise edge tools. [L1257, undated]
- For a precise cutout, such as clothing with blemishes or detailed edges, use Photoshop: the selection tool, brushes, and edge refinement such as Shift Edge. [L1257, undated]
- Canva colour adjustment: open Edit photo > Adjust > Color, pick individual colours Canva detects, and change saturation or vibrance. Canva's contrast control is less precise than Lightroom's. [L1199, undated]
- Canva can make a simple change to a supplier image: cut the product out and change the background colour. This is enough to make a generic image look distinct when no major edit is needed. [L1218, undated]
- Edit supplier product photos so their backgrounds match the colour scheme of the landing page, using Canva or Photoshop, to create a branded look. [L0387, 2022]
- Canva's Magic Grab, Background remover and AI erase tools form the cheaper beginner option, with basic colour correction. [L1199, undated]

### PhotoRoom and paid tools

- Tools overview: [L1199, undated]
  - Lightroom: basic colour correction and cropping.
  - Photoshop: cutting out products and adding shadows.
  - Canva: cheaper beginner option with basic colour correction, background removal and an AI erase tool.
  - PhotoRoom: AI cutout service.
- Photoshop costs about £10 per month for the home photo procedure. [L0561, 2023+]
- A freelancer can remove backgrounds and produce clean white-background images when a supplier's photos show busy showroom backgrounds that Instagram rejects. [L1279, 2021]

### Making supplier images look unique

- Procedure: [L0921, 2022]
  1. Take the supplier product image.
  2. Remove the background in Canva.
  3. Place the product on a new background.
  4. Adjust the colouring slightly so it looks like a different image.
  5. Add your brand name to the image before publishing.
- Modify competitor photos for uniqueness; AI tools can transform original photos into unique variations. [L0276, 2024]
- Replace generic supplier photos with Canva-edited images that match the brand. [L0692, 2023]
- Edit backgrounds to match the landing page colour scheme for a branded look. [L0387, 2022]
- Use a sizing guide built in your brand colours instead of a generic supplier image. [L0387, 2022]

### Product page image set

- Every product page needs more than one photo. A single photo makes a purchase unlikely. If the supplier has only one, ask for more, search the internet, or create variations: the product alone, the brand logo, and the available colour options. [L1293, 2014]
- Upload the clean white contoured studio images first, plus at least two styled images in the product media section. Use both studio and lifestyle images with people wearing the product. [L1331, 2025-26]
- Order product images for a listing: [L0470, 2023]
  1. Place the main straight-on product shot first, as customers expect.
  2. Add images of the product from different angles.
  3. Add close-up images showing material or mechanism.
  4. Add lifestyle images showing the product in use, optionally embedded in the description instead of the gallery.
  - Upload all images that are relevant to the purchase decision.
- Image set order, one source 2026: [L0887, 2026]
  1. Product hero image: the product on a pure white background, generated with the hero image prompt.
  2. Value proposition headline image: an image with a large caption based on the value proposition headline.
  3. Homepage photos.
  4. Product photos for the product page.
  - Every image headline, bullet and visual suggestion must reflect real customer language and the emotional desire of the target buyer.
- A homepage hero image is valuable space and can be a tight crop of one of the product photos. Use a strong product photo for it. [L0884, 2025-26]
- Show the product's dimensions and benefit callouts on the images. [L1346, 2020-26]
- Apparel product set example: [L0884, 2025-26]
  - An indoor morning-light living room scene.
  - An early-morning urban coffee room scene.
  - A mirror selfie.
  - A zoomed hero image.
  - A close-up of the fleece layer.
  - Each scene should show the product in a different everyday setting.
- Use high-quality product imagery and close-ups showing fabric, weight and detail. [L1438, 2023+]
- For a product that solves a problem, show it in use in the main product images and in a short how-to-use video, such as a person using it while sitting or lying down. Where the product suits presents, also offer it as a gift option. [L0549, undated]

### Lifestyle images and the emotional brief

- Product photos should create emotion and desire by showing the life the buyer wants, not only the item. Plan each image around what the target consumer aspires to. [L0884, 2025-26]
- Lifestyle photos can sell an aspirational version of a product's use, even when most buyers do not actually do that activity. Choose scenes that match the dream of the target consumer. [L0884, 2025-26]
- Show the product in use, such as a parent using a baby product on a picnic or a traveller using a bag, to demonstrate outdoor, travel and everyday uses. [L1346, 2020-26]
- Models showing the product in use, including opening the package and comfort, give buyers a clear picture. Use real people, not only the product on its own. [L0492, 2023+]
- Product photos can include recognisable local scenery, such as mountains behind the model, and local products on the table. These cues signal that the brand is local rather than shipped from abroad, which helps build trust. [L0492, 2023+]
- Use original photos and models instead of supplier stock or AliExpress images. Original content can be reused in Facebook ads and makes the product look more trustworthy. [L0492, 2023+]
- Benefit-led product images: one image per benefit, each showing the product solving a specific problem, with a short callout. [L1346, 2020-26]
  - Example: a camera bag photographed with a laptop inside, labelled "fits 15-inch laptops".
  - Example: a kitchen knife set photographed on a cutting board, labelled "razor-sharp blades for daily use".
- Product images and GIFs that show exactly how the product is used are strong selling assets. They can convert buyers even when the store is simple. [L1172, 2023+]
- The product image should show the product in its intended setting and look like it does when set up. A plain product shot grabs less attention than a product in use, so use the strongest image available. [L1169, 2021-22]
- Photo styles can be set by describing the camera look: a film photo, an iPhone-style snapshot, or a studio shot by a professional photographer. Asking a chat assistant which style the target consumer prefers is a valid way to choose. [L0884, 2025-26]
- A candid hard flash style fits a trendy, authentic look that resonates with consumers from short video platforms such as TikTok. It pushes existing iPhone-style photos towards a stronger, more distinctive look. [L0884, 2025-26]
- Model clothing can be changed to brand colours, such as dark purple or a pink sweater, so the photos reinforce the brand palette. Adjust clothing in the prompt instead of accepting the default colours. [L0884, 2025-26]
- Apparel case: [L0884, 2025-26]
  - Starting point: an apparel brand's website used eight product images: five on the product page, one homepage hero, one fleece interior detail, and one everyday-wear shot. The existing photos were already candid phone-style shots, and the goal was to replace all eight with a stronger brand look.
  - Actions: a chat assistant generated eight scene prompts. The first image run showed the same woman in every photo, so the prompts were edited to describe a different woman each time. The hard flash style prompt was added, product photos were attached as reference, a night scene was changed to daylight, and clothing was set to brand colours such as dark purple and pink. All eight images were then generated and placed on the site.
  - Result: the before and after comparison was judged a large improvement, the photos looked good on mobile, and no conversion figures were reported.
- Discount sticker on product photos: [L0387, 2022]
  1. Take the product photos from the supplier or AliExpress listing.
  2. Open Canva and search for a Black Friday sticker template.
  3. Upload the sticker, remove its extra layers if needed, and place it on the product photo.
  4. Resize the sticker and fit the text, for example "60% off" or "50% off".
  5. Put the sticker on all product images, for example in the top-right corner, to improve click-through rate.
- For home decor and furniture ads on Pinterest, the visual quality of the product images is the main driver of performance. Invest in strong product imagery before testing campaigns. [L0788, undated]

### Social and Pinterest imagery

- Pin images should show the product in use and the result or feeling it creates, not only a product shot on a white background. Lifestyle scenes such as a summer picnic engage the Pinterest audience more than plain product photos. [L1136, 2014]
- Dropshippers with only supplier photos should create their own images showing people using the product and getting the desired benefit. Story-based images are the main way to make dropshipped products stand out on Pinterest. [L1136, 2014]
- Raw user-style images found through Instagram hashtag research produced a conversion rate of about 8% to 9% in one test with a weighted hula hoop. Real-looking UGC-style photos can outperform polished shots. [L1345, 2021+]
- The same Instagram hashtag research produced a conversion rate of 8-9% for one product. [L1260, undated]
