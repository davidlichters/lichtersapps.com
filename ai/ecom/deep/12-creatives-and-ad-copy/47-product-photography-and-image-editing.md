## Product photography and image editing

### Sourcing and licensing product images

- Search order for finding images: Pexels, Pixabay, Google Images, Alibaba, AliExpress. If none works, search for a very similar product or research hashtags on Instagram and use raw UGC-style images. [L1260, undated]
- Stock photo sites suit homepage images. Product pages need the actual product photo, so use filtered Google image results or supplier images there. [L0359, 2020]
- Stock sites cost about $1 to $2 per image. [L0804, 2023]
- Canva's free stock photos work well for popular niches. For niches with few stock photos, such as mechanical or basic furniture products, download photos from a stock photo website and upload them to Canva. [L1045, 2020-26]
- Google Images, licensed use (2023 method): [L0804, 2023; L0934, 2023]
  1. Search the product on Google Images.
  2. Click Tools.
  3. Click Usage rights and pick a Creative Commons option.
- Google Images, licensed use (2019, older method): [L0960, 2019]
  1. Search for the product on Google Images.
  2. Click Tools below the search bar.
  3. Open the usage rights menu.
  4. Filter for images labelled for reuse, and use only those.
- Google Images results are often small and their licensing is unclear. Check the dimensions of every result before deciding to use it. [L1310, 2021-22]
- Do not use Alibaba pictures on product pages. Most listings do not match the product you sell, and the images are not licensed for resale. Replace watermarked supplier photos too. [L0804, 2023]
- AliExpress and CJ Dropshipping photos are often good enough for home decor ads, so a photographer is not needed to start testing these products. [L0642, undated]
- For early fashion tests, use supplier photos from AliExpress as ad creatives instead of paying for a photo shoot. Keep production cost low until the product has validated sales. [L0661, undated]
- Once you scale, avoid showing raw AliExpress images on a product page. Use a sizing guide built in your brand colours instead of a generic supplier image. [L0387, 2022]
- Generic supplier photos from marketplace-style listings look unbranded and reduce trust. Replacing them with Canva-edited images that match the store's branding improves credibility. [L0692, 2023]
- Use royalty-free images, or photos supplied by the brand that you have permission to use. Do not use competitors' product images on the store or in ads. [L0468, 2020]
- Competitor photos can be modified for uniqueness. AI tools can transform original photos into unique variations. [L0276, 2024]
- Images can be sourced from competitor websites if they are not protected. If a site blocks saving images, take a screenshot on a Mac or use an image enhancement tool. [L0577, 2023]
- Image enhancement tool: a website that upscales low-resolution product images to higher quality. It can be used for free after creating an account. [L0577, 2023]
- Stock Images (Free) was named as a free stock photo source in the December 2020 coaching call. The lesson names the resource without naming the provider or describing its licence. [L0426, 2020]
- Until a supplier is approved and its images can be used, use placeholders such as AI-generated demo product photos or licensed stock photos. This avoids using supplier images without permission. [L0970, 2023]
- Stock product photos with a single angle are fine for demo products. Use real photos once products are approved and sold. [L0855, 2023]
- Supplier product photos that look like 3D renders are a weak choice. If you must use them, disclose that they are renders and ask customers for real photos. [L0968, 2021]
- Keep watermarks very subtle and hard to see. Do not set a watermarked image as the primary image for a product variant, because Google Merchant Center rejects listings whose primary image has a watermark. Keep them on the product page. [L1357, 2018]

- Adding an external stock photo to Canva: [L0342, 2020-26]
  1. Download the free image from a stock photo site to the computer.
  2. Drag the file into Canva; it uploads to the Uploads section and may take a moment for large files.
  3. Open Uploads, drag the image onto the page, right-click it and choose Replace background (or Set image as background).
  4. The image is cropped to the template size automatically.

### Own photos versus supplier and stock images

- Taking your own product photos is usually not worth the cost, because matching supplier-quality images requires a professional photo shoot. Use supplier-approved images when available. [L0934, 2023]
- Keep production cost low until a product has validated sales. [L0661, undated]
- Invest in photo shoots and edited videos once a product shows success. Generic supplier footage on Google performs poorly. [L0577, 2023]
- Shooting your own photos of received samples is a practical starting point when no professional production shoot is available. [L1438, 2023+]
- Images and video must look professional. [L0577, 2023]
- Reconciliation: stock and supplier images apply while a product is untested. Shoot your own photos once sales validate the product. A low-budget home setup is covered below. [L0934, 2023; L0642, undated; L0661, undated; L0577, 2023; L1438, 2023+]

### Low-budget and home photo setup

- Budget smartphone setup (USD): a used coil light bought second-hand for about $20 works as the key light. Cheap LED ring lights from Amazon at $10 to $15 serve as additional or fill light. White card plus a few sheets of paper from a stationery shop cost about $7 in total. Add a sturdy stand with a spirit level, an adjustable tripod, and a cheap smartphone clamp adapter. [L1199, undated]
- Home photo procedure (Photoshop costs about £10 per month): [L0561, 2023+]
  1. Photograph the product on an ordinary surface such as a bed or a table.
  2. Cut the product out of the background in Photoshop.
  3. Place the cutout on cleaner or different backgrounds for the ad.

### Starting from a clean source photo

- Before generating a Shopping image with AI, start with a clean source photo: front-facing, high resolution, with the product clearly the focus. Input quality determines output quality, so replace a weak source photo first. [L0882, 2025-26]
- Delete photos that are too soft or pixelated, even if they look usable at a glance. They will look worse once enlarged. [L1178, undated]
- Discard shots that are too low in resolution for the intended use. Camera resolution matters. [L1132, undated]
- If product images are pixelated, edit them with an AI image tool, or hire a Fiverr freelancer for about $5 to $10 to retouch the background and make the product stand out. [L1260, undated]
- Never enlarge a small image to fit a larger banner space. It becomes pixelated and unprofessional. The image should be at least the size of the area where it is displayed. [L1310, 2021-22]
- Resize an image before cropping it. Reduce width or height to the target first, and crop only the side you do not need if one dimension is still too large. [L1310, 2021-22]
- Before cutting out a product, check the original shot. A straight-on, centred angle and a clean, well-lit photo give a better result. Odd angles or low-quality sources are harder to cut out. [L1257, undated]
- Product photos should be shot straight on at a suitable angle. An odd angle can look awkward even after a clean cutout. [L1258, undated]
- A perfectly head-on, dead-centre shot can look flat on a product page. Choose a better angle during the shoot, because background removal cannot fix composition. [L1257, undated]
- Products that already sit on a clean, separated background need little or no cutout work. [L1257, undated]
- A white product on a white backdrop is hard to cut out because the edges blend in. Shoot light-coloured products on a plain grey or contrasting backdrop. [L1199, undated]
- Good lighting and correct setup matter more than heavy editing. Good light lets the phone capture the most detail. Blurred or dark images can be partly rescued, but not fully corrected. [L1199, undated]

### Photo shoot setup: backdrop and surface

- Use a matte white or neutral backdrop that is monochromatic, with no blue or green tones, because colours bleed into the product. Matte surfaces avoid harsh reflections. Darker backdrops such as black or grey also work if they are monochromatic. [L1199, undated]
- Set up the surface: [L1199, undated]
  1. Lay white paper over a flat surface such as a chest of drawers so it curves upward into the wall in a cove shape with no visible horizon line.
  2. Tape it to the wall and floor.
  3. Make it large enough to frame the product from several angles.

### Photo shoot setup: light

- Diffuse hard light before it reaches the product. A direct source creates harsh glare and shine. An umbrella-style diffuser in front of the light, or a white pillowcase hung in front of a ring light, softens it into cloud-like light. [L1199, undated]
- Natural light works best, ideally from large windows on an overcast day, because clouds filter sunlight into soft light. When natural light is unavailable, use artificial lights with a diffuser. [L1199, undated]
- Adjust the key light's distance before adding diffusion, since moving it changes how strong and harsh the light is. The diffuser then works on the right level. [L1199, undated]
- Reduce shadows with a fill light: [L1199, undated]
  1. Place a second light or a white reflector card on the opposite side of the key light.
  2. Move the fill light farther away so it only softens the shadow edges.
  3. Check the result by watching how the shadow lightens when the card is introduced.
- Watch for shadows the photographer casts on the product. Keep the camera's position on the side opposite the key light so it does not block the light. [L1199, undated]
- For apparel shoots, check that light falls evenly on the product. Lighting that falls on elbow pads or blocks the face makes the subject hard to cut out cleanly. Re-shoot with more even light rather than trying to fix it in Photoshop. [L1132, undated; L1178, undated]

### Photo shoot setup: camera and phone

- Use a camera with at least 1080p resolution, which most phones support. Use the basic camera app and avoid manual settings such as ISO or aperture. Turn on the 3x3 grid overlay to keep the product centred and level, and tap the subject to set focus. [L1199, undated]
- Smartphones show a level guide when tilted. Use it together with the 3x3 grid to align a straight product shot so both sides are symmetrical. [L1199, undated]
- Do not use the telephoto or zoom lens on a smartphone for product shots, because image quality is poor. Use the standard main camera and move the phone closer for close-ups. Shoot wider than the final crop so the image can be cut to a square later. [L1199, undated]
- Keep the phone away from shutter buttons on the stand. An accidental tap can fill the camera roll with duplicates. Position the stand where no physical buttons sit under the lens or near your hands. [L1199, undated]

### Photo shoot setup: angles and details

- Shoot a product from several angles: [L1199, undated]
  1. Take a dead-level straight-on shot at product height.
  2. Take additional angles from the sides, front and back.
  3. Take a high-angle shot from above.
  4. Take a flat shot from below; for drink cans, this makes them look powerful.
  5. Take a top-down angled shot.
  6. Keep the camera fixed and rotate the product to capture other sides if it has several good angles.
- Shoot a wide variety of angles, since not every shot will be usable. Choose the best few for cutout and keep the others for social use without editing. [L1178, undated]
- For apparel, capture several angles such as front, side, bent-arm and full-body frames. Accessories or equipment should not cover the model's face. Only the best frame of each angle needs a full cutout. [L1132, undated]
- Photograph product features: take close-ups of buttons, controls, bases, lids and other distinctive details. Show functional features, such as a product floating in water. [L1199, undated]
- Shoot close-ups at minimum focus distance. Move the phone back slightly until the focus is sharp. [L1199, undated]
- A product image must show the entire product in its natural state and highlight its unique features. Pick sharp, well-lit shots and include a close-up of the most distinctive feature for each product. [L1199, undated]
- Capture close-ups of fabric, weight and detail so buyers understand quality before purchase. [L1438, 2023+]

### Lightroom corrections

- Division of work: Lightroom handles quick crops and simple exposure adjustments. Photoshop handles masking, cutouts and retouching. [L1132, undated]
- Basic light correction order: [L1199, undated]
  1. Exposure: brighten dark photos.
  2. Contrast: sharpen or soften the difference between light and dark areas.
  3. Highlights down: reduce overly bright white areas.
  4. Shadows down: add depth.
  5. Check Whites and Blacks for the tonal range.
  6. Use the crop tool and choose the 1:1 square ratio for product images.
- Colour Mixer (HSL panel): select a colour channel such as yellow and increase its saturation to make it pop, or desaturate a channel such as purple to remove an unwanted colour cast. [L1199, undated]
- Keep the product's true colours. Adjust colour only to correct a colour cast or to match the real item, because heavy shifts make the product look different from what the customer receives. [L1199, undated]
- Judge exposure and contrast by eye on the screen, not by fixed values. Move each slider until the image looks right, then check the result side by side with the original. [L1199, undated]
- Use a simple Lightroom crop for photos that only need framing. [L1178, undated]
- Once a cutout is saved from Photoshop, the file returns to Lightroom. [L1178, undated]

### Square canvas and image size

- Standard product image size is square at 1080 × 1080 pixels. Crop to a square with the 1:1 ratio and save at this size for consistency across listings. [L1199, undated]
- Non-square product images create uneven gaps in the image layout. Crop all product images to the same square ratio before uploading. [L1331, 2025-26]
- Set up a square canvas in Photoshop: [L1132, undated]
  1. Hold the Selection tool icon in the toolbar to reveal the Artboard tool.
  2. Draw a square artboard and resize it to the size you need.
  3. Fit the product photo inside it so the subject sits roughly centred.
- Draw the square artboard with the Artboard tool, hidden under the selection tool group, then scale it with Ctrl+T while holding Shift to keep the ratio. [L1178, undated]

### Background removal in Photoshop: full workflows

- Cutout workflow, Photoshop (eight steps): [L1178, undated]
  1. Draw a square artboard with the Artboard tool (hidden under the selection tool group), then scale it with Ctrl+T while holding Shift to keep the ratio.
  2. Click Select Subject in the top bar and check the result.
  3. Add missing areas with the brush (plus) or remove extra areas with the brush held in minus mode.
  4. Deselect with Ctrl+D and refine openings and edges by brush or lasso.
  5. Add a layer mask with Select and Mask, using the left toolbar to clean up the cutout first.
  6. Smooth, feather and Shift Edge in the right-side panel last.
  7. Click OK with Output To set to Layer Mask, so the original photo is kept.
  8. Retouch spots with Spot Healing Brush and Clone Stamp, and save with Ctrl+S so the file returns to Lightroom.
- Cutout procedure, Photoshop (six steps): [L1132, undated]
  1. Click Select Subject in the top menu bar.
  2. Check the result, and add or subtract missing areas with the Quick Selection tool (plus sign to add, hold Shift for minus, or Alt for minus in the lasso).
  3. Open Select and Mask.
  4. Refine edges with the Refine Edge brush for hair and the brush for gaps.
  5. Use Smooth, Feather, Contrast and Shift Edge in the right-hand panel.
  6. Under Output To choose Layer Mask (not Selection, which would delete the background) and click OK.
- Cutout checklist, Photoshop (eight steps): [L1132, undated]
  1. Cut out the subject (Select Subject, then refine).
  2. Refine edges with the brush, lasso, and Refine Edge for hair.
  3. Smooth the edge subtly.
  4. Feather only if the background contrast is low.
  5. Apply Shift Edge (about 30 to 40) to remove dark fringes.
  6. Output as Layer Mask.
  7. Remove spots with Spot Healing Brush, and blemishes or stitching with Clone Stamp.
  8. Add an E3E3E3 fill layer behind the subject, and resize the subject as needed.
- Select Subject, then Layer Mask: [L1199, undated]
  - Check the edges after Select Subject.
  - Add missing areas with a Layer Mask brush set to hard brush hardness (100%).
  - Use the Polygonal Lasso tool for straight edges.
  - Use Select and Mask to smooth edges and shift the edge inward slightly, then click Layer Mask.
- If a selection is active when you click Add Layer Mask, the mask is built from that selection and the background is hidden automatically. Use this right after Select Subject for a non-destructive cutout in one step. [L1178, undated]
- Use a layer mask rather than deleting pixels. The mask is non-destructive: the original is preserved, the mask can be deleted to restore it, and it can be edited later by clicking it. [L1132, undated; L1178, undated]
- A layer-mask cutout can be reversed or reworked. Drag the mask onto the trash icon and confirm to restore the original photo. Or click the mask thumbnail in the Layers panel to reopen Select and Mask. In Select and Mask, use the brush in minus mode to remove leftover background, and check the result before clicking OK. [L1178, undated]
- Always edit through layers rather than painting directly on the photo. Adjustment layers and layer masks keep the original intact. [L1178, undated]

### Select and Mask settings

| Setting | Typical value | When it applies | Ids |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shift Edge | About 30 to 40 (pixels) for product cutouts | Product cutouts on a light background; move left to pull in dark or coloured halos, right to expand; check each step at zoom | [L1132, undated; L1178, undated; L1199, undated] |
| Smooth | Small value | Keep subtle; too much pulls corners inward | [L1132, undated; L1178, undated] |
| Feather | Hardly used | White-background shoots, where dark and white contrast is already crisp; use feather only when background contrast is low | [L1132, undated; L1178, undated] |
| Contrast | Rarely changed | Only needed for low-contrast backgrounds | [L1132, undated; L1178, undated] |

- Shift Edge removes dark or coloured halo pixels left around a cutout. Shifting inward removes the fringe; shifting outward expands the selection. Do rough selection work first with the left toolbar, then smooth and feather in the right-hand panel. [L1132, undated]
- Brush controls: hold Alt, right-click and hold, then move left or right to change brush size and up or down to change feather. Ctrl+D deselects. Alt with the mouse wheel zooms in and out. [L1132, undated]
- Lasso or polygon selections create pixelated, jagged edges. Smooth them only after all cutout edits are finished, and keep edge refinement subtle so the outline does not look like a polygon at normal size. [L1132, undated]
- If a cutout is a bit off, refine it with the brush or lasso, but keep it close to the natural outline. Parts of clothing that look odd when cut out, such as jeans at the leg, can stay if they look correct in the final image. [L1178, undated]

### Halos, edge fixes and colour differences

- A thin dark or blue halo after cutout comes from the original background colour. Fix it with Shift Edge inward. Then remove remaining colour tint with a Hue/Saturation adjustment layer that reduces saturation on the affected area. [L1178, undated]
- For minor colour differences between garments and the background, use a Hue/Saturation adjustment layer clipped over only the affected area to reduce colour cast. For small differences it is often not worth the extra layering. [L1132, undated]
- Resize a cutout subject inside its frame: [L1132, undated; L1178, undated]
  1. Select the subject's layer in the Layers panel.
  2. Press Ctrl+T to open free transform.
  3. Hold Shift to keep the aspect ratio, and hold Alt to scale outward from the centre.
  4. Drag to the target size, then press Enter to confirm.

### Retouching in Photoshop

- Spot Healing Brush removes small spots, hair and blemishes. Use short sharp clicks rather than dragging, keep the brush small, and use some feather so the repair blends in. Dragging across high-contrast areas can smear the repair. [L1132, undated]
- Clone Stamp: hold Alt and click to sample a clean source area, release Alt, then paint over the defect. Clone from an area with matching light direction, because lighting changes make the copy look too light or too dark. Use it for stitching or larger blemishes the Spot Healing Brush cannot fix. [L1132, undated]
- Remove overlaid text or a white background from a lifestyle shot: [L1132, undated]
  1. Cut out only the subject (for example an arm) with Select Subject and refine it in Select and Mask.
  2. Add a layer mask so the subject is isolated without deleting pixels.
  3. Add a grey fill layer (Edit > Fill, Colour E3E3E3) behind the subject so the overlaid text and white areas disappear.
- When a photo has text or other objects on it, cut out only the usable part of the subject and place it on the plain grey background, so the text disappears. [L1178, undated]
