### Repurposing winning creatives

**Same-platform and cross-platform reuse**

- Repurposing on the same platform can test a different angle on a previous video, such as a new hook, a new voice or a new call to action. [L0795, undated]
- Ways to build more videos from a winning video:
  1. Make variants that change the hook. [L0795, undated]
  2. Shorten the duration without cutting key points, for example from 15 seconds to 11 seconds. [L0795, undated]
  3. Recreate the same video with a different product variant, such as a red version after a blue one went viral. [L0795, undated]
- Turning a proven organic video into a paid ad: take the top organic videos that performed well, keep the core content, and make small adjustments to fit the ad platform. Add a call to action and scarcity. [L0820, undated]
- Hook-led rebuild of a winning ad:
  1. Record a set of scenes covering the hook, the use case, the relief and the result, about 12 scenes in total. [L0871, 2021+]
  2. Find the hook that is running longest and keep the best-performing clip for it. [L0871, 2021+]
  3. Build new ads by pairing the winning hook with different body clips. [L0871, 2021+]
  4. Keep the voice and action the same and change only the camera angle or opening clip for each new version. [L0871, 2021+]
  5. Track how long each ad runs and keep the ones that run past 60 days. [L0871, 2021+]
- Reuse user-generated video by recutting the footage, changing the narrative, cropping out unwanted text, and adding simple transitions. Basic editing skills are enough. [L1426, undated]
- Use one creative for different audiences by changing only the caption, for example one caption for a girlfriend, another for a boyfriend, and another for family members. [L1469, 2023+]
- Recycle good product visuals by reposting them every couple of months on social media. This keeps accounts active without needing new material each time (source undated). [L0921, 2022]
- Save finished ad assets such as images and videos so you can reuse them across campaigns and change only the text. Recognizable creative helps shoppers identify the event quickly. [L0799, 2021-22]

### Seasonal planning

- Check seasonal buying patterns before planning creatives. Determine whether customers buy your product only in certain seasons or all year round. The answer determines when ads and creative themes should run. [L1267, 2026]
- A seasonal angle lets a seasonal product sell where the weather is not yet right. A pre-summer sale, for example, lets summer dresses sell in countries where the weather is not yet warm. [L1171, 2024+]
- For a holiday, start organic testing early. For example, begin on January 1 for Valentine's Day. Test many different hooks before the peak period begins. [L1469, 2023+]
- Holiday remarketing: use a gift-related image, such as gift boxes, with the current offer printed on the image. The link should go to the holiday collection page. [L0467, 2019]
- Stop running Christmas ads from mid-December, because products ordered then will not arrive before Christmas and customers will be disappointed. [L0780, 2024+]
- Reuse Black Friday ads that still perform by changing the headline and ad copy and launching them as a new Christmas ad. Test new ads only if they stop performing. [L1225, 2025]

### Peak CPM seasons and Black Friday / Cyber Monday

**Managing peak cost**

- During BFCM, when CPM is high, do not create many new creatives. Scale existing winning creatives by adding offer banners and testing those variations. [L0298, 2023]
- Reducing ad cost during peak CPM seasons:
  1. Test better creatives, with new ones in parallel at small budgets. [L0298, 2023]
  2. Add offer banners to existing winning creatives, for example "Early BFCM Sale" or "Halloween Sale Ongoing". [L0298, 2023]
  3. Banners can be static images, animations, or continuous slideshows. [L0298, 2023]
  4. Focus scaling spend on banner-enhanced versions of proven creatives, not on building entirely new creative. [L0298, 2023]
- For Black Friday, test one or two dedicated creatives that clearly show the offer, for example with a banner, rather than producing complex productions. Keep the message simple: the offer itself is usually enough. [L0692, 2023]
- Use dedicated Black Friday creatives in these formats:
  - Square image ad: black background with a sales banner, three product images below, headline "Black Friday sale is now on", and text "Get up to 50% off, get yours now". Stories ad: the 9×16 portrait version of the same. [L0308, 2023]
  - Overlay method on proven video creatives:
    1. Take existing winning video creatives. [L0308, 2023]
    2. Import them into Canva. [L0308, 2023]
    3. Resize to 4:5 ratio (1350×1920px). [L0308, 2023]
    4. Add a black banner at the top: "Black Friday event now on". [L0308, 2023]
    5. Add a black banner at the bottom: "Get up to 50% off". [L0308, 2023]
    6. Export and run as a video ad. This overlay method works extremely well on proven winning creatives. [L0308, 2023]
  - Note: 1350×1920 px is a ratio of about 0.70, not 4:5 (which is 0.80, for example 1080×1350). Check the export size before upload. [L0308, 2023]
- Black Friday approach used in the course example: take the best-performing existing creatives, add a Black Friday sale banner or overlay with "up to 60% off" and the end date, and export in both 9:16 (Stories, Reels) and 1:1 square (Facebook feed). Videos were cut to 4:5 for the Facebook feed with a top and bottom banner. [L1363, 2023]
- Save finished BFCM assets so they can be reused across campaigns. Only the text changes, and recognizable creative helps shoppers identify the event quickly. [L0799, 2021-22]
