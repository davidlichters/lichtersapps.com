## Reading results, iterating and scaling creatives

### Read the data and the creative together

1. Review the ad performance data. [L0008, 2023]
2. Examine the creative assets: visuals, headline, body copy and hooks. [L0008, 2023]
3. List hypotheses about why performance is what it is, based on both the data and the creatives. [L0008, 2023]

Full troubleshooting order when an ad underperforms:

1. Review ad performance metrics to find the weak point in the funnel: attention, interest, desire or action. [L0067, 2023]
2. Open the ads in creative view. [L0067, 2023]
3. Watch the ad journey and identify where it could improve. [L0067, 2023]
4. Go through the creative review checklist below. [L0067, 2023]
5. List all hypotheses about why the ad is underperforming. [L0067, 2023]
6. Prioritize the most important hypothesis. [L0067, 2023]
7. Create variations: some as iterations of the working ad, some as brand-new concepts. [L0067, 2023]
8. Run the new ads and test them. [L0067, 2023]

Creative review checklist:

| Check | Source |
|---|---|
| Attention-grabbing hook that is relevant to the target audience | [L0067, 2023] |
| Clear problem statement or main benefit highlighted | [L0067, 2023] |
| Compelling offer presented | [L0067, 2023] |
| Clear call to action: tell people what to do and where to go | [L0067, 2023] |
| Benefits-focused copy, not just features | [L0067, 2023] |
| Social proof: testimonials, reviews, case studies, customer results | [L0067, 2023] |
| Scarcity or urgency: limited stock, end date | [L0067, 2023] |
| High-quality visuals that illustrate the point | [L0067, 2023] |
| Educational or entertaining content | [L0067, 2023] |
| Builds curiosity without being unclear | [L0067, 2023] |
| Problem-Agitate-Solution framework: outline the problem, make it worse, present the solution | [L0067, 2023] |

Creative review procedure:

1. Look at the ad data to find where the funnel falls. [L0640, 2023]
2. Open the creative and identify which ads worked and which did not. [L0640, 2023]
3. Check the middle section of the ad for where it loses viewers. [L0640, 2023]
4. List all hypotheses for why it falls down. [L0640, 2023]
5. Pick the most important hypothesis and test it. [L0640, 2023]

Creative review after every ad set test:

1. Use the data and visual analysis to work out why each ad performed as it did. [L0652, 2023]
2. Write down why the winning ads worked and why the losing ads did not. [L0652, 2023]
3. Create three new ads that build on the analysis, iterating on the tested ads with new ideas expected to improve them. [L0652, 2023]
4. Test a whole new creative concept alongside the iterations. [L0652, 2023]

After scoring the ads, list which hook styles, message angles, formats and talent types appear among the winners and which among the losers. Turn these patterns into the direction for the next creative round. [L0613, 2026]

### Diagnose weak funnel stages from metrics

**Soft metrics point to the stage**

- Use attention rate and CTR to diagnose a creative. Low attention points to the hook. Low CTR points to the middle content. No sales point to the offer. Optimize the weakest stage rather than changing everything at once. [L0639, 2023]
- Poor hook rate with good interest and desire means the opening needs better hooks. Good hook rate with poor CTR means the middle messaging is not generating interest, and the hook may be attracting the wrong audience. Strong hook and CTR with weak sales point to the offer or page rather than the creative. [L0652, 2023]
- Read hook rate and hold rate together. A strong hook with a weak hold means the opening works but the rest of the video loses viewers. A strong hold with a weak hook means the rest of the video works and only the opening needs a rewrite. Reuse the strong part in new creatives. [L1024, undated]
- Judge creatives by consistency across several days, not one sale. A low hook rate or low CTR points to the creative's opening. A low conversion rate points to the page. [L1315, 2023]
- Judge a product's soft metrics on the same budget over several days, not on one day. A single day of poor results is not a reason to cut a product that is still in its learning phase. [L1030, 2026]
- Engagement rate ranking, quality ranking and conversion rate ranking rate an ad against other advertisers aiming at the same audience. Use them as optional diagnostics for engagement, content quality and conversion quality. [L1024, undated]
- Ad ranking order: ROAS first, then CTR, then average play time. Some ads have lower CTR but strong conversion, so ROAS takes priority when it is clearly positive. [L0398, 2023+]
- If two or more creatives have similar ROAS, use CTR to choose between them. If CTR is also similar, use average play time. Give a creative with a large CTR gap more time to collect data before deciding. [L0398, 2023+]
- Judge a creative on hook rate, click-through rate and conversion rate after launch. [L0950, 2023]

**Video retention, in detail**

- Build extra custom metrics for 25%, 50% and 75% video views, each divided by impressions. With high traffic these show exactly where viewers drop off, so you know which part to fix. [L0644, undated]
- Video length matters for hook analysis. A 15-second video that averages 8 seconds of watch time means many viewers drop out in the middle. Check this alongside the hook rate. [L0950, 2023]
- Hook-first diagnostic:
  1. Test many hooks for the first seconds of the video. [L1426, undated]
  2. Check the video attention rate. If it is low, keep testing hooks. [L1426, undated]
  3. Check the click-through rate. If it is low, improve the middle of the ad: story, offer timing, engagement. [L1426, undated]
  4. Check add-to-carts and purchases to confirm the funnel works. [L1426, undated]
  5. Keep the hook messaging consistent with the landing page copy. [L1426, undated]
- Test better 3-second hooks to raise CTR and lower CPC. Weak CPC, CPM and CTR usually point to the creative, so the first seconds must capture attention and the ad must look high quality. [L0874, 2026]

**The four numbers, read together**

1. Check the video attention rate to judge how well the opening hook holds viewers. [L0644, undated]
2. Check the click-through rate to judge whether the middle and offer make people click. [L0644, undated]
3. Check content views and add-to-carts as extra signals, since Facebook may not record every event. [L0644, undated]
4. Check purchases as the final result. Read the four numbers together, not one alone. [L0644, undated]

| Symptom | Likely cause | Action | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low video attention | Weak opening seconds | Test several new hooks in front of the same video body | [L0644, undated] |
| Good attention, low CTR | Weak middle, offer shown too late, or irrelevant viewers | Strengthen the middle, show the offer sooner, make the message more specific | [L0644, undated] |
| Content views but few add-to-carts | Landing page does not match the ad | Fix relevance and copy on the landing page | [L0644, undated] |
| Add-to-carts but no purchases | Checkout problem | Test the checkout, then rerun the original ad to compare | [L0644, undated] |

**Functional problems come before creative**

- Troubleshooting order by funnel stage:
  1. Check the funnel stage with the biggest drop: attention, interest, desire or action. [L0640, 2023]
  2. For weak desire or action with good attention and interest, check functional problems first: broken checkout, wrong currencies, shipping charged by mistake, missing add-to-cart button, wrong pricing display. [L0640, 2023]
  3. Then check price shock, poor product page, lack of trust, an unclear ad that misleads, and whether the offer is good enough. [L0640, 2023]
  4. For poor attention with good interest and desire, test better hooks aimed at the right audience. [L0640, 2023]
  5. For good attention with poor interest, test better messaging and the middle section of the ad. [L0640, 2023]
- Clicks and add-to-carts but no sales: check the checkout first. Then rerun the original ad to find out whether the ad or the checkout caused the problem. Either sales return because the checkout is fixed, or the test shows the ad was less targeted than the current one. [L0644, undated]
- Zero add-to-carts after a large spend may mean customers do not care about the product, which is the real reason. Creative changes alone rarely fix a product that generates no add-to-carts. [L0361, 2024]
- Make sure the creative, meaning the video and product pictures, is strong before adjusting the offer. While CTR is good, focus on making the offer convert. [L0549, undated]
- A strong creative and a strong offer allow more spend per customer acquisition, because more qualified people enter the funnel and average order value rises through upsells and cross-sells. Scaling then depends on the quality of the funnel rather than on tricks. [L0646, 2023]
- A high view count from an organic video does not guarantee sales. A viral toy-type product with 1.8 million views produced only 2 orders in the observed period, so check organic reach against real order numbers. [L0655, undated]
- Comments such as "I'll buy because you're honest" are not reliable purchase signals. Place a real test order and check the confirmation or order number to verify whether the audience converts. [L0655, undated]
- Use CTR as a sign of interest in the product. If no one clicks, the product is not appealing enough. Keep the ad running only if CTR holds up over the test budget. [L0661, undated]
- Judge advertising only by whether it makes people act: ordering, requesting information or paying. An ad that people merely admire and forget has failed its purpose. [L0340, undated]
- The purpose of a Facebook ad is to drive traffic to the store. The store must then convert visitors into customers, so the website offer deserves improvement if ads are performing. [L1212, undated]
- Measure readership by how many people actually travel through the full copy, not by circulation or impressions. Only readers who go through the copy can buy, so an ad with a large audience that reads little is weak. [L1366, undated]
- Plan for delayed responses. Some readers clip an ad and act months later when the need appears, so a good ad can produce sales long after it stops running. [L1366, undated]
- Formula: readers x response rate = responses. Example: if 50 percent of 100 tear-sheet recipients read it and 10 percent of readers respond, you get 5 responses per 100 pieces. A standard letter that starts at 10 percent reading would need 50 percent of its readers to respond to match that. [L1370, undated]

### Benchmarks and thresholds

Several sources give different values for the same metric. Each value is listed with the condition it applies to.

| Metric | Value | When it applies | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| CTR | Below 0.7% | Replace the creative, because the video is not stopping viewers | [L1377, 2022+] |
| CTR | Below 1% | Creative is failing; fix the hook or creative and iterate once | [L0398, 2023+] |
| CTR | About 1% or higher | Creative works and the funnel can keep running | [L1377, 2022+] |
| Hook rate | 25%+ okay, 30s good, 40s excellent ('wicked'); rule of thumb | Video ads on Facebook, read with CTR and purchases; details and source conflicts in 09-meta-and-facebook-ads/26 | [L1426, undated, 12:54; L0067, 2023: 30%+ / 40% great / 50%+ excellent; L1215, 2023+: 45-60% good] |
| CTR | 1 to 1.5% | Workable only with a cheap CPM and CPC; otherwise it means paying more per visitor and fewer conversions. Below this range the creative has clear room for improvement | [L1177, 2023] |
| CTR | 1.5% (minimum acceptable, Facebook) | General floor | [L1177, 2023] |
| CTR | 2% or more | Excellent | [L1177, 2023] |
| CTR | Above 2% | Keep iterating if the landing page can be improved | [L0640, 2023] |
| CTR | About 3% | Judged strong on a winning creative | [L1177, 2023] |
| CPC | Below $1.5 | Good | [L0398, 2023+] |
| CPC | Below $1.4 to $1.5 (US) | Acceptable. The source notes costs are rising, so $1.5 is the current industry benchmark | [L1177, 2023] |
| CPC | Above $1, combined with CPM above $10 and CTR below 1.5% | Ads are not attracting interest; test new, more engaging creatives rather than touching the store | [L1375, undated] |
| CPM | Above about $30 (sometimes about $35) | Suggests a competitive audience or weak creative that forces people to see the ad | [L0398, 2023+] |
| CPM | Above $10, combined with CPC above $1 and CTR below 1.5% | Creative problem, see above | [L1375, undated] |

- Rough diagnostic: a creative with a CTR of 1 percent and a cost per link click of $1.52 was judged weak. A CTR of about 3 percent on a winning creative was judged strong. [L1177, 2023]
- Low CTR with cheap CPM and CPC can stay profitable. A CTR of 1 to 1.5 percent must be paired with cheap CPM and CPC to remain profitable. [L1177, 2023]
- If CTR is above 1% but CPC or CPM is high, simplify the campaign structure. Reduce the number of ad groups per campaign, for example from 4-5 down to fewer, because more complexity tends to increase CPM. [L0363, 2022+]
- If CTR is poor on day 1, the ads will not improve on day 2 or 3. Test new creatives. A bad first-day CTR is a signal to change the creative, not to wait. [L0363, 2022+]
- Use the click-through rate of an ad as a sign of interest. Keep the ad running only if CTR holds up over the test budget. [L0661, undated]
- Diagnose a campaign that is not converting, in this order:
  1. Check CTR first. If CTR is below 1%, the creative is failing; fix the hook or creative and iterate once. [L0398, 2023+]
  2. Check CPC next. A CPC below $1.5 is good. [L0398, 2023+]
  3. Check CPM. A CPM above about $30 (sometimes about $35) suggests a competitive audience or weak creative that forces people to see the ad. [L0398, 2023+]
  4. If CTR is good but CPM is high, rework the creative anyway, because Meta penalizes weak ads with higher costs. [L0398, 2023+]
  5. If the creative still fails after one iteration, switch to the next product instead of pushing more budget into it. [L0398, 2023+]
- Peak-CPM fix for high CPM: improve creative quality and engagement ranking. Add social proof by manually posting customer reviews and comments on ads, such as 5-star reviews and user photos. Test different interest targeting to find less-competitive audiences. Let engagement such as likes and comments accumulate naturally. CPM typically drops from 35-40 to 20-23 within 3-4 days as engagement builds. [L0298, 2023]
- Creatives are the main deciding factor once a product is performing. The coach's estimate is around 90-95% of results. Test more creatives when the low-performing metric is CTR or cost-per-click is high, since better creatives lift conversion across the funnel. [L0363, 2022+]
- For the first 1-2 weeks, normal creatives are enough to see whether a product sells. Better creatives are needed once you want to scale the product further. [L0363, 2022+]

### Kill, keep and wait decisions

- Kill criteria: stop an ad or product if you have spent at least the break-even cost per purchase and the metrics (sales, add-to-cart, attention, CTR) show no promise. Keep iterating if attention is above 40% or CTR is above 2% and the landing page can be improved. [L0640, 2023]
- Once a product fails after one creative iteration, switch to the next product rather than adding budget. [L0398, 2023+]
- Do not switch off an ad that makes revenue only because its CTR is low, if it at least breaks even. Improve the middle section of the ad to raise CTR while keeping sales coming in. [L0644, undated]
- Keep a winning ad running as long as it delivers a good return on ad spend. Test new creatives in parallel with a new angle to prevent ad fatigue. [L0519, 2020+]
- Turning off campaigns too soon, before enough frequency, is a common mistake. Mere exposure effect requires about 21 impressions before a brand is remembered. Do not turn off ads before they have built the repeated exposure needed to make an impression. [L0196, 2020+]
- Stay on message repeatedly and consistently. Use retargeting with increased frequency on individual ads, and do not turn them off too soon. Be the familiar barista your audience knows and prefers. [L0196, 2020+]
- Facebook ads with near-zero sales: check reach in Facebook Business Manager reporting. If the audience is small, the sample is too small to judge. If the audience is large and sees ads but does not buy, test different ad copy. [L0665, 2022]
- Pinterest and other secondary platforms: judge by return on ad spend, not just cost per click. If traffic is high and sales are low, the page or offer needs work. If traffic is low and sales are still low, the account has a bigger structural problem. [L0665, 2022]

### Iteration loops

- Test new creatives in an iterative loop: take an ad that performed well earlier, make variations, relaunch, and keep the ones that hold their results. [L0950, 2023]
- A winning ad keeps getting sales because it sends the right message to the right audience. Keep testing new creatives and iterate on the ones that previously worked, rather than assuming the cause of a performance drop is known. [L0950, 2023]
- Build a new test set after a creative review:
  1. Create three new ads based on the analysis. [L0640, 2023]
  2. Make some of them iterations of the best-performing ad. [L0640, 2023]
  3. Make the rest brand-new concepts. [L0640, 2023]
- Creative testing is central to scaling. A good message and creative attract the right audience, and small spend on each ad helps identify winners quickly. Small, controlled tweaks to a creative can improve results and should not be skipped because they feel dull. [L0646, 2023]
- Set up new creatives in the existing testing campaign. When relaunching a product with new ad copy, copy your best-performing ads into the existing testing campaign and run the new copy variations on them. Do not start a fresh campaign for each copy change. [L1215, 2023+]
- Loop for turning a winning message into new concepts:
  1. In Ads Manager, find the creative with the highest website purchases in your CBO. [L1195, 2026]
  2. In Google AI Studio (Gemini), upload the winning video and transcribe its messaging, plus the primary text and headline from the ad. [L1195, 2026]
  3. In Claude, hand over the transcript and rebuild the messaging: the angle, the promise, and the objection it handles. [L1195, 2026]
  4. Turn the winning message into new concepts in different formats, such as podcast, conference, seminar, claymation, cartoon, or UGC AI ads. [L1195, 2026]
  5. Launch each concept for 3 days in CBO 2 or CBO 3, then read website purchases and ROAS to see whether it resonates. [L1195, 2026]
  6. Fold the winners back into the main CBO and repeat the loop. [L1195, 2026]
- Process for a new product on a proven layout:
  1. Keep a file of ad creatives that have produced sales. [L0950, 2023]
  2. For a new product, replace only the product imagery in a proven ad layout instead of building new creatives from scratch. [L0950, 2023]
  3. Write or check the core benefits and the offer in the copy yourself before launch, rather than handing this to a freelancer. [L0950, 2023]
  4. Launch the ad and judge it by hook rate, click-through rate and conversion rate. [L0950, 2023]
- Case (informational test): a test used a knee anatomy image. The first output was a 3D render. A follow-up asked for two cross-sectional drawings instead, one profile view facing left and one front-facing view. The second version was closer to the target because it looked like a textbook illustration. [L0882, 2025-26]
- Pattern to watch: a creative is a likely winner only when it has the best CTR consistently across all winning audiences, not just in one. Replace the older creative with it once that pattern is confirmed. [L0648, undated]
- Before scaling an audience, build a winning combination of the audience and two of the best creatives, selected for strong CTR and CPM. [L0646, 2023]

### Creative fatigue and rotation

- Ads on a single audience can lose performance over time, so new creatives keep the reach alive. Test alongside the winner rather than as a replacement for it. [L0519, 2020+]
- Advertising styles wear out. Verse, narrative confessions and comic-strip stories each lost believability as they became familiar. Keep the strategy and rotate the style when it grows stale. [L0330, undated]
- Ad creatives need regular refreshing because customers get used to the same ads. Produce new photos and videos as the previous ones lose effect. [L1439, 2024+]
- TikTok account-level fatigue: the same material can stop an entire account from performing after roughly three to four weeks. Pausing the account for about a week and then restarting was reported to restore reach. Rotate new content in before results decline. [L0819, 2020+]
- TikTok single-creative lifespan: a creative rarely holds more than about a week. Copied creatives often perform poorly after a few days, sometimes within one day in the US. [L1177, 2023]
- The two TikTok values in [L0819, 2020+] and [L1177, 2023] describe different scopes: one creative versus the whole account. Both apply.
- Pinterest creatives saturate much more slowly than TikTok creatives. A video can run for six to seven months and older videos still work, so TikTok winners can be reused directly there. [L1135, undated]
- Video age in paid tests: a video that is 2 to 3 months old has usually been seen by many people in the target market. This tends to lower CTR and raise CPM. Prefer creatives posted within about the last two weeks, and treat older videos as a weaker option for paid tests. [L1377, 2022+]
- Viral video reuse: a creative running for more than about 15 to 30 days after going viral may already be known to TikTok's algorithm, so reuse gives less reach. A video with views in the 20,000 to 30,000 range three days after posting may still have room to scale. [L1382, undated]
- Run length as a performance sign: ads that ran for 68 and 79 days were winners, so keep them and reuse their hooks and structure. [L0871, 2021+]

### Scaling winners

- Scale by combining winners. Find the winning hook, video, concept, thumbnail and ad copy through the separate tests, then combine the best assets in the scaling campaigns. [L0902, undated]
- Only shift the best-performing ads into one ad set and let Facebook optimise once the separate tests have shown which ads and countries work. [L1426, undated]
- Prepare multiple creatives in advance when scaling, so you can quickly test whether a new video or picture beats the current winner. [L1212, undated]
- Scaling through new creatives on TikTok: create new creatives every week, even if older ones stop working. Duplicate a working ad set and put in four new creatives each week to double its effective budget without raising the budget. [L1374, undated]
- Once a product shows steady sales for one to two weeks, switch to UGC or TikTok-style creative as soon as possible, since this can multiply results. Start UGC production in parallel with the first winning ads. [L0398, 2023+]
- Use a winning ad's CTR, CPM and ROAS, not impressions, as the basis for scaling decisions. [L0646, 2023; L0648, undated]
- Judge a creative in the scaling phase by its ROAS first, then CTR. [L0398, 2023+]
