### Pictures, art and reuse

- Use pictures only when they sell better than the same space filled with text, such as showing the product itself or people the buyer admires in a setting they desire. Decorative or eccentric pictures take attention from the offer and should be avoided. [L1261, undated]
- Example: a striking picture that increased an ad's space by 50 percent raised cost per reply by the same 50 percent, with no extra sales. The added cost produced no benefit, so the picture was removed. [L1261, undated]
- Use expensive art only when tests show it earns more than cheaper art. Mail-order advertisers often get good results with simple illustrations. Art cost is small compared with the cost of space, so verify its value with a test. [L1261, undated]
- Pictures can account for one-third to one-half of an ad campaign's cost, so they need the same proof as copy. Some advertisers paid up to 2,000 dollars per drawing; that premium is justified only when a test shows expensive art beats cheaper art. [L1261, undated]
- Reuse the same proven picture across ads when possible. New customers rarely remember a picture from an earlier ad, and repetition lowers cost. Change the picture only when a test shows a new one wins. [L1261, undated]

### Canva, Snappa and workflows

**Tools**

- Snappa and Canva are the recommended tools for creating ad images in multiple sizes. Snappa is used for all ad images, including video cover images. Canva is a long-established alternative. [L1238, 2021-22]
- Canva can make ad creatives from basic color theory, balance and graphic design knowledge. Photoshop is not required for good spacing, color and clear messages. [L1348, 2023]
- Canva can make a checkout banner at 1500 x 300 px with a logo, privacy/secure icons and payment processor logos. [L1260, undated]
- Canva has a typical Facebook-style comment design, but Facebook comments are harder to recreate, so a designer may need Photoshop for the background fill and text replacement. [L1348, 2023]
- If you do not want to design retargeting images yourself, outsource to freelance graphic designers on Fiverr, Upwork or freeup.com. Choose designers with experience in display network or Facebook ad images. [L1238, 2021-22]

**Resizing one master design in Snappa**

1. Open the finished base design. It can be started in any size.
2. Click Resize in the left menu.
3. Choose a pre-made size or enter a custom graphic size in width and height in px.
4. When asked whether to override the current graphic or open a new one, choose to open it as a new graphic. Snappa carries all elements over to the new template size.
5. Move the text and other elements to fit the new shape. Increase text sizes where there is more space, and adjust spacing.
6. Resize the product image to fit the layout and place it.
7. Save the design, then Download it.
8. For the next size, open the saved design and repeat from step 2. All elements carry across.

[L1169, 2021-22]

- Most static display sizes share similar shapes, so after the first design, resizing takes little time. The tall filmstrip format needed the most re-spacing and took a couple of minutes. Design one master layout and resize it to save the most time. [L1169, 2021-22]

**Building a retargeting image set**

1. Create one image for the new retargeting campaign.
2. Produce that image in every required size for each network (Google static, Google responsive, Facebook, Twitter, Taboola).
3. Upload the sized versions to the networks.

The goal is to load one image across all networks without redesigning it each time. [L1238, 2021-22]

**Rebranding a landing page template for a new niche with Canva**

1. Open the product image in the browser (right click, Inspect) and copy the image URL. Remove the surrounding size and parameter text so the full-size image is loaded.
2. Paste the image into Canva. Remove unneeded shadows and reflections.
3. Pick fonts that fit the brand: niche-specific fonts (e.g. Font Space for themed fonts, checked for commercial use) or Google Fonts for readability.
4. Upload font files to Canva. A zip containing the font files can be uploaded.
5. Take colours from the product image using Canva's colour suggestions. Use a dark background for a sophisticated look.
6. Keep text short and well-spaced. Avoid squeezed lines and cramped line heights.
7. Export the three main graphics as PNG and compress them before upload. The lesson's examples were about 900 KB for two images.
8. Upload the images to Shopify, then in PageFly choose the fonts and apply them to headings.

### Banners and display ads

- On Facebook, text covering the image may not exceed 20% of the image area. Text on Facebook images is optional, but can help text stand out when used. [L1238, 2021-22]
- To check the 20% limit, lay a grid overlay over the image in any image tool. [L1238, 2021-22]
- A retargeting image has one job: stop the scroll on social media. It can do this by showing something the viewer has seen before, such as branding or a known product picture, or by standing out visually, for example with contrasting colors or something interesting. [L1238, 2021-22]

### Store graphics and hero images

- A desktop hero image should be about 1600 px wide and 400 to 600 px tall. A taller hero image pushes the rest of the homepage below the fold, and the customer may never see it. [L1310, 2021-22]
- A checkout banner at 1500 x 300 px can carry the logo, privacy/secure icons and payment processor logos. [L1260, undated]
- Landing page graphics: export as PNG and compress before upload, then check the fonts in PageFly under the procedure above. [L1363, 2023]

### Logo, profile and cover images

- The profile photo is a square 1:1 portrait, direct to camera, warm and friendly. It must not look like a corporate headshot, stock-photo perfection or heavy polish. [L1475, 2026]
- Never use a logo or a product shot as the profile picture. [L1475, 2026]
- The cover photo uses a 3:1 aspect ratio and shows a warm, aspirational, editorial lifestyle scene from the character's world, such as a desk setup, gym or outdoor space. It may show the character or the scene alone. [L1475, 2026]
- Profile and cover photos must contain no product shots, logos or text overlays. [L1475, 2026; L1476, 2026]
- Putting a product shot or a discount graphic on the cover or profile photo immediately signals a brand and undermines the character. Keep those images strictly lifestyle and personal. [L1475, 2026]
- Whitelist character page images use two formats: a square 1:1 direct-to-camera profile portrait, and a wide 3:1 lifestyle scene for the cover. [L1476, 2026]

### Letterheads and printed samples

- A picture on the letterhead must add essential background to the offer, such as the setting of a product's use, because decorative pictures alone may not help. Test a plain letterhead against a pictorial one before you commit. [L1369, undated]
- A colored nature scene printed across the top and one side of a letterhead doubled the orders for a book series offer compared with a plain letterhead. [L1369, undated]
- A small full-color reproduction of the product in the upper left corner of the letterhead added nearly 50 percent to the pulling power of one letter. [L1369, undated]
- When a business was built around one person, a photograph of that person on the letterhead added 10 to 20 percent to the pulling power of the letters. [L1369, undated]
- Tip a small sample of the product onto the letterhead, such as a fabric swatch for a coat or a leather swatch for luggage, so the reader can judge quality. A tactile sample is an effective attention-getter when the product has a visible quality. [L1369, undated]

### Video thumbnails

- Thumbnails must be attractive and clearly show what the video will deliver, because YouTube rewards a higher click-through rate, as it does on other Google properties. [L1511, 2021-22]
- Preset YouTube sizes: Thumbnail 1280 x 720 px, End Screen 1920 x 1080 px, Channel Art 2560 x 1440 px. [L1169, 2021-22]

### Text legibility on mobile

- Put overlay text near the bottom of the frame and shrink it if it is too large, so the main action and the product stay visible. [L1427, 2023+]
- Check the layout on a phone-sized preview before exporting. [L1427, 2023+]
- Keep body type normal and readable, not oversized. Larger type adds cost without adding response. [L1261, undated]
