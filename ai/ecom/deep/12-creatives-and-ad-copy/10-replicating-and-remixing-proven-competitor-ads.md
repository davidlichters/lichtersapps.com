## Replicating and remixing proven competitor ads

### Core rule: copy the structure, never the words

- Do not copy competitor ad creatives straight from the Meta Ad Library without changes. The competitor's established account history gets preference from Meta, so direct copies do not generate equal reach. [L0037, 2026]
- When you use a competitor creative as a reference, edit at least one element: the hook, the body copy, or something else that differentiates your version. [L0037, 2026]
- Imitation means learning from structure and form, not copying word for word. The line between plagiarism and genius is fine, and imitation is a logical learning step before innovation. Great poets imitate and improve; small poets steal and spoil. [L0196, 2020+]
- Competitor ads are the best starting point for your own creative. If they cannot be used, build your own ad from competitor content and improve it rather than copying it. [L0693, 2024]
- A competitor ad works best as a structure to follow, not a look to copy. Reuse the arrangement of headline, product and offer, and take colours, fonts and brand identity from your own brand. This keeps the test about the structure and avoids using another brand's identity. [L0794, 2026]
- Look for patterns across several videos, such as a hook or ad copy used repeatedly, and replicate the pattern with your own footage. Do not copy a brand's custom content. For drop-shipping style videos, produce a better version or rearrange existing clips. [L0639, 2023]
- Use a competitor's idea as a starting point only if you test it yourself. Keep it only if click and result numbers reach your targets; otherwise change the creative or the offer. [L1219, undated]
- Copying other dropshippers' creatives is acceptable at the very beginning. In the long run, your own creatives perform better. Ordering the product and filming your own material usually beats reusing other ads. [L1216, undated]
- Use a proven line from your Structure Library's Proven Line-Patterns as an example of a psychological move, not as a phrase to repeat. Default to reproducing the move with fresh words. Use a proven line verbatim only if it is genuinely the strongest option, never to avoid the work of writing a fresh version. [L0039, 2026]
- Competitor creatives can serve as inspiration. Take their images or GIFs, edit them, and improve headlines and angles with editing tools rather than using them unchanged. The aim is a clearly better version than the competitor's ad or landing page. [L0708, undated]
- Recreate a viral competitor video by improving its hook or other weak parts, not by copying it. [L0795, undated]
- Repurpose existing competitor footage by cutting out their text and logos, changing the brand colours and adding a new narrative. The goal is a different story built on the same footage, not a copy of the original ad. [L0644, undated]
- Exact copy-paste is risky. The platform can detect it, and the competitor may get the original ad taken down, which takes your ad down too. Always alter the metadata, logo and details of any replicated creative. [L0398, 2023+]
- Copying a competitor's ads and store exactly can produce high CPM and poor CTR. Change elements such as music, speed, length and hook so Meta does not treat them as duplicates of another advertiser's content. [L1316, 2024+]
- Instead of reusing a brand's ad video, copy its hook or structure and source your own clips from AliExpress, Alibaba or YouTube. This keeps the selling pattern, avoids the brand's content and reduces copyright risk. [L1218, undated]

### Sourcing proven creatives

**Where to look and what to save**

- Ripped creatives are videos that already perform for other advertisers. Find them on Kalodata, the TikTok Creative Center or the Facebook Ad Library. Their audience psychology is already proven, which makes them a safe first test. [L1252, 2026]
- Ad libraries show what creative types work, including posts that go viral. Identify which trends, post types and ad types work, then recreate the winning approach in your own style without copying it. [L1425, undated]
- Pool-building: Kalodata lists more than 4,000 video and ad creatives for one product. Use that pool to pull hooks and testimonials and build many variations, not one or two new ads. [L0901, 2026]
- Content from other products in the same category can serve your product. Eyeliner ads, for example, can supply hooks and testimonials for another eyeliner. This widens your pool well beyond one product's ads. [L0901, 2026]
- Do not run the same top-performing competitor ads that many advertisers already show on Facebook. The audience has probably seen them. Bring content the market has not seen yet, even when the product is the same. [L0901, 2026]
- Choose only videos that feature real people. Abstract videos and product-only videos are excluded. [L0704, 2026]

**Filtering and quantities**

| Source | Filter before saving | Quantity per product | Source ids |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kalodata | High CTR, high spend or proven conversions | At least 20 top videos, whichever filter you use | [L1252, 2026] |
| TikTok Creative Center or Facebook Ad Library | High CTR, high spend or proven conversions | 3 to 5 top ads; prefer high engagement and many comments | [L1252, 2026] |
| TikTok Creative Center or Facebook Ad Library, short on time | Filtering can be skipped; go straight to the top-performing ads | 3 to 5 top ads per product | [L1252, 2026] |

- For every saved video, also save its caption, text overlays, hook and call to action. These are the basis for later comparison. [L1252, 2026]
- Download only clean versions without a watermark. A watermark shows another account's branding inside your ad. [L1252, 2026]

**Selection checks**

- Judge each candidate on two checks. The opening hook must grab attention, and the video must show a problem your product actually solves. A video can pass the hook check and still fail the second one, so both checks are needed. [L0704, 2026]
- Skip a video if the outcome it shows is not the result your product delivers, even when the hook performs well. [L0704, 2026]
- Filter for high CTR and proven conversions before you test a video in your own account. Recycling earlier ads with the same structure also saves production time. [L1252, 2026; L1398, 2021-22]

### Analysing a winning ad

**Framework for a working ad**

1. Read the hook and check whether it uses the customer's own language. [L0772, 2026]
2. Identify the awareness stage the ad targets. [L0772, 2026]
3. Identify the unique mechanism that explains what makes the product different. [L0772, 2026]
4. Note where the ad sends traffic (product page, advertorial or inverted product page) and what offer is shown. [L0772, 2026]
5. Check how long the ad has run and how many duplicates use it. [L0772, 2026]
6. Rebuild the same structure around your own product, changing the words rather than copying the text. [L0772, 2026]

**Breaking down a video**

- Break each successful competitor creative down every five seconds to identify the hook, the angle and the copywriting. Then write similar copy using the same angle. [L0693, 2024]
- Study the competitor's ad for the same product: save the videos, review what worked, and copy the structure, text-to-speech and sounds in your own way. This is often more effective than writing a script from scratch. [L1383, 2022]
- Copy the style of the largest pages or videos for your product, then change it based on what viewers say in the comments and add something dramatic or unusual. Changes that work include new trending sounds, different backdrops, switching between a male and a female presenter, trending video effects, voice-overs and challenge-style framing. [L1367, undated]

**Tool for ad analysis**

- Use Facebook Ad Library or Kalodata to find bottom-of-funnel image ads from any competitor in any niche. Screenshot the ad, upload it to Claude, and ask what makes it work and why it is effective, then have Claude create a prompt so you can produce similar ads for your niche. This reverse-engineers winning structures without copying them. [L0316, 2026]

### Prompts for analysis and recreation

**Analysis prompt for a proven rival video** (send with the video file and the exported brand data; procedure in [L0374, 2026])

```
Here is a proven rival video, sent along with its video file and the brand data I exported. Give me a complete breakdown of why it wins. Go through it frame by frame, and for each moment describe what appears on screen, what is spoken, and what effect that has on the viewer. Give special weight to the hook in the opening seconds and to the individual words and phrases doing the heaviest persuasive work, explaining per frame why each one lands. Using the supplied brand data, identify the persona, the core desire, the emotions in play, the awareness level, the concept, the angle, the USPs, and the offer behind the video. End with a brief list of what to keep and what to modify if this same structure is applied to a different product.
```

**Recreation prompt for your own product** (send with the video file, the brand data and your product files)

```
Here is the winning video from my competitor and its exported brand data. Recreate this ad script exactly as it is, keeping the same structure, the same order of scenes, the same timing, the same hook, the same emotional beats and the same length. Replace only the product, its facts, prices, ingredients and claims with those of my product, using only the facts in the attached product files and inventing no new claims. Output the full script for my product, scene by scene.
```

**Rewrite prompt for ad copy and product page text**

```
I sell [PRODUCT] in the [NICHE] niche in [MARKET]. Below is the ad copy and product page text of a brand whose ads have been running for a long time: [PASTE AD COPY AND PAGE TEXT]. Rewrite the whole text for my product and keep the same structure, hooks, sections and emotional angle. Write it for this target avatar: [TARGET AVATAR: age, gender, main problem, what they want]. Do not copy any sentence word for word. Avoid medical or guaranteed-result claims and keep all claims within what is allowed for [MARKET]. Return the output in this order: 3 hook variations for ads, 1 primary ad text, 1 headline, then the product page sections in order: hero headline and benefits, testimonials, FAQ.
```

### Ripping and editing a winning video

**Ripped-creative test (unchanged launch)**

1. Find top-performing videos on Kalodata, the TikTok Creative Center or the Facebook Ad Library, filtering by high CTR, high spend or proven conversions. [L1252, 2026]
2. Save at least 20 top videos per product if you use Kalodata, or 3 to 5 top ads per product if you use the TikTok Creative Center or the Facebook Ad Library, preferring high engagement plus comments. [L1252, 2026]
3. Save the captions, text overlays, hooks and CTAs of each video for reference. [L1252, 2026]
4. Download the clean videos without watermark using Snaptik, ssstik or Downloader Bot. [L1252, 2026]
5. Upload the videos directly to Meta Ads Manager (TikTok Ads is also possible) without any editing. [L1252, 2026]
6. Do not change the hook, caption or length; test the product and the offer. [L1252, 2026]
7. Once you get consistent profitable sales with ripped creatives, begin hook testing to raise CTR. [L1252, 2026]

- The purpose of the first launch is to test exactly what already works, so do not change the hook, the caption or the video length. [L1252, 2026]
- Testing ripped videos unchanged isolates whether the product and offer work, because the videos have already been proven with real viewers. [L1252, 2026]
- Changing the hook, caption or length of a ripped video before the first launch removes the advantage of testing a proven ad. The result no longer shows whether the original works. [L1252, 2026]
- Case: an operator sold a product using only ripped creatives, with no original content. The proven videos were downloaded without watermark and uploaded unchanged to the ad account. The reported revenue was more than 16,000 USD in a single day from ripped ads alone, which shows that ripped creatives can scale. [L1252, 2026]

**Remove identifying mentions**

- Remove every spoken or on-screen mention of TikTok Shop from a copied video, and every mention of Orange Cart, before you use it in your ads. [L0704, 2026]
- For proven videos, remove only the problematic parts. Adding new hooks or heavy edits to a video that already sells can break what works. [L0704, 2026]

**Ripping and editing a competitor's winning video (method for the Ad Library and TikTok)**

- Save the competitor's video from the Ad Library: open the ad in the Facebook Ad Library, right-click the video and save it to your computer. If the file does not play, change the format to MP4. [L1172, 2023+]
- Download the competitor's current videos, upload them to your Business Manager, and test how they perform for your product. [L1347, undated]
- Once you see results, produce your own edited version of the winning creative so it is not a copy of the competitor. [L1347, undated]
- Change the logo and the call to action at the end, or combine several videos, so the result is not identical to the original. [L1347, undated]

**Editing a winning video: step by step**

1. Find competitor ads and download the ones performing well. [L0637, 2023+]
2. Keep the first 1 to 5 seconds (the hook) unchanged. [L0637, 2023+]
3. Replace the rest of the clips, for example swap a 10 to 15 second clip with a 20 to 25 second clip. [L0637, 2023+]
4. Change the slides and add your logo and product name. [L0637, 2023+]
5. Use the result as a new creative. [L0637, 2023+]

**Adapting a competitor's TikTok ad**

- To adapt a competitor's winning TikTok ad, change the caption colour, add your own logo, replace the caption text, and adjust the speed. You can also swap or reorder clips in the middle, but keep the opening hook unchanged. [L0363, 2022+]

**Replicating a competitor ad without copying it exactly**

1. Keep the same hook and the same overall structure, because the hook is what made the ad work. [L0398, 2023+]
2. Change the logo, the metadata and some details of the creative so the ad is not an exact copy. [L0398, 2023+]
3. If the competitor logo is blurred in the centre of the video, do not reuse that video. [L0398, 2023+]
4. If the competitor logo sits to the left or right, replace it with your own logo or cover it with a coloured bar and white text. [L0398, 2023+]
5. After about 7 to 10 seconds, replace or reorder the clips, because the early hook matters most. [L0398, 2023+]
6. Keep any clip that shows the benefit and proof the competitor uses, since it may be what converts. [L0398, 2023+]
7. Add your own logo at the top of the frame. [L0398, 2023+]

- A clip that many advertisers run with the same hook usually comes from a common supplier source such as AliExpress, YouTube or Alibaba. Reusing that common clip is acceptable if you keep the same hook and change the logo, captions and later clips. [L0398, 2023+]

**Adapting a stock-footage ad to a new niche**

1. Find a matching stock clip on Pexels (for example, a person riding a bike in winter). [L1316, 2024+]
2. Download the clip and keep only the first one to two seconds as the hook. [L1316, 2024+]
3. Add the niche-specific voice-over or caption. [L1316, 2024+]
4. Edit the on-screen text with CapCut or Camtasia to match the niche (for example, "the best thermal gloves for bike riders in winter" instead of "the best thermal gloves for winter"). [L1316, 2024+]
5. Show the product after the hook. [L1316, 2024+]

**Repurposing footage from the start of a test**

- Reusing an existing video for testing saves time and budget, so you can test several products at once. Once a product shows good performance, invest in custom content for it. [L1218, undated]
- Video creative alone is only a starting point. Keep the first 3 to 5 seconds (the hook) unchanged because it usually carries the performance. Alter the rest by swapping or reordering clips, changing caption colour or placing a white layer behind existing captions, speeding up clips, and adding your own logo. If you cover the original captions, use the same caption text on top. Replace the hook with your own only when you build custom content later. [L1218, undated]

### Hook and structure remixes

**Changing only the hook**

1. Take a winning video ad. [L0693, 2024]
2. Change only the hook. [L0693, 2024]
3. Keep the rest of the video the same. [L0693, 2024]
4. Launch the variant in a new campaign with your best interests to test it. [L0693, 2024]

**Mashups of existing material**

1. Collect several ads that already run for similar products, for example a surprised-reaction clip and product b-roll. [L0347, 2026]
2. Cut the clips together into one mashup that fits the season's vibe, such as fall colours and sounds. [L0347, 2026]
3. Run the versions against each other and keep the one with the best conversion rate. [L0347, 2026]
4. Rotate new mashups when a version stops performing. [L0347, 2026]

**Hook from one ad, testimonial from another**

Case and method: a NAD+ berry drink mix was running top ads on Meta and TikTok, and the leading ad was shown with a figure of 27k. Several competitors the speaker knew personally were running the same ads, so running them again would reach a saturated audience. The seller searched Kalodata, found the same NAD+ product in drops form with a separate set of video ads, and saved a hook from one of its ads showing a benefit for looking younger. In CapCut, the seller cut the hook off after it ended, combined it with a testimonial from another ad in which an older parent looked young because of the product, and produced a new creative. The seller reported that this method produced the first winning ad from the first winning product, and Kalodata listed over 4,000 creatives per product to repeat the process. [L0901, 2026]

1. In Kalodata, set the region to United States and the period to Last 30 Days, and choose a product that is already advertised. [L0901, 2026]
2. Do not use the top-performing ad itself. Look for the same product in another form (for example drink mix versus drops) that has its own video ads. [L0901, 2026]
3. Open that product's Video & Ad list and watch the videos until you find a strong hook, meaning the opening seconds that stop the scroll. [L0901, 2026]
4. Save the video by downloading it without watermark, keeping the plan download limit in mind. [L0901, 2026]
5. Import the clip into CapCut using Stay in original location. [L0901, 2026]
6. Cut the clip right after the hook ends. Do not keep the full video. [L0901, 2026]
7. Find a testimonial clip, where a person describes a result, from another ad for the same product or from the same category. [L0901, 2026]
8. Combine the hook and the testimonial into one new creative. [L0901, 2026]
9. Repeat with other hooks and testimonials from the list, which holds thousands of creatives per product, and test the new versions. [L0901, 2026]

- Cut each hook clip at the point where the hook ends, so the saved file contains only the hook. Playing the full ad wastes the test, because the hook is the only part you take from that ad. [L0901, 2026]
- Pairing a hook from one ad with a testimonial from another creates a new creative, which is new to the audience even when the product stays the same. The combination gives a fresh test without new filming. [L0901, 2026]

**Keep the hook close to the competitor's**

- Replicate the hook of a winning competitor video closely, because the hook usually drives most of the engagement for both the competitor and the copy. [L1177, 2023]
- Keep the creative elements the same, especially the video creative and hook. Each change is an extra variable that makes it unclear what is working. Avoid changing the music, caption or hook without a test. [L1177, 2023]
- Changing the music in a copied winning ad flopped while the original music performed. An extra variable can break a creative that works for a competitor. [L1177, 2023]
- When copying a winning ad, change the hook, the first image or the first seconds so the ad is unique. The more different the creative, the better; an identical copy is the lazy option. [L1161, 2024+]
- Test a winning video approach by starting with existing high-performing videos from the market. Edit the first three seconds to find the best CTR, and only then produce unique videos or go to creators or influencers. [L0513, undated]
- A winning video follows a clear structure: a strong hook, grabbing attention, a benefit-driven storyline and a call to action. Copy a competitor video that already works and add two to three original creatives to build the test. [L0638, 2023]

**Patterning an admired ad**

- Choose a high-performing ad for a similar product and copy its structure, such as headline length, number of captions and sequence of blocks, but not its layout. Keep the format only as a guide and write new words for the new product. Do not copy the layout or wording so closely that the ad could be mistaken for the original. [L1366, undated]

**Keep the benefit and proof**

- Keep any clip that shows the benefit and proof the competitor uses, since it may be what converts. [L0398, 2023+]

### Remapping layouts and patterns

- Existing competitor footage can be repurposed by cutting out text and logos, changing the brand colours and adding a new narrative. [L0644, undated]
- Recycle a proven ad's structure: keep it, then swap in new headlines, copy and reviews to create the next version. [L1398, 2021-22] **
- Recreate the winning approach in your own style, using the same post types and ad types that work on the platform. [L1425, undated]
- Ads should look local to the target country. Show locally known stores, brands or products, because this builds trust with buyers instead of looking like a random internet ad. [L1184, 2023+]
