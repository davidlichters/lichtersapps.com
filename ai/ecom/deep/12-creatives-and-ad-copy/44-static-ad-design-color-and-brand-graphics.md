## Static ad design, color and brand graphics

### Canva setup and Brand Kit

- Canva is a beginner-friendly drag-and-drop design tool suitable for creating store graphics without design experience. [L0098, undated]
- The free plan is sufficient for building all store image assets, but transparent-background export is a paid feature (see Logo and icons). [L0098, undated]
- Canva Pro has a 30-day free trial and costs about $12 per month after the trial. It can be cancelled before billing starts. Its Magic Media, BG remover and Animate features are used to create themed ad and email images. [L1115, 2024]
- Set up the Brand Kit for consistency: go to Canva Home → Brand Kit → Brand Colors, and add 3 brand colors (black and white can still be used in addition). This speeds up design work and maintains visual consistency. [L0098, undated]
- Use a consistent set of colors and fonts across all designs for one brand. The Brand Kit stops you from restyling each design from scratch and keeps style consistent across assets. [L0754, undated]
- Canva is the tool for creating ad images and graphics. [L0238, 2019]
- Canva is a suitable tool for designing product banners and other promotional images. Photoshop also works, as long as the designer is comfortable with the tool. [L0667, 2025]

### Logo, favicon and icons

**Logo specifications (Manhattan theme):** 800×300 pixels, PNG format, transparent background preferred. [L0098, undated]

**Create a logo:**
1. Use your domain name words as the logo text.
2. Edit font, boldness, size, line spacing, and letter spacing.
3. Keep the design simple: text plus one small icon.
4. Add the icon via Elements → search → Graphics.
5. Avoid overly complex logos; keep them clean and readable. [L0098, undated]

**Transparent background (paid feature):** Transparent background export is a paid Canva feature. Free workaround: download the PNG with a white background, use a free online background remover tool, then upload the transparent PNG to Shopify. [L0098, undated]

**Favicon specifications:** 64×64 pixels, jpg or png with transparent background preferred. Shown in browser tabs. [L0098, undated]

**Create a favicon:** Open the favicon Canva template, copy and paste your logo icon into the favicon template, and download as PNG with transparent background if possible. [L0098, undated]

**Mega menu icon specifications (Manhattan theme):** 250×250 pixels, jpg or png format, up to 5 icons supported. [L0098, undated]

**Create multiple mega menu icon variations:** Open the Mega Menu Icons template, create different icon options using Add Page (examples: a letter icon like "S", a wave icon, a surfboard icon), then download all pages at once as a set. [L0098, undated]

**Icon sources and colors:**
- Canva's Elements panel has a Graphics tab for icons and illustrations and a Photos tab for stock photos. Free stock photos are not available for every niche, so basic or technical products may need images from external stock sites. [L0342, 2020-26]
- Icons for mega menus and USP sections can come from Canva's free graphics library, and some icons allow more than one color. Keep icons consistent in style and color so they look like one set. [L1045, 2020-26]
- Some free Canva graphics can be recolored in more than one color, so a single icon can use two or more brand colors without being drawn by hand. Single-color icons can only be recolored to one color. Check the color count when choosing icons for a multi-color mega menu or logo. [L0342, 2020-26]
- USP icons work best as simple single-color icons on a white or brand-colored background, with a short label. Use the brand palette so they match the rest of the store. [L1045, 2020-26]

**Pre-footer "Why Buy From Us" icons (Manhattan theme):** Less than 285×285 pixels, jpg or png with transparent background, up to 7 icons supported. Examples: Free Shipping, Secure Checkout, Customer Support, Niche/Founder-owned. [L0098, undated]

**Create pre-footer icons:**
1. Use the icon template.
2. Elements → search icons → Graphics.
3. Download as PNGs.
4. Keep icon style consistent across all icons (same visual vibe and color scheme). [L0098, undated]

### Banners and product page graphics

**Banner without text:**
1. Elements → search niche keyword → Photos.
2. Drag the image onto the canvas.
3. Right-click → Set image as background.
4. Adjust crop and position as needed. [L0098, undated]

**Banner with text:**
1. Text → add heading.
2. Optional: add shapes (e.g., a rectangle) behind the text for contrast.
3. Alternatively, use Canva templates and swap in your own images. [L0098, undated]

**Import external stock photos into Canva:**
1. Download the image from a stock photo site.
2. Drag the image into Canva.
3. Use Uploads → drag onto the design → Set as background. [L0098, undated]

**Use your own or downloaded photos:**
1. Drag the image file from your computer into the Canva window to upload it, and wait for the upload to finish, which can take a minute for large files.
2. Go to Uploads and drag the image onto the design.
3. Right-click the image and choose Replace background.
4. Canva crops the image to the size the template needs. [L1045, 2020-26]

**Product page graphic purpose:** A promotional banner on product pages for sales, urgency, or coupon codes. It can be updated or removed anytime. Create it using a template with bold brand colors. Text examples: "Huge Sale", "Save 10% with code [CODE]", and an optional expiry date. [L0098, undated]

**Collection page banner:** Use a template, set niche-relevant images as the background, and keep the design simple with no text. [L0098, undated]

**Button for a banner in Canva:**
1. Open Elements and add a square shape.
2. Resize the square to fit the button label and set its color to a brand color.
3. Add a heading with the label (for example "Shop now") and drag it over the square.
4. Select both elements, use the three-dot menu, and choose Position > Center. [L0342, 2020-26]

**Outline on text:** In Canva, select the text and open Effects to add an Outline around the letters. Test the outline color against the background before using it, because a poor color pairing can make the text harder to read. [L0342, 2020-26]

**Gradient banner in Canva:** Select the banner box, click Colors, and choose a gradient fill instead of a solid black fill, so the banner blends with the image while staying readable. [L0692, 2023]

**Promotional banner on an ad:** It must be clearly visible, so use a high-contrast color and a readable font. The banner colors should harmonize with the creative, and the font should match the product page. [L0692, 2023]

**Add a Black Friday banner to a proven winning ad:**
1. Pick an ad that already performs well.
2. Add a banner at the top with the offer text.
3. Use a box with a high-contrast color and readable font.
4. Use a gradient fill instead of plain black if the banner should blend better with the image.
5. Match the font to your product page so the customer journey looks consistent. [L0692, 2023]

**Discount badges:** They should be the most eye-catching element on a sale design, placed so the eye lands on them first. Their color should come from the brand palette, but the badge must stand out against the photo. [L0992, 2025-26]

**Monthly promotion images:** Use Canva for simple designs or hire on Fiverr. Create a few unique images for each month's promotion to use across Display, Email, and Social campaigns. Adjust the aspect ratio slightly for Discovery ads. [L0210, 2022]

**Black Friday and holiday templates:** Search Canva for "Black Friday" to find ready-made designs usable as thumbnails and ad visuals. This saves production time when running volume tests. [L0309, 2024+]

**Banners for affiliate use:** Banners created for affiliate use can serve as display network ads or on individual websites. A designer may describe their service as AdWords or AdRoll banners, but those same assets work for affiliate banner placements. [L0288, 2021-22]

### Collection images, blog images and square formats

- Select one consistent format for all collection images: either banner-style or square. Square images perform better in the homepage grid layout, and standardizing on a single approach eliminates design conflicts. [L0098, undated]
- **Create square collection images for the homepage:**
  1. Use the square template.
  2. Add 3-6 images (one per collection).
  3. Center the subject of each image so it crops well when Shopify displays it as a square. [L0098, undated]
- Keep blog image sizing consistent by always using the same Canva template for blog posts and blog previews. This ensures visual consistency across your blog. [L0098, undated]

### Layout and visual hierarchy

- Design should complement copy, bringing focus to main points and playing with flow to engage readers. Fonts, style, and design matter; aim for legibility and simplicity. Start with a simple design and slowly ramp up complexity if needed. If it feels unbalanced or off, it is. [L0196, 2020+]
- Ad graphics affect conversion rate because poorly made visuals make a store and its ads look untrustworthy. Learn core design and photography principles before launching many ads instead of rushing the launch. Canva offers free graphic design and video editing courses that help beginners. [L0561, 2023+]
- If brand weight is low, make the product benefit and offer absolutely clear in one image. Include: product visual, the offer amount, scarcity (event end date), and core benefits. Everything must be readable at a glance. [L0308, 2023]
- Eight basic design principles are universally used best practice for harmonious layouts: emphasis, contrast, white space, movement, repetition, proportion, balance and alignment. Apply them together in every creative unless a jarring effect is deliberately wanted. [L0755, undated]
- **Emphasis:** Make the most important element stand out through color, and minimise others when needed. Decide first what the viewer must see first, such as the offer, the sale tag or the buy-now button, and emphasise that. [L0755, undated]
- **Visual hierarchy:** Order the elements of the whole layout by importance, and use color, size, placement and direction to show each level. For example, a title in one color, a subtitle in a second color and body text in a third make the reading order clear. [L0755, undated]
- **Contrast** comes in four types: color contrast, size contrast, type contrast (different font styles) and shape or direction contrast. Contrast is especially important in e-commerce for sale tags, buttons and other elements that must stand out. [L0755, undated]
- Size contrast can change how equal objects look: the same circle looks smaller when surrounded by larger shapes and bigger when surrounded by smaller ones. Judge sizes in context, not in isolation. [L0755, undated]
- **Mistake:** Stuffing too much information into one design creates cognitive overload, and viewers leave without reading it. Titles pushed against the edge of a layout also make a design look like it has no breathing space. [L0755, undated]
- **Movement:** The layout should guide the eye from the most important element to the next, such as title to subheading to text to call to action. Arrange elements so this path flows without breaks. [L0755, undated]
- **Repetition:** Repeated headers, colors, button styles and navigation items signal that elements belong together and create consistency. Use the same format for similar elements across a page or campaign. [L0755, undated]
- **Proportion:** Larger elements are read as more important than smaller ones, so size is a tool for emphasis. Keep real-world sizes realistic (for example, a product next to a cup) unless oversizing is a deliberate choice. [L0755, undated]
- **Golden ratio:** A proportion system used to size and place elements so they look harmoniously related, often shown as a spiral. The exact formula is not explained in the lesson and should be verified before use. [L0755, undated]
- **Balance:** Harmony comes from distributing visual weight across the design, using a center line, diagonal or top-bottom axis. Balance can be symmetrical or asymmetrical, and a large or bold element can be offset by a group of smaller elements on the other side. [L0755, undated]
- Color can create balance in an asymmetrical layout when colors switch sides in a symmetrical way. Elements of similar weight, such as a product and its new version, can balance each other without being identical. [L0755, undated]
- **Alignment:** Lining up elements to a shared edge, such as left alignment, gives order, improves readability and lets people scan quickly. Misaligned text blocks make a design look sloppy and slow down reading. [L0755, undated]
- Even spacing, consistent alignment and symmetry make a design look balanced and professional. Use the alignment and space evenly tools rather than placing elements by eye. [L0754, undated]
- Symmetry: symmetrical layouts feel ordered and complete, but a design does not need full symmetry. Asymmetrical layouts still feel harmonious when elements of similar visual weight sit on both sides of a central line. [L0755, undated]
- **Reading order:** Western viewers scan from left to right and from top to bottom, so place the headline at the top left and the most important visual next to it. Put the offer, price and call to action in that order along the path toward the bottom right. [L0755, undated]
- Lines or shapes that shrink towards a central point create a sense of movement into depth. Pointing shapes, such as an arrow or a letter with a directional stroke, pull the eye along a path toward the next element. [L0755, undated]
- **Example:** A bold headline in bright red on a near-black background creates strong color contrast and draws the eye first. Use this pairing when the headline must be read before anything else on the layout. [L0755, undated]
- **Example:** A contrasting background panel or box placed behind secondary information, such as a benefits list, makes that information stand out from the main layout. The panel should be clearly different in color from the surrounding area so the eye reads it as a separate block. [L0755, undated]
- **Example:** A cool blue and a warm orange work well together as a palette. Use the orange as the accent for the promotion or price so it stands out against a blue-based design. [L0755, undated]
- An element can be made more important by giving it a distinct texture or a different shape and style, not only a larger size or a new color. A sale tag or key offer can use this treatment to separate it from the rest of the layout. [L0755, undated]
- Transparency or opacity controls how strongly an element stands out: lower it to push an element into the background and keep higher values for the element that should be emphasized. [L0754, undated]
- Visual hierarchy can be built in 3D with shadows as well as in flat design. [L0341, 2023]

**Break down an existing ad or template and check its design:**
1. Identify which elements are grouped by proximity, similarity or shared space, and which are separated.
2. Check how much negative space surrounds the product and the main message.
3. Read the layout in the usual reading order and note which element the eye reaches first, second and last.
4. Draw a diagonal line across the layout and check whether the visual weight on each side is roughly balanced.
5. Confirm that the promotion and the call to action are easy to find, and that the layout does not have too many competing elements. [L0755, undated]

### Grouping and perception (Gestalt principles)

- Gestalt principles describe how people perceive a whole before its parts, fill in gaps, dislike incomplete shapes, and group things by similarity and difference. Designers use these tendencies to decide how elements are arranged in ads, banners and product pages. [L0755, undated]
- **Closure:** Viewers mentally complete a shape when the designer leaves gaps or implied lines in negative space. This lets a design use fewer elements while still reading as a complete image. [L0755, undated]
- **Common fate:** Elements that point or move in the same direction are read as one group. An element that breaks that direction and color pattern stands out from the rest. [L0755, undated]
- **Continuation:** The eye follows the smoothest line through a layout, so the most important elements should sit along that path. Using this path guides the viewer toward the intended message or action. [L0755, undated]
- **Similarity:** Elements sharing color, shape or size are grouped even when they are far apart. Making one element clearly different in color, shape or size is a way to make it stand out. [L0755, undated]
- **Figure ground:** The brain separates a focal foreground object from its background, and some images can flip which part reads as foreground. Use clear foreground and background separation so the product or focal point is immediately readable. [L0755, undated]
- **Law of Pragnanz:** The brain reduces complex or ambiguous shapes to the simplest known shapes. Adding color or separation can reveal the simple parts inside a complex shape, which helps when designing logos and icons. [L0755, undated]
- Overlapping elements link them more strongly than simply placing them near each other, so a deliberate overlap can signal that two items form one unit. Use overlaps sparingly, because an overlap that crowds the layout reduces readability. [L0755, undated]

### Depth and shadows

- Shadows add depth to show importance: a larger, darker and broader shadow below an element makes it look further above the background, which signals higher importance. This follows Google's Material Design logic, and it is often used for sales tags and other key elements. [L0755, undated]
- In Canva, a drop shadow can be set with a lower opacity and a blur to create a lifted, subtle depth effect. Heavy shadows look dramatic, so use them sparingly and only where the lift is meant to signal importance. [L0755, undated]
- Use a three-dimensional layer approach on top of the flat 2D hierarchy: bring important elements forward with shadows and push secondary elements back. This adds a further way to show importance beyond size and color. [L0755, undated]
- Drop shadows create visual hierarchy by making key elements stand out, such as a satisfied-customers counter. [L0341, 2023]
- Use a shadow or gradient behind text or key elements to create visual hierarchy and make text easier to read. Use a white gradient behind dark text and a dark gradient behind white text. [L0343, 2023]

### Product graphics and templates

**Quick image ad with Canva:**
1. Get a product photo.
2. Remove the background, which can also be done inside Canva.
3. Open Canva templates and search by product features or by offer type such as a free gift.
4. Choose a template, place the product in it and add the offer text.
5. Make small brand adjustments so the ad looks consistent with your store. [L0692, 2023]

**Build a product graphic in Canva from a template:**
1. Search for the product type in Canva to find product templates.
2. Open a template that fits the layout you want and replace the text and imagery in it.
3. Copy a product image from the web and paste it into Canva so it uploads to your Uploads section.
4. Open Edit photo and run the Background remover on the product image.
5. Brush out any leftover background or unwanted parts with the brush control.
6. Position the product near the center of the design using Alt or Option drag and the arrow keys, then add a shadow from the element shadow options.
7. Add the offer text, group it, and move the group behind the product if it should sit beneath it.
8. Apply your brand colors from the Brand Kit, and mute harsh colors by adding a gray tone rather than leaving them at full strength. [L0754, undated]

**Offer badge pattern:** A short label such as "Limited edition" or "Free gift" is placed on a shape or band near the product, and it reads as an urgency or bonus signal. Pattern: [short scarcity or bonus word] + [product or offer noun], placed in the top or bottom corner of the image in a contrasting brand color. Example you can adapt: "Only 50 Left" and "Free Carry Case Included." [L0754, undated]

**Product layouts:** Product layouts that work well usually have a product image, space for several short benefits, and a clear call to action. Find inspiration by browsing product templates and copying the structure, not the exact design. [L0754, undated]

**Them vs Us creative:**
1. Place your product cut-out on the right side of a two-column layout.
2. Place the competitor product on the left side.
3. Desaturate or grade the competitor background so it looks weaker than yours.
4. Add a subtle pattern and a white gradient overlay on top for texture.
5. Write the product benefits as short points next to your product. [L0343, 2023]

**Photo framing:** Use photo framing to show the product being sold. When a model's face takes up most of the image, reposition or reframe the photo so the product stays visible. [L0992, 2025-26]

**Overlay text on product images:** Graphic overlays with text on product images (offer, benefits, social proof) can be used in ad imagery. Google may stop showing an image that contains text, but the product is not banned, so the image is simply not shown. This was being tested as a way to create a strong hero section without scrolling. [L0353, 2023]

**Ad breakdown:** See the procedure under Layout and visual hierarchy. [L0755, undated]
