### Hook, copy, thumbnail and angle testing

- Each new video should come with about three hook variations, where only the first five seconds differ. Meta then optimises the combinations. [L0398, 2023+]
- Hooks should be tested by making one video with three different first 3 to 5 seconds, keeping the rest identical. Ideas can be taken from what is trending on TikTok. For image ads, make three images around the same marketing angle. [L1335, 2023+]
- Ad copy angle testing: keep the winning video and hook identical and change only the ad copy, so each version speaks to a different motivation within the same winning audiences. [L0398, 2023+]
  - Formula: one copy per audience motivation, with the video held constant. [L0398, 2023+]
  - Example 1: for a camping product, one copy focuses on night-time visibility and another on gadget features. [L0398, 2023+]
  - Example 2: for a fitness product, one copy focuses on saving time and another on feeling confident during workouts. [L0398, 2023+]
- Ad copy matters more once several audiences need different angles. Test ad copy only after spending more than about 1,000 per day on testing, and keep the video and hook identical while you test it. [L0398, 2023+]
- Thumbnails and ad copy are tested separately from the video. [L0902, undated]
  1. Keep the same winning video and vary only the thumbnail (thumbnail 1, 2, 3) to find the thumbnail winner, since thumbnails are the first thing customers see and can raise CTR. [L0902, undated]
  2. Keep the same video and thumbnail while varying the ad copy (ad copy 1, 2, 3) to find the ad copy winner. [L0902, undated]
- Testing a new angle on a video that did not get the expected views. [L0795, undated]
  1. Pick a good video idea that did not perform. [L0795, undated]
  2. Add a hook that has already worked for you in the past. [L0795, undated]
  3. Publish the new version and compare its results with the previous one. [L0795, undated]
- Find the strongest marketing angle without a large budget. [L1218, undated]
  1. Pick one marketing angle. [L1218, undated]
  2. Make a creative for that angle alone, or use only its first 5 to 10 seconds as the hook, and add UGC-style clips. [L1218, undated]
  3. Test each angle's creative separately on Facebook. [L1218, undated]
  4. Once one angle clearly wins, add a landing page section for it and move the other sections down. [L1218, undated]
- Creative testing by splitting a winning ad. [L1315, 2023]
  1. Split a winning ad into separate features or benefits, one per creative. [L1315, 2023]
  2. Run them for a few days. [L1315, 2023]
  3. Pick the best-performing problem or angle. [L1315, 2023]
  4. Create new creatives around that problem. [L1315, 2023]
  5. Keep iterating on existing ads and test new concepts continually. [L1315, 2023]
- Creative testing as a test of four creative types against interest audiences. [L0642, undated]
- A winning angle is tested in video before it is scaled. Launch VSL (video sales letter) ads first and static image ads afterwards, because VSLs convert better and test the full message. The winning angle then gets iterated into static image ads. [L1019, 2026]
- Replace a Google text ad's single element with one change at a time. Iterative text ad testing isolates single variable changes to identify which specific word or phrase your audience prefers. Only one element varies while all other copy remains identical; performance shifts directly show what caused them. Examples: Join the Community versus Become a Member (different verb, same offer), or Learn More versus Discover More (same action, different tone). [L0085, undated]
- Evolutionary text ad testing uses fundamentally different messaging frames, such as leading with a customer pain point versus leading with the solution benefit, to discover which overall approach converts best. Examples: Tired of Back Pain (pain-point focus) versus Professional Spinal Support (solution-forward), or Don't Get Left Behind (urgency) versus Trust the Industry Leader (authority and quality). [L0085, undated]
- Iterative testing changes only one element per test and keeps everything else identical, for example changing the call to action from Buy now to Shop now. It is worth doing, but it produces only small differences. [L1401, 2020-26]
- Evolutionary testing compares two very different ad styles, such as a question (Struggling with acne?) against a direct statement (The acne solution). On Google text ads, evolutionary tests usually produce bigger gains than tweaking single words, so new concepts should be tested more often. [L1401, 2020-26]
- Test sales copy on real members of the target market before launch. [L0340, undated]
  1. Give the copy to 3 to 5 people who fit the buyer profile and do not know the product. [L0340, undated]
  2. Ask each reader to say in their own words what the product does and what they should do next. [L0340, undated]
  3. Mark every sentence where the reader hesitates, misreads, or asks a question, and rewrite those sentences with simpler words and one claim each. [L0340, undated]
- Free pretest for a sales letter, in order. [L1370, undated]
  1. Read the letter aloud and fix any tongue twisters or awkward phrases. [L1370, undated]
  2. Read it to several people who match the target customer, possibly in a casual setting, and watch whether they ask how to buy, which signals that the approach works. [L1370, undated]
  3. Have a young reader read it aloud and change any words or phrases they struggle with. [L1370, undated]
  4. Check whether typical customers have questions the letter leaves unanswered, and if so add a page that answers them. [L1370, undated]
- Testing a voiceover against text-only overlays before committing. A voiceover reaches people who cannot read the screen, and text reaches people who cannot hear, and many text-only ads perform well. [L1427, 2023+]
- Case: a text-only ad with rain overlays and heavy effects reached about 2.5 ROAS and was treated as a failure. A simple UGC-style ad with plain text overlays and a voiceover reached 4 to 6 ROAS. [L1427, 2023+]
- Subtitles: when a video from abroad is strong but not in the local language, add subtitles if the creative is good; otherwise create or hire a similar one. Image ads were the main format used for testing new products. [L1108, 2023]
- Testing AI presenters for a proven product. [L1479, 2026]
  1. Take an ad angle and offer from a brand whose ads have run for a long time. [L1479, 2026]
  2. Create AI presenter versions of the same ad, each with a different avatar type (for example a doctor, an everyday woman, a man). [L1479, 2026]
  3. Keep the product, script and offer identical so that only the presenter changes. [L1479, 2026]
  4. Let the versions run and compare their results. [L1479, 2026]
  5. Scale the avatar that performs best and stop the rest. [L1479, 2026]
- Test platform-specific hooks: write two or more strong hooks, titles or captions for the same video and publish each variant on a different platform. [L0784, 2025-26]
  1. Write two or more strong hooks, titles, or captions for the same video. [L0784, 2025-26]
  2. Publish each variant on a different platform, for example variant A on TikTok, variant B on Instagram, and a third variant on YouTube. [L0784, 2025-26]
  3. Compare the results per platform. [L0784, 2025-26]
  4. Adjust the messaging for each platform based on what performed best there. [L0784, 2025-26]

### Offer testing

- Keep the offer out of the ad creatives while testing, because the offer changes often. Once a winning offer is found, add it to the ads; every offer change then requires updating the ads as well. [L0502, 2020+]
- Test offers with simple static creatives, such as buy one get one free, a free gift or 40 percent off, to see which offer responds best across markets and products. [L0341, 2023]
- Image creatives can be built fast from templates and tested before video creatives are made. [L0343, 2023]

### Format choice: static before video

- Video takes longer to produce, so use it once a static layout has shown it converts. [L0343, 2023]
- When product footage is limited, run quick image ads first to see which market responds, and only then produce a full video for the winning market. If footage is good enough for a clear narrative, use it, but the hook and offer come first. [L0344, 2023]
- Use simple image ads with product imagery in an AI tool, Photoshop, or a freelance graphic designer to test quickly before committing to video. [L0950, 2023]
- Test formats on your own product before committing budget to one type. [L1127, 2024]
- Swipe file creatives: the full testing workflow. [L0343, 2023]
  1. Choose a product and find a matching swipe file layout, such as Them vs Us, testimonial, benefit callout, meme or offer. [L0343, 2023]
  2. Replace the text with your branding, product benefits and offer. [L0343, 2023]
  3. Swap in your product cut-out and adjust colours and gradients. [L0343, 2023]
  4. Publish the variants as ads and compare which layout performs best for each product. [L0343, 2023]
  5. Scale the winning layout and build video versions only after the static test shows a clear winner. [L0343, 2023]
- Concepts that do not show the product at all, such as a close-up of two faces contrasting a problem and a solution, can be tested when competitors use this style successfully in search. Test such images against product-visible versions rather than replacing them. [L0882, 2025-26]
- Replacing the image of a working Shopping listing without a split test can lose performance that was already earned. Duplicate the listing first so the new image competes against the original. [L0882, 2025-26]
- Study competitors' ads in the Meta Ad Library and note recurring hooks, ad copy and story structure. [L0639, 2023]
- Reused TikTok-style video pins on Pinterest held a CTR of roughly 3 to 4 percent for about a month, which is well above the 1 percent day-three minimum used as a go or no-go gate. [L1135, undated]
- Simple video formats: a new Spark Ad test on a new account. [L1381, 2022]
  1. Choose the best organic video. [L1381, 2022]
  2. Use no interest targeting, only an age range such as 18 to 45 depending on the product. [L1381, 2022]
  3. Let about 20 dollars of spend run completely. [L1381, 2022]
  4. Do not reopen Ads Manager for the account afterward, since the account was seen to get an organic boost once the spend finished. [L1381, 2022]
- Creative testing: find the strongest format. Testing many creatives early (for example an image test and a video test in one ad set) lets the platform tell you which format responds. [L1379, 2020+]

### Pre-launch checks

- Before launching, have a finished landing page, 4 to 5 creatives, and ready ad copy. [L0648, undated]
- Check the spacing and line breaks of ad copy before publishing. [L0348, 2026]
- Check that the website URL points to the correct product page for that product. [L0348, 2026]

### Thresholds and stop rules

- A clear winner is a video creative with at least 3 sales and a positive ROAS above break-even. One sale can be luck, so wait for more data before calling a creative a winner. [L0398, 2023+]
- To pick a winning creative, read hard metrics such as purchases once the ad has spent around its target cost per acquisition. If it has not yet spent that much, use softer signals such as attention and CTR to decide which ad deserves more budget. [L0646, 2023]
- A reused creative with 3 to 4 percent CTR above a 1 percent day-three minimum passed the go or no-go gate. [L1135, undated]
- Turn off ads that spend 20 to 30 USD without sales and keep the ones that produce sales. [L0774, 2023]
- Stop a product if metrics do not show promise after break-even spend. [L0640, 2023]
- A hook and creative combination counts as a clear winner only when it performs best in every ad set of the test, not just one. A result that wins in one audience but loses in the others should be retested before it is scaled. [L1335, 2023+]
- Start creative testing when CTR or CPC is poor. [L0902, undated]

### Declaring winners

- Choose the winner by ROAS first; if ROAS is equal, use CTR; if CTR is equal, use average play time. [L1335, 2023+]
- A winner must perform best in every ad set of the test. [L1335, 2023+]
- Decide on the winner with the hard metric once the spend passes the target cost per acquisition; before that, use CTR and attention. [L0646, 2023]
- Once one angle clearly wins, add a landing page section for it and move the other sections down. [L1218, undated]
- Keep the three best performers after the test. [L1219, undated]
- Compare the highest CTR creative in each ad set. [L1212, undated]
- Scale the winning layout and video version only after the static test shows a clear winner. [L0343, 2023]
- Decide which creative stays by the data and the sales it brings, not by how polished it looks. [L0562, undated]
- Let the platform decide which performs best, then turn off the weak ones. [L0562, undated]
- Decide quickly on what performs early; waiting a month at 50 per day is not practical for most sellers. [L0950, 2023]
- Keep creative testing going: the winning creative is reused in audience testing campaigns, CBO scaling campaigns, and new audience tests. [L1335, 2023+]

### Batch naming and benchmarks

- Testing creatives in batches works by naming ads by batch number, not by individual creative number. The best-performing creative becomes the benchmark, and each new variation is tested against it. Any variation that beats the benchmark moves into a scaling campaign. [L0819, 2020+]
  - Example pattern: Batch 12, Creative B vs Batch 11 benchmark: move to scaling if it beats the benchmark's cost per purchase. [L0819, 2020+]
  - Second example: Batch 7, skit hook vs Batch 6 benchmark: scale only if it beats benchmark ROAS for three days. [L0819, 2020+]

### Testing cadence and weekly planning

- Testing creatives is a weekly job: plan 6 to 9 videos per week from 2 to 3 concepts, and test a minimum of three concepts per week. [L0298, 2023]
- Creative testing frequency: 2 to 3 times per week at small scale, 10 to 15 times per week at higher scale. [L1335, 2023+]
- Angle tests are cycled: after a winning angle is found, keep testing new concepts continually while iterating on existing ads. [L1315, 2023]
- A creative test that starts with a lot of spend will show a high CPM at first. [L0298, 2023]
- For new creative tests, expect initially high CPM regardless of creative quality, because new ads lack social proof. CPM reduces as real user engagement accumulates. [L0298, 2023]
- Creative testing begins on a small budget of about 5 per day per ad set to see whether an audience responds. [L1077, undated]

### Common mistakes

- Changing many things at once makes results unreadable. Keep a control ad and change one element. [L0640, 2023]
- A single creative inside a shared ad set can absorb most of the budget. Test each new creative in its own ad set first. [L0646, 2023; L1177, 2023]
- Forgetting to test a new creative separately lets the winning creative hide new results. [L1177, 2023]
- Judging an ad before it has spent enough. Wait for the target spend before reading hard metrics. [L0646, 2023; L0640, 2023]
- Launching a single polished ad late. Launch many creatives quickly and iterate. [L1077, undated]
- Changing the offer and the creative in the same test. Keep the offer fixed while testing creatives. [L0502, 2020+; L1382, undated]
- Starting hook testing before ripped creatives have proven profitable. [L1252, 2026]
- Blindly trusting platform budget allocation. Force spend onto a creative before calling it weak. [L0640, 2023]
- Declaring a product dead after a weak creative. Test new creatives first. [L1038, 2026]

#### Further details

- Test ads for a product in the US first, but if CTR stays low with the same product, test it in another market such as Canada or Australia. Australia is named as a good test market. [L0513, undated]

- Keep testing ad creative and the wording of customer messages, because small changes in phrasing can have large effects on sales. Spending money on testing was a core part of this seller's approach. [L1437, 2018]
