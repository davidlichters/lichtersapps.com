### Curiosity, suspense and pattern breaks

- **Suspense:** Start with a curiosity gap and delay the reveal of the product until after the first clip. Curiosity keeps people watching long enough to reach the product intro. [L0398, 2023+]
  - "I almost threw this out before I tried it on my kitchen counter."
  - "The one thing my gym instructor never told me until I bought this."
  [L0398, 2023+]
- **Pattern break:** Ads must interrupt what people are doing, not blend in. Short headlines of two to three words, paired with an unusual image, stop a reader mid-scroll long enough to click. The goal is a pattern break that moves attention to the offer. [L0523, 2015+]
- **Familiar word, unexpected format:** Open by speaking directly to the target buyer and pair a familiar, high-intent product word with a format the buyer does not expect. The curiosity from the mismatch stops the scroll, and the buyer identifies with the speaker at once. [L0707, 2026]
  - "Sunscreen in a stick? Here is why it leaves no white cast and why my daily routine changed."
  - "Espresso-style foam in a 30-second jar? I cannot believe this replaced my morning cafe run."
  [L0707, 2026]
- **Unusual question or event:** Open with a specific, unusual question or event the reader cares about, then bridge to the offer as the way to understand or solve it. Curiosity holds attention long enough for the reader to reach the offer. [L1369, undated]
  - "Why do some bakeries sell out by nine in the morning while the one across the street throws away its bread every night?"
  - "What is the one check that tells a warehouse manager a shipment will be late before the truck leaves the dock?"
  [L1369, undated]
- **Offbeat news hook:** Find an unusual article or statistic and use it to open the ad, then link it to the product through one clear step. Keep a file of such clippings so they are ready when a product needs a surprising opening. [L1366, undated]
  - "A survey shows that many people reuse their water bottles for days without washing them, so here is a bottle that cleans itself."
  - "A new report finds that most office chairs are sat on for more than eight hours, which is why this chair was designed around the spine."
  - "A survey finds many people reuse water bottles for days without washing them, so this bottle cleans itself."
  - "Most office chairs are sat on for over eight hours a day, which is why this one is built around the spine."
  [L1366, undated]
- **Educational hook:** Open with a useful fact or warning that names a problem the audience cares about, then reveal the product as the solution. Viewers accept the lesson first and the sale second. [L0524, 2023]
  - "Three everyday habits that quietly dry out your skin and what to use instead."
  - "Two cleaning products that leave a film on your floors, and the simple swap that fixes it."
  [L0524, 2023]
- **Educational reveal:** Ads that reveal a problem the viewer did not know about tend to perform well, for example "did you know your dog could suffer from...". They pull the viewer into the problem. [L1425, undated]
- **Question about owning the product:** Open with a question that asks the viewer to picture owning or using the product, then cut to text-to-speech listing the reasons to buy in a few sentences, and end with a short closing line. [L1383, 2022]
  - "Do you see yourself keeping this on your desk every morning?" followed by benefits and a closing line about the daily routine.
  - "Do you see yourself giving this as a gift this winter?" followed by reasons it suits gifting and a closing line urging a purchase.
  [L1383, 2022]
- **Case: "Do you see yourself needing this?":** A jewelry store used the hook "Do you see yourself needing this?" and reportedly reached about 2.5 million views with it. The speaker cited this as evidence that the hook can perform well across different products. [L1383, 2022]
- **Dialogue hook:** Start with an emoji of a person and a text-to-speech question such as "Why did you give me this free product?". Reply in character that the product is a gift, change the wording slightly each time, and have the other person say they would like to see more videos and ask whether they won. Finish by confirming they won. [L1383, 2022]
  - "Why did you give me this free bike?" answered by a gift reply and a follow-up asking whether they won.
  - "Why did you send me this free candle set?" answered by a thank-you and a question asking whether they can join the giveaway.
  [L1383, 2022]
- **Case: dialogue hook performance:** The dialogue hook "Why did you give me this free product?" was uploaded about three to six times before one version got traction. That version reached about 370,000 views and brought about 5,000 followers. Earlier uploads of the same format got barely any views. [L1383, 2022]
- **Text-story hook:** Use on-screen text that takes longer to read than the video length, for example 15 seconds of reading in a 7-second video. This forces viewers to replay, which raises average watch time, but the text must be engaging enough to keep them reading. [L1384, 2022]
  - Example A: a short story where the outcome is revealed only in the final frame.
  - Example B: a running dialogue where each line shows a small surprise.
  [L1384, 2022]
- **Story opening:** Begin with a short exchange in which someone with a real problem asks a respected person for advice. Let the answer introduce a principle that leads to the product. A concrete person and a question make the reader listen, and the answer frames the product as the practical result of wise advice. [L1369, undated]
  - "A plumber with a full schedule asked his most experienced mentor how to stop losing jobs to cheaper rivals, and the answer was to sell the result the customer will see first."
  - "A new mother asked her pediatrician what matters most in a stroller, and the reply began with the one feature that parents regret not checking."
  [L1369, undated]

### Audience, POV and social-proof hooks

- **Niche-targeting hook:** Address only a specific group in the first line, so non-members scroll past and members engage. Engagement from that group signals TikTok to show the video to similar users, which can give better-converting reach than a large but broad view count. [L0795, undated]
  - "Every runner needs this free route tracker."
  - "Calling all home bakers: this tool fixes sticky dough in seconds."
  [L0795, undated]
- **Niche hook with a cold or problem:** Name a specific audience, name the cold or problem they face in their activity, then give the product as the direct fix. The hook works because it addresses one identifiable person at the moment of the problem. [L1316, 2024+]
  - "For every football fan at winter games: cold feet ruin the match, so wear these insulated socks all game long."
  - "For the dog walkers stuck in the rain: the waterproof jacket that keeps you dry on every walk."
  [L1316, 2024+]
- **Hobby hook, boredom and free time:** Name the boredom at home, then show how the product changed the way the viewer spends free time. [L1426, undated]
  - "Tired of scrolling every evening? Here is the one thing that filled my weekends."
  - "Your free hours deserve more than the couch. Try this build."
  [L1426, undated]
- **POV hook:** Place the viewer in a first-person situation in the first second. This makes the scene feel personal and encourages them to keep watching. POV works well when the scene leads straight into a comparison or product demonstration. [L0795, undated]
  - "POV: you finally find a charger that survives a full week of travel."
  - "POV: your kitchen floor stays clean after the first week with this mop."
  [L0795, undated]
- **Shopping-finds hook:** Frame the product as something people discover on TikTok, Amazon or shopping feeds. This leverages curiosity about what others buy, and viewers trust products that appear socially trending. [L0398, 2023+]
  - "The kitchen tool everyone on my feed keeps buying."
  - "Three reasons I stopped paying for the expensive version."
  [L0398, 2023+]
- **Fear of missing out and insider knowledge:** Suggest the knowledge or product is unknown or underused, and position yourself as the one sharing the secret. FOMO triggers curiosity and urgency, and viewers do not want to be left behind. [L0160, undated]
  - Formula: "Why is nobody talking about [product]," "Calling all [audience] tired of [problem]," or "If you're not [using X / doing X], you're missing out."
  - "Calling all runners tired of blisters-why isn't anyone using this?"
  - "If you're not using this app for your business, you're doing it the hard way."
  [L0160, undated]
- **Follow-for-giveaway hook:** It grows followers quickly ("follow my account and I'll give you a giveaway"), but has become less effective for conversion because many sellers use the same style. Use it sparingly and do not rely on it as the main sales driver. [L0657, undated]

### Copy openings (written ads and landing copy)

- **Open with "Even if...":** Start copy with "Even if..." to address the main objection the reader might have. This jugular objection, the single most common one, must be crushed first or you will lose readers. Illusory superiority means most people think they are better than average, so they assume "it might work for them but not for me". [L0196, 2020+]
- **Mistake: skipping the jugular objection:** Not addressing the common main objection at the start of copy is a mistake. Start with "Even if..." to pre-empt what readers are thinking, especially given illusory superiority ("it might work for them, but not for me"). [L0196, 2020+]
- **Open with customer language:** Effective ad copy opens with customer language. State their pain point directly, reference their insecurity or deepest desire, or make a bold benefit claim that resonates immediately. [L0204, undated]
- **Short first sentence of body copy:** The first sentence should be very short, nearly incomplete, and easy to read, with no long or multisyllable words. Its only job is to make the reader read the second sentence. [L1366, undated]
- **Headline-to-first-sentence pull (print):** Every element of a print ad (headline, subheadline, photo, caption, logo, layout) is judged by one question: does it get the reader to read the first sentence? The first body sentence should be very short, almost incomplete, with no long words. [L1366, undated]
  - "Your inbox is a mess. Here is how to clear it in ten minutes."
  - "Your back hurts by noon. Four changes can stop that before lunch."
  [L1366, undated]
- **Real-life scenario:** Open with the buyer inside a stressful everyday moment, describe the feelings and pressure, and only then reveal how the product resolves it. Keep technical specs for later so the scene does the emotional work first. [L1366, undated]
  - "You are stuck in a hotel lobby, the bill is waiting, and the card reader fails; this portable terminal fixes the moment."
  - "You are on a crowded train with a phone that is about to die; this solar pack keeps you connected until you get home."
  - "The card reader fails and the line is growing behind you. This portable terminal fixes that moment."
  - "The phone is at 3 percent on a crowded train. This solar pack keeps you connected until you get home."
  [L1366, undated]
- **Familiar scene from daily life:** Open with a scene the reader recognises, then move to the problem the product solves within the same scene. A familiar setting makes the reader picture himself, so the offer feels relevant. [L1369, undated]
  - "After you finish the morning school run and drop the car keys on the counter, you still have three invoices waiting to be paid."
  - "On the first cold morning of the year, the water in the shower takes nine minutes to warm, and your kids are already late for the bus."
  [L1369, undated]
- **News angle tied to the reader:** A news angle or an unusual, specific detail draws attention and can be bridged to the offer. Attention must tie to the reader's own interest in the business or life, not stand alone as a shock. [L1369, undated]
- **Newsworthy first, then their affairs:** Businesspeople compete with many distractions. Give the reader something newsworthy first, then connect it to their own affairs. Once attention is diverted, bring it back by showing how the offer serves those affairs. [L1369, undated]
- **Simple first line that is relevant:** The first sentence or headline must instantly say something important, valuable and useful to the reader, because an unwanted letter is treated as a pest until it proves otherwise. Gimmicks such as fake hazard warnings tend to fail. A genuinely relevant headline usually succeeds. [L1370, undated]
- **Sensational shout (mistake):** A sensational shout, such as an alarming word with no link to the offer, draws the wrong response. Tie every attention-grabbing element to a reason the reader cares about the offer. [L1369, undated]
- **Find the point of contact:** Use this procedure to build an opening.
  1. List the reader's dominant thoughts, such as income, family, comfort, status, promotion, business survival or a current worry.
  2. Pick the thought most closely tied to what you sell.
  3. Open with a sentence about that thought and bring in the offer as its answer.
  [L1369, undated]

### On-screen text, sound and visual hooks

- **On-screen text carries much of the effect:** The on-screen text and the hook have a large share of a video's effect. The hosts estimated the text alone at about 30% of the video. A weak opening can turn a good demonstration into a low-converting video. [L0657, undated]
- **Text plus unexpected background:** Combine an on-screen text hook with something happening in the background. The background element should be unexpected or slightly odd, so it catches viewers off guard and makes them curious. [L0764, 2022]
- **Text-to-speech or short text:** Text-to-speech in the opening can keep viewers engaged. Alternatively, show a few sentences of on-screen text with a quick transition so viewers must re-watch the video to finish reading. [L0820, undated]
- **Study viral on-screen text:** Viral product videos often succeed because of their on-screen text. Study the text and structure of viral product videos and reuse the proven text pattern while filming your own product. [L1381, 2022]
- **On-screen hook example:** On a short product clip, the first second shows the hook text "Do you see yourself drinking this?" on screen, followed by a recreated pouring sound and a text line describing the product's benefit. [L1383, 2022]
- **Short, fast hooks and music:** A short, fast hook is key to TikTok creatives. Spend effort on the first seconds, the music and hook variations to find winning versions. [L1376, undated]
- **Thumbnails matter less for video ads:** Feed placements often skip past thumbnails. Test the first seconds of the video (the hook) more than the thumbnail. [L0648, undated]
- **Hook formats for variations:** Hook formats include a clip change, a music change, a "three reasons why" opener, a "wish I knew earlier" opener or a before-and-after opener. [L0902, undated]
- **Hook scene template:** Use this template as written for the first seconds.

```
Hook, first seconds. Option one, spoken: tell viewers this is for them if [PET] hair has taken over their home. Shot: a slow pan across a living room buried in [PET] hair. Option two, spoken: say that [PRODUCT] changed their [SPACE] and gave them back their health. Shot: show the product, cut to a before view of a grimy, hair-covered [SPACE], then cut to the same [SPACE] spotless afterward.
```

  [L0392, undated]

- **Hook as a pattern break in the visual:** Ads must interrupt, not blend in. The unusual image is part of the pattern break that stops the scroll. [L0523, 2015+]

### Building hook variations

- **Variations per concept:** Write one script for the concept, then make three versions in which only the first three to five seconds change while the rest of the video stays the same. [L0902, undated]
- **Keep the hook separate for testing:** The hook, meaning the first three to five seconds, decides whether viewers stay, so it must be tested separately from the rest of the video. [L0902, undated]
- **One video, three tests:** Keep the video and change only the opening hook, which is one of the most important parts for grabbing attention. One video can become three test videos this way. [L1107, 2023]
- **Start with about six hooks:** Build a list of about six hooks and make several videos for each one. Once a hook starts performing, duplicate that video and post more variations of it. [L1383, 2022]
- **Hook library:** A library of about 50 hook ideas can produce around 50 variations of the same product video and supply roughly three to six months of creative. Keep the library current, because hook trends change constantly. [L0398, 2023+]
- **Test list of 50 hooks:** A video hook test list of 50 hook ideas is recommended for TikTok-style first seconds. Use it to pick which hook goes in the first position. [L0637, 2023+]
- **Keep notes on hooks that work:** Hooks can start with a question about the audience's issue or with a direct solution statement, and should be tested. Keep notes on hooks that work so they can be reused later, including in TikTok ads once organic results fund them. [L0795, undated]

### Testing hooks

- **Test systematically:** Take a winning creative and test many hook variations until one clearly outperforms. Hook ideas should come from audience research on language, problems and context. [L0640, 2023]
- **Three variations of one video:** Create three variations of one video, replacing only the first 3 to 5 seconds of the clip with different openings. Run all variations inside the winning audiences. [L0648, undated]
- **Lift a weak attention rate:** To raise a weak attention rate, take the video that works and place several new hooks in front of it. Make sure every hook fits the marketing angle and matches the landing page copy. Keep testing new hooks continually, even when attention is already good. [L0644, undated]
- **Hook testing with the 13-pattern classification:** Use the 13-pattern set from the classification procedure to label each tested hook, so results show which pattern type performs. [L0613, 2026]
- **Hooks decide the click too:** A strong opening drives views beyond the hook into the click, so the hook test should track clicks as well as watch time. [L0950, 2023]

### Mistakes to avoid

- Spending too long before the main benefit. If the ad takes 10 seconds or more to introduce the product, demonstrate the benefit within 3 to 5 seconds, then introduce the product. [L0067, 2023]
- Leading with product features in the opening. Show the problem first, then the solution. [L0280, undated]
- Not addressing the jugular objection at the start of copy. [L0196, 2020+]
- Attention-grabbing content with no relation to the product, which attracts viewers who will not buy. [L0640, 2023]
- Delaying the product in a non-unboxing video. Unless it is a strong unboxing, show the product within the first five seconds. [L1377, 2022+]
- Sensational shouts with no link to the offer. [L1369, undated]
- Gimmicks such as fake hazard warnings, which tend to fail. [L1370, undated]
- Relying on the follow-for-giveaway hook as the main sales driver. [L0657, undated]
- A product introduction that takes around 10 seconds, which loses viewers before the benefit appears. [L0640, 2023]
