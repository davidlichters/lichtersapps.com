### Face-to-face selling adapted to print

Sequence for print copy, adapted from face-to-face selling [L1366, undated]:

1. Set up the selling environment through layout, tone and the first paragraphs [L1366, undated].
2. Get the prospect's attention with the headline [L1366, undated].
3. Introduce yourself and keep the attention through the subheadline, photo and caption [L1366, undated].
4. Deliver the sales pitch in the body copy while keeping the reader saying yes [L1366, undated].
5. Show the reader that you know the product and relate it to their needs [L1366, undated].

### Print ad copy sequence

Write the copy in this order [L1366, undated]:

1. Write the headline and check it grabs the reader [L1366, undated].
2. Write the subheadline and check it makes the reader want to read further [L1366, undated].
3. Write the photo caption, since captions are read often [L1366, undated].
4. Write the first sentence, short enough to pull the reader into the second sentence [L1366, undated].
5. Write each next sentence so it answers the question the reader is likely to have at that point [L1366, undated].
6. Build the body in blocks [L1366, undated]:
   - hook and set the mood [L1366, undated]
   - explain how the product differs and how it works [L1366, undated]
   - justify the price with comparisons [L1366, undated]
   - address lasting value [L1366, undated]
   - address service and reliability concerns [L1366, undated]
7. Finish with the request for the order and the response device [L1366, undated].
8. Block-diagram the complete ad to check the flow and that each question is answered in the right order [L1366, undated].

### Problem-agitate-solution (PAS)

**Principle.** Problem-agitate-solution is a copy framework: surface the problem, make it feel worse, then present the product as the solution. It is especially useful for direct response video ads [L1426, undated].

**Structure.** PAS works because it first makes the viewer feel the pain and then offers relief, which gives a clean structure for surfacing a customer's problem [L1425, undated]. The three parts are [L1425, undated]:

1. **Problem:** surface the problem the customer experiences [L1425, undated].
2. **Agitate:** make the problem worse and attach an emotion, so the viewer feels the pain [L1425, undated].
3. **Solution:** present the product as the answer to that problem, followed by features and benefits [L1425, undated].

**Examples.** [L1425, undated]

- Example A, window-cleaning product: Streaks on your windows after every clean? Every wipe just smears the grime around and the glass looks worse than before. This squeegee with a rubber edge and a built-in drip tray leaves a streak-free finish in one pass [L1425, undated].
- Example B, back-pain cushion: Sitting at your desk all day leaves your lower back aching by 3 p.m., and each week it gets worse. This ergonomic cushion with a contoured memory-foam core takes the pressure off your spine so the afternoon slump is gone [L1425, undated].

**Example from snowboarding.** The problem is repeated falls that cause pain, slow progress and embarrassment. The solution is thick padding that absorbs shock on hips and body. The desired outcome is progressing faster to jumps and becoming a better rider. This sequence is the structure of a video ad [L1426, undated].

**Video ad sequence.** Use these seven steps [L1427, 2023+]:

1. State the problem the viewer recognizes [L1427, 2023+].
2. Agitate it by attaching a feeling to it, such as tiredness, frustration or fear of falling behind [L1427, 2023+].
3. Present the product as the solution, stressing the instant benefit and low effort [L1427, 2023+].
4. Turn the benefit into a desired outcome [L1427, 2023+].
5. Amplify that outcome with what it says about the viewer, such as looking sharp or standing out [L1427, 2023+].
6. Add a social proof element [L1427, 2023+].
7. End with a call to action [L1427, 2023+].

**When to use it.** PAS fits products that solve a real problem [L1427, 2023+]. For a nice-to-have product with no strong problem, skip the agitation and go straight to the solution, making the benefit clearly better than the alternatives [L1427, 2023+].

### Ad layout sequence

A full ad layout follows this order [L1425, undated]:

1. Hook [L1425, undated].
2. Educational statement [L1425, undated].
3. Curiosity line [L1425, undated].
4. Product introduction with the benefit [L1425, undated].
5. How to use the product, demonstrating how it solves the problem [L1425, undated].
6. Social proof with agitation of the problem [L1425, undated].
7. Call to action with social proof [L1425, undated].

Demonstrating the product's use is important because it shows how the product solves the problem [L1425, undated].

**Examples.** [L1425, undated]

Give one soft chew a day with food. Join thousands of cat owners seeing fewer hairballs. Learn more below [L1425, undated].
- Wrinkle serum: Skin lovers, never ignore these three signs of early fine lines. Fine lines form when skin loses moisture, and most people miss them until later. Our night serum restores hydration with one pump each evening. Our customers report smoother skin within two weeks. Shop now and join thousands of happy customers [L1425, undated].

### Problem-solving ad script

**Scripting procedure.** [L1425, undated]

1. Fill in the problem and the emotion attached to it [L1425, undated].
2. Describe the feature and the benefit it creates, then the result of that benefit [L1425, undated].
3. Keep asking what each benefit means until a deep outcome is reached, such as a clean home, which leads to comfort and having people over [L1425, undated].
4. Add the avoid-negative-outcome angle, since people are more driven away from pain than towards pleasure [L1425, undated].
5. Add testimonial and call to action [L1425, undated].
6. Turn the outline into a full script and plan the shot for each line [L1425, undated].

**Fill-in outline.** Complete every line before turning the outline into the script [L1425, undated]:

```
Hook: [target audience] + [problem, fact or curiosity line]
Problem: [what the customer sees or experiences every day]
Agitate: [how the problem gets worse over time] + [emotion it creates, e.g. embarrassment, anxiety, low energy]
Solution: [product name] + [bold claim or unique selling point]
Features: [feature 1], [feature 2]
Benefit ladder: [feature] -> what does that mean? -> [result] -> what does that mean? -> [deeper outcome, e.g. comfort, having people over]
Avoid negative outcome: [what happens if the customer does nothing]
Social proof: [testimonial or review line]
Call to action: [clear action, e.g. Shop now, Buy now, Click here, Learn more]
```

#### Further details

- Ad copy formula for ecommerce: (1) Identify and confirm the problem your target audience faces. (2) Agitate the problem slightly to increase attention. (3) Present the solution your product provides. (4) Explain benefits of clicking the ad, particularly using your unique mechanism. (5) For Facebook ads with extended space, add text-based customer reviews as quotes. (6) Confirm a clear call-to-action. [L0241, 2021-22]

- Pattern for a problem-agitate-solution ad: name the problem the buyer already knows, make it worse by showing the consequence, then present the solution. Example: 'Tired of guests seeing pet hair on your sofa? Every visit feels like a cleaning test. This brush clears the hair in minutes.' Second example: 'Still buying lint roller paper every week? Each roll runs out in days and costs more than you think. Sweep up the hair and keep going.' [L0353, 2023]
