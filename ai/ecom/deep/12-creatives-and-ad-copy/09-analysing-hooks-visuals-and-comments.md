### Analysing hooks, visuals and comments

#### Hooks and visuals

1. Analyze 20 to 25 of the most successful hooks from TikTok, Instagram and Facebook ads, identify the patterns that work most often, and note which hooks have run longest. [L0613, 2026]
2. Analyze visuals: picture of the benefit, picture of the problem, picture of the product, product in action, environments, people shown, what visuals create belief, production quality (professional vs authentic UGC), color schemes and text overlay styles. [L0613, 2026]
3. Analyze at least 100 comments on viral posts, grouped by questions, objections, enthusiasm and testimonials. Note sentiment, 'wish I had known' and 'I need this because' comments, tagging behavior, and identity-resonance comments. [L0613, 2026]
4. Study emotional storytelling: transformation versus feature-benefit ads, character arcs, before-state portrayal, vulnerability moments, and at least 10 ads that tap identity-level emotion. [L0613, 2026]
5. Map content structures (see table below) and note the balance of emotion versus logic, typical ad length, objection handling and narrative patterns. [L0613, 2026]

#### Content structures in winning content

| Structure | Description | Source ids |
|---|---|---|
| Symptom → Problem → Solution → Product → Offer | Full funnel from symptom | [L0613, 2026] |
| Problem → Solution → Product → Offer | Starts at the problem | [L0613, 2026] |

- Deliverables for this section: 20 to 25 viral hooks with links; 20 to 30 representative comments; 15 to 20 visual approaches; 7 to 10 effective structure patterns; 10 to 15 successful calls to action; 5 to 7 trending visual styles; 3 to 5 trending hashtags. [L0613, 2026]

#### Problem and solution messaging

- Problem-and-solution messaging on the product page and in the ads is an effective way to explain why a product is worth buying. Check how a competitor presents the problem and solution before writing your own. [L1172, 2023+]
- Competitor analysis on the landing page: [L0708, undated]
  1. Open each competitor landing page you found.
  2. Note the marketing angles, the copywriting and the offers they use.
  3. Identify weaknesses such as brand colors or layout that you could improve.
  4. Use these findings to create a stronger version and beat the competition.

### Interrupt-style ad models

Collect interrupt-style ad models:

1. Search Google Images for tabloid newspaper headlines. [L0523, 2015+]
2. Note the unusual or shocking images used to grab attention. [L0523, 2015+]
3. Note how headlines stay short and make the reader curious. [L0523, 2015+]
4. Adapt these patterns to your own product and audience in your ads. [L0523, 2015+]

### Finding gaps and building differentiation

- Gaps in your analysis are fears or mechanisms that the avatar likely has but that none of the winning competitor ads address. These are white-space openings worth testing. [L0039, 2026]
- Identifying gaps means recognizing unaddressed emotional pain, unmet anxieties or unstated objections in the customer data that the swipe file does not hit. These signal potential differentiation angles. [L0039, 2026]
- Before analyzing your audience, do competitor creative research first. Understanding how competitors frame problems and structure messaging simplifies the audience investigation, because you already know which angles and language resonate in your market. [L0258, undated]
- Competitor creative research process: [L0258, undated]
  1. Identify your 3 most important competitors.
  2. For each competitor, find their 3 most commonly used creative types (UGC, lifestyle images, high-production video, memes, etc.).
  3. For each creative type, document: the concept or angle (testimonial, unboxing, advertorial, problem-solution), hooks used in the first 3 seconds, problems and desires addressed, benefits, features, pain points, objections overcome, target character attributes and age, scenery, and slang or buzzwords.
- Start every market analysis by checking how many similar products have already advertised to the same prospects. Competitors' ads are the best evidence; a few hours of research should gather samples of every competing ad. [L0330, undated]
- For a new competitor that scales, the ad is usually the main reason it works, not the website. The product matters most, but the ad hook and visual approach are what sell it. [L0335, 2023]
- Afterlip shows trending products and features beyond Dropispy. Check that the products found are not oversaturated before testing them. [L0349, 2024]
- Review competitors' Facebook Ad Library and identify the ads they run for a long time, because repeated ads signal winners. Analyse their storyline and marketing angle, then replicate or adapt that angle for your own product. [L0902, undated]

### TikTok research

#### Finding a winning hook on TikTok

1. Open the TikTok Creative Center. [L1374, undated]
2. Select your niche. [L1374, undated]
3. Look for products being sold with paid traffic in that niche. [L1374, undated]
4. Identify the hook, structure, and creative style that repeat across the advertisers that appear to be working. [L1374, undated]
5. Replicate the same structure and hook with your own product, adapting sound, visuals, and presentation. [L1374, undated]

#### Value and aspiration

- Competitor analysis for TikTok means identifying what value competitors offer their audience through education, entertainment or aspiration, and then providing that same kind of value. Aspiration works through people wanting to be like a respected figure who uses the product. It is typically used in influencer-led categories such as beauty and cosmetics. [L1374, undated]
- Study TikTok videos of similar products, log why they work, then reproduce the style rather than copying a single post. [L0561, 2023+]

#### Researching before filming

1. Search for other sellers of the same product. [L1381, 2022]
2. Find videos for that product with decent views. [L1381, 2022]
3. Study how they made theirs, including their text and sound, and replicate the approach in your own videos. [L1381, 2022]
4. Save viral sounds to favorites for later use. [L1381, 2022]

### Idea boards and inspiration in the editing app

#### Edits app: brainstorming and ideas

- Edits includes a brainstorming section that suggests content styles which perform for other accounts in a similar niche. Use it to test formats before producing them. [L1453, 2025-26]
- The Ideas tab has three sections: [L1453, 2025-26]
  - Stickies (sticky notes);
  - Reels (saved reels, whose audio can be reused directly);
  - Collections (folders you create by category, e.g. by location, person, or topic).
- To add a sticky note: Ideas tab > plus button at the bottom > sticky note. Sticky notes can be given a different color and act as a quick place to note video ideas. [L1453, 2025-26]
- Process for organizing video ideas in Edits: [L1453, 2025-26]
  1. Save reels with audio you want to use in the Reels section of the Ideas tab.
  2. Add sticky notes for ideas that come up while watching other content.
  3. Group saved reels into Collections by category, such as location, collaborator, or video type.
  4. Use the saved audio and notes when producing each video.

#### Inspiration tab

- The Inspiration tab is a Reels-style feed of popular content, filterable by accounts you follow. It has no like, comment, follow or send function, so it is for browsing only. [L1453, 2025-26]
- Actions on a reel in the Inspiration tab: save it, use its audio immediately for editing, or add a sticky note. The 'use audio' action opens the audio directly in the editor. [L1453, 2025-26]

### Swipe files and boards

#### What a swipe file is

- A swipe file is a digital or analog archive of ads, creatives, headlines, page layouts and other material you have saved from others. Open it when you need ideas and reuse the patterns, not the exact content. Photos taken with a phone are enough to build one. [L1343, undated]
- Keep a swipe file of brands that inspire you and review it regularly, for example every other day. This shows how similar products are marketed in your niche. [L1194, undated]

#### Building a swipe file

1. When you see an ad, landing page or creative worth reusing, save it to your swipe file immediately. [L1343, undated]
2. Keep the file in a form you actually use, digital or analog, and add to it regularly. [L1343, undated]
3. When you need inspiration for a new ad or creative, open the file and look for patterns that match your product and audience. [L1343, undated]
4. Adapt the pattern to your own offer rather than copying it directly. [L1343, undated]

#### Boards in Foreplay and similar tools

- Foreplay is a swipe file tool where brands save ads. Use it to collect proven examples, such as Black Friday ads from e-commerce brands, before producing your own creatives. [L0692, 2023]
- Foreplay is a paid ad inspiration tool, about 50 USD per month. It saves ads from the Facebook Ad Library into boards through a Chrome extension. It works as a mood board of ideas rather than a performance tracker, because it does not show metrics. [L1348, 2023]
- Collect ad inspiration in this order: [L1348, 2023]
  1. Find ads in the Facebook Ad Library and check how many days each has been running, as longer-running ads suggest performance.
  2. Save ads that look like good performers or show good design practice into a board in an ad collection tool such as Foreplay, using its Chrome extension.
  3. Follow industry experts who post their ads and build a swipe file from them.
  4. Analyze saved ads for the psychological principle, layout and spacing that make them work.

#### Reverse-engineering a saved ad

1. Pick a product, ad or brand you admire. [L1343, undated]
2. Write down what it leaves out or could have done better. [L1343, undated]
3. Create your own version that fills one of those gaps. [L1343, undated]
4. Compare your version with the original to see which choices are your own tendencies. [L1343, undated]

### Automating competitive ad intelligence

- Set a trigger for when a competitor uploads a new ad to their ad library. The automation downloads the ad, optionally uses an AI step to transcribe it, feeds it to Claude to rewrite it for your brand's voice and offer, and saves the adapted version to Google Drive or Google Docs. This builds a continuously updated competitive creative reference library with zero manual effort. [L0287, 2025-26]

### Older advice (2018)
