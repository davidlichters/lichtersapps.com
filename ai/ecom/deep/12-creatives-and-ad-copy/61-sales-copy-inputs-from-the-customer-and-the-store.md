### Sales copy inputs from the customer and the store

- Desire, pain and scarcity lines should be built from real customer words and real stock situations. Use the customer research as the source of wording [L0567, 2025-26], and use scarcity only when it is true [L0567, 2025-26].
- Lead with the feelings and situation, then one concrete benefit, rather than generic claims [L0774, 2023].

### Organic Instagram features

- Instagram's Replace Audio, launched July 21, lets you swap the music on already published public feed posts and carousels without losing likes or reach. Changing the track does not reset the engagement the post has already earned. [L1338, 2025-26]
- Replace Audio is useful for older posts that unexpectedly go viral months or years later. Switching their sound to a track that is trending now can help the post reach the new audience it is being shown to. [L1338, 2025-26]
- Instants is a separate Instagram app that also appears as a plug-in in the Instagram DM tab. It added video recording, so users can send short disappearing clips to close friends, not only photos. [L1338, 2025-26]
- Brands can use Instants for spontaneous behind-the-scenes clips, such as a quick video while packing orders in the warehouse or during a product photo shoot. The goal is to give followers an inside look. [L1338, 2025-26]
- On Instagram carousel posts, you can write a different caption for each slide instead of one caption for the whole post. A five-slide educational carousel should get five unique captions. [L1338, 2025-26]
- Per-slide captions make a carousel more interactive and give viewers a reason to keep swiping and spend longer on the post. They also let you share more information without overloading one caption. [L1338, 2025-26]
- Instagram is expanding screenshot alerts to Stories, so creators get notified when someone screenshots their Story. The creator in the lesson believed this applies only to subscription and close friends Stories, and this was not yet confirmed. [L1338, 2025-26]

### Trend-driven organic content

- Jimothy the Raccoon is a raccoon in Seattle with a very distinctive body shape who became a viral internet meme, with fan pages and people posting his sightings. Some brands tied him into their content, such as a bakery that etched his image into a sourdough loaf, and the trend can be searched on TikTok for inspiration. [L1338, 2025-26]
- Tying a viral trend into one piece of content while it is peaking is a low-cost way to plan organic reach. It works best when the trend is used in the same week it is popular. [L1338, 2025-26]
- Reposting content you already have to Snapchat takes almost no effort and can help reach a new audience. Founder-led stories are a stronger option if you want to build a more personal presence there. [L1338, 2025-26]

### Testing new Instagram features

- Test the new Instagram features on your own account to see whether they give a reach boost. [L1338, 2025-26]

  1. Replace the audio on older carousel posts that have gained traction.
  2. Write a unique caption for each slide in new carousels.
  3. Post a short video via Instants, such as a warehouse or photo shoot clip.
  4. Compare reach and engagement before deciding which features to keep using.

### Viral Reels production and posting

- Produce and post viral Instagram content for a product. [L1367, undated]

  1. Order the product from Amazon to your own address so you can check it firsthand.
  2. Do not post any reels until you have produced 3 weeks' worth of content.
  3. Study the largest pages and videos that made content for your product.
  4. Copy their video style, change elements based on comment feedback, and add something dramatic or crazy.
  5. Produce 2 to 3 videos per day. Make the full 3 weeks of content before posting. After 1 to 2 weeks, raise the daily count by 1 to 2 videos to avoid shadow bans for spam.
  6. Once all 3 weeks of content are ready, move on to the posting routine.
  7. Post on a strict schedule of 2 to 3 times a day for 3 weeks, for example at 2 pm EST, 6 pm EST and 10 pm EST.
  8. After 1 week, change posting times if you spot time-of-day trends.
  9. Post content immediately, sticking to your content strategy.
  10. Use the hashtags with the highest volume and those your competitors use. Write hashtags inside short sentences that describe the audience and the look, for example a shark slides product tagged with a university students group and a fashionable look.
  11. Read the feedback and comments on every video and look for recurring comment themes.
  12. Reply to as many comments on your videos as possible.
  13. Aim for 10k views in the first 5 days to build momentum.
  14. Use boost post on early content to add initial momentum to your account or to gain followers.

- Target 2 to 3 videos per day on Instagram, with 3 weeks of content finished before the first post, and a posting schedule of 2 to 3 times daily for 3 weeks. [L1367, undated]
- Ramp up daily video output gradually: after 1 to 2 weeks, add 1 to 2 more videos per day, so the account does not look like spam and risk a shadow ban. [L1367, undated]
- Posting reels before 3 weeks of content is ready, or posting identical clips without changes, makes the account look like spam and can lead to shadow bans. [L1367, undated]
- One Reels video reached 18,000 views on day one and about 3.5 million total, so Reels can drive large view counts even for products that will not sell well on them. Cross-post proven TikTok content to Reels once TikTok sales appear. [L1429, 2022]

### Comment-driven engagement and calls to action

- Use a call to comment: the video shows a short clip with a clear reason to act, and the caption asks viewers to comment one keyword to receive a link. The automatic message then delivers the link, which raises comment volume and engagement. Example: a 30-second clip of a product being built, with the caption asking for a comment of one word to get the download link. [L1457, 2025-26]
- Posting organic Instagram content three times per week using AI-generated images and copy had a goal of 5x monthly engagement, starting from 100 engagements per month. [L1509, undated]

### Landing pages

- The hero section (top of the page) of a landing page carries most of the conversion weight, so it must answer the buyer's questions immediately: what they get, the main benefit or desired outcome, why trust them, reduced risk, the offer, and a call to action. The rest of the page is for visitors who still need more information. [L1363, 2023]

### Email and SMS copy

- Email copy and ad copy use the same persuasion mechanisms as product copy, but they are formatted differently. Writers should apply the same building blocks to these formats, adapting length and layout to each. [L1420, 2021-22]
- For welcome emails, the offer must be clear and easy to understand, because strong products sell themselves and a simple discount drives the click. Long product copy is less important than a clear offer line. [L1470, 2025-26]
- Welcome email prompt (use with the placeholders filled in): [L1470, 2025-26]

```
You are an ecommerce email copywriter. Write a welcome email for {BRAND_NAME}, sent to new subscribers who signed up through {SIGNUP_OFFER}. Include a warm greeting, one short sentence about the brand, the offer {OFFER_DETAILS} stated clearly, and one call-to-action button text. Keep the entire email under 200 words, use plain and concise language that is easy to understand, and focus on the offer rather than long product descriptions.
```

### Content mix and formats

- Content should be produced in a mix of content types rather than one format, and the format itself is not fixed: the same idea can be a blog post or a video. Six types are the core of a content production system: how-to, listicle, ultimate guide, best of the interwebs, expert roundup and controversial content. Other formats are allowed when they serve a clear purpose. [L1421, 2021-22]
- Controversial content works because people engage strongly with it. Taking a clear opinion may push some people away, but it ties the rest closer to the brand; staying in the middle makes a brand boring and unnoticed. [L1461, 2021-22]

### Video content for stores

- Video content for an online store works best when it is evergreen, meaning it keeps delivering traffic for years after publishing, rather than being posted only for a short social media feed where the algorithm stops showing it after a while. Evergreen value depends more on how the video is used and distributed than on how it is produced. [L1424, 2021-22]
- Video formats that suit a store include unboxings, how-to videos, listicles, product reviews, customer video reviews and FAQ videos. Unboxing videos filmed from above are a simple format that can attract searches for a popular product. [L1424, 2021-22]
- A reusable content strategy is to write a blog post first and then turn the same content into a video by recording narration that reads the post aloud, publishing the video on the store's content page and on YouTube so both the text and the video can rank and bring traffic. This is a low-effort entry point for a store that already produces written content. [L1424, 2021-22]
- Production quality matters less than openness, honesty and helpfulness for content videos, while the same is not true for video ads. A video should move quickly, skip padding, open with a clear introduction that says what the viewer will learn, and be concise. [L1424, 2021-22]
- A strong introduction tells the viewer what they will get from the video in the first moments, because YouTube rewards videos that viewers watch for a long time. Viewers who leave early lower the ranking of a video, so an intro that explains why to watch to the end matters more than a fast start. [L1424, 2021-22]
- Simple video production workflow for a store owner: [L1424, 2021-22]

  1. Record the raw video or audio without worrying about mistakes, since retakes and bloopers can stay in.
  2. Send the raw files, for example from the phone via cloud storage, to a freelance editor.
  3. The editor cuts out mistakes and pauses and assembles the final video so it flows.
  4. Publish the edited video.

- LED Grow Lights Depot is a reseller drop-shipping store selling grow lights from many brands and does not hold or produce its products. Its YouTube channel had about 22,000 subscribers after roughly two years, with videos earning between about 21,000 and 35,000 views each, and it produced reviews, buyer's guides and top-7 lists. Its videos are often slideshows or product walk-throughs with recorded voiceover, and the descriptions link to products, so the videos drive traffic back to its store. The channel shows that simple, low-budget videos can attract engagement and traffic without expensive equipment. [L1424, 2021-22]
- Video content for a drop-shipping store can be made by a slideshow of product images with recorded narration, which gives a list or guide style video at very low production cost. Descriptions should link to the products shown and to related pages on the store. [L1424, 2021-22]
- Content videos do not need professional production to work. A creator with no prior video experience can start immediately and get better by making videos regularly. [L1424, 2021-22]
- Opening line for a video, said in the first moments before the main content: [L1424, 2021-22]

```
In this video you are going to learn [the specific outcome the viewer gets] and why [the reason it matters to them].
```

### Product review videos

- Structure of a product review video for a store: [L1424, 2021-22]

  1. Show the product and list its features.
  2. Explain its benefits, what it does for the viewer and how it does it.
  3. Say why the viewer should buy it.
  4. Mention drawbacks honestly if there are any.

- For product review videos, choose a popular product that the store sells as the subject. Reviewing a product people already search for makes the video easier to find. [L1424, 2021-22]
