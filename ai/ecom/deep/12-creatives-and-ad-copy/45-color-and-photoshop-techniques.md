### Color and Photoshop techniques

- Mixing a color with tint, tone or shade rather than picking a new color keeps related colors consistent across a design. Muting a bright color by adding gray usually makes it easier to read next to brand colors. [L0754, undated]
- **Photoshop gradient:** The Gradient tool creates an editable gradient on any layer, and clipping it to an object applies it only to that object. Double-click a color stop to change the gradient color. [L0754, undated]
- **Blend modes:** In Photoshop, blend modes change how a layer interacts with the layers under it: Darken shows only the darker parts, Lighten shows only the lighter parts, and Color Burn or Color Dodge create texture effects such as a moon texture on a shape. [L0754, undated]
- **Texture or detail overlay pattern:** A texture image placed above a shape with the blend mode Color Burn adds darkness and detail while keeping the base color. Pattern: base shape + grayscale texture above it + Color Burn blend + mask to limit the effect to the area that needs it. Example you can adapt: a cracked stone texture over a dark badge, and a fabric weave overlay over a sweatshirt product shape. [L0754, undated]

### Color: meaning, mood and balance

**Principles**
- Color sets mood, tone and brand perception, so pick the base color first according to the meaning you want to convey and how you want the brand to be perceived. Choose the color meaning deliberately before building the rest of the palette. [L0659, undated]
- Warm colors (red, orange, yellow) feel energetic and attention-grabbing, while cool colors (green, blue, violet) feel calm, relaxing and cooling. Use this split to decide which family fits the emotional effect of a design. [L0659, undated]
- Use a limited number of key principles when building a palette: choose a color for the brand meaning, use neutral colors as backdrops, control contrast with tints, tones and shades, choose a typography that suits the brand, and test colors and type in action. [L0659, undated]
- Adding white, gray or black to a palette prevents colors from clashing and looking like a rainbow. Adjusting how much white, gray and black you add is one of the most effective ways to balance a design. [L0659, undated]

**Color terms**
- Hue is the base color, and a color can change in lightness through tint (white added), tone (gray added) and shade (black added). Tint, tone and shade are the main tools for adjusting a palette to create balance. [L0659, undated]
- Chroma measures how pure or saturated a hue is, with higher chroma giving more intense colors. Saturation is about removing color, which makes the color look washed out, as opposed to adding white, gray or black. [L0659, undated]
- Hexadecimal color values use six characters, with the first two for red, the middle two for green and the last two for blue. Many page builders, including Shopify page builders, use RGB values with three numbers from 0 to 255 instead. [L0659, undated]

**Individual colors**
- **Red:** Red is an intense attention color that can overwhelm quickly, so use it sparingly as an accent. Brighter reds feel more energetic, while darker reds feel more powerful and elegant. [L0659, undated]
- **Red across cultures:** In Western markets red signals anger, alert, danger and passion, in China it signals prosperity and happiness, and in some Eastern cultures it is worn by brides. Check the target market before using red as a main color. [L0659, undated]
- **Orange:** Orange signals vibrancy, energy, change and creativity, and it draws attention without the aggression of red. Its typical use is for buttons, alerts and clickable elements, and it can look friendlier and more inviting than red. [L0659, undated]
- **Accent colors:** An accent color should appear only in small pieces such as links, buttons or a title detail, not flood the whole page. Used this way it draws the eye to clickable or important items. [L0659, undated]
- **Yellow:** Yellow is the brightest, most energizing and warmest color and is associated with happiness and cheerfulness. Softer or muted yellows feel calm and neutral and are often used as a gender-neutral choice. [L0659, undated]
- **Yellow across cultures:** Yellow is associated with deceit and cowardice in some contexts, and with hope in Egypt, courage in Japan and merchants in India, so meaning varies by country. Darkened yellows and golds can look antique. [L0659, undated]
- **Green:** Green stands for growth, new beginnings, renewal and abundance, and it is strongly linked to wealth and stability. Dark green signals affluence and prosperity, bright green feels energizing, and muted olive green feels natural. [L0659, undated]
- Green can also signal envy or jealousy and a lack of experience. It combines the calming effect of blue with the energy of yellow, which makes it suitable for health, well-being and nature-related designs. [L0659, undated]
- **Blue:** Blue meaning depends strongly on hue and shade: light blues feel refreshing and friendly, bright blues feel energizing, and dark blues signal strength, trust, reliability and peace. Dark blue is therefore a common choice for finance-like or trust-focused brands. [L0659, undated]
- **Purple:** Purple combines red and blue, so it carries both energy and calm. Dark purple signals royalty, wealth and luxury, while lighter purples like lavender feel romantic, and purple is associated with creativity and imagination. [L0659, undated]
- Purple is the mourning color for widows in Thailand, so check cultural meaning before using it in a target market. [L0659, undated]
- **Black:** Black is the strongest neutral color and is associated with power, elegance and formality, while negative associations include mystery, death and mourning in many Western countries. Black is widely used for typography and for making images stand out against it. [L0659, undated]
- **White:** White stands for cleanliness, purity, simplicity and health care, and it works as a neutral backdrop that lets other colors shine. White is worn by brides in the West and represents morning in much of the East, so consider the target audience. [L0659, undated]
- **Brown:** Brown is hard to balance because of its lightness and darkness, and it is associated with earth, wood, stone, reliability and warmth. Avoid using heavy brown in large amounts, since it can make a design look heavy. [L0659, undated]
- **Ivory and beige:** Ivory is a tinge of white with cream, and beige is a tinge of brown; both act like grays or neutrals and are used in a similar way to black and white in different shades. [L0659, undated]

**Neutrals and grays**
- Neutral colors take on the characteristics of the colors around them: gray next to blue feels cooler and gray next to red feels warmer. Use neutrals as backdrops that are shaped by the accent colors placed beside them. [L0659, undated]
- Charcoal gray instead of pure black softens contrast on screen, making white text easier on the eyes while keeping strong readability. Light grays can replace whites and dark grays can replace blacks in layouts and typography. [L0659, undated]
- Gray text on white backgrounds is easier to read and softer than black on white, but contrast must still meet web accessibility standards so that users with weaker eyesight can read the content. [L0659, undated]
- For section backgrounds, use an extremely light tint of the palette color rather than a dark gray, because heavy gray behind colored elements looks jarring. A near-white tinted background separates sections gently against the text. [L0659, undated]

**Palette patterns**
- **Analogous:** Analogous palettes use colors that sit next to each other on the color wheel and feel harmonious. Beginners should use analogous palettes, adjusting tint, tone, shade and chroma, because they are the easiest way to build balanced designs. [L0659, undated]
- **Monochromatic:** Monochromatic palettes use one hue with different tints, tones, shades and chroma levels. They are easy to build and reliable for clean designs. [L0659, undated]
- **Complementary:** Complementary colors sit opposite each other on the color wheel and create strong contrast. Use one as an accent and not in equal amounts, and tone it down with gray, white or darkening to avoid a jarring result. [L0659, undated]
- Complementary colors must be used carefully, since a complement of a pale pink can clash badly. Darkening or muting the complementary color, or using it only as a small accent, can make it work. [L0659, undated]
- Blue and orange are a complementary pair that works well when orange is kept as an accent against blue. Keeping the design built on similar colors first and only then taking bold contrast steps produces the best results. [L0659, undated]
- **Triadic:** Triadic color schemes use three colors evenly spaced on the color wheel and are very difficult to get right, so beginners should avoid them. [L0659, undated]

**Build a palette:**
1. Choose a base color that fits the meaning you want to convey.
2. Pick neighboring colors on the color wheel or variations of the same color.
3. Use the color wheel in the design software to add white, gray or black.
4. Move the color up (towards white) to lighten, down (towards black) to darken, and diagonally from top right to bottom left to add gray.
5. Test the palette until it looks balanced. [L0659, undated]

**Tool:** Adobe Color can create palettes from color wheel options such as analogous, monochromatic, triad and complementary, and it saves palettes as color schemes. Search by a theme such as nature to generate starting palettes. [L0659, undated]

**Canva palette suggestions:** Canva can read the colors of a placed photo and suggest matching palettes, so product or brand colors can be applied to a layout quickly. Use the suggested palette as a starting point and adjust it to your brand colors. [L0343, 2023]

**Buttons and backgrounds**
- Button colors should stand out against the background and be darker and more saturated than surrounding colors, while avoiding pure black because it is too heavy. A call-to-action such as "Shop now" should be the most noticeable element to support visual hierarchy. [L0659, undated]

**Apply the palette in practice (procedure):**
1. Start with a color palette in Adobe Color or a similar tool.
2. Download a font pair, such as a serif title font and a sans-serif body font.
3. Place a title, subtitle and body text in the design software and set sizes.
4. Use dark text on white backgrounds, keeping the text readable without being too contrasting.
5. Lighten or darken colors on dark backgrounds, such as adding white to make text punch out.
6. Test the palette in action before committing. [L0659, undated]

**Start simple:** Build from similar colors first and only then take bold contrast steps. [L0659, undated]

### Typography

- **Google Fonts:** Google Fonts provides free font families and pairings that can be downloaded as full families. Use them to test typography pairings alongside a color palette before building designs. [L0659, undated]
- **Serif and sans-serif:** Serif fonts have small decorative strokes called serifs and often look traditional or formal, while sans-serif fonts are cleaner, simpler and more minimalistic. Sans-serif fonts are usually more readable for body text, while serifs often work well for titles. [L0659, undated]
- **Font pairs:** Font pairs can combine two serifs, two sans-serifs, or a serif with a sans-serif, and a common pattern is a serif for titles with a sans-serif for paragraphs. Each font has its own voice, so choose fonts that match the desired look and feel. [L0659, undated]
- **Choose a font pair (procedure):**
  1. Identify the niche or desired mood.
  2. Search blogs, font library catalogs or design platforms for font pairings that fit that niche.
  3. Download and test the pairs next to each other.
  4. Adjust font weight, size and layout until the look matches the intended feeling. [L0659, undated]
- Use a plain sans-serif font such as Open Sans for readable ad copy, and replace it with your brand font if you have one. [L0341, 2023]
- Use a line height of 1.5 times the font size for good readability and a balanced layout. Most website builders apply this automatically, but adjust it when text feels cramped after increasing the font size. [L0659, undated]
- Font weight affects importance and mood, with heavier weights usually used for titles. Letter spacing should be wide enough for easy reading and should not be too condensed. [L0659, undated]
- Ascenders are the upper parts of letters like f, h and capitals, while descenders are the lower tails of letters such as g and p. Knowing these terms helps adjust line height and text spacing. [L0659, undated]
- In Canva, the logo and headline fonts should match the store: use the heading font for headlines and the body font for body text. [L0992, 2025-26]
- Change any all-caps template text so it fits the brand style. [L0992, 2025-26]

### Text legibility on mobile and over images

- **Mobile first:** Text placed on an image shrinks when the ad is shown on mobile, so keep headlines large and short. Most people see ads on phones, and a very wide banner gets squeezed into a small, thin strip. Square or near-square images keep overlaid text readable. Check text legibility at phone size. [L0341, 2023; L0343, 2023]
- **Gradients behind text:** To keep overlaid text readable, place a white gradient behind dark text and a dark gradient behind white text. [L0341, 2023; L0343, 2023]
- **Dark and light backgrounds:** On dark backgrounds, lighten the text color by adding white or more color, because dark text on a dark background is unreadable. On light backgrounds, darken the text instead of using pure white to keep it legible. [L0659, undated]
- Text over a busy photo or video in Canva:
  1. Align the text block to the left edge so it looks clean.
  2. Change the text color to white (or black on light backgrounds).
  3. Add an effect such as a lift or a dark drop shadow, then blur it slightly and lower its opacity if it looks heavy.
  4. If the text is still hard to read, add a gradient graphic (search "gradient" in Canva graphics) that goes from a solid color to transparent behind the text, using black for white text and white for black text, and lower its opacity if needed. [L0755, undated]
- **Mistake:** Text placed over an image with both very light and very dark areas, such as bright sky above dark buildings, becomes hard to read. Before adding text, check the tones under the text block and fix the problem with a gradient or shadow if the contrast is low. [L0755, undated]
- **Text limit:** Keep text under about 20% of the image. Above that, Meta still runs the ad but limits its reach or raises the cost per result. [L0649, 2019]
- Snapper's grid feature shows how much of the image the text covers, so you can check the text limit before publishing. [L0799, 2021-22]

### Packaging and print

**Packaging copy prompt:**

```
I'm making custom packaging for [PRODUCT, for example a fleece legging] shipped in a [PACKAGING TYPE, for example a poly mailer, 10 x 13 inch]. Attached are inspiration images of other packaging designs and our brand guideline. Based on our brand voice, give me options for words to print on the packaging in these groups: 1) inside flap or inner message, 2) texture or product callouts that describe benefits in plain, non-technical words, 3) short playful brand-voice lines. Keep every line short, on brand, and suitable for [SEASON OR THEME, for example the cold season]. Then recommend one line for the front and one line for the side, with a short reason for each.
```
[L0884, 2025-26]

**Mailer decision case:** The lesson chose a 10 x 13 inch poly mailer for leggings and a fleece product because apparel ships well in this format. The packaging design was made for that exact size, and the same process applies to any product type. [L0884, 2025-26]

**Free-gift ebook visual system:** A free-gift ebook can use a consistent visual system: a simple motif repeated on the cover and in the diagrams, a serif font for headings, a sans-serif font for body text, and a monospace font for small labels. This gives a calm, editorial look that suits a health or wellness topic. [L0862, 2026]

### Imagery, emotion and brand fit

- Emotional messages can be carried by imagery instead of long text, so image and video creatives can lead with feeling and leave the specifications for later. Audiences differ in how they think, some in words and some in pictures, so combining copy with strong visuals reaches more buyers. [L0611, 2021-22]
- Use 4:5 for feed image and video ads, or 9:16 for TikTok-style vertical video. These are the recommended formats for image and video ads. [L0648, undated]
- **Applying brand guidelines to a multi-format template:**
  1. Select all artboards in the template and copy them (Cmd+C).
  2. Open the brand guidelines document and paste the artboards there, so all work happens in one file.
  3. Select an element, then pick the brand color from the Selection colors panel; click See all colors if the palette is needed.
  4. Shift-select the artboard titles to apply the same color change to all selected artboards at once.
  5. Select all headline text and switch it to the heading font; select all body text and switch it to the body font.
  6. Change any all-caps template text so it fits the brand style.
  7. Drag the matching logo version (for example the black or black-and-white logo) from the logo panel onto each artboard and align it with the center tool.
  8. Duplicate the brand photos, use Remove Background for a cut-out effect, and adjust brightness and saturation in Photo Filter to match the brand energy.
  9. If a photo crops off the model's head, use Remix > Extend Photo with 9:16 vertical format, then regenerate and remove the background if needed.
  10. Move the photo so the product is clearly visible instead of the face.
  11. Rewrite all copy using the brand voice in ChatGPT, then paste the chosen options into each artboard.
  12. Review each draft for brand fit, then repeat the same process for the next template. [L0992, 2025-26]

### AI-generated and edited images

- AI-generated images are often restrictive to edit after generation, so the layout of text and elements may need to be changed in a design tool. Canva can edit an image generated by an LLM or image model, so the workflow does not need to restart in the chat each time. [L0882, 2025-26]
- **Edit an AI-generated image in Canva:** Open the image in the editor and use Edit image, then Magic Layers to split it into editable layers. Layers can then be removed, moved, swapped for another picture, or given new headline text. [L0882, 2025-26]
- Canva Magic Layers separates an AI-generated image into editable layers. It is used to remove elements, reposition them or replace headlines and offer text without regenerating the whole image. [L0882, 2025-26]

### Seasonal and holiday ads

**Canva steps for a holiday Instagram ad:**
1. Create a design with the Instagram Post size 1080×1080.
2. Paste the product image with a white background.
3. Remove the image background with Magic Background Remover.
4. Add a seasonal background from Magic Media, for example a spooky Halloween background.
5. Add the text and apply Text Effects. [L1105, 2024]

**Canva is an online design tool for building social media ad images.** Its Magic Background Remover and Magic Media features can cut out a product photo and add a seasonal background quickly. [L1105, 2024]

**Procedure for a holiday-themed ad and email image in Canva:**
1. Click Create a design and choose the Instagram post template, a square at 1080 x 1080 pixels. Use it for Facebook, Instagram, and Google remarketing.
2. Paste a product image from your store into the design and enlarge it.
3. Click BG remover to remove the white background. This feature requires Canva Pro.
4. Scroll to Magic Media and enter a prompt such as "spooky Halloween backgrounds" with the filmic style. Click generate image, which returns four variations.
5. Choose a background, go to Layer, select Send to back, and enlarge it to fill the canvas.
6. If the background competes with the product, lower its transparency.
7. Click Text, add a text box, search for a themed font such as Halloween, and set it large.
8. Paste the promotion text, for example "10% off Halloween", and use Effects > Background for a behind-text box.
9. Click an element, then Animate, and choose a motion such as moving up.
10. Click Share and download as an MP4 for Facebook remarketing ads, or as a GIF to embed in emails so the animation plays in the inbox. [L1115, 2024]

### Layout and visual hierarchy

- Keep creatives clean, clear and simple, because simplicity beats finesse. Unpolished design is acceptable only when it is deliberate or native to the platform, such as memes that match the style of the feed. [L1348, 2023]
- Learn five basic graphic design skills for ads: spacing (separating elements), alignment, grouping related items, color and balance. Spacing and grouping are among the most common weaknesses in beginner creatives. [L1348, 2023]
- Use a larger opening block or a bold highlight to start the reader. Large opening type draws readers in, and smaller body type later does not stop them once they are reading. Use a bold guarantee or a key claim as the lead-in. [L1366, undated]
- Leave no wasted space in direct response ads. Every line should carry a selling point, and avoid borders or decorative blank areas. Include a coupon as a reminder device when the reader needs to act later. [L1261, undated]
- Returns scale with ad space. Halving a full-page ad's copy area on the same page roughly doubles cost per reply, and doubling the space roughly doubles replies. Justify any extra space or larger type with a traced test before buying it. [L1261, undated]
- Use normal, readable body type in the style of newspapers and magazines. Larger type does not increase response and costs more; louder, bigger type adds cost without adding sales. [L1261, undated]
- Use graphic devices that add voice to the copy, not decoration: bullets, numbering, underlining, bold type, varied type, simulated handwriting, boxes, asterisk lines, yellow highlighter overprint, shaded screens, photographs with text wrapped around them, and many subheads. The copywriter should choose which devices to use and where, not leave that to the artist. [L1370, undated]
- Default to a clean layout for ads. Illustrated letters with photos, charts, cartoons and deliberately messy handwriting can outperform neat letters in split tests, so test a busy version before rejecting it as unprofessional. [L1348, 2023; L1261, undated; L1370, undated]
- For tall, narrow display formats, stack two product images with the same copy in the middle to use the extra space. This avoids empty areas in the layout. [L1169, 2021-22]

### Color and typography

- Use color only where tests show it pays, such as for food, dessert or fruit that looks more appealing in color. In general, black and white ads perform as well or better. Color chosen to be attractive for its own sake rarely pays. [L1261, undated]
- Pick fonts that fit the brand. Niche-specific fonts (for example Font Space for themed fonts, checked for commercial use) or Google Fonts for readability are both options. [L1363, 2023]
- Keep text short and well-spaced. Avoid squeezed lines and cramped line heights. [L1363, 2023]
- Comment-card style reference (Facebook-style comment card in the lesson): light gray background, red-accented text and links, and a soft drop shadow, with the card slightly overflowing the image edge for depth. Open Sans Bold was suggested to match the look, but this was an untested guess, so compare against a real reference before copying the style. [L1348, 2023]
