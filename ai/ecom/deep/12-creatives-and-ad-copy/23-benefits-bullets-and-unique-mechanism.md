## Benefits, bullets and unique mechanism

### Start from the Outcome the Customer Wants

- Identify and emphasize the outcome the customer wants to achieve, not only the product's features or technical benefits. For a gaming keyboard, customers do not primarily want a wireless phone holder. They want to be sharper, beat competitors, keep a higher kill-to-death ratio, and outdo opponents. Build the ad's message around these desired outcomes first, then position the product as the solution. Outcome-focused ads drive desire and conversions; feature-focused ads do not. [L0067, 2023]
- Lead with the main benefits of the product, meaning what the customer can do with it, rather than listing features first. [L0206, undated]
- Customers buy what a feature does for them, not the feature itself. A bare feature, such as a built-in charging station, has little meaning until the buyer sees the result it gives them. [L0612, 2021-22]
- Pair every relevant feature with a matching desire and state what it means for the customer in plain terms, such as *saves time and effort* or *lets you stay relaxed*. Focus on the few features that truly separate the product from competitors rather than listing everything. [L0516, 2023]
- Each feature in a "what it does" list needs a matching meaning line that shows what changes in the customer's home, time, or comfort. A feature alone gives proof; the meaning line gives the reason to buy. Keep feature claims short and visible, and always state the effort saved, such as minimal effort or quick cleaning, because customers respond to less work as much as to results. [L0392, undated]
- Shoppers compare products by features, especially online with several tabs open, so features must still be listed. Omitting a real feature leads buyers to assume the product lacks it, which makes competitors look better. [L0612, 2021-22]
- Never leave the meaning of a feature for the reader to work out. Spell out exactly what it means for the buyer in a way they can picture. [L0612, 2021-22]
- Keep a technical term unchanged when it is the real name of the technology, but add a plain-language benefit after it. A technical name alone sounds impressive but means nothing to an uninformed buyer, so always follow it with its effect. [L0612, 2021-22]
- Quantified technical claims should show the result to the buyer. A carbon-ceramic low-EMF heater producing infrared heat 5x more effective than standard infrared sources is best written as: *uses low-EMF carbon-ceramic heating that gives 5x more effective infrared heat than standard sources, so you spend less time in the sauna and more time on your day.* The star footnote on a comparative claim such as *5x more effective than standard\** must stay with the claim. [L0612, 2021-22]
- A benefit that only restates a quantified claim, such as *five times more effective*, is still too abstract. Add a power benefit that converts it into time, money, or daily life, because buyers often feel they lack time. [L0612, 2021-22]
- Non-unique features still belong in the list. Place unique features and the unique mechanism first, then include standard features that have real value, since buyers compare across products. [L0612, 2021-22]

### Feature-to-Benefit Patterns

- **Bullet formula: Feature + Benefit + Benefit of That Benefit.** Example:
  *KEEP THEM ON YOUR PAGES LONGER! [Feature: longer engagement] Bounce rates indicate engagement quality, boost engagement with these engineered questions to instantly increase conversion rates! [Benefit: more conversions]* [L0196, 2020+]
- **Interactive feature storytelling.** Start with a benefit statement or what the product does, then describe features as vivid, interactive experiences the user will have. Why it works: it creates sensory imagery and shows the product in action, making it tangible and desirable.
  - *These shoes learn your gait. Run, walk, or stand and watch them adjust cushioning automatically with every movement throughout your day.*
  - *This mug knows your temperature. Pour your coffee and feel it maintain the perfect warmth for hours, cooling just as you need it.* [L0206, undated]
- **Use case lead with feature checklist.** Start with the primary benefit and emotional payoff, state relevant use cases, then list features with checkmarks. Why it works: it connects to customer needs immediately, then validates with clear, scannable proof.
  - *Save 10 hours a week on admin work. Our scheduling software handles: ✅ Multi-timezone appointment sync ✅ Automated reminders and confirmations ✅ Integration with your calendar apps ✅ Client self-service booking.*
  - *Build muscle faster with precision. Our workout tracker includes: ✅ Form analysis with video feedback ✅ Personalized progression algorithms ✅ Recovery recommendations based on performance ✅ Offline workout access on mobile.* [L0206, undated]
- **Benefit-feature pairing with visual indicators.** List each benefit with a supporting feature or proof point, using bullets or checkmarks, structured as *benefit description + supporting detail*. Why it works: scannable lists let different customers find their own reason to buy, and the pairing anchors abstract benefits in concrete features.
  - *Instant relief + cooling technology; All-day comfort + invisible design*
  - *Reduces pain in 7 days + clinically tested; Works with your body + no medications required* [L0313, 2026]
- **Effort-equivalence benefit pattern.** Translate abstract benefits into concrete ones by comparing the effort or outcome of using the product with a familiar, quantifiable activity or baseline the audience already understands. Why it works: customers grasp benefits faster when they can imagine doing something they already know.
  - *20 minutes with our app saves the same time as a two-hour coffee meeting.*
  - *Using our template takes as much time as reading three emails.* [L0321, undated]
- **Dimensionalized benefit pattern: [feature] so that [practical benefit] and you can [emotional outcome].** It turns a feature into a practical result and then into a feeling the reader can picture, which is what triggers emotion.
  - *Double-walled steel keeps lunch warm until noon so that you can skip the canteen queue and enjoy a relaxed break.*
  - *Lumbar support that adjusts to your posture so that your back stays comfortable after eight hours at the desk and you can leave work feeling fresh.* [L0396, 2021-22]
- **Body copy order under a performance headline.** Follow this fixed order: (1) a question that names what sets the product apart; (2) the specific material or technology behind it; (3) concrete equipment or spec details in plain language; (4) one practical convenience or comfort feature; (5) the control options. Every claim in the body copy must be backed by evidence, and health results are left out when the product is in a health or finance space. [L0416, 2021-22]
  - Two new examples in the same pattern. All figures are placeholders that need real evidence.
  - *What sets the Ridgeline 20V Drill apart from cheaper cordless drills? Its brushless motor and two-speed gearbox give steady torque on long runs. The 21-piece bit set and magnetic tray keep every job organized, and a battery indicator on the handle shows when to swap packs.*
  - *What makes the Lumen Desk Lamp different from other desk lights? Its adjustable 3,000 K to 6,500 K color range lets you match daylight at any hour. A touch dimmer and a USB-C port add convenience without extra setup.* [L0416, 2021-22]

### Power Bullets

- **Rule.** State the feature first, then follow it with the benefit, and take the benefit further by describing a concrete picture of how it will be experienced in real terms. [L0612, 2021-22]
- **Power bullet pattern.** Name the feature, state the practical benefit it gives the buyer, then add a short emotional clause that addresses a likely fear or doubt (for example, reassurance for a buyer with no prior experience) and produces relief, security, or comfort.
  - *Installs in about one hour, so you can enjoy it as soon as possible, even if you have never installed one before.*
  - *Heats up in 10 minutes, so you can start your session without waiting around while you get ready for your day.* [L0612, 2021-22]
- **Steps to write a power bullet for a product feature.**
  1. Write the feature exactly as the product has it, keeping the technical name if that is the real name of the technology.
  2. State the practical benefit that this feature gives the buyer.
  3. Describe in real terms what the buyer will be able to do or experience because of that benefit, so they can picture it.
  4. If the buyer may doubt or fear something about the feature, add a short clause that removes that doubt and gives relief, security, or comfort.
  5. Read the bullet and check that the buyer does not have to work out its meaning on their own. [L0612, 2021-22]
- **Example: reservations mode.** A reservations mode that lets the buyer schedule the product to switch on up to 36 hours ahead can be written as a power bullet: *it is ready at the time you choose, so you can step in after a long day without waiting for it to heat up, even if you plan the session a day and a half in advance.* Keep the 36-hour figure exact, because a precise number makes the feature believable and lets the buyer picture the schedule. [L0612, 2021-22]

### Structuring the Feature List

- Structure the feature list as bullets that each pair a feature with a benefit and an emotional image, not as bare feature statements. Keep the primary promise, story, and unique mechanism before the feature bullets. [L0612, 2021-22]
- A standard feature list that only names features, such as tablet controls, reservation modes, warranties, and installation time, gives the buyer nothing to picture and misses the chance to explain value. Avoid publishing bare feature lists on product pages and in ads. [L0612, 2021-22]
- A bulleted list is the preferred presentation for features and benefits because it separates them visually from body text and is easy to scan. Paragraphs with subheadings are an acceptable alternative if they suit the page. [L0612, 2021-22]
- Bullet points for technical products can be longer than usual. Break long bullets into shorter paragraphs inside the bullet so they remain readable; length itself is not a problem. [L0461, 2021-22]
- Extra features in a product copy's bullet list serve three jobs at once: they let the buyer compare the product with others on the market, they reinforce the power benefits, and they give the logical argument for the purchase. Choose each extra feature for at least one of these jobs. [L0461, 2021-22]

### Role of Bullets and Fascinations

- The core function of bullets and fascinations is to keep prospects reading continuously through the entire message. If bullets bore the reader, they start skimming or stop reading, which directly causes lost sales. Weak links in bullet sections are often the deadliest vulnerability in copy because they are easy to spot and escape from. [L0068, undated]
- Bullets and fascinations do several jobs at once: they stimulate curiosity and intrigue, seduce the prospect to keep reading, build desire through revealed secrets, seize and hold attention, improve readability, add variety to prevent monotony, crush the headline block by generating dozens of headline possibilities, and create multiple selling angles on one piece of information. [L0068, undated]
- Dull, repetitive bullets or fascinations that lack intrigue or curiosity are a critical weak point. They cause the prospect to skim and scan instead of reading fully, which is one step away from the prospect putting the letter down or throwing it away. Systematically weed out weak links in bullet sections, because they cause loss of readership and ultimately loss of sales. [L0068, undated]
- Roughly 80% of promotions for certain products and markets are built almost entirely on strong bullets and fascinations rather than long body copy. This is particularly true for book promotions, large mail packages, and certain online promotions. [L0068, undated]
- One direct mail control featuring roughly 80% bullets and fascinations was mailed profitably for over 5 years, defeating previous controls, which indicates strong long-term performance of bullet-heavy copy. [L0068, undated]
- When you have a series of benefits, steps in a procedure, or other copy points, group them using bullets, numbers, letters, or other formatting. This adds clarity and impact to the message structure. Clayton often uses this approach to organize multiple related benefits or steps in a process. [L0068, undated]

### Research and Volume

- **Gene Schwartz research approach for bullet writing.**
  1. Read the source material multiple times, highlighting key points each time.
  2. Compile the notes into a comprehensive document (60-70 pages of distilled information is typical).
  3. Organize the research information thematically.
  4. Begin filling in the copy structure, creating bullets and fascinations as they naturally emerge from understanding the material. [L0068, undated]
- **Parris Lampropoulos selection approach.**
  1. Write approximately 700 fascinations covering every shocking, fascinating, and emotionally stirring piece of information in the research.
  2. Select only the strongest approximately 100 for the final package (a 7:1 ratio).
  3. This selection guarantees exceptional variety and quality in the bullet sections, preventing the reader from skimming due to mediocre bullets.
  4. Arrange the selected fascinations throughout the package in the most important positions: front cover, cover deck, and back cover teasers. [L0068, undated]
- **Example of the 7:1 ratio.** Parris Lampropoulos writes approximately 700 fascinations during research and writing, then selects approximately 100 for the final package. This ensures only exceptional material makes it into the final copy and creates substantial variety in the bullet sections, making them more intriguing and enjoyable to read throughout the promotion. [L0068, undated]
- **Write many more bullets than you will use.** The elite strategy is to write 7x more bullets than will appear in the final draft (for example, 700 written fascinations, selecting down to 100 for the promotion). This ensures only exceptional bullets make it into the final copy, selected from the cream of the crop rather than mixing exceptional and mediocre bullets. [L0068, undated]
- **Archive what works.** Create an archive of every bullet or fascination that performs well or generates strong response. When similar bullets are needed for different products, audiences, or platforms, check the archive first. High-performing bullets often work across applications when the core benefit remains relevant to the audience. [L0068, undated]

### Quality Checks for Each Bullet

- **The Makepeace Makeover: three-step bullet improvement.**
  1. Add specificity by replacing vague claims with exact numbers, mechanisms, or credibility signals.
  2. Add intrigue and curiosity by choosing one of the 21 bullet formulas that creates tension, questions, or hidden information.
  3. Add benefit clarity by ensuring the reader immediately understands why this information matters to his life or business.

  Attack each piece of information from 2-3 different angles using different formulas, then select the strongest version. [L0068, undated]
- **Specificity separates believable bullets from generic claims.** Numbers, percentages, and specific mechanisms signal research and analysis. *How to profit from stocks* is flat and unconvincing. *307% profit from gold stocks using one source Buffett's Bull Market X-Factor* includes a mechanism (the named strategy), specificity (307%, not *up to 500%*), and credibility (a referenced expert). [L0068, undated]
- **Name the mechanism in bullets.** Revealing the precise mechanism by which the prospect will achieve the benefit (a proprietary system, a specific nutrient, a technology, a supplier relationship, or a process) is very powerful in saturated markets. It differentiates the claim by showing a unique means or system, and it introduces benefit, credibility, and intrigue at once. [L0068, undated]
- **Emotional triggers allow less proof.** When an emotional trigger aligns with what the prospect desperately wants (sexual desire, financial security, fear of missing out), a bullet can work with significantly less proof and specificity than normal, because desire overrides skepticism. Balance this with sufficient specificity in the other bullets of the same section. [L0068, undated]
- **Find the emotional goldmines.** Identify the prospect's core emotional desires or fears, such as financial security, sexual desire, or fear of missing out, and build bullets that target those emotional centers for maximum resonance. [L0068, undated]
- **Gut-level filter.** Use *gut-level, greed-tickling impact* as the filter when selecting bullets for the final copy. A bullet that creates an immediate visceral sense of desirability has significantly higher conversion power. [L0068, undated]
- **Use colloquial language and strong verbs.** Colloquial expressions and strong action verbs communicate more meaning in fewer words than formal language, which makes bullets more impactful and memorable. [L0068, undated]
- **No exclamation marks.** Exclamation marks signal hype and decrease response rates in bullet copy. Use other punctuation, such as colons, which boost response by 50% or more. [L0068, undated]
