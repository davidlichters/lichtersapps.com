## Video production and editing

### Phone filming: camera and resolution

- A modern smartphone is enough to shoot Facebook ad video. Learn the basics of lighting, camera movement and shot technique from free online tutorials or a low-cost course of about $50 before you outsource production. [L0349, 2024]
- A recent smartphone with a 4K camera can produce product footage good enough for organic and paid video. Shoot on the phone and edit on a computer. Expensive cameras are not needed at the start. [L1077, undated]
- TikTok compresses uploads to at least 1080p, so a back camera with good light is enough. 4K is not needed for phone uploads. [L1403, undated]
- Video must be at least 1080p, with 4K preferred. Older advice: do not overthink production. A modern smartphone can produce video as good as a DSLR or a lower-end video camera, and the video does not need depth or movement to work as an ad. [L1238, 2021-22]
- Both rules apply: 1080p is the floor in both sources. Use 4K if the phone offers it, but do not pay for it. [L1238, 2021-22; L1403, undated]
- an iPhone X records 4K at up to 60 fps, which is enough for product shoots, unboxings and talking-head videos. A mid-range recent smartphone (used in 2020) is a sufficient starting camera. [L1424, 2021-22]
- The main difference between a smartphone and a dedicated video camera is the lens. Telephoto and other interchangeable lenses can cost a lot on their own, while current phones already produce high-quality video. [L1424, 2021-22]
- about 80% of the instructor's videos were recorded on a phone. Footage was moved from phone to Google Drive, then downloaded to the computer for editing. [L1238, 2021-22]

### Lighting

- Lighting has the biggest effect on video quality. Shoot indoors with artificial light or next to a window rather than outdoors in direct sunlight. Direct sun produced weaker quality even on an older phone. [L1383, 2022]
- Light matters more than the camera. Control the light on the subject and let the phone camera handle the rest. [L1403, undated]
- Light sources and softening:
  - A ring light, clip-on light or phone light adds light to the subject.
  - A diffuser (umbrella filter) over a light softens it so it is less harsh.
  - If your body blocks daylight, compensate with an extra light source. [L1403, undated]
- Do not rely on the back camera's low quality when the light is poor. The light setup decides the result more than the camera. [L1403, undated]
- dim lighting turns viewers off quickly. Natural daylight in a room is the best light source, but avoid direct sunlight. Without enough natural light, use affordable artificial lights. [L1424, 2021-22]
- for a simple talking-head ad, place the speaker so daylight from a window lights the face. Natural light was enough for good results without a studio. Avoid large or expensive lighting kits as a starting point. [L1238, 2021-22]
- In the smartphone UGC workflow, set up lighting first: a ring light or a cheap LED light, plus daylight from a window. [L1403, undated]

### Production workflows

Choose the workflow that matches the type of video.

**Smartphone UGC production, in order** [L1403, undated]

1. Know the product, its unique features, its benefits and the audience. [L1403, undated]
2. Write the script covering each feature and benefit. [L1403, undated]
3. Set up lighting first (a ring light or a cheap LED light, plus daylight from a window). [L1403, undated]
4. Film the A-roll (the talking-to-camera voiceover) first, using the teleprompter app if needed. [L1403, undated]
5. Film the product showcase shots: hook, pan, zoom and parallax. [L1403, undated]
6. Film the feature and benefit footage that matches each script line. [L1403, undated]
7. Film context and lifestyle shots (establishing shots, props, people using the product). [L1403, undated]
8. Assemble the footage over the A-roll audio in an editor such as CapCut. [L1403, undated]

**Short footage-led ad, in order** [L0343, 2023]

1. Write the script first and decide what each line says. [L0343, 2023]
2. Find footage that matches each line of the script. [L0343, 2023]
3. Cut the footage to the script with plain cuts and remove dead time. [L0343, 2023]
4. Add text overlays that support the key benefit. [L0343, 2023]
5. Add transitions only after the narrative works. [L0343, 2023]
6. Add sound effects, music and polish last. [L0343, 2023]

**Presenter ad with A-roll and B-roll, in order** [L0344, 2023]

1. Pick the background music and the mood first, since the atmosphere shapes the rest of the edit. [L0344, 2023]
2. Write the script and decide the main talking or presenting footage (the A-roll). [L0344, 2023]
3. Cut the main footage into the final narrative order and remove every mistake, pause and filler word. [L0344, 2023]
4. Lay supporting footage (B-roll) over the main track, and only then add product demonstrations and close-ups. [L0344, 2023]
5. Add the music bed, set its volume and fade it out at the end. [L0344, 2023]
6. Generate auto captions, delete the unwanted ones and style the remaining text. [L0344, 2023]
7. Add transitions, animations, effects, sound effects and keyframe motion last. [L0344, 2023]
8. Check that all text and graphics stay inside the safe zone for each placement. [L0344, 2023]

Note: the footage-led ad [L0343, 2023] and the presenter ad [L0344, 2023] order the work differently (music first in the presenter ad). Use the footage-led order when the ad is built from found footage, and the presenter order when a person speaks to camera.

**Principles for the order of work**

- Decide the order on purpose: script and footage decide the ad, text comes after cutting, and effects come last. Adding text before the cut is final wastes time, because wording and timing will still change. [L0344, 2023]
- Place the main talking footage on the main track and put supporting product footage above it, so the spoken narrative stays readable. [L0344, 2023]
- Record the A-roll first, then match each script line to footage, so you know exactly where each scene belongs. Footage can be layered over the voiceover later. [L1403, undated]
- A-roll can be faceless. The voiceover can be recorded by you, a script writer or an AI voice, as long as the script is recorded before the B-roll is filmed. [L1403, undated]
- Start uploading right away. While waiting for equipment such as a ring light, upload drafts and experiment with phone footage, because consistent uploads are needed to learn what performs. [L1381, 2022]
- UGC-style video can look rougher than polished brand content and still work for ads. The core requirement is that the shots clearly show what the product is, its features and its benefits. Beginners can start with this rougher style, and high polish is optional. [L1403, undated]

**Traditional TikTok-style ad from a reference video** [L1381, 2022]

1. Edit the video in Splice. [L1381, 2022]
2. Screen-record the on-screen text and speech of a reference TikTok video. [L1381, 2022]
3. Add matching text and only non-copyrighted sounds in Splice. [L1381, 2022]
4. Export the video to the computer. [L1381, 2022]
5. Upload it in TikTok Ads Manager and run it as a traditional ad. [L1381, 2022]

### Shot list and footage

- Write a shot list before filming that matches the story the ad tells. [L0561, 2023+]
- Film several angles of the product being made and of the product in use. [L0561, 2023+]
- Film more angles of the product than you think you need: a static shot, a slide or pan across a surface, and slow zooms. Keep the product centred and the camera level, so it does not look skewed when cut in as B-roll. [L0344, 2023]
- Main shot types for UGC and ads:
  - Product showcase.
  - Close-up when discussing a specific feature.
  - Use-case shot showing the product in action. [L1425, undated]
- Shot types and their effect:
  - A wide shot shows the setting and context. A close-up isolates a detail or shows facial emotion.
  - Upward-angled cowboy shots make a subject look more powerful; downward-angled shots make it look smaller. [L1427, 2023+]
- B-roll is secondary footage layered over the main narrative to support it, for example a product being made while the presenter talks. Text and shapes can sit on the upper layers too. [L1427, 2023+]
- Establishing shot: open with a wider shot of the setting (for example the bath) before zooming in on the product, so the viewer understands the context. [L1403, undated]
- Props should fit the product's context (for example rubber ducks in the bath). Avoid props that distract from the product, such as bath bombs that create foam or colour. [L1403, undated]
- Test the product's claims on camera, such as waterproofing, by actually doing it, then film that test as a proof shot. [L1403, undated]
- To show built-in lights:
  1. Film the product lit in a normal room. [L1403, undated]
  2. Switch off the room lights and film the product glowing in the dark. [L1403, undated]
  3. Cycle through each light mode as its own shot. Keep the phone steady and use a slow zoom or parallax move for each state. [L1403, undated]
- To prove a connectivity claim such as Bluetooth pairing, start a screen recording on the phone, pull down the quick-settings panel (Android) or Control Centre (iPhone), and open the device connection menu while the product connects. Add this clip next to a close-up of the product's pairing mode. [L1403, undated]
- For a floating product, film a movement shot by following it across the water surface at the same speed as the product drifts. This shows buoyancy in motion rather than in a static frame. [L1403, undated]
- If the home has no suitable location, film in a public place such as a shop aisle, handling the product the way a customer would, or at a pool or beach for water products. Shoot when the location is quiet so the shot stays clean. [L1403, undated]
- Supplier warehouse footage: filming in the supplier's warehouse makes a small brand look larger and differentiates it from competitors selling the same product. Owner-filmed content shows the business as real and established. [L0921, 2022]
- Demonstrations work for physical products: live product demonstrations, how-to tutorials and entertaining presentations. Video is effective for showing benefits and differentiating from competitors, even where it is under-tested in some categories. [L0227, 2014]
- ASMR-style close-ups of products, such as bottle caps or drops, are popular on TikTok on a small budget. 10 to 15 second videos are also popular. [L0561, 2023+]
- When a product must be shown in use but you do not want to appear on camera, hide your face with a mask, film only the body or the back of the head, or use a full costume. [L1186, 2022]
- Video backgrounds strongly affect conversion. A clean, aesthetic, branded background looks more professional than a plain wall and can make the same product convert noticeably better. Use better backgrounds when starting out, even if the product is simple. [L0821, undated]
- Check the background before uploading. A messy or busy background, such as a checkered red-and-white pattern, made a video look old and interfered with the camera. Use a clean, plain setup. [L0764, 2022]

### Camera moves and framing

- Turn on the 3x3 grid in the smartphone camera settings to frame shots. If the grid is unavailable, keep the product in the exact centre of the frame, because the camera is sharpest at the centre. [L1403, undated]
- Do not film the product in a way that hides its main features or leaves it off-centre. Off-centre footage loses sharpness and clarity on the key feature. [L1403, undated]
- Choose the aspect ratio by platform before filming: square or 4:5 for Facebook feed ads, and vertical portrait for TikTok and Instagram Stories. Leave enough space in the frame for text and overlays. [L1403, undated]

**Focus lock** [L1403, undated]

1. Press and hold the screen on the product until the focus lock appears. [L1403, undated]
2. Move the camera without the focus shifting. [L1403, undated]

**Pan shot with a towel slider** [L1403, undated]

1. Place the phone on a towel laid across a surface so it can slide smoothly without sticking. [L1403, undated]
2. Tap and hold the screen on the product to lock focus. [L1403, undated]
3. Slowly drag the phone across the product in one fluid movement. [L1403, undated]

**Level pan** [L1403, undated]

1. Turn the phone upside down on a flat surface. [L1403, undated]
2. Slide it along the base, keeping the lens low so the product is filmed straight-on at a consistent height. [L1403, undated]

**Slow zoom** [L1403, undated]

1. Keep the product centred in the 3x3 grid. [L1403, undated]
2. Zoom in slowly on the product while holding the camera steady. [L1403, undated]

**Parallax shot** [L1403, undated]

1. Walk slowly around the product with the feet placed wide and the body braced. [L1403, undated]
2. Keep the product centred in frame as you move. [L1403, undated]
3. Practise before the final take. It is the hardest shot to keep stable. [L1403, undated]

**Reveal technique** [L1403, undated]

1. Open the clip with the presenter reacting to the product close to the lens. [L1403, undated]
2. Move the phone or body out of the frame to reveal the product as a transition. [L1403, undated]
3. Alternatively, start the reveal in darkness and move into light as the product comes into view. [L1403, undated]

**Common framing and movement mistakes**

- Avoid sloppy handheld pans and zooms. Move slowly, lock focus first, and use a support (towel or the phone base) to keep movement fluid. [L1403, undated]

### A-roll and script recording

**A-roll recording** [L1403, undated]

1. Set the phone to the back camera with good light and frame yourself in shot, or flip to the front camera. The back camera is lower quality but usable when the light is good. [L1403, undated]
2. Use a teleprompter app to read the script. [L1403, undated]
3. Record the full voiceover. [L1403, undated]
4. Write down which footage should go over each line of the script. [L1403, undated]

- For scripted videos, write the full script word for word beforehand. Read it while recording, looking at the script and then at the camera. The editor cuts out the glances at the script. This also removes hesitations and filler words such as "um" and "ah". [L1424, 2021-22]
- Teleprompter options:
  - A free online teleprompter on a computer or tablet screen, placed right next to the camera, costs nothing.
  - A paid teleprompter attached to the camera, or an iPad held near the lens, are other options. [L1424, 2021-22]
- Record narration separately when the person does not need to appear on camera, so the audio can be combined with images or other video later. Reading the script or blog post aloud works well for this. [L1424, 2021-22]
- Mistakes on camera are normal and should not stop a UGC shoot. They are removed in the edit. [L1423, undated]

### Cutting and trimming

**Cutting the A-roll** [L1423, undated]

1. Drag the footage onto the timeline and find the start of the speech. [L1423, undated]
2. Use B to cut at each unwanted word, pause, "um" or "ah". Then use A to select the unwanted segment and delete it with Delete or Backspace. [L1423, undated]
3. Trim clip edges by dragging their sides to shorten them. [L1423, undated]
4. Go through the whole script, then play the result to check the flow. [L1423, undated]

**Trim and cut clips** [L1427, 2023+]

1. Drag the start or end of a clip to shorten it. [L1427, 2023+]
2. Use the razor tool to cut out a section and delete it. [L1427, 2023+]
3. Hold Shift and click several clips to select them, then move them together. [L1427, 2023+]
4. Close any gap left behind by deleting a section so the cut flows. [L1427, 2023+]

- Cut B-roll to fit the spoken words. Each B-roll clip must be cut so it does not carry unwanted audio. UGC does not need high polish, but a few clean product shots (for example slow pans, zooms and close-ups) make the ad look credible. [L1423, undated]
- Cut dead footage that repeats what viewers already saw. In one example, a 9 to 10 second pouring segment was removed because it was too long. [L1383, 2022]
- Shorten a decent video by trimming dull moments, removing unnecessary frames or speeding up sections. This is a valid way to repurpose it. [L0795, undated]
- Keep the middle of the video fast-paced by changing the clip roughly every 3 seconds, so viewers do not lose interest. The middle carries the education, solution, benefits and features. [L0777, undated]
- On-screen text should animate in and out quickly, so viewers cannot read it all in one pass and must replay the video. This pushes rewatches, but only works when the story itself is compelling. [L1384, 2022]
- Before overlaying B-roll, lower the clip's own volume to zero in the Tools panel. Sound recorded with a shot, such as a phone scratching across a desk, would otherwise play under the ad. [L1423, undated]
- Mistake: dragging a clip onto a higher track in CapCut pushes the clips below it along the timeline, which shifts the whole edit. Duplicate the clip onto the upper track and trim the copy instead, so the main timeline stays in place. [L1423, undated]
