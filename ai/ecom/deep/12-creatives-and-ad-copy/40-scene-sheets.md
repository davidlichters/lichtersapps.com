### Scene sheets

#### Scene sheet fields
Scene-by-scene script template for handing to a creator or editor. Fields per scene: [L1425, undated]
- Scene: a label such as Hook 1, Hook 3, the pain, the solution, or the scarcity point.
- Emotion (optional): the feeling to convey.
- Script: the spoken lines.
- Visual: secondary footage, b-roll or talking-head shot.
- Editing Instructions: the editing style, for example trending on TikTok, or to showcase the mood.
- Start with the ad angles and scripts, then table the full script so creators and team members can follow it easily. [L1425, undated]

#### Twelve-scene creator brief
1. Create a brief for video creators requesting 12 five-second scenes instead of one 60-second video. [L0321, undated]
2. Specify 5 hooks, 5 different use cases, and 3 different calls-to-action. [L0321, undated]
3. Send the product to the creator for free; they keep it after shooting. [L0321, undated]
4. Receive the raw video clips. [L0321, undated]
5. Send the clips to an internal video editor. [L0321, undated]
6. The editor combines the clips into 5 to 10 different final videos with varied hook and scene orders. [L0321, undated]
7. Test the videos on Facebook or TikTok. [L0321, undated]
- This structure yields more content variations for testing than a single video. [L0321, undated]

#### Creative talking point in a brief
- Name the feature or launch news the video must show, then describe the tone and the feeling the viewer should get. [L0579, 2025-26]

### Production sequence and reuse

#### Turning a winning ad into a script
1. Pick the winning creative with the highest website purchases in the Kalodata testing CBO. [L1195, 2026]
2. Paste it into Google AI Studio and transcribe it. [L1195, 2026]
3. Use the transcript as the basis for a custom VSL with the same winning messaging. [L1195, 2026]

#### Reusing formats
- Reuse the same intro across videos to make three uploads a day easier to produce. Repeated intros keep production fast without losing the format that works. [L1383, 2022]

### Calls to action and endings

#### Placement of the call to action
- Add a call to action at the end to raise conversion, but expect it may lower views, because viewers who spot an ad often skip immediately. Test it per product rather than using it by default. [L0764, 2022]
- Include the call to action inside the video itself, not only in the ad text or buttons. [L0488, 2021-22]
- The last 3 to 5 seconds must contain a clear call to action that tells viewers to tap the Shop Now button. Include the store URL in the closing frame. [L0777, undated]
- Conflict between sources: the standard video ad closes with a Shop Now tap in the last 3 to 5 seconds [L0777, undated]. The 90-second talking-head script closes with an invitation to take a look, not a tap-now push [L1140, 2026]. Use the invitation for talking-head scripts that name a private wound; use the Shop Now close for standard video ads, and test per product [L0764, 2022].

#### Retargeting reminder
Script for a collection ad reminder, spoken by the store owner on camera. [L1243, 2017]

```
Hey, thanks for checking out my store. I noticed you never finished checking out, so maybe you got busy. I just want you to know that your cart is still waiting for you. Point up to the link in the ad copy, or point down and say: Click below to go straight back to the product.
```

- Keep it short, face the camera and speak as the owner, because a personal video stands out more than a generic image. [L1243, 2017]

### Business-owner introduction videos
- In a video introduction ad, keep the video to a couple of minutes at most, or even shorter, because it plays directly in the news feed. [L0488, 2021-22]
- Order for a short introduction video from the business owner. It should stay within a couple of minutes: [L0488, 2021-22]
  1. Greet the viewer briefly and say who you are.
  2. State that you are the owner of the business.
  3. Offer help choosing the right product for the viewer's situation.
  4. Say that your team is ready to help and invite the viewer to get in touch.
  5. Briefly describe what the business does and which problems the products solve.
  6. Mention key selling points such as free shipping or easy returns, if they apply.
  7. Repeat the call to action at the end.

### Lesson-style episodes

- Keep each lesson video at about five minutes, which was the chosen length for this format. A consistent length makes the format easier to repeat and to produce regularly. [L0888, 2023+]
- Episode procedure: [L0888, 2023+]
  1. Open with a short greeting and a hook that names the topic.
  2. Read the source text aloud in its original language.
  3. Give the translation.
  4. Explain one clear takeaway for daily life.
  5. Close with a call to watch the next part.
  6. Keep each episode to one passage so the video stays within about five minutes.
