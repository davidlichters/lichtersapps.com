### Upsells, order bumps and downsells

- Show an order bump, a one-time offer, only at checkout. [L0196, 2020+]
- Upsell headlines work when they invite the buyer to add one more unit, with the lower price and a short countdown next to it. The short question makes the add-on feel like a natural extension of the purchase. [L1141, 2026]
- Upsell headline pattern: name the purchase that just happened, offer a second unit or companion product, state a clear discount, and add a time limit. The buyer is still in buying mood and needs only one click. [L1412, 2023]
  - "Your order is confirmed. Add a second set now at 20% off, offer ends in 10 minutes."
  - "Complete your setup with the matching charger at 15% off, this offer closes in 15 minutes." [L1412, 2023]
- Downsell headline pattern: keep the same product but present a last-chance deeper discount after the first decline. This gives a lower-risk way to say yes without re-entering payment data. [L1412, 2023]
  - "One last chance, grab it now at 30% off before this page closes."
  - "Still thinking? Your extra unit is available at 30% off for the next few minutes." [L1412, 2023]
- Upsell template: a limited-time discount at 25% that will not recur. Majority of customers (exceeding 75%) select this upgrade option. Exclusive access is granted for immediate purchase. Add an additional product at the stated price, with immediate savings shown. [L0291, undated]

### Holiday and promotion copy

- Holiday deals and specific offers need different copy. For a specific offer, state the deal clearly in the ad. It has to look irresistible, be something the viewer does not see elsewhere, and be better than what competitors offer. ** [L0799, 2021-22]
- For a holiday deal, do not spell out the discount. Allude to a big, never-to-be-repeated sale that ends soon, such as a sitewide sale or a deal on specific products. If the deal is product-specific, feature that product in the image. ** [L0799, 2021-22]
- Some hype is acceptable for a holiday deal if it is justified, because Black Friday is often one of the biggest sales of the year. Keep the claim tied to the real size of the sale. ** [L0799, 2021-22]
- Holiday deal ad elements: ** [L0799, 2021-22]

| Element | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Image/Video | Use either a high-quality image of the specific product, or an image that signals the nature of the irresistible deal. |
| Headline | Name the specific nature of the deal, or hint at a big offer for holiday-type irresistible deals. |
| Body Copy | Build curiosity and/or urgency. The deal must be genuine and available only for a fixed time. Do not overdo it; say just enough to get the click to the website. |
| Call To Action | Test options such as Save Now, Buy Now, Shop Now and Get Deal. |

[L0799, 2021-22]

- Body copy should create curiosity or urgency without over-explaining. The deal must be real and time-limited, because a fake or open-ended offer loses its urgency. Long-form copy is usually unnecessary for this kind of Facebook deal ad. ** [L0799, 2021-22]
- Deal ad pattern: a short, exciting event headline, then a teaser body line that signals a limited or never-repeated sale, then the call to action. For holiday deals, leave the discount unstated. For specific offers, name the exact deal in the headline or body. ** [L0799, 2021-22]
  - Holiday example: "Summer Sale Is Live: Our Biggest Markdowns of the Year End Sunday. Shop the sale before it closes."
  - Specific offer example: "Save 15% on Your Favorite Pick: 24 Hours Only, Use Code SAVE15 at Checkout. Shop Now." [L0799, 2021-22]
- Black Friday email subject lines follow a scarcity pattern. Each email in the sequence increases scarcity pressure: time remaining decreases, stock quantity decreases and the discount expiration approaches. [L0307, 2023+]
  - "48 hours until launch" (countdown urgency)
  - "We're going live in 24 hours" (final-push urgency)
  - "[Product] is live, limited stock" (depletion urgency)
  - "Last day to save [discount]%" (deadline urgency)
  - "[Discount]% off ends tonight" (time-limit urgency) [L0307, 2023+]
- Holiday ad headlines use the same seasonal formulas as email subject lines. Use a theme word plus a savings promise, or a deadline plus scope such as sitewide or one day only. Keep headlines short so they fit ad placements and stay readable on mobile. [L0548, 2024+]
  - "Black Friday Doorbuster: Up to 50% Off Sitewide"
  - "Christmas Gifts Are Almost Gone: Shop 30% Off Today" [L0548, 2024+]
- Seasonal wordplay formula: pair a seasonal verb or noun, such as decorating, traveling or gifting, with the offer, and keep the benefit obvious. The wordplay makes the headline memorable, and the clear benefit stops it from being read as only a joke. [L0547, 2024+]
  - "Dress Up Your Home for Less This Spring"
  - "Pack Your Cart Before the Summer Break Ends" [L0547, 2024+]
- Exclusivity formula: a "just for you" or early-access frame works only for a real segment, such as email subscribers or past buyers. Name that segment in the ad or landing page, because the reader feels selected. [L0547, 2024+]
  - "Early Access for Our Email List: Spring Deals Open Now"
  - "Your Summer Preview Is Ready for Past Customers" [L0547, 2024+]
- Holiday promotion copy formula: state the offer and the time window first, then add a short hook tied to the holiday, then a clear call to action with the coupon code. The time limit creates urgency, and a themed hook helps the email or ad stand out. [L1115, 2024]
  - Example headline: "Spend the weekend in a jacket, save 15% with code WINTER15 until Sunday."
  - Example primary text: "Three days only: 15% off the whole range with WINTER15. Shop before the offer ends." [L1115, 2024]
- Start from the top-performing holiday email subject lines and ad headlines of the previous month's Q4 material, then adapt them to your own store and offer. [L1115, 2024]
- Link each monthly offer to a holiday, a season or an event, with a short playful hook. This stops the promotion feeling repetitive to people who see many ads a day. Pattern: "Celebrate [event] by [action]" or "Get lucky with [product]". [L1204, 2022]
  - "Start the new year on the right foot with 10 percent off, because your goals should not wait"
  - "Beat the Monday blues with a free gift on every order this week" [L1204, 2022]
- Ad prompt for remarketing ads during a promotion. Full text, unchanged:

```
We'll also run Facebook and Google ads during this promotion, aimed at remarketing audiences so they come back to the store for the deal. Create three sets of ad headlines and primary text for these ads. They should echo the emails but be shorter and written for pay-per-click advertising. Keep each headline within Google's 30-character limit and each Facebook primary text under roughly 125 characters so the key message isn't cut off. Every set must contain the coupon code “happyhalloween” and the dates October 29-31, 2024, and each set should use a different angle.
```

### Consistency across ad, email and landing page

- Keep the offer identical from ad headline to email subject to landing page, so the customer sees one consistent promise along the whole path. A mismatch between ad and email lowers trust and conversion. [L0547, 2024+]
- Keep the offer wording identical across emails, ads and website banners. [L1222, 2025+]
- Use the same offer, coupon code and time window across emails and remarketing ads for each promotion, so the message is consistent for the customer. [L1115, 2024]
- Ad content must match the landing page. An ad that promises an offer or checkout route the store does not show creates a mismatch that hurts conversion. [L0704, 2026]
- Ad copy should match the product description and the landing page. This consistency speeds up the click-through and confirms to the shopper that they landed in the right place. ** [L1169, 2021-22]
- Mismatched ad copy and landing page confuse shoppers after they click. Do not promise one thing in the ad and show another on the page. ** [L1169, 2021-22]
- Use the same message across emails and remarketing ads, so the same people see the same promotion in their inbox and on Facebook, Instagram and Google. The headline of each remarketing ad should match the email subject line of the same holiday. [L1223, 2024+]
- Assets required for each promotion, prepared before it starts: [L1222, 2025+]
  1. Email copy for the full email sequence.
  2. Ad copy for the ads that run during the promotion.
  3. Images for both the emails and the ads.
  4. High-converting email subject lines and ad headlines.
- Ad copy should be taken directly from the email sequence of the same promotion, so ads and emails carry the same message. [L1222, 2025+]
- Holiday asset workflow for each promotion: [L1224, 2025+]
  1. Write the email sequence first, using the gain-logic-fear structure.
  2. Review every email for errors or invented facts (AI can hallucinate) before using it.
  3. Import the reviewed emails into Shopify Email or your email platform.
  4. Generate ad copy from the email sequence, not from scratch.
  5. Generate promotion-specific images that match the offer.
  6. Edit images in Canva if you need to add a logo or text, and resize them for each ad format.
- Ad copy derived from the email sequence: [L1224, 2025+]
  1. Ask for ad copy based on the email copy, for Meta and Google Display remarketing.
  2. For Meta, take the primary text and headline from each email, with the call to action set to Shop Now.
  3. For Google Display, take a headline and a description for each email stage, matched to the days it runs.
  4. Check all copy against the emails and the actual offer before publishing.

### Answering objections

- Use ads to answer objections that appear in comments, such as cheaper hacks people suggest. Read comments for price objections and reframe the product around its benefit. [L1225, 2025]
- A Staged FAQ section addresses common objections and misconceptions, reframes the risks and provides facts to counter them. [L0196, 2020+]

### User-generated content and third-party voices

- When an offer appears in third-party user-generated content, phrase it gently rather than as a direct discount. A direct "get 40% off today" works in brand-made ads. In UGC it looks like a sales pitch. A softer framing such as "two for one as it's their summer sale" fits better. [L1348, 2023]
- Endorsements from a trusted person can strengthen an offer lead. [L0756, undated]

#### Further details

- Place testimonials in the offer between bonuses or after them, not in the credibility section. Case studies and testimonials are reserved for the offer section, while the credibility section uses authority signals such as TV appearances and track record. [L1356, 2019+]

- Write the recap in the offer as a rapid, bullet-driven list. Give each piece of the standard offer and each bonus its own bullet with a short description, assign a dollar value to each bullet to create a total, and then state the total value. Show that total as a large, prominent number, then state the price is far below it. [L1356, 2019+]
