### Store readiness before traffic

- Traffic is brought in only after the site is optimized and has real products, so that visitors who arrive are able to buy. [L0506, 2020-26]
- A store should carry at least 50 products; with only 10 products, the chance that a visitor buys one of them is too low. [L1293, 2014]

### Delivery tiers for large or heavy products

When the product is large or heavy and requires freight shipping, offer two delivery tiers:

1. **Standard:** free or freight shipping to curbside; the customer unboxes and sets up. [L0245, 2020]
2. **Premium:** upcharge; professional installation and setup included. [L0245, 2020]

### Outreach to website owners about broken links

Use this outreach email and follow-up:

```
Subject: Broken link on [page title]

Hi [first name],

While reading your page [URL of their page], I noticed the link to [name of the resource] now returns an error (404). I thought you would want to know, since it is a great resource and the broken link could frustrate your readers.

If you want to replace it, I have a page on [topic of the resource] that fits the same spot: [your URL]. Feel free to use it. If not, no problem at all, I just wanted to help.

Best,
[your name]

---

Follow-up (send a few days later):

Subject: Re: Broken link on [page title]

Hi [first name],

I wanted to follow up on my note about the broken link on [URL of their page]. If [your URL] fits, it is yours to use; if not, I will leave it there.

Best,
[your name]
```

### Door-to-door sampling

Use this script for a trial approach:

```
"Hello, I am visiting you today to offer you a free sample of [PRODUCT_NAME]. I have [NUMBER_OF_OPTIONS] options here, so please choose the one you like best. There is no charge and no obligation to buy. I will come back in [NUMBER_OF_DAYS] days to hear how you liked it, and you can tell me what you think of it at that time."
Do not ask for an order at the first visit. Let the sample carry the claims, and only return to hear the response.
```

### Focus rule

- Choose one idea and push for it. Before a meeting, choose the one outcome you want. At a networking event, choose the one person and the one thing to say to them. This focus usually produces a result more often than covering many goals at once. [L0756, undated]

### Learning copywriting

- Beginners find it very hard to design ads from a blank canvas, so start by imitating well-made examples found on YouTube and social media. Imitating an existing principle is a practical way to learn motion graphics and layout before creating original work. [L1348, 2023]
- Breakthrough Advertising by Eugene Schwartz is a recommended read for beginners learning copywriting for ads, and 100 Million Dollar Offers and Dotcom Secrets are commonly suggested for offer and funnel thinking. Taking notes while reading and turning them into concrete ideas for the store is what makes these books useful. [L1347, undated]
- For anyone who wants to study copywriting in depth, Breakthrough Advertising by Eugene Schwartz is the first book to read. It is widely regarded as the core reference for writing good copy and marketing. [L1523, 2021-22]
  - *Older advice (2021-2022).*
- Avoid buying used copies of Breakthrough Advertising by Eugene Schwartz on Amazon, because sellers often charge far more than the normal price. The book is not cheap even new, so buy it from a legitimate new-book source. [L1523, 2021-22]
  - *Older advice (2021-2022).*
- Breakthrough Advertising by Eugene Schwartz was out of print for a long time and has been republished recently. The new edition can be ordered through a site linked to Brian Kurtz, a longtime marketing figure. [L1523, 2021-22]
  - *Older advice (2021-2022).*

### Copywriting case studies

**Video sales letter method (Eugene Schwartz-style copy system, as described by its author)**

- *Starting point:* Before adopting the method, the author averaged $2 to 3 million in sales per year, with a hit ratio of 20 to 40 percent, meaning only one or two of every five sales letters worked. [L1365, 2019+]
- *First use:* In early 2017 he used the method for the first time in a video sales letter that went live in July 2017, and sales started coming in within an hour; that one letter generated more than $30 million. [L1365, 2019+]
- *Scaling:* He then applied the method to every project, and his copy generated over $120 million in about two years across different offers and products, with his hit ratio rising to 60 to 80 percent. [L1365, 2019+]
- *Output:* His output also doubled, from one full package every two months to one new sales letter per month, and some full packages were finished in as little as two weeks. [L1365, 2019+]
- *Other results:* A crypto offer built on the method brought in over 100,000 new paid names before the platforms banned it. Mentored copywriters also reached multi-million-dollar sales, with one mentee's first letter generating over $5 million in five months. [L1365, 2019+]

**Industrial parts seller (direct mail)**

- A dull industrial parts seller sent 100 pink-and-red letters with the headline about 69 things to do after work when parts arrive on time, plus a catalogue with a sticker reading "BORING BUT NECESSARY". [L1370, undated]
- The mailing produced 22 phone calls and 18 first-time orders, all of which became long-term accounts, worth more than $200,000 in new business that year. [L1370, undated]

**Health charity telethon (sponsorship letter)**

- *Starting point:* a local chapter of a health charity needed corporate sponsors for its annual telethon. Typical nonprofit letters to companies described the charity's own needs. [L1370, undated]
- *Rebuild:* The writer rebuilt the letter around three donor questions: what benefit justifies the cost, who else already chose this cause, and where the money would come from in the budget. [L1370, undated]
- *Offer:* The letter offered sponsorships that required little or no company cash because staff events and customer promotions would raise the funds. [L1370, undated]
- *Results:* One employee promotion raised over $50,000 and a small company's bowl-a-thon raised $5,000. [L1370, undated]
- *Response:* Experts had predicted the letter would fail, and the response rate was only 0.5 percent, but the responders were important new donors. One of them gave $13,000, which covered about half of the local telethon's costs that year, and the campaign was the chapter's most successful fundraising effort by return on investment. [L1370, undated]

**Mortgage broker (first sales letter)**

- *Starting point:* A newly independent mortgage broker, who had recently left a loan officer job, wrote his first sales letter after using a copywriting book and seminar kit. [L1370, undated]
- *Mailing:* He mailed an eight-page illustrated booklet with black text on white paper and handwritten notes in blue ink, at a cost of $1.00 per piece, or $5,000 for 5,000 pieces, and paid for the first mailing with a credit card. [L1370, undated]
- *Results:* Within the first 30 days it brought in $40,000 in fee income from funded loans, and within 60 days the total reached over $75,000, which he reported as a return of 15 times the cost. [L1370, undated]
- *Follow-through:* He kept mailing the same letter, and after four months his bank balance had grown substantially, which he credited to the letter as the foundation of his business. [L1370, undated]

**Wartime candy (product-led insight)**

- Forrest Mars spotted a chocolate pellet in a candy shell in soldiers' field kits during the Spanish Civil War, and he patented his own version in 1941. [L0756, undated]
- The candy entered wartime rations and then reached the general public after the war. [L0756, undated]
- Mars then hired a copywriter for television advertising, and the key insight came from the product itself: it melts in your mouth, not in your hands. [L0756, undated]
- Within four years, Mars was selling one million pounds of the candy per week. [L0756, undated]
