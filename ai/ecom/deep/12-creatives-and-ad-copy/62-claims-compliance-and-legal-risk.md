## Claims, compliance and legal risk

### Ground rules for every claim

- Never publish a statement without backing it with proof. If you claim "lose three kilos in a week," include a customer testimonial or a data point from a clinical trial. If you cite dosage or effectiveness, link to or reference the study. Provide evidence before asking for the purchase. [L0261, 2025-26]
- Check every claim for truth and relevance before publication. Readers who doubt a claim or cannot see how it relates to them stop reading. [L1366, undated]
- Ad creative must reflect the product's real ingredients and results. You may copy a winning structure in format, but the claims must be true for your product, or the ad will fail platform review and lose customer trust. [L0707, 2026]
- The reframe, the proof and the call to action must match the actual offer. A script that promises more than the product delivers makes buyers feel misled after the click. [L1140, 2026]
- Advertise the product accurately, so that buyers who purchase it are the ones who want to keep it. Accurate imagery and descriptions help reduce returns. [L1438, 2023+]
- Imitate the form and structure of researched ads, never their exact wording. Copying ad text word-for-word is plagiarism and illegal copyright infringement. [L0196, 2020+]
- Raise stakes with real, serious risks, not with very unlikely risks presented as likely. Fear-mongering without substance is manipulative and ethically questionable. [L0196, 2020+]
- Review AI-generated ad copy against Google Ads policies before enabling any ad. Google enforces strict rules on health claims, personalised content and protected categories. Non-compliant ads are disapproved or suspended. [L0220, 2025-26]
- Platforms also review the landing page that the ad sends traffic to, not only the ad. Health or financial result promises on that page can lead to disapproval or suspension of the ad account. [L0416, 2021-22]
- Any claim about tax deductibility needs a note that tells the reader to consult a tax advisor. Keep the claim qualified and never promise a specific tax result. [L0340, undated]

### Health, finance and regulated claims

- Never make health, finance or general life-quality claims in ads, such as "earn more money," "improve your health" or "make your life better." Ad networks flag these as potentially misleading, unsupported or misaligned with the landing page, and they regularly lead to account suspension.
  - ** [L0241, 2021-22]
- Claims that get health or finance products into trouble include stating that a product cures a serious disease, or guaranteeing a specific income within a set time. Both are result promises that platforms and regulators review.
  - ** [L0416, 2021-22]
- A health promise with a fixed timeframe, such as "lose 10 pounds in 8 weeks," is high risk unless a peer-reviewed scientific study proves that exact result. Such a study is very unlikely for most small product brands.
  - ** [L0416, 2021-22]
- For products where health claims are not allowed, such as an infrared sauna, do not make health claims in the copy. Let the reader draw health conclusions personally. The aim is to send paid traffic to the page, not to educate on health benefits there.
  - ** [L0580, 2021-22]
- Health or ingredient-harm claims need a verifiable source before launch and must be checked against Meta's ad policies. Claims about attraction, status or body image also carry policy risk. Test them cautiously and keep a proof file for each claim. [L0524, 2023]
- Avoid large claims about health or prevention that tests do not support. Keep the primary headline on benefits readers already want. Errors in health claims can lead to rejection by media and damage trust. [L1261, undated]
- For health-adjacent food products (low-calorie, keto, diabetic-friendly), do not make medical claims about treating diabetes or obesity, and do not promise guaranteed weight loss. Use lifestyle wording instead, such as "supports fitness goals," "macro-friendly" or "low-calorie alternative." [L0876, 2025-26]
- When copy compares a product reading against a recommended limit (for example, an electromagnetic field of 1 mg against a recommended maximum of 3 mg), the figures must come from the manufacturer's testing and be stated as that source's data. Do not turn the comparison into a promise of health or safety outcomes. [L0416, 2021-22]
  - ** [L0416, 2021-22]
- For products outside health and finance, the promise can describe what the product actually does for the buyer. It must be specific: write the how into the promise instead of vague phrases such as "improves your life."
  - ** [L0416, 2021-22]
- An expert presenter with a stated credential, such as a coach or clinician, makes a claim easier to accept. Name the credential early in the ad, but use only credentials that are real and relevant to the claim. [L0524, 2023]
- Tax claims: see the ground rule above. Do not write tax results as promises. [L0340, undated]

### Superlatives, hype and numeric claims

| Claim type | Avoid | Use instead | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unsupported superlatives | "number one," "highest quality" | A best or highest-rated claim only with a measurable proof point next to it, such as the number of reviews | [L0411, 2021-22] |
| Hype | "multiply your life by ten," "ten thousand percent better than everything on the market" | Specific, checkable facts | [L0416, 2021-22] |
| Generic superlatives | "best in the world," "lowest price" | Specific facts that can be checked | [L1261, undated] |
| Numeric certainty | "100% certain," especially in niches where it is implausible (for example the stock market) | A specific figure such as 93%, kept legally defensible | [L1356, 2019+] |

- Generic and superlative statements are discounted by readers and suggest exaggeration. [L1261, undated]
- A 100% claim reads as unbelievable in many niches and increases skepticism. [L1356, 2019+]

### Urgency, scarcity and sale timing

- Fake urgency must never appear in a call to action. Fake sales, fake countdown timers and false stock levels (for example, showing 5 left while 1,000 sit in the warehouse) are dishonest and deceive customers.
  - ** [L0461, 2021-22]
- Repeating a 10% off sale that ends on Sunday and is replaced by another 10% off sale on Monday is effectively a lie, not real urgency. Habitually deceiving customers also tends to spread into other areas of the business.
  - ** [L0461, 2021-22]
- Black Friday: keep creatives and sale banners running for a week or more after the sale ends, so the offer still appears active. The source frames this as slightly shady. Treat it as a judgement call and avoid misleading claims. [L1363, 2023]
  - *Conflict:* [L0461, 2021-22] calls a sale that is repeated back-to-back a lie. [L1363, 2023] allows banners to stay up a week after a sale ends. Keep the banner only if the offer genuinely remains available, and never present it as a new deadline.
- Aggressive "breakthrough" claims combined with urgency can get ads and offers banned by ad networks. One source reports that a cryptocurrency offer written this way brought in over 100,000 new paid names before Google and other networks banned it. Check each network's policies and keep claims defensible before launch. [L1365, 2019+]

### Proof, reviews and credentials

- Use only product reviews that the supplier has given you permission to use. Never import reviews from other websites or from Amazon.
  - ** [L0671, 2021]
- Amazon reviews must not be copied and pasted into ads. Amazon treats them as genuine customer reviews and may penalise sellers who misuse them. A copied review can also send viewers to Amazon, where the product is often cheaper. [L1348, 2023]
- Ads with fake comment screenshots carry a risk of being caught by platforms or competitors, with consequences. A lower probability of detection does not make such practices risk-free. Using copyrighted footage or others' ads as hooks can also lead to takedown demands or legal claims. [L1348, 2023]
- Celebrity or famous-name mentions (for example, "used by a famous person" or "an expert credited the product for results") draw attention. Check that the link is real and that the product is genuinely connected to the person or event. [L1370, undated]
- Never take another person's creative work, such as ad copy, videos, images or store text, and present it as your own. Create original material, or adapt it with clear permission. [L0534, undated]
- Tools for seeded reviews: the source's paste-ready template for fake reviews in the ad comment section is not reproduced here, because it is a format for invented testimonials. Its structure, for reference only, is:
  - a quoted testimonial about one specific benefit, in plain language;
  - a five-star rating, a first name, a city or state, and "USA";
  - a photo of the product or a customer using it;
  - two or three similar comments with different names and testimonials, so the ad does not look artificial.
  - Presenting invented comments as real customer proof is deceptive and is not permitted. [L0645, 2023]

### Copying competitors and third-party content

**Legal and platform risks**

- Copying competitor ads that use their own branding or custom content can get the video taken down or the product shut down. [L0363, 2022+]
- Competitor video content should not be copied directly. The competitor can file a copyright complaint and have the videos removed. Short-term testing with adapted scripts and a shoot-your-own approach is safer. Supplier videos can be a base, but re-edit them with your own captions. [L0349, 2024]
- Reusing footage or layouts from other creators carries legal and platform risk. Make the decision to use third-party content deliberately. Creating original footage or licensing content avoids the risk. [L0343, 2023]
- Drop shippers may send legal notices that can shut down a domain. Take inspiration from competitor stores, but do not copy their pages exactly. Rewrite all copy in your own words, for example with ChatGPT. [L1345, 2021+]
- Do not copy the layout or wording of another ad so closely that it could be mistaken for theirs. A close copy violates the rights of the original writer. [L1366, undated]
- Do not copy competitors' product images, even when a competing listing shows good ideas. Create your own images that show the same benefits in your own way. [L1346, 2020-26]
- Copying a competitor's product images directly can lead to a DMCA takedown of the Shopify store. Use your own or substantially edited images. [L0660, 2019+]
- Big brands have legal teams and can pursue action beyond DMCA notices. Avoid copying their content. [L1218, undated]
- TikTok competitor content: avoid big brands such as Adidas and Gymshark, because they enforce DMCA takedowns within one to two weeks. Use recent content (less than one to two months old) rather than outdated videos. Do not claim celebrity endorsements or false associations. [L0321, undated]
- Do not copy videos from large brands. Skip any video in which the product or brand logo is clearly visible. Copying is limited to short tests of content not owned by a major brand. [L1377, 2022+]
- Once a dropshipping brand has proof of concept and sales, replace copied content with original content and build a distinct brand. A large, known brand that gets caught copying faces more reputational damage. [L1348, 2023]
- Original footage separates your brand from competitors and makes copying harder. Copycats are a common problem once an ad performs well. [L1427, 2023+]

**Test windows for competitor and third-party content**

Sources give different windows. Use the one that matches the situation.

| Content | Window or rule | When it applies | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Competitor or shared video | 3-4 days in testing | Only if many people are already using the same content | [L0321, undated] |
| Competitor video | About one to two weeks, then switch to custom content | Standard short test; longer runs raise takedown risk that can remove the landing page or ads | [L1377, 2022+] |
| Competitor logo, product name or shot content | One to two weeks at most in testing | Best avoided altogether | [L0361, 2024] |
| Another store's video or an Instagram photo | Test only; replace with your own creator content within one or two days | Any use outside a test is not permitted | [L1345, 2021+] |
| Brand face, hired model or custom content | DMCA notice or ad ban within about seven to eight days | The risk appears within this time | [L1218, undated] |

- Check whether a supplier photo is generic or custom before using it. [L1218, undated]
  1. Run a Google image search on the photo.
  2. If the same photo appears on many stores and on AliExpress or Alibaba, treat it as a generic supplier image.
  3. If other stores do not use it, or your version differs from the supplier listing, someone probably made it custom, so do not use it.
- Using a supplier or competitor product image without edits can lead to a DMCA notice even after months of use, and the site can go down for an hour or two. Remove the image and republish the site to bring it back online. [L1218, undated]
- Using a brand's face, hired model or custom content in your ads or store risks a DMCA notice or ad ban within about seven to eight days. [L1218, undated]

### Images and product photos

- Google Ads can reject or hide images that contain text. Text overlays on product images are not a ban, but Google may stop showing that image and show other images instead. [L0353, 2023]
- The store must not depend on images with text only. Check the text on each image against Google's policies. [L0353, 2023]
- Only upload display ad images you have the legal rights to distribute. Google asks advertisers to confirm this before uploading.
  - ** [L1342, 2021-22]
- Images that require attribution need a credit line. Shopify product pages have no built-in place for image credits. Check each image's licence terms before using it on a store. [L0934, 2023]
- Do not copy product images from other websites or from general Google results. Use only images that are explicitly labelled as allowed for reuse.
  - ** [L0960, 2019]
- Using a competitor's logo, product name or shot content in ads carries a copyright strike risk against your ad account. Avoid these creatives, or use them for one to two weeks at most in testing. [L0361, 2024]
- Reusing a competitor's logo and product name can make customers distrust your store when the landing page shows a different brand. Replacing the logo and product name is quick and reduces that mismatch. [L0361, 2024]
- Copying a competitor's logo, brand name, trademark or text branding into a remapped ad is a legal and brand risk. Recreate only the layout, composition and visual arrangement, and replace every branded element with your own. [L0794, 2026]

### Reused video and platform detection

**Methods that change metadata**

- Changing a video's playback speed (for example, between 1.02x and 1.05x) changes its metadata without a visible change. The source uses this method, but it goes against the spirit of platform rules on duplicate content and carries a risk of penalties. [L0637, 2023+]
- Changing playback speed or clips alters metadata, which can reduce the chance that Facebook detects reused content. This applies only to short tests, not to long-term use. [L1345, 2021+]
  - *Conflict:* [L0637, 2023+] treats speed changes as a penalty risk. [L1345, 2021+] treats them as a test-only tactic. Use neither for ongoing campaigns.
- Video editing apps such as CapCut or Filmora can change a video's file metadata and aspect ratio. This is a practical workaround that has worked for some sellers, not a guaranteed method, because platforms can still detect reused video. [L0638, 2023]
- For tests, short clips under five seconds from supplier or stock sources such as AliExpress, Alibaba or Fiverr are considered lower risk. TikTok content can also be used cross-platform in tests because Facebook cannot check TikTok data. [L1345, 2021+]

**Rules on identical or reused content**

- Do not copy-paste the same video from another store into your ad account. Identical reused videos can cause problems with the ad account. Download several creatives and edit them so each is clearly different. [L1216, undated]
- TikTok penalises accounts that run identical copies of the same video. Ripping a competitor's content is more likely to fail quickly in the US market than in Europe. [L1177, 2023]
- Controversial or offensive jokes can lead TikTok to report and remove an account. Indirect wording and clearly signalled humour lower the risk. Avoid direct jokes on sensitive topics. [L0372, 2022]

### Music, trademarks and branding

- Music taken from a TikTok sound list is a risky choice for a paid ad, because its rights may not cover advertising. Use music you have the rights to, such as licensed library tracks. [L1427, 2023+]
- Trademark and branded elements: see the logo and branding rules above. Only layout, composition and visual arrangement may be recreated. [L0794, 2026]

### Mailings and offline tactics

These tactics appear in the source as known techniques. They are listed here so that you know the compliance requirements, not as recommended methods.

- Official-looking imprints, such as verified or audited delivery wording, or seal-style labels about enclosed important documents, are used to get past postal workers and company gatekeepers. The source says they work best in political, fundraising and business-to-business mailings. Using them to imply an official or priority item that is not one is deceptive. [L1370, undated]
- Tear-sheet mailings and sneak-up tactics must be cleared with a printer and mailing house that know the laws governing them. Some large users print the word "Advertisement" in small type on both the sticky note and the tear sheet, with no measurable loss of response. The sender must not use real publication names, logos or trademarks. [L1370, undated]

### Pre-launch compliance check

1. Confirm that every claim has a proof file, and that health claims have a verifiable source checked against the relevant platform policy. [L0261, 2025-26; L0524, 2023]
2. Read the landing page as the reviewer does, because it is reviewed together with the ad. [L0416, 2021-22]
3. Check each image for text overlays and confirm the text meets Google's policies. [L0353, 2023]
4. Confirm the rights to every image, video and piece of music, and the attribution requirements. [L1342, 2021-22; L0934, 2023; L1427, 2023+]
5. Confirm that no competitor logo, product name, trademark or custom content appears, and that third-party videos stay within their test window. [L0361, 2024; L1377, 2022+]
6. Confirm that urgency and sale timing are true, and that any tax reference includes the advisor note. [L0461, 2021-22; L1363, 2023; L0340, undated]
7. Check the policies of each network (Google, Meta, TikTok) before launch, since a rule set can differ by platform. [L0220, 2025-26; L1365, 2019+]
