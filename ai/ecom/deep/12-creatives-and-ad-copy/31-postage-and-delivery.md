### Postage and delivery

- Third-class bulk mail can have a non-delivery rate of 10 to 30 percent according to older postal audits, and the author believed it was worse. Bulk mail is only justified when the economics cannot support first-class postage, since non-delivery can wreck a campaign without the sender ever knowing. [L1370, undated]
- Use first-class postage with a real stamp instead of a meter imprint to improve delivery odds, because postal workers are least likely to discard mail they believe the recipient expects. Automated stamp-affixing machines are available for mailers who cannot hand-stamp. [L1370, undated]
- Stamps and small postage details (historical tests, US mail of the 1930s):
  - Two red 2-cent stamps pulled more replies than one 4-cent stamp, and one red 2-cent stamp pulled better than two green 1-cent stamps. Test stamp color and denomination before a large mailing. [L1369, undated]
  - A brown 1.5-cent stamp looked like a 4-cent stamp and pulled better than a green 1-cent stamp, but no better than two half-cent stamps. Postage stamps also pulled better than metered postage or postal indicia in the same tests. [L1369, undated]
  - Printing the 1-cent metered postage in black ribbon may outpull a 2-cent stamp or meter in some post offices. Small details such as ink color and postage type are worth testing, but only after the core letter already works. [L1369, undated]
- Fold, indents, postage and stamp color are minor details that matter only after the opening, description, argument and close are working. Adding them to a weak letter will not produce results. [L1369, undated]

### Gatekeepers

- When a gatekeeper such as an assistant screens mail for an executive, send the sales package this way: [L1370, undated]
  1. Place the sealed sales package inside a plain kraft envelope. [L1370, undated]
  2. Address the kraft envelope personally to the decision-maker, with no company name on the front. [L1370, undated]
  3. Put the sender's personal name in the return-address corner. [L1370, undated]
  4. Send it by overnight courier, placed inside the courier company's outer envelope. This makes the piece look like a personal, urgent item rather than advertising. [L1370, undated]

### Letter presentation and folds

- Fold the letter so that only the salutation and first line show when the reader picks it up, using a military-style fold. This fold raised orders by as much as 10 percent in tests. [L1369, undated]
- Indent the paragraph that carries your most important point. In tests, indentation drew more attention than underlining or all-capital text. [L1369, undated]

### Executive mailings and grabbers

- For executive and business-owner mailings, use superior paper and an envelope with texture or watermark, keep enclosures to a minimum, and place brochures and order forms in a second sealed package inside the main envelope. Prestige words such as *exclusive, select, superior, charter member, prominent, worthwhile, yield* and *individual membership* raise perceived status. [L1370, undated]
- A grabber is a physical object attached to or sent with the letter to create curiosity, such as a coin, tea bag, miniature object or a trade-show invitation in a bottle. Pairing the grabber with a message that explains why the object was sent gives a reason to read the letter. [L1370, undated]
- Cases: a seminar operator mailed a miniature trash can with peanuts and a letter framed as a squirrel calling the prospect *nuts* for not responding, with the point that not responding equals throwing a large sum of money away. Separately, a trade-show mailing sent a bottled treasure-map invitation with a compass, magnifier and highlighter, and produced a 75 percent increase in targeted traffic compared with prior years. [L1370, undated]
- Consumer mail needs bold, colourful and attention-grabbing pieces that give the reader something to do in every section, such as tear-off stamps, scratch-off cards, tokens or stickers. Colour is practically essential in consumer mailings, and colour photographs help. [L1370, undated]

### Folders and multi-panel mailings

- A folder works like a magazine advertisement and needs the same parts: an illustration and headline to win attention, an opening that arouses interest, a clear description, a logical argument, a clincher, and a coupon or card that makes ordering easy. [L1369, undated]
- A circular enclosed with a letter adds about 25 to 33 percent to the pulling power of almost any letter. The enclosure should carry the illustrated detail and the letter should carry the argument. [L1369, undated]
- Layout of a multi-panel mailing folder: [L1369, undated]
  1. Front panel: place a headline and illustration that win attention on their own. [L1369, undated]
  2. Opening pages: use a story or question that arouses interest in the offer. [L1369, undated]
  3. Center pages: describe the offer and present the logical argument with its key features. [L1369, undated]
  4. Last page: place proof, such as a letter from a third party who verifies the claims. [L1369, undated]
  5. Include a card or coupon that makes ordering a single action. [L1369, undated]

#### Further details

Build opening-line ideas from published books:
1. Go to a physical bookstore and pick up novels from authors who write in your reader's genre.
2. Write down the first line of each book and note the hook it uses, such as danger, a question, or a loss.
3. Group the lines by hook type and see which types appear most often.
4. Write several opening lines for your own product using the most common hook types, then test the strongest ones against your current opener.

- Sales copy is a core online marketing skill, because it sells through words in print and in search results. Developing this skill can improve results more than most other changes. [L0382, 2017]
