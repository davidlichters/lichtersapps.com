### Quality control and proofing

**Checking generated copy and images**

- Check every important fact in the generated copy and brief before publishing. AI tools like ChatGPT can make mistakes. [L0992, 2025-26]
- Check that the offer is visible in the final design. [L0992, 2025-26]
- Rewrite template copy for the brand instead of keeping defaults, even when you plan to edit it again later. [L0992, 2025-26]
- Check every word of a generated infographic before publishing it. [L1075, 2025+]
- Always check the generated text for correct offers before running it. [L0972, 2025]
- Check that every image shows a clearly different model, and regenerate any image that repeats a person. [L0884, 2025-26]
- Check that the output looks realistic and does not misrepresent the product before using it in ads or on the store. [L1260, undated]
- Review and adapt every AI output for a specific product and region before use. [L1431, 2020+]

**Checking subtitles and video text**

- AI subtitles make errors on complex or less common words, because the tool is built around basic vocabulary. Always compare the generated subtitles with the original script in a second window and correct each mistake before export. [L0888, 2023+]

**Testing copy against results**

- Every generated text still needs to be tested against real conversion results before it is scaled. [L1184, 2023+]
- Check that the output still matches the brand voice and the offer after applying a saved prompt. [L0992, 2025-26]
- A quick test is the first step. If the test works, send the concept to designers for more variations. [L0972, 2025]

**Human control**

- AI can handle scale better than a single human, but a human must stay in control and check that the AI is learning from the right sources. Oversight is needed for nuance and final decisions. [L1478, 2025-26]
- Use one clean, well-lit product photo as the source. Blurry or low-quality uploads produce weak variants. [L1428, undated]
- Spend more time on mask painting for a clean removal. [L1428, undated]

### Case: AI presenter ads at scale

**Simple Brand, supplement, US (2025)**

- Starting point: a small brand, Simple Brand, sold a dietary supplement in the US. Its product showed strong sales on Kalodata in summer 2025. [L1479, 2026]
- What was done: the brand ran a large set of ads that were all AI-generated, with different AI presenters (an AI lady, an AI doctor, an AI man). It launched on July 9, 2025 and had about 71 active ads in Meta Ad Library after roughly one month. [L1479, 2026]
- The main brand behind the product had about 650 active ads, all AI-generated with avatar variations. [L1479, 2026]
- Result: the ads kept running for about a month, which indicates that the spend returned enough money to continue. The brand scaled the presenter versions that held up. [L1479, 2026]

Note on conflicting rules: the case shows an AI doctor presenter performing well, while the persona prompt says never to design a medical doctor or any persona that suggests medical qualifications. The 'Doc Ellie' title example in the same prompt conflicts with that rule. Follow the prompt rule for your own personas. [L1479, 2026; L1476, 2026]

Note on conflicting advice on AI people: real users build trust faster early on, while the case scaled AI presenters that held up in tests. Use real people for early trust and AI presenter variants for testing and scaling. [L1225, 2025; L1479, 2026]

### Limits of AI output

- AI tools like ChatGPT can make mistakes, so check every important fact in the generated copy and brief before publishing. [L0992, 2025-26]
- AI tools work as a virtual assistant rather than a lead. Their output is only as good as the input, and they miss contextual details that a human finds in forums and reviews. [L0999, 2023]
- Use an AI tool to draft ad copy from audience research, but feed it the right inputs. The quality of the output depends on the quality of the information given. [L0995, 2023]
- The AI generates text and images from the prompt. Unprompted visual or layout suggestions were weak, so tie image prompts to a specific offer. [L1224, 2025+]
- Image generators return the same person when prompts describe models identically. Describe each model differently. [L0884, 2025-26]
- AI-generated infographics can contain small text errors. [L1075, 2025+]
- Generated subtitles make errors on complex or less common words. [L0888, 2023+]
- ElevenLabs reads digits correctly only in English. [L0888, 2023+]
- Realistic-looking AI people build less trust than real users early on. [L1225, 2025]
- Decisions that need a trained opinion must be defined in the training, not left to the AI. [L1478, 2025-26]
- Generated product visuals must be checked for realistic product representation before use. [L1260, undated]
- A generated text still needs to be tested against real conversion results before it is scaled. [L1184, 2023+]

### Testing presenter avatars with AI-generated ads

- AI-generated ads let you test many presenter types (avatars) for the same product at low cost. [L1479, 2026]
- Work in this order: [L1479, 2026]
  1. Produce several presenter versions of the same product ad.
  2. Run them and find the one that keeps running.
  3. Scale that version.
