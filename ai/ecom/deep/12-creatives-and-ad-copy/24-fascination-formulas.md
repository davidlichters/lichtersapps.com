### Fascination Formulas

Each formula below appears with its pattern and examples. Use the formulas to write many variants of the same information, then select the strongest.

**Curiosity and hidden knowledge**

- **"How to" bullet.** The bullet works because prospects are naturally curious about procedural information, especially when it is tied to their deepest desires or pain points. Open with the benefit-related action, not just a generic process.
  - *How to source inventory without tying up working capital for 90 days, using the payment-term negotiation script that works with 8 out of 10 tier-one suppliers.*
  - *How to prevent competitor price undercutting by positioning your brand so customers ignore price-focused competitors entirely.* [L0068, undated]
- **"Secret to" bullet.** Reveals non-obvious information positioned as a closely guarded secret. It works because prospects believe they have discovered something most people do not know. It must be based on genuinely uncommon information and varied carefully to avoid overuse.
  - *The secret to 40% repeat orders from first-time buyers: it has nothing to do with product quality.*
  - *The little-known secret that separates $50K-per-month shops from $500K shops: it is one email sequence that reverses abandonment decisions.* [L0068, undated]
- **"Why" bullet.** Builds intrigue by promising to reveal the reason or cause behind something, implying that understanding why will meaningfully change the prospect's life. Both the reason and the benefit of knowing it must be directly valuable to the prospect.
  - *Why most dropshippers hit a $100K ceiling and never break through (and why understanding this single obstacle decides if you are stuck or scaling).*
  - *Why customers who receive tracking updates spend 35% more on their next order, and what you are missing if you do not tell them.* [L0068, undated]
- **"What" bullet.** Works in two ways: it either gives specific action instructions to avoid a problem, or it reveals elusive valuable information not commonly shared. Both increase perceived value while teasing the answer.
  - *What you must include in supplier contracts that protects you from the 12 most common vendor betrayals.*
  - *What your fulfillment partner doesn't want you to know about how they handle damage claims, and how knowing this saves you $3K-8K monthly.* [L0068, undated]
- **"Gimmick" bullet.** Assigns a creative name to a specific idea extracted from research, combining a benefit with specificity and memorable framing. It makes a technique feel like a discovered thing with a name, which increases perceived sophistication.
  - *The little-known supplier health score that predicts six months in advance which vendors will fail you or degrade quality.*
  - *The competitive pressure point: the exact moment when your competitor will need to raise prices due to supplier costs, giving you two weeks of market advantage.* [L0068, undated]
- **"Sneaky" bullet.** Reveals something hidden, guarded, or deliberately obscured by a group, information that competitors or suppliers do not want revealed. Use it selectively so it does not seem contrived; it suggests a conspiracy element.
  - *The sneaky way suppliers hide quality degradation in cost optimizations that your factory inspector never catches, until customers start returning product.*
  - *Sneaky fulfillment tricks most 3PLs use that make their performance metrics look better while customer satisfaction actually drops.* [L0068, undated]
- **"Single" bullet.** Works when you have one benefit that exceeds all others and you have proof to back it. It positions one exclusive or most powerful possibility.
  - *The single highest-impact metric for predicting which customers will buy again: customer acquisition cost in week 2 of purchase, which predicts repeat rate with 87% accuracy.*
  - *The single most important factor in supplier selection that determines 80% of your margin variance: it is not price but response speed on urgent issues.* [L0068, undated]
- **"Truth" bullet.** Demystifies a controversial, confusing, or commonly misunderstood issue by offering a clear resolution. Prospects enjoy reading about contradictions being clarified.
  - *The truth about influencer partnerships: micro-influencers (5K-50K followers) with 8-12% engagement deliver 3x the ROAS of mega-influencers claiming higher reach.*
  - *THE TRUTH ABOUT DROPSHIPPING PROFIT: most calculations hide fulfillment complexity. Here is how to measure real margin after every cost is included.* [L0068, undated]

**Fear and danger**

- **"What NEVER" bullet.** Alerts the prospect to a mistake or danger, then promises information that protects them from making it. It builds curiosity about the hidden mistake while triggering fear-based motivation.
  - *What NEVER to do with your best-performing product when your supplier suggests a minor update: one founder lost 60% of revenue by ignoring this warning.*
  - *What NOT to say to customers when shipping is delayed: the wrong approach loses 40% of repeat customers, while the right one loses only 8%.* [L0068, undated]
- **"WARNING" bullet.** Alerts the prospect to a danger or threat, triggering fear, and must pair the threat with proof that you have the solution. Variations such as CAUTION, ALERT, or DANGER have the same effect.
  - *WARNING: your supplier's quality standard changed last month, and your customers are seeing it in unboxing videos even though you have not noticed it yet.*
  - *ALERT: the shipping carrier you selected is about to raise rates 18% in Q2. Here is the exact timing and how to lock in today's pricing before the deadline.* [L0068, undated]

**Questions and challenged assumptions**

- **"Right...WRONG!" bullet.** Plays on the prospect's assumptions about something they think they understand, then immediately debunks the assumption with the correct information. It works because everyone wants to know they have been wrong about something, with a strong benefit attached.
  - *Most dropshippers think faster shipping guarantees loyalty? Wrong. Our data shows speed barely registers. Here is what actually determines repeat buys.*
  - *You think supplier payment terms don't matter for 30-day inventory cycles? Wrong. Here is how to turn terms into your biggest competitive advantage.* [L0068, undated]
- **"Are you...?" bullet.** Asks the prospect about something you strongly believe he is already doing or experiencing, so he mentally answers yes. This builds momentum and agreement before you deliver the benefit, and it creates credibility because the prospect nods. Variations: *Do you...?*, *Does your...?*, *Is your...?*
  - *Are you pricing like a commodity seller instead of a brand, and leaving 40% margin on every sale?*
  - *Are you using the same supplier for products in completely different categories when one small change could cut costs 25%?* [L0068, undated]
- **"Specific Question" bullet.** Opens with an intriguing question of importance to the prospect, then offers the solution either in the bullet itself or in the product being sold. It must include credibility, benefit, intrigue, and curiosity.
  - *Do you know that only 8% of dropshippers ever negotiate payment terms with suppliers, even though this decision predicts success more than product selection?*
  - *Have you discovered that the #1 reason for chargebacks isn't fraud but customer misalignment about product details, and this one email prevents 70% of chargebacks?* [L0068, undated]
- **"If...Then" bullet.** Engages the prospect by inviting him to meet a simple requirement or asking whether he is experiencing something specific. He becomes interested in whether he meets it. The second half reveals the benefit that follows if he does.
  - *If your supplier uses 30-day lead times, you are missing the first two weeks of every trend cycle, while competitors with 14-day vendors already have 200+ sales.*
  - *If you are losing customers at the cart stage, the problem isn't shipping cost. It is that you are revealing it too late in the buying journey.* [L0068, undated]

**Direct, comparative, and benefit-first**

- **"Statement of Interest + Benefit" bullet.** Opens with an exciting or intriguing fact, then immediately follows with a strong benefit statement that explains why it matters. It often combines a factual lead with a how-to or why structure.
  - *Your competitors are already using this supplier. Here is how to partner directly with them at their lowest-tier pricing.*
  - *Facebook's algorithm just changed how it prioritizes video content. Here is exactly how to structure your ads to capture the new traffic window.* [L0068, undated]
- **"Direct Benefit" bullet.** Makes a powerful claim in the opening line (usually with an action verb) that instantly promises the benefit. The second line deepens, proves, or adds dimensions to that benefit.
  - *ELIMINATE slow-moving inventory: here is how to validate demand before ordering a single unit.*
  - *STOP losing customers at the upsell stage: the one change to your offer sequence that increases order value 45%.* [L0068, undated]
- **"PLUS" bullet.** Adds greed and momentum. It is one of the other formulas combined with PLUS at the beginning, promising additional benefits beyond the main claim. It works best at the end of a bullet list to finish with power and accumulated desire.
  - *PLUS how to turn returns into repeat purchases that increase customer lifetime value by 120% instead of losing the customer.*
  - *PLUS my complete supplier database, already vetted for quality, communication, and margin, organized by category and minimum order.* [L0068, undated]
- **"Number" bullet.** Groups multiple ways of doing something, multiple secrets, or multiple reasons why something will happen, condensing related information into one statement. The reader perceives more value when learning multiple solutions at once.
  - *The 5 overlooked sources of repeat customer revenue that don't require building email lists.*
  - *Seven supplier negotiation leverage points that are in plain sight but never used, because most entrepreneurs don't know what to look for.* [L0068, undated]
- **"When" bullet.** Promises a benefit at a very specific time, implying a guaranteed result if the prospect acts at that moment. The prospect wants to know exactly when he can expect the benefit.
  - *When you update product photos on Friday evenings, the platform algorithm serves them with full power by Monday morning, capturing the week's traffic window.*
  - *When you negotiate supplier terms before inventory arrives, you control cash flow for 60 days. Waiting until after arrival costs you 30% of that control.* [L0068, undated]
- **"Quickest, Easiest" bullet.** Reveals the fastest, easiest, or simplest way to achieve a benefit, tapping into the desire for instant gratification and minimal effort.
  - *The easiest way to identify trending products in your niche without paying for expensive research: use your competitor's Shopify code and social proof.*
  - *The quickest way to recover abandoned carts without hiring email specialists: this single 48-hour sequence reverses 40% of abandonment decisions.* [L0068, undated]
- **"Better" bullet.** Works when something is already perceived as good, but you offer something superior. It slips under the prospect's skepticism and introduces your benefit while building curiosity and momentum. Variations include leading with BEYOND.
  - *BETTER THAN Facebook ads for cold traffic: TikTok organic reach costs zero and reaches your exact demographic six months earlier than you see it on Facebook.*
  - *BEYOND the standard 30-day return window: offer 60 days and watch repeat purchase rates increase 35% because customers trust you more.* [L0068, undated]
- **Pure benefit bullets.** Used when there is no elusive information to hide. They work best for straightforward advantages and for guarantee presentations. They need strong action verbs, clear language, and emotional weight rather than curiosity-driven structure, and they must be made readable and interesting through word choice and specific detail. [L0068, undated]

### Combining, Sequencing, and Placing Bullets

- **Combine two or more formulas on the same information.** Combining formulas gives greater effect than using one alone. For example, combine a PLUS bullet with a How-to bullet to multiply the impact. [L0068, undated]
- **Do not overuse Sneaky or Secret-to bullets.** Overuse makes copy feel contrived and causes excessive skepticism. When everything is presented as a secret, each secret loses its uniqueness and intrigue. [L0068, undated]
- **Vary bullet length, structure, and formula.** Variety prevents monotony and flat benefit syndrome, where bullets blur together. Mix long and short bullets, blind bullets with revealing bullets, and different formulas on the same topic. This creates a roller coaster feeling that keeps the prospect excited about each next bullet. [L0068, undated]
- **Mix blind and revealing bullets.** Mix bullets that tease unrevealed information with bullets that immediately reveal the benefit. This unpredictability prevents monotony and keeps readers excited about what comes next rather than skimming. [L0068, undated]
- **Theme bullets to cover one topic from several angles.** Grouping related bullets that hit one topic with different formulas makes the prospect feel he has comprehensive coverage. Varied themed bullets keep him engaged because he does not know which direction the next one will take. Themed bullets at the beginning (front cover or early deck copy) signal a thorough investigation inside. [L0068, undated]
- **Position matters.** The most powerful, intriguing fascinations belong in the most valuable real estate: the front and back cover, the opening headline area, and the final closing bullets. Opening bullets in promotional body copy should bribe the prospect into reading on. Closing bullets on the back cover should compel him to open the report to find the answers. [L0068, undated]
- **Sequence bullets so they feed each other.** Tightly and sequentially connected bullets reinforce each other rather than standing alone. Individual bullets need not do as much because the cumulative effect does the heavy lifting. Sequencing lets you introduce a benefit in one bullet and explain why it works in the next. [L0068, undated]

### Unique Mechanism

**Purpose and placement**

- The unique mechanism is the part of product copy that explains the logic of how the product delivers its main promise and why it differs from competing products. It normally comes after the story or thought-command opening and before the detailed features and benefits. [L0465, 2021-22]
- Buyers decide emotionally but need a logical reason to confirm the decision, and the unique mechanism is where that logic enters the copy. Without it, the copy only makes promises. [L0465, 2021-22]
- Skeptical buyers who have been over-promised before need to see how a product works before they believe a headline promise. Explain the mechanism right after the primary promise. [L0465, 2021-22]
- Buyers want something different from what they have tried before, so the unique mechanism also separates the product from alternatives on the market. [L0465, 2021-22]
- The unique mechanism of your product is the strongest single benefit to highlight in ad copy, because uniqueness encourages clicks through the promise of learning something different. [L0241, 2021-22]
- Brands print their unique mechanism prominently on packaging and use it in offline and online sales alike. Once you know the concept, study competitor packaging and product listings to spot the mechanisms they highlight. [L0465, 2021-22]
- The length of the mechanism section depends on the product: simple products may need only three or four short paragraphs, while technical products need more space. Explain as much as needed so the mechanism is understood. [L0465, 2021-22]

**Writing the mechanism sentence**

- Before writing the mechanism, list the concerns buyers have before purchase, for example never having used this type of product. Then connect the mechanism to resolving that concern, which adds a benefit to the logic. [L0465, 2021-22]
- **Formula for a benefit-linked mechanism sentence:** name the mechanism, state a measurable advantage over the previous or competing version, then state the benefit that answers a specific buyer doubt. The name creates uniqueness, the number makes the difference believable, and the benefit removes the reason to hesitate.
  - *Our 3-layer Thermo-Lock lid keeps coffee hot for up to 6 hours, 40% longer than our previous model, so first-time buyers can brew at 6 am without making a second cup.*
  - *TrailGrip sole technology holds grip on wet stone 2x better than standard rubber soles, so hikers new to trail running stop worrying about slipping.* [L0465, 2021-22]
- State directly what the reader should conclude, for example that they need all components of the mechanism. Do not assume the reader will reach the conclusion you want on their own. [L0465, 2021-22]
- Do not leave technical terms unexplained. Jargon confuses buyers and drops them from the copy. Explain each term in short, plain paragraphs, but keep it short for simple products, since they need less explanation. [L0465, 2021-22]

**Technical mechanism**

- **Formula for a technical mechanism section.** Open with the trademarked technology name and a measurable output compared with the closest competitor, then add a second attribute that goes beyond raw power, then explain each component in simple terms, and close with a firm statement of what the buyer needs. The number proves superiority, the second attribute widens the advantage, and the plain explanation makes the technology understandable.
  - *Our TerraSeal boot uses a 4-zone membrane technology that keeps water out 3x longer than the closest competitor. It is not just about waterproofing; the same membrane lets moisture escape, so your feet stay dry on long days. You need both functions, because a boot that only blocks water traps sweat.*
  - *The AeroFlow kettle heats water with a 2200 W element, 30% faster than the closest competitor. It is not just about speed; the sensor shuts it off automatically, so it is safe to leave unattended. You need both the speed and the auto shut-off to get a fast, safe kettle.* [L0465, 2021-22]
- **Case: infrared sauna, first claim.** The trademarked True Wave heaters produce 1000 W, which the copy states is 25x more than the closest competitor, so the same benefits come in less time. The claim was supported by a test showing higher output than competitors. The session takes about 15 minutes after the sauna preheats, and a reservation mode can preset the sauna to switch on up to 36 hours in advance. [L0465, 2021-22]
- **Case: infrared sauna, second attribute.** The heating is full spectrum, meaning it uses near, mid, and far infrared together.
  - Near infrared is absorbed in shallow skin tissue and linked to sweating.
  - Mid infrared reaches deeper soft tissue and is linked to oxygen and circulation.
  - Far infrared reaches the deepest and is linked to releasing stored substances from muscle and fat.

  The copy then says you need all three wavelengths, not just one or two as some competing saunas offer. [L0465, 2021-22]

**Verifying mechanism claims**

- **Case: wax strip pack.** The named gel technology is the first and most visible claim on the front. The claims below it state that:
  - The gel flows around and grips the shortest hairs;
  - it works on hairs from 1.5 mm and is easier than the previous formula;
  - it removes hairs from the root for up to 28 days of smoothness;
  - The pack holds 20 strips and 2 finishing wipes;
  - a sensitive-skin version contains almond oil and vitamin E.

  The narration gave 20 days for the smoothness claim, while the pack prints 28 days. Use the printed claim only after verifying it. [L0465, 2021-22]

### Intensification

**Principles**

- Intensification is the process of building desire by showing new images of how the product satisfies it. It works in two directions: horizontally, by reaching more people, and vertically, by making the existing desire stronger. [L0330, undated]
- Where space is limited, as in catalogs, small print, radio, and television, use two techniques: compression, where images are boiled down to a few key words, and campaign repetition, where those key words repeat across ads with changing embellishments. In long-form media such as large print ads and direct mail, the length is limited only by how many fresh ways you can present the image without boredom. [L0330, undated]
- The reader will remember one main image from an ad. Every new and different way of presenting that image adds detail, sharpens it, and builds emotional weight, so walk around the product and show it at each point where it enters the buyer's life. [L0330, undated]
- Do not repeat a description; reinforce it with a new viewpoint. Each new setting for the same basic promise strengthens the descriptions before it and makes the reader more eager to take part. If the new viewpoint is not different or dramatic enough to renew interest, leave it out. [L0330, undated]

**Techniques**

- **Technique 1: describe the satisfaction in complete detail.** Use the result's specific numbers, such as counts, sizes, colours, and time spans, so the reader can picture the outcome before reading on. [L0330, undated]
- **Technique 2: put the product in action.** Walk the reader step by step through how it does what it does, including what he sees and feels at each step, so he can imagine using it. [L0330, undated]
- **Technique 3: place the reader inside the story.** Describe what will happen to him on the first day he owns the product, in concrete scenes such as a moment with family, a traffic light, or a shop counter. [L0330, undated]
- **Technique 4: turn the demonstration into a test the reader can run himself.** Give clear steps to measure the result, such as checking a number before and after, or completing a task on the first evening, so the claim is proven in his own hands. [L0330, undated]
- **Technique 5: stretch the benefits over days, weeks, and months.** Show the product working in stages over time, so the reader sees a continuing flow of benefits instead of a single event. [L0330, undated]
- **Technique 8: compare side by side.** Compare the product with the old way or with competitors, with the specific price or cost difference shown in parallel sentences, so the advantage stands out sharply. [L0330, undated]
- **Technique 9: show the negative side.** Paint the problem or the inadequate old product in full, dark detail, then give the parallel solution item by item. The reader feels the push away from the problem and the pull toward the product together. [L0330, undated]
- **Technique 10: show how easy the product is to use.** Stress how little effort, skill, or time it takes, and set that against the large benefits it delivers, so the reader sees a short effort for a long-lasting return. [L0330, undated]
- **Technique 11: use metaphor, analogy, and imagination.** Use them to make facts more dramatic when a literal description is dull or unbelievable. Personify the product or the method if that helps the reader grasp it. [L0330, undated]
- **Technique 12: summarise before closing, using a catalog.** There are two kinds.
  1. **Horizontal catalog:** list all applications and buyer types in one-line items to widen the market. It is a shotgun approach that hedges the choice of one dominant appeal.
  2. **Vertical catalog:** pile up the desires in summary to deepen the desire the ad has built.

  Use short sentences, one per item, with no further explanation. [L0330, undated]
- **Technique 13: end with the guarantee as the climax.** State the terms in clear numbered points, each with its condition and refund or replacement promise, and repeat the product's main performances in the process. The close becomes a summary of the product's benefits, made safe by the guarantee. [L0330, undated]

### Research benefits before writing copy

1. Read Amazon reviews of the product and of similar products, focusing on the five-star reviews that describe the experience after use. [L0916, 2022+]
2. Note the life changes, enhancements and positive outcomes buyers describe, since similar products often solve the same problem. [L0916, 2022+]
3. Search Google for "benefits of [product name]" and collect articles and blogs that contain benefit points and data. [L0916, 2022+]
4. Collect all points in one document and use them in the page copy. [L0916, 2022+]

Interest copy should describe the end experience, such as comfort, relief or a better daily routine, rather than technical specs and features, because most buyers care more about how the product will make them feel than about volume, functions or technical details. [L0916, 2022+]
