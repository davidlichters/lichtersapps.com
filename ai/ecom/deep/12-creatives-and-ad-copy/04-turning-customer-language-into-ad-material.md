### Turning customer language into ad material

- Strong hooks borrow the exact language customers use when they describe a problem in forums, reviews, and comments. Because only people living that problem would use those words, they feel immediately recognised. [L0772, 2026]
- Ad copy should reuse the exact questions, language, and concerns the audience uses in forums and communities. Collect these phrases during research and use them in headlines and hooks. [L0395, undated]
- Reuse the customers' exact phrasing from research for hooks, headlines, and primary text. [L1019, 2026]
- Customers describe their problems in simpler, more emotional language than marketers do, and that language converts better in ads. Copy the exact phrases from survey answers and reuse them as hooks, headlines, and ad copy. Ads built on what marketers guess customers care about often miss the real pain. [L0906, 2026]
- Build hooks from what buyers say, not from marketer assumptions. Writing ads based on what you think customers want, instead of asking them, leads to guessing and weak messaging. Survey data removes the guessing. [L0906, 2026]
- Problem-solution leads start from the prospect's most pressing worry, named precisely and tied to emotional weight, before offering hope. Good worries are found by listening to customers in service emails, calls, forums, and the publications they read, and by noting the patterns in what they repeat. [L0756, undated]
- Example: for men in their mid-50s to 60s with savings to invest, common worries in financial publishing include running out of money before running out of life, bills in retirement after paychecks stop, inflation shrinking savings, taxes, and missing a big opportunity. These worries are useful starting points for problem research in a retirement or investing niche, and the most pressing one should be named precisely in the opening. [L0756, undated]
- Reuse the phrasing in copy as follows: buyers describe problems in plain words, so match that language in the first lines of every ad. [L1140, 2026]
- Older advice: producing content by guessing what customers want, putting random material together and hoping something fits, wastes time. Content built on documented customer problems is what makes content valuable and improves conversion rate. [L0872, 2021-22]

### Testimonials and review automation

- Set LUX Reviews to request reviews 28 days after the order, so the product has arrived and fulfilment is complete. [L0477, undated]
- Collect testimonials by first deciding the story you want the customer to tell, then asking 3 to 5 open-ended questions that guide them to it. Example questions: "Did you make a lot of money thanks to [seller]?", "How much money and how fast did you do it?", "How did you do it?", and "Would you recommend [seller]'s service?" [L1356, 2019+]
