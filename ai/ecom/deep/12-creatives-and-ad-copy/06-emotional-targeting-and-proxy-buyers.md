### Angle research and angle banks

- An angle is a separate layer from format and concept: it is the argument or approach the ad takes to the viewer. Do not confuse angle, concept and format when reading a competitor's data. [L0374, 2026]
- Mark any angle that is well-supported by avatar data but already saturated in the market. These angles have emotional validity but need a fresh mechanism or differentiated positioning to stand out against existing competitor ads. Saturation does not eliminate an angle; it signals the need for innovation within that emotional framework. [L0039, 2026]
- When a niche's main angle becomes saturated, reframe the same product for a different buyer, such as a gift bought for a partner instead of a purchase for oneself. Test that new angle as its own ad set before dropping the product. [L1315, 2023]
- Compare the buyer breakdown in the ad account (for example by gender) with the people shown in the creative. If the actual buyers differ from the people in the ad, make a new set of creatives aimed at the real buyer group. [L1315, 2023]
- Build a unique marketing angle by attacking the problem from different perspectives, so the same product can be marketed to different audiences or platforms. For example, Facebook for one group and TikTok for a younger group affected by the same problem. [L0513, undated]
- Create ad angles that match the problem: one shows the product as protection from the cold, another shows use with coffee and friends, and a third shows the product as a gift. Men also buy it, so the audience can stay open to both genders. [L0492, 2023+]
- Marketing angles generated for zodiac jewelry included showing off a star sign, a birthday or holiday gift, self-expression, and compatibility and connection between signs. A lead-generation quiz could return each buyer's matches as a one-page PDF and unlock a discount after completion. [L0549, undated]
- Also target a secondary problem identified in the product map, such as convenience of using the product at home or saving time, within the same picture. [L0580, 2021-22]
- Turn the research into concepts: from the collected data, list five ad angles (for example comfort, sustainability, more confidence, better sleep). Combine research with consumer-behavior principles such as survival, enjoyment of life, life extension, enjoyment of food and drink, envy, jealousy, curiosity, expression of beauty and style, economy, profit, convenience and efficiency to refine each angle. [L0902, undated]
- Pick the most promising angles by checking what customers repeat in Amazon and Reddit reviews. [L0646, 2023]

#### Angle selection procedure

1. List the marketing angles for the product. [L0646, 2023]
2. Check what customers repeat in Amazon and Reddit reviews to see which angle is most likely to land. [L0646, 2023]
3. Ask ChatGPT which audience is more likely to respond, keeping in mind its data may only go back to 2021, and judge its answer against the review evidence. [L0646, 2023]
4. Produce one concept per angle, or at least the most promising one if time is short. [L0646, 2023]

- A video that mentions a product's flaw as a joke or confession can attract many comments and views, but views alone do not show buying intent. Test whether the audience actually buys before scaling a honesty-style angle, because it can work well or fail badly depending on the product. [L0655, undated]
- A video with 100,000 views that converts well can be worth more than a video with one million views that attracts an unrelated audience, because conversion depends on the viewers' fit with the product. [L0795, undated]

#### Building one angle

1. Choose the problem type and write one overview line naming the product, the problem theme and the target customer. [L0392, undated]
2. Write the problem or hook: the visible symptom the customer sees first. [L0392, undated]
3. Write two to three agitate lines that make the problem practical and concrete (what it does to breathing, cleanliness, time or social life). [L0392, undated]
4. Write one or two feeling lines describing how the customer feels because of the problem. [L0392, undated]
5. Write the solution in one line, naming the product category and its main claim. [L0392, undated]
6. List the features in the "what it does" row, each as a short claim with a visible result. [L0392, undated]
7. For each key feature, write a meaning line describing what this changes in the customer's home or day. [L0392, undated]
8. Write the outcome: the emotional result the customer reaches after using the product. [L0392, undated]
9. Write the avoid-negative-outcome row: what happens if the customer does not buy. [L0392, undated]
10. Add social proof, such as customer testimonials that match this angle's problem. [L0392, undated]
11. Write the call to action with a concrete offer. [L0392, undated]
12. Start a new column for each different problem type, and keep columns separate so each one can be tested as its own angle. [L0392, undated]

### Angle patterns for specific audiences

- Gift-based angle: frame the product as a gift from a partner or friend and describe the feeling it creates, so the customer sees a reason to buy for someone else or for themselves. Example for a ring, in italics: *My partner surprised me with this ring and it makes me feel seen every time I wear it.* Example for a keychain, in italics: *My sister gave me this so I always feel safe walking home at night.* [L0999, 2023]
- Cultural or identity-based angle: name a specific group or identity the product speaks to, and show how the product fits their life, which makes the creative more intimate and easier to target. Example: a jewelry piece for couples in a specific culture, described as worn for a special family occasion. Example: a hair brush for owners of large dogs that shed onto beds, shown with a before and after result. [L0999, 2023]
- Pattern for a niche angle: take a product that a broad audience sees as generic, name a specific situation in which it is used, and let the copy describe that situation. This works because the viewer can imagine using the product right away, which sells more than a general claim.
  - Example 1: a stainless steel travel mug for early-morning commutes on cold days. [L1161, 2024+]
  - Example 2: a desk lamp for late-night study sessions in a shared room. [L1161, 2024+]
- For products that appeal mainly to men, combine the product with a concrete situation where it is used. This narrows the message and helps a weak conversion rate. [L1161, 2024+]
- A broad ad for a watch case asks whether you want to keep your watches in a box, which speaks to everyone and stands out less. A segment-specific ad speaks to the traveler's situation of choosing the right watch for each outfit on a trip abroad. [L0998, undated]
- Models in an ad should look like the person the viewer wants to become, because viewers are more attracted to the product when the person in the ad matches their aspiration. [L1184, 2023+]
- For ads targeting older audiences, use a younger person speaking to the older audience, because older viewers want to look young and do not identify with ads showing people their own age. [L1184, 2023+]

### Angle patterns for products

- Show the target audience using the product in the real setting, such as a cyclist or snowboarder, and name the specific problem it solves. Avoid generic product shots on plain backgrounds for cold traffic. [L1225, 2025]
- Use a gifting angle with a product that is a gift, such as showing the item as the thing a buyer's family member would want. Keep the copy simple and clear about what the product is. [L1225, 2025]
- The same product can be marketed with different angles, such as a gift for couples, a memorial keepsake or a pregnancy milestone. Using different angles keeps the product fresh and gives you unique content. [L1171, 2024+]
- Same product, different angle: take a product that already sells broadly and promote it to a specific audience with its own interest or problem, then brand the product page to match.
  - Example: a foldable mat promoted to yoga teachers for studio use. [L1185, 2023]
  - Example: a phone stand promoted to home bakers for recipe videos. [L1185, 2023]
- Angle for children's toys: position the toy as a way to reduce a child's screen time by offering a hands-on or educational activity instead.
  - Example of your own: a building kit that replaces tablet time with a 20-minute task. [L1176, 2023]
  - Example of your own: a drawing set pitched as a screen-free weekend activity for kids. [L1176, 2023]
- Family-benefit angle for educational toys: frame the product around reducing children's screen time and creating shared quality time with parents or grandparents.
  - Example: a wooden building kit that replaces an hour of tablet time. [L1185, 2023]
  - Example: a puzzle set designed for weekend time with grandchildren. [L1185, 2023]
- When a product has a technology feature, sell it as a classic or traditional look that keeps the buyer from falling behind on modern function. The formula is to name the timeless style the buyer already wants, then add the modern capability they fear missing. It works because it narrows the message to one specific audience and ties the product to status and belonging.
  - Example 1, in italics: *Keep the warm look of a wooden desk lamp without working under dim, outdated light.* [L0997, undated]
  - Example 2, in italics: *Carry a classic leather wallet that still works with every new payment app.* [L0997, undated]

### Naming angles and concepts

- An angle is the argument or approach the ad takes to the viewer. It is separate from the concept and the format. [L0374, 2026]
- Naming: for each distinct angle, give a clear name. [L0039, 2026]

#### Angle-naming prompt

```
Combine the fears and angles discovered in customer research with the gaps between what competitors currently advertise and what the market isn't hearing. For each distinct angle, provide a clear name, state the underlying fear and mechanism in one line, cite the supporting evidence from customer data and market gaps, assess whether competitors are already saturating this angle or whether it remains open territory, and rank by expected strength, with well-supported customer fears that competitors ignore rising to the top.
```

- To give a name to what people feel but have not yet expressed, use verbal creativity. This is part of introducing a new product to a market that does not yet know it. [L0330, undated]
- Renaming an old product can create a new market appeal. The South Beach Diet was initially named "Modified Carbohydrate Diet" and failed until it was renamed. Apple's iPod was marketed as "1,000 songs in your pocket" though it was not the first MP3 player. [L1356, 2019+]

#### Planning a new ad (concept development)

1. State the problem in plain words, for example that you want to sell this product. [L1366, undated]
2. Restate the problem in a different way, for example that you want to introduce the unique aspects of the product to the prospect. [L1366, undated]
3. Restate it again in terms of the buyer's experience, for example that the product should be easy to buy and seem fun. [L1366, undated]
4. Choose the restatement that feels closest to the ad you want to write. [L1366, undated]
5. List several big ideas for that restatement and pick the one or two that make the most sense. [L1366, undated]
6. Visualize how the concept fits into the ad and check it against the restatement. [L1366, undated]
7. Stop and incubate. [L1366, undated]
8. Write the headline, then the subheadline, then the first sentence. [L1366, undated]
9. Block-diagram the finished ad to check its flow. [L1366, undated]

### Unique selling points and value propositions

- Value propositions can be functional (solve a technical or practical need, typical for tech or electronics) or emotional (typical for kids' products and beauty products). Define one value proposition per customer archetype based on which reason drives the purchase. [L0902, undated]
- List the unique selling points of best-selling products, such as manufacturing process, eco-friendly features, press coverage or charity donations, because these can serve as credible marketing angles. [L0902, undated]
- Compare products by a single value-based claim that rivals cannot easily copy, because copied general claims become commonplace and lose value. Look for a unique, specific, verifiable advantage. [L1261, undated]
- The USP formula from Rosser Reeves has three parts: the product must actually do what the copy says it does, the benefit must be original compared with competitors, and the promise must target a core desire the prospect already has. Desire cannot be created, only awakened. [L0756, undated]
- The unique selling proposition in the offer must make the service different, typically through the phrasing "The only service that...". A USP can be created in three ways:
  1. By genuinely unique features like a proprietary system or patented technology. [L1356, 2019+]
  2. By labeling a common feature as unique because competitors do not talk about it. [L1356, 2019+]
  3. By giving an old thing a new name. [L1356, 2019+]
- Labeling a common feature as unique works by highlighting something every competitor also has but no one mentions. For example, a manufacturer's gold-plated connectors that all rivals also use but never advertised. The reader treats the newly highlighted detail as distinctive. [L1356, 2019+]

### Models, presenters and identity in creatives

- Ads should use a wide variety of avatars, including different genders, ages and ethnicities, to promote the same product. This variety allows testing which audience responds best. [L1187, 2026]
- Models in an ad should look like the person the viewer wants to become. [L1184, 2023+]
- A well-known person can be used as an opponent or challenge rather than an endorser, because a challenge creates a story and an endorsement only adds a name. Build the ad around the contest and make the product the thing being challenged. Get written permission before publishing any name, since an unauthorized use caused a legal dispute in the original case. [L1366, undated]

### Copy flow across stages on multi-page stores

Units in this group come from the 2021-22 course and are marked older.

- Long-form sales pages make it easier to test different messages and to address different awareness segments. Traffic sources can also be used to send each awareness segment to a different message. [L0904, 2021-22]
- When a store has many product pages, write the copy so it starts at the Problem Aware stage instead of writing separate copy for each stage. Copy that starts at Problem Aware works for visitors at that stage and at every stage above it up to purchase. [L0904, 2021-22]
- Copy that starts at a later stage such as Product Aware does not work for visitors who are at an earlier stage. Copy that starts at Unaware works for all stages, but it is harder to write well. [L0904, 2021-22]
- People who are at Problem Aware or above are easy to target because they already know their pain and what they want to solve. Using one copy flow that starts with the pain is simpler than writing different copy for each awareness stage. [L0904, 2021-22]

#### Copy flow procedure

1. Name the customer's pain or problem first, since Problem Aware visitors recognize it. [L0904, 2021-22]
2. Build the desire for a solution by showing how the solution addresses that problem. [L0904, 2021-22]
3. Lead the reader through the options to the point of purchase within the same copy. [L0904, 2021-22]

[L0904, 2021-22]

- Example for a surfboard store selling to beginners: the Problem Aware stage is the realization that they have no board and cannot surf without one. The Solution Aware stage is choosing between renting and buying. The Product Aware stage is comparing beginner boards by budget, shape, style and brand. The Ninja Aware stage is choosing a specific brand, shape and style from a specific seller. This course adds Ninja Aware after Product Aware, which is not part of Schwartz's five stages. [L0904, 2021-22]
- At the Ninja Aware stage, the solution itself creates the desire. Once a buyer understands how a solution works, they want the products that deliver it, so copy for late-stage buyers should connect the product to the solution the buyer has already accepted. [L0904, 2021-22]

### Testing, planning and checklists

- Ads aimed at product-aware audiences differ from ads aimed at unaware audiences, a framework covered in the classic book Breakthrough Advertising. Creating creatives for unaware audiences is a way to grow a product that has already reached its aware market. [L1127, 2024]
- Ads that run a long time can keep working even after a product is obsolete, as long as the core need and buyer motivation are unchanged. Work out the buyer's underlying need so the copy stays valid across product cycles. [L1366, undated]

#### Pre-launch ad checklist

1. Does it tap into a desire the audience already has? [L1348, 2023]
2. Does it show the desirable outcome the customer can reach with the product? [L1348, 2023]
3. Does it future-pace the viewer, letting them picture life after using the product? [L1348, 2023]
4. Does it show social proof, such as evidence that other people already use it? [L1348, 2023]
5. Is there an authority signal, such as an expert endorsement, where the product category makes one credible (for example an orthopedic surgeon backing a back-support product)? [L1348, 2023]
6. Does the angle match the buyer's journey stage? [L1348, 2023]
