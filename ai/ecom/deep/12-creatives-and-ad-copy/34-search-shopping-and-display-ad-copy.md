## Search, shopping and display ad copy

### Principles for search ad copy

- **Write for one action.** Before writing a search text ad, define exactly which action the searcher should take. [L1351, 2021-22]
- **The ad sells the click, not the product.** Its only job is to make the searcher curious enough to visit the landing page, where the offer and the full sales argument belong. [L1351, 2021-22]
- **The landing page must match the search term.** The visitor should find what the ad promised. Because the ad only creates curiosity and sets expectations, its copy can stay short within the limited character space. [L1351, 2021-22]
- **Earn the click to lower effective cost.** The main job of a Google search text ad is to earn the click. A higher click-through rate usually lowers the effective cost per click compared with competitors bidding on the same queries. [L1401, 2020-26]
- **Lead with the buyer's benefit, not the product name.** Either move the buyer away from a pain (for example, weight gain) or toward a gain (for example, muscle growth). Benefit lines can be framed around the seller ("we have helped a million customers") or around the buyer ("seven ways to build muscle"). [L1401, 2020-26]
- **Trigger an emotion tied to the outcome.** Write for how the buyer will feel after the result, not only for what the product does. "Feel beautiful" instead of "look beautiful" connects emotionally when the product improves how people live. [L1401, 2020-26]
- **Avoid copying competitor offers.** If every competitor in the auction uses the same offer, such as "40% off", the ads blend together and clicks drop. Check competitor ads before writing yours and find a distinct angle. [L1401, 2020-26]
- **Reflect the searched keyword in three places.** Include the targeted search keyword in the display URL, the headline and the description text. Google bolds the matching keyword in these three places, which makes the ad stand out and signals relevance. [L1351, 2021-22]
- **Address the reader directly.** Write as if speaking to one individual, using direct words such as "you" and "you can" instead of dry factual statements. [L1351, 2021-22]
- **State the next action explicitly.** Always include a clear call to action and state the action directly. Do not assume the searcher knows what to do next. [L1351, 2021-22]
- **Make the call to action match the landing page.** Use a call to action that tells the searcher what happens after the click, such as a shop page, a quiz or a product comparison, so the click matches their expectation. [L1401, 2020-26]
- **Use symbols where they fit the offer.** Registered trademark (®), percent (%) and dollar ($) symbols can make a Google text ad stand out and are worth using where they fit the offer. [L1401, 2020-26]
- **Use ad extensions.** Include ad extensions in Google search text ads and choose which ones to use based on the specific ad type during setup. [L1351, 2021-22]
- **Keep the copy simple.** Ad copy does not need overthinking. Keep it simple, personal and clear about the next action; the detailed setup of campaigns and ads follows in the campaign setup stage. [L1351, 2021-22]
- **Show the promotion when one exists.** Shoppers search for the exact product and compare sellers. Name the product and its use clearly, and show the promotion in the ad when one exists, since a promo tag raises clicks. [L0801, 2021]
- **Match the ad to its page.** Google Shopping ads always link to product pages, so ad creative and page content should match. [L0801, 2021]

### Search text ad formulas and structures

**Standard formulas**

- **Result-plus-keyword formula.** State the result the buyer wants in a time-bound, concrete way, then add a keyword the searcher typed. This works because the searcher sees the exact outcome they came for, which raises relevance and clicks. Examples: "Stop back pain in 30 days" with keyword "back pain relief" in the display URL; "Dry skin fixed by Friday" with keyword "dry skin cream" in the display URL. [L1401, 2020-26]
- **Simple Google text ad structure.**
  1. Headline: include the keyword or the buyer's goal.
  2. Description line: state one benefit or feature.
  3. Second description line: add social proof (for example, a customer count) or an emotional hook.
  4. Call to action that sets the expectation for the landing page.
  5. Put the keyword or search query into the display URL path.

  [L1401, 2020-26]
- **Three-line text ad pattern.** The headline names the result the buyer wants and includes the searched keyword. The first description line turns that result into a feeling or confidence benefit. The second description line adds social proof with a customer count. The call to action states the next step. The keyword is repeated in the display URL path. Each line answers one question the searcher has at that moment (is this what I want, why this one, what happens next), and the URL confirms the match. Examples:
  - Example 1 (US sleep supplement): headline "Fall asleep faster in 7 nights" with keyword "sleep aid" in the URL; line "Wake up rested, not groggy"; line "Trusted by 250,000 sleepers, see the results"; URL path sleep-aid.
  - Example 2 (running shoes for flat feet): headline "Run pain-free this month" with keyword "running shoes flat feet" in the URL; line "Every stride feels supported"; line "Chosen by 80,000 runners, shop today"; URL path running-shoes-flat-feet.

  [L1401, 2020-26]
- **Keyword-and-benefit formula with personal address.** Put the targeted keyword in the display URL, headline and description; address the reader as "you"; end with an explicit instruction such as "Get yours now". The keyword reflection signals relevance, bolding draws the eye, the direct address feels personal, and the explicit instruction removes doubt about the next step. [L1351, 2021-22]
  - Example 1 (running shoe keyword): Headline "Trail Running Shoes - Free Returns", description "You'll find trail running shoes built for rocky paths. Shop the range today."
  - Example 2 (dog leash keyword): Headline "Reflective Dog Leashes For Night Walks", description "You can walk your dog safely after dark. Order your reflective leash now."
- **Offer-and-shipping headline formula for online stores.** The headline is the product category plus a clear price offer, such as a percentage off or an up-to discount, and then a short benefit such as free shipping. The description line adds a reason to act now, such as a limited-time sale, a free-shipping threshold, a fast pickup option, or a generous return window. Shoppers scan these quickly, so the offer and the shipping or return promise do most of the persuading. [L0751, 2021-22]
  - Example 1: "Ceiling Fans - Up to 35% Off | Free Delivery Over $75", description "Weekend Clearance Ends Sunday. Order Today for Next-Day Dispatch and 45-Day Returns."
  - Example 2: "Kitchen Organisers - 25% Off Storage Sets | Fast Local Pickup", description "Limited Stock at This Price. Free Shipping on Orders Over $60 and Easy 30-Day Returns."

**Branded product search ads**

- **Exact name headline.** The headline is the exact brand name plus product name. The description repeats that name, then adds stock status, shipping speed, price and a short call to action such as "order now". It works because the ad mirrors the search query, so the searcher sees immediately that the exact product is available. [L0837, 2025]
  - Example 1: Headline "Northline Trail Backpack 30L". Description "Northline Trail Backpack 30L in stock, ships fast, $89. Order now."
  - Example 2: Headline "Brightwave Air Purifier H13". Description "Brightwave Air Purifier H13 in stock, ships fast, $179. Shop now."

**Informational and transactional headlines**

- **Informational headlines (guide titles).** Use a "how to choose" or "what to look for" phrase, followed by the product type, followed by the use case or the reader's situation. The searcher is looking for help, so a headline that reads like a helpful article matches their intent and avoids a sales tone. Examples: "How to Choose Ski Boots for Wide Feet" and "What to Look for in Hiking Boots for Rocky Terrain". [L1350, 2020-26]
- **Transactional headlines.** Use "Shop" plus the product type, plus the use case, plus one purchase trigger such as free shipping or free returns. This works for bottom-of-funnel queries where the searcher is ready to compare or buy. The same wording fails on informational queries because it signals that a sale is the only goal. Examples: "Shop Waterproof Hiking Boots for Wet Trails, Free Returns" and "Shop Kids Rain Jackets for Muddy Play, Free Shipping". [L1350, 2020-26]

**Restricted categories**

- **Savings offer instead of a direct call.** For remarketing copy in restricted categories, replace a direct call to a person with a plain savings offer that names the store and a percentage. Formula: offer percentage + product or store + store domain, without addressing the viewer as someone who needs the product. Examples: "Save 5% on your next order at [store domain] this week." and "Get 10% off [product category] at [store domain] before the offer ends." [L0807, 2025]

### Dropshipping keyword signals in search ads

- **Phrases that surface dropshipping products.** Search ad copy keywords such as "percent off" combined with a discount figure and phrases like "get yours today", "get yours now" or "get yours" are used by about 90 to 95 percent of dropshippers in their ad text, so these keywords surface dropshipping products. [L1436, undated]

### Google Responsive Search Ads

**Headline and description mix (current, 2025-26)**

- **Use AI for volume, with a clear brief.** AI-generated ad copy solves the volume problem of manual copywriting, producing on-theme, intent-matched variants that Google rewards through Quality Score. The key is understanding what effective copy does (matching search intent and enabling combination testing) before feeding material into AI. [L0220, 2025-26]
- **Split headlines into two intent categories.** Allocate Responsive Search Ad headlines roughly two-thirds to keyword-relevance headlines that mirror the search theme, and roughly one-third to promotional headlines containing offers, guarantees or trust signals (free shipping, lifetime warranty, verified reviews). [L0220, 2025-26]
- **Do not write every headline yourself.** Do not write all 15 Responsive Search Ad headlines yourself. Populate the required headlines with keyword content and reserve slots for promotional headlines controlled at the ad group level, allowing Google to test which combinations perform best. [L0220, 2025-26]
- **Split descriptions the same way.** Structure Responsive Search Ad descriptions with the same two-part split: approximately two descriptions containing keyword-relevant content, plus one or two descriptions featuring calls-to-action, social proof or differentiators. [L0220, 2025-26]
- **Punctuation limits.** Responsive Search Ad headlines cannot contain exclamation marks. Each description may contain a maximum of one exclamation mark. [L0220, 2025-26]
- **Vary lengths.** Vary headline lengths within Responsive Search Ads instead of making them uniform. Different device widths and ad slots require both short and long text options for optimal display. [L0220, 2025-26]

**Conflict: number of headlines and descriptions**

Sources disagree on how many slots an ad has. Apply each value to its ad format:

| Ad format | Headlines | Descriptions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Responsive Search Ad (current, 2025-26) | 15 headlines, with keyword and promotional slots as above | Approximately 3-4: two keyword-relevant plus one or two call-to-action, social proof or differentiator lines | [L0220, 2025-26] |
| Older text ad format (2021-22) | 3 headlines, each maximum 30 characters | 2 descriptions, each maximum 90 characters including spaces | [L0262, 2021-22] |

**Older text ad guidance (2021-22)**

- **Headline 1 carries the keywords.** Write 3 headlines, each maximum 30 characters. Only the first headline always displays completely; others may be truncated. Always put target keywords and the product identifier in Headline 1. [L0262, 2021-22]
- **Headline 1 matches the search term.** In search ad headlines, include the target search terms (the keywords being bid on) in Headline 1 to create relevance between what the customer searched for and what is shown. [L0262, 2021-22]
- **Headline 2 names one differentiator.** For product-specific search ads, focus Headline 2 on one best feature or unique selling point that differentiates from competitors, not a generic description. [L0262, 2021-22]
- **Use the description space.** Write 2 descriptions, each maximum 90 characters including spaces. Use most of the available space to maximize information displayed. [L0262, 2021-22]
- **Create curiosity in descriptions.** In ad descriptions, create curiosity by hinting at benefits without fully explaining them. Guide readers to click through to the landing page to learn the full story, and use the landing page to answer their curiosity and provide conversion opportunities. [L0262, 2021-22]
- **Address the reader with "you" and monetary bonuses.** Personalize description copy using "you" to address the reader directly. Include special offers or bonuses with their monetary value (for example, "$700 in bonus accessories") to spark curiosity and desire. [L0262, 2021-22]
- **Use two curiosity hooks.** Build multiple curiosity hooks across your descriptions to pull readers through to the landing page. Create a primary curiosity element in the first description by teasing a key benefit without fully explaining it. Introduce a secondary curiosity element in the second description with an additional benefit, bonus value or special offer. Answer both curiosity elements on the landing page to drive conversions. [L0262, 2021-22]

**Example: product-specific search campaign**

- **Campaign and ad group structure.** Name the campaign with product identifiers combined with geographic targeting (for example, "Product IDs - Search Text - US"). Create ad groups organized by specific product models, such as the Sunray Cayenne Sauna HL400D. [L0262, 2021-22]
- **Bid and keywords.** Set the default cost-per-click bid (this example uses $0.50). Target keywords with broad match modifier syntax (the + symbol before core terms, such as +cayenne +hl400d) to capture variations while maintaining relevance. [L0262, 2021-22]
- **Headlines.** Write three headlines with varying character limits (30, 30 and 21 characters). Lead with product identification and purchase intent, then highlight key benefits such as space efficiency for compact areas, and emphasize shipping advantages. [L0262, 2021-22]
- **Display path.** Use a display path that reflects the product URL structure, for instance bathvault.com/sunray/cayenne-hl400d. [L0262, 2021-22]
- **Descriptions.** Compose two description lines with character limits (71 and 83 characters). Explain the functional advantage, such as fitting into small outdoor spaces, and communicate a concrete incentive, such as $700 in bundled accessories. [L0262, 2021-22]
- **Extensions.** Enhance the ad with call extensions containing business contact information and callout extensions that communicate competitive strengths: price-match policies, service quality and complimentary shipping options. [L0262, 2021-22]

**Callouts and sitelinks**

- **Use callouts, not sitelinks, on conversion ads.** Use callout extensions (not sitelink extensions) on product-specific conversion-focused ads. Sitelinks distract from the conversion goal by providing links to shipping pages or other products. Callout extensions can highlight offers like free shipping, guarantees or special bonuses. [L0262, 2021-22]

**Pinning and display paths**

- **Pin headlines that must appear.** Responsive search ads do not always show every headline and description, and the ad editor warns about this. To guarantee that a specific headline or description appears, pin it to a fixed position in the ad, then check the desktop and mobile previews before saving. [L1391, 2021-22]
- **Display path fields.** Each text ad has two display path fields after the domain, in the form www.yourdomain.com/Path 1/Path 2, and each path field is limited to 15 characters. Use them for short, relevant labels that reflect the brand or the product category. [L1391, 2021-22]
- **Repeat the ad group theme.** The display path fields are the visible part of the ad URL beyond the final URL, and they can repeat the ad group theme so the searcher sees a reason to click. Enter the theme keyword there when all ad group keywords are very similar. [L1352, 2020-26]

### Lead generation search ads

**Principles**

- **Grab attention with a different angle.** Lead generation ads should have a headline that grabs attention and hints at what the reader will learn by clicking. Do not use the same product-focused wording as competitors, because a different angle makes the ad stand out in search results. [L0922, 2021-22]
- **No sitelinks.** Do not add sitelink extensions to lead generation ads. Sitelinks pull attention away from the single goal of the ad, which is getting the visitor to the content page. [L0922, 2021-22]
- **Call to action and no answer in the ad.** Lead generation ads need a call to action in the description, such as "Take the quiz today", and the ad should tease the content without giving the answer away in the ad itself. [L0922, 2021-22]
- **Combine curiosity and fear.** The ad should use one or more curiosity hooks plus a fear or desire to avoid mistakes, because these are the strongest emotions when searching for expensive products. [L0922, 2021-22]

**Headline and description patterns**

- **Negative or contrarian headline.** Open with a warning that opposes what competitors in the same search term say, such as telling the searcher not to buy before reading something. This works because nearly every other ad for the term promotes buying, so the contrarian line stands out, and it sparks curiosity because the searcher feels they may be missing information. Use this sparingly, since negative headlines are rarely appropriate. [L0922, 2021-22]
- **Fear-of-mistake description.** Tease the content without revealing it by stating that the reader will learn how to avoid a costly error for this product type. This works because buyers of expensive products fear choosing the wrong item and having to return it, so the promise draws clicks. The content page must deliver the promised answer. Examples: "Learn the one measurement most buyers get wrong before you order." (standing desk) and "Find out which grind setting avoids the bitter cups beginners always buy." (espresso machine). [L0922, 2021-22]
- **Curiosity tease description.** A short description names a benefit of the content and ends with a teaser, such as "Learn which models are best for you right now." This keeps the ad curiosity-driven instead of sales-driven, and it works because the reader expects to learn something rather than be sold to. [L0922, 2021-22]
- **Quiz headline and description.** A headline invites the searcher to find their answer, such as "Find Out Which X You Need", followed by "Take the Online Quiz", then a description that promises a personal result and a call to action, such as "Take the quiz today." Quizzes attract people who want to find the right choice, and people enjoy the gamified format, which also separates the ad from competitor ads. [L0922, 2021-22]
- **Topic-tease headline.** For content-led search ads, name the expensive decision or the search term, then promise an answer, such as "Find out which [product] suits your needs before you buy." The headline should spark a question the searcher wants answered, rather than naming a product to sell. [L0922, 2021-22]
