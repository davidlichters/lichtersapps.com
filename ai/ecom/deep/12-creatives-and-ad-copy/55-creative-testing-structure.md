## Creative testing structure

### Why creative testing comes first

- Creative testing is one of the largest parts of scaling paid ads, whether it is done in-house or outsourced. Make many creatives and test them continually, because only a small share will work. [L0999, 2023]
- A weak creative can make a product look like a failure even when the product works, so test new creatives before deciding that a product is not viable. [L1038, 2026]
- Structured testing frameworks during the ads phase keep decisions based on results rather than opinion. [L0914, 2026]
- Testing many creatives early matters more than the size of the budget. For example, test about double the number of creatives on the first day, then add budget only to the creative that looks promising. [L1379, 2020+]
- Creatives are usually tested for the first time around week three. A creative that fails is information about what to change, not a final verdict on the product. [L1445, 2026]
- Smaller budgets do not give enough data to judge an ad. Decide quickly on what performs early, since waiting for a month of data at 50 per day is not practical for most sellers. [L0950, 2023]
- Creative testing often requires enough spend for the algorithm to learn. At very low daily budgets such as 5 per day, controlling spend manually gives faster, clearer results. [L1426, undated]
- Simple homemade or cropped creatives can outperform professionally shot ones, so appearance is not a reliable predictor of performance. Decide which creative stays by the data and the sales it brings. [L0562, undated]
- Unconventional or aesthetically imperfect designs can outperform polished creative. Test unusual aesthetics even when they do not match typical advertising polish. [L0316, 2026]
- Test across different concepts such as humour, education, before-and-after and shock, and let audience results decide. Launching quickly and iterating on feedback costs less than planning one polished ad and launching it late. [L1077, undated]
- Test creatives aimed at different audience types, because it is not possible to know in advance which audience will respond. Find the creative that works, then scale it. [L1016, 2026]
- Combine educated guesses about why an ad should work with variation tests of different angles, because psychology alone cannot predict results. Buyer context often only becomes clear once an ad is live, so launching and testing usually beats waiting for a perfect creative. [L1348, 2023]
- Test formats on your own product before committing budget to one type. In one fashion store, image ads generated most of the revenue while video ads did not work. [L1127, 2024]
- A product that splits its audience can be tested with creative that deliberately provokes a clear reaction. The mix of positive and negative responses shows which buyers lean toward the product. [L1315, 2023]

### When to start testing

- Start creative testing when click-through rate (CTR) or cost per click (CPC) is poor, since weak CTR signals a need for new creatives immediately. [L0902, undated]
- Also start once a product is a consistent seller and has been profitable for more than two to three weeks. [L0902, undated]
- Start creative testing only after two weeks of consistent profitable results, with two or three winning audiences. Early in a product test, focus on finding winning audiences first. [L1177, 2023]
- Before launching a test, have a finished landing page, 4 to 5 creatives (images, videos or carousels) and ready ad copy. [L0648, undated]
- Move to hook testing only after you get consistent profitable sales with ripped creatives. At that point you have a validated angle, avatar and product. [L1252, 2026]
- Hook testing is the step after ripped creatives prove profitable, and its aim is to raise CTR. Starting it earlier mixes the creative variable with the product test. [L1252, 2026]
- Start creative testing when a product is already a consistent seller, and use the audience stage first to find where it sells. [L0902, undated; L1177, 2023]

### Test structure: audiences first, then creatives

- Stage 1, audience testing for a new product, works as follows. [L0398, 2023+]
  1. Produce three to four videos for the product. [L0398, 2023+]
  2. Run them in an audience testing campaign across several audiences, such as interests, countries and age groups. [L0398, 2023+]
  3. Goal: find two to three winning audiences whose performance shows the product sells and the message reaches the right people. [L0398, 2023+]
  4. Once two to three winners are found, move to the creative testing campaign described below. [L0398, 2023+]
- Creative testing campaign setup, using ABO with the Sales objective. [L1335, 2023+]
  1. Create an ABO campaign (Sales objective) for creative testing. [L1335, 2023+]
  2. Add three ad sets, each targeting a proven winning audience (for example one winning interest, one lookalike winner, one further consistent winner). [L1335, 2023+]
  3. Place the same three video variations in each ad set; the variations differ only in the first 3 to 5 seconds (the hook) while the rest of the video is identical. [L1335, 2023+]
  4. Run the test for 2 to 3 days to collect enough data. [L1335, 2023+]
  5. Choose the winner by ROAS first; if ROAS is equal, use CTR; if CTR is also equal, use average play time. [L1335, 2023+]
  6. Use the winning creative in audience testing campaigns, CBO scaling campaigns, and wherever new audiences are tested, and reuse it for audiences that are dying down. [L1335, 2023+]
- Separate campaigns: run an interest testing campaign (winning creatives, new interests) and a creative testing campaign (winning interest, new creatives), so a new creative gets its own budget. [L0640, 2023]
- Creative testing in a separate campaign, with a new CBO or ABO campaign. [L1107, 2023]
  1. Pick two or three interests that currently perform well. [L1107, 2023]
  2. Place them in a new CBO or ABO test campaign with two ad sets. [L1107, 2023]
  3. Test three new creatives in each ad set. [L1107, 2023]
- Duplicate the winning ad set so that its audience and placements stay identical, then test the new creatives in the duplicate. [L1335, 2023+]
  1. Duplicate the winning ad set so its audience and placements stay identical. [L1335, 2023+]
  2. Put the duplicate into a new creative testing campaign. [L1335, 2023+]
  3. Change only the launch date and replace the videos with the new creatives. [L1335, 2023+]
  4. Keep the targeting unchanged, so any performance difference comes from the creative and not the audience. [L1335, 2023+]
  5. Run the test and compare the hook or creative variants inside the same audience. [L1335, 2023+]
- Test new creatives on a winning audience. [L0774, 2023]
  1. Take the winning ad sets with the best audience, age, gender and interest. [L0774, 2023]
  2. Create a separate campaign with the new creatives and compare CTR. [L0774, 2023]
  3. Move the best-performing creatives into the running ad sets. [L0774, 2023]
  4. Turn off ads that spend 20 to 30 USD without sales and keep the ones that produce sales. [L0774, 2023]
- Keep new creatives out of ad sets that contain the old winner. Facebook and TikTok send most budget to the creative with the most data, which hides how well new creatives would perform. [L1177, 2023]
- Facebook creative testing structure. Keep the winning audience and copy, change only one variable at a time (hook, creative or creator), and put each new creative in its own ad set, or put several new creatives together in one ad set to compete. Do not mix them with the old winner. [L1177, 2023]
- TikTok launch structure. One winning ad set and winning creative can be duplicated into a new campaign, while new creatives are tested in a separate ad set to avoid budget going to the old winner. [L1177, 2023]
- Standard Pinterest test structure: one campaign, one ad group with stacked interests, and three creatives (ads). Let it run for at least five to seven days, and let the platform optimise. [L1134, undated]
- Structure each test as one campaign, one ad group and three creatives, because one creative may be rejected and two others still give a valid test. [L1135, undated]
- Creative testing setup based on a winning interest. [L1212, undated]
  1. Find the winning interest or audience first. [L1212, undated]
  2. Duplicate that ad set (for example three times) with the same interest. [L1212, undated]
  3. Keep only one ad (creative) in each ad set. [L1212, undated]
  4. Compare which creative has the highest CTR. [L1212, undated]
  5. Only increase the budget once a winning audience is found, then scale the winning campaign by raising the budget. [L1212, undated]
- Keep each angle in its own column, and do not mix problem types inside one column. Testing angles separately shows which problem drives clicks and purchases, and empty columns are kept for future angles. [L0392, undated]
- Full setup for a Meta CBO test ad. [L0348, 2026]
  1. In the ad set, select Advantage+ creative / ad format Single image or video and leave Multi-advertiser ads unchecked. [L0348, 2026]
  2. Upload 6 to 10 creatives (a first round of about 9 creatives across ad sets is the target). [L0348, 2026]
  3. Choose the Facebook Page and the Instagram account. [L0348, 2026]
  4. Confirm the website URL points to the correct product page for that product. [L0348, 2026]
  5. Press Next, then use the regenerate option on the primary text and headline generated by Meta to draft copy. [L0348, 2026]
  6. Paste the copy from your external prompt into the ad primary text and headlines, and check the spacing and line breaks. [L0348, 2026]
  7. Press Publish. [L0348, 2026]
- Ads per ad set: keep the number small (about three to four creatives) so that spend per creative is controlled and each result can be read. Facebook is good at choosing the best creative for a specific audience, so several options give more chances to work. [L0646, 2023]
- Reuse the same set of three to four ads across ad sets with the same goal. Meta then tests them and spends on the best one, so a single ad is never enough. [L0649, 2019]
- Full setup needs about 13 ads in total across the campaigns. Lead generation and promotions need 3 to 4 each, the goodwill campaign needs one video, and each sandbox test needs two ads. [L0649, 2019]
- For a clean creative test, place each of four creatives in its own ad set with about 5 to 15 dollars of spend per ad. This costs more but shows which creative wins on CTR and CPC. In one ad set with four ads, one creative can take most of the budget, so the result is harder to read. [L0646, 2023]
- Do not blindly trust Facebook's spend allocation. If a creative receives little spend, duplicate the ad set into its own ad set and force spend onto it for a test period (for example three days) before deciding it is weak. [L0640, 2023]
- Standard test per product uses about three creatives: one or two videos and one or two pictures. [L0349, 2024]
- Test one interest set at a time... see section on budgets for per-ad-set amounts. [L1177, 2023]
- Test each new creative with about 10 to 20 dollars of spend in its own single-creative ad set to read its performance on its own. Combine creatives in a shared ad set only after each one has shown its results separately. [L1177, 2023]

### Isolating variables: one change per test

- Test one variable at a time. Start with the hook, because it is the most important variable at the start. Thumbnails, overall style and ad copy come later. [L0398, 2023+]
- Keep each level above the one being tested fixed, so that only one variable changes at a time. [L0902, undated]
- Creative testing works as a chain: an ad angle becomes a concept (the storyline), the concept becomes one script, the script gets three hook variations, and those hook variations run as ads. [L0902, undated]
- Static ads mean image ads and carousel ads. A concept can be built as either a video or a static ad, because the concept is the angle or script the test is based on, not the format. [L0902, undated]
- Keep a control ad with unchanged interest, copy and hook, and change only one element (such as the mid-section), so any lift is attributable to that change. [L0640, 2023]
- During creative testing, keep the offer and ad copy identical across all ads and change only the video, clip or creator. This makes the creative the only variable. [L1382, undated]
- Only change one variable at a time: in creative tests keep the targeting identical to the proven winning audience, so the performance difference comes from the creative and not the audience. [L1335, 2023+]
- Test one variable at a time: keep audience, creative, copy and targets identical and change only the offer, so a conversion uplift can be attributed to the offer. [L1315, 2023]

- Concepts per week: identify two or three marketing concepts (angles, such as comfort or sustainability), write one script per concept, and shoot three hook variations per script, giving 6 to 9 videos per week. [L0298, 2023]
- Test a minimum of three concepts per week. [L0298, 2023]
- Duplicates count only if they are changed, for example with a different call-to-action button, so five different videos can become ten ad variants. [L1383, 2022]
- Creative mix per ad set depends on the product. If the product needs a video, use at least two videos plus one picture ad. If it does not need video (such as shoes or fashion), use one carousel, one picture and one slideshow ad. A slideshow ad rotates pictures about every second. [L1212, undated]
- More creative variety gives Meta more data to optimise from the start and raises the chance of a strong creative. With a 5 USD ad set budget, 2 to 3 creatives are enough. [L0648, undated]
- Use two to three rounds of creative iteration per product at minimum. [L0640, 2023]
- Test four images of likely winning products: build one ad with a single image, one slideshow, one carousel and one meme ad, and test them all against the interest ad sets to see which has the best CTR. [L0642, undated]
- Test several creatives first, then several interests. The creative with the best CTR and the interest that brings sales are the ones to keep. [L0642, undated]
- Launch with 3 to 5 creatives per interest from the start. [L0639, 2023]
