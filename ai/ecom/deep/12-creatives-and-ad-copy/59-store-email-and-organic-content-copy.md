## Store, email and organic content copy

### Product page copy: persuasion order

- Work through product copy in this order:
  1. List the problems and pains the product solves, and the problems current and potential customers feel now, which may have brought them to the page. [L1157, 2021-22]
  2. For each problem, identify the opposite desire: the pleasurable outcome the buyer wants. [L1157, 2021-22]
  3. Describe the end result the product delivers in the buyer's life, for example at home, in the car, or wherever they use it. The product itself is not the ultimate pleasure; the result it creates is. [L1157, 2021-22]
  4. Write the copy so it shows that you understand the buyer's pain and that the product delivers the pleasure they seek. [L1157, 2021-22]
- Marketing means educating the market that a business can solve problems, fill gaps or achieve goals better than any other business. Product copy has the same purpose: show the buyer that this product solves their problem in a way no other product does. [L1157, 2021-22]
- The ultimate pleasure for a buyer is not owning the product but what the product does for them. Describe this outcome rather than the ownership itself. [L1157, 2021-22]
- Use this order for product copy:
  1. Name the customer's pain or problem back to them so they start to feel the negative emotion it causes. [L0611, 2021-22]
  2. Paint a picture of the pleasure and positive end result the product brings, so they feel the emotion they would have when the product is working for them. [L0611, 2021-22]
  3. Tell a story that makes the reader imagine the situation and feel something, to create emotion and a sense of connection. [L0611, 2021-22]
  4. Show proof, either through demonstration or through testimonials from customers who describe what they loved about the product. [L0611, 2021-22]
  5. Only then give logical support: features and specifications such as size, weight, speed and colour options, plus manuals or other documents and social proof such as reviews. [L0611, 2021-22]
- Use pain before pleasure and pleasure before features. Talking about pain alone produces negative emotions, so follow it with the positive outcome the product delivers. [L0611, 2021-22]
- Emotion-before-logic pattern: open with a pain point, move to the desired outcome, support it with a short story or proof, then close with specs and reviews that justify the choice. [L0611, 2021-22]
  - Kitchen knife, pain and pleasure: "Tired of slicing dinner into uneven chunks every night? Imagine prepping in half the time with a blade that glides through tomatoes like butter." Then add: "A 20 cm forged steel blade, 180 g, with a 5-star rating from 2,300 buyers." [L0611, 2021-22]
  - Dog harness, pain and pleasure: "Walks that end with a yanked neck and a frustrated owner? Picture every walk calm and enjoyable, like the one Maria shared after a week with her rescue dog." Then add: "Padded chest strap, reflective stitching, sizes XS to XL in four colours." [L0611, 2021-22]
- A story in product copy does not have to be fiction. A real-sounding scenario the buyer can picture creates emotion and a sense of connection with the brand, and makes the benefit feel real rather than abstract. [L0611, 2021-22]
- Product copy should open with emotional hooks (unique mechanism, power bullets, benefits) before proof points. Proof confirms an emotional decision with logic; it does not start the persuasion. [L0295, 2021-22]
- Product copy should lead with how the product makes the customer feel and how it fits into their life, not with generic claims such as high quality or shipping in two days. [L0774, 2023]
  - Pattern: name the situation the buyer cares about (a family gathering, a gift moment), show the outcome they want, then give one concrete benefit. [L0774, 2023]
  - Example one: 'Make your next family dinner easier and bring everyone together with this product.' [L0774, 2023]
  - Example two: 'Give your partner a gift that feels personal and arrives ready to use this week.' [L0774, 2023]
- Common elements developed for product page copy (benefits, unique mechanisms, problem statements) can be reused directly in emails and ads without rewriting from scratch. [L0241, 2021-22]
- Before writing ad copy from scratch, audit the website's existing copy for benefit-driven lines, bold headlines and strong claims. Adapt these proven elements for Facebook and TikTok ads so the language already resonates with the audience. [L0204, undated]

### Product page copy: headlines and claims

- Primary promise formula (the opening headline of a product description): [verb promising an outcome] + [everyday benefit] + [in the setting where the customer lives] + [superlative claim for the category]. It names the desire directly; the superlative hooks the reader but creates a claim the rest of the copy must prove. [L0396, 2021-22]
  - Example 1: 'Keep your coffee hot all morning with the most leak-proof travel mug available at this price.' [L0396, 2021-22]
  - Example 2: 'Sleep cool every summer night with the most breathable cotton bedding in its price range.' [L0396, 2021-22]
- Desire headline: name the outcome the buyer wants, using the words from your customer research, and tie it to the product's use. [L0567, 2025-26]
  - Example: 'Gear that keeps your longest weekend ride comfortable' [L0567, 2025-26]
  - Example: 'Stylish kit that holds up on every climb' [L0567, 2025-26]
- Scarcity lines: name what the shopper loses if they leave, such as stock running low or a price ending. Use one only if it is true. [L0567, 2025-26]
  - Example: 'Only a few units left in your size' [L0567, 2025-26]
  - Example: 'This colourway is sold out soon, so hold it now' [L0567, 2025-26]
- Shipping claims: do not include 'Free Shipping' in product page copy if Google Merchant Center flags it as redundant. Put the shipping offer in the meta description instead, or rely on Shopify free shipping settings. (2023) [L0274, 2023]

### Product page structure

- one source pattern, product description in three blocks (2025-26):
  - 'What it is' paragraph: material, use case, colours, size range. [L1331, 2025-26]
  - 'How it works' list: three features, each tied to a function. [L1331, 2025-26]
  - 'What you will feel' list: short sensory benefits. [L1331, 2025-26]
  - Why it works: it sells the product at the top, keeps the buy button high, and keeps the details easy to scan. [L1331, 2025-26]
  - Example pattern: 'Water-resistant upper' followed by 'helps keep damp floors out'; 'Foam insole' followed by 'steps feel softer by the door'. [L1331, 2025-26]
- Structure that worked in one one source example:
  1. Unique mechanism.
  2. A bridging paragraph that introduces the extra benefits.
  3. A bulleted list of extra features, with each benefit shown as a short paragraph.
  4. The call-to-action paragraph.
  - The primary promise may be replaced by a convenience-based promise when a product is health-related and a health promise is restricted. [L0461, 2021-22]
- Use a clear visual difference between the bridging paragraph and the feature list, such as an optional features heading, if it helps readability. Headings are optional. [L0461, 2021-22]
- Product page conversion depends on a clear, irresistible offer and on a product with real demand. A great product page cannot save a product with no demand or heavy competition. [L0690, 2022+]
- Study high-performing brand product pages (for example Blendjet and hoodie brands) to see how they structure copy, and compare them with weak product pages. Frameworks such as AIDA are a starting point. [L0690, 2022+]

### Product page proof and reviews

- Include 3 to 5 customer reviews directly within the product page copy, at the proof section. Extract them from review widgets and place them into the sales copy. [L0295, 2021-22]
- Reviews can be embedded as text, images or video. Select reviews from existing review widgets and integrate them into the copy. [L0295, 2021-22]
- A video walkthrough showing the product in actual use demonstrates the claimed functionality and works as proof where features can be shown visually. It is not viable for every product type. [L0295, 2021-22]
- Reference media coverage when a product has been featured in recognised outlets: news, magazines, newspapers, current affairs shows, reality-based TV shows, blogs or online publications such as Buzzfeed. Include optional links to the original coverage, because audiences perceive media-featured products as high quality. [L0295, 2021-22]
- Pair proof for claims with the product page. Testimonials, clinical studies and statistics used in abandoned cart emails should stay accessible from the product page or a pop-up, not be linked externally, because external links break the purchase flow. [L0261, 2025-26]
- Authorised retailer badges, procedure (2024):
  1. Add brand logos or text to the home page as a 'Brands We Carry' section. [L0253, 2024]
  2. Add 'Authorized Retailer for [Brand Name]' text to product descriptions. [L0253, 2024]
  3. Create a 6th product image in Canva showing an 'Authorized Dealer' badge. [L0253, 2024]
  4. Place this image at the end of the product image gallery, after images 1-5. [L0253, 2024]
  5. This works across the home page, collection pages and product pages. [L0253, 2024]

### Product descriptions at catalogue scale

- Run the product description prompt product by product for the whole catalogue. A short, fixed format keeps the work fast and avoids outsourcing product copy. (2025) [L1116, 2025]
- If a supplier has no product description, write one before the product goes live. Writing many descriptions by hand takes a long time; for example, 500 products require a large amount of focus, so outsourcing is an option. [L1163, 2021-22]
- Prioritise unique product descriptions by traffic (2021):
  1. Pull a monthly report in Google Analytics. [L0671, 2021]
  2. Open the Content section. [L0671, 2021]
  3. Sort pages by traffic. [L0671, 2021]
  4. Add unique copy to the highest-traffic pages first, then work down the list. [L0671, 2021]
- Beat Amazon with unique, benefit-led product descriptions rather than generic supplier text. Amazon's own descriptions are short, so a longer, story-driven description with a call to action is a clear advantage. [L1293, 2014]
- For product page images and copy, reuse supplier assets when they are allowed. Logos, product images and descriptions from the supplier can be used directly if no restrictions apply. Customise only for SEO where needed. [L0272, 2022]
- Add GIFs cut from the product video to the product page and ad creatives as a low-effort test of a richer description. Test them against the current description. [L1173, undated]
- one source pattern for organic product posts is covered under social content; for product page copy use the structure above. [L1331, 2025-26]

#### Product description prompts

Direct-response copywriter prompt, use with the product name or URL filled in (2024):

```
Adopt the persona of a direct-response copywriter and draft a product description for [product name and/or URL] following this arrangement: a headline, a short description of one to three sentences, a features-and-benefits block, and bulleted points. Base everything solely on the supplied product information and avoid fabricating specs, measurements, materials, or assertions. Before release, reword the draft into a natural, human-sounding voice while keeping the same arrangement.
```

### Product videos and media

- Add videos to product pages only if the supplier provides high-quality video assets that genuinely demonstrate the product. Do not source or create videos for every product. If the supplier sends videos, embed them from YouTube rather than uploading them directly to Shopify. [L0272, 2022]
- Short product videos placed directly at the point of sale can answer the buyer's last questions, such as unboxings, ease of use, education and common FAQs. They work in place of or alongside a written description, so the shopper gets the answer without leaving the purchase decision. (2025-26) [L0951, 2025-26]
- Short product videos of 15 to 30 seconds, modelled on the style used by Zappos, can be made for each product even without access to the physical item. [L1293, 2014]
  - Show the product, state the top benefits (such as easy-adjust knobs or easy to clean), add a call to action such as 'Buy today', and mention authorised dealer status if it applies. [L1293, 2014]
  - Example: a 20-second video for a best-selling product, where a presenter says the three strongest benefits and ends with 'Buy today'. [L1293, 2014]
  - Example: a green-screen clip of a presenter describing a kitchen gadget's ease of use and ending with 'Order now'. [L1293, 2014]
- To reuse supplier product videos on a store YouTube channel, follow these steps in order (2025):
  1. Ask the brand for permission to upload its product videos to your own channel. [L0667, 2025]
  2. Create a YouTube channel for the store. [L0667, 2025]
  3. Upload each supplier video with a correct file name and the right tags. [L0667, 2025]
  4. In each video description, add a link to the matching product page on the store. [L0667, 2025]
  5. Embed each video on its matching product page. [L0667, 2025]
- Asking viewers a question in a YouTube description can prompt replies and engagement, which can help the algorithm and conversion. Choose questions that push the viewer to respond. [L0655, undated]
- Customer videos and photos are valuable user-generated content. Use them as social proof on the site and in ads, and repost them on YouTube, where they can rank in Google because Google often places video results above other organic results. [L1012, 2021-22]

### Store structure and landing pages

- Traffic distribution for ecommerce stores: send 90 to 95 percent of traffic to the product page and only 5 to 10 percent to the home and collection pages. Optimise the product page first; home and collection pages need minimal attention. [L0321, undated]
- When a store sells several items in a niche with a similar theme, a home page featuring all products together can work. Each product should still link to a dedicated product page. Keep visitors on the landing page where possible and include add-to-cart options directly on it. [L0918, 2023]
- Hero section principle: above the fold, a landing page hero should carry a big benefit call-out, striking visuals, trust, social proof and risk reduction, so the visitor understands the offer without scrolling far. [L0353, 2023]
- Hero section workflow with AI:
  1. Know the hero section's job: visitors should immediately see what the product is, what the offer is and how it helps them. [L0353, 2023]
  2. Give the AI the product and the problem it solves, then ask for a big benefit statement. [L0353, 2023]
  3. Ask for trust elements such as social proof and authority. [L0353, 2023]
  4. Ask for copy that reduces risk, such as guarantees. [L0353, 2023]
  5. Combine the outputs into the hero section. [L0353, 2023]

### Post-purchase and packaging

- Branded insert cards can serve as a built-in upsell channel. They include a personal thank-you message, the brand logo, a preview of upcoming products and a discount code for the next purchase, which turns the package into a re-engagement tool. [L0403, undated]
- Shopify order confirmation for returning customers, to be used as written with the placeholders in square brackets replaced (2024):

```
Thank you for placing your order with [YOUR STORE NAME]! Did you know we also offer [NICHE] products at [NICHE STORE NAME]? As a thank you, all of our returning customers save [10 percent] for the next 7 days, so don't wait. Enter coupon code [GRATITUDE] at checkout to redeem your savings. Your coupon code works at [NICHE STORE URL] for the next 7 days.
```

### Email: role, length and format

- Email copy and website copy have different jobs. The email's main job is to get the reader to click through to the website, not to make the full sale. The sales pitch belongs on the product page or destination page. [L0568, 2021-22]
- Inbox attention spans are short, and readers receive more emails per day than they did five or ten years ago, so an email has very little time to hold a reader. Keep it brief rather than covering every selling point. [L0568, 2021-22]
- Long copy in an email is often not read. Product images, videos, full copy, price and the complete sales argument work better on the website, which presents them far better than an email can. [L0568, 2021-22]
- When an email links to a blog post, it does not need to prove the post's merits. It only needs to make the reader click the link, so it should not try to prove the post is great. [L0568, 2021-22]
- Image-heavy emails are more likely to land in the Promotions or Spam tab. Simple text emails that use a name and read like a normal message tend to reach the inbox and get opened more. Split test plain text against image plus text. [L0605, undated]
- Promo block for a time-limited sale, as a reusable structure. Keep the same structure for every promo so readers learn where to look (2025-26):
  - A headline with the sale name, for example 'Flash deals'.
  - A time window line, for example '24-hour flash deals'.
  - An extra discount on sale items, for example 'Extra 15% off sale items'.
  - One or two sentences that tell the reader why the sale matters.
  - A SHOP NOW call-to-action button.
  - Full block as a reusable structure:

```
Email newsletter promo block for a time-limited sale, as a reusable structure: a headline with the sale name (for example 'Flash deals'), a time window line (for example '24-hour flash deals'), an extra discount on sale items (for example 'Extra 15% off sale items'), one or two sentences that tell the reader why the sale matters, and a SHOP NOW call to action button. Keep the same structure for every promo so readers learn where to look.
```

- Pain-point email pattern (2025-26): open with the frustration the customer has when the product fails, phrased as a question. Then show you understand it, say how the product removes it, and give the CTA. [L0567, 2025-26]
  - Example: 'Tired of shoes that pinch by mile three?' [L0567, 2025-26]
  - Example: 'Sore wrists after every typing day? Fix that today.' [L0567, 2025-26]

### Email: subject lines and sign-up

- Problem-focused subject and teaser formula:
  - '[Emotion word] because [common problem]'
  - 'Entrepreneurs: [time-based guilt problem]'
  - 'Students: [achievement-limiting problem]'
  - Example: 'Guilty because you don't spend time with family?' (problem exposure), followed by 'World of distractions makes it impossible. We fixed this.' (solution claim) [L0307, 2023+]
  - Example: 'Can't stay focused at work?' (problem), followed by 'Entrepreneurs are losing 10+ hours weekly to distractions.' (severity), followed by 'New focus solution reduces distraction by 90% in 7 days.' (outcome) [L0307, 2023+]
- Sign-up headline: name the discount and the condition of the first purchase, phrased as a question or an invitation. Stating the reward up front makes the email exchange worth it. [L0607, undated]
  - Example: 'Want 15 percent off your first order?' [L0607, undated]
  - Example: 'Get a free gift with your first order when you join today.' [L0607, undated]
- Sign-up button: use a verb that describes what the visitor receives, such as unlock or reveal, so the button promises the reward instead of asking for a submission. [L0607, undated]
  - Examples: 'Unlock my 15 percent off' and 'Send me my code.' [L0607, undated]
- Decline link: write the opt-out as a short, friendly statement that keeps the visitor's dignity and makes the choice feel deliberate. [L0607, undated]
  - Examples: 'No thanks, I will skip the savings' and 'Not right now, I will pay regular price.' [L0607, undated]

### Email: transactional and review requests

- Testimonial request email, sent within 24 hours of purchase:

```
Testimonial request email sent within 24 hours of purchase. Subject: hey, we would like to refund you your purchase. Body: thanks for buying, I really appreciate that you trust me so much. You are eligible for a refund of your purchase. The only thing I need from you is a testimonial. Please fill out this form, which asks for your first name, last name, a video upload, and a checkbox confirming you agree that the content can be used in our ads.
```
