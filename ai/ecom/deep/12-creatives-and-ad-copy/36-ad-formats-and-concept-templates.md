## Ad formats and concept templates

### Format, concept and how to choose

- An ad format is the medium an ad uses, such as video, static image or carousel. A concept is the type of piece made in that format, for example a VSL, a listicle, an explainer, an unboxing, a before-and-after or a text-only black screen. [L0374, 2026]
- Choose the format by the product and the audience, then test. A format that wins for one product can underperform for another, and some products will not fit a layout at all. [L0341, 2023; L0343, 2023]
- Test a mixture of formats to find the winner: lifestyle images, offer graphics with features and reviews, and "this or that" benefit layouts. [L0067, 2023]
- Use the best assets available, and clip or repurpose competitor videos if needed. [L0640, 2023]

### Testing system: swipe file and test rules

- Keep a small swipe file of proven ad creative templates, so several products or offers can be tested quickly without waiting for slow or unreliable outsourced creative. Fast testing shows which product and angle respond best before you invest in polished assets. [L0341, 2023; L0343, 2023]
- A testimonial-style Facebook ad run at a £20-30 daily spend produced a strong early result, so social-proof formats are worth testing first. [L0341, 2023]
- Test each template against each product rather than assuming it transfers. [L0341, 2023]
- Keep templates editable in Canva, so product photos, colours and text can be swapped in minutes. [L0341, 2023]
- Track which template and offer combination gets the best click-through rate and spend efficiency before you scale it. [L0341, 2023]
- Vary creative formats within one set: one with text on the image, one with no text, and one short video with a slowly moving background. Winners differ by audience, so test all of them. [L0649, 2019]

Template overview from the swipe file:

| Template | Format | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| Us vs them | Your product on one side, the competitor's on the other, with 2-3 named differences | You can name clear, visible advantages in build quality, materials or protection |
| Testimonial | Several customer quotes with speech-bubble style and a trust-signal line at the bottom | You have many genuine reviews from different customers |
| Feature callout | One large benefit headline, cut-out product in the centre, features pointing to parts of it, and a short step line | The product has several visible features that support one promise |
| Single benefit callout | One bold benefit headline with a social-proof badge underneath | You want one direct promise with no competing message |
| Offer / free gift | Product name, top three benefits, product image, the discount or free gift with its price difference, and a call to action | You want to test a discount or bundle offer quickly |

### Image, video and carousel: where sources differ

Sources disagree on the best format. Use both values, depending on the product:

- **Video first:** Video creatives significantly outperform image-only ads, so use video whenever possible. [L0280, undated]
- **Video often wins:** Video often outperforms static images for engagement, but image ads can also work well. [L0640, 2023]
- **Video not always:** Video creatives do not always beat static images. A product suited to video can still perform better with pictures, so test both formats rather than assuming video wins. [L0690, 2022+]
- **Image cheaper, sometimes better:** Image ads can be cheaper and convert well, sometimes better than video, especially for products that are not obvious problem solvers. Video usually converts better overall but costs more to run. [L0692, 2023]
- **Image for self-selling products:** Image ads can suit visually appealing products that sell themselves, such as home decor, even though video is usually expected to convert better for problem-solving products. [L0692, 2023]
- **Demonstration needs video:** Video is the better format for products that need demonstration, such as a scent, a texture or a visible use. Image ads can perform well for self-explanatory products. Jewellery usually needs close-ups and in-context shots, so test both. [L0524, 2023]
- **Case: picture ads won on traffic:** A clothing brand hired a photographer for a product video and ran both video ads and picture ads. The picture ads drove more traffic to the store, so the video moved to retargeting to keep the product in front of people who had already seen the brand. Video ads were also more expensive per thousand impressions than picture ads. [L0642, undated]
- **Static jewellery in context:** Static images of products such as jewellery can work when shown in context, such as worn with an outfit or in an elegant setting. Video or UGC adds more story and is recommended when images do not tell the story well. [L0995, 2023]
- **Reframing with UGC:** Reframing a product through a different audience angle, such as a gift from a partner or a personal story, can turn a flooded market into a new segment, because the ad speaks to a specific person in a specific situation. Customer testimonials in a natural voice (UGC) are often more convincing than a static product image. [L0995, 2023]

Carousel and multi-image rules:

- When using image creatives instead of video, use the carousel format so customers can view multiple angles and understand the product from different perspectives. [L0280, undated]
- In one fashion test, three formats were used: a single picture ad, a carousel ad and a slideshow ad with images changing every few seconds. The carousel performed best in the lesson. [L0661, undated]
- Carousel ads work well for products with a lot of lifestyle imagery, such as fashion. Single image ads can also be strong. Natural lifestyle image ads should be included in testing alongside designed graphics. [L0343, 2023]
- Carousel ads can show several images in one unit, which helps vary the message within a single ad. [L0649, 2019]
- Carousel ads that show several products and point to a collection page, rather than a single product page, work well, especially for a buy two, get the third free offer. [L0388, 2024+]

Format combinations:

- Split-screen layout with an endcart display alongside vertical video: the endcart carries static product information, while the video adds dynamic engagement, so both formats sit in one creative unit. [L0258, undated]
- Founder videos filmed to camera are a common workhorse format if the founder is comfortable on camera. Each video can carry a different message, idea or angle at low cost. [L0483, 2025-26]
- A product demonstration filmed with a camera on a desk, showing how the product works, is a common workhorse format. It needs little setup and can be repeated for different products or angles. [L0483, 2025-26]
- Other UGC formats include before-and-after and testimonial videos, but the problem-solution framework is a reliable default structure for most products. [L0561, 2023+]
- A user-generated style clip, where a buyer describes receiving a product as a gift, makes the message feel personal and credible. Static images can work for products whose appeal is in the look, but a story in context often gives more. [L0999, 2023]

### Same message, different styles

- The same message can be delivered in different styles: large text on a static image, a person showing the problem in a video, a founder ad or a versus ad. Hooks can differ within one style, for example a scientific diagram versus a real-life demonstration, and they attract different kinds of viewers. [L0485, 2025-26]
- For an interview-style ad, the visuals should look like a real interview, the copy should mention the interview with an expert, and the audio should clearly show a question followed by an answer. Every lever makes the format obvious. [L0485, 2025-26]
- Storytelling suits simple gadgets such as security cameras, because creators can invent scenarios that show the product being useful. Use it only if you can tell a convincing story, and keep the claims honest. [L0372, 2022]

### Testimonial ads

**Core formula**

- Pain-to-relief transformation: the customer states the original problem or emotional state, then describes the moment of change, then ends with a specific result with no negatives. This works because it shows transformation through experience rather than claims, and the absence of drawbacks ("no meds") is as persuasive as the benefit. [L0313, 2026]
  - Example 1: "Dealt with afternoon fatigue for years, now I have steady energy all day without jitters." [L0313, 2026]
  - Example 2: "Couldn't focus during meetings, this changed my work life and I'm actually productive again." [L0313, 2026]
- Keep testimonials short. Testimonials under 20 words outperform long narratives because social media users scan rather than read. Each testimonial should name one specific transformation ("life back", "control again", "migraines stopped"). Negative comparison (what the product is NOT: "no meds", "no side effects") increases credibility. [L0313, 2026]
- Avoid generic testimonials such as "Amazing product!", "Great quality" or "5 stars". They provide no social proof, because any competitor could claim the same. Specific testimonials tied to the exact problem and transformation ("Finally, real relief without meds", "Gave me my life back") work because they are hard to fake. [L0313, 2026]

**Testimonial concept template**

```
Build a testimonial ad by pairing a customer's face with a direct quote about their experience, such as "I tried three things before this and nothing worked until I found it." Display the product clearly in the creative, ideally showing the customer actively using or holding it. This establishes credibility by demonstrating how a real person achieved success with the product.
```
[L0316, 2026]

**Targeting skeptics**

- Testimonial ads for skeptics and proof-driven buyers follow: past struggle → tried other solutions → discovered your product → current success. [L0315, 2026]
  - Example 1: "Tested four competitors until finding this one-finally solved the problem." [L0315, 2026]
  - Example 2: "Spent weeks searching for alternatives before discovering ours works better." [L0315, 2026]
  - This works because skeptics require peer validation from someone relatable before trusting. [L0315, 2026]

**Testimonial creative pattern**

- Formula: volume of happy customers + believable, varied reviews + two trust badges. Buyers trust visible proof from other people more than brand claims. [L0343, 2023]
  - Example: "Over 12,000 happy homes" with three different reviews and a free returns badge. [L0343, 2023]
  - Example: "Trusted by 5,000 runners" with a 30-day fit guarantee badge. [L0343, 2023]
- Use several strong testimonials and set the colour scheme to your brand colours. Do not use testimonials from one reviewer only, because it looks unrealistic. [L0341, 2023]
- Use testimonials from different people, because a set that all comes from one reviewer looks fake. Adapt the brand colours and the font to your own branding, and keep the background open for colour changes. [L0343, 2023; L0341, 2023]

**Trust signals**

- Place trust signals at the bottom of the ad, such as a 60-day money-back guarantee and free shipping for life. They reduce purchase hesitation in the ad itself. Use only guarantees you can actually honour. [L0341, 2023; L0343, 2023]
- Trust signals at the bottom of a testimonial creative: use them to reduce purchase hesitation in the ad itself. [L0343, 2023]

**Video testimonials**

- Video testimonials are an affordable way to share the message and generate many sales. In the example, the best video testimonial ad produced a couple hundred thousand dollars in revenue. Reviews were reinforced by sending the reviewer a brief covering the required topics. [L0334, 2017]
- A client testimonial video can send viewers straight to the product page, followed by retargeting of everyone who watched it. [L0643, 2015]
- A testimonial video ad can send viewers straight to a marketplace listing through an affiliate link, and the people who watched can then be retargeted. The lesson's example used an Amazon affiliate listing as the destination. [L0643, 2015]

**Proof-heavy layouts**

- In print, a flyer with a headline, a photo of the seller and dozens of participant quotes arranged in columns feels overwhelming in its proof. A numbered list of 22 reasons to buy, or a brochure with 101 customer stories, reads as convincing even before the reader checks the claims. [L0340, undated]

### Social proof ads

- Social proof ads target herd-followers and bandwagon buyers using: customer volume + satisfaction rating + credible third-party mention. These buyers trust decisions validated by large customer bases combined with respected authorities. [L0315, 2026]
  - Example 1: "150,000+ customers. 4.8 stars. Featured in Forbes and TechCrunch." [L0315, 2026]
  - Example 2: "98,000+ users. 4.9 rating. Recommended by Wall Street Journal." [L0315, 2026]

**Social proof concept template**

```
Social proof concept: "50,000+ customers. 4.8 stars. Featured in Vogue and Men's Health." Use numbers, ratings, and media mentions to signal credibility and show that others trust the brand.
```
[L0316, 2026]

- Social proof ad: a short customer quote with a five-star rating and the store name, linked to the product or store. It works because buyers trust other buyers more than the brand itself. [L0649, 2019]
  - Example 1: "Five stars. The fit was perfect and delivery took three days, says a happy customer." [L0649, 2019]
  - Example 2: "Five stars for the quality, says a repeat buyer. Shop the store today." [L0649, 2019]
- Stacking emotion and credibility: merge a customer testimonial with social proof stats in one concept. [L0316, 2026]
  - Example: "I had back pain every day by 3pm. After 2 weeks with Posture Pro, it's totally gone. 50,000+ relieved users. 4.8 stars. Featured in Vogue and Men's Health." This stacks emotional trust (personal story) with credibility (numbers, ratings, media mentions). [L0316, 2026]

### Us vs them and comparison ads

- Us-vs-them comparison: place your product on one side and the competitor's on the other. Grade the competitor's background down so your side looks like the clear winner. Name specific points where you are better, such as seam quality or protective features, so the viewer sees the difference in one glance. A light pattern and a white gradient overlay add texture and keep the layout from looking flat. [L0341, 2023]
  - Example: "Our jacket has double-stitched seams and reinforced zips, while theirs has single stitching and exposed zip edges." [L0341, 2023]
  - Example: "Our kettle boils in 90 seconds with auto shut-off, while theirs takes 4 minutes and has no safety cut-off." [L0341, 2023]
- Them-vs-us creative: competitor product on the left, your product on the right, with a short list of what makes yours better. Formula: the competitor side shows the weakness, your side shows the strength, and both sides stay visually simple. It frames the comparison in one glance without long copy. [L0343, 2023]
  - Example: "Our zipper outlasts their plastic clasp", with the old brand dimmed on the left. [L0343, 2023]
  - Example: "Their bottle leaks, ours seals in 3 seconds", with a matching split layout. [L0343, 2023]
- Comparative framework ("We vs the Others"):
  1. Use a split-screen layout.
  2. Display your product's unique selling proposition and advantages versus what competitors lack.
  3. Include a clear call to action.
  [L0258, undated]
- Comparison video pattern: show a well-known product the audience already knows, then introduce your product as the better alternative. The hook says the audience has seen the known product and asks whether they have seen yours. It works best when the compared product is familiar to viewers. [L0795, undated]
  - Example hook: "Everyone has tried this popular kitchen gadget, but have you seen the one that actually cuts prep time in half?" [L0795, undated]
  - Example hook: "You know the classic desk lamp everyone owns, now meet the one that adjusts itself to your workday." [L0795, undated]
- Trend comparison pattern: use a trending audio where a speaker compares a competitor with their own product, then overlay on-screen text that lists the product's benefits in quotation marks. Comparisons and POV formats also perform well. [L0795, undated]
  - Example: a kitchen tool shows a messy competitor result, then its own clean result with on-screen text such as "Cleaner edges, no sticky residue". [L0795, undated]

### Benefit and feature callouts

**Benefit concept template**

```
Benefit concept: Place a headline at the top (e.g., "Sleep through the night, wake without back pain. Every morning."), list 3-4 concrete benefits below (e.g., "Relieve back pain in as little as 7 days," "Improve posture and boost confidence"), put the product image in the middle, and add a discount or CTA at the bottom. Keep it simple and outcome-focused.
```
[L0316, 2026]

- Benefit ad pattern for outcome-first and problem-aware buyers: problem state → transformation promised → concrete result. These buyers already recognise their problem and respond to specific outcome promises. [L0315, 2026]
  - Example 1: "Stop dealing with [pain point]. Achieve [desired state] daily." [L0315, 2026]
  - Example 2: "Never again experience [problem]. Get [benefit] consistently." [L0315, 2026]
- Worked example, posture corrector: headline "Wake Up Pain-Free. Every Single Morning." Bullet points: "Relieve back and neck pain in as little as 7 days," "Improve posture and boost confidence," "Lightweight, comfortable and invisible under clothes." Product image centred. CTA "SHOP NOW" with discount "20% OFF Today, Offer ends Sunday" and the regular price crossed out. This structure shows benefit first, proof second, urgency third. [L0316, 2026]
- Feature callout pattern: one large benefit as the headline, a few features pointing to specific parts of a cut-out product placed in the centre, and a simple step-by-step line at the bottom. It links one clear promise to visible product details. It works best for products that can be cut out and placed centrally, and does not fit every product. [L0341, 2023]
  - Example: "Big benefit: Sleep through the night. Features: quiet motor, blackout cover, 8-hour timer, with arrows pointing to each part." [L0341, 2023]
- Benefit callout creative: one large headline states the main benefit, and several short feature lines with arrows point to product parts that support it. Formula: headline benefit + two to four supporting features + a simple call-to-action step at the bottom. One clear promise is easier to grasp than a list of features. [L0343, 2023]
  - Example: "Sleep deeper in minutes", with arrows to a weighted lining and a cooling layer. [L0343, 2023]
  - Example: "Clean floors without bending", with arrows to a telescopic handle and a swivel head. [L0343, 2023]
- A benefit callout creative works best for products that can be cut out and placed centrally, with features written around them. Products that do not fit this layout may still work with a different structure, so test each product. [L0343, 2023]
- Feature point-out framework:
  1. Start with a close-up image of the product.
  2. Add 4-5 text-based advantages surrounding the product.
  3. Use Canva or Photoshop to enable easy creation of variations and iterations.
  [L0258, undated]
- Single big-benefit creative: one benefit in a large headline with a small social-proof badge, such as a count of happy customers. Formula: one benefit + one badge + short direct language, with no feature list. A single promise reads at a glance and the badge adds trust. [L0341, 2023; L0343, 2023]
  - Example: "Stops leaks in one wipe", with a badge reading "Thousands of happy customers". [L0343, 2023]
  - Example: "Dries in 90 seconds", with a badge reading "4,000 five-star reviews". [L0343, 2023]
- One clear benefit headline with a badge reading "Thousands of happy customers" avoids the bad outcome. [L0341, 2023]
- Test the single benefit callout against feature-led layouts, since results vary by product. [L0341, 2023]

### Offer, free gift and urgency ads

**Free gift concept template**

```
Free gift concept: Lead with "If you order today in the next 24 hours, we'll throw in [companion product or resource] free with your order." This creates urgency by setting a 24-hour deadline and adds perceived value. Use as primary text and headline.
```
[L0316, 2026]

- Free gift ad pattern for fence-sitters and value-focused buyers: core product + bonus offer + time anchor. These buyers decide on total perceived value relative to price, and bonuses shift that ratio favourably. [L0315, 2026]
  - Example 1: "Order this month and get the [accessory] free." [L0315, 2026]
  - Example 2: "Buy now and receive [complementary product] included." [L0315, 2026]
- Offer creative: product image and name at the top, the top three benefits, a big discount or free-gift element with the price difference shown, and a clear call to action. It answers what the product is, why it is worth buying and what the deal is, in one image. Use it to test offers quickly before building video. [L0341, 2023]
- Offer test creative: product image, three core benefits and one clear offer, such as buy one get one free, a free gift or 40 percent off. Formula: product + three benefits + one offer in a single static layout. It isolates which offer a market responds to, faster than a full video. [L0343, 2023]
  - Example: "Buy one, get the second free", with three benefits and a product shot. [L0343, 2023]
  - Example: "Free travel pouch with every order", with three benefits and a price-anchored gift image. [L0343, 2023]
- Offer images that display "choose this or that" options for a free gift, list multiple benefits and showcase the main product prominently are particularly effective. [L0067, 2023]
- Offer graphics highlight the promotion prominently, with features called out and customer reviews displayed. [L0067, 2023]
- Promotion ad: name the event and the offer clearly, then add a call to action. A named deadline and a concrete offer make people act during the sale window. [L0649, 2019]
  - Example: "Cyber Monday: buy one desk lamp, get the second free, tap to claim." [L0649, 2019]
  - Example: "Summer clearance: 30% off all bags this weekend only, shop the deal now." [L0649, 2019]

**Urgency concept template**

```
Urgency concept: "Only 45 left in stock. Sale ends Sunday at midnight." Combine scarcity (limited inventory) with a deadline (specific day and time) to push procrastinators over the line.
```

- Urgency ads for procrastinators and almost-sold buyers use scarcity indicator + specific deadline. Procrastinators need both a reason to act now (time limit) and proof of consequence (scarcity) to overcome delay patterns. [L0315, 2026]
  - Example 1: "Only 22 left. Sale ends Friday midnight." [L0315, 2026]

### Giveaway ads

- Headline pattern: start with WIN, state the prize count as 1 of N, name the product, and add a clear call to action such as Enter Now. The pattern works because a specific prize and a small win ratio create curiosity and produce cheap clicks. A disclaimer about the win ratio must still appear on the ad. [L0797, 2025-26]
  - Example: "WIN 1 of 3 Limited-Edition Hoodies: Enter Now." [L0797, 2025-26]
  - Example: "WIN 1 of 5 Summer Bundles, Enter Below." [L0797, 2025-26]
- Static image ads work well for giveaway funnels. Run several variations and test them. Use the giveaway for new product drops, because it generates a large amount of awareness at very low CPM. [L0797, 2025-26]
- The example giveaway ad carries two calls to action: an entry button linking to the giveaway landing page, and a separate Shop now button linking to the store. Using both serves entrants and buyers at the same time. [L0797, 2025-26]
