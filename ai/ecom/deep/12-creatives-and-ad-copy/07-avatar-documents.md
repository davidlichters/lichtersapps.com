### Avatar documents

Write the avatar before any copy. Use the ten questions below to build it, then check the result against an example persona. [L1370, undated]

**Ten questions about the target buyer, answered in this order:**

1. What keeps them awake at night, or what worries them? [L1370, undated]
2. What are they afraid of? [L1370, undated]
3. What makes them angry, and who do they blame? [L1370, undated]
4. What are their top three daily frustrations? [L1370, undated]
5. Which trends are changing their business or life now and later? [L1370, undated]
6. What do they secretly want most? [L1370, undated]
7. Does the way they make decisions have a built-in bias? [L1370, undated]
8. Do they use their own vocabulary? [L1370, undated]
9. Who else sells something similar to them, and how? [L1370, undated]
10. Who has tried to sell them something similar, and why did it fail? [L1370, undated]

### Research before writing

- Research the buyer before writing any copy, because the copy only works if the writer truly understands the buyer. [L1370, undated]
- Visualise a typical recipient's whole day, from arrival at work to how and when their mail reaches them. Note what else occupies their attention at that moment. [L1370, undated]
- Study the reader before the product. Find the features that match the reader's needs and build the letter around them. A letter that starts from the buyer's reactions and presents only the matching product features is almost always effective. [L1369, undated]
- Let the buyer's priorities drive the copy, not the seller's. A sales letter gets no live feedback to correct a misunderstanding. [L1370, undated]
- A corporate buyer weighing a donation or purchase typically asks three things: [L1370, undated]
  1. What benefit justifies the cost?
  2. Who else already chose this?
  3. Where will the money come from in the budget? [L1370, undated]

### Ad angles and concept building

- Define the ad angle before the script. An ad angle is a specific take on a problem for a target audience. [L1425, undated]
- Not every product solves a problem. Some products fulfil a need, such as self-expression, identity, or a hobby. For these, connect the ad emotionally to the desired feeling rather than to a problem. [L1426, undated]

**Lateral thinking exercise for a stuck ad concept:**

1. Pick a random source of words, such as a shuffled set of words or a dictionary. [L1366, undated]
2. Pull out the first three words you see. [L1366, undated]
3. Force a connection between those three words and the product you are selling. [L1366, undated]
4. Build an ad concept from the connection, keeping the selling goal in mind. [L1366, undated]

### Awareness stage and funnel messaging

- For audiences who do not yet know a product category exists, or do not realise they have a problem it solves, do not push a purchase. Open with the symptom or desire they already feel, such as tiredness. Explain the benefits and present the product as a possible answer, so the first step is awareness, not a sale. [L1386, 2020-26]
- Showing a buy-now offer to people who are not ready to buy wastes ad spend. It also drives away prospects who would have converted later. Serve the right message at each funnel stage to keep those prospects in the funnel instead of losing them. [L1386, 2020-26]

### On-screen models

- Use one consistent on-screen model across all videos. In competitor videos, a consistent model increased sales, especially when the product targeted women. Using her in every video can build recognition. [L1444, 2022]
