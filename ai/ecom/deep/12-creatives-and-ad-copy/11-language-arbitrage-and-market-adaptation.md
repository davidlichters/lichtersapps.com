### Language arbitrage and market adaptation

**Converting a winning ad to a new language**

- Language arbitrage: convert a winning ad to English by replacing the voice-over with ElevenLabs and changing the book cover with GPT image editing. [L1019, 2026]
- Google AI Studio (Gemini) transcribes a winning ad, including its primary text and headline. Claude rebuilds the messaging around the angle, promise and objection. [L1019, 2026]
- When expanding to new markets with existing winning ads, apply the same competitor research process to your successful creatives, then reproduce them in the language and cultural context of the new market. [L0258, undated]
- When entering a new market, adapt a competitor's proven ad copy and landing page by translating and improving it instead of copying it unchanged. Ad copy tests are a key way to find better results for the same product. [L1185, 2023]

**Adapting a competitor's foreign-language creatives**

1. Save each image one after another from the competitor's store or ads. [L1172, 2023+]
2. Translate the text in each image using a translation tool such as Google Translate. [L1172, 2023+]
3. Recreate the image in Canva with the translated text, or crop out the text if the image does not need to show the product's function. [L1172, 2023+]

**Testing a Spanish book in English**

1. Choose a Spanish book that shows demand in Kalodata and has a clear problem it solves. [L1175, 2026]
2. Remove the Spanish text from the captions and product page details. [L1175, 2026]
3. Translate the content into English and produce an English voice-over with ElevenLabs. [L1175, 2026]
4. Replace the product page content with the English version. [L1175, 2026]
5. Test the English version with English ads. [L1175, 2026]

- Converting a Spanish book to English is possible but not guaranteed, and it needs a higher skill level to translate fine details well. Avoid it as a beginner and consider it once the basics and scaling are in place. [L1175, 2026]

**Improvement cycle for a winning creative**

1. In Ads Manager, identify the creative with the most website purchases. [L1019, 2026]
2. Convert the winner to text in Google AI Studio (Gemini), capturing both the primary text and the headline. [L1019, 2026]
3. Rebuild the messaging with Claude by defining the angle, the promise and the objection it addresses. [L1019, 2026]
4. Adapt the winning message into new formats: podcast, conference, seminar, claymation, cartoon and UGC AI ads. [L1019, 2026]
5. Run a 3-day launch test in a testing CBO and evaluate website purchases and ROAS. [L1019, 2026]

- Take the best-performing creative, pass it through the transcribe-and-rebuild cycle, and release two new formats that carry the same message. [L1019, 2026]

### What to change and what to keep

**Elements and rules**

| Element | What to do | Ids |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Keep it when copying a winner. Change it only when the copy must be unique, or as a later test once a product sells consistently. | [L1177, 2023; L1161, 2024+; L1252, 2026] |
| Video length | Keep it in the first launch of ripped creatives. | [L1252, 2026] |
| Caption | Keep the text; if you cover original captions, use the same text on top. Change caption colour if needed. | [L1252, 2026; L1218, undated; L0363, 2022+] |
| Music | Keep the original music unless you are testing; changing it can break a working creative. | [L1177, 2023] |
| Speed and clip order | Alter after about 7 to 10 seconds (or after the first 3 to 5 seconds), not the hook. | [L0398, 2023+; L1218, undated] |
| Logo | Replace or cover the competitor logo; add your own at the top of the frame. | [L0398, 2023+] |
| Metadata | Always alter it for a replicated creative. | [L0398, 2023+; L1316, 2024+] |
| Personal details | Remove TikTok Shop or Orange Cart mentions. | [L0704, 2026] |
| Product claims | Replace only product facts, prices, ingredients and claims; invent no new claims. | [L0374, 2026] |
| Messaging | Keep the structure; rewrite the words. | [L0772, 2026; L0694, 2024; uncertain] |

Note: the messaging row cites [L0772, 2026] and [L0374, 2026] for structure-only rewriting; use the framework above as the reference.

**Replace borrowed footage**

- Replace borrowed footage with your own UGC once the product shows traction. [L0693, 2024]
- Once a product shows good performance, invest in custom content for it. [L1218, undated]

**Selection and fit**

- A strong hook alone is not enough. Check that the outcome the video shows matches what your product delivers. [L0704, 2026]

### Testing copies

**Test design**

- Test the new version in a controlled budget before scaling. [L0335, 2023]
- Testing a winning competitor ad: pick the competitor ad that is scaling, copy the hook exactly, edit the later part of the video (clips or pacing), change the metadata (for example video speed or clip layout) with a metadata-editing tool so the ad is not identical, then test the new version with a controlled budget before scaling. [L0335, 2023]
- Keep a competitor's idea only if the click and result numbers reach your targets. Otherwise change the creative or the offer. [L1219, undated]
- Testing competitor creatives for a new product: download the videos competitors run, upload them to your Business Manager, test their performance for your product, and produce your own edited version once you see results. [L1347, undated]
- Rotate new mashups when a version stops performing. [L0347, 2026]
- Run a 3-day launch test in a testing CBO and evaluate website purchases and ROAS. [L1019, 2026]
- Ripped creatives: start with unchanged videos for the product and offer test, then begin hook testing to raise CTR once profitable sales are steady. [L1252, 2026]

**Test order**

1. Start with existing high-performing videos from the market. Edit the first three seconds to find the best CTR. [L0513, undated]
2. Only then produce unique videos or work with creators or influencers. [L0513, undated]
3. Reuse existing videos to test several products at once, and invest in custom content for products that perform. [L1218, undated]

### Cases

- A German acupuncture pen store was launched by copying a US competitor's product page and ad feed in full. With a EUR 50 test, the first day brought five sales. The product scaled to about EUR 5,000 to 6,000 per day at roughly 30 percent margin. Sales dropped later as the creatives wore out, and new creatives were sourced from a freelancer to replace them. [L0660, 2019+]
- A ripped-creative operator sold a product with no original content and reported more than 16,000 USD revenue in one day from ripped ads alone. [L1252, 2026]
- The NAD+ hook-and-testimonial remix described above produced the first winning ad from the first winning product. [L0901, 2026]
- A ripped copy sent to a drop-shipping style test: a copied competitor page and ad feed, with the EUR 50 test above, followed the same path as the acupuncture case. [L0660, 2019+]
