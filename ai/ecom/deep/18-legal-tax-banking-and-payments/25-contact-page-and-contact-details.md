### Contact page and contact details

#### Contact page

- Shopify provides a default contact page for every store. Add its link to the footer [L1233, undated].
- The default page named Contact is already present under Online Store > Pages > Add page. If the store is password protected, remove the password before viewing it [L1233, undated].
- Create or confirm a contact page with at least two contact methods, for example a phone number and a business email, optionally Shopify's contact form [L0377, 2020-26].
- Use a business email address, not a personal email, on the contact page and in the footer [L0377, 2020-26].
- Use a support mailbox on the store's own domain, for example support@yourstorename.com. Use the same address in the return policy, shipping policy and contact page, and make sure someone reads it [L1233, undated].

#### Minimum contact details: sources compared

| Source | Minimum required | Notes | Ids |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google (contact information rule) | At least two methods, recommended email plus phone, with business hours | Address not required for this model. Email and phone must be real and working | [L0167, undated] |
| Google (general expectation) | At least two of: physical address, phone number, email address | Phone plus email meets the requirement without a physical address | [L0724, 2020-26] |
| Cross-border minimum | Email is essential; business address and phone are optional | Use the email in every policy | [L1233, undated] |
| one source | Three details: phone number, email address, postal address | Phone and address should also appear in the footer. Google may check these on the site | [L1145, 2021-22] |

- Google's contact information rule and the general expectation both accept a phone plus email without an address [L0167, undated; L0724, 2020-26].
- The 2021-22 source asks for all three details. Older, so treat it as the stricter option, not as the current rule [L1145, 2021-22].

#### Filling the contact information section

1. Open the policy in Shopify Settings > Policies and scroll to the contact information section [L0724, 2020-26].
2. Click the template option, which prefills the store information [L0724, 2020-26].
3. Confirm the email address, which can be a support or sales address, uses the real domain [L0724, 2020-26].
4. Add a phone number labelled as a call option [L0724, 2020-26].
5. Add hours of operation, for example 9am through 5pm EST [L0724, 2020-26].
6. Confirm there is no placeholder text, then click publish [L0724, 2020-26].
- A physical address is not needed in this section [L0724, 2020-26].
- A valid hours entry for a US-based store can read 9am through 5pm EST, with the time zone stated. Only the contact section of the policies needs this. Customers rarely see it, but Google's crawler reads it [L0724, 2020-26].

#### Real, working details

- Every email address and phone number in policies and contact pages must be real and current [L0724, 2020-26].
- In every policy page, use the correct real email address and real phone number, remove any "555" placeholder phone number, and delete all bracketed or template filler text [L0167, undated].

### Footer, menus and visibility

#### Footer content

- Create and link these pages in the footer menu before launching traffic: privacy policy (use a Privacy Policy Generator), terms and conditions (use a Terms and Conditions Generator), contact us, shipping policy, refunds and exchanges [L0162, undated].
- Add two contact methods to the footer [L0162, undated; L0377, 2020-26].
- Display accepted payment methods in the footer [L0162, undated; L0377, 2020-26].
- Link the refund, privacy, terms of service and shipping policy pages in the footer before submitting to Google [L0724, 2020-26].
- Refund, shipping, privacy and terms pages should appear in the footer Quick Links menu, not hidden. Make them easy to find before running paid ads, since these pages are required storefront elements [L1319, 2020-26].
- Put a footer link to the policy pages so customers can find them easily [L1326, undated].
- The footer phone number and address should match the Contact Us page details [L1145, 2021-22; older].

#### Creating the footer menu (Shopify)

- Create the menu under Online Store > Navigation > Add menu. Name it, for example, "Our policies". Add a menu item for each policy page (Privacy policy, Terms of service, Shipping policy, Return and refund policy, optionally Contact). Then Save menu [L1233, undated].
- In the theme footer:
  1. Go to Online Store > Themes > Customize.
  2. Open the footer section in the theme editor.
  3. Click Add block, choose Menu and select the policy menu.
  4. Rename the heading from "Quick links" to "Our policies".
  5. Check that all policy links appear in the footer and save [L1233, undated].
- Main menu dropdown:
  1. In the main menu, click Add menu item and name it Our Company.
  2. Link it to the About Us page.
  3. Drag the item under Our Company so it becomes a dropdown.
  4. Add the child items Contact Us, Shipping, Easy Returns, Price Match, Privacy and Terms of Service, linking each to its matching page.
  5. Click Save menu [L1303, 2021-22].

#### Easy to find

- Make the refund and return policy clear and easy to find; the website footer is a recommended placement location [L0014, undated].
- Terms and privacy pages must be easy to find for customers, because some traffic platforms refuse to run ads without easy access [L1303, 2021-22].
- Policies saved under Settings are also linked in the checkout footer. Pages and footer links are added afterwards so visitors can find them [L1233, undated].

### Checkout legal settings

#### How the checkout gets its policies

- The Shopify checkout is hosted separately from the main site. Footer policy links do not appear there, so legal policies must be entered separately, even though the checkout appears to be on your domain. Older (2021-22) [L0677, 2021-22].
- Policies entered in Shopify's policy settings are the texts linked at checkout, so fill them in there first to meet the checkout requirement [L1233, undated].
- Policies in the legal settings are linked in the checkout footer. They do not appear automatically in the storefront footer, so add them to your online store menus [L0677, 2021-22].
- Legal pages must be visible in the checkout, because customers enter personal and card details there [L1233, undated].
- Publish each policy after saving. Each policy shows "No policy set" until it is added [L1167, 2026].

#### Menu name: sources compared

| Source | Menu name in admin | Date | Ids |
|---|---|---|---|
| one source | Settings > Legal, which lists refund, privacy, terms of service and shipping policies, each with a Create from template button | 2021-22 | [L0677, 2021-22; L1145, 2021-22] |
| one source | Settings > Policies: return, privacy, terms of service, shipping and contact information | 2026 | [L1233, undated] |
| one source | Settings > Policies (the legal settings page) | 2026 | [L1167, 2026] |

- Use the menu name your admin shows. The policy text must be the same in both places [L0677, 2021-22].

#### Copy the policy text into the checkout

1. Go to Online Store > Pages and open the Refund policy page [L0677, 2021-22].
2. Select all text and copy it [L0677, 2021-22].
3. Go to Settings > Legal and paste it into the Refund policy box [L0677, 2021-22].
4. Repeat for the Privacy policy, Terms of service and Shipping policy pages [L0677, 2021-22].
5. Make sure each policy in Legal matches the text on your site exactly [L0677, 2021-22].
6. Click Save [L0677, 2021-22].
- Do not keep two different versions of the same policy on the site and in the checkout. Pages and the Legal section must contain identical text [L0677, 2021-22].
- enter the return and shipping policy, privacy policy and terms of service in Settings > Legal. These feed the checkout page, and their text must match the corresponding site pages exactly [L1145, 2021-22].

### Placeholders, sample values and accuracy

#### Placeholders to remove

- Do not publish a template with unfilled placeholders such as "(INSERT TRADING NAME)", "[INSERT BUSINESS ADDRESS]" or "[INSERT BUSINESS PHONE NUMBER]". Replace them with your real business details before publishing [L1167, 2026].
- Templates contain bracketed placeholders, and each one must be replaced with real store information before publishing. Leaving a placeholder such as the trading name or the privacy policy link in the terms makes the page incomplete [L1048, 2020-26].
- Shopify's generated policy texts contain bracketed placeholders and instruction notes. Replace every bracket with your store name, contact email and address, and delete the notes before publishing [L1233, undated].
- Sample values left in a prompt's input fields, such as a template's example brand name, carry into the generated policy or About Us text. Replace or delete every sample value before pasting the output into Shopify [L1048, 2020-26].
- Placeholder text, bracketed template fields and fake phone numbers such as 555-555-5555 are flagged by Google as signs of an unprofessional or incomplete store [L0724, 2020-26].
- Shopify's policy generators insert placeholder text, so each generated policy must be checked and edited [L0724, 2020-26; L0167, undated].
- Confirm there is no placeholder text before you publish [L0724, 2020-26].
- Suppliers may click through your policies during review. A policy with placeholder links, missing sections or references to pages that do not exist makes the store look incomplete. Remove or fix every such item before submitting the store for approval [L0509, 2020-26].
- Templates contain placeholders that must be replaced before submission. Review every generated policy under Shopify Admin > Settings > Policies [L0167, undated].

#### Revising with real figures

- After a generator (such as ChatGPT) writes a policy, reply in the same chat with your real figures and ask for a revised version. Include the exact processing days, delivery days, return window and contact email in each revision so the sample numbers are replaced [L1250, 2026].
- In every policy page, the placeholders for the store name, the privacy policy link and the contact email must be replaced in turn [L1048, 2020-26].

#### Accuracy and reality

- Policies must describe what the store actually does, not just exist as placeholders. In most US states, the core legal pages (privacy policy, terms of service, return and refund policy) are binding legal documents [L0421, 2020-26].
- Every page has to describe what the shop really does in practice [L1250, 2026].
- Promises the store cannot keep lead to customer complaints. Replace rough delivery estimates with actual figures before publishing [L1233, undated].
- Conditional statements, such as financing availability, exclusions, sales tax by state or a shipping restriction to certain states, must link to a page that explains the details. Without that, the visitor becomes confused and worried. Older (2021-22) [L0409, 2021-22].
- Statements such as "Some exclusions apply" or "No shipping to" a state, with no explanation or link, make a store look unreliable. State the restriction plainly and explain it, even when there is a good reason for it. Older (2021-22) [L0409, 2021-22].
- The price match guarantee can be stated clearly as applying to the listed price before coupon codes. This helps customers understand it before ordering. Older (2023) [L1283, 2023].

### Google review requirements

- Contact information read by Google's crawlers should list at least two contact methods, recommended as email plus phone, along with business hours. An address is not required for this business model, but the email and phone must be real and working [L0167, undated].
- Adding an at-a-glance summary, such as a table or a short summary section, to policy pages helps Google's crawlers parse the key details such as return and shipping terms [L0167, undated].
- Google Merchant Center flags accounts automatically when required pages are missing, and a manual review must be requested [L0377, 2020-26].
- Policy pages must match the policies entered in Google Merchant Center [L0966, 2025].
- Google Ads requires privacy policy and terms of service pages that match its requirements. Check them whenever the generator updates [L0965, 2024].
- Missing a privacy policy can result in Google Merchant Center account suspension. Repeated violations can lead to account banning [L0014, undated].

### AI review of policy pages

#### Review prompt

Paste the policy text directly into the chat. The tool may crawl the live site, which may still show old policies, and results based on the live site can point to errors that have already been fixed [L0724, 2020-26].

```
Hello. I own [your domain] and I am about to submit my website to Google Merchant Center and Google Ads for approval. Please review the policy pages I paste below, not the live version of the website, and tell me whether anything is not compliant with Google Ads and Google Merchant Center policies. If you find anything non-compliant or questionable, reply with the exact text in question, why it is a problem and your recommended update.

[paste the text of the policy page here]
```
[L0724, 2020-26]

#### Revision prompt

```
Thank you for your feedback. I have revised our [policy name] and pasted the new version below. Please check this revised text against Google Merchant Center and Google Ads policies and tell me whether it is compliant. If anything is still questionable, reply with the exact sentence, the reason it is a problem and your recommended wording.

[paste the revised policy text here]
```
[L0724, 2020-26]

#### Review procedure, one page at a time

1. Send one policy page to Google Gemini and ask for a compliance review [L0724, 2020-26].
2. Read every issue it lists, including the exact sentence and the recommended update [L0724, 2020-26].
3. Apply every recommended change to that policy in Shopify [L0724, 2020-26].
4. Paste the revised policy back to Gemini and ask for a second check [L0724, 2020-26].
5. Repeat until Gemini reports the policy as fully compliant, then move to the next policy page [L0724, 2020-26].
- Repeat this process for the refund policy, shipping policy, privacy policy and terms of service [L0724, 2020-26].

### Country-specific content

- For the four main markets (US, Canada, Australia and the UK), add the required privacy policy and terms text to the legal pages. Other countries, especially in Europe, have separate rules that must be added too [L1213, 2023].
- If the store ships into the European Union, keep the 14-day right to cancel or return for any reason in the returns policy [L1327, 2026].
- Check consumer rules in each country you sell to when writing refund, return and shipping text [L1188, 2026].
- Customs duties and taxes that the customer must pay belong in the shipping policy [L1233, undated].
- In the US, the core legal pages are binding in most states. State-specific wording, such as for California, must be checked with a legal source rather than assumed. Older (2022) [L0421, 2020-26; L1092, 2022].
- A product built for one market needs an explicit notice on the shipping page, for example for 110V electrical systems and US-only certification. Older (2021-22) [L1264, 2021-22].

### Legal notice and disclaimers

- Legal pages are not legal advice. Have a lawyer review the privacy policy, terms of service and refund policy, especially for stores with custom situations [L0421, 2020-26].
- Shopify's legal page templates are not legal advice and must be customised for your store [L0677, 2021-22].
- Shopify policy templates are not legal advice. You are responsible for their accuracy and for compliance with the laws that apply to your business [L1167, 2026].
- Create the privacy policy and terms of service in Shopify admin under Settings > Policies, not as ordinary pages. This makes Shopify treat them as legal pages, and the links in Google Merchant Center should point to those pages. Older (2023) [L0470, 2023].
- Legal ownership details: use the trade name in the policy text, and check the LLC question with a lawyer [L0964, 2023; L0853, 2021].

### Payment and billing terms

- The terms or a separate payment page should describe how payments are processed, which payment methods are accepted, and any recurring billing if subscriptions are offered [L1233, undated].
- Payment and billing belong in the terms of service, which should cover payment [L1233, undated].
- Display accepted payment methods in the footer [L0162, undated; L0377, 2020-26].
- Customers enter personal and card details at checkout, so the legal pages must be visible there [L1233, undated].
- Legal pages help resolve disputes with customers and payment providers [L1233, undated].

### Setting up policies in Shopify

1. Go to Settings > Policies. [L1331, 2025-26]
2. Open the Shopify policy templates, for example the refund policy. [L1331, 2025-26]
3. Draft the policy text, for example with ChatGPT. [L1331, 2025-26]
4. Read every promise and make sure you can keep it. [L1331, 2025-26]
5. Have a lawyer check the policies if you are unsure they are fully compliant. [L1331, 2025-26]
6. Save each policy and link it in the footer menu. [L1331, 2025-26; L1446, 2020-26]

### Placeholders to replace in the templates

- `{Store name}`: replace with the name of your store. It appears throughout the Privacy Policy and Terms of Service. [L1327, 2026]
- `{Business email}`: replace with your business email address. It appears in the Privacy Policy contact section and in Terms of Service Section 20. [L1327, 2026]
- `{Month Year}`: replace with the month and year of the last update. [L1327, 2026]
- `[Replace with the governing law of your country or state.]`: the template's Section 18 names the laws of the United States by default. Replace this with the governing law of your own country or state. [L1327, 2026]
