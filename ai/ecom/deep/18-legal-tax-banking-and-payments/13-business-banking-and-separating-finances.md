## Business Banking and Separating Finances

### Core principle: keep business and personal money apart

- Never co-mingle business and personal funds, and keep the business as a separate entity from you personally. [L0247, 2021]
- Use a business bank account separate from your personal banking. Suppliers accept either a personal or a business account, and the business account keeps business and personal finances apart. [L0665, 2022]
- Keep business and personal finances separate for clearer tracking and for a future sale of the business. This is recommended but not legally required for a sole proprietor. [L0268, 2020]
- Keep business and personal money strictly separate. Customer deposits from a job or other businesses, personal bills, and business costs such as ad spend and Shopify fees should never be mixed in one account, because commingled funds make it hard to see what the business earns and spends. [L0697, 2020-26]
- Keep every credit card, checking account and PayPal account used for the business completely separate from personal accounts. This applies whether you operate as an LLC or as a sole proprietor. [L1148, 2021-22]
- Mixing personal spending such as groceries with business transactions in the same accounts creates a large mess that becomes a serious problem later. Separate the accounts at the beginning instead of cleaning them up afterwards. [L1148, 2021-22]
- Keep all business finances in separate accounts from personal finances from the first day. Mixing them makes accounting messy, complicates tax reporting and creates problems as the business grows. [L1405, 2021-22]
- Running a dropshipping business through a personal bank account mixes frequent sales and supplier payments with personal money, which makes accounting and tax filing messy. [L0702, 2021-22]
- Using a personal bank account for business transactions can work for a few months, but the bank will likely later send a notice that it can no longer be used for business. Open a dedicated business bank account from the start. [L0360, 2024]
- Personal accounts can technically be used for a business, but this is not recommended. Banks may flag personal accounts used for business and ask the owner to stop. (Older advice, 2021-22 for the first point; 2023 for the second.) [L1415, 2021-22; L1094, 2023]
- Mixing personal and business money is allowed but raises personal risk, and it complicates the books when the business is later sold. [L1069, 2019]
- Commingling the finances of unrelated businesses for two to three years makes it hard for a future buyer to separate them. Plan the entity structure before the business grows. [L0963, 2022]
- Personal bank accounts do not work well at scale, because high transaction volume can lead to restrictions or suspension. Start with a personal account when launching, then move to a business account once sales volume is high. [L1384, 2022]
- Use a business bank account once daily deposits begin, because banks flag personal accounts used for business volume. A personal account can start the business, but it should be switched and kept separate early. [L1281, 2022]
- When a business is sold, buyers verify transactions. Separate business accounts make due diligence much easier. [L1415, 2021-22]

### Is a business account required, and when to open it

- A business bank account is not legally required to sell online, receive customer payments, pay suppliers or sign up for Shopify. Opening one early is strongly recommended, because running everything through a personal account works but makes bookkeeping messy. [L0697, 2020-26]
- Opening a business checking account is optional, but it is recommended once you are serious about the business. [L1275, 2019]
- Once a store is running and earning sales, open a business entity and business bank accounts instead of using personal accounts. This keeps bookkeeping clean and reduces personal liability. Forming the entity can be done cheaply by yourself. (Older advice in a 2024 source: the advice is current.) [L0971, 2024]
- Open a business bank account as early as possible. [L1094, 2023]
- A business bank account is needed early because a merchant account opened directly with a bank often requires an existing bank account there. Having one early keeps future options open. [L0697, 2020-26]
- Use a business credit or debit card for store expenses as early as possible. This makes expense and profit tracking cleaner and easier to audit later. [L1069, 2019]
- A business bank account signals professionalism to larger B2B customers who order in bulk (for example $20,000 to $100,000) and prefer paying by bank wire or check instead of credit card. A personal account name looks far less professional. [L0697, 2020-26]
- A history of business activity with a bank can later unlock larger loans at lower interest rates and better credit card offers with higher limits and more rewards. [L0697, 2020-26]
- Business banking requires the business name on the account. A nickname can be anything. [L0834, 2021]
- A sole proprietor can open a business bank account using their SSN and switch over once the business entity is formed. Forming a business entity separates business assets from personal assets. [L1094, 2023]
- Apply for business bank accounts and business cards early rather than waiting until a supplier asks for them. Banking delays can hold up supplier onboarding. [L1405, 2021-22]

### Account structure: one entity, one set of accounts

- Keep a separate business bank account and credit card for each unrelated business to avoid commingling funds. Related businesses that might be sold together can share one entity. (2023 source, current.) [L0804, 2023]
- Each LLC needs its own business bank account, its own Shopify Payments link and its own business credit card. Funds must never be commingled between entities. [L1092, 2022]
- Multiple LLCs require separate bank accounts, because a shared account exposes each entity to the legal risks of the others. [L1092, 2022]
- Open a separate business bank account for the business, using the LLC information and EIN, to isolate all transactions for bookkeeping. [L1415, 2021-22]
- Use a separate company bank account for Shopify Payments and for payment providers such as Klarna. Keep a separate day-to-day account for ad payments. Some payment providers do not accept every neobank, so a traditional bank account linked to them is needed. [L1216, undated]
- A DBA bank account can be used for the store if it is set up cleanly. Check the requirements in Shopify before relying on a Shopify Balance business account. [L1380, 2022]

### Account names and registration

- Open the business bank account in the company's name. Domain names do not need to match the bank account name. [L0247, 2021]
- Open bank accounts in the name of the LLC entity, not the store name. [L1113, 2022]
- Open the business checking and savings accounts in the registered business name, not the owner's personal name. Customers who pay by bank wire or check see the business name on the account. (Older advice, 2021-22 for the source of the account context; the rule itself is current.) [L0697, 2020-26]

| Question | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Whose name is on the account? | Registered company or LLC name. A store name or nickname is not enough. | [L0247, 2021; L1113, 2022; L0697, 2020-26; L0834, 2021] |
| Must the domain match? | No. | [L0247, 2021] |

### Proof of address, business history and country requirements

- Some online business banks rejected a non-US owner's application over proof of address for a US business. Ways past this were a US mailing address for the business or a setup through Stripe Atlas. An existing bank account opened without proof of address is another route worth checking first. (2024 source, current.) [L0360, 2024]
- A US business bank account opened directly in the US usually requires a US physical or virtual address. An account opened overseas in US dollars may not need one. (2024 source, current.) [L0360, 2024]
- In one case, a business checking account was opened online at an existing bank without any proof of address, while new banks may ask for proof of address. Check the existing bank route first. (2024 source, current.) [L0360, 2024]
- Opening a US bank account from abroad often requires being physically present. Some store owners reported a virtual US dollar account through OFX as an alternative. These are unverified reports and should be checked in community sources before use. [L0359, 2020]
- A business bank account may require proof of business history in some countries, so the business may need to operate before the account is granted. Ask the provider what it requires before you build the account. [L1112, 2021]
- A company registered in Canada that sells in the US may open a Canadian bank account with a US dollar option, or a US business account. (2023 source, current.) [L1094, 2023]
- In Canada, opening a business account requires a business license, and registered businesses must handle business taxes. Set up a business account once sales justify it, because you need a business for tax purposes. [L1384, 2022]
- The bank account and card should be based in the country you sell to. PayPal, Stripe and Shopify Payments can hold money for long periods when payouts cross borders, which makes dropshipping cash flow very hard. [L1405, 2021-22]
- It is ideal to have a bank account in the country you sell in, for example a US-based business bank account for US sales or an Australian bank account for Australian sales. This is strongly recommended for receiving funds. [L1480, 2021-22]
- The business bank account must be based in the country where you sell. For Australia, any bank the owner is comfortable with is fine, because differences in business account fees are usually minor. [L0702, 2021-22]
- If you cannot get a bank account in your target country, rethink which market you sell in or get advice on the options. No fully perfect banking setup exists. Plan the banking and business structure before you start selling. [L1480, 2021-22]

### Choosing a bank

- Choose a bank where you already have a personal account and are satisfied with the service, and open the business accounts there. Business offerings across banks are broadly similar. [L0697, 2020-26]
- Open a business account with low or no fees in the country where you sell. Many banks offer a business version of your personal account with extra business features. [L1405, 2021-22]
- Traditional local banks are not a barrier to multiple accounts, though some banks are inflexible. At the lesson's local bank, opening each account took two hours or more, and a 10 dollar monthly fee applied when minimum balances were not met. [L1200, 2021-22]

### Bank fees and minimum balances

- Most major banks offer free business checking only if a minimum balance is maintained. Minimum balance requirements differ by bank, so check them before opening an account. The example given for Bank of America is a $16 monthly fee if the minimum is not met. (2024 source, current.) [L0697, 2020-26]
- Before opening, confirm the bank's minimum balance requirement for a free business account. [L0697, 2020-26]
- The local bank example charged a 10 dollar monthly fee when minimum balances were not met. [L1200, 2021-22]

### Opening the accounts

Procedure (from the course):

1. Choose the bank where you already hold a personal account, or compare the online options above. [L0697, 2020-26]
2. Confirm the minimum balance requirement for a free business account. [L0697, 2020-26]
3. Prepare the registered business name, EIN or entity documents, and proof of address if the bank asks for it. [L0247, 2021; L1415, 2021-22; L0360, 2024]
4. Open the business checking account by visiting a branch, calling the bank, or applying online. [L0697, 2020-26]
5. Most banks let you open a business account online in about 10 minutes, and cards for the account are then sent to you. [L0702, 2021-22]
6. Some banks take about 20 minutes to open a business checking account online. [L1275, 2019]
7. Open the savings account after the checking account. [L0697, 2020-26]

### Checking and savings structure

Procedure (from the course):

1. Open two business bank accounts. [L0697, 2020-26]
2. Checking account: the transactional account where all revenue arrives and all expenses are paid from. [L0697, 2020-26]
3. Savings account: profit is moved from checking into it so it stays clearly separated, for distributions or tax payments. [L0697, 2020-26]
4. The savings account is for separation, not long-term wealth building. [L0697, 2020-26]

Example:

- With a goal of $20,000 monthly net profit, the profit is transferred from the checking account to the savings account instead of staying in checking. The money earmarked for owner distributions or taxes is then visibly separate from daily transactions. [L0697, 2020-26]

### High-yield savings

- US figures from the lesson: traditional big-bank savings accounts paid about 0.0001% interest, while online banks such as Ally or Marcus paid about 1.9% to 2%. Choose an online account with no fees and low minimums that still pays a real rate. [L1126, 2018]
- Outside the US, the lesson names YouSaver and ING (Australia) and Marcus (UK) as high-yield online savings options. Check current rates, fees and eligibility for your country before opening one. [L1126, 2018]

### Payment routing: Shopify Payments, PayPal and customers

- The Shopify checkout belongs to Shopify. Shopify uses payment gateways to capture payments, then pays the money out to a PayPal account or a nominated business bank account. Payments should go to a business bank account and a business PayPal account, never a personal one. [L0677, 2021-22]
- Route all business revenue into the business checking account: Shopify Payments (card, debit, Apple Pay, Google Pay), PayPal withdrawals transferred to the bank, and bank wire or check payments from customers. [L0697, 2020-26]
- Payment gateways deposit customer payments into the business account. Suppliers are typically paid from it or from a card linked to it. [L0702, 2021-22]
- PayPal is an acceptable payment method if funds can be withdrawn to a business bank account. If a bank does not allow linking to PayPal, open a different business bank account with a bank that permits third-party access. (2025 source, current.) [L0278, 2025]
- PayPal balances must be withdrawn from PayPal to your bank. Shopify Payments transfers go directly to your bank. Plan cash flow with that difference in mind. (2023 source, current.) [L0934, 2023]
- Shopify Payments deposits sales funds within 48 hours. A Shopify Balance debit card gives faster access to funds, but compare it against rewards cards before use. [L0963, 2022]
- Use a separate day-to-day account for ad payments, separate from Shopify Payments and payment providers. [L1216, undated]
- Currency mismatch adds cost. When a store prices in one currency, such as USD, but pays out to a bank in another currency, such as GBP, each payout can carry conversion fees. A company and bank account in the store's own currency, or a bank with low real-time conversion rates, reduces this cost. [L1512, 2023]

### Supplier payments and card hygiene

- Never give a supplier your business bank account number when all purchases are paid by credit card. You can ask the supplier why it needs the number. (2025 source, current.) [L0667, 2025]
- Paying suppliers from a personal account can cause transfer problems. Keep store money separate from personal money by using a dedicated business bank account. [L1380, 2022]

### Travel and cash access

- A no-fee card, such as the Charles Schwab card in the example, lets you withdraw cash abroad from ATMs without ATM fees. Owners who work from abroad should carry one. [L1515, 2019]

### Shopify Balance

- Shopify Balance is Shopify's built-in banking option. It holds payments inside Shopify and lets you see your balance. It is a useful interim option when you cannot get a business account, but it has eligibility requirements to check. [L1384, 2022]
- Shopify Balance may offer a business bank account if the account qualifies. Check the requirements in Shopify before relying on it. [L1380, 2022]
- The Shopify Balance account is not used by the people who run stores in this model. Separate business bank accounts are preferred instead. [L1281, 2022]
- Limits: a Shopify Balance debit card gives faster access to funds than Shopify Payments' 48-hour deposits, but it should be compared against rewards cards before use. [L0963, 2022]

### Backup and risk

- Payment processors and banks can change which options they allow at any time. An account used for cross-border selling may be banned without warning. Plan a backup banking route rather than relying on one provider. [L1405, 2021-22]
- Opening a US bank account from abroad is not guaranteed. Verify each route before committing your store to it. [L0359, 2020; L0360, 2024]
