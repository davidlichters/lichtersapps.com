## Intellectual Property, Trademarks and Copyright

### Trademark, patent and licence checks before naming or selecting products

- Before selecting a product, check it for trademark or patent infringement. Generic products such as garlic presses and resistance bands rarely have trademark issues, while unique design patents require a trademark search. [L0228, 2015]
- If trademark infringement occurs, switch products and recover the inventory investment by selling the remaining stock at wholesale price, so the financial loss is avoidable. [L0228, 2015]
- Do not sell products that carry trademarked or licensed characters, such as Game of Thrones merchandise, unless you hold the rights to sell them. Check every product in a store for such content and remove the ones you cannot sell. [L0355, 2020-26]
- Confirm you have rights to a product before scaling it. Selling copyrighted products without authorized-reseller status raises the risk of DMCA complaints through Meta and Shopify, which can take down a store and its ads. [L0351, 2026]
- Avoid selling merchandise that uses a singer's or public figure's likeness or name without checking copyright, because such products can violate copyright. [L1131, 2018+]
- Before selling branded merchandise, such as a card game with a licensed game theme, confirm you have the right to sell that brand. A marketplace or supplier listing the product does not prove that permission exists. [L1216, undated]
- Products that use famous brand IP, such as Nintendo characters, carry copyright risk. If you test one, use a separate Shopify store and a separate Facebook ad account, never your main ad account, because stores can be shut down for copyright violations. [L1185, 2023]
- Before production, hire a patent attorney to check that your product does not infringe existing patents. Patent infringement is a bigger risk for large products, but small products are also exposed, so budget for this check. [L1154, 2021-22]
- A patent offers a limited period of protection, and the name tied to it may lose exclusivity when the patent expires. Check name protection and trademark status separately from patent status. [L1261, undated]

### Checking a store name and buying a domain

- Before picking a store name, search the US trademark registry for conflicts. Do not plan to rely on being able to absorb a lawsuit, since avoiding infringement is the safer approach. [L0468, 2020]
- Quick name check procedure [L0989, 2025-26]:
  1. Search the chosen name on Trademarkia, a free trademark search covering more than 12 million trademarks.
  2. Search the name on Google and check that it has no slang, swear word or unwanted meaning, for example on Urban Dictionary.
  3. If both searches return nothing problematic, the name is acceptable for the store.
- The trademark check described here is a quick screen, not a formal clearance. A brand that will be built seriously should still get proper trademark advice before major investment. [L0989, 2025-26]
- When buying a resold domain from a third-party marketplace, prefer a platform that holds payment in escrow and offers a guaranteed domain transfer. Marketplace pages of this kind also show a best-price guarantee, but compare the price yourself on other sites before paying. [L0989, 2025-26]

### Registering trademarks, designs and Amazon Brand Registry

- Register your brand on Amazon Brand Registry even if you do not sell on Amazon yet. A lawyer can handle this, and it protects your trademark rights. [L0041, 2025-26]
- Get as many trademarks as possible and make sure they are fully registered, not pending. Google, Alibaba and AliExpress only process takedown requests when you provide fully registered trademark numbers. [L0041, 2025-26]
- Get registered designs, where available in your country, for product design, packaging and paint or colour if possible. These create takedown grounds beyond trademark. [L0041, 2025-26]
- Trademark registrations list Nice classes. Record the class of each registration per country. For an apparel and textile brand, check class 24 (textiles, including blankets) and class 25 (clothing), and confirm the registrations cover the products being copied. [L0991, 2025-26]
- Use EUIPO (European Union Intellectual Property Office) for EU trademarks. Its registry entries give the registration number and the direct link used in infringement reports. [L0991, 2025-26]
- Keep a Google Sheets trademark register that lists each country, trademark name, word mark, class, status, registration number and registry link, so all trademark evidence is ready for reports. [L0991, 2025-26]
- Attach proof of ownership to trademark reports, such as registry certificates or registry search pages. Two registrations give extra verification. Include the registration number and a direct registry link when available. [L0991, 2025-26]

**Timing of trademark filing (sources differ):**

### Reporting infringement on Meta and Instagram

- Brand infringement reports on Meta can only be filed for a trademark registered in the jurisdiction where the infringing party targets customers. A trademark registered in the EU supports a report against an ad aimed at the European market. [L0991, 2025-26]
- Infringers may use a different jurisdiction than their actual location. Check the advertiser details and page transparency information, and use the claimed location when it is the only information available. Record any location in the log. [L0991, 2025-26]
- The Meta Ad Library shows active ads and advertiser details, including the country and EU transparency data. Use it to find infringing ads and to identify the jurisdiction where the advertiser targets customers. [L0991, 2025-26]
- The Ad Library's EU transparency section for an ad shows the advertiser details, the beneficiary and the payer. Capture this section in the screenshot attached to the report. [L0991, 2025-26]
- Report two kinds of infringement separately: copyright (stolen product photos or images) and trademark (unauthorized use of the brand name or logo). Each needs its own form and its own argument. [L0991, 2025-26]
- Meta's trademark form defines a trademark as a word, slogan, symbol or design used to tell a company's products apart from others. It also offers a separate option for content believed to offer counterfeit goods. Choose the path that matches whether the content copies a product or only makes unauthorized use of the brand name or logo. [L0991, 2025-26]
- Use the trademark form only for alleged trademark infringements. Other claim types are not handled through it, and the form warns that misuse can lead to the reporter's account being terminated. Keep other complaints on their own forms. [L0991, 2025-26]
- On the Facebook trademark form, the question *Why do you believe this content infringes rights owner's trademark rights?* offers these options: *This photo, video, post or story uses rights owner's trademark.*, *This ad is using rights owner's trademark.*, *Rights owner's trademark is used in the username.* and *Other*. Pick the option that matches the type of content reported. [L0991, 2025-26]

**Facebook trademark report procedure** [L0991, 2025-26]:
1. Find the infringing ad (e.g. in the Meta Ad Library) and copy its link.
2. Open the platform's 'report a violation' form (found by searching the platform name plus the words report form).
3. Choose the trademark option and confirm you are reporting a trademark infringement.
4. Select 'I am the rights owner' (or the matching relationship if you act for an organisation or client).
5. Enter your full name, mailing address, and a dedicated reporting email address on the brand's domain.
6. Enter the rights owner's name and a link to the brand's official online presence (website or official page).
7. Enter the trademark name, the jurisdiction, the registration number and a direct link to the registry entry for each trademark (one trademark at a time, then add more at the end).
8. Attach a scan or screenshot of each registration certificate. Accepted formats: JPG, GIF, PNG, TIFF, PDF. For registry pages, the first page is usually enough.
9. Select the reason, e.g. 'This ad is using rights owner's trademark'.
10. Paste the direct link to the ad and the link to the page that posted it.
11. Describe where the ad appears (e.g. the Meta Ad Library, with the library ID).
12. Attach a screenshot of the ad, including the advertiser details section if visible.
13. Add any extra information that supports the claim, e.g. that no authorization was given and a request to remove the content.
14. Tick the declaration that the information is accurate and that you are authorized to act.
15. Submit, enter the security code sent to the reporting email, and complete the final check.
16. Wait for the automatic confirmation email, which contains the complaint ID. Record the complaint ID in your log.

**Instagram copyright report procedure** [L0991, 2025-26]:
1. Find the Instagram copyright report form (search the platform name plus copyright report form).
2. Select 'I am the rights owner' (or the matching relationship if you act for an organisation, client or someone else).
3. Enter your full name, mailing address and a dedicated reporting email address on the brand's domain.
4. Enter the rights owner's name and the email address.
5. Select the type of copyrighted work (here: photo) and provide public links to the original images on your official website or official Facebook page, or describe the work in the box.
6. Select the type of content you are reporting (photo, video or post) and paste the direct links to the infringing content.
7. Describe why the content is infringing and add any extra information that supports the claim.
8. Tick the declaration, submit, enter the security code sent to the reporting email, and complete the additional check if asked.
9. Record the complaint ID once it arrives.

**Who files, which email, which links:**
- Use a dedicated reporting email address on the brand's own domain (e.g. name@brand-domain), used only for infringement reports. Platforms check that the sender is an authorized rights owner, so a generic or personal address increases the risk of rejection. [L0991, 2025-26]
- Platforms regularly give the rights owner's name, the reporter's email address and the nature of the report to the account that posted the content, and that account may use these details to contact the reporter. Use a generic business or professional email address in reports. [L0991, 2025-26]
- The rights owner name in a report must be the person or company that actually holds the trademark or copyright. If a founder personally holds the rights, enter that person's name as rights owner and have them authorize the staff who file reports. [L0991, 2025-26]
- Authorize staff to file legal reports on your behalf only after training them properly and trusting them, because the declarations in the form are legally binding. [L0991, 2025-26]
- The complainant must confirm that the report is made in good faith and that the information is accurate. The platform sends a security code to the reporting email, and the report is only submitted after that code is entered. [L0991, 2025-26]
- Every link submitted in a copyright report is sent to the accused party. Only provide publicly accessible links, such as live product pages or official social posts. Never share private cloud folders with unedited, high-resolution or unreleased images or videos, because this gives the infringer access to your assets. [L0991, 2025-26]

### Managing copycats and takedowns

- Manage counterfeit products and copycats with a VA team, not AI software. The team monitors each platform, reports listings and tracks which takedowns succeeded. This is a manual process but necessary. [L0041, 2025-26]
- Use trademark numbers in takedown requests. When a competitor uses your brand name in a Google Ad, lodge a takedown form and provide your registered trademark numbers. [L0041, 2025-26]
- For large-scale counterfeiting, you have the option to attempt factory raids and seize counterfeit products. Provide trademark numbers in all takedown requests to Google, Alibaba and AliExpress. [L0041, 2025-26]
- Plagiarism is unavoidable once a store becomes a major player in a niche, so it must be dealt with quickly. Copycat stores often pull your watermarked images, copy your customer reviews, use your PDFs and copy your policy pages. [L1357, 2018]
- Account risks on the backend include Meta bans, DMCA takedowns and Shopify account removals due to DMCA claims. Ignoring these while focusing only on growth leads to repeated losses. Avoid them by reviewing your brand, content and listings for copyright issues before scaling. [L0906, 2026]
- Check your store for plagiarism of your content by reviewing these areas: 1) watermarked images, 2) customer reviews, 3) documents, 4) return policy, shipping policy and FAQ pages. [L0920, 2018]

**Shopify DMCA procedure** [L0920, 2018]:
1. Collect all instances of the copied content.
2. Submit a single notice listing all instances at https://www.shopify.com/legal/dmca, since Shopify prefers one submission and multiple instances can weaken the case against the copycat store.
3. If you must submit more than once, space submissions 1 to 2 weeks apart.

**Checking competitor stores for copied content** [L1357, 2018]:
1. Check for your watermarked product images.
2. Check for your real customer reviews.
3. Check your unique PDFs for your metadata or logo.
4. Compare their return policy, shipping policy and FAQ pages with yours.
5. Submit a DMCA takedown through the Shopify DMCA page, found by searching Shopify DMCA.
6. Submit each instance of plagiarism. Shopify generally prefers one submission listing all instances, but submitting multiple times spaced one to two weeks apart was reported to hurt copycat stores more.
7. Shopify reportedly requires the copied page to be taken down within 24 to 48 hours or shuts the store down.

Note on submission count: one source says multiple submissions weaken the case [L0920, 2018], while another reports that submissions spaced one to two weeks apart hurt copycat stores more [L1357, 2018]. Both agree that one consolidated submission is the default. [L0920, 2018; L1357, 2018]

- Rights holders can take action through DMCA notices from competitors or rights holders. Shopify provides the DMCA notice process for store-level reports. [L1218, undated]

### Responding to claims against your store

- If you receive a copyright claim over a product image, act in this order [L1113, 2022]:
  1. Remove the disputed image and the page it appears on right away.
  2. Ask the supplier where the image came from and whether they can show proof of rights or permission.
  3. Keep the claimant informed of progress.
  4. Consider a free lawyer question platform for advice, since this is not legal advice.
- If you receive a trademark complaint about your store name, ask for a free answer on a legal Q&A platform before changing the name, and do not decide based only on general advice. [L0854, 2022]
- If you get an email from a company warning of trademark infringement or a legal violation, search the sender's domain together with the words scam and trademark. Emails can be spoofed, so verify before responding. [L1285, 2024]

### Books and copyrighted written content

- Running a big-name author's book at scale can trigger DMCA takedown notices against your Shopify store and your Meta ad accounts, including ad agency accounts and personal accounts. Books by large, well-known authors carry the highest risk. [L0560, 2026]
- Case: a famous author's book was run as a main product with a proven concept and was running for an extended period. The store received a DMCA notice in Shopify and a DMCA notice in Meta. Lesson: running an unchanged big-author book at scale exposes the store and ad accounts to takedowns, so customisation must come first. [L0560, 2026]
- Customise a book only after it is a proven concept with a consistently profitable P&L, typically starting around day 6 or 7 of running it. Customisation is the lesson's safeguard against DMCA and plagiarism flags. [L0560, 2026]
- A DMCA claim from the main author of a book can lead to a Meta ad ban or a Shopify DMCA action against the store. Before launching a book that its author actively sells, plan a customised cover or internal edition. [L1175, 2026]
- Running a competitor's cover photo next to their ad raises the risk of a DMCA claim, which can get the Meta ad account or the Shopify store banned. The risk applies to both platforms. [L1030, 2026]
- Custom covers and custom UGC keep an ad from matching competitors' ads. This reduces DMCA exposure and makes it less likely that competitors can report the ads. [L1030, 2026]
- Rewording a copyrighted book, changing the author name or altering its images does not make the content your own. If the structure and meaning are still copied, it is infringement. A rights holder can send a cease-and-desist letter requiring you to stop selling, then pursue a claim. Use only book content you own or are licensed to use. [L0794, 2026]
- Customising a book to reduce DMCA and plagiarism risk [L0560, 2026]:
  1. Get the soft copy (PDF) of the book from your agent.
  2. Rewrite the wording with Claude while keeping the same meaning.
  3. Change the title completely.
  4. Change the formatting of the internal pages and the images.
  5. Change the cover page.
  6. Customise the UGC and AI ad creatives used to sell the book.
- Never copy or resell other people's ebooks or content. Use royalty-free PLR material or write original content yourself. [L0513, undated]
- Reselling ebooks or digital products without rights can cause copyright issues. Ask the copyright owner for permission or create a very similar product. [L1184, 2023+]
- Copying competitor text, testimonials and photos word for word can infringe copyright and creates a trust problem if the claims do not match your product. Rewrite copy in your own words and replace photos and testimonials with ones you have the rights to use. [L0600, 2026]
- Copying competitor images or product text led to copyright reports from another company, and the store was shut down. Create your own content and product text instead of reusing competitor material. [L1444, 2022]

### Video, music and sound

- Reusing footage from other creators or ads carries rights risk. Weigh that risk consciously before publishing an adapted ad. [L0341, 2023]
- Use only royalty-free music, sound effects and footage you are licensed to use, because copyrighted audio taken from video platforms can get an ad into trouble. Check the licence before publishing any clip or track you downloaded. [L0344, 2023]
- Before using a trending sound in a store's product video, verify that the sound is licensed for commercial use in the platform's own commercial sound library. A sound can be trending widely and still not appear on that list. [L0655, undated]
- Sounds that list a real artist or named creator are usually copyrighted and will be blocked for ads. Bootleg sounds without a real artist name, including sped-up or slowed versions, are generally usable. [L1383, 2022]
- Music must be checked for copyright before it goes into an ad, because copyright strikes can stop the ad from running. CapCut has a copyright check in the Audio tab. A licensed library such as Envato, with a subscription, is an alternative for music cleared for use. [L1423, undated]
- Downloading sound effects from YouTube through MP3 converters, or using free icons without checking the licence, can create copyright problems. Free icons often require attribution, so confirm the licence before publishing an ad. [L1423, undated]
- Reusing another creator's footage carries copyright risk. Large channels are more likely to request takedown or payment, and reused footage can be flagged even when you recut it. [L1427, 2023+]
- Speeding up, trimming or re-cutting another creator's footage does not reliably hide it from the ad platform, and the original creator can still request a takedown or demand payment. Reviews or customer videos that are verified content carry extra risk. Use footage you own, licensed stock, or supplier footage you have the right to use. [L1427, 2023+]
- Test a concept with existing footage as a proof of concept before ordering stock or shooting your own footage. Once the concept shows results, invest in original footage. [L1427, 2023+]
- Do not run another brand's video in ads beyond short tests, because copyright takedowns can disable your store or content. Treat copied videos as a short-term test, not a foundation for the campaign. [L1377, 2022+]
- Running another seller's video unchanged can breach copyright or the rules of the ad platform. Check the rights and the platform policies before launching ripped creatives. [L1252, 2026]
- Never take a real creator's UGC video, screenshot the person and build a character page that copies them. This counts as impersonation and is fraudulent. [L1476, 2026]

### Fonts, stock assets and images

- Using fonts without a proper commercial licence exposes your business to lawsuits. Font foundries actively scan the internet for unauthorised font use. One company using the wrong font in the wrong places was sued for significant damages. Always use system fonts available in Shopify or legally licensed sources such as Google Fonts. [L0317, 2025-26]
- Stock images, audio and videos included in a Canva subscription were understood to be licensed for use in designs. Verify the licence terms in the Canva licence before using them in paid ads or products. [L0754, undated]
- Supplier photos are commonly used with written permission, and a written price list alone is not a contract. Ask suppliers to confirm permission in writing before using their images. [L1283, 2023]
- A free or demo image may be used on a test site, but a live store that sells products needs commercially licensed images. Without a commercial licence, the creator can ask for the image to be removed for copyright reasons. [L1310, 2021-22]
- An image licensed through Adobe Stock can be used on a live selling website as many times as needed. Use only images you have bought a licence for on any store that sells products. [L1310, 2021-22]
- For product images on demo stores, use Google Image Search filtered for 'labeled for commercial reuse'. Do not purchase expensive commercial licences or copy images from supplier websites. [L0245, 2020]
