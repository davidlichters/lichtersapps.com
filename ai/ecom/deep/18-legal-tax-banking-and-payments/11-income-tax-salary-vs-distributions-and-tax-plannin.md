## Income Tax, Salary vs Distributions and Tax Planning

### Structure choice and how profit is taxed

- Sole proprietorships, LLCs and S-Corps differ in their tax obligations, and each carries distinct business and personal tax implications. [L0006, undated]
- Under a sole proprietorship, business profit is added to personal income such as a job or investment income and taxed at the personal rate. Several income streams can push the owner into a higher tax bracket. [L0702, 2021-22]
- In a company, profit is taxed at company level before money is paid to the owner. Outside the US, company tax rates are usually much lower than personal income tax rates, which can make a company the better option for some owners. [L0702, 2021-22]
- No fixed tax percentage exists. Your liability depends on business structure (sole proprietorship, LLC, S-corp), state location, business income, personal income and deductions. Consult the business-tax module or an accountant for your situation. [L0251, 2023]
- In the US you pay tax on profit, not on sales. The rate depends on the business entity, because a sole proprietor and an S-corp paying salary and distributions are taxed differently. [L0941, 2022]
- Taxable income for a sole proprietor is business profit minus deductions. Example: $100,000 profit minus deductions is counted as income and taxed at the owner's tax bracket. [L0941, 2022]
- Net profit from an online store is taxable like any business income, and the business structure affects how much tax is owed. Ask a tax professional about structuring options before relying on them. [L0759, 2020-26]
- Dropshipping income is taxed like any other income. Personal income tax depends on tax bracket and state, plus federal rates. Consult an accountant for the exact setup. [L0803, 2022]
- Income tax depends on business structure and any other income, so there is no single answer for every seller. In past experience a sole proprietor setup did not block supplier approvals, and choosing a legal entity for liability is the owner's decision. [L1110, 2019]

### Pass-through treatment (US)

- An LLC by itself does not reduce income tax in the United States. Profit passes through to the owner's personal return and is taxed at the owner's normal income tax rate, the same as a sole proprietorship. Only liability protection changes. [L0703, 2020-26]
- An LLC or S-Corp is a pass-through entity. The business still files its own return, but profit is taxed on the owner's personal return and not at business level. The owner pays tax through the personal return either way. [L1344, 2020-26]
- Example of pass-through taxation: a sole proprietorship or plain pass-through LLC with $1,000,000 in sales and $200,000 net profit adds the full $200,000 to the owner's personal return, taxed at the owner's normal rate. Net profit is the money left after all expenses, such as advertising, before income taxes. [L0703, 2020-26]
- If the business is taxed as an S-corp, profit and losses flow through to your personal return, so pre-launch expenses can offset other income. Check the exact situation with an accountant. [L1113, 2022]
- An LLC can elect to be taxed as an S-corporation through a tax election form. Under an S-corporation you pay yourself a salary and take the remaining profit as distributions. [L0801, 2021]
- Once the business is established, consider forming it as an LLC and electing S-corp tax treatment, which can be a significant benefit. Discuss the specifics with an accountant. [L1283, 2023]

#### Procedure: form an LLC and elect S-corp treatment (US)

1. Form an LLC for the store. [L0703, 2020-26]
2. File the form that elects S-corporation tax treatment for that LLC, typically with help from an accountant. [L0703, 2020-26]
3. As the only working owner, set a reasonable salary for the work performed and run it through payroll, which is taxed at the owner's normal income tax rate. [L0703, 2020-26]
4. Take the remaining net profit as distributions to the owner rather than as salary. [L0703, 2020-26]

### S-corp salary and distributions (US)

#### How the split works

- The S-corp benefit applies only to profit paid out as distributions, not to the owner's salary. The advantage grows as net profit rises above the salary level. The benefit becomes meaningful once the business is consistently profitable, not in the first months. [L0703, 2020-26]
- S-corp distributions still flow onto the owner's personal return and remain subject to income tax. The estimated saving applies only to the Social Security and Medicare payroll tax that would otherwise be charged on the salary portion, not to income tax on the whole profit. [L0703, 2020-26]
- Distributions are not subject to Social Security or Medicare tax and can be taken at any frequency, such as monthly or quarterly. Profit is usually taken out as distributions rather than salary. [L0835, 2023]
- Once the S-corp is formed, pay yourself through payroll or automatic payments as an employee. Social Security and Medicare tax (about 15.3 percent on the year cited) applies to salary but not to distributions, which is why the structure can lower tax. [L0803, 2022]
- Taxes for an S-corp separate salary and distributions. If the business is not taxed as an S-corp, consult an accountant to clarify how payouts are classified. [L0801, 2021]
- The S-corp salary and distribution split can reduce the total tax burden, but the salary must be reasonable, so set it with an accountant. [L1344, 2020-26]

#### Reasonable salary and audit risk

- In the United States, the IRS expects an S-corp owner who runs the business to pay a reasonable salary for the work done, and an audit can check this. The salary must be a defensible amount for the actual workload, not an arbitrary minimum. [L0703, 2020-26]
- Setting the salary very low to push more profit into distributions is risky in the US. The IRS expects a reasonable salary for the actual work, so an audit could reclassify distributions as wages and cancel the payroll tax saving. [L0703, 2020-26]
- Only pay a salary if the business can still cover its expenses and keep cash accumulating. Take distributions when you want, throughout the year, and let the accountant reconcile them at year end. [L0803, 2022]
- Do not pay yourself a salary from day one, because the business should first prove what it can earn. Distributions can be taken whenever you want, but keep money in the business when a big initiative needs investment. [L0835, 2023]
- Pay a salary only after the business has about three months of history showing consistent monthly profit. Salaries of around $500 or $1,000 per week are examples of what can be paid. [L0835, 2023]
- Taking small distributions regularly keeps cash flow consistent. Larger distributions can be taken quarterly or annually, based on monthly goals. [L0803, 2022]

#### Worked examples

- **Example A, part-time owner.** An S-corp owner who works about 10 to 15 hours a week on the store might pay themselves a $50,000 salary, taxed at their normal income tax rate. The remaining $150,000 of a $200,000 profit is paid out as a distribution, which avoids the Social Security and Medicare tax that applies to salary. [L0703, 2020-26]
- **Example B, round figures (not tax advice).** With net profit of $200,000 for the year, a salary of $2,000 per week equals $104,000 over 52 weeks. The remaining $96,000 of profit is taken as distributions, which generally carry lower tax than salary. [L0803, 2022]
- **Example C, $1 million profit.** A business earning $1 million profit and paying the owner a $100,000 salary leaves $900,000 that passes through as a K-1 distribution. The quoted saving on that portion is around 15.3% self-employment tax, compared with an LLC taxed on the full $1 million. Verify the figures with an accountant. [L0671, 2021]

#### Procedure: pay the owner from an S-corp (US)

1. Pay yourself a small minimum salary, for example $500 to $2,000 a month, on a regular schedule. [L1281, 2022]
2. Take the remaining profit as a distribution, monthly or quarterly. [L1281, 2022]
3. Keep business and personal funds separate instead of mixing them. [L1281, 2022]

The salary is subject to payroll taxes, while distributions avoid the extra tax of about 15.7% on the salary. [L1281, 2022]

#### Estimating the saving

- Estimated Social Security and Medicare savings (US only) = distribution amount × about 15%. Example: a $150,000 distribution gives an estimated saving of about $22,500 per year, and the saving grows as profit rises. [L0703, 2020-26]
- The rules in this section (LLC, S-corp, reasonable salary and the roughly 15% Social Security and Medicare saving) apply to the United States only. Other countries have different structures and rules. Before choosing a structure, confirm the decision with a licensed accountant or attorney who knows the owner's jurisdiction. [L0703, 2020-26]

### Tax reserves and cash discipline

*(All units in this group are older advice, 2021-22 source.)*

- Set aside a fixed tax percentage at every allocation, for example 10% of real revenue, so that a tax bill can be paid in full when it is due. This matters most after a high-revenue month, because that month's gains can be consumed by taxes. [L1200, 2021-22]
- Treating a record sales month as spendable cash without setting aside a tax share can leave nothing to pay the tax bill. Plan the tax reserve before spending any part of a strong month. [L1200, 2021-22]
- Case: in 2018 the owner had a long revenue decline, then one record sales month in March that felt like a turnaround. No tax had been planned for. When taxes were paid in April, the entire gain from that March sales month went to the government, and possibly more. Because the owner had not saved or planned for the tax bill, the one-off month left the business in a worse position. [L1200, 2021-22]

### Deductible expenses and income classification

- In the US, mentorship, mastermind and course costs for growing a business are often treated as deductible business expenses. Confirm the treatment with a CPA before relying on it. [L1281, 2022]
- Run all real business expenses through the business account, have an accountant check that every deductible cost is written off, and reinvest extra profit where it can grow the business. Track every expense from the start so it can be deducted. [L1344, 2020-26]
- Reinvesting profit is a legitimate way to reduce taxable profit. Large companies often appear to pay less tax because they reinvest profit in growth, which tax rules allow and encourage. [L1344, 2020-26]
- Home-based operation: write-offs include a proportional share of rent or mortgage based on the area used for the business, plus product costs and advertising. Full rent or mortgage cannot be written off. A CPA should categorize the expenses. [L1283, 2023]
- Cash back from the Capital One card is described by the issuer as a rebate rather than income, so the issuer would not send a tax form reporting it. This is the issuer's position. Confirm it with a qualified accountant in the owner's country before relying on it. [L0903, 2021-22]

### Tax-advantaged jurisdictions and where to register

- Register the business in the simplest place you already live while profits are small. Once profit creates a large tax bill (around $40,000 per year in one example), invest effort in moving the personal address or structuring the business. [L1515, 2019]
- Register the business where you live at first. Once it starts making profit, research countries or US states with lower tax and consider moving the business there, since the tax burden can be reduced significantly this way. [L0827, 2019]
- A Hong Kong company can be registered as an offshore, tax-free entity once net profit reaches about $100,000 to $200,000 USD. Specialist providers offer this for roughly $100 to $300 USD. The benefit depends on the structure and must be checked with an accountant. [L1512, 2023]
- Australian structure, as a procedure:
  1. Invest funds through a trust structure. [L0041, 2025-26]
  2. The trust invests into a holding company. [L0041, 2025-26]
  3. The holding company owns both a trading company (which handles all sales and bears operational liability) and an IP company (which holds intellectual property and remains saleable if the trading company fails). [L0041, 2025-26]
  4. The holding company receives dividends from the trading company, protecting cash reserves at a lower tax rate. [L0041, 2025-26]

  This structure separates liability, protects assets and optimizes tax. For the US, consult local tax specialists. [L0041, 2025-26]

### Residency planning and relocation

- Moving the personal residence from California (about 8% state income tax) to Texas (0% state income tax) eliminates state income tax, but federal taxes still apply. [L1515, 2019]
- Choosing a state with no state income tax, such as Texas in the lesson's example, can reduce tax separately from the foreign income exclusion. Plan residence changes with a tax professional. [L1126, 2018]
- One US business owner paid about 40% or more of income in combined federal and state income tax while living in California. After relocating the personal residence and working location-independently, the total tax burden dropped to about 5% of income. Results vary by residence, visa status and business structure, so check against your own situation. [L1515, 2019]
- Combined top personal income tax in California was given as around 52%. This applies to the owner's total income, not to dropshipping as a separate category, and lower rates are possible in other states. Confirm the exact rate and structure with an accountant. [L0803, 2022]
- Residency by investment: Ukraine offered a residency option requiring about $100,000 in 2021, while comparable programs in other European countries were often in the range of $300,000 to $500,000. These thresholds were recorded before 2022, so check current requirements before relying on them. [L0863, 2022]
- Thailand: foreigners can legally buy a condo, but staying there still depends on a tourist visa or visa runs, so ownership does not provide residency or tax status. Furnished short-term rentals were cheap and easy to find in 2018, so buying usually only makes sense where short-term rental is hard to get. [L1126, 2018]
- Citizenship by investment or a second passport used for tax planning is a large-scale tool mainly used by big companies with professional advisors. Individuals should not assume they can copy it. US citizens still owe US tax on worldwide income even with a second passport. [L1126, 2018]
- Offshore company setups, such as the Belize example where the owner works for their own foreign company, are complex and need professional setup. [L1126, 2018]

### Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE)

- US citizens living abroad may exclude earned income from federal income tax. Self-employment tax still applies, and the exclusion typically requires long physical presence outside the US. Confirm current qualification rules with a tax professional before relying on it. [L1515, 2019; L1126, 2018]
- The two sources give different exclusion limits. Use the limit confirmed for the current year.

- The tax benefits of living abroad apply to citizens of many countries, not only the US. Check the exact test for your passport and residence. [L1126, 2018]

### Lawyers, accountants and specialists

- Hire a creative tax lawyer, not an accountant. Accountants check boxes in black and white, while lawyers help you think creatively within tax law to minimize your burden. [L0041, 2025-26]
- Set the S-corp salary with an accountant, and have an accountant reconcile distributions at year end and categorize expenses. [L1344, 2020-26; L0803, 2022; L1283, 2023]
- Non-US owners of a US LLC can learn the basics of US tax obligations from CPAs who specialize in working with foreign owners. Use their public educational content as a starting point, then confirm your own situation with a qualified professional. [L1127, 2024]

### Family and asset protection

- Tax planning must also consider family protection and asset protection. Trust structures vary if you have dependents, a partner you don't trust, or other personal liabilities. Consult a tax lawyer on your full situation. [L0041, 2025-26]
- In the Australian structure, the IP company holds intellectual property and remains saleable if the trading company fails, which separates liability and protects assets. [L0041, 2025-26]

### Cross-border tax obligations

- US citizens owe US federal income tax on worldwide income, no matter where they live or which company earns the money. Moving abroad or using a foreign company does not remove that obligation. [L1126, 2018]
- Citizenship by investment and second passports do not remove the US tax obligation on worldwide income. [L1126, 2018]
- Profit received abroad and spent at home is still taxable income in the home country. A basic company at home, such as a sole trader or sole proprietorship, lets business expenses be deducted, which can reduce income tax on that profit. [L1512, 2023]
- Money sent from an offshore company to a personal home bank account must be documented for the tax authority, so the origin of the funds has to be explainable. A basic company at home remains useful even when an offshore company handles payments. [L1512, 2023]
- Rules for LLCs, S-corps and the reasonable salary apply to the United States only. Confirm the decision in the owner's jurisdiction with a licensed accountant or attorney. [L0703, 2020-26]
