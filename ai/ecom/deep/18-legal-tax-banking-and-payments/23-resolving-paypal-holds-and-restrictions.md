### Resolving PayPal Holds and Restrictions

- *Older, 2018:* To resolve a PayPal account hold, call repeatedly until you reach a useful representative, and explain that you run an e-commerce business. Emails and live chat were not effective. [L0466, 2018]
- *Older, 2019:* Recovering a locked PayPal account:
  1. Send PayPal the proof of delivery and any other documents they request. [L0960, 2019]
  2. Call PayPal directly, since email responses are slow and impersonal. [L0960, 2019]
  3. Keep calling and speaking with different representatives, because a later call can reverse an earlier decision. [L0960, 2019]
  4. Provide whatever documents are requested until the account is reopened. [L0960, 2019]
- *Older, 2021:* When PayPal holds a payment, ask PayPal for the reason. Check whether the account is new and whether a tracking number is required. [L0962, 2021]
- *Older, 2022:* If a PayPal account is suspended, keep the account under follow-up and contact PayPal regularly, because some suspended accounts are restored after reaching the right person. Meanwhile keep other payment methods running, such as Shop Pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay and Klarna. [L1092, 2022]
- *Hold procedure, one source:* Procedure when PayPal places a hold on sales:
  1. Connect a tracking app (TrackShow was named) to PayPal and the store, so tracking numbers are sent automatically when orders ship. [L1512, 2023]
  2. When a hold appears, provide proof of shipment for the sample transactions PayPal requests, such as a supplier invoice, tracking information or a shipping label. [L1512, 2023]
  3. Expect the release after about 10 to 20 days once proof is accepted. [L1512, 2023]
  4. Check the PayPal account regularly and resolve disputes early to protect the account. [L1512, 2023]
- PayPal can permanently restrict a dropshipping account and ask for fulfillment proof and delivery proof, which sellers usually get from their supplier. Sending these documents does not guarantee the account is restored. [L1347, undated]
- Recovering a restricted PayPal account:
  1. Send the fulfillment proof and delivery proof from the supplier when PayPal asks for them. [L1347, undated]
  2. Verify the PayPal account with the registered company details. [L1347, undated]
  3. Keep syncing tracking numbers for every order. [L1347, undated]
  4. Stay in contact with PayPal and ask for the specific reason for the restriction, because some reviews are made by automated systems that a conversation can override. [L1347, undated]
  5. At the same time, open a second PayPal account with a different email address and verify it. [L1347, undated]
- Step 5 conflicts with the advice on replacement accounts below. Both apply to different situations: the second account in step 5 is a parallel option during a restriction, while the replacement after a ban should wait until the cause is fixed. [L1347, undated; L1128, 2023]
- Around 300 orders produced only one PayPal dispute, which was answered with a tracking number and documents. [L1347, undated]
- Missing a registered company was given as a cause of restrictions. PayPal accounts were verified with the registered company details; in the example, a UK Ltd. [L1347, undated]
- Not registering a company and not verifying the PayPal account led to about 15,000 USD being blocked. Registering the company and verifying the account released the funds. [L1431, 2020+]

### Tracking Sync

- Not updating tracking numbers on PayPal for each order is a likely trigger for restrictions, because PayPal needs evidence that orders are being fulfilled. [L1347, undated]
- Apps such as tracky.pay sync tracking numbers from a supplier account like CJ Dropshipping to PayPal automatically, so every new order gets its tracking number without manual entry. [L1347, undated]
- Connect a tracking app (TrackShow was named) to PayPal and the store, so tracking numbers are sent automatically when orders ship. [L1512, 2023]
- To lift a PayPal withdrawal limit, register a tracking app with PayPal to show the business is legitimate. [L1363, 2023]
- Keep delivery proof for every order. Processors may require it before releasing withheld payouts. [L1441, 2018]

### Withdrawing Funds Regularly

- Prevent PayPal from holding funds by withdrawing regularly (ideally daily), keeping only a small balance in PayPal, and transferring bulk funds to your business bank account. [L0278, 2025]
- PayPal may lock large balances for extended periods, six months or longer. [L0278, 2025]
- Plan cash flow around holds. A hold on incoming payments at the start of sales, and a delivery hold until delivery, both delay access to money. [L1173, undated]
- If frozen funds leave you short of working capital, the only options are a credit loan, a personal loan from a friend, or refunding customers. [L0324, 2025+]

### Diversifying Processors and Backup Payments

- Use PayPal as an additional payment method next to Shopify Payments, not as your main merchant account. PayPal can apply holds, such as seven days or 30 days of payout delay, when volume jumps suddenly. [L0468, 2020]
- Keep PayPal as one payment option rather than the only method, because a share of buyers prefer it and it increases total revenue. [L0854, 2022]
- Keeping all sales and working balances on a single payment account leaves the business exposed: a ban can freeze funds for around 180 days. Keep a backup payment option ready before problems start. [L1128, 2023]
- While a PayPal account is suspended, keep other payment methods running, such as Shop Pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay and Klarna. [L1092, 2022]
- Shopify Payments runs on Stripe in the background but has been reported to hold funds earlier than Stripe, around 20,000 to 30,000 USD in volume. Stripe itself was reported to show holds around 35,000 to 40,000 USD. Every processor can hold funds at scale. [L1512, 2023]
- Stripe holds usually last about 30 days, and a hold can be extended by another 30 days if the dispute rate keeps rising. Resolving holds quickly and lowering disputes can speed up the release and remove the hold. [L1512, 2023]
- Case: a Hong Kong company after PayPal holds. At about 30,000 to 40,000 USD in monthly PayPal volume, about 40,000 USD was held on a 21-day rolling hold. The seller moved to a Hong Kong company with Hong Kong PayPal and Stripe accounts and later reported much higher volume without holds. [L1512, 2023]
- Opening a company in Hong Kong to obtain a PayPal account is a high-risk shortcut. In one reported case the setup cost about 700 USD and the account was banned within a week, with about 5,000 USD held for 180 days. Use a payment account that matches the country where the owner lives instead. [L1128, 2023]
- The two Hong Kong reports differ in outcome. The shortcut in [L1128, 2023] ended in a ban, while the relocated seller in [L1512, 2023] had accounts matching their new residence. Use a payment account in the country where the owner actually lives. [L1128, 2023; L1512, 2023]

### Account Setup, Identity and Bans

- Register a PayPal account in the country where the owner lives. When you move, open a new account in the new country. A mismatch between residence and account location can contribute to bans and frozen funds. [L1128, 2023]
- Sales outside your store's region, and payouts across borders, expose the account to holds. See [L1480, 2021-22] and [L0962, 2021] in the sections above. [L1480, 2021-22; L0962, 2021]
- Connect the PayPal account to the primary business email, and keep name, address and email the same across the store, PayPal and other platforms. [L0677, 2021-22]
- After a payment account is banned, opening a replacement account in another country can be closed within days, as happened with a US account opened two days after a ban. Find out the cause of the ban and fix it before opening any new account for the same business. [L1128, 2023]
- Shopify Payments banned an account with unfulfilled orders after about two months, and the whole payout was lost. [L0351, 2026]

### Scaling Volume Gradually

- Scale the store gradually. A sudden jump from a few thousand dollars to very large monthly volume can trigger a payment processor's risk review. [L0468, 2020]
- *Older, 2021:* A new PayPal account went from zero to about $50,000 in sales within a few weeks, and the rapid growth triggered a reserve on the account. A merchant account may place a reserve, which holds part of your funds, when chargebacks are high or revenue rises very quickly on a new account. [L0671, 2021]
- Holds are likely when sales volume jumps sharply, for example from about 200 to 300 USD per day to 5,000 USD per day. Match growth with proof of delivery. [L1512, 2023]
- At $100k-500k per month, chargebacks, payment processing holds and monthly processing limits become likely. A payment processing agent can monitor chargebacks, monthly volume and processing statistics, at about $8-12 per hour. [L1129, undated]
- A payment processor can hold a first high-value order for up to 120 days when addresses differ. Scaling into high-ticket products is safer with fraud tools in place. [L0962, 2021; L0551, 2020]
- *Older, 2019:* Shopify fraud protection costs 1.2% per order, so include it in the cost of scaling when you use it. [L0960, 2019]
- *2023:* A new store with traditional merchant accounts that later added PayPal processed about $50,000 in sales in a short time. PayPal flagged the account as high risk and held the balance for 180 days. Sudden high volume on a new account can trigger holds. [L0970, 2023]
