## Liability, Insurance and Risk Protection

### Insurance types for e-commerce

- The main e-commerce insurance types are Product Liability Insurance and DNO (Direct Officer) Insurance [L0041, 2025-26].
- Recall insurance exists but is very difficult to obtain [L0041, 2025-26].
- Product liability insurance is optional. If you want it, compare quotes from several insurers [L0671, 2021].

### Where lawsuits come from

- As a successful business you will be sued, possibly unfairly, through an accident, competitor action or totally unfounded claims. Expect it and prepare by keeping legal reserves [L0041, 2025-26].
- Most litigation risk in e-commerce comes from the business itself: terms of service, compliance, intellectual property and subscriptions. It does not mainly come from uncontrollable external events. Identify and fix the controllable risks [L0041, 2025-26].
- Lawsuits against a small e-commerce business are rare, but the financial consequences can be severe. This is a low-probability, high-impact risk, and choosing the right business structure is a protective measure against it [L1405, 2021-22].
- Customers in the US can sue almost anyone, so general liability cover is a matter of your risk tolerance [L0961, 2020].
- If a product could cause injury, a lawsuit may name every party a lawyer can find, including the seller and the supplier [L0961, 2020].
- Plaintiffs often sue every party in the chain, including the store [L0247, 2021].

### Supplier protection

- Suppliers do not protect you from product liability claims [L0247, 2021].
- If you white label through a manufacturer, it will often cover your product liability under its umbrella insurance policy [L1154, 2021-22].
- If the manufacturer does not cover you, you need your own product liability insurance, which is usually not very expensive [L1154, 2021-22].

### Product liability and product safety

- Assess product liability by considering every way the product could injure someone, including misuse, and every person who might use it, not only the intended buyer [L1154, 2021-22].
- Address all required warnings on the product to protect your company [L1154, 2021-22].
- Products sold in plastic bag packaging need a suffocation hazard warning printed on the packaging [L1154, 2021-22].
- Confirm the exact legally required wording rather than relying on memory, and budget money to get all required warnings right [L1154, 2021-22].
- Avoid products that could be dangerous. If that risk concerns you, consider a general liability policy [L0961, 2020].

### Business structure and asset separation

- Business liability insurance depends on the business entity, the expected liability and the owner's risk tolerance. Consult a professional before buying policies, because brokers are salespeople [L1111, 2020].
- Choosing a business entity is similar to choosing insurance: the choice is up to the owner [L0472, 2025].
- Run the business through an LLC or S corporation and keep business expenses separate from personal ones [L0853, 2021].
- Consider general liability insurance once the business holds meaningful assets, and confirm the approach with a professional [L0853, 2021].
- An LLC or S-corp limits personal liability only when assets are kept separate. Consult an accountant once the business earns money [L0247, 2021].

### General liability insurance: is it required?

- General liability insurance is optional for a US e-commerce business. Some suppliers require it before they will work with you [L0472, 2025].
- It is optional and depends on supplier requirements [L0468, 2020].
- Most suppliers do not require business insurance [L0855, 2023].
- If a gold supplier requires it, request general liability quotes from the insurer you already use for home or auto cover [L0855, 2023].
- It is optional and depends on risk versus reward, meaning how much you have to lose [L0934, 2023].
- It is a personal decision. The business can run without it, though it may be useful depending on the risk [L1112, 2021].
- It is optional and depends on where you operate [L1279, 2021].
- It is recommended for peace of mind [L0249, 2022].
- It is useful as the business grows [L0665, 2022].
- Business insurance is not required for dropshipping in the US. For the UK, the speaker did not know of a requirement, so a local answer should be checked [L0964, 2023].

- Coverage size should be set according to revenue, which the insurer will ask about [L0468, 2020].

### Buying without brokers

Sources differ on which channel to use. They agree on avoiding small or unvetted brokers and on comparing several quotes [L0249, 2022; L1094, 2023; L1279, 2021; L0671, 2021; L0961, 2020].

- Use an independent quote-comparison website and choose the lowest price for suitable coverage. A higher price does not mean better protection [L1088, 2020].
- Buy from a large direct insurer rather than through a broker, since commissions make broker policies expensive [L0468, 2020].
- Get general liability quotes through the same insurer that holds your personal insurance, such as auto or home. This usually gives better rates and less risk of being taken advantage of than online small business brokers [L1281, 2022].
- Ask the insurer you already use for home, auto or life policies for a business policy, and compare it with other quotes [L0934, 2023].
- Start with a large insurer you already use for other insurance [L0804, 2023].
- Shop through major agencies such as Geico for Business, or ask connections in insurance [L0249, 2022].
- Avoid small brokerages and use major agencies [L0249, 2022].
- Be careful with brokers who charge high premiums, and compare multiple quotes before buying [L0671, 2021].
- Brokers can overcharge heavily for weak coverage [L1279, 2021].
- Agents may overcharge for policies you do not need [L0961, 2020].
- Buy business insurance without overpaying [L1069, 2019]:
  1. Get a general, catch-all business policy first, since it covers the basics [L1069, 2019].
  2. Ask several insurance brokers for quotes, not only one [L1069, 2019].
  3. Be careful with agents who offer every policy they can sell, because many coverages may be unnecessary [L1069, 2019].
  4. Review the coverage again as revenue grows, since insurers may audit the business and premiums can rise [L1069, 2019].
- Start with a small general liability policy. It is a cheaper starting point than a bundle of policies, and more coverage can be added later. Pick coverage based on actual risk [L1111, 2020].

### Premium audits and renewals

- Insurance audits can happen retroactively based on revenue growth. They can result in bills above $10,000 for past premiums, even if no claims occurred [L0249, 2022].
- A revenue audit on a broker-sold policy can lead to tens of thousands in extra premiums [L1088, 2020].
- Do not buy general liability through a broker, because a revenue audit after the policy starts can produce large extra premiums [L1088, 2020].
- A carrier or broker audit after the first year can produce a large retroactive premium bill when sales grow. Check how audits and premiums are calculated before signing [L1111, 2020].
- Before buying, ask the provider how year-end premium audits adjust the price [L0804, 2023].
- Check the premium once revenue grows. Low-price quotes can produce large bills at audit [L0665, 2022].
- A cheap first-year rate can increase sharply later [L1094, 2023].
- Quotes can rise sharply at renewal, so compare quotes from several insurers [L0934, 2023].

### Shipping insurance

- Shipping insurance is important for expensive products, since it covers damage claims and lets you resolve them without a full return [L0970, 2023].
- Carrier shipping insurance policies determine whether a damaged item in transit is refunded along with shipping costs. Check the carrier's policy before deciding who pays [L1094, 2023].

### Licensing for food and special categories

- Before selling food or beverage products, check the licenses and regulations that apply. They add steps beyond normal drop shipping [L0854, 2022].

## Contracts, Partners, Contractors and Agencies

### Written contracts and agency agreements

- Use a written contract even when the relationship feels casual or informal. It protects both parties legally, clarifies expectations, and gives you recourse if problems arise. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Every contract should specify:
  - payment structure
  - timeline of work
  - cancellation terms
  - performance expectations
  - communication protocols
  - penalties for unlawful behavior, such as deleting accounts without consent [L0217, 2025-26]
- Get a written contract even if the agency offers performance guarantees, refund guarantees, or payment-only-if-successful terms. Legal protection matters regardless of how confident either party feels. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Written contracts with offshore freelancers and staff are often seen as unnecessary for simple engagements, because cross-border legal issues are complicated. The main value of a contract is making each party's position and obligations clear. [L1359, 2014]

### Contractors, freelancers and employee classification

#### United States: 1099 contractor or employee

- In the US, pay independent contractors, including family members who work as freelancers, as 1099 contractors. Agree on this and on the expectations in writing before the work starts. [L0832, 2019]
- The team in the one source example was hired as 1099 independent contractors, not employees. If the owner sets fixed hours and full-time control over the work, the contractor may need to be converted to an employee. That brings insurance and benefits obligations. Check local labor and tax laws before deciding. [L0337, 2022]
- Where the two rules meet: a 1099 setup assumes the contractor controls their own working pattern. If you set fixed hours and control the work full-time, the arrangement may need to become employment, as described above. [L0337, 2022; L0832, 2019; L1000, 2017; L1473, 2019]

#### Paying hourly contractors

Pay hourly contractors every two weeks:

1. Each contractor logs their hours on their personal board in Trello.
2. Every other Thursday the owner checks the logged hours.
3. Pay US contractors through QuickBooks.
4. Pay Philippines-based contractors through Wise with direct deposit.

[L0337, 2022]

#### Virtual assistants

- Sign a VA contract before training starts. Include:
  - pay
  - weekly work hours (for example Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:00 Eastern Standard Time)
  - The holidays observed (here US holidays)
  - vacation time and how much notice is required for it
  - how much notice is required to quit
  - The training obligations [L1473, 2019]
- Sign a short agreement letter with the assistant that repeats the job post terms in writing. Include an exclusivity clause stating the assistant will not work for other businesses. [L1000, 2017]
- Classify the virtual assistant as an independent contractor rather than an employee in the signed agreement. Confirm the tax reporting rules for your country with a qualified adviser before paying. [L1000, 2017]

#### Non-disclosure agreements

- For an NDA template, search Google for an NDA template in Google Docs, then adapt it to your needs before sharing it with a contractor. [L1285, 2024]

### Data and account ownership

- Clarify data ownership in the contract. Verify that ad accounts, creative files, and account configurations remain your property, and that the agency cannot delete or withhold access if the relationship ends. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Store all supplier, shipping and app logins in one restricted accounts sheet, so the assistant has what they need without searching. Keep the owner's social media logins there too when the assistant manages those accounts. [L1000, 2017]
- Do not give Fiverr freelancers access to Google Ads or Google Merchant Center accounts. If you need help unsuspending an account, use a reputable PPC agency with a public reputation, since logins from unfamiliar IP addresses can flag an account. [L0803, 2022]

### Payment providers and bank access

- Give a team member access to your payment providers only after you have built enough trust with them, and handle this step with great care. They can install the payment providers in the store so it goes live. [L1358, undated; L1360, undated]
- If an assistant pays for orders, give them a dedicated payment method, such as a PayPal account or a credit card, rather than the owner's main account. [L1173, undated]
- While delegating everything else, the owner kept control of the bank accounts and of supplier payments that needed transfers. This kept financial control in one place during a seven-week absence. [L1437, 2018]

### Business partnerships

- Two partners can own a store through a new corporation split 50/50 or 51/49, or through their existing LLCs owning the corporation. Whichever structure is chosen, a clear written partnership agreement is required. [L0834, 2021]
- Any business partnership needs a written agreement covering:
  - The responsibilities of each partner
  - The money split
  - every foreseeable event
- Typical clauses cover one partner wanting to buy out the other, the death of a partner, and a partner failing to meet their obligations. [L1097, 2025]
- AI tools can produce a useful first draft, but a lawyer should review the final agreement before anyone signs. [L1097, 2025]
- Prompt for drafting a first partnership agreement:

```
I run a dropshipping store called [STORE NAME] on Shopify with my business partner [PARTNER NAME]. We have no written partnership agreement yet. Please help me draft a one-page partnership agreement. First, list everything a standard business partnership agreement should contain. Then include clauses for: what happens if one partner wants to buy the other out; what happens if one partner dies; what happens if one partner does not meet their obligations. Add a responsibilities section that splits tasks between us, for example: [PARTNER A] handles website design and maintenance, product uploads and supplier relationships; [PARTNER B] handles customer service and marketing. Also add a section on how profits, costs and finances are split and paid out. Ask me for any details you need before drafting, and mark which clauses should be reviewed by a lawyer before signing.
```

[L1097, 2025]

### Influencers and ambassadors

- A simple one-page, three-paragraph agreement with each influencer formalizes the arrangement and confirms the posting obligation. It acts as a digital handshake and does not create liability, but it helps set expectations. [L0790, 2017]
- A simple written influencer agreement with the creator's name and key terms creates a record, even if it is not a strict legal contract. Enforceability across countries varies, so get local legal advice before relying on agreements abroad. [L1213, 2023]
- Keep ambassadors unpaid and in the ambassador role. According to general counsel's advice, unpaid ambassadors who sell or refer products but are not paid can be treated as non-employees, which helps avoid employment liability. [L0790, 2017]

### Community rules

- Members must not post or offer paid services to other members of the program, and must not use the course community or direct messages to sell anything to other members. Anyone who wants to sell a service must build a separate business rather than use the program's audience. [L1498, 2021-22]
- Members may not copy or share program content, such as lesson videos, attachments, and spreadsheets, with anyone who is not a member, unless the program owners give express permission. The content is copyrighted and owned by the company behind the program, and sharing it outside the membership can lead to legal action. [L1498, 2021-22]
- Permitted sharing with a non-member, such as a virtual assistant helping build the business:
  1. Message the program owners before sharing.
  2. Name the specific videos or materials you want to share and explain why.
  3. Share only after receiving written approval.

  Sharing without approval is not allowed. [L1498, 2021-22]

### Scam avoidance

#### Unsolicited offers and messages

- Shopify and Google never send unsolicited emails to a store asking for money to optimize the store. Emails from agencies offering store optimization are usually cold outreach and should be ignored. [L0472, 2025]
- Legitimate mentors, coaches, and staff of an online community never send unsolicited direct messages first. Any direct message that arrives right after joining, claiming to be from the community's team, is a scam until proven otherwise. [L1442, 2026]
- Anyone asking for money in a direct message, including for store setup help, course access, or a service fee, is a scammer. Real staff do not request payment through private messages. [L1442, 2026]
- Requests for payment in cryptocurrency made by someone posing as a community coach or support person are a clear fraud signal. Refuse and do not send any funds. [L1442, 2026]
- New members often trust the first private message they receive after joining a server, which is exactly what impersonators count on. Treat every unsolicited message from a community member as untrusted until it is verified in an official channel. [L1442, 2026]

#### Spotting impersonators

- Impersonation scams copy a real person's display name, and sometimes profile picture, then add a small change such as an extra word, a letter, or trailing punctuation. Check the exact spelling of every name against the official member list before trusting a message. Example pattern: a real handle `Alex_Store` becomes `Alex_Store vs` or `Alex_Store.` in a fake account. [L1442, 2026]
- Official announcements, pinned messages, and public channels are the reliable source for who the real staff and coaches are. Compare any suspicious account against these before replying. If a fake account is confirmed, block it and do not engage further. [L1442, 2026]
