Content inside the store's own blog is best started early, and a done-for-you package or paid content waits until traffic data exists [L0500, 2021-22; L0835, 2023; L0804, 2023]. ### Role of Content in the Funnel

#### Content Compared with Sales Copy

- Educational writing that only informs, such as a general guide or blog post, is not sales copy and does not move a visitor toward a purchase. It can have a place on an e-commerce site, but product, collection and sales copy should each be written to earn one defined action. [L1460, 2021-22]
- Authoritative, educational content in text, image, video or social form raises conversion, especially for high-priced or technical products. It engages buyers earlier on the customer awareness path than product pages alone. You do not need hundreds of blog posts; you need more useful content than your competitors. [L1522, 2021-22]
- Content is a long-term investment that builds organic traffic and rankings over time. Unique product descriptions, collection page copy and blog posts should include the correct keywords and be formatted properly. [L1283, 2023]

#### What Makes Content Worth Producing

- Great content must be deep, relevant to what is happening now, shareable, well researched and worth linking to. Content that meets these criteria attracts attention, builds trust, shows relevance and authority, gives search engines what they reward, and makes a business look unique. [L1461, 2021-22]
- Original content is a competitive advantage because no competitor can copy it, which makes the business feel more unique to visitors. [L1461, 2021-22]
- Create unique general content such as blog posts, guest posts, and downloadable supplier catalogs, sales sheets and spec sheets. Unpublished text from supplier materials gives a large SEO advantage once you publish it on your store. [L1357, 2018]

#### Depth Before Width

- Shallow content is common online, so producing deep content is the way to stand out in search and social channels. Search engines and consumers can see large numbers of thin pages, so depth is the differentiator. [L1461, 2021-22]
- There are over one trillion individual web pages online, and most of them are shallow, with very little substance. Because thin pages are so common, deep and comprehensive pages stand out to both search engines and consumers. [L1461, 2021-22]
- Content should be deep before it is wide; only a small share of web content is deep, so depth is a differentiator that compounds over time. A practical test is whether the piece gives visitors more value than any competitor's version on the same topic. [L1461, 2021-22]

#### Mixing Content Types by Funnel Stage

- Problem-solving content is often the best starting point for a business that has no content yet, because the team already knows the customer problems from running the business. It ranks well in search and YouTube and also improves conversion by helping buyers move through the customer journey. [L1461, 2021-22]
- Entertainment content is the most widely engaging content and is the main tool for filling the top of the funnel, since it does not require the visitor to be problem aware. It should still be relevant to the target market and appeal mainly to that audience rather than everyone. [L1461, 2021-22]
- Process for using entertainment content to fill a sales funnel:
  1. Publish interest-based or best-of content that attracts relevant visitors to the website.
  2. Offer an opt-in on the page to add visitors to the email audience.
  3. Place a cookie for retargeting so visitors can be shown ads later.
  4. Serve further content to move them through the funnel toward the desired awareness stage.

  [L1461, 2021-22]
- Example: a beard-care brand's blog mixes content types on one landing page: a product-related review of nose hair trimmers (problem solving), a controversial opinion piece against neckties (entertainment), a personal-story piece about facial hair (entertainment), and a men's hat style guide (a mix of both). This combination of types is presented as a good model for a mixed blog strategy. [L1461, 2021-22]

#### Store Launch and Blog Timing

*Source: one source.*

- Blog posts are not required before supplier approval. If they are holding up the store launch, skip them and add them later. [L1281, 2022]

### Content Types

#### Priority Order

*Source: one source.*

- These six content types are the most shared and most linked to, and they lend themselves to depth, which makes them the strongest choice for SEO and engagement results. Backlinks from other sites are a key reason to favor them. [L1421, 2021-22]
- Focus first on how-to, listicles and ultimate guides, since they are the easiest to produce and the best starting point. Best of the interwebs and expert roundups come after these. [L1421, 2021-22]

#### Problem-Solving Content

*Source: one source.*

- Problem-solving content is usually the easiest type to produce because most problems have known answers, and the product itself is typically the answer. It gives immediate value and can engage people at the problem-aware, solution-aware and product-aware stages of the customer awareness pyramid. [L1461, 2021-22]
- Types of problem-solving content: [L1461, 2021-22]

  | Type | Purpose |
  |---|---|
  | How-to guides | Step-by-step instructions to solve a specific problem |
  | Ultimate guides | Most comprehensive resource on a topic, helping choose products and answer purchase questions |
  | Product comparisons | Pros and cons of specific products for middle-of-funnel buyers deciding between options |
  | Listicles (solution lists) | Numbered lists of solutions or products to a problem, such as 'top 10 ways' or 'five ways to avoid' |
  | Explanatory content | Simple explanations of technical terms, industry lingo or product technology to help customers understand |
  | FAQ content | Answers to common questions, optionally combined with a listicle format, such as 'top 5 questions when shopping for X' |

#### How-To Guides

*Source: one source.*

- How-to content, built step by step:
  1. Pick a concrete problem or pain point the reader has and make the piece a direct answer to it.
  2. Break the solution into logical steps, one segment per step, presented in order (step one, step two, step three and so on).
  3. Explain each step as clearly as possible in jargon-free language, written for the reader with the most basic knowledge.
  4. Use as many images as possible: in written content, insert images; in video, show each step either with an edited static image or with a hands-on recording of the action.

  [L1421, 2021-22]
- Breaking a how-to into small, simple steps makes it feel easy for a beginner, which is what drives the best result for this content type. [L1421, 2021-22]

#### Listicles

*Source: one source.*

- For listicles, the title contains a number and the list covers different items. They suit both problem solving and entertainment, and they are among the most shared and linked content types. [L1421, 2021-22]
- Listicles have a low barrier to entry because most of the information already exists online or offline; the work is mainly collecting, organizing and rewriting it in a unique way instead of copying. [L1421, 2021-22]
- Listicles often work well with a short introduction, a table of contents with anchor links to each section, practical info such as required gear, and a short description per item covering location, who it suits and the conditions. [L1421, 2021-22]
- Listicle research before publishing:
  1. Search for the topic and see how many existing listicles already cover it.
  2. If a competitor has published a top N list, publish a longer or differently sized list instead, for example top 17, top 20 or top 21.

  [L1421, 2021-22]
- Listicle numbers that are not round (for example 7, 17, 23 or 33) tend to get higher engagement and more clicks than round numbers such as 5, 10, 15 or 20. [L1421, 2021-22]
- Title pattern and examples are in [Pattern Matching](#pattern-matching) below. [L1421, 2021-22]

#### Ultimate Guides

*Source: one source.*

- Ultimate guides are the most valuable content and are highly linkable, which makes them strong for problem solving and link building. Their title must be earned: if a piece is called the ultimate guide, it must be the deepest and best resource on that topic. [L1421, 2021-22]
- Ultimate guides can be very long, from roughly 5,000 to 10,000 words. Even when little competing content exists, the piece should be written as if someone could publish a better one soon. [L1421, 2021-22]
- Ultimate guides suit high-volume, more competitive search terms, which are broader and harder to rank for. These pieces are easier to build backlinks to, and such terms generally need more backlinks to reach top positions on Google. [L1421, 2021-22]
- An ultimate guide can combine how-to and listicle styles, for example an ultimate guide to choosing a product that works as a how-to, or an unlimited-count list where each item is covered in detail. [L1421, 2021-22]
- Not every piece should be an ultimate guide because it takes more work, but some should be included in the mix. [L1421, 2021-22]

#### Best of the Interwebs

*Source: one source.*

- Best of the interwebs pieces collect existing online content, which makes them quick to produce. Images can be taken from the original sources, so custom visuals are usually not needed. [L1421, 2021-22]
- Each item in a best of the interwebs piece needs commentary: explain why it is good, what it does particularly well or what you like about it. A simple link with no insight is not enough; personal opinion is acceptable. [L1421, 2021-22]
- Entertainment form: curated lists of the best blogs, YouTube channels or resources in the target niche, bringing relevant visitors to the site. [L1461, 2021-22]

#### Expert Roundups

*Source: one source.*

- Expert roundup production:
  1. Choose a popular question or statement in your market.
  2. Identify experts with large audiences using the attention matrix, meaning people regarded as authorities who already have audiences through websites, social media or YouTube.
  3. Find their contact details on their website or social media, then email each one with the simple question.
  4. Contact more experts than you need, because not all will respond.
  5. Keep the question simple so that a few-line answer is enough; for example, ask for one favorite item.
  6. Build the piece around each answer, adding a short explanation and a relevant image sourced from the internet.
  7. When published, email the participants with a thank-you, a link and a request to share the piece.

  [L1421, 2021-22]
- Expert roundups use experts' names and trust to attract attention to your piece, which is useful when your own name is not yet trusted in the market. [L1421, 2021-22]
- An expert roundup should state in its headline that it is based on expert feedback; leaving that out weakens the piece. [L1421, 2021-22]
- Expert roundups can take longer, from a few days to a couple of weeks, because the work depends on experts replying. [L1421, 2021-22]
- Roundup content gains reach through reciprocity: after publishing, send the contributing experts the link and ask them to share it with their audience, which lets the business use their audience to gain exposure. [L1461, 2021-22]
- Roundup content in the entertainment table: expert quotes gathered by emailing experts for a short comment; the published roundup is shared with the experts, who often share it with their audience. [L1461, 2021-22]

#### Controversial Content

*Source: one source.*

- Controversial content takes a position that goes against commonly held beliefs. It should be used sparingly, because it is highly engaging but polarizing. [L1421, 2021-22]
- A controversial position must be explained in depth with supporting evidence and a real attempt to convince the reader. A fake position cannot be defended in depth, so it should not be used. [L1421, 2021-22]
- Controversial content in the entertainment table: taking an opposing position on a commonly held belief in the market to attract attention. [L1461, 2021-22]

#### Entertainment Content Formats

*Source: one source.*

- Types of entertainment content: [L1461, 2021-22]

  | Type | Purpose |
  |---|---|
  | Interest listicles | Lists about a topic of interest to the audience, such as top surf breaks or top destinations, without selling products |
  | Best-of-the-internet lists | Curated lists of the best blogs, YouTube channels or resources in the target niche, bringing relevant visitors to the site |
  | Roundup content | Expert quotes gathered by emailing experts for a short comment; the published roundup is shared with the experts, who often share it with their audience |
  | Breaking news content | Coverage of new developments in the market; high engagement at the time but not evergreen, so weak for long-term ranking |
  | Controversial content | Taking an opposing position on a commonly held belief in the market to attract attention |

### Idea Sourcing

#### Keyword Research as the Source

*Source: one source (no year given).*

- Take topic ideas for pages and blog posts from the keyword research worksheet created during organic keyword research, not from guesses. [L1321, 2020-26]

#### Community and Audience Questions

*Source: one source (no year given).*

- Blog post ideas can come from the most popular threads on community sites such as Reddit within a niche. Note which questions get the most engagement and turn them into content candidates. [L1321, 2020-26]

*Source: one source.*

- A public container such as a blog or website listing content in reverse chronological order keeps you producing because the newest item is what visitors see first. Use it also as a place to develop ideas that are not finished yet. [L1343, undated]

#### Platform Analytics and Search Questions

*Source: one source.*

- Rising volume of AI-generated, recycled and syndicated content causes audience fatigue, and attention is split between creators, streaming services and daily life. Content needs a clear strategy to stop the scroll and hold viewers. [L1457, 2025-26]
- Idea-sourcing procedure:
  1. Open the analytics of the main platform, TikTok being the most detailed for audience insight, and Instagram analytics for audience behaviour.
  2. Read the search terms your audience uses and the videos they interact with.
  3. Note the questions behind those searches.
  4. Build videos that answer these questions before the viewer asks them.

  [L1457, 2025-26]

#### Problem-Based Ideas

*Source: one source.*

- Before producing any piece of content, follow these steps:
  1. Research what already exists on the topic by searching for it and reviewing who has covered it.
  2. Identify which aspects the existing top content has not covered.
  3. Plan how to go deeper and provide more value than the existing pieces.
  4. Produce the content so it aims to be the most comprehensive resource online on that topic.

  [L1461, 2021-22]

### Competitor and SERP Research

#### Reviewing Existing Coverage

*Source: one source.*

- Listicle research: search for the topic, count how many existing listicles cover it, and if a competitor has published a top N list, publish a longer or differently sized list instead (for example top 17, top 20 or top 21). [L1421, 2021-22]
- Ultimate guides target high-volume, competitive terms that need more backlinks to reach top positions on Google. [L1421, 2021-22]
- Shallow content is common, so a depth-first approach is the way to stand out against what already ranks. [L1461, 2021-22]
- Gap test before production: find what the top content misses, go deeper than it, and aim to be the most comprehensive resource. [L1461, 2021-22]

#### Competitor and Keyword Data

*Source: one source.*

- Six-month done-for-you content plan process: [L1281, 2022]
  1. Research the niche, the site, the competitors and the keywords.
  2. Build a content marketing plan from that research.
  3. Write blog posts and source images.
  4. Publish the content on a schedule over six months to build organic traffic from Google.

### Pattern Matching

#### Listicle Titles

*Source: one source.*

- Listicle title pattern: [Number] + [best/top] + [item] + [location or qualifier] + [audience or experience level qualifier]. The qualifier at the end widens the appeal to a segment, such as beginners, advanced riders or first-time visitors, while the base title still captures the broad search. [L1421, 2021-22]
  - Travel niche example: "11 Best Hiking Trails in Colorado for Every Fitness Level"
  - Finance niche example: "19 Best Budget Apps for Freelancers Who Are Just Starting Out"
- Use non-round numbers such as 7, 17, 23 or 33, which tend to earn higher engagement and more clicks than 5, 10, 15 or 20. [L1421, 2021-22]
- Listicle structure: short introduction, table of contents with anchor links, practical info such as required gear, and a short description per item covering location, who it suits and the conditions. [L1421, 2021-22]

#### Trend Patterns

*Source: one source.*

- Trend search example: a skincare brand checks the search terms in TikTok analytics and finds a viral technique, such as slugging, being searched for often. The brand then builds videos that answer the questions viewers are about to ask, before they search for them. [L1457, 2025-26]

### Content Planner

#### Starting Plan Structure

*Source: one source.*

- The six-month plan runs research, a content marketing plan, writing and sourcing images, then scheduled publishing. [L1281, 2022]

*Source: one source.*

- Content planning method: for the first 12 ideas, think of a few different versions of each content type and include them in the plan. [L1421, 2021-22]

#### Content Pillars

*Source: one source.*

- Each content pillar should align with the brand's expertise, its business goals and what the audience cares about. Pillars keep content balanced, prevent random posting, simplify monthly planning and show which formats to scale. [L1457, 2025-26]
- Content pillars are assigned to fixed weekdays. Two pillars per week is the minimum, five pillars fit daily posting Monday to Friday, and seven pillars are possible but usually too demanding. [L1457, 2025-26]
- Test a set of content pillars for two months, then review performance data. In the next month, increase the posting frequency of the pillars that perform well and reduce the frequency of those that do not. [L1457, 2025-26]
- Posting without pillars leads to disorganized content and makes batch production much harder. Without a system, the creator also loses motivation more easily. [L1457, 2025-26]

#### Flexibility in the Plan

*Source: one source.*

- Keep some flexibility in the schedule, for example one fixed weekday for trend-based videos. Trend slots are harder to batch but allow a fast reaction to trends while the rest of the plan stays fixed. [L1457, 2025-26]

### Cadence

#### Publishing Pace

*Source: one source (no year given).*

| Stage | Pace | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Starting | One new piece of content per month, enough to get started and to see how the content performs | [L1321, 2020-26] |
| Later, once the process runs smoothly | One new piece of content every two weeks | [L1321, 2020-26] |

- The sources set different pace figures for different situations: the monthly and fortnightly paces above apply to a single store's own content plan, while the per-writer rate below applies to a writer's weekly workload. [L1321, 2020-26; L1396, 2021-22]

#### Batching and Production Rhythm

*Source: one source.*

- Batching means filming a whole series in one session. For example, two hours of filming can produce ten videos for one weekday slot, covering the next ten weeks. [L1457, 2025-26]

#### Writer Throughput

*Source: one source.*

- Ask writers to focus on one product category per week, for example twelve collection pages for similar products, so they keep the same subject and write faster. A writer needing about 1.5 hours per 500-word page can usually finish about two pages per day. Plan about twelve content items per week per writer as a starting point. [L1396, 2021-22]
- Writing a 500-word collection page took a writer about 1.5 hours, so a typical day allows two or three pages. Set weekly targets based on this measured rate, then adjust after one week of real results. Do not expect a 500-word article in half an hour. [L1396, 2021-22]
