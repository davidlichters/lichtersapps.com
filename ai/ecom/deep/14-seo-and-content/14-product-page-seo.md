## Product Page SEO

### Timing and Priority

- Product pages are the pages that convert into sales, so SEO effort goes there before other content. [L0469, 2021]
- Apply product page SEO to every product page, not just one or two, and use the same keyword and product information standards across the whole catalog so every product matches its relevant searches. [L1254, 2020-26]
- Product page SEO makes products appear for the exact product names shoppers search for and for closely related niche terms. Relevance between the search and the product page decides whether a product shows up at all. [L1254, 2020-26]
- Keyword research for product descriptions is done after launch, once ads are live and traffic is coming in. It is not required before uploading products. [L0970, 2023]
- Launch with supplier-provided descriptions. After two to four weeks, check which products already receive impressions, clicks and sales, then rewrite and enhance those descriptions first. [L0471, 2024]
- Start with the top 20% of products by traffic. You do not need to rewrite every description at once. [L1190, 2020-26]
- For a large catalog, writing unique descriptions for every product is too much work for too little return. Focus the effort on pages that already receive traffic. [L1275, 2019]
- Products you want to rank for need unique, longer descriptions. Give your writing time to those products first and decide which descriptions get the most effort. [L1274, 2018]

#### Finding the Highest-Traffic Pages

**Procedure**

1. Pull a monthly report in Google Analytics.
2. Open the Content section.
3. Sort pages by traffic.
4. Add unique copy to the highest-traffic pages first, then work down the list. [L0671, 2021]

**Procedure**

1. Upload all products with the supplier's specifications as a starting description.
2. Run Google Shopping ads to drive traffic.
3. In Google Analytics, open Behavior > Site Content to see which product URLs visitors view most.
4. Write unique product descriptions for the top 20 percent of those pages, following the 80-20 rule. [L1275, 2019]

- Unique descriptions on the most visited product pages raise conversion, because shoppers get more information. The same pages also tend to rank better organically. [L1275, 2019]
- For a monthly view of traffic, use the Content report. For a view of which product URLs visitors view most, use Behavior > Site Content. [L0671, 2021; L1275, 2019]
- Rewrite the pages that receive the most traffic in Google Analytics first. (2023.) [L0470, 2023]

### Supplier Descriptions: Launch Versus Rewrite

Sources agree that the target is a unique description. They differ on whether copied text is acceptable at launch. Both positions are listed below, with the conditions for each.

**Target state**

- Write unique product descriptions for every product. Copied manufacturer or competitor descriptions do not rank in search results. [L0337, 2022]
- Product descriptions must be at least 300 words long and must be unique, not copied from other sources. [L1467, undated]
- Use reverse image search and competitor product pages to find more information for descriptions. Write descriptions that differ from the supplier's copy to avoid duplicate content. [L0801, 2021]
- Use an SEO-based method for product pages rather than generic AI-generated text. (2025.) [L0966, 2025]

**Acceptable at launch, rewritten later**

- Copied supplier descriptions do not cause a blacklist, but original descriptions tend to rank better over time, so rewrite them as traffic grows. [L0970, 2023]
- Supplier product descriptions copied at launch are generally acceptable, because duplicate manufacturer copy is common across many sites. Later, find the highest-traffic product pages and rewrite them to be unique and more useful. (2023.) [L0470, 2023]
- When a new supplier is approved, its default catalog descriptions can be uploaded on day one and rewritten later. Leaving them unchanged at launch does not hurt the product page. [L1190, 2020-26]
- Supplier stock descriptions are used by many stores, and duplicate content is not a reason to avoid them, because product content can still rank well. Rewriting them gives better results, so do it when time allows. [L1190, 2020-26]
- Stock supplier descriptions are the base of product descriptions, then built out in the SEO lesson of module six. [L0964, 2023]
- In the early launch phase, supplier descriptions and specifications may be copied and pasted into the store, because duplicate content matters little for paid ads and speed to the first sale matters more. Rewrite them later during the growth phase. [L1163, 2021-22]

**Older position**

- Copying a competitor's product descriptions or supplier default text weakens your chances of ranking for those products, because search engines see duplicate content. [L1274, 2018]

### Product Titles

**Principles**

- The product title is the most reliable keyword target because it is the H1 heading and the most relevant term for that page. [L0881, 2021-22]
- Broad product type terms are better left to collection or content pages, unless the site is small and the product is the only one of its type. [L0881, 2021-22]
- Use a unique custom title instead of the generic supplier listing name. Unique names usually face less competition on Google when shoppers search for them. [L1207, undated]
- Titles and descriptions should target the brand name, the supplier's name and product identifiers, rather than many extra keywords. [L0964, 2023]
- Match product titles and URL slugs to how top future competitors write them. Include SKU or variant numbers only if those competitors include them. (2024.) [L0471, 2024]
- Titles should match what buyers actually search for, such as brand, color and product type, rather than the long technical name from the supplier. Full technical details and variations belong in the description. [L1085, 2018]

**Title order**

Sources give different orders. Each is listed with its conditions.

| Order | Conditions | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Brand name, product name, SKU number | Standard order. Variants may occasionally be included. (2023.) | [L0470, 2023] |
| Brand name, product name, SKU number | Format in Smart SEO. (2025.) | [L1097, 2025] |
| Brand, model name, product type, optional descriptor | Pattern for brands with sibling products. Separates the item from similar products of the same brand. | [L1163, 2021-22] |
| Brand, product name, SKU, supplier identifier | Built into the product name field for long-tail targeting. | [L0854, 2022] |
| Search term in the exact order people type it | Used when optimizing from search data. | [L1271, 2020-26] |

**Formula**

Structure each product title as:

Brand > Model name > Product type > Optional descriptive words that separate the item from similar products of the same brand

Example pattern: Brand Model Product type in Colour

Search engines use the title as a primary signal to match products to shoppers' searches, so the product type (for example electric skateboard) should be included even if the supplier omits it. [L1163, 2021-22]

Examples in the same pattern:

- Trailride Lumen 2 carbon road helmet in matte white
- Harbor Loft 4 ceramic pour-over coffee dripper in sand

It works because shoppers search by product type and search engines treat the title as a main signal for matching a product to a query. [L1163, 2021-22]

**Common mistake**

- Supplier product titles often lack the brand name or the product type, and that gap hurts search matching. Edit titles when uploading. A title with only a brand and model number should have the product type and colour added. [L1163, 2021-22]

**Search-term order**

Example: change 'one source zip up hoodie' to 'one source hoodie zip up' when shoppers type the second form. [L1271, 2020-26]

**Keyword choice**

- On a product page, the focus keyword should be the product name, brand name and SKU or model number, not the broader niche keyword. Keyword cannibalization becomes a real problem mainly when many blog posts cover related topics, or when a collection page targets the same keyword as the product pages. (2024.) [L0666, 2024]
- For the target keyword in a rewrite, use the short product name that starts with the product type, not the brand name, size or finish. Shorter names match what shoppers actually search for. [L1190, 2020-26]

Sources differ on the focus keyword. [L0666, 2024] uses the product name with brand and SKU or model. [L1190, 2020-26] uses the short product name starting with the product type. Use [L0666, 2024] for the page's focus keyword and [L1190, 2020-26] when choosing the target keyword for a description rewrite.

**Keyword in the title**

- The focus keyword should be placed as early as possible in the page title, since the title is the H1 of the product page. [L1191, 2021-22]
- Place the focus keyword at the front of the page title. [L1191, 2021-22]
- Smart SEO rule: do not put targeted keywords in the URL or the title. (2025.) [L1097, 2025]

Sources differ: [L1097, 2025] forbids keywords in the title, while [L1191, 2021-22] and [L1191, 2021-22] place them at the front. [L1097, 2025] applies to the Smart SEO workflow. Outside it, place the focus keyword at the front of the title.

**Feed title**

- Use the SEO-optimized product title as the Shopify page title. [L1170, 2021-22]

### URLs and Product Handles

**Decision: whether to include a SKU in the URL**

1. Search for the product on Google without the SKU.
2. Look at the top organic results, not the ads.
3. If the top results show one product page with all variants selectable, leave the SKU out of the URL and keep all variants on one page.
4. If the top results are split by SKU, include the SKU and create a separate page for each SKU. [L0469, 2021]

**Handle changes**

- Do not change the product handles, and do not put targeted keywords in the URL. (Smart SEO, 2025.) [L1097, 2025]
- Change the URL handle to the short keyword form (for example one source-hoodie) and let Shopify create the automatic redirect from the old URL. [L1271, 2020-26]
- Use a short page URL containing the focus keyword. [L1191, 2021-22]
- When optimizing a product, keep the URL the same. Avoid changing the URL unless needed, and use redirects when URLs must change. (2023.) [L0964, 2023]

Sources differ. [L1097, 2025] says do not change handles. [L1271, 2020-26] changes the handle when optimizing from search data, and Shopify creates the redirect. [L0964, 2023] says keep the URL unless a change is needed, and use redirects for any change. Use [L1097, 2025] under Smart SEO. Otherwise change a handle only when the search data justifies it, and create a redirect as [L1271, 2020-26] and [L0964, 2023] describe.

### Variants Versus Separate Listings

**Decision procedure**

1. Search Google for the product name without any color or variant word.
2. Check how the top results list their variants.
3. If the top results list variants together on one page, do the same.
4. If they break variants apart, break them apart too.
5. Test the choice over time, because it varies by supplier and product. [L0359, 2020]

**Competitor check (2025)**

- When choosing product variants vs separate product listings, check how the top 2-3 competitors handle the same products, and replicate their structure. [L0255, 2025]

**Competitor check**

- Check the top two to three competitors in the niche. If they use separate product pages for variants, use separate pages. If they use product options, use options. [L0969, 2022]

**Shopify handling**

- Shopify product variant handling should follow what the top search results do for the same product name, because variant pages affect search and Google Shopping results. [L0359, 2020]
- Use the niche keyword in the store name and in product variant pages. Breaking variants into separate pages usually creates more direct traffic for specific keywords, but test it. [L0359, 2020]

Sources differ in emphasis. [L0359, 2020] notes that separate variant pages usually bring more direct traffic for specific keywords. [L0359, 2020] and [L0969, 2022] say to follow the top results. Use the competitor check by default, and test separate pages where you expect direct traffic for specific keywords. [L0359, 2020; L0969, 2022]

**Size and color tables**

- Unique product content such as size tables, color tables and variant charts helps both SEO and the customer's buying decision. Presenting your own version of this information also makes your listings different from competitors. [L1357, 2018]

### Keyword Placement

**Where keywords belong**

- Place keywords throughout the product page: in the title, bullet points, description, and the Seller Central keyword field. Write naturally for human readers, not search bots, but ensure keywords appear organically. [L0228, 2015]
- A week-long oversight omitting keywords from Seller Central significantly reduced visibility and conversions until corrected. [L0228, 2015]
- Use the niche keyword in the store name. [L0359, 2020]

**Long-tail keyword procedure**

1. Build the keyword from the brand (supplier name), the product name, the SKU and any unique supplier identifier.
2. Put the full string in the product name field.
3. Repeat it in the product description and the SEO description section of the product page.
4. Do not stuff the same phrase repeatedly. [L0854, 2022]

**Focus keyword and synonyms**

- Choose a focus keyword plus relevant synonyms, based on the keyword research already done for that product. [L1191, 2021-22]
- Keywords from the product's own search data are used first. Keyword research for product descriptions is done after launch. [L0970, 2023]

**Revenue-based keywords (Google Ads)**

Procedure:

1. Identify the high-converting Google Ads keywords by revenue.
2. Place them in the product title.
3. Place them in the product description.
4. Place them in the meta title and meta description.

This improves organic conversions and helps your ads show for those keywords more often. [L1357, 2018]

**Keyword counts**

Sources give different counts. Each applies to a different measure.

| Measure | Value | When it applies | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Occurrences in the product description | 3 to 5 times | Effective optimization without keyword-stuffing penalties | [L0176, undated] |
| Occurrences on top-visited pages | 3 to 5 times | The product name counts as one occurrence, so two mentions in the rewritten description plus the product name on the page gives three | [L1190, 2020-26] |
| Occurrences across the whole page | 5 to 10 times | Typical placements that reach 5: page title, one H2 heading, the first 100 words of body copy, one product image file name, one product image alt text | [L1191, 2021-22] |
| Number of distinct keywords on the page | 3 to 5 keywords | Include the brand name, product name, and product ID such as a SKU or GTIN when one exists (2023) | [L1094, 2023] |

- Placing the target keyword in nearly every line of the description causes keyword stuffing, which reads awkwardly and hurts the page. Use the keyword naturally. [L1191, 2021-22]
- Include the focus keyword at least once in the first 100 words of plain text, most often the product description. [L1191, 2021-22]

**Google and the feed**

- Google reads the product description first and the features second when it reads the Merchant Center feed. [L0964, 2023]

### Writing the Description

**Procedure**

1. Write at least 300 words that are unique to the product.
2. Describe the important features of the product.
3. Explain how each feature benefits the customer.
4. End with a call to action that encourages the customer to buy the product.
5. Add a list of 4 dot points that highlight the main features.
6. Add a list of technical details such as dimensions, weight and similar specifications. [L1467, undated]

**Length and readability**

- Product descriptions should contain at least 300 unique words. Below this, Google may treat the page as thin content, which is why the threshold matters for indexing. [L1191, 2021-22]
- Product descriptions should be set in at least 18pt font and use short sentences and short paragraphs to keep the text readable. [L1191, 2021-22]
- Long, technical or expensive products should be chunked with subheadings, each describing the few paragraphs beneath it. Short product descriptions do not need this. [L1099, 2021-22]
- Putting too much information on a product page is said to hurt SEO. Keep product page tabs to basic information at launch and expand them later. [L1300, 2021-22]

**Content formula**

Describe what the product is, who it is for, and what problem it solves, with specifics about use and fit. Such content works because it answers the questions a buyer types into search, which helps the product rank and convert. [L0801, 2021]

Example 1: Solid oak dining table that seats six, with a finish that resists scratches, for families who host weekly dinners.

Example 2: Compact standing desk with a bamboo top and a 60 to 120 cm height range, for home offices with limited space.

### Headings

- Each product page should have exactly one H1 tag, which is the page or product title. No other H1 tags should appear on the product page. [L1191, 2021-22]
- Headings in the description area should be H2 tags. No heading tagged H1 to H6 should ever contain a link. [L1191, 2021-22]
- Links inside heading tags are a negative signal for Google, so they should never be used on any page type. [L1191, 2021-22]
- The focus keyword should appear in at least one H2 heading, preferably the first one. [L1191, 2021-22]
- Tag every heading in the description area as H2, with no link attached. [L1191, 2021-22]

### Images and Video

**Feed-optimized page procedure**

1. Use the SEO-optimized product title as the Shopify page title.
2. Give images descriptive file names.
3. Write a unique alt tag for each image.
4. Write a solid product description. [L1170, 2021-22]

**Image rules**

- Make product images unique where possible, each with a unique descriptive file name. At least one image, ideally the first, carries the focus keyword in its file name. [L1191, 2021-22]
- Compress product images as far as possible without visible quality loss. [L1191, 2021-22]
- Give images in the description area unique file names and alt tags. [L1191, 2021-22]
- Typical placements for the focus keyword include one product image file name and one product image alt text. [L1191, 2021-22]

**Video rules**

- Load videos in the description area with a thin (lazy) load. [L1191, 2021-22]

### Question and Answer Sections

- Add a question and answer section to product pages. It serves shoppers and supports SEO. [L0894, 2020-26]
- Add a short question-and-answer section near the end of each product description, using an H2 or H3 heading for each question. Only include questions and answers that do not already appear on the FAQ page or in other content on the site. [L0658, 2021-22]
- Product-page Q&A sections can improve conversion rates by answering buyer questions on the spot. They also add unique content to product pages and give search engines extra question-and-answer material to display. [L0658, 2021-22]

### Meta Title and Meta Description

- Give the meta title and meta description a unique text that includes the focus keyword. [L1191, 2021-22]
- Set the page title to the keyword, followed by the brand name. Rewrite the meta description with the keyword at the front. [L1271, 2020-26]
- Meta titles and descriptions can be improved with keywords when the URL stays the same. [L0964, 2023]
- Place the revenue-based keywords in the meta title and meta description. [L1357, 2018]
- Check the meta description on each listing as part of the checklist. [L0337, 2022]

### Shopping Feed and Search Listing

- Customize product page copy for SEO. Google Merchant Center pulls information from product pages, so better-optimized pages can rank higher in free listings. [L0671, 2021]
- Aim to rank in the top 1 to 3 organic Google results for your brand name plus product name by improving your product pages. This gives you two spots on the first page when you also run Google Shopping ads. (2023.) [L0804, 2023]

**Search engine listing check**

1. Open the Search engine listing section in the product.
2. Read the title and description and make sure they are readable and make sense.
3. Shopify pulls them automatically from the product title and description, so fix any gibberish before saving. [L1331, 2025-26]

### Using Search Data After Launch

**Optimization procedure**

1. Open the product page on the live store to see how it currently looks.
2. Open the same product in the Shopify admin and edit it.
3. Change the product title so it contains the search term in the exact order people type it (e.g. 'one source hoodie zip up' instead of 'one source zip up hoodie').
4. In the SEO section, set the page title to the keyword, followed by the brand name.
5. Rewrite the meta description with the keyword at the front.
6. Change the URL handle to the short keyword form (e.g. 'one source-hoodie') and let Shopify create the automatic redirect from the old URL.
7. Save the page.
8. Check Google Search Console again after two weeks for more impressions, a better average position and a higher CTR. [L1271, 2020-26]
