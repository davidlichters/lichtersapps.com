## Keyword Research and Tools

### Tool stack and when to use each tool

- Google Keyword Planner is free with a Google Ads account and shows search volume, competition level and bid estimates. Use it to find high-volume, low-competition keywords for content [L0226, 2017; L1272, 2021-22]. Without a Google Ads account it is not needed for SEO [L1272, 2021-22].
- Keyword Planner is most useful for SEO keyword research in a niche, and its data can serve as a fallback if the main research tool is not working [L1097, 2025].
- Keyword Planner has a landing page option. Enter the URL of a competitor's or manufacturer's page and it returns keywords relevant to that page. Export the results into a spreadsheet to build a master keyword list [L0380, 2014].
- Keyword Planner numbers can lag behind Google Trends, so do not rely only on it for trending topics [L1120, 2024+].
- Keyword Finder allows 2-3 free keyword searches per day. Paid access provides keyword suggestions, search volume, competition metrics, and shows how competitors rank for those keywords [L0226, 2017].
- Ubersuggest costs about $10 per month and is a cheap option for keyword research [L0758, 2021-22].
- Google and YouTube autosuggest are free and generate ideas from root terms [L0758, 2021-22].
- Google Ads search data from existing Google Shopping campaigns is a usable source of keywords [L0758, 2021-22].
- Freelancers on Fiverr can be hired to pull keywords [L0758, 2021-22].
- Answer the Public is a question-based keyword tool for finding the questions buyers ask [L0949, 2021-22].
- Alexa.com provides website traffic rankings, organic and paid traffic sources, keyword performance, backlink analysis and competitor performance metrics. Use it to assess micro-influencer websites before outreach and to analyse competitor traffic [L0226, 2017].
- SpyFoot.com, SimilarWeb.com and Alexa.com show where competitor traffic originates, which keywords competitors rank for, and their backlink sources. Free versions offer limited data; paid versions provide detailed insights [L0226, 2017].
- Ahrefs is the SEO research tool used for keyword and competitor research and has a free trial [L0801, 2021]. It is preferred over Keyword Planner for keyword research because Keyword Planner has hidden more data over time [L0382, 2017].
- Ahrefs Competing Domains and Content Gap show competitor keyword gaps [L0382, 2017].
- Ahrefs is expensive and is not needed in the early stage of a store. Invest in it once paid traffic is profitable enough to fund content work [L0801, 2021].
- Paid suites such as Ahrefs, Moz and SEMrush are more accurate and show more data points, but are not necessary at the early stage [L0758, 2021-22].
- Cheap tools such as Ubersuggest and Google Keyword Planner are sufficient for content keyword research [L0758, 2021-22].

| Situation | Tool | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Early stage, tight budget | Ubersuggest, Keyword Planner, autosuggest | [L0758, 2021-22] |
| Paid traffic not yet profitable | Keyword Planner and free Google methods; Ahrefs only on free trial | [L0801, 2021] |
| Competitor keyword gaps | Ahrefs Competing Domains and Content Gap | [L0382, 2017] |
| Niche SEO research with a Google Ads account | Keyword Planner | [L1097, 2025] |

### Market settings and the data you need

- Set the country in every keyword tool to the market where customers are, for example English / United States for a US store or Australia for an Australian store, before pulling data [L0758, 2021-22; L0877, 2021-22].
- Search volume and difficulty differ by country, and data from the wrong country gives wrong numbers [L0758, 2021-22; L0877, 2021-22].
- At the content research stage you need only two data points per keyword: search volume in the country your customers search from, and search difficulty [L0877, 2021-22].
- Search difficulty is usually scored from 0 to 100 in tools such as Ubersuggest and Ahrefs. A higher score means harder to rank [L0758, 2021-22; L0877, 2021-22].
- Paid difficulty and average CPC are also reported in tools like Ubersuggest and are useful for paid ads [L0758, 2021-22].
- Extra metrics and backlink-level data are needed only later, when a specific page will be promoted with a link building campaign [L0877, 2021-22].

### Collecting keywords

#### Keyword research procedure

1. Broadly collect all relevant keywords in your market for your products, niche and the interests you serve [L0758, 2021-22; L0877, 2021-22].
2. Group the keywords into themes or topics [L0758, 2021-22; L0877, 2021-22].
3. Allocate the keywords to page types: content, collection or product page. Map them to specific pages as you build content [L0758, 2021-22; L0877, 2021-22].
4. Record everything in a living keyword research document that is easy to access and update, typically a spreadsheet [L0758, 2021-22; L0877, 2021-22].
- Before writing a content piece, decide which search term it should rank for, so it does not overlap with product or collection pages [L0877, 2021-22].
- Keyword research is needed for both content marketing and general SEO [L0877, 2021-22].

#### Starting from root terms

- Start with root keywords, meaning the broadest search terms relevant to your products. Run each root term separately through the keyword tool [L0877, 2021-22].
- Expand from three kinds of root terms: the product type (for example surfboards), the activity or use it serves (for example surfing), and each product line (for example wetsuits) [L0758, 2021-22].
- Activity and use terms bring in entertaining and problem-solving content ideas that product-type terms alone do not reveal [L0758, 2021-22].
- Run terms about the use or problem your product solves, such as a sport or activity, to get content ideas [L0877, 2021-22].
- Run every relevant root through the same export process [L0758, 2021-22].

#### Ubersuggest collection workflow

1. Enter a root keyword in Ubersuggest.
2. Open Keyword Ideas.
3. Export the list to CSV.
4. Repeat for every relevant root term and related root term. For a surf shop, this includes surfboards, surfing and wetsuits.
5. Keep only the terms that match the business [L0758, 2021-22].

#### Master sheet procedure

1. Paste all exported CSV files one after the other into one spreadsheet [L0758, 2021-22; L0877, 2021-22].
2. Sort all rows by search volume, highest first [L0877, 2021-22].
3. Remove terms with less than 100 searches per month [L0877, 2021-22].
4. Remove terms you will never rank for, such as *used* if you only sell new products [L0877, 2021-22].
5. Move the remaining terms to the Content, Collection or Product Page tab according to their intent [L0877, 2021-22].
- Sort by search volume and judge each term manually. Terms with zero or very low volume are ignored [L0758, 2021-22].
- Create separate tabs or categories for Content, Collection and Product Page, and move each term into the matching category [L0758, 2021-22].
- Exclude terms irrelevant to the business, such as used products when only new products are sold, or drawings when no art is sold [L0758, 2021-22].
- Some terms are not relevant to your business, such as *drawing* for a surfboard shop or *rentals* if you do not rent. Ignore them even if their volume and difficulty look attractive [L0877, 2021-22].

#### Grouping variations

- Term variations with the same meaning, such as *surfboards for sale* and *surfboards on sale*, or singular and plural, or word-order differences, should be treated as one theme. Combine their volumes for a realistic estimate, since ranking for one usually helps with the other. Treat them as one target instead of two [L0758, 2021-22; L0877, 2021-22].
- Keyword research action item for this stage: run the full research for the market and products, export the raw data to a spreadsheet, sort and sift it, and build a list of search terms to target with content (video, blog or other format). Keep the list updated as the program continues [L0758, 2021-22].

### Finding related keywords on Google

#### Related searches procedure

1. Search the focus keyword on google.com.
2. Scroll to the bottom of the first page of results.
3. Read the box labelled *Searches related to* for the most common variations.
4. Skip variations that name a competitor, for example a marketplace site [L0881, 2021-22].
5. Copy each relevant related search into the worksheet as a related keyword ([L1120, 2024+]).
6. Use the remaining relevant variations, such as adjectives like big, retro, hybrid or cheap, on the page, unless another page already targets that exact term [L0881, 2021-22].
7. Repeat with the next keyword ideas if needed [L1120, 2024+].

- Related searches are a free way to find keywords and variations [L1120, 2024+; L0881, 2021-22].

#### Keyword groups

- The main keyword of a group is the keyword with the highest search volume found in Keyword Planner. Related keywords and variations are added to that main keyword to form a keyword group [L1120, 2024+].
- Brand names such as IKEA are not targeted, because people searching a brand already know what they want and the brand is not yours. Variations with the prefix *best* can be used on collection pages [L1120, 2024+].

### Competitor research

#### Competitor check on Google

1. Search the keyword on google.com.
2. Ignore the sponsored ads at the top and look only at the organic results.
3. Open the top results and note the page type, such as a product page, a collection page or a blog.
4. Note what each top page includes, so your own page can offer more value and rank better [L1120, 2024+].

- Use competitor pages to see what already ranks, then build a better page with more useful content around the same keywords. Copying other sites' content is not part of this process [L1120, 2024+].
- A competitor's top results also show which marketplaces, large retailers and specialist stores compete for the term [L1486, 2021-22].

#### Competitor blog topics

1. Search your niche keyword followed by the word *blog* on Google.
2. Open the top two ranked blogs.
3. Note the topics in their posts, including buying tips, news sections and older posts.
4. Add each relevant topic as a keyword in the worksheet [L1120, 2024+].

- A competitor blog that looks unmaintained, is not fully functional and has not been updated for a long time is usually easier to outrank for its topics. Its older posts and news section still show which topics are covered and can be used as a list of ideas. Check these sections as well as the newest posts [L1120, 2024+].

#### Ahrefs Content Gap procedure

1. In Ahrefs, open the Competing Domains tool and enter your domain. Look at the list sorted by common keywords and pick a competitor that is closely related to your store [L0382, 2017].
2. Open the Content Gap tool. Set the competitor as the target that ranks for keywords and your own site as the one that should not rank for them, so it shows keywords the competitor ranks for and you do not [L0382, 2017].
3. Enable the checkbox *At least one of the targets should rank in the top 10* so only keywords that the competitor ranks in the top 10 for appear [L0382, 2017].
4. Run the search and export the keyword list [L0382, 2017].
5. Vet each keyword with the RAW method (see Vetting keywords) [L0382, 2017].

- The Content Gap method produces a long list of article ideas that audiences already search for, because the keywords come from competitor rankings. Every keyword must still be vetted before writing an article for it [L0382, 2017].
- Keywords from competitor rankings are often irrelevant to your audience, such as brand names or product-specific terms of a competitor. Use judgement to remove keywords your buyers would not care about [L0382, 2017].

#### Ahrefs Keywords Explorer

- Research starts in Keywords Explorer. Its keyword ideas tabs are All keyword ideas, Phrase match, Having same terms, Also rank for, Search suggestions, Newly discovered and Questions [L0878, 2021-22].
- Filters include KD, Volume, Word count, SERP features, Include, Exclude and More filters [L0878, 2021-22].
- The Questions tab is an Ahrefs feature that other tools do not offer in the same way [L0878, 2021-22].
- The SERP overview lists the top-ranking pages with Backlinks, Domains and Traffic columns. Clicking a page's backlink count opens that page's backlink report, which shows the linking sites to contact for outreach [L0878, 2021-22].
- The Parent topic field shows the broader topic a keyword belongs to, with its own search volume and the #1 ranking page for that topic. Use it to check whether a niche term is broad enough to attract links, and to compare it with narrower variations [L0878, 2021-22].

The GOOGLE SERP list in Ubersuggest's Keyword Overview shows the domains currently ranking in the top 10 for a keyword [L1486, 2021-22].

### Vetting keywords

#### RAW vetting procedure

1. **Relevance:** Does your audience care about this keyword? Remove keywords specific to a competitor or that do not match what your buyers want. Keep keywords that answer a real question buyers have about your category [L0382, 2017].
2. **Achievability:** In Ahrefs, use Site Explorer to find the keywords your site already ranks for, add filters, export, and paste the list into Keyword Explorer to see keyword difficulty. Use the difficulty of those keywords as a benchmark for what you can rank for [L0382, 2017].
3. **Worth it:** Keep keywords with cost per click of at least $1 and a monthly search volume that matches your site size [L0382, 2017].

#### Difficulty

| Difficulty signal | Meaning | Applies when | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| KD below 20 in Ahrefs | Target for a store building its content presence | Store is building content | [L0382, 2017] |
| Higher difficulty | Dominated by large brands such as big media companies | Competing with major brands | [L0382, 2017] |
| About 45 | Moderately hard; needs backlinks to reach the top of page one | Planning a content page | [L0758, 2021-22] |
| Very low difficulty with some volume | Good target for a content page, relatively easy to rank for | Choosing content targets | [L0758, 2021-22] |

- Keyword difficulty of about 45 was the example for *surfboards for beginners*. It is not easy but not extremely hard to rank, and page one still requires backlinks, so it is a good candidate for an ultimate guide content page [L0877, 2021-22].
- Very low difficulty with some volume, such as *surfboard volume calculator*, is a good target for a content page [L0758, 2021-22].
- Work on low-competition keywords with good search volume and a high CPC first, because they are the easiest to rank for. High-competition keywords are still worth creating content for later [L1120, 2024+].

#### Cost per click

- Choose keywords with a cost per click of at least $1 in Ahrefs. A higher cost per click means advertisers consider the keyword profitable, so the traffic may be worth more [L0382, 2017].
- The $1 CPC rule is the worth-it check in the RAW method [L0382, 2017].
- Average CPC is also available in Ubersuggest and is useful for paid ads [L0758, 2021-22].

#### Search volume

| Rule | Value | Applies when | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum monthly volume for a small site | 500 average monthly searches | Small site | [L0382, 2017] |
| Minimum monthly volume for a larger site | 1,000-2,000 average monthly searches per keyword | Larger site | [L0382, 2017] |
| Common cut-off in the master sheet | 100 or more searches per month; below that is usually not worth including | Building the target list | [L0877, 2021-22] |
| Low-volume keywords with high purchase intent | 1,000 or less monthly searches still worthwhile | High purchase intent | [L0278, 2025] |

- Keywords with lower monthly search volume (1,000 or less) are still worthwhile if they show high purchase intent. One thousand monthly searches of qualified traffic can yield significant sales over time [L0278, 2025].
- A store with a standing desk height calculator keyword showed 260 monthly searches, low competition and a $5.17 CPC, and it was a breakout term in Google Trends. Because it is evergreen, it was changed from a planned blog post to a permanent page [L1120, 2024+].

#### Validating a category keyword

1. Enter a candidate keyword such as *non-toxic running shorts* into Ahrefs or SEMrush [L1389, 2025-26].
2. Check how many people search for it per month [L1389, 2025-26].
3. Create the category if monthly searches exist, and skip it if they do not [L1389, 2025-26].
4. Repeat the check monthly for hybrid keywords, since their volume can change [L1389, 2025-26].

### Long-tail versus broad terms

- Long-tail keywords reportedly make up over 70% of online searches, according to a Moz figure [L0462, 2021-22].
- Longer queries, such as a product with size, colour and price, show a later buying stage than short broad queries [L0462, 2021-22].
- Broad, generic niche keywords, for example the name of the whole niche, bring a lot of traffic but few sales. Ranking for them takes a lot of time and money, so a dropshipping store should not aim for them first [L0594, 2020-26].
- Long-tail keywords are the better organic target. They are easier to rank for and their searchers have buying intent, while broad keywords attract people who are only curious [L0594, 2020-26].
- Long-tail keywords include collection names (store subcategories), brand names the store sells, and combinations of product names, brand names and SKU numbers [L0594, 2020-26].
- A new store can start ranking sooner for search terms that name specific supplier brands or exact SKU numbers, because those searches are narrow and specific [L0586, 2020-26].
- For a single-brand store, most search volume goes to the brand name, while product-name and niche keywords often have little volume. Long-tail questions and categories are where extra traffic can come from [L0949, 2021-22].
- Term variations and word-order differences may carry the same intent. Group them rather than treating each as a separate target [L0758, 2021-22; L0877, 2021-22].

### Search intent

- Choose informational long-tail queries such as how-to questions, setup questions and questions about choosing a product, since each one can become an article. For a podcast gear store, examples are *how to start a podcast*, *how to get a show listed on Apple Podcasts* and *which hosting service to choose* [L0380, 2014].
- Comparison keywords such as *X versus Y* and definition keywords such as *what does X mean* passed the relevance and value checks for a dropshipping store. These queries match buyers who are comparing options or learning category terms [L0382, 2017].
- Keywords for brands are not targeted; keywords with the prefix *best* can be used on collection pages [L1120, 2024+].

### Question-based research

- Research the questions buyers ask before they buy, using a question-based keyword tool such as Answer the Public. Write content that answers each question and cover each stage of the buying journey, including material, colour and feature variants [L0949, 2021-22].
- The Questions tab in Ahrefs Keywords Explorer provides question keywords [L0878, 2021-22].
- Informational questions are the content targets for how-to, setup and product-choice pages [L0380, 2014].

### Year-stamped and trend keywords

- Search queries that include a year usually lag the current calendar year by about one year, so people may still type the previous year's version of a term. Before writing a headline or ad for a year-stamped topic, check which year the query actually uses and match it [L0385, 2021-22].

#### Google Trends procedure

1. Go to trends.google.com [L1120, 2024+].
2. Enter the niche keyword and click Explore [L1120, 2024+].
3. Set the country to the country where you do business [L1120, 2024+].
4. Set the time filter to Past 90 days to see what is relevant now [L1120, 2024+].
5. Scroll down to see the trending searches and note any breakout terms that are doing better than ever before [L1120, 2024+].

- Breakout search terms in Google Trends show a rise before Keyword Planner volumes catch up. Keyword Planner numbers for these terms can lag, so content can pay off early even when the planner shows low volume [L1120, 2024+].
- Do not rely only on Keyword Planner for trending topics. Missing a breakout term while waiting for planner numbers means arriving late to the topic [L1120, 2024+].

#### Verifying a breakout term in Keyword Planner

1. Copy the trending keyword from Google Trends [L1120, 2024+].
2. Open Keyword Planner and go back to keyword ideas [L1120, 2024+].
3. Search the copied keyword to get its average monthly searches, competition rank and high-range top-of-page bid [L1120, 2024+].
4. Record these values in the worksheet even when search volume is low, because Keyword Planner numbers lag behind Google Trends [L1120, 2024+].

### Search Console query data

#### Finding keywords to optimise

1. Open Performance and scroll down to the Queries report [L1271, 2020-26].
2. Sort queries by impressions to see which searches show your store most often [L1271, 2020-26].
3. Click a relevant query, for example a product name your store actually sells. This filters the graph to that query over the last three months [L1271, 2020-26].
4. Open the Pages tab to see which page ranked for that query and compare its impressions, average position and clicks [L1271, 2020-26].

- Search Console shows which queries get clicks, which Google Analytics hides as *not provided*. Connect it with the same Google account used for Analytics [L0468, 2020].
- High-impression queries that do not match what the store sells, such as generic phrases about dropshipping or a category, bring impressions but almost no clicks. Optimise pages for queries that match the actual product, not for broad industry terms [L1271, 2020-26].

### Keyword worksheet

- The keyword research worksheet exists as a Google Sheets file, which can be copied into your own Google Drive, and as an Excel download for Excel, Apple Numbers or other spreadsheet software [L1120, 2024+].
- Make your own copy before adding keywords so the original template stays unchanged [L1120, 2024+].
- Organic keyword research finds topics that people in your niche already search for on Google or Bing, so you can create content that can rank for those terms. Keywords are stored in a tracking sheet, and that sheet becomes the basis for content planning [L1120, 2024+].
