## Duplicate and Thin Content, Canonicals and URLs

### Duplicate and Thin Content: What Counts

- Duplicate content means substantial blocks of text that either fully match other content or are appreciably similar, whether within one domain or across domains. Google does not define how similar counts as appreciably similar, so minor word swaps on copied text will not make it look unique. [L0556, 2021-22]
- Changing a few words in a competitor's text, such as swapping a single word or sentence, does not make the copy original and can cause conflicts with the original owner. Use competitor pages only to learn the structure, never the wording. [L0467, 2019]
- Multiple URLs that show the same content waste crawl time and add no SEO value, even though duplicates do not directly reduce the crawl rate. Shopify stores are a known source of such duplicate URLs and need checking. [L0397, 2021-22]
- Google usually does not index duplicate pages but still crawls them, which spends crawl budget and dilutes the perceived quality of the whole site. Duplicate content therefore mostly results in lost ranking opportunities rather than a penalty. [L0556, 2021-22]
- A manual action penalty for duplicate content is rare and typically only applies when the copying looks deliberately manipulative, such as scraping a whole competitor site to take its rankings. Accidental duplication within a site is very unlikely to lead to a penalty. [L0556, 2021-22]
- Search engines do not penalize duplicate product descriptions but rank them less willingly, which hurts organic traffic over time. Organic ranking matters later, not during the first paid-ads launch. [L1163, 2021-22]
- Identical product descriptions across many products are common in dropshipping and make the site look weak to buyers. Rewrite them as unique descriptions by the six-month mark so the change has time to settle. [L1468, 2014]
- On a large site, duplicate and thin content on 20 to 30% of pages can noticeably hold back overall SEO. Auditing and fixing these areas is therefore often one of the fastest SEO improvements available. [L0556, 2021-22]
- A large shop with about 1,000 product pages copied supplier descriptions that also appear on other websites and put real effort into only about 100 of them. Google still crawls all 1,000 pages and reads the site as 900 pages without value and 100 with value, so the whole site ranks lower and receives fewer visitors. Unique text on the pages that matter most is the fix, instead of reusing shared supplier copy across the catalogue. [L0556, 2021-22]

### Thin Content: Definition and Effects

- Thin content means pages with little or no text content that search engines consider to have little value to users, which can drag down the ranking performance of the whole site. Because Google can choose from an enormous number of pages, such pages are unlikely to rank even if they are indexed. [L1372, 2021-22]
- Thin pages dilute how Google rates the overall quality of a site, waste crawl budget by pulling crawler attention away from better pages, and forfeit a keyword opportunity that every page could otherwise target. Adding real content to nearly any page tends to improve customer experience, conversion rate and retention. [L1372, 2021-22]
- A site should have fewer low-value pages in the index rather than more. Past a tipping point, too many indexed pages, especially low-quality, keyword-confused or never-visited ones, weaken overall SEO. Pages that attract no visitors and serve no purpose should be removed from the index. [L1372, 2021-22]
- Content on a store is only worth adding if it is valuable to the reader. Thin content added only for search engines does not help results. [L1092, 2022]
- Thin content is not a concern for very small stores with only a few pages, such as a homepage, one product page and an about page. The audit matters most for sites with hundreds or thousands of pages, such as drop shippers with large product catalogues or resellers of other brands' products. [L1372, 2021-22]
- Blog posts are normally unique and well over 300 words, so they usually do not need to be included in a thin or duplicate content audit. A blog post of 300 words or less or copied content signals a problem. [L1372, 2021-22]

### Word and Percentage Targets

These targets come from different sources and apply in different situations. Use the one that matches the page you are checking.

| Target | Applies to | Source |
|---|---|---|
| At least 300 words of unique text | Pages you want indexed and ranking; the minimum, and more is better | [L1372, 2021-22] |
| About 300 words as a guideline only | Duplicate-content pages, which need most of their text to be unique and original and of good quality | [L1372, 2021-22] |
| At least 75% unique text | Every page; a personal working target, not a figure documented by Google. Unavoidable factual specifications can stay identical, so extra unique content must make up the rest of the page | [L0556, 2021-22] |
| Less than half of SEO-relevant text duplicated | Step in the tab-replacement procedure on product pages | [L0556, 2021-22] |
| About 2% or less duplicated text | Product pages after the boilerplate pattern is applied | [L0556, 2021-22] |

- A product page with unique text of 300 words or more is neither duplicate nor thin and can be removed from the thin-content list. [L1372, 2021-22]
- Shared content that is minimised as far as possible still cannot be removed entirely, because it often supports conversion. [L1191, 2021-22]

### Original Content and Copying Rules

- You may copy a competitor's general layout, because most store layouts are similar, but never copy their written content or their logo and custom graphics. All product and store text must be original. [L0356, 2019]
- Do not republish other sites' articles on your store, even with credit and a link. Write unique content yourself or hire a freelance writer. [L0468, 2020]
- Do not paste text copied from other websites, such as supplier descriptions or blog posts, directly onto your pages. For data from another source in a blog post, summarize it in your own words and link out to the original source, which is acceptable. [L0556, 2021-22]
- Your website should resolve under only one domain version, either www or non-www, and every other version should redirect to it. [L0575, 2014]

### Thin and Duplicate Content Audit: Procedure

- A page audit identifies problem pages before any change is made. [L1372, 2021-22]
  1. Identify all pages with duplicate content. [L1372, 2021-22]
  2. Identify all pages with thin content. [L1372, 2021-22]
  3. Identify all pages with 0 views in the past 6 to 12 months. [L1372, 2021-22]
  4. Identify orphan pages that are not part of the site structure. [L1372, 2021-22]
  5. Decide per page whether to add content, delete with redirect, or noindex. [L1372, 2021-22]
- Order of options for fixing or avoiding thin content:
  1. Run a page audit to find the problem pages. [L1372, 2021-22]
  2. Add at least 300 words of unique text to pages worth keeping. [L1372, 2021-22]
  3. If a page cannot get content, delete it and set a 301 redirect to a relevant live page, for example the homepage. [L1372, 2021-22]
  4. If a page cannot be deleted but is thin, add a noindex tag. [L1372, 2021-22]
- Full audit procedure for product and collection pages:
  1. Start with product descriptions: if a product page has unique text of 300 words or more, it is neither duplicate nor thin and can be removed from the list. [L1372, 2021-22]
  2. Check collection pages: if a collection page has no unique content, add it to the thin content list. [L1372, 2021-22]
  3. Skip blog posts unless they are 300 words or less or copied; those are a separate problem to fix. [L1372, 2021-22]
  4. If product descriptions are duplicated across the store, add all of those product pages to the list. [L1372, 2021-22]
  5. For each remaining page, record the page title and URL, then mark duplicate content yes or no and thin content yes or no. [L1372, 2021-22]
  6. For each problem page, decide Fix content? If no, decide Delete? and enter the redirect target if deleting; if neither applies, mark No-Index. [L1372, 2021-22]
  7. Finish the full map before changing any page, then hand the work to a VA or writer if the site is large. [L1372, 2021-22]

### Audit Tracking and Tools

- Before using a shared audit spreadsheet, go to File > Make a copy, choose a folder in your own Google Drive and leave the sharing and comments options unchecked. Work only in your copy and never in the original. [L1372, 2021-22]
- Audit tracker workflow:
  1. Make a copy of the shared spreadsheet into your own Google Drive rather than editing the original. [L1372, 2021-22]
  2. For each relevant page, record page title and page URL. [L1372, 2021-22]
  3. Mark whether it has duplicate content and whether it has thin content (under 300 words). [L1372, 2021-22]
  4. Mark Fix Content yes or no; if no, mark Delete yes or no. [L1372, 2021-22]
  5. If deleting, enter the redirect target URL. [L1372, 2021-22]
  6. If neither, mark No-Index. [L1372, 2021-22]
- SEO tools such as Ahrefs, Raven Tools and Semrush each include a site audit feature that crawls a site and flags duplicate and thin content. Raven's site audit was described as the most accurate for finding these issues, though all such crawlers can make errors, so a manual check is still recommended. [L1372, 2021-22]
- Fixing a large site is a gradual process. A mapped spreadsheet can be handed to a VA or writer for deletions, redirects, noindex entries and content tasks, with clear instructions for each. Work steadily until all problems are fixed. [L1372, 2021-22]
- Pages with zero views over the past 6 to 12 months that are not going to change should be deleted and 301-redirected to a relevant page with value. [L1372, 2021-22]
- A site lost 40% of its search traffic overnight after a Google update targeting thin, low-value listings, which shows how a single algorithm update can remove rankings for many low-quality pages at once. [L1372, 2021-22]

### Boilerplate Tabs and Shared Text

- Boilerplate content is static text that loads on many pages, such as shipping, returns or guarantee tabs. It can still lower the value of each page even if it appears on only five pages. Shared text that makes up a large share of a page's SEO-relevant text is the main source of duplication on many shops. [L0556, 2021-22]
- Example from a large sauna shop: about 500+ product pages each carried the same three tabs, with a shipping tab of at least 200 words, a second block of roughly 150 words, and product texts that were mostly identical. About 50% of the SEO-relevant text on the page was duplicated, and about 90% of site pages carried the same tab content. [L0556, 2021-22]
- Boilerplate should be minimised as far as possible, because it can crowd out the unique content of the page. [L1191, 2021-22]
- When product descriptions are mostly images, the page has very little unique crawlable text, so shared tabs and boilerplate can make up most of the visible text. Even a few hundred words of shared content can then dominate the SEO value of the page. [L0556, 2021-22]
- Tab replacement procedure:
  1. Open a product page and check whether tabs or sections with long text repeat across other product pages. [L0556, 2021-22]
  2. Count how much of the SEO-relevant text on the page is identical to text on other pages; aim for less than half duplicated. [L0556, 2021-22]
  3. Replace long shared blocks with a short statement of the main point, such as free shipping or a 30-day return policy. [L0556, 2021-22]
  4. Link the short statement to a full policy page that opens in a new window. [L0556, 2021-22]
  5. Review the site and, for each large duplicate chunk, shorten it while keeping a findable link to its own separate page. [L0556, 2021-22]
- Replace shared boilerplate with a two-line summary that answers the customer's main question, then add a call-to-action link to the full policy page. This keeps the important information visible and keeps the duplicated text to about 2% or less of the page text on a product page. [L0556, 2021-22]
  - Shipping summary example: `Free shipping on all orders within the country, see our full shipping policy.` [L0556, 2021-22]
  - Returns summary example: `30-day free returns, read our full returns policy.` [L0556, 2021-22]
  - Both lines link to their policy pages, which open in a new window. [L0556, 2021-22]
- Moving a long shipping or policy section off the product page can help the page rank for high-value model searches. Customers who need the information must still find it easily from the product page. [L0556, 2021-22]

### Shopify Duplicate URLs and Indexing

- Shopify can create several URLs for the same product, such as `/products/product-id` and `/collections/products/product-id`. Both show the same page and count as duplicate content. A canonical tag reduces the problem, but the two versions still exist. [L0556, 2021-22]
- Shopify generates several URLs that display the same page, which creates duplicate content. Shopify's behaviour has some advantages, but it can cause an SEO problem that needs to be managed. [L1414, 2021-22]
- The preferred base URL of a Shopify product is the root domain followed by `/products/` and the product handle, for example `domain/products/product-handle`. This is the URL that should always be indexed. [L1414, 2021-22]
- A product reached through a collection page gets a different URL that includes `/collections/collection-name/products/product-handle`. Google treats this as a separate page even though the content is identical. [L1414, 2021-22]
- On a Shopify store connected to Google Search Console, it is normal to see many pages listed as known but not indexed. Shopify sets this up by default so that product variants, collection-based product URLs and paginated pages do not get indexed as duplicates. [L0373, 2024+]
- On Shopify stores, many pages show as not indexed, usually product URLs with collection paths or parameter variants, while the clean product URL is indexed. This is expected, because indexing the variants would split ranking across several URLs for the same product. (Older advice? No: 2025.) [L0807, 2025]
- The fix for duplicates caused by Shopify navigation variants is generally not required when the main collection URL is the indexed page. [L1283, 2023]
- A drop in indexed pages can be caused by duplicate versions of the same page, such as URLs with UTM parameters being counted as separate pages. To check, review which sitemap pages are actually indexed and look for errors on the ones that are not. [L0835, 2023]

### Canonical Tags, Noindex and Hidden Products

- A canonical tag is HTML code that marks one URL as the original version of a page and suggests that search engines do not index the other versions. Shopify uses canonical tags, but they do not guarantee which version Google indexes. [L1414, 2021-22]
- Canonical tags for product variants on platforms other than Shopify are needed, because each colour or size variant can create its own URL with the same content. Shopify adds these canonical tags for product variants automatically. [L0556, 2021-22]
- On platforms other than Shopify, a colour or size variant often gets its own URL with a modifier added to the end, while the description, measurements and price stay identical and only one or two words change. These variant URLs count as duplicate pages unless a canonical tag points them to the main product, so check platforms such as Magento, WooCommerce or BigCommerce for this setup. [L0556, 2021-22]
- Use a noindex tag for pages that must exist but should not be indexed. Avoid serving the same content on dynamically generated URLs. [L0556, 2021-22]
- If Google indexes a duplicate URL instead of the intended one, backlinks pointing to the non-indexed URL lose much of their value. The aim is to serve content consistently on a single URL. [L1414, 2021-22]
- Shopify creates a hidden collection URL for every product tag, for example `/collections/collection-name/tag-name`. These pages cannot be edited in the Shopify admin, have very limited value, and can still be indexed, which creates a potential problem. [L1414, 2021-22]
- The supplied code also sets noindex on tag-generated collection pages. It does not delete them, but it keeps them out of the index automatically. [L1414, 2021-22]
- To organise products by type, create a collection page, add content to it and include it in the site structure, rather than relying on product tags. [L1414, 2021-22]
- If a hidden product stays hidden long enough, Google can drop it from the index. When it is reactivated, it must be re-indexed from scratch, and any ranking and traffic it had can be lost. [L1414, 2021-22]
- Canonical and noindex setup procedure:
  1. Insert the supplied canonical code into `theme.liquid` in the Shopify theme. [L1414, 2021-22]
  2. Avoid using product tags as much as possible. [L1414, 2021-22]
  3. Instead of hiding products, set them to zero stock or delete them and redirect the URL. [L1414, 2021-22]

### Multi-Storefront and Localised Setups

- Check canonicals and hreflang only if the store runs localised storefronts, such as language or market subfolders. If localised URLs compete with the main pages in search results, that can suppress rankings across the site. If the store has no such subfolders, skip this check. [L1269, 2025-26]
- The canonical and hreflang check for multi-storefront setups is a one-time audit of roughly 15 minutes. It should confirm that localised URLs do not compete with the main pages, and it should not turn into an ongoing cleanup project. [L1269, 2025-26]

### URL Handles: Format and Setup

- The product URL handle should follow the pattern brand name, product name, then SKU or GTIN, written in lowercase with dashes between words and no spaces. Add the SKU or GTIN after a final dash if the supplier provides one. [L1190, 2020-26]
- Add a SKU or GTIN to the end of the product URL handle only when the supplier actually provides one. Check the product details or specifications first. If no identifier exists, use just the brand name and product name. [L1190, 2020-26]
- Put the target keyword into the URL handle, written in lowercase with hyphens between words and no spaces, for example `customizing-your-standing-desk`. The URL handle is the first on-page element to check for the keyword. [L1321, 2020-26]
- In Shopify, the product URL follows the pattern `domain/products/[handle]`, and the handle is edited in the URL and handle field of the Search engine listing preview. Changing the handle changes the live URL, so the focus keyword belongs in the handle only when the page is new or the change is deliberate. [L1026, 2021-22]
- Setting path: Product page > Search engine listing > URL handle. After editing the URL, a line appears below the field offering to create a URL redirect from the old URL to the new one. Keep that option checked, then click Save so old links forward to the new page. [L1190, 2020-26]
- Shopify generates the product URL by copying the full product title by default, so long titles with barcodes or extra words produce long URLs. Shorten the URL slug manually before publishing. [L1414, 2021-22]

### URL Handle Rules for New and Existing Pages

- Keep the URL short. Google prefers short URLs, so product URLs should not be overly descriptive. There is no fixed word count; keep it as short as possible and avoid stacking descriptive words. [L1414, 2021-22]
- The page URL should contain the focus keyword and be short, because long URLs get truncated in search results. [L1191, 2021-22]
- Include the page's focus keyword in the URL. For product pages this is usually the product title, and for blog posts it is the target keyword. [L1414, 2021-22]
- A URL does not need to match the page title exactly. It needs to be short and contain the focus keyword. [L1414, 2021-22]
- A URL is the unique identifier Google uses to recognise a web page and its content as one entity, so each page should have a correctly formatted URL tied to content that is as unique as possible. [L1414, 2021-22]
- Set the URL with the focus keyword when creating a new page. For pages already ranking well, do not change the URL, since a change can cause temporary traffic losses without a clear gain. [L1191, 2021-22]
- Change URL handles only for new products or stores with weak organic traffic. Stores that already rank well organically should leave their URLs unchanged. [L1190, 2020-26]
- Do not rewrite existing URLs just to add keywords. Changing URLs has consequences, so decide carefully before changing them. Apply the keyword-in-URL rule primarily to new pages. [L1026, 2021-22]
- Do not change the URL and handle of a page after it is created, because changing it damages SEO. Leave these fields alone once the page is saved. [L1264, 2021-22]
- New page creation procedure:
  1. When creating a new page, edit the URL before publishing it. [L1414, 2021-22]
  2. Check that the URL is short and contains the focus keyword. [L1414, 2021-22]
  3. Only then publish the page. [L1414, 2021-22]

### Changing URLs and Redirects

- After a page is published, changing its URL creates a redirect. Google treats the result as a new page that is not indexed immediately, even if only one letter changed. URLs should therefore be right before publishing. [L1414, 2021-22]
- Changing a URL handle requires a redirect from the old URL to the new one, so existing links and search results keep working. Shopify's URL handle change can create this redirect automatically; confirm it was created. [L1271, 2020-26]
- A 301 redirect passes SEO value to the new URL. A 302 redirect is temporary and is not meant as a permanent move. [L1414, 2021-22]
- Redirects keep visitors who bookmarked or linked to an old URL on the site, which can salvage a sale. Without a redirect they land on an error page and usually leave. [L1414, 2021-22]
- Deleting a page does not immediately remove it from Google's index, and the old URL can keep returning a 404 for a while, which also hurts bookmarked or linked visits. A 301 redirect, which is permanent, to a page with value prevents this. [L1372, 2021-22]
- Deleting pages without setting a 301 redirect leaves visitors and Google with error pages. Deleting is safe when paired with a redirect to a relevant page. [L1372, 2021-22]
- Avoid redirects unless they are absolutely necessary. Large numbers of redirects use up crawl budget and make Google's crawling of the site inefficient. [L1414, 2021-22]
- Google does not value links that point to redirects as highly, so a redirected backlink loses some of its value. The better practice is to ask the sites linking to the old URL to update the link to the new URL. [L1414, 2021-22]
- Before changing or deleting a URL, check the following:
  1. Check the page's index status. [L1414, 2021-22]
  2. Check its organic traffic, since a change and redirect will probably reduce it at least temporarily. [L1414, 2021-22]
  3. Check its backlinks in a tool such as Ubersuggest or Ahrefs before deleting or changing the URL. [L1414, 2021-22]
  4. If the page is deleted or changed, set a 301 redirect to another relevant page. [L1414, 2021-22]
- Where to set it in Shopify: create 301 redirects by hand in the navigation settings (Online Store > Navigation), no app needed. For many old URLs at once, install a bulk redirect app. When you change a product's URL handle, keep the 'Create redirect' checkbox selected.
