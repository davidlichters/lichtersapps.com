## Content Strategy, Formats and Planning

### Role of Content in the Funnel

- Without content, a store depends on paid channels such as Google ads, paid social and affiliate marketing for traffic. Content adds organic traffic that does not depend on ad spend alone. [L0500, 2021-22]
- Search engine optimisation cannot be done effectively without content, because product pages give search engines too little material to rank for informational searches. [L0500, 2021-22]
- Content has four jobs: answering customer questions and solving problems, gaining attention and engagement, generating traffic from search engines and YouTube, and building backlinks to the site. Plan each piece for at least one of these jobs. [L0386, 2021-22]
- Product pages are commercial, while content is educational and entertaining. Product pages fit the product-aware and ninja-aware stages. Buyers at the problem-aware and solution-aware stages need educational content, such as a guide that explains what to look for and recommends specific products. [L1117, 2021-22]
- A store made only of collection pages and product pages can answer questions only at the product-aware and ninja-aware stages. Content lets a store serve the whole awareness pyramid more easily than any other marketing mechanism. [L1117, 2021-22]
- Competitors who reach people in the early awareness stages first gain attention, engagement and trust, and lock those buyers into their own sales funnel. A brand that appears only at the product-aware stage must win attention away from sources the customer already trusts. [L1117, 2021-22]
- To be present everywhere in the buyer's journey, a brand must engage customers with both content and products. Product pages belong where the buyer already knows what they want, but they should not be the entire journey. [L1117, 2021-22]
- Educational content builds know, like and trust and helps buyers decide intelligently. Show both the positives and the negatives of a product, because not every visitor is a potential customer. [L0382, 2017]
- Pain-point content can rank in search engines and bring traffic, and it guides people through their customer journey. This supports conversion and authority, so it is a strong starting point. [L0386, 2021-22]
- A buyer's guide raises the authority of a store, educates customers and links to products that fit what the reader wants. It can be offered as a downloadable PDF with a clear format and relevant product links. [L0837, 2025]
- Buyer's guides, content on the store, and content co-created with suppliers help organic traffic and build the store's content base. [L0801, 2021]
- Publish unique long-form content as video on YouTube or as articles on a blog, and use social platforms to link people there. These platforms are consumption-focused, so viewers who stay are highly qualified. [L0487, 2018]
- Written blog content is a worthwhile format on its own, because readers like to read and search engines favour written content. It can also be combined with video. [L0462, 2021-22]
- Content is one part of a connected marketing system, not a standalone task. The same pieces feed email flows, SEO, link building and retargeting. Automated email sequences can use site content in their first emails. [L0381, 2021-22]
- Content pieces published on the store's own website are later reused for link building and SEO. Plan each piece with a place on the site, not only for social channels. [L0381, 2021-22]
- Produce content from a researched, documented plan made in advance, not from topics picked at random. Random content usually performs poorly, and the failure is then wrongly blamed on content marketing. [L0386, 2021-22]
- Content delivers its value when it is produced and maintained on an ongoing basis. Treating it as a one-off project limits the results it can bring. [L0500, 2021-22]
- Most e-commerce stores have no regular content production system when they start. A store without one should build it from the beginning. A store already publishing content should first check whether the content fits the market, then promote it. [L0500, 2021-22]

### When to Start Content

- Start blog content marketing once the store has traffic, so you know which topics attract visitors. Content made for the blog can also be reused on social media. [L0835, 2023]
- Buyer's guides and content are most useful after the store has traffic, because the data then shows which products visitors actually want. [L0855, 2023]
- Start a done-for-you content package only after the store is complete and has traffic data, so you know which products visitors view. Spending on content before there is data is wasted. [L0804, 2023]
- Done-for-you content packages add long-term value through unique content and organic traffic, but they do not produce early sales. If a store still has open site bugs or unprofitable ads, fix the site and make the paid traffic profitable first. [L0801, 2021]
- On a new affiliate site, publish three to five blog posts before buying traffic. The posts show visitors that you know the topic and give you something to link to when you share the site elsewhere. [L0514, 2015]
- Sending traffic to a new affiliate site with no posts leaves you nothing to link to when you use free methods such as forum replies or YouTube comments. Publish a few posts before you send people there. [L0514, 2015]
- If there are no competitor blogs in your exact niche, use the closest related content, such as blog posts from related products or from the buyer's own interests, as a starting reference for ideas. [L0804, 2023]
- Do not spend time deciding the perfect order of content ideas. Pick any idea and start producing, because the cost of waiting is higher than the cost of a suboptimal first choice. Research can start immediately and the plan can be adjusted later. [L0381, 2021-22]
- Trying to fully prioritise content ideas before starting wastes time that could be spent producing content. [L0381, 2021-22]

### Content Types and Formats

#### Choosing the format

- Use one of three content formats: written only, video only, or a combination of both. Pick the format before planning or producing anything. [L0383, 2021-22]
- Suitable content formats for keyword topics include buyer's guides, how-to articles, entertainment or list posts, industry announcements and new product type alerts. Choose the format to match the search intent of the keyword. [L1120, 2024+]
- A search term does not depend on the format of the page that targets it. The same keyword can be targeted with a video, a blog post or another written format, so the format choice does not change the keyword assignment. [L0877, 2021-22]
- Mix the content themes in your plan. Problem-solving pieces include how-tos, listicles, ultimate guides and product comparisons. Entertaining pieces include best-of lists, breaking news and controversial takes. [L0384, 2021-22]
- Mixing text, images and video suits readers who prefer different formats. Some want the reasons in text, some want photos of real customers with the products, and some want a video that repeats the text. [L0467, 2019]
- Every blog post should include three elements: text, images and a video, with the images and video placed within the text. Videos should come from the suppliers or the store owner. [L0467, 2019]
- Extensive content libraries such as comparisons, how-tos and buying guides are a strong SEO strategy and can drive a large part of a competitor's traffic. Note the content types and count them. [L0893, 2021-22]

#### Type reference

Recommended length and purpose by content type:

| Content Type | Recommended Length | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Product release (new item announcement with photos) | ~500 words | Announce inventory and drive immediate interest |
| How-to guides, repairs, tutorials, tips, user guides | 1,000-2,000 words | Build authority and rank for informational keywords |
| Competitor comparisons | 1,000+ words | Capture 'best of' searches |
| Product reviews | 1,000+ words | Rank for specific product queries |
| News and announcements | 500+ words | Show fresh inventory and promotions |
| Testimonials/customer stories | 500-1,000 words | Build trust and provide social proof |

[L0226, 2017]

- A listicle can work as top-of-funnel content for a shop that sells related products. A surf shop publishing a top 10 list of local surf spots attracts searchers who are not ready to buy. Product pages remain the commercial targets, and the listicle serves to attract the audience. [L0662, 2021-22]
- Best-of pages and listicles place your own product first, so they only work if the product is genuinely the best option in its category. Build the product quality first, then the pages. [L0768, 2021-22]
- Brand versus brand comparison pages can either compare your lesser-known brand with a well-known brand, or compare two well-known brands and recommend yours at the bottom of the page. [L0768, 2021-22]
- News-jacking content from a source article, such as a medical article:
  1. Find the article.
  2. Quote the relevant parts.
  3. Link to the original source and mention it.
  4. Add your business commentary.
  Do not use content behind a paywall. [L0804, 2023]

### Length and Depth

- Use the type-based lengths in the table above as the starting point for each piece. [L0226, 2017]
- Blog post length is 300 to 1,000 words depending on the topic. Do not inflate with filler; write what is necessary to explain the topic fully. Blog posting every other week is effective. [L0272, 2022]
- Aim for quality over quantity in each article, because that is what currently ranks. Google increasingly favours authoritative, long pages that cover several related keywords rather than thin pages for one keyword. [L0382, 2017]
- To rank for a competitive search term, a new piece should cover its subject in more depth than the pages already on page one. Google tends to favour longer-form content, so a thin article is unlikely to outrank existing pages. [L0386, 2021-22]
- When judging a competitor's content, check its volume and quality separately. Long, in-depth pages with images and links to products suggest higher quality. Short pages that answer simple questions suggest lower quality. Quality gaps reveal opportunities. [L0893, 2021-22]

### Idea Sourcing

#### Customers and their questions

- To find content ideas, work through four questions in this order:
  1. Which questions, problems and frustrations do my buyers run into that connect to what I sell?
  2. Which content subjects related to my products are already being shared on social platforms and through email?
  3. Which content already ranks in search engines for the keywords I am targeting?
  4. Which product-related content topics are other websites already linking to? [L0386, 2021-22]
- Customer service records and customer surveys show real pain points, the questions customers ask before buying, and what they struggle with or wish they knew more about. Ask existing customers directly and treat their answers as sources for early content topics. [L0386, 2021-22]
- Choose which content to create based on keyword research, using the questions real customers ask in the niche. [L1227, 2020-26]
- Keyword-based content should match the questions customers ask, so the question itself can become the post's target keyword. [L1227, 2020-26]

#### Communities and social signals

- Content research starts with what already performs in your market. Check what popular creators publish and which topics their audiences watch. Use this to model and adapt topics rather than copying content. [L0478, 2021-22]
- Content gaps are topics that large audiences in your market do not cover well. Finding them gives you content opportunities where you can serve buyers with less competition. [L0478, 2021-22]
- Shareable content can be found by looking for existing topic pieces with high social engagement or shares. Create something similar but more in-depth, so it has a good chance of getting the same engagement. [L0386, 2021-22]
- Shareable topics can be found with BuzzSumo, which shows which topics in your niche are trending by displaying Facebook engagement counts, Twitter mentions, share frequency and related articles. Use this research to align content strategy with audience interest. [L0286, 2018]
- Topics with many upvotes, likes or comments show what the niche audience engages with and make good content candidates. Reddit threads with question lists can also provide related keywords for a blog post. [L1120, 2024+]
- Content idea research on Reddit:
  1. Search your niche keyword plus Reddit on Google.
  2. Open the niche subreddit (for example the standing desks community with about 27,000 members).
  3. Look at the recent posts sorted by the number of upvotes, and note topics that could become a blog post.
  4. Click the top sort option and change the time filter to all time to see the most upvoted posts that can serve as evergreen topics.
  [L1120, 2024+]
- Example: a Reddit question about whether paint on the legs of a standing desk wears off led to a blog topic on customizing a standing desk, including which paint lasts. The question idea was added to the worksheet as a blog post keyword. [L1120, 2024+]
- Content idea research on Facebook groups:
  1. Go to Facebook and type your niche into the search bar.
  2. Click Groups.
  3. Join relevant groups and look for posts with many likes and comments.
  4. Add those topics and keywords to the worksheet.
  [L1120, 2024+]
- Example: question-style titles from competitor blogs can be used directly as blog topics in a niche such as standing desks. Examples are *how long should you stand at a standing desk* and *tips for buying an adjustable standing desk converter*. Each one should be added to the keyword worksheet as a blog post keyword. [L1120, 2024+]
- Log content ideas from communities:
  1. Join Facebook groups in your niche and note the questions people ask.
  2. Join relevant Reddit communities and collect the discussion topics that come up repeatedly.
  3. Search Google for niche topics to see what people are asking about.
  4. Log each content idea in a Google Sheet, with one column for the idea and one column for the link to the source it came from.
  [L0472, 2025]
- Example of an AI-assisted idea chain, applied to a food brand:
  1. Every Monday, scrape a relevant Reddit forum for the niche, for example r/cooking for a food brand.
  2. Use Chat2T to summarise all new posts and extract key insights.
  3. Send those insights to Claude to generate new marketing angles the brand has not yet tested.
  4. Send each angle back to Claude to write a complete UGC video script.
  5. Add all generated scripts to ClickUp for the production team.
  This chain turns raw community conversations into ready-to-produce creative briefs automatically. [L0287, 2025-26]

#### Brainstorming the first ideas

- Plan content topics six months ahead by collecting 12 content ideas as the starting point. Include any ideas already in your head, and brainstorm the rest across different content types for your market or niche. [L0384, 2021-22]

### Keyword and SERP Research

#### Keyword discovery

- Keyword and traffic tools such as Ubersuggest, Ahrefs, Semrush and Moz show, for a search term, what content already exists, its social share counts, its organic traffic and its backlink counts. Use them to judge what already exists for a topic. [L0386, 2021-22]
- Keyword research for the content plan can be done yourself or outsourced. If it has not been done yet, do it before entering the first content ideas. [L0381, 2021-22]
- Keyword pass output: the minimum output of a first keyword pass is a list of terms to target with content. [L0877, 2021-22]
- Build a keyword list from informational queries before writing. [L0380, 2014]
- Style-based keyword research for homewares, using a style name rather than a product term:
  1. Search for style-based keywords related to your products, such as an interior style name, and note monthly volume and keyword difficulty.
  2. Choose styles with usable volume and low difficulty, and estimate the number of backlinks needed to rank.
  3. Write a style guide that explains the defining characteristics of the look and how to recreate it at home.
  4. Create product-type guides for the style, such as beds, dining tables or wardrobes.
  5. Link from the guides to lists of your top products for a themed room, so the content leads down to products.
  [L0719, 2021-22]
- Example of a style-based entry point: a keyword such as *Hamptons furniture* had 2,000 to 3,000 monthly searches, keyword difficulty of 20, and an estimated need for about 20 backlinks to rank. [L0719, 2021-22]
- Content aimed at style searches sits higher in the funnel and suits homewares, because buyers start with a look or style rather than a specific product. The content can then lead down to product lists. [L0719, 2021-22]
- Keyword research workflow step 1 uses the Ahrefs Content Gap method to choose keywords, and RAW is used to vet them. [L0382, 2017]
- Evergreen keywords are optimised once on existing pages such as collections, product pages or permanent pages. Trending keywords can justify one or two new blog posts per month. Plan the two groups separately. [L1120, 2024+]
- Holiday and promotion keywords are covered in the holiday section below. [L1222, 2025+]

#### Checking what ranks

- To check what already ranks for a target keyword:
  1. Search the target keyword in Google.
  2. Note the URLs of the content pages in the top five organic positions on page one.
  3. Record those URLs in the content research document.
  4. Have the person producing the content review those pages before writing and identify where they can go further with a better piece. [L0386, 2021-22]
- If no content pages rank on page one for a keyword, do not drop the keyword. It means ranking should be easier with reasonable effort. If three or four content pages already rank, they are the competition to beat. [L0386, 2021-22]
- Finding no similar online content is not a bad sign and does not mean the content will fail. It only means the topic may be new ground with less existing competition. [L0386, 2021-22]
- Research does not have to be finished for every planned idea, but some research should always be done in advance so that part of the planned content is already researched. [L0386, 2021-22]
- Content research for a niche keyword:
  1. Search Google for your main niche keyword, for example ring light.
  2. Analyse all organic results on the first page and ignore paid ads.
  3. Note the format Google returns for the query, such as top 10 lists, best-of pages or buying guides.
  4. Build your guides, videos and reviews to match those formats and topics, instead of producing large volumes of content blindly.
  5. Over a few months, this content that mirrors what already ranks lifts a new site against established competitors. [L1274, 2018]
- Publishing more content on a fixed weekly schedule does not automatically improve rankings. SEO gains for a new site usually build slowly, often over several months. [L1274, 2018]
- A niche that is new in a country can have high-volume keywords with little competition, so ranking can be possible with content alone. Content should target those keywords before adding backlinks. [L1153, 2016]
- Case: the seller found keywords with high search volume and low competition in the adult coloring book niche, which is new in the US. The seller wrote seven blog articles of about 500 words each, targeting 10 keywords, with no backlinks, press releases or other link building. The result was page 2 rankings for nine of the 10 keywords and a page 3 ranking for one, which the seller saw as the basis for future free organic traffic. [L1153, 2016]

### Competitor Research

- Check competitor content for every new content piece: have competitors covered this topic? If they have, note it and aim to do it better. If no competitor does content marketing in the niche, this part of the research can be skipped. [L0386, 2021-22]
- The competitor research document made early in the programme asks whether each competitor does content marketing. Check that answer before searching for competitor content. Competitors with no content marketing can be skipped in content research. Those with content get noted and must be beaten. [L0386, 2021-22]
- Do not reinvent the wheel. In markets with existing content, study what works and do everything better. In markets with little content, you are breaking new ground, and organic competition is usually low. [L0386, 2021-22]
- The attention matrix lists the people, pages and organisations the audience already follows. Use it to find out whether those sources have already produced similar content. It can be shared with any writer or producer. [L0386, 2021-22]
- The attention matrix is a document that maps where a store's audience pays attention, and it can be delegated to a virtual assistant. [L0381, 2021-22]
- BuzzSumo and Social Animal work like a search engine for content that was shared on social networks. Enter a topic to see shared pieces and how others approached it. Use this for inspiration rather than copying. [L0386, 2021-22]
- BuzzSumo has a free sign-up that lists the most popular pages and articles for a niche keyword. Results are sorted by social shares and engagement on Facebook and Twitter, which shows what the audience cares about. [L0467, 2019]
- Steps to pick content topics from a competitor's blog:
  1. Use the free version of BuzzSumo to search your niche keywords.
  2. Find which of the competitor's posts have the most shares or comments.
  3. Identify the theme of the best-performing content.
  4. Create your own content on a similar but different angle, rather than rewording theirs. [L0961, 2020]
- Competitor collection pages, step by step:
  1. Search the competitor category on Google (for example *L-shaped standing desks*).
  2. Note the content types ranking (product pages, collection pages, top 10 lists).
  3. If lists rank, create your own top 10 article on the collection page.
  4. Review the top results and determine the features and topics they cover.
  5. Write your own content covering the same topics, but better.
  6. Add it below the products on the collection page.
  [L0274, 2023]
- one source research procedure for a product you sell:
  1. Select a product you are selling.
  2. Search Google for articles on that product.
  3. Read the top three to five ranking articles on the first page of results.
  4. Note the repetitive patterns, topics and information covered across those articles.
  5. Combine the best information from all of them.
  6. Write a new article in your own words covering those same topics (you can use AI to assist).
  7. Post it to your blog to acquire Google SEO ranking.
  [L0280, undated]
