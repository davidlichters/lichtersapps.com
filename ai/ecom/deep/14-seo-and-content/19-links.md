### Links

**Internal links**

- Blog posts are excellent opportunities for internal linking because of their length. Include internal links to collection pages, product pages and other content pages wherever relevant. This helps Google crawl and understand the site structure and is beneficial for users. [L0311, 2021-22]
- Aim for approximately one internal link per 300-word section. For each 300-word segment, include at least one relevant internal link to a product page, collection page or other content page. [L0311, 2021-22]
- Add internal links wherever they are relevant, with no fixed number. Link a product type to its collection page, a specific product to its product page, and a topic covered in another post to that post. Cornerstone content follows different linking rules than standard posts. [L0462, 2021-22]
- Use the one-per-300-words rule as a working guide and the no-fixed-number rule as the principle. Cornerstone content is excepted. [L0311, 2021-22; L0462, 2021-22]

**Outbound links (sources differ)**

- For a 1200-word blog post, up to 3 outbound links is appropriate. [L0311, 2021-22]
- For a 1500-word post, include at least three external links to high-quality sources that extend the topic, including resources you drew inspiration from. [L0462, 2021-22]
- The two rules agree at three links, but [L0311, 2021-22] sets a ceiling and [L0462, 2021-22] sets a floor. Use three as the working number for a 1,200 to 1,500-word post.
- Always check the domain authority of the target site. Linking to higher-authority sites is acceptable. Common uses for outbound links include crediting image sources, citing statistics and referencing videos. [L0311, 2021-22]
- Link out to other reputable sources in articles. It looks natural and shows search engines that the content is part of the wider web. An article without any external links can look suspicious. [L0382, 2017]
- External links give little direct link value, but linking to credible sources is a minor ranking factor that Google rewards. Sending readers to another site adds value for them. [L0462, 2021-22]

**Link behavior**

- Configure external links to open in a new window or tab. This keeps the reader's place on your site and their time on site. [L0311, 2021-22; L0462, 2021-22]

### Above the Fold and Layout

- Place the most important information above the fold, especially on mobile. On product pages this means the product image and product title. On blog posts it means the headline, possibly an image, and roughly the first paragraph of text. [L1099, 2021-22]
- On mobile screens, ensure the post title, featured image and opening paragraphs appear above the fold. Avoid large images at the top that push text down. Consider placing explanatory text first, followed by visuals, so readers immediately understand the topic. [L0311, 2021-22]
- Oversized images at the top that push text down should be avoided because they do not explain what the reader will learn. [L1099, 2021-22]
- Add user interaction points where possible: comment sections on blog posts, Q&A sections on product pages and review widgets, especially with video or image reviews. These add content and social proof that keep visitors on the page longer. [L1099, 2021-22]

### Tables of Contents and Long-Post Navigation

**Threshold (sources differ)**

- For posts of about 3,000 words or more, add anchor links at the top so readers can jump to each section. For a 1,500-word post this navigation is not necessary. [L0462, 2021-22]
- Long blog posts (for example around 5,000 words) should get a clickable table of contents at the top that links each heading to its position on the page. A 1,500-word post does not need one. [L1099, 2021-22]
- Both sources agree that a 1,500-word post needs no table of contents. The threshold is about 3,000 words in [L0462, 2021-22] and about 5,000 words in [L1099, 2021-22]. Use the table at about 3,000 words or more, and at least from 5,000 words.

**Build method** [L0462, 2021-22]

- On a long post of about 3,000 words or more, add a chapter list at the top where each entry links to a heading on the same page.
- Each heading gets an anchor id, and the link takes the form page-URL#heading-id, so a click scrolls straight to that section.
- On a 1,500-word post this navigation is not needed.

### Comments, Interaction and Calls to Action

- Enable an active comment section on the blog post. Encourage comments by posing a question or inviting reader thoughts. Reply to every comment, even briefly, to demonstrate that the business is real and cares about engagement. [L0311, 2021-22]
- Allow comments on blog posts, but moderate them to block spam and abusive posts. Visible replies from the business show responsiveness and build trust and social proof. Comments do not harm rankings. [L0462, 2021-22]
- Turning off comments on blog posts misses a chance to show responsiveness and build trust. Enable comments with moderation instead of disabling them. [L0462, 2021-22]
- End the blog post with a call to action using phrases such as *read more* or *learn more*. [L0311, 2021-22]
- Offer readers an opportunity to join the audience, such as an email subscription. [L0462, 2021-22]
- Use inline opt-in forms or static opt-in forms to capture leads or distribute lead magnets. Do not use pop-ups or interstitials that load within the first 30 seconds of a page visit, because pop-ups are disliked by both users and Google. [L0311, 2021-22]
- A conclusion with a soft call to action fits the standard skeleton for informational articles. [L0883, 2025-26]

### Blog Post Checklist

Blog post checklist, in this order [L0462, 2021-22]:

1. Write 1500 or more words. [L0462, 2021-22]
2. Write an engaging H1 headline that clearly explains the content and includes the primary keyword if possible, without hype. [L0462, 2021-22]
3. Add images at a ratio of at least 1 image per 500 words. [L0462, 2021-22]
4. Add plenty of sub-headlines, with H2 for main sections and H3 for sub-sections. [L0462, 2021-22]
5. Include at least 3 external links. [L0462, 2021-22]
6. Add internal links where relevant (cornerstone content is excepted). [L0462, 2021-22]
7. Keep paragraphs to no more than 4 lines. [L0462, 2021-22]
8. Use a minimum font size of 18. [L0462, 2021-22]
9. Use bulleted or numbered lists wherever you make a series of short points. [L0462, 2021-22]
10. Allow moderated comments. [L0462, 2021-22]
11. Offer readers an opportunity to join the audience, such as an email subscription. [L0462, 2021-22]
12. Run through the whole list again before publishing and before promoting the post. It takes only a few minutes. [L0462, 2021-22]

- Give the blog post checklist to freelance writers and content producers so they get the structure right from the start. This reduces the guidance you need to give on each piece. [L0462, 2021-22]

### Meta Title and Meta Description

- The meta title must be unique and include the focus keyword. The meta description must be unique. [L0311, 2021-22]
- Prompt for the meta description [L0178, undated]:

```
Compose a meta description that summarizes the [Blog Post Name] blog post for search engine display.
```

- In Smart SEO, generate meta titles and descriptions, compare them with your own, keep the stronger version and save. [L1321, 2020-26]

### Keeping Posts Live and Updating Them

- Keep every blog post live rather than rotating them, because rotation hides pages from search results and loses traffic. Display the top three posts on the homepage and list the rest in the blog section. [L0468, 2020]
- Publish at a starting pace of one piece per month, later move to one every two weeks, and then focus on improving the pages and posts that already perform well. [L1321, 2020-26]

### Publishing in Shopify

**Built-in engine and location**

- Shopify accounts come with a built-in blogging engine. Publishing blog posts with valuable content generates organic traffic from Google and Bing. [L0310, 2024+; L0311, 2021-22]
- Create a blog post in Shopify: Online store > Blog posts > Add blog post. For a page, use Online store > Pages and the same process applies. [L1321, 2020-26]

**Publishing procedure** [L1321, 2020-26]

1. Go to Online store > Blog posts > Add blog post (or Online store > Pages for a page).
2. Enter the title. An AI-suggested title can be pasted here if it fits.
3. Write the body in the editor based on the outline, either directly in the Shopify editor or in a document editor such as Microsoft Word or Google Docs, then paste the finished version into the post.
4. Add an image using a royalty-free image or an AI-generated image.
5. Scroll to the search engine listing preview section at the bottom of the editor.
6. Check the URL and handle, title and description fields (see the URL and handle steps).
7. Save the post.

**Test content**

- Do not publish test or unedited content to a live store. Leave such posts unpublished, where they stay hidden from visitors. [L1321, 2020-26]

**Full on-page SEO sequence for a Shopify page or blog post** [L1321, 2020-26]

1. Compress existing images in Smart SEO: open Image optimization, click Configure, and optimize the images in the Files and Articles sections.
2. Decide whether the content is a page (evergreen tool or calculator) or a blog post (evergreen guide or how-to).
3. Take the topic and target keyword from your keyword research worksheet and from popular threads on community sites in your niche.
4. Ask a free AI chat tool for an outline with bullet points, expand the bullets with ideas as needed, and write the final text yourself in the Shopify editor or a document editor.
5. Add a royalty-free or AI-generated image.
6. Save the post, then set the URL handle to the target keyword in lowercase with hyphens, set the page title, and delete the description Shopify pulls automatically.
7. Generate a meta description with an AI tool, check it against the actual content, paste it into the search engine listing, and save.
8. In Smart SEO, confirm that JSON-LD is enabled and valid for blogs and articles.
9. Generate meta titles and descriptions in Smart SEO, compare them with your own, keep the stronger version, and save.
10. Publish at a starting pace of one piece per month, later move to one every two weeks, and then focus on improving the pages and posts that already perform well.

**Quality first**

- Quality content is the base of every page or blog post. Once the content is good, technical steps such as optimized images, a keyword URL, a clear title, a meta description and JSON-LD give an extra edge over competitors. [L1321, 2020-26]

### Style Guide

- The website style guide applies to every piece of content produced or edited for the site, including product descriptions, policy pages, images and blog posts. Anyone working on site content must follow it, not only the designers. [L1467, undated]
- Write AI copy in active voice, without em dashes and without filler. Keep a blacklist of words typical of AI writing, remove them from every output, and apply a written style guide where one exists. [L0883, 2025-26]
- Blog posts should be at least 1000 words wherever possible. [L1467, undated]
- Paragraphs should be about 3 lines long and never more than 4 lines, to improve readability. [L1467, undated]
- Use the H1 tag only once per piece of content, for the title. Use H2 tags for secondary headings that separate sections, as many as needed. [L1467, undated]
- Use H3 tags only as subheadings inside a section marked by an H2. H4, H5 and lower heading levels should not be needed. [L1467, undated]
- Blog posts should start with a main image where possible, followed by at least one image in every section marked by an H2 tag. [L1467, undated]

### Using AI and Its Limits

**Role of AI**

- Use AI for content outlines and assistance, not copy-paste. Take AI-generated text, refine it with human writing to make it unique. Bulk AI-generated articles no longer rank. Quality weekly content beats 1,000 AI articles. (2023) [L0274, 2023]
- Do not publish AI-generated text unedited. Search engines may recognize fully AI-written content and rank it lower, so a human should review, rewrite and enrich AI output before publishing. The same editing applies to AI-written product and collection descriptions. [L1321, 2020-26]
- AI-generated content carries long-term uncertainty, since search engines may change how they handle it. Using AI drafts as a starting point and rewriting them by hand keeps the content more reliable. (2023) [L0970, 2023]
- AI tools can create a first draft of a buyer's guide, but all numbers, data and statistics must be double-checked because AI can be wrong. (2023) [L0855, 2023]
- AI copy is only as accurate as the brand data you give it. Supply the full product catalog with sizes, materials and claims. Without it, the model invents specifications that do not exist. Use placeholders for any fact you have not supplied. [L0883, 2025-26]

**Prompt for a full research-based article** [L0883, 2025-26]

```
You are an ecommerce SEO content writer. Write a blog article for the target keyword [TARGET KEYWORD], pattern-matched to the search results.

Step 1: Search Google US for the target keyword with the Firecrawl MCP and analyze the top 10 ranking results. For each result, record word count, H2 count, H3 count, use of bullet points, tables, FAQ section and conclusion, and the main entities covered.

Step 2: Calculate the median word count of the 10 results. Set the target length to the median plus or minus 100 to 200 words, and state the target length before writing.

Step 3: Match the common heading skeleton and format elements of the top results, but do not copy any text and do not imitate forum formatting.

Step 4: From forum and Q&A results (Reddit, Quora, Facebook groups, debate articles), collect real customer concerns and pain points. Use them as substance only.

Step 5: Write the article in active voice, without em dashes and without unnecessary fluff, avoiding these words: [BLACKLIST OF AI-TYPICAL WORDS]. Work in the entities from Step 1 where they fit. Include [NUMBER] internal links to these URLs: [LIST OF COLLECTION AND BLOG URLS]. Add one unique angle that none of the top 10 results covers, and label it.

Facts: use only facts from [PASTE BRAND AND PRODUCT FACTS]. Write [PLACEHOLDER: missing fact] where a fact is missing.

Output: first the pattern analysis table from Step 1 with the median and target length, then the article, then a list of the pain points used and the unique angle added.
```

**Prompt for competitor style matching** [L0972, 2025]

```
I'm attaching a blog post I recently wrote for my store at [store URL]. I looked at my competitors, including [competitor link 1], [competitor link 2] and [competitor link 3]. This is a rough draft, and I like their writing style better. Please modify and improve the attached draft so it matches the style of those competitors, while keeping all of my facts, product details and the structure of the post.
```

- Use the competitor style prompt only with your own facts. Check the output against the research and the style guide before publishing. [L0972, 2025; L0883, 2025-26]
