### Link Sources

**Supplier and partner sources**

- Suppliers can link to the store from their retailer or where-to-buy pages when you sell their products. This is a backlink source that often comes from selling a supplier's product. [L1227, 2020-26]
- Earn backlinks by getting listed in a where-to-buy section on supplier sites. [L0971, 2024]
- Exhibiting at trade shows or taking part in industry collaborations can produce links from the event's or partners' websites. [L1227, 2020-26]

**Editorial, guest and media sources**

- Writing guest posts for related blogs earns a link back to the store in exchange for the content you provide. [L1227, 2020-26]
- Guest post opportunities are easiest to find on sites that already accept guest posts and link back to the author's site. Cold requests to content sites with no guest post history are unlikely to succeed. [L1094, 2023]
- Podcast interviews about your industry usually include a link to the store in the episode description. [L1227, 2020-26]
- Contributing a useful section to an existing post on a related high-authority website, or speaking at an industry event, can earn a link back to the store. [L1227, 2020-26]
- Earn backlinks by contributing content to relevant sites. [L0971, 2024]

**Affiliate, owned and channel sources**

- A store's own YouTube channel can link to the store. Paid links and affiliate deals in which other sites post links in their descriptions can also send backlinks. [L1227, 2020-26]
- Becoming an affiliate for other sites that promote your products earns backlinks from those sites. [L1227, 2020-26]

**Link-attracting content**

- Shareable, valuable content is the backlink source that keeps compounding the most over time, because other websites keep finding and linking to it. [L1227, 2020-26]
- Content ideas that attract links include:
  - buyer's guides for the niche
  - Q&A pages that go deeper than competitors
  - how-to guides
  - top-5 or top-10 lists
  - announcements of industry news or new product types [L1227, 2020-26]

**Edu links through scholarships**

- Edu backlinks from university pages are very powerful for SEO. A scholarship for students can attract these links, and the seller estimated the cost per link at about $10 when a $3,000 scholarship was listed on about 300 college pages. [L1153, 2016]
- A charity-linked product can become a scholarship for high school seniors to earn edu backlinks. The seller framed it as a way to help students while gaining SEO value from college pages that link to scholarships. [L1153, 2016]

### Using Another Site's Authority for Listicles and PR

**Procedure: borrowing authority for a competitive listicle keyword**

1. Find a credible publication in your niche that covers the topic.
2. Pitch a guest post offering a listicle, such as best electric skateboards of the year.
3. Link back from the article to your store.
4. Make your own product the top item in the list. [L0949, 2021-22]

- A new store with low domain authority, such as a score of 7, needs backlinks and brand mentions in its market before it can rank for competitive terms. PR helps people learn the brand exists locally. [L0949, 2021-22]

**Case: Australian electric skateboard brand**

- Situation: an Australian electric skateboard brand appoints an exclusive distributor for Australia. The brand's domain has a domain authority of 7, it sells five models of electric skateboards, and most of its search traffic comes from its own brand name.
- Keyword research shows that product-name and niche terms have very little volume, for example foam grip tape skateboard with about 40 monthly searches.
- Recommended plan: build backlinks and brand mentions in Australia through PR, write content answering the long-tail questions buyers ask before purchase, and pitch a best electric skateboards listicle to a skateboarding publication with a link back to the store.
- The lesson gives no results from this plan. [L0949, 2021-22]

### Paid Links and Guest Posts

Advice from one source's 2021-22 material is marked older. Where it conflicts with one source's 2023-2024 material, both positions are kept below and the applicable case is stated.

**Price ranges**

- Paid link offers range from about $10 to $1,000 per link, with price tied to the linking page's perceived importance and domain authority. A commonly quoted flat fee for low-tier sites is around $50 per link. Subscription-style deals bill a link annually for as long as it stays live. [L0947, 2021-22]

**Judgment rules**

- Paying for a link is a judgment call: it is reasonable when the price is affordable, the site is good quality, and the expected result justifies the cost. [L0947, 2021-22]
- Links bought on an annual subscription should be judged by the same value test before renewal. [L0947, 2021-22]
- When a site sends a formal rate card, check whether it offers bundle prices, such as several links for one fee. Compare the cost per link across the bundle and against the linking page's authority before deciding. Bundles can look cheaper while still carrying the same risk as single paid links. [L0947, 2021-22]
- An informal, unprofessional offer with a low flat fee often signals a site that is testing whether you will pay. Treat such offers as a negotiation opening and redirect them to a guest post rather than paying the quoted amount. This keeps the spend low while still securing a link. [L0947, 2021-22]
- A guest post offered in place of a paid link gives two returns: a backlink to your site and exposure for your name on another site. If you do not write the article yourself, a content writer can produce it, so the offer is still workable without your own time. Frame the offer as adding value to their readers. [L0947, 2021-22]

**When each position applies**

| Situation | Position | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Buying backlinks from link service providers | Do not buy | [L0971, 2024] (2024), [L1094, 2023] (2023) |
| Buying backlinks in general | Risky, can lead to a penalty; stick with guest posting on sites that already publish guest content | [L1094, 2023] |
| Paid banner on a niche site that already reaches the target audience | Acceptable | [L0971, 2024] |
| Paid link with affordable price, good-quality site, and value justifying cost | Reasonable, judgment call | [L0947, 2021-22] |
| Link farm (site that exists mainly to sell links) | Do not pitch; remove from outreach list | [L0947, 2021-22] |
| Informal low flat fee | Negotiate and redirect to guest post | [L0947, 2021-22] |

**Link farms**

- Do not pitch paid links to sites that appear to exist mainly to sell links, often called link farms. Such sites are the most likely to be flagged by search engines, and a link from one can harm your site. Remove them from the outreach list instead of negotiating. [L0947, 2021-22]

### Finding Prospects

**Procedure: finding likely linkers for a target keyword**

1. Pick the target keyword and check that its SERP contains many ranking pages with many backlinks.
2. Open the SERP overview in an SEO tool (Ahrefs Keywords Explorer in the example, but any SEO tool works) and export the full results list as CSV.
3. Repeat step 2 for every similar secondary keyword, since more keywords uncover more likely linkers.
4. Merge all exported CSV files into one sheet in Excel or Google Sheets.
5. Remove duplicate rows based on the URL column only, keeping the first instance of each.
6. Sort the sheet by the backlinks column from highest to lowest.
7. Work down the sorted list only while domains have at least 10 backlinks.
8. For each domain, paste its URL into Site Explorer, open the Backlinks report, filter to Dofollow, group similar links as one link per domain, and export the full list as CSV.
9. Save all per-domain exports in the same folder.
10. Merge the per-domain exports into one master sheet.
11. Filter the master sheet by Domain Rating and keep only domains between 10 and 75. [L1443, 2021-22]

**Principles and rules**

- A likely linker is a site that has already linked to a page on the same topic as your content, so it is the most probable source of a new link to your better piece on that topic. [L1443, 2021-22]
- A keyword can still be worth using for link building even if its keyword difficulty is above the target of 10, as long as many ranking pages for it have many backlinks. [L1443, 2021-22]
- Use as many secondary keywords as possible with the same topic when collecting SERP results, because each extra keyword surfaces additional likely linkers. [L1443, 2021-22]
- For this link-building method only dofollow backlinks are pursued; nofollow links are excluded from the list of likely linkers. [L1443, 2021-22]
- Process only domains with at least 10 backlinks to the ranking page. Below that threshold the time spent per link drops sharply, and in the example about 35 domains exceeded 10 backlinks. [L1443, 2021-22]
- Do not manually research what each referring page is about or who runs it at this stage; exporting the data and moving on saves a lot of time. [L1443, 2021-22]
- In one example the first domain had 137 dofollow backlinks from unique domains, and the second had 19. Each domain's full list is exported separately before merging. [L1443, 2021-22]

**Domain Rating thresholds**

| Domain Rating | Use | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Below about 10 to 20 | Lower bound; do not go below this | [L1443, 2021-22] |
| 10 to 75 | Sweet spot for outreach | [L1443, 2021-22] |
| Above 75 | Do not go above 75 | [L1443, 2021-22] |
| 90 to 95 or higher | Skip; very large organizations, hard to find the right contact, links unlikely | [L1443, 2021-22] |
| Lower bound relative to your own site | If your own Domain Rating is about 15, skip domains at 10; if it is about 5, targeting domains at 15 to 20 is a good choice | [L1443, 2021-22] |

- The Domain Rating column header differs between SEO tools, and some tools call the same metric Domain Authority. Find the equivalent column in your tool before filtering, and keep the 10 to 75 range on that column. [L1443, 2021-22]
- In Excel, add the Domain Rating filter with Data > Filter, which is the same step as Data > Create a filter in Google Sheets. Then keep only rows with Domain Rating from 10 to 75. [L1443, 2021-22]

**Resource pages**

- A resource page is a section of a larger website that links out to other valuable pages, websites and tools on one topic. Its labels are typically resources, useful resources, links or useful links. Getting listed there is a supplementary link-building tactic, not a main driver, and works best as an extra link source for content pages, blog pages or the home page. [L1237, 2021-22]
- Resource page link building is not a volume tactic, because a given niche usually has only a few such pages. Expect a small number of wins and treat the search as a recurring task rather than a campaign. Broader adjacent niches can also yield relevant pages. [L1237, 2021-22]
- Build search terms from the topics and use cases people have for your products rather than from product names, because the people who write resource pages think in topics. For example, a bathroom products store should search on the home renovation topic rather than on bathtub or vanity. Product-name searches are less productive for this tactic. [L1237, 2021-22]
- Google results vary by country, so run the searches in the Google version for the market you sell to. Results in another country may return irrelevant pages for the same search terms. [L1237, 2021-22]

**Procedure: finding and qualifying resource pages**

1. Choose topic-based keywords that describe what your customers use your products for.
2. Search Google for each keyword combined with resources, helpful links, useful links and useful resources, for example keyword followed by the word resources.
3. Search keyword followed by inurl:links to find URLs that contain the word links, and try resources as well.
4. Go through pages two and three of the search results, since useful pages often appear there.
5. Quickly assess the quality of every candidate page, starting with its domain authority.
6. Discard candidates with a domain authority of zero or below 10.
7. Check that the page is relevant to your topic and that its audience matches your target market; discard irrelevant pages.
8. Decide which of your own pages fits the page (homepage, blog page or blog collection page, or a newly written blog post) and pitch only that page.
9. Find the email address on the site, or use Hunter.io if none is listed.
10. Send a personalised outreach email from your template and fill in the specific details.
11. Handle any link negotiation (payment or link exchange requests) with a prepared response.
12. Send follow-up emails at a regular interval. [L1237, 2021-22]

**Search operator and tools**

- The Google search operator inurl:links returns pages with the word links in their URL, which often reveals links pages that are resource pages. The same operator works with the word resources. Combine it with the topic keyword, for example keyword followed by inurl:links. [L1237, 2021-22]
- Ubersuggest is used to check the domain authority of a candidate resource page's website before you pitch it. Run every candidate through an SEO tool, such as Ubersuggest, as part of the quality check. [L1237, 2021-22]
- Hunter.io finds email addresses for a website's owner or contact when no email is published on the site. Use it as the fallback for locating outreach contacts. [L1237, 2021-22]
- The tools are used in sequence: first keyword and competitor research to identify content and likely linking sites, then email extraction to find contact addresses for those sites, and finally cold email outreach to request links. [L1388, 2021-22]
- Hunter.io can be searched with a website URL to return the email addresses it holds for that domain, or with a single email address to check whether it can be found. You are charged only for each address that is successfully returned, so searches with no result cost nothing. [L1388, 2021-22]

**Domain authority thresholds for resource pages**

- Domain authority below 10 should be avoided for resource page link building, and above 10 is generally worth pursuing. A domain authority of zero passes no SEO value, and accumulating many such links can hurt your ability to rank. [L1237, 2021-22]
- Check the quality of a resource page's site before you pitch it, using an SEO tool to confirm its domain authority is above 10. Only proceed to pitching once the site passes this check. [L1237, 2021-22]
- The procedure discards 10 and below, while the rule text says above 10; treat exactly 10 as the boundary case and check it against the other filters. [L1237, 2021-22]

**Choosing which of your pages to pitch**

- If a resource page lists businesses, suppliers or similar companies, pitch your homepage as the page to be linked. If it links to individual content on other sites, pitch a blog post or your blog collection page instead. [L1237, 2021-22]
- Pitch a blog collection page that holds many useful articles when the resource page links to blogs or useful content in general. Choose a page that gives the audience real advice relevant to the resource page's topic. [L1237, 2021-22]
- If no existing page of yours fits a content-linking resource page, write a new blog post on a topic the audience is interested in, ideally one that can rank for keywords. Pitch that post to the resource page after it is published, and do not rush it. [L1237, 2021-22]
- If a resource page mixes trade bodies, government sources and service providers without a single clear theme, pitch either your homepage as a trusted supplier of the product category or your blog collection page with practical how-to content. Choose the page that best matches the mix of links already on the list. [L1237, 2021-22]
- Do not pitch product pages or collection pages to resource pages. These are commercial pages, so the request reads as a sales ask rather than a value offer for the site's audience, which lowers the chance of being listed. [L1237, 2021-22]
- Do not pitch pages on your site that are only loosely related to the topic of the resource page. An irrelevant page significantly reduces the chance of being added to that page. [L1237, 2021-22]

**Judging neglected sites and mixed lists**

- A site that looks neglected or dated is not automatically disqualified. If the blog has been online for many years and the author still publishes new posts, it can still be worth pitching after you pass the domain authority check. [L1237, 2021-22]
- Some blogs offer guest post exchanges, where they publish your guest post in return for one of yours. If the link is to a commercial site, the blog may charge according to rates published on its advertising page, so check that page before agreeing. [L1237, 2021-22]

**Example of search volume**

- A search combining a topic keyword with inurl:links in Google Australia returned about 202,000 results. Many hits were ordinary business or service pages rather than resource pages, so every result needs a manual check of what it actually is before you spend time on it. [L1237, 2021-22]

### Outreach Emails

- All outreach emails should be short, to the point, and polite. Long messages or off-topic digressions reduce response rates, so keep each message to the request and a clear question. [L0947, 2021-22]
- Resource page outreach does not require a bulk email or mail-merge tool. Send each email individually, because the volume of relevant resource pages in one niche is small. [L1237, 2021-22]
- The pitch should frame your page as a valuable resource for the site's audience rather than as a way to promote your products. Your page's usefulness to the audience is the reason the owner should add the link. [L1237, 2021-22]
- Many blogs do not publish a public email address. Look for a contact page, an about page or an author page, where a named author's email may be listed. If none is found, use Hunter.io. [L1237, 2021-22]
- Prepare a standard response to payment and link-exchange requests before you start outreach, and use it consistently. [L1237, 2021-22]
- For backlink outreach, send no more than two to three emails to an influencer or publication. If there is no response, switch to a different channel such as a Facebook page or Instagram direct message, then check back in the future. [L1094, 2023]

**Opening formula for a resource page outreach email**

Formula: name the specific resource page, give one brief reason it helps its readers, name the single page of yours that adds value, and ask directly whether they would consider adding it. It works because it shows you read the page, moves the request from self-promotion to audience value, and gives the owner an easy yes or no.

- Example 1: Hello [Name], your links page on [topic] is a great find for people researching [subtopic]. I have written a guide on [subtopic] that would fit well alongside your listings, so would you consider adding it?
- Example 2: Hi [Name], your resources list on [topic] saves readers a lot of searching. I put together a practical checklist for [subtopic] that could be useful to your audience, and I wondered if you would be open to including it. [L1237, 2021-22]
