## Link Building and Off-Page SEO

### Backlink Basics and Why Links Matter

- Backlinks from external websites signal authority to Google. Each link is a vote of confidence, and websites with more backlinks rank higher. [L0226, 2017]
- Working with influencers generates backlinks, because they link to your store in their articles, which supports search rankings. [L0226, 2017]
- Off-page SEO covers actions outside the website that make search engines see it as trustworthy and authoritative, such as building backlinks, earning branded searches, and gaining engagement and shares on social media. [L0462, 2021-22]
- ** A 2020 ranking factors study found a clear correlation between total backlinks and Google rankings. When URLs with zero backlinks were excluded, top-ranking pages had more backlinks than lower-ranking pages. [L0462, 2021-22]
- ** Google has stated that it still uses PageRank alongside many other undisclosed signals. Links are one part of off-page SEO, not the whole. [L0462, 2021-22]
- ** Google's Quality Rater Guidelines rely heavily on off-site reputation, checked through online reviews, expert recommendations, news articles and awards. A large number of positive user reviews counts as evidence of a good reputation. [L0462, 2021-22]
- ** Google has publicly named three main ranking priorities: content, external links and the RankBrain algorithm. External links are one of the few ranking factors Google has explicitly confirmed. This statement dates from around 2020. [L0565, 2021-22; L0944, 2021-22]
- ** Google has officially confirmed only these three organic ranking signals. Other factors are known mainly through testing, so treat any specific claim about them as a hypothesis to verify. [L0944, 2021-22]
- ** Google treats backlinks as social proof. Links from other sites signal that content is good and the site is trustworthy, and sites with weak content rarely attract them. Backlinks also help Google place a site in its wider context. [L0944, 2021-22]
- ** Google values backlinks as a core signal for understanding the web. If any of the three core signals is missing, strong on-page and technical SEO will not earn significant rankings. [L0944, 2021-22; L0947, 2021-22]
- A site can earn some organic traffic without links through technical SEO, content and on-page work. Reaching high organic traffic with no external links is very hard, because Google uses links as third-party votes and combines them with on-page quality signals. [L0565, 2021-22]
- When competing pages have similar content quality, the page with more external links is usually harder to outrank. Links act as a tie-breaker that good content alone cannot provide. [L0565, 2021-22]
- Google also evaluates on-page quality, so pages that look dodgy or carry spammy links are judged negatively. Clean external links from relevant sites support page quality, not just link count. [L0565, 2021-22]
- A backlink passes most of its authority to the exact page it points to, not to the whole domain. Links on that page to other pages on your site pass only part of that authority onward. [L0470, 2023]
- Social media shares are nofollow and do not create backlinks that raise search rankings. They still help the trust signals of the site, so they are worth doing. [L0470, 2023]
- ** Once a page reaches the top of the results for its target term, the goal shifts to holding that position. Watch whether competing pages keep adding backlinks and whether you add enough quality links to stay ahead. [L0944, 2021-22]
- ** The E-commerce Skyscraper Method is the main link-building approach. Smaller supplementary methods can be added alongside it to find more opportunities, but they do not replace it. [L0944, 2021-22]
- ** Backlink outreach is a form of link building only when all links are genuine. Black hat tactics such as link farms are recognizable to Google and carry a risk of penalties. [L0944, 2021-22]

### Measuring Authority and Setting Link Targets

- Domain Authority Checker is a free tool that assesses a website's authority for SEO analysis. [L0173, undated]
- ** The number of backlinks needed depends on what competing pages already have, not on a fixed target. The goal is more or better-quality backlinks than the direct competitors ranking for the same search term, rather than the most links in the whole market. [L0944, 2021-22]
- ** Benchmarks from the course differ by how strong the competition is. Use each one where it fits:
  - **Weak competition:** if the top-ranking competitors have about five backlinks, a page usually needs roughly 5 to 10 quality backlinks, provided its own content is better than theirs. This is a rough benchmark, not a guaranteed result. [L0944, 2021-22]
  - **Competitive niche:** the average top-10 page for "surfboards" had 14 backlinks and a domain score of 54. For "wetsuits" the average top-10 page had 33 backlinks and a domain score of 72. These show how many links a page needs to compete. [L0877, 2021-22]
  - **No fixed number:** there is no fixed backlink number a top result must have. Look for ranking pages with hundreds of backlinks or high-authority linking sites, such as those rated in the 80s or 90s. Accept lower numbers in very small niches. [L0878, 2021-22]

### Why E-commerce Pages Struggle to Earn Links

- E-commerce sites find it hard to earn many inbound links because their core pages are product and collection pages, which are commercial in nature. Most websites do not want to link to them unless they gain something, since such a link gives their audience little value. [L0565, 2021-22]
- Resellers face an even harder time than brands selling their own products. A truly unique, self-made product can make commercial pages easier to link to. [L0565, 2021-22]
- Websites are generally unwilling to link to a commercial page for nothing in return. Link outreach to product or collection pages therefore needs an offer that gives the linking site something of value. [L0565, 2021-22]
- ** Pursuing backlinks to product or collection pages is usually a poor use of outreach effort, because most website owners prefer to link to informational content. A store that wants links needs content worth linking to before starting outreach. [L0944, 2021-22]
- ** Content pages such as guides and articles are the preferred targets for backlinks, because other site owners rarely link to product or collection pages. Build outreach around content that is created first. [L0944, 2021-22]
- Content pages attract links more readily than product pages, but content type and structure strongly affect how many links it earns. Choose content by researching which formats are most linked to in the niche. [L0565, 2021-22]
- Before producing link-attracting content, research which content types are most linked to in the niche, and produce those alongside the other content the store needs. Targeting and research are part of the link strategy, not an afterthought. [L0565, 2021-22]
- Product or collection links can still matter. Building links to product pages and collections that sit on page two of Google can move them up. It takes a few months before the effect shows, but organic sales then replace part of the margin lost to higher costs. [L0942, 2022]
- ** Sources differ on product-page links. Use the page-two approach from [L0942, 2022] only where a product or collection page is close to page one and the outreach offers the linking site something of value ([L0565, 2021-22]). Otherwise follow [L0944, 2021-22] and focus on content. [L0942, 2022; L0565, 2021-22; L0944, 2021-22]
- Affiliate link arrangements are a separate topic. They are set aside when analyzing why commercial pages struggle to earn links, and the outreach method for e-commerce stores does not cover them. [L0565, 2021-22]

### Choosing Content Roles and Projects

- Not every content piece needs a backlink campaign. Content serves one of three roles: ranking for target keywords, earning backlinks, or helping existing customers on the site. Only pieces meant to earn links need to go through a link-building process. [L0943, 2021-22]
- Build backlinks to content pieces wherever it makes sense for that piece. [L0383, 2021-22]
- ** A skyscraper project can be run on any suitable high-quality piece, which speeds up link acquisition. It is optional for each piece, so a monthly publishing plan does not require a skyscraper process for every item. [L0943, 2021-22]
- ** Skyscraper content is built to attract large numbers of backlinks. It is not needed in every piece and should not be the starting point of a beginner's plan. [L0384, 2021-22]
- ** The practical minimum is one completed skyscraper project, run through the full process including link negotiation, so that the workflow is learned and some links are built. [L0943, 2021-22]
- ** The skyscraper-style method aims for a very low cost and time investment per link. It is cheaper per link, per piece of content and per outreach than most other online link building. It makes up for lower effort per link with much higher outreach volume. [L0565, 2021-22]
- ** Only earn genuine links from real websites. Never buy links or build unnatural ones. This white hat approach keeps the risk of a Google penalty or algorithm-related loss very low. [L0565, 2021-22]

### Operating Sequence

1. Identify one piece of content to run a skyscraper project on. If no suitable content exists, choose a topic that fits the niche and create the content first. [L0943, 2021-22]
2. Work through the skyscraper project step by step, including the link negotiation process. [L0943, 2021-22]
3. Once the first project is complete, repeat the process on an ongoing basis and review which parts of the work can be outsourced. [L0943, 2021-22]
4. Research opportunities in your niche for resource page link building and broken link building. [L0943, 2021-22]
- ** Keyword research is the first step before creating content and running outreach. The skyscraper-style method starts there. [L0565, 2021-22]
- ** Link building is still partly manual and time consuming, so the owner should not keep spending large amounts of their own time on it for months or years. [L0945, 2021-22]

### Topic Research for Link-Attracting Content

- ** For link-focused content, keyword research is really topic research. The goal is to find topics that already attract many backlinks, not keywords to place inside the text. The chosen topic should be one that other sites have linked to repeatedly. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** Choose keywords relevant to your niche or product category, even if they are not directly about your products. Focus on informational queries, such as how-to or what-does-this-mean questions. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** Avoid commercial keywords (buying intent, such as "where can I buy") and navigational keywords (finding a place or site, such as "near me" or a specific park or brand). Even when their keyword difficulty is very high, they do not produce linkable content. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** Use broad keywords for this method, the opposite of typical SEO writing that targets narrow terms. Broad topics appeal to a wider audience and attract more links. The final article topic must still be specific enough to write one complete piece about. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** Keyword difficulty (KD) in Ahrefs, Ubersuggest and similar tools estimates how many linking domains a page needs to reach the top 10 organic results. In Ahrefs it is a weighted average of the linking domains of the current top-ranking pages, shown on a logarithmic scale from 0 to 100. A higher KD means the top pages already have many backlinks, which is what this method looks for. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** Target informational keywords with a keyword difficulty above 10 as the ideal. Values such as 15, 16, 20 or 30 are all good. In very small or specific niches, KD values somewhat below 10 can still produce usable link targets. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** When a KD is slightly below 10 but several ranking articles have decent backlink counts, the topic is still worth pursuing. Borderline values work as long as enough linking sites can be targeted. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** Search volume and clicks are not important for this method, because the purpose is to earn links rather than traffic for that exact term. Judge candidates by KD, search intent and the backlink profile of the ranking pages. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** Keyword and topic research workflow for the skyscraper method (Ahrefs Keywords Explorer, Google, United States):
  1. Open the keyword tool and select Google with the United States as the country.
  2. Enter the broadest term that describes your niche, for example the main product category, and run the search.
  3. Read the overview for keyword difficulty, search volume and CPC.
  4. Open the Questions report to see question-style searches around the term.
  5. Keep only informational queries and remove commercial and navigational ones, even if their KD is high.
  6. Keep candidates with KD above 10, accepting somewhat lower values in very small niches.
  7. Open the SERP overview of each candidate and check the top results: they should be mostly informational articles with many backlinks, not store, government or navigational pages.
  8. Ignore Wikipedia results when judging backlink strength, because they collect links for being Wikipedia.
  9. If the broad term is too general for one article, return to the question variations and choose a more specific candidate.
  10. Record each promising topic and its top-ranking competitor URL in the research spreadsheet.
  11. Use the Having same terms report to find secondary keywords close to the primary one and record them.
  12. Choose the topic that has the strongest set of linking sites and a clear article idea you can make better than the current top result.
  [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** Example: the broad term "camping" in the United States had a KD of 32, a search volume of about 127K and a CPC of $0.80. Ahrefs estimated that backlinks from about 40 websites were needed to rank in the top 10, but the term was too broad to write one specific article about. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** Example: question-style variations from the same niche had lower KD values. "What is dispersed camping" had a KD of 17, and "how to cook while camping" had a KD of 26. Both returned mostly informational articles with a good number of backlinks, making them suitable candidates. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** Keyword difficulty labels and estimated linking websites needed for the top 10 (Ahrefs, United States):

| Example keyword | Keyword difficulty | Ahrefs label | Estimated linking websites needed for top 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| camping | 32 | Hard | ~40 |
| how to cook while camping | 26 | Medium | ~30 |
| how to start a campfire | low | Easy | ~8 |

  [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** Example: "how to start a campfire" had a low KD and an estimated need of about 8 linking websites, while its top pages still carried many backlinks from individual sites. Because the query had enough linked articles beyond Wikipedia, it was treated as a viable topic despite the low difficulty. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** Example of the same topic: for the informational keyword "how to start a campfire" in the United States, Ahrefs showed a search volume of about 1.6K, a CPC of $1.60 and an Easy label with about 8 linking websites needed for the top 10. Its broader parent topic "how to start a fire" had a search volume of about 5.5K. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** To find secondary keywords, open the Having same terms report for the primary keyword and record the similar search terms that fit the same article. Sites that link to those variations are also likely to link to your article. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** To validate an existing topic idea, enter it into the keyword tool, check its KD and SERP overview, and look at the ranking articles and their backlinks. If several informational articles have decent backlink counts, add the idea to the outreach list as a content piece for this method. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** Build the article so it is at least as good as the current top-ranking page, for example as a more complete guide that adds extra items or sections. Better, more useful content is what attracts links away from existing pages. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** Choosing a high-KD navigational keyword, such as a "near me" search, wastes effort, because no informational article can satisfy that intent. Always check the search intent in the Questions and SERP results before committing to a topic. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** Counting Wikipedia pages as link targets inflates the apparent backlink strength of a SERP. Wikipedia accumulates links for its own reasons, so focus on other websites in the results. [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** Research spreadsheet layout for recording topics:

| Competition URL | Keyword/Topic | Secondary Keywords |
|---|---|---|
| URL of the top-ranking competitor page | Primary keyword or topic | Similar search terms from the Having same terms report |

  Example row: https://www.chowhound.com/food-news/204186/camping-food-guide-ideas/ | how to cook while camping | how to cook biscuits while camping; how to cook baked potatoes while camping; how to cook food while camping [L0878, 2021-22]
- ** The backlinks of current top-ranking articles reveal the sites that already link to this kind of content. These are the likely linkers to target in outreach for a new, better article on the same topic. [L0878, 2021-22]

### Building Link-Attracting Content

- ** Content built to attract backlinks for an e-commerce store must contain no commercial angle. It should not recommend buying specific products from the store, promote the store's products as better than others, or imply a buy call-to-action. [L0564, 2021-22]
- ** The main goal of this content is to earn links, not to rank for keywords or serve buyers directly. Keywords and customer relevance appear naturally, but they do not decide the topic or purpose. [L0564, 2021-22]
- ** Middle-of-funnel content that compares the store's own products or recommends the best product in a category is excluded from link-attracting content. That content should still be produced for customers, but it cannot be used for outreach. [L0564, 2021-22]
- ** Many websites refuse to link to overtly commercial pages, and link outreach works on volume. Promoting commercial content to many potential linkers wastes outreach effort. [L0564, 2021-22]
- ** The best topics for this method have broad appeal and sit at the top of the funnel. The wider the audience a topic reaches, the more linkers it attracts. [L0564, 2021-22]
- ** Only four content types are used for this method: ultimate guides, how-to posts, list posts (listicles), and infographics. Research into non-homepage pages shows these attract the most links. [L0564, 2021-22]
- ** An ultimate guide is a very large, very detailed page that delivers a lot of value on one niche topic. It should cover a subject related to the store's niche, not the store's own product or buying process. A cornerstone page can be promoted this way if it fits the ultimate guide format. [L0564, 2021-22]
- ** A how-to post explains how to do one specific thing, usually by fixing a common problem people have in the niche. It can be less detailed than an ultimate guide but is still highly linkable. A how-to can also be expanded into an ultimate guide on the same subject. [L0564, 2021-22]
- ** A list post, also called a listicle, presents a numbered set of items, such as "the top 12 places" for a niche topic. Longer and more detailed lists attract more links. List posts are among the easiest of the four types to produce. [L0564, 2021-22]
- ** An infographic is visual content that people embed in their own articles with a source credit, which creates a backlink. Existing articles can be converted into infographics and shared. Even if the infographic is not promoted, it can serve as the source for a skyscraper outreach campaign that earns a link back to the site. [L0564, 2021-22]
- ** Title formula for how-to pages:

  How to [fix or do a specific thing] [a common problem in the niche]

  It works because people searching for a concrete fix tend to link to the page that solves that exact problem. Examples in the same pattern: How to Stop Dog Harness Chafing Under the Front Legs; How to Keep a Camping Stove Flame Steady in Wind. [L0564, 2021-22]
- ** Any skyscraper content must be at least as good as the content ranking on page one of Google for the same topic. Thin sections, such as a few lines per list item, do not meet this standard. [L0564, 2021-22]
- ** Each piece of link-attracting content should go deeper on its subject than any existing page has gone. Depth of coverage is the main quality measure for these pages. [L0564, 2021-22]
- ** Before creating new link-attracting pages, audit the existing site for content that already matches one of the four formats and can be promoted as it is. Commission new pieces only in the formats the site is missing. [L0564, 2021-22]
- ** Check whether these four formats attract the most links rather than assuming it. Look up which non-homepage pages in a specific niche have the most backlinks and note their formats before choosing one. [L0564, 2021-22]
- ** For a camping-gear store, an ultimate guide to choosing tent stakes for different soil types, or a directory of campsites by US state, fits the ultimate guide format. A guide to buying the store's own tents does not, because it is commercial. [L0564, 2021-22]
- ** Original statistics attract links. People link to pages to cite specific statistics on them, not only because the content is high quality. Publishing original statistics or a data list can attract backlinks automatically. [L0462, 2021-22]
- ** Case: the author wanted more backlinks and suspected that people link to pages that cite specific statistics, not only to high-quality writing. A list of 80 email marketing statistics for 2019 was built at considerable time and money cost and published as a page. The first backlink arrived a few days later, which supported the hypothesis that original statistics attract links automatically. [L0462, 2021-22]
- ** Whenever content links out to or uses material from another source, such as quotes, graphs or research data, the original source must be credited on the page with a backlink. Then email the source to let them know. [L0385, 2021-22]
