## Hooks, Scripts and Video Structure

### Hook Design and First Seconds

#### The Opening Hook

- The first five seconds decide whether viewers keep watching; a weak opening makes people swipe away, so the hook must grab attention immediately [L0820, undated].
- Retention rate is treated as the most important signal for pushing a video, so the first seconds deserve the most effort. Some early drop-off is normal, but the goal is to make the curve as flat as possible [L1186, 2022].
- Viewers often read on-screen text or an emoji in the first seconds before deciding to keep watching. A short caption with a visible emoji in the opening frame can hold attention for the hook [L1444, 2022].
- TikTok hooks need to be more shocking than standard ad hooks and should create curiosity that keeps the viewer watching. Other hook formats that work include before-and-after and point-of-view (POV) shots [L1427, 2023+].
- A hook must promise something specific that the video then shows, so viewers stay to see whether it works [L1444, 2022].
- Proven hook structures can be borrowed from product videos in other niches, because the same opening works across different products. Copy the structure of the hook, not its exact wording, and adapt the content to your product [L1384, 2022].
- Virality depends more on video quality than on luck. An engaging hook, such as a surprising question that makes viewers want to finish, is a key driver [L1429, 2022].
- On TikTok, going viral depends on the hook, the wording, the angles and the sound choices in the video. Once these are found, they can be repeated across new videos [L1469, 2023+].

#### Hook Pattern for a Short Product Video

Pattern: open with a short statement in the first seconds that makes viewers curious about a clear visible result, and keep the total length around seven seconds to raise watch time [L1444, 2022].

Example patterns to adapt to your own product:
- "Your phone screen is dirtier than you think, see it clean in 7 seconds." [L1444, 2022]
- "This drawer organizer fits any kitchen sink, watch it fit in one try." [L1444, 2022]

- A hook that makes viewers wait for the result (for example "I won't buy until you do it") can produce very high watch time. High watch time keeps the video in circulation and gives viewers a reason to watch again [L0657, undated].
- A shocking or surprising ending placed near the end of the video encourages viewers to rewatch. The video can be cut slightly short right after the twist to increase rewatches [L1384, 2022].
- A very short video that ends on a cliffhanger, around 5 seconds long, can produce a high rewatch rate and a strong completion percentage. Leaving a question open pushes viewers to watch to the end [L1384, 2022].

#### Hook Example from a Beauty Demo

- A demonstration video for a beauty product that goes viral shows the product being used on screen, for example applying lashes to one eye while the other eye stays bare for a side-by-side comparison. Viewers keep watching to see the result, which holds attention better than talking about ingredients or specifications [L0820, undated].

### Native and Raw Entertainment Style

#### Core Principles

- TikTok creatives perform when they feel native and raw and trigger an emotion, rather than reading like a sales pitch. The same content principle can be applied to Facebook [L0363, 2022+].
- On TikTok, content is judged as entertainment rather than as advertising. Viewers leave quickly if something looks like an ad, so hooks should lead with value or entertainment and the product should appear inside that content, not be the focus of it. Skits with the product woven in tend to outperform straight product ads, while direct response styles that work on Facebook often fail here [L0819, 2020+].
- TikTok functions as an entertainment platform rather than a social network. Users come to be entertained, so a hook must either create aspiration or entertain before any product is shown. Ads that open with features or selling points are skipped quickly [L1374, undated].
- Content on TikTok should deliver one of three forms of value: aspiration, entertainment, or education. The ad must lead with one of these, otherwise viewers feel sold to and leave [L1374, undated].
- Virality depends more on video quality than on luck [L1429, 2022].

#### Clean Versus Wild Style

- Unconventional or overly wild video styles can go viral without converting well, because viewers enjoy the spectacle but do not buy. Clean, professional-looking videos that show what the product does usually convert better [L0657, undated].
- Talk-heavy or dialogue-driven videos often get views but convert less than videos that show the product clearly. Use a talking-to-camera opening only when it leads into a demonstration [L0657, undated].

#### Sound, Satisfaction and Creative Angles

- Colorful, satisfying visuals with ASMR-style sound performed well on TikTok organically, especially with younger audiences. In one comparison, a candy-like colorful product outperformed a healthy-looking one [L0657, undated].
- Look for creative advantages in each product: ASMR-style sound, recreating a sound better than the original, cool transitions, a clear wow moment, and relevant trends. Use these to stand out from similar videos [L0764, 2022].
- A simple pencil product (Infinity pencil) went viral on TikTok although it is not a high-converting product. Its success came from a trending sound used at the right time, a well-executed video with a good, aesthetically pleasing background, and a sound that matched the video. The same product with a plain background would likely have performed much worse [L0821, undated].

#### Case: Phone Case Demonstration

- A phone case ad that works on TikTok can show the case being knocked, clicked and removed, with satisfying ASMR sounds and no on-screen text or spoken sales pitch. The features are visible through the demonstration alone, so the viewer does not feel sold to [L1374, undated].

### Video Length and Clip Pacing

#### Length Targets

| Context | Length | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum recommended length for TikTok videos | At least 6 seconds | [L0764, 2022] |
| Target for creators new to video | 7 to 12 seconds | [L0764, 2022] |
| Starting point for a new account, then extend | Around 6 seconds, then gradually to about 10 seconds | [L1384, 2022] |
| Hook-focused short product video | Around 7 seconds | [L1444, 2022] |
| Dropshipping ads on TikTok | 15 to 30 seconds, never more than one minute | [L1374, undated] |
| Long video, foreshadow applied | Around 15 seconds | [L0764, 2022] |
| Very short cliffhanger video | Around 5 seconds | [L1384, 2022] |
| Fake conversation video | Around 15 seconds if the story is engaging | [L1384, 2022] |
| Clip that holds well with strong visuals | About 5 seconds | [L0530, 2022] |

- Keep TikTok videos at least 6 seconds long. Anything shorter usually causes a large drop in retention and makes it harder for the platform to find the right audience, although very short videos can work occasionally [L0764, 2022].
- Shorter videos are easier to keep viewers watching and hit higher watch-time percentages. Start with videos around 6 seconds, then gradually extend to about 10 seconds once you are comfortable with retention [L1384, 2022].
- A clip of about five seconds can still perform well when it has strong visuals, clear sound and a styled background, because the short length raises watch time and replays. This format works best when the product is satisfying to watch and the video loops easily [L0530, 2022].
- Video length for dropshipping ads on TikTok: aim for 15 to 30 seconds, and never exceed one minute. Two-minute ads only work for big brands with a famous founder who already has trust [L1374, undated].
- Making videos that run a minute or longer lowers average watch time, because viewers swipe away within seconds. Overly long videos are a common reason for weak retention [L1384, 2022].
- Fake conversation videos, such as a customer and company exchange, work because viewers stay to see how the story ends. A longer video of around 15 seconds can raise watch time when the story is engaging [L1384, 2022].

#### Pacing Rules

- Remove every dead moment and keep TikTok videos fast paced. Clips or sections under 3 seconds work well, though the right length depends on where each clip sits in the video [L0764, 2022].
- Dull moments drive viewers to swipe away, so remove any pause or slow scene, even half a second. Longer videos should be edited down aggressively without removing the key content [L1384, 2022].
- Change the camera angle, scene or use case every 2-3 seconds to hold attention. A clip that stays visually unchanged for more than two to three seconds causes people to scroll away [L0398, 2023+].
- When a video has longer clips, place them toward the end rather than the beginning so viewers are eased into the pace. Starting with long clips tends to lose attention early [L0764, 2022].

#### Clip Length Ladder

Increase the clip length step by step. Jumping straight from a 2-second clip to a 6 to 7 second clip makes viewers lose interest [L0764, 2022].

| Clip | Length |
|---|---|
| 1 (hook) | 2 seconds |
| 2 | 3 seconds |
| 3 | 4 to 5 seconds |
| 4 | 5 to 6 seconds |

#### Editing Procedure for Trimming

1. Cut every dull moment from the video, even if it is only a fraction of a second [L1384, 2022].
2. Trim longer videos by keeping only the key content; a 20-second video may be cut to about 12 seconds [L1384, 2022].
3. Open with a strong hook and avoid slow scenes that let viewers swipe [L1384, 2022].
4. Have someone else or yourself rewatch the cut to find slow parts and make split-second edits [L1384, 2022].

### Standard Product Video Structure

#### Structure for a 7 to 12 Second Product Video

1. **Hook clip, 1 to 3 seconds, ideally 1.5 to 2 seconds:** on-screen text plus an unexpected background action [L0764, 2022].
2. **Product show clip:** hold the product and spin it so viewers know what they are looking at [L0764, 2022].
3. **Demonstration clip:** use the product on screen for about 2 to 3 seconds [L0764, 2022].
4. **Second demonstration clip:** show the product in a different way, for example a different use or a different feature, for about 2 to 3 seconds [L0764, 2022].
5. **Further demonstration angles:** keep adding demonstration angles so no single clip stays on screen too long and viewers do not swipe away from boredom [L0764, 2022].

#### Longer Videos with a Foreshadow

- If a video runs long, around 15 seconds, play the most interesting moment in the first second as a foreshadow. That opening clip must be very satisfying, because it makes viewers stay to see the full payoff [L0764, 2022].

### Loops and Rewatch Logic

- A seamless loop, where the end flows back into the start, raises watch time. It is harder and more time-consuming to edit, so make it when the edit allows [L0764, 2022].
- A video with a clear, satisfying ending that flows back to the start rewards rewatching; combine with a loop where the edit allows [L0764, 2022].
- Re-watches and full completions matter more than likes for virality, because a high rewatch rate pushes distribution further even when the like-to-view ratio is modest [L0820, undated].
- A shocking ending near the end encourages rewatches [L1384, 2022].

### Retention and Distribution Logic

- TikTok shows a video to a small test audience first and only expands distribution if watch time passes the threshold, so watch time and full completion are the key performance signals [L0820, undated].
- Watch time strongly affects reach and sales. High watch time keeps the video in circulation and gives viewers a reason to watch again [L0657, undated].
- Retention rate is treated as the most important signal for pushing a video [L1186, 2022].
- Re-watches and full completions matter more than likes for virality [L0820, undated].
- Reusing the same intro and video structure over and over lets viewers recognize the content, and views drop over time. In the case described, repeated intros eventually caused an account to get almost no views [L1444, 2022].

### Script and Story Formats

#### Story Script Template (Comment-Driving)

Template: write a short-video story script that makes viewers comment at the end. Structure it in this order:

1. HOOK (face to camera, intense eye contact): one bold opening line that challenges the advice most people around you give.
2. CONFLICT: what you saw around you that made you doubt the common advice, and what happened when you shared your own goal.
3. TURNING POINT: the decision you made and how you began learning the skill.
4. CALL TO ACTION: ask viewers to comment a specific keyword, such as their country code, and promise to send them a free guide.

Use my story: [describe your background, location, starting situation, what you did and what changed]. Keep the tone emotional but humble, avoid bragging and close with the message that the goal is possible.

Write two completely different versions:
Version 1: emotional, personal 'mini series' style that emphasises pain, rebellion and redemption and builds emotional connection.
Version 2: bold, punchy, controversial 'debate starter' style that is faster paced, opinionated and built to trigger comments and shares, with a hook that challenges a common income benchmark.

[L0887, 2026]

- Story-based scripts that end with a comment request get more replies when the call to action asks for one simple keyword and promises something specific in return. The hook and conflict should create tension first, so viewers want to see the outcome [L0887, 2026].

#### Debate-Starter Hook Formula

Pattern: name a common income figure that people use to judge what is realistic, reject it as the limit of someone's goals, and set it against a dream worth protecting. It works because it creates immediate tension and gives the viewer a position to agree or disagree with, which drives comments and shares [L0887, 2026].

Examples in this pattern:
- "Don't let a $35K paycheck decide how big your store is allowed to grow." [L0887, 2026]
- "Stop taking money advice from someone who has never built anything they own." [L0887, 2026]

#### Story and Scenario Videos

- Story-based or scenario videos can drive virality for a product, and viewers respond to relatable situations more than to plain product demonstrations [L0656, undated].
- Products applied to the face require the creator to show the face or part of it, which limits who can film the product comfortably. Account for this before choosing a face-care product for a faceless or privacy-focused account [L0657, undated].

### Talking-Head Videos

- For talking-head videos, prop the phone up and film as if you were on a FaceTime call with a friend. Formats that suit this approach include a day-in-the-life clip, a founder routine, or answers to frequently asked product questions [L0576, 2025-26].
- Talk-heavy or dialogue-driven videos often get views but convert less than videos that show the product clearly. Use a talking-to-camera opening only when it leads into a demonstration [L0657, undated].

### Scenario and Demonstration Examples

- For a product meant for a pet or a baby, a stuffed animal or a doll can stand in for the real subject in short demonstration videos. This keeps the demo realistic while avoiding the need for a live child or animal [L1380, 2022].
- Products applied to the face require the creator to show the face or part of it. Account for this before choosing a face-care product for a faceless or privacy-focused account [L0657, undated].
