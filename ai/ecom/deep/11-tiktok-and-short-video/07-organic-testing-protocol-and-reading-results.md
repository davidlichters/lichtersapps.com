## Organic Testing Protocol and Reading Results

### Test Setup and Account Plan

- Test one product at a time. Put all energy into that product instead of running several at once. [L0309, 2024+]
- Keep one testing account per product. Do not reuse a product's account for a different product unless the niche is the same. [L0501, 2022]
- Once a proven winner works on one account, repeating it on a second account is valid, because competitors copy visible winners, so capture the market while it works. [L0501, 2022]
- Judge a product only after a sustained posting period, not after a few videos. [L0821, undated]

**Accounts per product, by approach**

| Approach | Number of accounts | Condition | Source ids |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spread output across many accounts | 5 to 10 accounts, posting on all of them | Standard organic test of one product. A single account rarely gets enough distribution for the platform to show the product. | [L0561, 2023+] |
| Single testing account | 1 account per product | Keeps each product's account separate. Each new account's first videos usually get little reach, so post before the new-account advantage ends. | [L0501, 2022] |
| Two-phone setup | 2 accounts on separate phones | Post the product video organically and track its views. | [L1186, 2022] |
| Replacement account | 1 new account (new email) | Performance stalls by day 3 to 4. Repeat until one account gains traction or the product is confirmed as not resonating. | [L0309, 2024+] |
| Replacement account, no traffic | 1 new account | No organic traffic despite reasonable creative quality. Use the new-account advantage instead of continuing on a shadowbanned or under-performing account. | [L0309, 2024+] |

- Relying on one account and posting only a few videos per product is the main reason organic reach stays at roughly 500 to 1,500 views. [L0561, 2023+]
- A new account may need about 9 to 12 uploads before a video takes off, so do not judge it after a few posts. A breakout can come on day four after a steady routine. [L0372, 2022]

### Test Duration and Patience

**Duration by situation**

| Situation | Duration | Source ids |
|---|---|---|
| Kill check: no meaningful results at all (no views, engagement or conversions) | Run the test for 3 to 5 days, then kill | [L0309, 2024+] |
| Standard product judgment | About 2 to 3 weeks per product | [L0820, undated] |
| Standard product judgment, applied to a trending-product test | Two weeks of posting before judging | [L0821, undated] |
| Organic test, minimum patience | About two weeks or more. A few days of low views is not a reliable verdict. | [L0561, 2023+] |
| Early viral window | Most content goes viral within the first week, usually 3 to 7 days, and can take up to two weeks | [L1429, 2022] |
| Hook and angle testing when videos show zero views | About two months before videos begin to go viral | [L1469, 2023+] |

- Sources disagree on how long to wait. The 3 to 5 day rule applies only when nothing happens at all. The 2 to 3 week rule applies when you have some signal and need sustained output before judging. The two-month rule applies when zero-view results come from creative problems and the team is still testing hooks, wording, angles and sounds. [L0309, 2024+; L0820, undated; L0561, 2023+; L1469, 2023+]
- Do not treat early zero-view results as proof that TikTok cannot work for the product. [L1469, 2023+]
- Posting only five videos and then expecting views is not enough. Upload consistently over days and weeks before judging a product. [L1383, 2022]

### Phase 1: Days 1 to 2

1. Upload 3 videos on day 1 and reply to every comment. [L0501, 2022]
2. Follow back and engage with accounts that liked or followed you, and with similar accounts in your niche, because mutual follows make TikTok more likely to show your videos. [L0501, 2022]
3. On day 2, keep replying to all comments, fix any mistakes in the account, and keep posting 3 to 5 videos per day while the new-account advantage lasts. [L0501, 2022]
4. Use video replies on relevant comments, ideally on the top 5 to 10 comments of a viral video, so new viewers who read the comments land on your newer video. [L0501, 2022]
5. In the first few days, use the story/dialogue scenario format (for example "follow me for a chance to win") to grow followers faster. It works with almost any niche or personal brand. [L0501, 2022]

### Phase 2: Days 3 Onward

**Posting volume**

Sources give different volumes. Use the one that matches the test:

- 3 to 5 videos per day for a single product on a test account. [L0309, 2024+; L0501, 2022]
- 2 to 5 short videos per day across TikTok and Instagram Reels, with about 2 to 3 weeks per product. [L0820, undated]
- 4 to 5 videos per day for about two weeks on a trending product. [L0821, undated]
- When running several products on one account, 3 to 5 videos per week or per day per product, logged in a spreadsheet. [L0561, 2023+]

**Stall check and replacement**

1. Upload 3 to 5 videos to a TikTok account for organic testing. [L0309, 2024+]
2. Within the first 24 to 48 hours, look for at least 500 to 1,000 views across videos. [L0309, 2024+]
3. If a video reaches 10,000+ views by the end of day 1, it signals strong potential. [L0309, 2024+]
4. If performance stalls by day 3 to 4, create a new account (new email) and test the same product again. Repeat until one account gains traction or you confirm the product does not resonate. [L0309, 2024+]

**Product test procedure (short)**

1. Upload 3 to 5 videos per day to a test account. [L0309, 2024+]
2. Run this test for 3 to 5 days. [L0309, 2024+]
3. If no meaningful results appear (no views, no engagement, no conversions), kill the product and move to the next one. [L0309, 2024+]
4. Do not run multiple products simultaneously. [L0309, 2024+]

**Creative angle testing before abandoning a product**

Content quality and creative angle are separate variables. One failing does not mean the product fails. [L0309, 2024+]

1. Test six angles before dropping a product: problem-solution framing, lifestyle, emotion, social proof, benefit statement, comparison. [L0309, 2024+]
2. If one angle performs poorly, test two to three different angles with the same product before moving to a new product. [L0309, 2024+]

**Trend-based product start**

1. Find a product that is getting strong views on other TikTok accounts. [L0821, undated]
2. Buy the product quickly so you can film with it. [L0821, undated]
3. Create several videos for that product using the trending sound and a hook in the style of the viral video, adding your own execution. [L0821, undated]
4. Post 4 to 5 videos per day. [L0821, undated]
5. Keep posting this way for about two weeks before judging the result. [L0821, undated]

- The referenced case reached first real traction only after about two weeks of 4 to 5 posts per day. [L0821, undated]

### Week-Two Cycling

1. Run cycles in which a few broad-view videos build reach first. [L0501, 2022]
2. Follow them with higher-converting videos that carry the momentum into sales. [L0501, 2022]
3. When views drop, go back to broad-reach videos. [L0501, 2022]
4. Re-introduce high-converting videos once views recover. [L0501, 2022]

### View Benchmarks

| Benchmark | Value | Meaning | Source ids |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early reach across videos | 500 to 1,000 views within 24 to 48 hours | Minimum early signal | [L0309, 2024+] |
| Strong day-1 signal | 10,000+ views by end of day 1 | Strong potential | [L0309, 2024+] |
| First-week range | 1,000 to 10,000 views | Expected range for the product video in its first week | [L1186, 2022] |
| Stalled account | 500 to 1,500 views | Typical reach of one account with few videos per product | [L0561, 2023+] |
| Growth to keep a product | Steady growth, for example 1,000 to 5,000 views | Keep the product; remove it after a set number of posts without growth | [L0561, 2023+] |
| Pivot trigger | 50,000 to 100,000 views with clear interest in another topic | Enough reason to pivot | [L1429, 2022] |
| Viral | More than 1 million views | Definition of viral | [L0795, undated] |
| Meaningful sales threshold | 1 million to 10 million organic views | Needed before a product produces meaningful sales | [L0706, 2020+] |

- A converting video at small scale: one video at around 40,000 to 60,000 views produced four to six sales, which was the breakthrough point for a seller who had tested for about two months. [L1469, 2023+]
- These two values describe different things. The 40,000 to 60,000 view figure is a converting video early in the testing phase, and the 1 to 10 million figure is the general organic threshold for meaningful revenue. [L1469, 2023+; L0706, 2020+]
- Views of 500 or more with strong engagement but no conversions point to product-market fit or the landing page, not the creative. [L0309, 2024+]

### Judging a Product

**Check the signals that matter**

- Judge a TikTok account by video performance and conversion, not by follower count. [L0657, undated]
- Followers on TikTok carry less weight than on other platforms: they do not guarantee views or sales. [L0657, undated]
- One account with about 1,000 followers produced 56 store sessions in its first few days, while its average video reached about 5,000 views. Store sessions were the clearest signal of audience response. [L0352, 2022]
- Check how consistently the seller posts, not only the view count. A seller who posts at least daily and keeps uploading is usually still getting some sales, even when individual videos stall. [L0656, undated]
- Read the comments for buying intent. Phrases like "where can I buy this", "I just bought one", "I placed an order" or "I want one" signal interest and possible sales. Complaints like "where is my order" signal fulfillment problems. [L0656, undated]
- Use views only as an early signal and compare them with actual orders before scaling. [L0372, 2022]

**Check conversion before scaling a product**

- If a product gets good organic traffic (500+ views, strong engagement) but no sales, check product-market fit and the landing page before changing the creative. [L0309, 2024+]
- If a product gets no organic traffic despite reasonable creative quality, create a new account rather than continuing on a shadowbanned or under-performing one. [L0309, 2024+]

### Kill and Pivot Decisions

**Kill rules**

- Kill a product if a 3 to 5 day test shows no meaningful results (no views, no engagement, no conversions). [L0309, 2024+]
- Kill a product when, after several days, the view count stays flat. Keep products whose views grow steadily and remove the product after a set number of posts without growth. [L0561, 2023+]
- Do not kill a product on zero-view results alone when the creative is the likely cause. Test angles and hooks first. [L1469, 2023+; L0309, 2024+]

**Procedure for several products on one account**

1. Post each product on a per-post basis, roughly three to five videos per week or per day. [L0561, 2023+]
2. Log engagement and views for each product in a spreadsheet over time. [L0561, 2023+]
3. Keep products whose views grow steadily, for example from 1,000 to 5,000. [L0561, 2023+]
4. After a set number of posts without growth, remove the product from the account. [L0561, 2023+]

**Testing a product before paid ads**

- Post organic content to see which products gain traction in views and comments before investing in paid ads. Kill those that do not grow. [L0561, 2023+]

### Comment-Driven Pivots

**Principles**

- Comments are the best feedback on what an audience wants to see. When one reference keeps repeating, pivot the whole angle toward it. [L0764, 2022]
- A small change of angle can turn a weak account viral. Recognise when to change the content completely. [L0764, 2022]

**Procedure: audience feedback**

1. Read the comments on every video, especially the top comments. [L0764, 2022]
2. Look for the same reference, theme or question appearing repeatedly. [L0764, 2022]
3. Reply to the top comment with a video reply that uses that theme. [L0764, 2022]
4. If the reply performs well, go hard on that theme and keep repeating it across uploads. [L0764, 2022]
5. If the existing angle keeps underperforming after a few days, change the content completely instead of waiting. [L0764, 2022]

**Procedure: pivot from an unrelated trend in the comments**

1. Check videos with real views but weak engagement, and read the comments for a different topic or interest. [L1429, 2022]
2. Find comments on that topic that have many likes, which signal real interest. [L1429, 2022]
3. Make a video reply on that topic and connect it to your product. [L1429, 2022]
4. Take the top comments of the new video and make further video replies from them. [L1429, 2022]
5. Use trending sounds or trends on that topic, and create your own sounds if that helps. [L1429, 2022]

**Reference case**

- A product had been tested on TikTok for six to seven days. About three days in, one video reached about 50,000 views, but its comments were about an unrelated trend instead of the product. [L1429, 2022]
- A video reply on that trend, connected to the product, reached about 250,000 views within 24 hours and about 1 million in total. Its top comments led to further video replies that generated about 3 million additional views. [L1429, 2022]
- The video was then completely reworked around that interest using a trending sound and custom sounds. It grew from about 200,000 to over 1 million views. [L1429, 2022]
- Later videos regularly reached over 100,000 views, several about 250,000, and others about 800,000. [L1429, 2022]
- Moderate view counts of 50,000 to 100,000 are reason enough to pivot when comments show clear interest in something else. Follow the audience's interest instead of the original plan. [L1429, 2022]

### Views Versus Sales Reality

- A large view count does not guarantee sales. One product video with about 15 million views produced only about 25 orders and under 500 dollars in sales for the store. [L0372, 2022]
- A viral video's sales usually peak for about 24 hours. After that they decline gradually or sometimes drop sharply. [L0706, 2020+]
- One viral video in a referenced case reached roughly 10 to 15 million views and produced about $1,000 in sales within about 2 hours. [L0821, undated]
- A product in the same referenced case kept getting around 4 million views per day for about a month after one video went viral. A single winning video can keep producing sales over an extended period. [L0821, undated]
- Videos keep generating views and sales long after you stop posting. A product account with a strong video can keep earning for weeks without new uploads. Count older winners as ongoing revenue when judging an account. [L1384, 2022]
- Plan for roughly 1 million to 10 million organic views before a product produces meaningful sales. Products reaching only a few thousand views rarely create enough revenue to matter. [L0706, 2020+]

### Retention and Traffic-Source Analytics

**Retention**

- Low average watch time means the video does not hold viewers long enough. Make the editing faster and the pacing tighter. [L1186, 2022]
- The retention chart shows the second where viewers leave. A sharp drop in the first seconds means the hook is failing and needs rebuilding. [L1186, 2022]
- Example: a retention curve falling from 100% to 87% and then to about 34% shows a weak hook followed by a steep loss of viewers. That curve needs a new opening. [L1186, 2022]
- A second drop later in the video points to a specific segment that viewers found boring or confusing. Fix that segment in the next edit so the curve flattens out. [L1186, 2022]
- Watch time drives distribution. Videos viewers watch to the end keep getting shown. [L1381, 2022]
- A low-performing video often reflects weak content rather than restriction. Identical copies of a video can still perform very differently, with one example reaching 17 million, 4 million and 2 million views. [L1381, 2022]

**Traffic source**

- Check the traffic-source split. A For You share of 98% shows the algorithm is pushing the video. [L1186, 2022]
- A split such as 54% For You and 42% Following suggests weak distribution and may point to a shadow-ban risk. [L1186, 2022]

### Profile and Engagement Tactics

- Pin up to three videos on the profile, because pinned videos are seen first. They should be professional, high-converting videos, especially when running Spark Ads. [L1381, 2022]
- Start Spark Ads on a video once it passes about 10,000 views in its first week. [L1186, 2022]
- Use trending sounds and hooks in the style of a viral video, adding your own execution. [L0821, undated]
- Avoid watermarks on re-uploads. In one case, re-uploads that still carried a watermark signalled low quality from the start. [L1384, 2022]
- Reply to every comment in the first days, and use video replies to lift the newest videos. [L0501, 2022]
- A product was tested as a quick experiment, with the account uploading for about two to three weeks and then being left with little attention. One video reached 9.5 million views. A YouTube video featuring the same clip produced a visible surge in engagement on the day it was published. The store was closed only a few days before the call, yet earlier videos kept bringing in sales for more than two weeks without new uploads. [L1384, 2022]

### Common Mistakes

- Relying on one account and posting only a few videos per product keeps reach at roughly 500 to 1,500 views. Spread output across multiple accounts. [L0561, 2023+]
- Posting only five videos and then expecting views is not enough. [L1383, 2022]
- A seller tested many videos with no views, and accounts were banned, shadow-banned and videos removed on the first day. After about two months of testing hooks, wording, angles and ASMR-style sounds, a video converted at around 40,000 to 60,000 views with four to six sales. From then on, the team could reproduce viral results consistently. [L1469, 2023+]
- Expect a testing phase of about two months before videos begin to go viral. Do not treat early zero-view results as proof that TikTok cannot work for the product. [L1469, 2023+]
