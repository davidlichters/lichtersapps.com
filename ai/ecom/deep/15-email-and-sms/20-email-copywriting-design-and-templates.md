## Email Copywriting, Design and Templates

### Copy Structure and Purpose

- Email copy has two parts: the subject line and the body copy. Each is written and optimised separately. [L0568, 2021-22]
- Marketing emails that aim to get the reader to visit the site need copywriting, because their purpose is persuasion toward a specific action. [L0568, 2021-22]
- Direct selling emails start from one core problem of the target market, which the product is positioned to solve. Named frameworks for structuring them include Problem-Agitate-Solution, the Bencivenga persuasion equation and AIDA. [L0475, 2026]
- The body copy of a marketing email has one job: generate clicks to a destination, not sell inside the inbox. Persuasion, full proof and purchase decisions belong on the website, where there is room for images, videos and complete copy. [L0602, 2021-22]
- If an email tries to make the full purchase case, readers who are not convinced do not click, and the store loses the chance to convert them on the site. Send traffic straight to the website where the full picture can be shown. [L0602, 2021-22]
- Do not answer the question or problem raised in the email. Tell the reader they will learn the answer through the click, because otherwise there is no reason to take the action. [L0602, 2021-22]
- Email shares many similarities with content. Knowing customers' experiences, problems and pain points lets email address them more effectively. Email marketing built on this research communicates more precisely with the customer. [L0872, 2021-22]
- The strategies that work in email are rooted in direct mail marketing and have stayed consistent for decades because human psychology has not changed. Tested direct mail principles can therefore be applied to email copy and sequencing. [L0609, 2021-22]
- Treat every email as a potential reusable template. Once an email is written, reuse it across other flows instead of writing from scratch, which saves significant time. [L0493, 2021-22]
- Message matters more than format. Postage cost and fancy stationery do not increase response; the offer and the opening sentences do. [L1261, undated]

### Persuasion Principles

The six power reasons customers buy:

1. **Ownership:** use language and lifestyle images to make them feel they already own it (car salesman technique: give control and choice). [L0261, 2025-26]
2. **Logic:** identify the right customer, answer why it is relevant, and confirm it solves their specific problem. [L0261, 2025-26]
3. **Social Proof:** include testimonials, reviews, ratings and credible endorsements. [L0261, 2025-26]
4. **Scarcity and Urgency:** use a limited-time offer, limited stock language, show others buying, and highlight the cost of inaction. [L0261, 2025-26]
5. **Reciprocity:** frame the offer as a gift (free shipping, discount), create obligation, and provide value first. [L0261, 2025-26]
6. **Status:** identify the status the customer wants (luxury, eco-friendly, athletic) and position the product to align with and elevate that status. [L0261, 2025-26]

### Body Copy Formula and CTA Rules

#### Order of Work

- Decide the call to action before writing the body copy. Typical CTAs in a copy email are learning more about a product, reading a blog post or downloading a lead magnet. [L0602, 2021-22]
- Write the body copy after the subject line, and make it follow on from the subject line's promise. Together they should create curiosity, so the body reveals only enough to make the reader want more. [L0602, 2021-22]

#### Body Copy Formula

Email body formula for e-commerce copy emails, in order:

1. Greet the reader and confirm the problem that the CTA will help them solve.
2. Tease the solution, which is the product's primary promise or the answer the content provides.
3. Explain at least one benefit of taking the CTA.
4. Offer the CTA.
5. Sign off.

[L0602, 2021-22]

- After the subject line, briefly name the primary value or benefit the reader gets by taking the CTA. Do not explain the whole content in the email. [L0602, 2021-22]
- For a content CTA, one primary benefit is usually enough. If there are several, list up to three primary benefits and keep each one short, because the content itself delivers the detail. [L0602, 2021-22]
- For a product CTA, focus the email on the product's primary promise. If the product copy already has a clear primary promise, reuse that promise in the email. [L0602, 2021-22]
- Open the email body with a short greeting that also introduces the sender, so the reader knows who is writing before the problem is named. [L0602, 2021-22]
- Confirm the problem by pointing to how often customers ask about it, for example by saying the store receives many emails or questions on the topic. This makes the problem feel recognised and real before the solution is teased. [L0602, 2021-22]
- Name the destination in the email, such as a blog post, guide, product page or download. This avoids surprising readers when the page asks for something other than a simple read, especially when the link leads to an opt-in download. [L0602, 2021-22]

#### Length

- A copy email for e-commerce should be a few hundred words in four or five short paragraphs. Longer formats are used by digital product, consulting and coaching businesses, not typical e-commerce stores that send traffic to a full site. [L0602, 2021-22]
- A complete copy email that follows the problem, solution, benefit and CTA formula, with two benefits and one guide link, can run about 145 words. [L0602, 2021-22]

#### Body Copy Checklist

Body copy format checklist, in order:

1. Use a minimum font size of 14 point or larger.
2. Use short, simple sentences in easy-to-read language.
3. Use short paragraphs of one to two lines, and single-line paragraphs are common.
4. Make the CTA or links stand out.
5. Use only one CTA per email.
6. Use a personalised signature.

[L0602, 2021-22]

- Sign emails with a real person's name, either the owner or a team member, rather than a generic business name or the word Team. Emails that feel personal from a person to a person tend to get better response rates. [L0602, 2021-22]

#### One CTA, Placed in Several Spots

- Focus most emails on exactly one call to action. Mixing several offers or links in one email reduces the response to each of them. Several different CTAs overwhelm readers, lower interaction and often lead them to ignore the email and not return to it. [L0569, 2021-22; L0602, 2021-22]
- Adding many calls to action to one email reduces the number of clicks for each one. Avoid daily-deal style emails stuffed with many offers unless that is actually your business model. [L0569, 2021-22]
- Several clickable elements in one email are acceptable if they all point to the same CTA and the same content. Multiple different CTAs to different content should be avoided. [L0602, 2021-22]
- Place the one CTA in several spots that all lead to the same page: a link within the sentence that names the topic, a clear click instruction in the middle, and a link or button near the end. These are multiple clickable areas for a single action, not several different offers. [L0602, 2021-22]

#### Button or Text Link

- Use a button rather than a plain text link for the main call to action. Research cited in the lesson indicates that buttons improve click-through rates and action. [L0569, 2021-22]
- A CTA can be a clickable button or a standard underlined text link in a different colour, such as "click here to view". Test both styles, since neither is proven to be better in general. [L0602, 2021-22]
- The two positions differ. The button is the recommended default, and the text link is an accepted alternative. When you cannot tell which works better for your list, run both as a test. [L0569, 2021-22; L0602, 2021-22]
- The call-to-action button must use a unique colour with strong contrast against the rest of the email, such as bright red, bright green or bright blue. Any colour works as long as the button clearly stands out. [L0569, 2021-22]

#### Pattern: Problem, Guide, Two Benefits, One CTA

Pattern:

- A curiosity-driven subject line.
- A greeting.
- One sentence that names a common buying problem.
- A sentence saying there is confusing information online.
- A sentence promising a guide or resource that settles the question.
- Two benefits the reader gets from clicking.
- One repeated CTA with a clear link or button.

Why it works: the reader sees a problem they recognise, a promise of an answer and a single clear next step, so the click is the only logical action. [L0602, 2021-22]

- Example A (running shoes, subject: "Flat feet, which shoe?"): "Hi Sam, choosing running shoes with flat feet is a big decision. Advice online contradicts itself on stability versus cushioning, so we wrote a short guide. Click here to see which features matter most and which model our fitters recommend for your stride." [L0602, 2021-22]
- Example B (mattresses, subject: "Firm or soft, which is right for your back?"): "Hi Lee, picking a mattress for back pain is not something to rush. There are many mixed opinions about firm and soft, so we put together a simple comparison. Click here to see the pros and cons of each and which one our sleep team suggests for back sleepers." [L0602, 2021-22]

#### Pairing a Discount With a Product

- Pair the discount with one specific product suggestion in a single sentence that links the saving to a second purchase. This turns the coupon into a reason to add to the basket rather than just a price cut. [L0606, undated]
  - Example 1: "Your 15% covers a second pair of socks, so add the matching cap while you are here." [L0606, undated]
  - Example 2: "Use your 15% on the refill pack and the saving pays for a travel size of the same cream." [L0606, undated]

### Writing Process and Scannable Style

- Keep emails easy to scan with one or two sentences per line, lots of white space and no long paragraphs. Readers scan first and read only what hooks them. Short lines keep attention on the story and offer. [L0523, 2015+]
- Use short, choppy sentences and short paragraphs of one to two lines. [L0569, 2021-22]
- Writing process for emails: [L0523, 2015+]
  1. Write the basic structure of the email first, including the story beats and the offer link.
  2. Add the juicy details, emotional hooks and personality afterwards.
  3. Dictate ideas into a phone recorder and send the recording to someone for transcription if writing is slow.
- Email body text for flows can be drafted with ChatGPT, but the draft still needs checking. Before going live, confirm the links point to the correct checkout and product pages and that the wording matches the offer. [L0476, undated]
- Small grammar errors are acceptable in emails and can make the character more human and relatable. Do not delay sending because of fear of mistakes, since consistent sending matters more than perfect text. The flaw is part of the bond. [L0523, 2015+]
- Keep a separate swipe file of emails collected from competitors and from marketers in other niches. Use it as a source of ideas when you lack inspiration for your own email content, without copying it directly. [L0609, 2021-22]

### Font Size and Readability

- Set the email body font to at least 14 point. Automatic sizes of 12 or 10 point that many email tools produce are too small and hard to read for some people. [L0602, 2021-22]
- Body copy should use a minimum font size of 18 pt, with up to 22 pt acceptable, because bigger text is generally easier to read. [L0569, 2021-22]
- The two figures conflict. Use 18-22 pt for body copy where you can, and treat 14 pt as the floor for copy emails. [L0569, 2021-22; L0602, 2021-22]

### Personalisation and Dynamic Content

- Email content can use personalisation variables such as {{firstName}} with a fallback value like 'Friend' for customers without a stored first name. [L0282, 2020-26]
- Personalise the greeting and subject line with the first name. Adding the subscriber's first name makes the email feel more personal. [L0607, undated]
- Add a default such as 'there' to every personalised greeting and subject line. Otherwise an empty first name in the profile leaves an empty greeting. [L0606, undated]
- Personalisation in Klaviyo (Liquid) for the greeting and the subject line:

```text
Greeting: Hey {{ first_name|default:'there' }},
Cart subject line: Still thinking it over, {{ first_name|default:'there'|title }}?

Use the fallback in both places, so an empty first name in the profile does not show up as a blank greeting.
```
[L0606, undated]

- The two fallback values differ. 'Friend' is the general personalisation fallback for variables ([L0282, 2020-26]). 'there' is the greeting and subject fallback used in the Klaviyo flow templates ([L0606, undated]). Use 'Friend' for general content variables and 'there' where the greeting reads "Hey there,". [L0282, 2020-26; L0606, undated]
- The placeholder syntax also differs between sources. Klaviyo uses {{ first_name }} ([L0606, undated] shows it without a fallback, although [L0606, undated] requires one). Generic text uses {first_name} and {discount_code}, which you replace yourself ([L0607, undated]). [L0606, undated; L0607, undated]
- Placeholder mapping in the flow templates: [L0606, undated]
  - {{ first_name }} and [NAME TAG] take the subscriber's first name from the Klaviyo profile.
  - [DISCOUNT CODE] takes the discount code of that flow.
  - [BUTTON] is a call-to-action button linking to the store.
  - [CART] is the block listing the products left in the abandoned cart.
  - [BEST SELLERS] is a product block of top sellers.
  - [REVIEWS] is a block of customer reviews.
  - [TIMER] is a countdown.
  - [SIGNATURE] is the sender sign-off.
- Dynamic content may not appear fully in email previews, so check the preview of each campaign before sending. [L0864, 2025-26]
