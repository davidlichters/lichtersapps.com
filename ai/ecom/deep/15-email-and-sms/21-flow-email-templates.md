#### Flow Setup and Review Block

Setting up the flows in Klaviyo: [L0606, undated]

1. Create or open the Klaviyo account.
2. Create the abandoned cart flow, the welcome flow and the post-purchase flow.
3. Load the ready-made team template for each flow into the matching flow, one email per step, in the order given below.
4. Replace every blank (______) and every bracketed placeholder with real store content, your real discount code and your real links.
5. Check the offer, the deadline wording and the review block in each email before turning the flow on.

- Place the review block at the end of each email, after the call to action. Reviews act as social proof and reinforce the decision without competing with the discount code. [L0606, undated]
- Keep the stock line only if it is accurate, and keep the 100% satisfaction line only if the store offers that guarantee. [L0606, undated]
- Every flow email should show the brand logo and footer, which Klaviyo takes from the brand assets once they are set up. A footer can also hold social media icons, which are added as their own section in the email builder. [L0607, undated]

### Claims, Accuracy and Launch Checks

- Vague superlatives and invented numbers such as "150% more awesome" or a "100% satisfaction guarantee" must not stay in the copy unless they are real. Replace them with a specific, verifiable benefit, and only mention a guarantee the store actually offers in its return policy. [L0606, undated]
- Leaving blanks (______), [NAME TAG], [BUTTON], [REVIEWS] or [DISCOUNT CODE] unreplaced before launch sends visible placeholder text to customers. Check a test send of every email in the flow before switching it on. [L0606, undated]
- An email inside a flow that is still in draft status is not sent, so an unwanted email can be kept safe by leaving it in draft. [L1297, 2023]
- Before using a bought email template, check that it works on mobile and across all email clients. Look-alike designs can fail in real inboxes even when they look good on a marketplace page. [L1263, 2025-26]

### Plain Text vs HTML

#### Positions in the Sources

| Position | Source | When it applies |
|---|---|---|
| HTML is preferred over plain text, though the gap matters less than it used to. HTML allows clickable buttons and a logo, so use it, but keep the layout simple. | [L0569, 2021-22] | When you need buttons and a logo. |
| Simple text-based emails are preferred for small and medium e-commerce stores. HTML remains useful when images must be shown. | [L0602, 2021-22] | Small and medium stores; HTML when images must be shown. |
| Write emails mostly as plain text with few or no images. The wording sells, and many email clients do not display images. | [L0572, 2014] | Default writing mode. |
| Plain text emails that look like a message from a friend can perform well, even compared with heavily designed templates. | [L0923, 2015] | Any list used for many offers. |
| For a new store, start with text-led emails that use a few images, since they are more likely to reach the primary inbox. Compare open and click results in a test rather than relying on taste. | [L0607, undated] | New stores; test to decide. |

- Heavily designed HTML emails are typically associated with promotional mailings and are not how people usually write to each other. [L0602, 2021-22]
- Text-based emails with a few images are more likely to land in the primary inbox. Image-heavy emails from the start risk the promotions tab, where they are opened less. [L0607, undated]
- Heavily formatted HTML emails may not display well across all inboxes, devices and email clients. Some evidence suggests they are more likely to be classed as promotional and land in the Promotions tab in Gmail or in spam. [L0602, 2021-22]
- Large big-box e-commerce stores often send three or four heavily formatted HTML emails per day with many offers. The author does not consider this the best approach for small to medium e-commerce businesses. [L0602, 2021-22]
- Elaborate HTML design is unnecessary for a simple email with one CTA. There is no evidence that heavily formatted emails achieve higher click-through rates, and some evidence points the other way. [L0602, 2021-22]
- Overloading HTML emails with decorative elements, many images and many colours is a mistake. Email providers may strip out part of the code, and the layout can break on some devices, so the email may look broken or make less sense. [L0569, 2021-22]
- Build email layouts with HTML and CSS instead of image banners. Images can be blocked, so the layout should work without them. [L0572, 2014]
- Include links to a few website categories in most emails. Recipients who are not interested in the email's topic may still click through to the shop. [L0572, 2014]
- Email HTML is not web HTML. It is still largely stuck in 1990s-era techniques and renders differently in each email host, for example Gmail, Yahoo and Hotmail render the same design differently. [L1263, 2025-26]
- Design and code emails for the lowest common denominator across all email clients. Learn what each client supports and does not support before building. [L1263, 2025-26]

### Images, Colour and Visual Layout

- Use images sparingly and choose only the best product images, such as the product itself or the product in use. Many images raise the risk of spam-folder placement and of formatting being stripped, and heavy HTML can look like scam emails. Product emails benefit from one product image, while many email types need no images at all. [L0569, 2021-22]
- There is no reliable evidence that emails containing images perform better than text-only emails. Add images only when they serve the message, such as a product photo, and choose your best product images rather than decorative graphics. [L0569, 2021-22]
- A combined image of a picture and short text, such as a collage of customer testimonial screenshots, can be made in Canva and placed as an image block in a Klaviyo email. Used in abandoned cart emails, this adds social proof without making the whole email an image. [L0573, undated]
- Include pinnable product images in emails so subscribers can save products to Pinterest when they are not ready to buy. This keeps the product visible and gives the customer a place to return to later. [L1136, 2014]
- Put the text on a white background so it is easy to read. A column of text with a different coloured background on either side is acceptable as long as the area behind the text stays white. [L0569, 2021-22]
- In a campaign email, white is a clean base for product-focused emails. The background colour is set in the editor's styling options, and offers, text blocks and product feeds are added as extra components on top of this background. [L0573, undated]
- Limit email colours to your brand colours, usually one or two primary colours already used on the website. Use these colours when you need to add colour to an email. [L0569, 2021-22]
- Apply one consistent email template to all emails so that subscribers recognise every message as coming from your brand immediately. [L0569, 2021-22]
- Consistent design builds on trust that already exists with subscribers, while changing styles and layouts from email to email creates doubt about who is sending. Keep the design recognisable across all emails. [L0569, 2021-22]
- Email design should stay simple and not be overthought. Most results come from strong copy, a clear message and a call to action that is easy to identify. [L0569, 2021-22]

### Klaviyo Editor Structure

- The Klaviyo email editor builds content from sections and blocks, like Shopify theme editing. A button block cannot sit between two blocks of text directly. Add two text blocks and insert the button between them. [L0573, undated; L0607, undated]
- In a campaign, add a product feed: click Product > Create product feed, then show a collection, best-selling products or most viewed products over a set period such as 90 days. This promotes the main products during an event. [L0573, undated; L0607, undated]
- The dynamic product section works the same way. Under Products, click Create product feed, then choose to show best-selling or most viewed products over a set time range, for example 90 days. [L0607, undated; L0573, undated]

### Modular Templates and Content Blocks

#### Building Blocks

- Email modules work like building blocks. Each one can be reused and stacked in any order inside an email. The same modular approach applies to both campaigns and flows. [L0610, 2025-26]
- Content blocks and modules are the individual parts of an email, while a template is the complete email assembled from them. Using one shared set of content blocks and templates keeps campaigns and flows consistent. [L0610, 2025-26]
- Build a system of reusable modular content blocks rather than one individually designed email. The same blocks can then be used for every campaign and every flow you send in the future. [L1263, 2025-26]
- Some modules, such as M19, are universal and nearly every brand can use them. Those are the first modules to build in a library. [L0610, 2025-26]
- With a ready module library, done-for-you clients can build an email in three to five minutes instead of designing each email from scratch. [L0610, 2025-26]
- All modules are tested on mobile and in dark mode, so emails built from them render correctly without extra checking. Building from tested blocks lets you produce emails quickly with consistent results. [L0610, 2025-26]
- The always-on programme covers both campaigns and automated flows and should remain editable in the email platform. Sliced-image designs look attractive but cannot scale into flows and break in real inboxes. [L1263, 2025-26]

#### Reusing a Design Across a Flow

Reusing one Klaviyo email design across a flow: [L0477, undated]

1. Build the first email from a pre-made template, editing the mobile version first.
2. Click the arrow next to edit email, choose save as template and name it after your store.
3. For each further email, click the arrow next to edit email, choose change template, drag and drop, open My templates, select the saved store template and click Use template.
4. Replace the text with your prepared copy and set the email from draft to live.
