### Gain, Logic, Fear Sequences

**Order and purpose**
- Each promotion's email sequence follows the order Gain, Logic, Fear: first the benefit, then the rational reason to act, then the fear of missing out. The series is meant to move the reader through different emotions that lead to a purchase. [L1222, 2025+]
- The three-email sequence can follow the gain, logic, and fear structure: the first email shows the benefit, the second gives the reason it works, and the third shows the cost of not acting. The structure is an older copywriting formula, and AI tools can help adapt it to an e-commerce store. [L0857, 2025]

**Gain-logic-fear sequence (2024 pattern)**
- Email 1 states what the buyer gains (the benefit plus the offer). Email 2 gives a rational reason the offer is the smart choice. Email 3 says the offer is ending now. The sequence moves the reader from desire to justification to urgency, which is why it converts consistently across promotion types. [L1224, 2025+]
  - Example A (kitchen knives, Black Friday): "Get 20% off the premium knife set, delivered before the holiday" / "Why one quality knife set replaces five cheap ones" / "Final hours: the 20% code ends at midnight". [L1224, 2025+]
  - Example B (pet beds, Cyber Monday): "Cyber Monday: 15% off orthopedic pet beds with fast shipping" / "Why a supportive bed reduces night-time restlessness for older dogs" / "Last chance: the 15% code expires tonight". [L1224, 2025+]

**Formula with examples (2025)**
- A three-email promotion sequence follows the gain, logic, fear pattern. Email one states what the buyer gains, email two explains why this offer is a sensible choice, and email three says the offer is ending, so the timing creates urgency and the sequence works across most promotion types. [L1287, 2025]
  - Example A: Gain, 15 percent off plus delivery before the holiday weekend. Logic, a gift that arrives on time saves a last-minute store trip. Fear, the 15 percent code expires Sunday at midnight. [L1287, 2025]
  - Example B: Gain, a free carry strap with every yoga mat ordered this week. Logic, the buyer avoids a shopping trip to a sports store. Fear, the top color is limited and the strap offer ends Friday. [L1287, 2025]

**Closing-email patterns**
- Closing-reminder email: the subject line says the discount ends at a specific time, and the body gives the code and a reason to act today. Reuse the same subject line as the headline of the remarketing ads. Example new subject lines: "Last Call: 20% Off Ends Tonight" and "Final Hours to Lock In Your Holiday Price". [L1223, 2024+]
- Buy-by-date email: the message states the last order date that still guarantees delivery before a holiday. The reason is the delivery window, not the discount. Example new subject lines: "Order by Dec 13 to Get It Before Christmas" and "Your Last Chance to Ship Before the Holidays". [L1223, 2024+]

**Remarketing during an open window (high-ticket)**
1. Send one pre-promotion email before a high-ticket offer starts. [L1205, 2020-26]
2. Send one email per day during the promotion window. [L1205, 2020-26]
3. On the closing day, send at least two reminder emails. [L1205, 2020-26]
4. Run Facebook and Google remarketing ads to the same people who receive the emails, for maximum reach. [L1205, 2020-26]

**Remarketing timing**
- Start retargeting ads after roughly two to three weeks of traffic, once you have stats to target from. [L0692, 2023]
- During an open promotion window, run remarketing alongside the emails, as in the procedure above. [L1205, 2020-26]

### Subject Lines by Holiday

**Formula and structure**
- Holiday subject line formula: event name or seasonal cue, then a concrete savings promise, then an optional emoji or short action word. The event cue shows why the email matters now, and the savings promise gives a reason to open it. Examples in the same pattern: "Spring Clearance Starts Today: Save on Your Favorites" and "Summer Sale Is Live, Up to 30% Off Our Picks". [L0547, 2024+]
- Write a separate set of subject lines for each seasonal moment (Halloween, Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Christmas, New Year) instead of reusing one generic sale line. Each moment has its own vocabulary and should be matched to its own campaign. [L0548, 2024+]
- Across a holiday campaign, mix three types of subject lines: direct deal statements, urgency lines, and mood or holiday lines. Varying the type keeps recipients from tuning out a repeated message format. [L0548, 2024+]
- Use one emoji per subject line as an accent, placed at the start or end, and choose it to match the season (ghost, cart, gift, fire, laptop). Skip emojis when the brand or audience does not suit them. [L0548, 2024+]

**Subject line patterns by holiday**
- Halloween: combine a spooky or playful theme (ghosts, treats versus tricks, monster-style wordplay) with a savings promise, often with one ghost or pumpkin emoji. The theme makes the offer feel fun and fits the season, so the discount reads as a treat. Examples: "Spooky Season Steals: Up to 30% Off Everything 🎃" and "Witch Better Believe These Deals Are Here 🧙". [L0548, 2024+]
- Black Friday: use the words "Black Friday" with a savings or deal promise and a sense of now, such as early access, the biggest sale, or a last chance. Exclusivity ("just for you", "early access") and time-bound language make the email feel important. Examples: "Black Friday Early Access: 25% Off Starts Right Now" and "Last Chance: Black Friday Prices End Tonight 🛍️". [L0548, 2024+]
- Cyber Monday: name the day and pair it with either a countdown of hours left or a sitewide percentage off, with "Extended" used when the sale runs longer. The online focus (laptop or phone emoji, "sitewide") signals that the whole store is in the sale. Examples: "Cyber Monday: 12 Hours Left for 40% Off Sitewide 💻" and "Cyber Monday Extended: Our Deals Stay Live Through Tonight". [L0548, 2024+]
- Christmas: blend holiday warmth (gifts, Santa, decorations, festive phrasing) with a savings message and a deadline-style phrase such as "before they're gone" or "countdown". The festive tone builds gift-buying urgency. Examples: "Santa's Gift List Is Ready: Save 20% on Top Picks 🎁" and "Christmas Countdown: Grab Your Favorites Before Stock Runs Out 🎄". [L0548, 2024+]
- New Year: frame the offer as a fresh start, using resolutions, "new year, new" phrasing, or "start the year" wording plus a savings promise. This ties the purchase to a personal goal, which makes the offer feel purposeful. Examples: "New Year, Better Savings: Start 2027 With 15% Off 🎉" and "Your Resolution Starts Here: Save Big on Your Favorite Brands". [L0548, 2024+]

**Placeholders and dates**
- Fill every placeholder before sending: X%, XX% and similar must become the real discount, NAME must become the merge field of your email tool, and any year must be the current year. A leftover placeholder in a live email is a visible error that damages trust. [L0547, 2024+]
- Use placeholders in subject lines for the discount percentage (X%) and the year (20XX), and for personalization use a NAME field that is filled per recipient. Replace every placeholder with the real figure before the email is sent. [L0548, 2024+]
- Do not leave an old year in a seasonal subject line, such as an outdated "Welcome 20XX" phrase from a previous year. Update the year in every subject line for the current season before sending. [L0548, 2024+]

**Reuse and extensions**
- Do not reuse the same subject line across different events or repeat an identical line in the next campaign. Tie wording to the specific offer of each event so recipients do not see the same message twice. [L0547, 2024+]
- When a sale is extended, send a follow-up with "Extended" wording instead of repeating the original subject line. The change signals a new reason to open the email and keeps the offer credible. [L0548, 2024+]

**Ready-made list**
- When AI-written email headlines are weak, use a proven list of holiday subject lines grouped by holiday (about 97 lines covering Halloween, Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Christmas, and New Year's). Use them as email subject lines or ad headlines. [L1224, 2025+; L1287, 2025]

### Scarcity, Urgency and Teasers

**Urgency must be real**
- Urgency must be real. Valid reasons include a webinar starting tomorrow, a limited number of seats, a limited stock of a physical product, or a video being taken offline. For evergreen products, use a special sale that ends soon or a coupon that expires in 24 hours. [L0523, 2015+]
- Fake urgency backfires and destroys credibility once subscribers notice. Invented deadlines teach readers to ignore future emails. Only use deadlines you can honor. [L0523, 2015+]
- Do not claim urgency that is not real. Lines such as "only 4 hours to go", "last day", or "extended" must match the actual sale timer or end date. Use "before stock runs out" only when inventory is truly limited, because subscribers who find a fake deadline stop trusting later messages. [L0547, 2024+; L0548, 2024+]
- Urgency phrases such as "hours left", "ends tonight", or "last day" should only be used when the deadline is real. False countdowns damage trust and make later emails less effective. [L0548, 2024+; L0547, 2024+]

**Deadlines and expiry**
- Within each flow, every later email shortens the deadline: 3 days, then 48 hours, then 24 hours or a few hours. The repeated shrinking window creates escalating urgency without changing the offer itself. Examples: "Offer ends in 72 hours" followed by "Offer ends in 24 hours"; "Your code expires this weekend" followed by "Last chance: expires at midnight tonight". [L0606, undated]
- Set the send delays so that each email arrives while its deadline statement is still true. The lesson does not give fixed delays, so the timing must be derived from the deadlines in the copy. [L0606, undated]
- If an email says the offer ends at midnight, name the time zone and make the coupon expire at exactly that time. A midnight claim that differs from the real expiry damages trust the same way a false stock claim does. [L0606, undated]

**Stock claims**
- Scarcity claims such as "selling out like crazy", "inventory is running low", or "we might not be able to fulfill your order" must be true for the store at the time of sending. False stock claims damage trust and can create refund and complaint problems. [L0606, undated]
- Use scarcity messaging in Black Friday campaigns, such as: "This exclusive discount releases to the public tomorrow. Act now to lock in the early-bird price before the public gets access." This creates FOMO without over-exaggerating the offer. [L0308, 2023]

**Teasers**
- Teasing a larger promotion:
  1. Send a teaser email to the broadcast list a few days before the offer starts, for example on the Friday before a Monday start. [L1204, 2022]
  2. Hint at the offer without giving the full details, so people start thinking about the purchase. [L1204, 2022]
  3. Send the full offer at the start of the window. [L1204, 2022]
- Show a product image with a blurred price a day before launch to create curiosity (e.g., "9.99"). [L0307, 2023+]
- For high-ticket offers, send a single pre-promotion email before the offer starts, saying a big offer is coming and asking the audience to check their inbox first thing on the start day at a specific time. Example: for leather sofas averaging $2,500, send the email the Friday before a Monday-to-Friday promotion. This prepares buyers for a larger purchase. [L1205, 2020-26]

### AI Prompts for Sequences

**Building the prompt first (procedure)**
1. Write one message that includes the store domain, promotion dates, discount code, percentage off, delivery guarantee, and the send date of each email. [L1224, 2025+]
2. Ask the AI to first write the best prompt for this task instead of asking for the emails directly. [L1224, 2025+]
3. Review and edit that generated prompt for your own store. [L1224, 2025+]
4. Send the edited prompt, then ask for the sequence using the gain-logic-fear structure. [L1224, 2025+]
5. Use a fresh, customized prompt for each promotion rather than one template for all of them. [L1224, 2025+]

**Remarketing and sequence build (procedure)**
1. Set the promotion's start date, end date, discount, and discount code. [L1286, 2025]
2. Add a delivery guarantee if the promotion has a deadline such as Christmas. [L1286, 2025]
3. Write a 3-email sequence and send one email on the first promotion day, one in the middle, and one on the last promotion day (for a Monday to Friday window: Monday, Wednesday, Friday). [L1286, 2025]
4. Write remarketing ad copy for Meta and Google Display based on the same email copy and offer details. [L1286, 2025]
