## Think creatively and solve problems

### Diagnosing a problem before fixing it

- Find the root cause before working on secondary issues such as page layout or copy. If the root cause stays unsolved, changes to other parts of the business bring little benefit. [L1254, 2020-26]
- Treat a business problem like a blocked stream: identify what blocks the flow, remove that block, and the rest of the business starts working. Judge which other changes are worth making only after that. [L1254, 2020-26]
- Changing five or more things at once hides which change actually fixed the problem and often leaves the real cause in place. Test the most likely root cause first. [L1254, 2020-26]
- When a plan does not work, adjust it, address the problem directly and push through instead of holding on to the original plan. [L0829, 2019]
- When a store is struggling, take responsibility for the cause, such as weak ads or too few suppliers, instead of blaming outside factors. Fixing the controllable causes is the main lever. [L0832, 2019]
- Successful entrepreneurs do not panic when a problem appears. They stay calm, break the problem into steps and solve them one by one. [L0887, 2026]
- Long hours do not automatically produce results. What counts is working on the right tasks. If effort brings no progress, check whether the problem lies in product choice, ad angle or offer rather than in how hard you work. [L0887, 2026]
- Limit the time spent on a single problem and keep working on the business in parallel. Spending hours each day on a payment dispute reduced the time and focus available for the product and the company. [L1128, 2023]
- A common beginner mistake is panicking when something breaks, then sending many messages to coaches or the community and waiting for replies while doing nothing. Asking AI first and trying to solve the problem yourself avoids this loss of time. [L0887, 2026]

Handling a problem in your business:

1. Describe the problem to AI with the platform, theme, exact symptom and any numbers.
2. Try to understand the answer and apply the suggested fix.
3. If you are still stuck after this, post your question in the community or ask a coach, with what you already tried.

This order builds independence and speeds up your growth. [L0887, 2026]

Example: a reset plan for a stalled store, given by AI, covers 3 days: launch 1 new product per day, test at least 3-5 creatives per product and spend small amounts purely to collect data. Kill products quickly when they show no traction. [L0887, 2026]

### Using AI as a thinking partner

- Talking through a problem in writing with AI forces you to organise your thoughts and reveals the bigger picture. Use AI to clarify decisions before acting on them. [L0887, 2026]
- AI can be used for personal problems too, such as low energy or demotivation at work. Describing the situation to it gives you an outside perspective you may not see yourself. [L0887, 2026]
- Use AI tools only at named steps where they measurably shorten the task, and state for each step when AI should be used. Applying AI to every step without that decision adds effort without saving time. [L0555, 2026+]
- Long AI chats drift and forget earlier brand instructions. Keep one restartable thread that holds the primer and the prompts, and re-prime it when the output stops matching the brand. [L0567, 2025-26]
- Use Google Gemini instead of ChatGPT to review policy pages for Google Ads and Merchant Center compliance. Gemini is Google's own model and is the better fit for checking content against Google's own policy rules. [L0724, 2020-26]

### Challenging AI recommendations

- Challenge AI-generated SEO and strategy recommendations before acting on them, especially big decisions about content direction. Ask for the strongest argument for and against each suggestion, and expect to drop some items from the list. This matters most for premium brands, where AI-written content can drift toward a cheap tone. [L1269, 2025-26]
- Use this prompt to test a recommendation:

```
Before we act on this recommendation, give me the strongest steelman argument for doing it and the strongest steelman argument against it.
Then give an honest verdict: is this necessary for our goal right now, and which items from the list would you cut?
Flag anything that does not fit our brand positioning, price level or target customer.
Keep the verdict short and tie every action to either a new visitor or a new customer.
```

[L1269, 2025-26]

- The same AI audit run repeatedly can produce a different priority order each time. Treat its ranking as a starting point and not a fixed plan. Reframe every suggestion by asking whether it will actually move traffic or revenue in the next weeks. [L1269, 2025-26]

### Approaching every new problem with fresh thinking

- Every new product, market and advertisement is a new problem. Solutions that worked before cannot be reused without copying, and success should push you to search again rather than to reopen old files. [L0330, undated]
- Analysis is a method of asking the right questions, making rough guesses from past successes, setting checkpoints and letting the problem dictate the answer. It can be learned in the same way as grammar, and it has to be repeated for each new product and market because fixed rules go stale quickly. [L0330, undated]
- The right approach is found through study and testing, and sometimes by accident. Expect to try many versions before one works, and keep learning from the results. [L1369, undated]
- You do not need to be a creative genius to start. What you need is to recognize what already works and copy its model. [L1252, 2026]

### Research before writing

- Good advertising results from deep research. Expert writers often spend weeks reading and testing before writing a single ad. Hard work and preparation separate successful ads from guesses. [L1261, undated]
- Motivation research helps the copywriter discover hidden desires, the taboos that limit how they can be expressed, the splits within a market, and early reactions to the wording of a headline. A research finding is a direction, not a headline. It tells you where not to go and the general area of the solution. [L0330, undated]
- Before scripting any ad, the seller must know the product inside out, including its features, the benefits they create, and which goal or pain each benefit addresses. Without that product knowledge, no script should be written. [L1425, undated]
- Before scripting, the seller must also understand the target audience's wants, needs, pain points, where they want to go and what they want to escape from. The ad must make the viewer feel the seller understands them and is on their side. [L1425, undated]
- The method described works for problem-solving products, which is most products, where the buyer is trying to get from point A to point B, reach a goal or escape pain. [L1425, undated]
- Customers arrive at an e-commerce store because they want to solve a problem, and that problem is the real reason they buy. Copy should start from the buyer's problem, not from the product's features. [L1157, 2021-22]
- Procedure for testing a concept before scaling:

1. Run a deep-dive research on Amazon reviews and Reddit threads for your niche and fill in the research form: pain points, exact customer language, USP, and ICP.
2. Write one clear main message per concept, built from what real users said.
3. Build one or two concepts with 3-5 creatives each. Use statics if you have not yet reached the proof-of-concept gate; use VSL once you hit $75-$100 per day.
4. Launch in a testing CBO, run it for 3 days, then read website purchases and ROAS.
5. Fold the winners into your main CBO.

[L1195, 2026]

- In e-commerce, a store does not need the best copy in the world to win. Beating most competitors' copy is enough to gain an edge, since many online stores have weak copy. [L1523, 2021-22]

### Planning an ad before writing

- Planning an ad can take weeks or months, and the result may be only five or ten words. Those words carry most of the ad's value. If they are right the ad can work, and if they are wrong nothing written after them will save it. Spend the time on the idea before writing body copy. [L0330, undated]
- The unique idea for a product lies in the one element that makes its product and market different. Find that element through research into the market and study of the product, not by borrowing a formula. [L0330, undated]
- Advertising is not about entertaining or pleasing the creator, but about the buyer's interests, so always write from the viewpoint of the typical buyer. Think of one real person who is likely to buy, not the mass audience. [L1261, undated]
- Advertisers who sell to the buyer's interests, not the seller's, succeed far more often, because the seller's view tends to focus on the product rather than the buyer's needs. Imagine the buyer's face-to-face reaction to every line. [L1261, undated]
- Give each letter one specific goal and make every sentence move the reader one step nearer to it. If a sentence does not support the goal, remove it. [L1369, undated]
- Learn the rules as a checklist until they become second nature, then write from the feel of the message rather than from the checklist. Mechanical rules build the letter, but the idea behind it makes it work. [L1369, undated]
- Procedure to plan a new ad before writing:

1. State the problem in plain words, for example that you want to sell this product.
2. Restate the problem in a different way, for example that you want to introduce the unique aspects of the product to the prospect.
3. Restate it again in a way that describes the buyer's experience, for example that the product should be easy to buy and seem fun.
4. Choose the restatement that feels closest to the ad you want to write.
5. List several big ideas or concepts for that restatement, then pick the one or two that make the most sense.
6. Visualize how the concept fits into the ad and check it against the restatement.
7. Stop and incubate.
8. Write the headline, then the subheadline, then the first sentence.
9. Block-diagram the finished ad to check its flow.

[L1366, undated]

- Persuasion principles: Cialdini's six principles of persuasion are reciprocity, scarcity/urgency/FOMO, authority, social proof, commitment and consistency, and liking. Getting these right makes an ad strongly persuasive. [L1425, undated]
- Reciprocity: giving value first, such as free ebooks in exchange for an email, free content, or value delivered up front, makes people more likely to buy when the offer arrives. [L1425, undated]
- Authority: people trust experts and people with an air of power, so being featured in a magazine or endorsed by a doctor increases persuasion because the audience assumes they know what they are talking about. [L1425, undated]
- Commitment and consistency: people want to stay consistent with their identity. Asking for small commitments, such as an email signup, followed by a small purchase and then a larger one, is easier than asking for a big sale up front, which can overwhelm people. [L1425, undated]
- Liking is built from three parts: similarity, genuine compliments and cooperation. Similarity signals safety to the viewer, so the brand should show that it was founded by people like the audience. Compliments must be genuine, and cooperation means positioning the brand as a helper that moves the customer toward their goal, not as an obstacle. [L1425, undated]
- Value proposition formula (Hormozi's value equation): Value = dream outcome × perceived likelihood of achievement, divided by time delay and effort and sacrifice. Increase the buyer's trust in reaching the outcome, then reduce time, effort and sacrifice, so the product seems quick, easy and costless. [L1425, undated]

### Incubation: letting ideas form

- Problem solving through incubation:

1. Think intensely about the problem and gather all the relevant information, including the possible options.
2. Define the desired end result clearly.
3. Stop working on the problem and put off the decision until later, dismissing it from your conscious mind.
4. Before sleep, tell yourself you want the answer or idea by morning, and keep a notepad by the bed.
5. Write down any idea or solution as soon as you wake, before it fades.

[L1206, 2015+]

- Creative solutions and insights often appear when the conscious mind is relaxed or engaged in something else, such as a routine activity or a walk. Continuing to struggle and worry during that time usually delays the solution. [L1206, 2015+]
- Forcing a solution with strained effort tends to jam the automatic problem-solving process, which then produces anxiety instead of answers. Trust the process and act on the best available information instead. [L1206, 2015+]
- Incubation procedure for copy:

1. Gather all material about the product and the customer.
2. Think through what you have read, and optionally jot down headlines, key points, the product's nature and the strongest reasons customers would buy.
3. Stop working on the project and do something pleasant and unrelated, without thinking about the copy.
4. When an idea appears, write it down at once.
5. Write a first draft from the material your mind has organized, without correcting spelling or grammar.

[L1366, undated]

- Incubation runs in the background even when you do not work on the project. Thinking about the project again interrupts it and weakens the result. Give the project real breaks rather than forcing continuous effort. [L1366, undated]
- Incubation speed and quality depend on time, creative orientation, environment and ego. Some pressure, such as deadlines or pride in the result, speeds up the process, but too little pressure slows it down. [L1366, undated]
- Keep the creative team separate from routine operations. Copywriters need freedom to incubate, and their output drops when they are held to the same visible schedule as operational staff. [L1366, undated]

### Lateral thinking

- Lateral thinking exercise for a stuck ad concept:

1. Pick a random source of words, such as a shuffled set of words or a dictionary.
2. Pull out the first three words you see.
3. Force a connection between those three words and the product you are selling.
4. Build an ad concept from the connection, keeping the selling goal in mind.

[L1366, undated]

- When solving a marketing problem, step outside the original frame by relating it to something unrelated. A solution that looks impossible inside the problem often appears when the problem is connected to a different context. [L1366, undated]

### Constraints and assumed limits

- Limitations force focused decisions. Setting a firm constraint on time, budget, materials or scope is a practical way to beat creative block. Waiting for unlimited resources usually stops the work entirely. [L1343, undated]
- Tight limits make creative choices easier, such as one color for a product ad, a 60-second maximum for a video script, a fixed 100 USD test budget for a campaign, or a set vocabulary for a product description. Open-ended options often cause paralysis, while a hard boundary forces a decision. [L1343, undated]
- Test assumed constraints instead of accepting them. Assumed constraints block good ideas. Common self-imposed limits include believing a product cannot sell at a high price or through mail, or that a certain audience will not respond. These limits should be tested rather than accepted. Disproved limits: a $190,000 limit on a used airplane was beaten by a $240,000 sale in 10 days, and a $600 product sold more than 3,000 units through mail order. [L1366, undated]
- Experiment freely with low-stakes projects before you have public attention, because attention brings expectations that limit experimentation. Use the early period to test ideas without worrying about audience reactions or revenue pressure. [L1343, undated]

### Practice, daily output and drafting

- Consistent small output compounds. Writing one page per day adds up to about 365 pages in a year, enough for a full novel. Measure progress by daily output over months, not by single big wins. [L1343, undated]
- Practice produces the largest gains in copywriting. Write every day in small forms such as letters, postcards, articles and ads, because volume builds skill faster than study alone. [L1366, undated]
- Accept that a first draft will be weak. Judge the draft by its emotional release, not by how it reads, because the real quality comes from editing. [L1366, undated]
- Draft with the intuitive, emotional side of the mind, then edit with the analytical side. Ignore spelling and grammar while drafting, and switch to logic when editing. [L1366, undated]
- In editing, refine copy so it expresses exactly the intended meaning with the fewest words. You may rearrange sentences, cut low-value words, substitute stronger words or add words for clarity, while keeping the same emotional feel. [L1366, undated]
- Waiting to know who you are or what your brand stands for before you start producing keeps you stuck in planning. Identity and style usually emerge from doing the work regularly. [L1343, undated]
- Base your creative output on subjects that genuinely interest you, not only on what you already know, because work drawn only from familiar experience tends to be dull. In e-commerce this means choosing products, angles and offers you would actually want to buy or use yourself. [L1343, undated]
- Side projects and experiments often produce the best results, so keep several projects running and switch between them when one stalls. Returning to a paused project later is a productive way to deal with a lack of motivation. [L1343, undated]
- When unsure what to do next, ask which option would make a better story for your work or career. This question helps you choose a direction that is interesting and worth pursuing. [L1343, undated]
- Staying relevant after a viral win is the hardest part. You need to produce many new video ideas every day to keep views steady. [L1429, 2022]

### Idea capture

- Idea capture method:

1. When a new idea appears during work, write it down in the Notes column or as a card without leaving the current tracking interface.
2. Return to the current task right away.
3. Review the Notes panels regularly so recorded ideas are not lost.

[L1364, 2021-22]

- Writing down an idea removes it from working memory, so focus can return to the current task without losing the idea. Ideas that are not recorded are often forgotten by the end of the day. [L1364, 2021-22]
- Feeling stuck or blank when brainstorming is normal and does not mean you lack the ability to find a market. Ideas often arrive later when you are not forcing them, so keep the list open and add ideas as they come. [L0996, 2021-22]

### Influences and reference collecting

- Almost every new idea is a combination of earlier ideas, so original work comes from recombining existing influences rather than starting from nothing. Treating influence as normal, not as a weakness, frees you to build on what already works. [L1343, undated]
- The quality of your reference inputs caps the quality of your output, so collect selectively and only keep sources that genuinely appeal to you. Indiscriminate collecting dilutes your taste and makes your work average. [L1343, undated]
- Influence tree procedure:

1. Pick one creator or brand you admire and study everything about their work.
2. Identify three people or brands that this creator was influenced by and study them the same way.
3. Repeat this chain as far back as you can go, building a tree of influences.
4. Start your own work as a new branch that grows from that tree.

[L1343, undated]

- Copy-and-amplify procedure:

1. Copy the work of the creators you admire closely enough to understand how it is built.
2. Note exactly where your version falls short of the original.
3. Amplify those differences, since they show what you add that is unique.
4. Develop that difference into your own product, ad or content.

[L1343, undated]

- Experience and broad knowledge give the copywriter more parts to combine into new ideas. Curiosity, varied hobbies (interests), travel and learning new skills all add material that later becomes an unexpected angle. [L1366, undated]
- Once you internalize the no-brand-loyalty principle, product opportunities become visible everywhere: in shopping malls, home interiors, magazines, and television. This accelerates your ability to identify viable dropshipping niches. [L0319, 2020-26]
- Copying what already works is a legitimate starting point. Recognize the model behind a successful product, ad or store and adapt it, then build your difference on top of it. [L1252, 2026]

### Working environment: analog and digital

- Computers are strong for editing and publishing but weak for generating ideas, because deleting and perfecting starts too early. Generate ideas by hand or in a sketchbook first, and bring in the computer only once the thinking is mostly done. [L1343, undated]
- Setting up two separate workstations:

1. Set up two separate workstations: an analog station with no electronics and a digital station with your computer, scanner and other devices.
2. Stock the analog station with paper, pens, markers, sticky notes and index cards, which can be bought for about $10.
3. Generate and sketch ideas at the analog station, standing up if possible, and pin ideas on walls to find patterns.
4. Move to the digital station to execute, edit and publish the ideas.
5. When you lose momentum, return to the analog station and keep exploring.

[L1343, undated]

### Walks, breaks and stepping away

- When stuck on a problem, leave the computer and take a walk, go to the gym, or do a routine task, because solutions often come when you are not actively searching. Searching online is still useful, but do not replace stepping away with constant thinking about the issue. [L1128, 2023]
- Schedule unstructured time with no goal, since boredom and routine tasks such as washing dishes or walking often trigger new ideas. Keeping a daily routine matters more than finding large blocks of free time. [L1343, undated]
