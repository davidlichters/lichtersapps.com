## Build beliefs, identity and self-image

### The chain from belief to result

- Your beliefs shape your results more than the actions you take right now. Results follow a chain in which beliefs shape thoughts, thoughts create feelings, feelings lead to actions, and actions produce results, so changing outcomes means changing the underlying beliefs. [L0831, 2019]
- The chain that produces results runs Programming, Thought, Feeling, Action, Result. Programming is the set of automatic beliefs and conditioning from the past, and it shapes every thought that follows. To change results you must change the programming at its root, not only the tactics you apply. [L1513, 2019]
- Most people try to improve results by adding skills or tactics while keeping the same underlying programming. This produces the same outcomes because actions still come from faulty automatic thoughts. Thoughts often arrive before any conscious decision, so they need deliberate review. [L1513, 2019]
- Skills such as niche selection, store building, copywriting, negotiation and paid advertising are actions that come out of beliefs. Beliefs about how to think as an entrepreneur matter more than any single skill, because faulty beliefs lead to poor actions even when the skills are sound. Inspired ideas also come from programming, so better beliefs produce better ideas. [L1513, 2019]
- Things have no meaning in themselves, because meaning is assigned by the person interpreting them. Only a few things are facts, while most of the rest is interpretation. Choose interpretations that empower you to act rather than ones that limit or discourage you. [L1513, 2019]
- Pain and pleasure are the primary drivers of human behavior. Master the ability to direct them consciously by creating deliberate mental associations: link discomfort with behaviors you want to eliminate, and positive reward with the outcomes you want to create. This conscious direction, rather than being unconsciously driven, is foundational to business success. [L0007, 2023]
- Treat your mindset as the foundation of the business: protect it from negative influences and sharpen it with deliberate practice. Use that mindset to guide every decision you make in the business. [L0631, 2026]

### Mindset as a limit on results

- In e-commerce, mindset usually limits results more than technical knowledge or strategy does. When execution stalls despite knowing the steps, the cause is often a mental habit rather than a missing skill. [L1042, 2026]
- Mindset is described as roughly 80 percent of what separates operators who succeed from those who do not. Attitude and the way problems are interpreted therefore matter more than tactics alone. [L1445, 2026]
- Tactics such as the latest ad targeting or platform strategy change quickly, while mindset determines long-term results. The more time you invest in personal growth and a business mindset, the less the tactics matter. [L0701, 2015]
- Cautious habits picked up from a risk-averse upbringing conflict with e-commerce, which rewards comfort with uncertainty. Expect the business to repeatedly test that comfort zone. [L1042, 2026]
- Early business mindset traps include thinking short-term, being easily distracted, expecting fast results, and expecting something for nothing. Recognizing these traits early and changing how you approach results is necessary before any success appears. [L1394, 2017+]
- Limiting beliefs about how far a product can grow can hold back revenue even when the numbers are strong. Speaking with experienced operators can help break through a growth plateau. [L1127, 2024]
- Your price is limited by how much you believe you deserve. If you struggle to raise prices, examine your self-worth and your belief about the value you deliver. [L0378, 2019+]
- Money flows along the path of least resistance, and limiting beliefs about earning money act as resistance that blocks it. Removing negative emotional attachments to money makes income easier to attract and keep. Money itself has no agenda or judgement about the owner. [L1513, 2019]
- Feelings of inferiority come mainly from measuring yourself against other people's standards, not from actual gaps in skill. Measuring yourself against your own standard, and accepting that everyone is unique, removes most of that feeling. [L1206, 2015+]
- Negative beliefs are usually built through repetition and emotion, so they are weakened by stronger, more consistent beliefs backed by emotion. Vividly picturing a desired outcome repeatedly generates good emotions in the same way that worry generates bad ones. [L1206, 2015+]
- A salesman was consistently earning about $5,000 a year regardless of how good or poor his territory was or how commissions changed. A consultant found the cause was the salesman's own belief that he was a $5,000-per-year man. When he was given a poor territory he worked hard to reach the figure, and in a good territory he slowed down once he got close to it. The case shows how a self-evaluation can limit earnings more than external conditions do. [L1206, 2015+]

### Self-image and identity

- Your self-image, meaning the mental picture you hold of the kind of person you are, sets the boundaries of what you can accomplish. Results and behavior tend to match that picture, so lasting change usually requires updating the self-image first. [L1206, 2015+]
- Positive thinking aimed at a single goal fails when it contradicts a negative belief about yourself. Such thinking only works once the underlying self-belief has shifted. [L1206, 2015+]
- Identity precedes results: decide who you want to become, label yourself as that person, and think, talk and act in line with it. Each daily thought, word and action is a vote for the person you are becoming. [L1041, 2023]
- Every thought, word, and action registers as a vote on the person you are becoming. Develop habits, through thoughts, words, and deeds, that align with your desired identity. [L0007, 2023]
- Build a deliberate identity that drives your behavior:
  1. Decide clearly on who you want to become. [L0007, 2023]
  2. Identify and explicitly label that identity. [L0007, 2023]
  3. Take consistent actions that reinforce it. [L0007, 2023]
  4. Actively seek and collect proof of the identity. [L0007, 2023]
- To take on the role of a designer, first learn beginner Photoshop techniques such as blending, then complete small finished projects. Each completed project serves as concrete proof that the new identity is real, which reinforces the behavior. [L1041, 2023]
- Adopt a gaming mindset toward business and personal development. Choose your desired character, continuously improve your skills, and understand that accumulating more skills, relationships, and resources unlocks larger opportunities and more significant games to play. [L0007, 2023]
- Think like a successful entrepreneur by asking what a successful person would do if they started from zero without that money, and reverse-engineer a lazy person's habits to find the opposite behavior. Successful people delay gratification and avoid overspending and emotional decisions. [L1041, 2023]
- Ignore opinions from people who have not done what you are trying to do. Their doubts often reflect their own beliefs about what is possible, not the reality of your goal. [L1041, 2023]
- Living the benefits of a successful business before the money arrives supports confidence. Waiting for the money first and only then trying to enjoy its benefits tends to erode confidence. [L0701, 2015]
- Confidence in the sense of self-efficacy means believing you can succeed in specific situations and believing in your own self-worth. It is a belief, not the amount of activity you produce. [L0701, 2015]
- When your business persona conflicts with your real self, stress builds and confidence falls. Aligning both into one consistent identity restores confidence without needing tricks or forced self-presentation. [L0701, 2015]
- Treat yourself with the same kindness you would show a good friend: avoid harsh self-criticism and reward good work with something positive. [L1041, 2023]
- When you feel guilt about an action your reasoning says is not wrong:
  1. Stop and name the cause of the feeling. [L1206, 2015+]
  2. Examine that cause in detail and test whether it holds up. [L1206, 2015+]
  3. If it is absurd, state clearly why and reject it. [L1206, 2015+]
  4. Whenever a similar irrational thought comes up later, examine and reject it the same way. [L1206, 2015+]

### Role models

- Identify people you admire, study their mentality, habits, and behaviors deeply, and emulate their actions and approaches. This accelerates skill development. [L0007, 2023]
- Choose role models whose behavior you can copy, such as how they outsource tasks and focus on their strengths, and deliberately emulate their habits and ways of thinking. [L1041, 2023]

### Acting as if

- Belief in yourself is foundational to action. If genuine belief is absent, act as if you have it until the belief catches up. You are not your past, so examine whether old beliefs still serve you. [L0007, 2023]
- Act as if you already hold the role you want, even before you feel it or have achieved it. Look for small pieces of evidence that support the new identity so the behavior compounds. [L1041, 2023]
- A writer decided to act as if she already had the ability she suspected she had, instead of acting tentatively. Within a year, her output and sales as a writer had increased many times over, and she discovered a talent for public speaking that she had never used. The change came from a deliberate decision to act on an assumed ability before any proof appeared. [L1206, 2015+]
- Two psychologists asked 45 men hospitalized with neuropsychiatric conditions to retake a personality test while answering as a typical well-adjusted person would. Three-fourths of them scored better, and some changes were large. Imagining the role of a well-adjusted person was enough to shift how the men answered and how they felt. [L1206, 2015+]
- In a demonstration, a participant's grip strength measured 100 pounds on a dynamometer in his normal state. After a suggestion that he was much stronger than he had ever been, the same grip reached 125 pounds, without any change in physical capacity. The example shows how a self-imposed limit can hold back ability that already exists. [L1206, 2015+]

### Core beliefs: the ten beliefs

#### Commitment and responsibility

- Commit fully to succeeding rather than only wishing for success. Commitment is the belief that separates operators who make it from those who only hope. [L0831, 2019]
- Belief 1: Commit to the outcome instead of only wanting it. Saying I want something declares that you do not have it and keeps the lack in place. Commitment treats the outcome as already decided and keeps you moving through setbacks. [L1513, 2019]
- A commitment test: imagine an opportunity with a 99.9 percent chance to return 10 times a minimum investment of 20,000 dollars, and then imagine the 0.1 percent failure happens and the money is lost. If you would still keep pursuing your goal, your commitment is strong. Commitment can be built up over time through repeated practice. [L1513, 2019]
- Take full responsibility for everything that happens in your business, both good and bad. Blaming markets, suppliers, or others removes your ability to change the outcome. [L0831, 2019]
- Belief 2: You are 100 percent responsible for everything that happens in your business, including the actions of staff. If a team member makes a mistake, the owner who hired and trained them shares the responsibility and must fix the training, the procedure or the hiring. Full responsibility gives full control over outcomes. [L1513, 2019]

#### Learning and growth

- The more you learn, the more you can earn. Continuous learning is treated as a direct driver of income. [L0831, 2019]
- Belief 3: The more you learn, the more you earn, and learning never ends because markets and platforms keep changing. Dismissing a new channel because you dislike it can cost a lot, as many large social platforms became major sales channels after early owners ignored them. Keep learning current platforms even when they feel unattractive. [L1513, 2019]
- The more you earn, the less you need to work. Growing income should eventually reduce the hours you personally put in. [L0831, 2019]
- Working harder as the only way to grow a business leads to burnout and a hard ceiling on income. Hours in a day are limited, so the business must be restructured through staff and systems before growth can continue. [L1513, 2019]
- Growth can come from two sources: repeated painful trial and error, or a sudden shift in understanding when a belief clicks. Consciously reflecting on a new belief until it feels true can lock it into the subconscious and change behavior faster than years of trial and error. Review the belief list regularly to give this process time to work. [L1513, 2019]
- Personal growth happens in two ways: through painful experience or through sudden moments of clarity. Both paths produce learning, and the second can come faster. [L0831, 2019]
- Adopt a growth mindset: treat every result, good or bad, as information for the next step rather than a final verdict on your ability. Failure is a lesson, not an identity. [L1039, 2019+]
- Do not judge yourself by old beliefs, do not accept others' feedback as the final answer, and learn from experiences yourself. Difficult results still carry useful information if you look for it. [L1039, 2019+]
- A fixed mindset treats a failure as proof of inability and leads to giving up. Avoid it by treating every failure as a lesson and a cue to improve. [L1043, undated]
- Test every limiting belief by asking whether it is useful for growth, not whether it is true. Discard beliefs that do not serve progress. [L1041, 2023]

#### Customers, money and tools

- Expect that customers will pay you for real value. Build the business on the assumption that people are willing to buy. [L0831, 2019]
- Belief 5: Treat money as a tool. Spend to make money, and do not let emotional ties to money prevent investment that has a proven return. [L1513, 2019]
- Belief 6: People pay you money, not PayPal, Stripe or any other tool. Keep customer psychology at the centre of planning, marketing and offers. [L1513, 2019]

#### Think big

- Think big when setting goals and planning the business. Small thinking limits the size of the store and the decisions behind it. [L0831, 2019]
- Belief 7: Always think big instead of playing it safe, since aiming high requires similar energy as aiming low. Set goals that would be worth reaching if only part of them succeeds. [L1513, 2019]

#### Receiving

- Be open to receiving money, help, and opportunities. Resistance to receiving blocks growth even when the business is working. [L0831, 2019]
- Belief 8: Learn to receive well. Accept gifts, money and compliments by looking the giver in the eye and saying thank you, without deflecting. Practising this regularly builds receiving as a habit and does not block others from giving. [L1513, 2019]

#### Play to win

- Play to win instead of playing not to lose. A defensive mindset makes you avoid the bold moves that growth requires. [L0831, 2019]
- Playing not to lose, such as aiming only to cover bills or break even, keeps a business small and makes the owner give up quickly under pressure. Starting a business is emotionally and mentally demanding, so it is worth aiming to win clearly rather than just to survive. [L1513, 2019]
- Belief 9: Play to win instead of playing to not lose. Set ambitions that go beyond covering costs, because the effort of building a business is similar either way. [L1513, 2019]

#### Keeping your word

- Always keep your word to customers, suppliers, partners, and yourself. Reliability is part of the business's long-term value. [L0831, 2019]
- Belief 10: Keep your word, because integrity means that what you say and what you do stay whole. Every unfinished promise to yourself keeps an open loop in your mind that fills your thoughts. Each broken promise also lowers how much you trust yourself to follow through. [L1513, 2019]
- To close open loops and stay in integrity, follow these two options for each commitment you have made:
  1. Complete it when you said it would be done. [L1513, 2019]
  2. If you no longer want it, cancel it clearly and explicitly, telling yourself it is over. Then focus on the next priority and avoid keeping unfinished goals in the background. [L1513, 2019]
- Integrity can be scored as a rough percentage showing how often you keep the commitments you make to yourself and others. Honest first ratings tend to come out low, with many people scoring below 50 percent, so tracking the score over time shows whether you are improving. [L1513, 2019]

### Effort and hustle

- Hustle is the only success factor you fully control, so it is the most important lever in the early stage of a business. [L0829, 2019]
- Hustle requires courage, confidence, self-belief and self-determination to keep working until the opportunities you want appear. [L0829, 2019]
- Hustle is effort you control, combining daily commitment with a real desire for the result. It means working consistently rather than only harder for a short time. [L0832, 2019]

### Gratitude and confidence exercises

- Daily gratitude exercise for quick confidence:
  1. Set a timer for two minutes at most. [L0701, 2015]
  2. List five to ten things you are grateful for, including ordinary things such as family, food, good people, or the electricity that powers a light. [L0701, 2015]
  3. Do this every day, for example after waking up. [L0701, 2015]
  4. Repeat the confidence rating from 1 to 10 afterwards and compare it with the earlier score. [L0701, 2015]
- Gratitude shifts the mind from a scarcity mindset to an abundance mindset, which makes opportunities around you easier to notice. In the test, most people rose one or two points on the 1 to 10 confidence scale. [L0701, 2015]
- Income or revenue often starts to rise about six to eight weeks after consistent work on the mindset and self-alignment steps, sometimes doubling or tripling in that period. [L0701, 2015]
- Build confidence with the Sacred Six:
  1. Forgive yourself for past mistakes. [L0007, 2023]
  2. Commit to doing what you say you will do. [L0007, 2023]
  3. Rack up wins, no matter how small. [L0007, 2023]
  4. Remove negativity from your environment. [L0007, 2023]
  5. Visualize your desired outcomes. [L0007, 2023]
  6. Seek knowledge continuously. [L0007, 2023]
- Sacred Six daily practice for building self-confidence, done each morning:
  1. Forgive yourself for yesterday and accept that you are human. [L1041, 2023]
  2. Commit only to what you say you will do, then do it, so you can trust yourself. [L1041, 2023]
  3. Collect small wins through the day instead of counting losses. [L1041, 2023]
  4. Remove negativity from your environment, such as news and negative content, and process negativity without letting it consume you. [L1041, 2023]
  5. Visualize your goals in as much detail as possible, so the brain filters for relevant opportunities. [L1041, 2023]
  6. Seek more knowledge and apply what you learn. [L1041, 2023]
- Write a short reminder into each calendar block that states the kind of person to be on that day or a brief affirmation. Reading it at the start of the block helps shift attention to the intended mindset. [L1181, 2023]
- Review your core beliefs every day to keep them active in your subconscious. Write down which beliefs you still struggle with and act on the ones that feel uncomfortable. Repeated review helps the new beliefs replace old automatic thoughts. [L1513, 2019]
- A new belief has settled into the subconscious when reviewing it produces an immediate felt sense of recognition, like a sudden "I get it" moment, rather than only intellectual agreement. After reviewing each belief, check for that felt response, and if it does not appear, reflect on that belief again before moving on. [L1513, 2019]

### Testing limiting beliefs

- Rewrite a limiting story that blocks a task:
  1. Write down, in one sentence, the story you tell yourself when a task becomes hard. [L0378, 2019+]
  2. Identify the expectation behind it, such as what should feel comfortable or what should already be true. [L0378, 2019+]
  3. Compare that expectation with what is actually happening and note the gap. [L0378, 2019+]
  4. Write a new sentence you can accept without pretending the discomfort is gone. [L0378, 2019+]
  5. Repeat the new sentence before the next hard task and track whether the old story still drives your decisions. [L0378, 2019+]
- Testing a limiting belief with four questions:
  1. Name the belief behind the behavior, for example that you cannot do a certain task, or that you deserve less. [L1206, 2015+]
  2. Ask whether it rests on an actual fact or only on an assumption or a false conclusion. [L1206, 2015+]
  3. Ask whether there is any rational reason to hold it. [L1206, 2015+]
  4. Ask whether you could be wrong, and whether you would reach the same conclusion about another person in the same situation. [L1206, 2015+]
  5. If there is no good reason, ask why you keep acting as if it were true. [L1206, 2015+]
  6. Answer each question seriously and emotionally, not casually. A flash of healthy indignation can help break an old false idea. [L1206, 2015+]
- Choose a past experience in which a result matched a belief you held about yourself:
  1. Write down what happened, including what you expected and how you reacted. [L1206, 2015+]
  2. Explain the experience using one principle, such as self-image, a limiting belief or the role of an automatic goal. [L1206, 2015+]
  3. Use the explanation to decide which belief to test next. [L1206, 2015+]

  Start the procedure by choosing that experience. [L1206, 2015+]
- Negative beliefs are usually built through repetition and emotion. [L1206, 2015+]
- Trying to prove or disprove a new mental method through intellectual argument or endless debate prevents you from testing it. The method can only be judged by practicing it and observing your own results. [L1206, 2015+]
