### Mastermind setup

- Small peer groups called masterminds keep members accountable, because committing to tasks before a recurring call makes people more likely to complete them. Look in community channels for people on a similar timeline and agree on a fixed call schedule. [L0804, 2023]
- Stay connected with other business owners through masterminds, community groups or small peer calls to keep momentum going. If no mastermind exists, start one or post in the community. [L0359, 2020]
- To find a mastermind group, search the community forum for mastermind and join any that fit. If none exist, post an introduction stating your stage in the program and that you want to start one. [L1275, 2019]
- Mastermind groups work best when members are at about the same stage. Sharing your current progress makes matching easier. [L1275, 2019]
- A mastermind group of peers who meet regularly gives a home-based operator the social contact that an office job would otherwise provide. It also creates synergy, since the group's combined progress beats what each member would reach alone. Peers who hit their targets motivate the others, and members hold each other to their goals. [L1000, 2017]
- Mastermind meetings need a fixed schedule to stay active. Without a set schedule and responsibility, the group dissolves. [L0832, 2019]
- Mastermind format, standard procedure:
  1. Fix a recurring call every two weeks at the same time. [L1000, 2017]
  2. Give each member 10 to 15 minutes to speak. [L1000, 2017]
  3. Assign one person to take notes. [L1000, 2017]
  4. At the next meeting, review the previous notes, check where each member stands, and touch base on progress. [L1000, 2017]
- Three questions to structure each mastermind session:
  1. What are you working on right now? Others may learn from it or find a faster method. [L1000, 2017]
  2. What do you need help with? Members can ask peers privately instead of posting in public forums. [L1000, 2017]
  3. What can you share that will help everyone else? This forces each member to contribute and creates the synergy effect. [L1000, 2017]
- Join or start a small recurring group call with about five peers in the same field, where members share mistakes and wins openly. Learning from the group's collective mistakes shortens the learning curve, and isolation during the early stage is reduced. [L1041, 2023]
- Sharing what worked and what did not over the past month builds familiarity in a peer group of established store owners. It tends to draw out others' experiences in return. Posting an open question with your own results first makes it easier for others to respond. [L1115, 2024]
- A peer community such as a Discord group is useful for sharing problems, even when others cannot solve them, because being heard reduces stress. Active members, mentors and students alike, often offer solutions. [L1128, 2023]

### Mastermind size, format and membership

- Keep a mastermind group at three to five members. Two members is just catching up with a friend, and more than five makes the group too diluted to be personal. [L1000, 2017]
- Mastermind size and cadence differ between sources:

  | Source | Size | Cadence | Note |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Standard guidance [L1000, 2017] | 3 to 5 members | Every two weeks [L0832, 2019; L1000, 2017] | Current format to plan around |
  | one source 2019 [L1069, 2019] | 3 to 5 members | Weekly | Older; members share struggles and others support them |
  | Peer call guidance [L1041, 2023] | About 5 peers | Recurring group call, no cadence stated | Shares mistakes and wins openly |

- Choose mastermind partners at a similar business stage. A person still selecting a niche should not be grouped with someone already running a large operation. Peers at the same level benefit most. [L0832, 2019]
- Build a network of peers who are at the same stage of their business by telling people where you stand and looking for others in the same process. [L0829, 2019]
- Members should click personally, share similar values and similar goals, and feel comfortable sharing business details. This makes collaboration much easier than it is with people at a different level. [L1000, 2017]
- Mastermind members do not have to share their niche with every member. Build friendships first, then work most closely with the people whose progress and feedback help you most. [L0832, 2019]
- Joining or starting a mastermind of about 3 to 5 members that meets weekly helps you stay on track after a pause. Each member shares struggles and the others support them. [L1069, 2019]

### Confidentiality and trust

- Protect confidentiality in a mastermind group by signing a one-page agreement in which every member promises not to share what is discussed with anyone outside the group. [L1000, 2017]
- Keep the talks light and useful rather than social chat, so members look forward to each session. [L1000, 2017]

### Finding peers

- Peer examples in person help make an ambitious goal feel achievable. Conferences, meetups, and casual conversations with people running similar businesses shift what a person believes is possible. [L0863, 2022]
- Operators working in different countries and markets can give market-specific answers that a single-region perspective cannot. Forming masterminds and connecting with peers are ways to keep learning and growing a business. [L0710, 2020-26]
- To find a business partner or mastermind partner, post a short introduction in an online community. State who you are, where you are, what you are looking for, and what kind of partner you want. [L0852, 2020]
- Celebrating peers' wins in a mastermind raises your own energy and motivation, because it shows that a goal is reachable. Seeing others succeed also pushes you to work harder. [L0832, 2019]
- Accountability works better when goals are told to peers, because unmet goals alone are easy to dismiss. Small stakes or wagers within the group can make goal tracking fun and increase follow-through. [L1000, 2017]
- Joining a network of entrepreneurs speeds up learning, because peers share mistakes and resources. Reciprocity is required so that value flows both ways. [L1077, undated]

### Networking and partner outreach

- Build relationships before you urgently need traffic or sales, because the work only pays off when it has already been done. Starting late is like digging a water source only after you are already in need. [L0412, 2021-22]
- Relationship building produces compounding results with consistency. The more regular effort you invest, the stronger the outcomes, while sporadic effort yields weaker results. [L0297, 2021-22]
- Introverts or people who find this outreach daunting can still succeed through small, consistent steps. Results usually turn out better than expected once action is taken. [L0453, 2021-22]
- Do the well-digging activity consistently rather than worrying about how long it will take. Spend a little time each day, and expect many days to have little to do, because most prospects publish only occasionally. [L0453, 2021-22]
- Do not interact with a Dream 100 prospect without a reason. Only comment when they have published something new, such as a weekly blog post or a YouTube video published once a week. This keeps the process manageable and avoids spamming. [L0453, 2021-22]
- A Dream 100 list does not have to reach exactly 100 names. The goal is to get as far as you can, and it can be built gradually in small daily sessions alongside other tasks. [L0526, 2021-22]
- Daily action for this stage: work through the whole Dream 100 list and follow, subscribe, like, join groups and forums, create forum accounts, and subscribe to email lists for each contact. [L0526, 2021-22]
- Partnership action plan:
  1. Keep engaging with each Dream 100 prospect's content. [L0525, 2021-22]
  2. Once you reach about 10 touch points, or about 5 for periodic publishers, reach out directly. [L0525, 2021-22]
  3. Set up calls and have conversations as your time allows. [L0525, 2021-22]
  4. Book any advertising opportunities you uncover that your budget can afford. [L0525, 2021-22]
  5. Repeat this over the coming months. [L0525, 2021-22]

### Reciprocity and giving first

- Build a supportive network that holds you accountable. Be valuable to others, and reciprocity returns. Leverage mentorship and community, because you are social by nature. [L0007, 2023]
- Build a network of people you both add value to and learn from. Share mistakes and wins within the group so everyone grows faster. Give value first without expecting a direct return, and use the network for accountability by publicly committing to goals. [L1041, 2023]
- Reciprocity is required so that value flows both ways in any entrepreneur network. [L1077, undated]

### Accountability and staying on track

- Set a concrete implementation goal between coaching calls and review progress at the next session. [L0806, 2024]
- Committing to tasks before a recurring call makes people more likely to complete them, so agree on a fixed call schedule with peers. [L0804, 2023]
- If you are unsure whether you are progressing well, talk to an experienced person who can review your situation. [L0419, 2021-22]

#### Further details

- A regular fixed call time with a mentor or accountability coach keeps students on track better than ad hoc calls that get cancelled at short notice. Reliable schedules also protect the mentor's credibility. [L1017, undated]
