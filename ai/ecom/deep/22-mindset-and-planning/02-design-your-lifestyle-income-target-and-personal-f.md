## Design your lifestyle, income target and personal finances

### Why a personal target comes first

- Building a business takes sustained effort before it generates income. Clarity on personal goals, called dreamlining, and regular reference to those goals keeps motivation up during unpaid phases. [L0276, 2024]
- Use this routine:
  1. Write down your ideal life: location, home type, vehicle, vacations, lifestyle. [L0276, 2024]
  2. Calculate the annual cost of that lifestyle. [L0276, 2024]
  3. Keep this document visible while you work. [L0276, 2024]
  4. When you do difficult, unpaid work, refer to these goals. [L0276, 2024]
  5. Remind yourself that you are working toward your defined life, not hoping the business works. [L0276, 2024]
- Goals without a cost figure give the business no money target and no clear measure of progress. Each goal should carry a cost. [L1043, undated]
- Define your values and priorities before you cut time or cost. Freedom means something different for each person. Every plan involves trade-offs between free time and maximum money, more stuff and less stuff, spending and investing, and stability and uncertainty. [L0827, 2019]
- Answer these in writing, without copying what others do:
  1. What financial freedom means to you: a large comfortable house, a small studio, or something in between. [L1515, 2019]
  2. How much luxury you need in cars, hotels and possessions. [L1515, 2019]
  3. Whether you value location independence or a stable home base with room for guests and family. [L1515, 2019]
  4. Whether you want more free time or the maximum possible income. [L1515, 2019]
  5. Whether money should go to experiences now or to investments for later. [L1515, 2019]
- A billable career is capped by available hours. Profit above your current income is what allows growth beyond that cap. The first goal is to replace the income from your current job. [L0832, 2019]
- one source used 100,000 dollars per month as a goal, tied to a life decision such as a partner leaving a job or starting a family. Set income targets as trigger points for life decisions, not as fixed dates. [L0832, 2019]
- Most drop shippers face money problems sooner or later. People usually fix financial control only after a problem forces them to, which costs more than planning ahead. [L0632, 2021-22]
- Knowing your numbers lets you step out of daily operations and reach higher levels of success. Without this knowledge you may reach some levels but stay stuck in daily firefighting. [L0632, 2021-22]
- Write down your money questions as homework:
  1. Set aside about five minutes. [L0632, 2021-22]
  2. Write down every question or problem you have about money and the numbers in your business. [L0632, 2021-22]
  3. Mark which common questions apply to you: product profitability, sales profitability, where money goes, supplier debt, hiring affordability, and selling the business. [L0632, 2021-22]
  4. Add any other financial questions or problems you need to solve. [L0632, 2021-22]
  5. Keep the list and use it to guide your next steps in financial learning. [L0632, 2021-22]
- Test your knowledge by trying to answer your key money questions within about ten minutes of looking at your records. If you cannot, you have a knowledge gap to close before you scale or sell. [L0632, 2021-22]
- Set the first goal around controlling your time and earning enough to cover current household costs, not around extreme wealth. A realistic income target that supports your current lifestyle is a sound starting point. [L1014, 2021-22]
- Every growing dropshipping store meets the same bookkeeping questions, such as where the profit went or why the bank balance is unexpectedly high. Meeting them is normal and does not mean you are doing something wrong. [L1516, 2021-22]

### Dreamlining and the cost form

- Dreamlining turns your ideal life into a money target. Follow these nine steps:
  1. Decide the business type: a lifestyle business with freedom to work from anywhere, or a corporate-style company with employees and many stores. [L1039, 2019+]
  2. Define where you want to live: city, state or region, and country. [L1039, 2019+]
  3. Decide whether you want to rent or own, and whether you prefer a house or a condo. [L1039, 2019+]
  4. Define your car: the specific model, its total price, and its monthly payments. [L1039, 2019+]
  5. Define vacations: how many per year, how long each one lasts, and where you go. [L1039, 2019+]
  6. List other lifestyle costs, such as dining out, a winter location, a personal trainer, or a chef. [L1039, 2019+]
  7. Note any debt you want to pay down each month, or the amount you want to save each month. [L1039, 2019+]
  8. Price every item with exact figures so you know the total cost of your ideal life. [L1039, 2019+]
  9. Calculate how much you must earn to fund that life, then derive your goals from that number. [L1039, 2019+]
- A shorter version of the same method:
  1. Write down what you want: where you live, what car you drive, what vacation you take, and what else you want. [L1043, undated]
  2. Estimate what each item costs. [L1043, undated]
  3. Add the costs to get the total the business must generate to pay for these goals. [L1043, undated]
  4. Use the items as rewards that keep you working toward the target. [L1043, undated]
- The 4-Hour Workweek Dreamlining Form is a template for listing personal life goals with their costs and turning them into a business money target. [L1043, undated]
- A downloadable cost form based on Tim Ferriss's *The 4-Hour Work Week* lets you total the costs of your ideal lifestyle and the income needed to fund it. It replaces guessing with exact figures. [L1039, 2019+]
- Set dreamline targets as a stretch beyond what you currently believe is possible, but not so far that almost nobody achieves them. Donating $5 million to charity within 18 months is unrealistic for nearly anyone. Upgrading to a comfortable apartment within a year is a fair stretch for someone living with parents. [L1039, 2019+]
- Knowing your wants in advance lets you spend your earnings deliberately, and lifestyle items can serve as rewards. Owning a house is not an automatic goal just because income rises. The right choice depends on how much flexibility you value. [L1039, 2019+]
- Before buying an expensive item such as a luxury car, write down why you want it, the exact model, its price, and its monthly payment. This prevents purchases you regret once income arrives. [L1039, 2019+]
- Targets can follow different patterns, for example one two-week vacation per year or one one-week trip every quarter. Choose the pattern and destination, then price it so you know the annual travel budget your income must cover. [L1039, 2019+]

### DEAL model and time-versus-money trade-offs

- Apply DEAL in this fixed order:
  1. Define: decide what you actually need from life, what you value, and what your priorities are. [L1515, 2019]
  2. Eliminate: cut every expense, possession, responsibility and daily task you can drop. [L1515, 2019]
  3. Automate: automate each task you cannot remove. [L1515, 2019]
  4. Liberate: outsource what still needs a human, once the process is clear and documented. [L1515, 2019]
- Eliminate in layers: reduce expenses first, then material possessions, then responsibilities, then daily routines. Whatever cannot be made smaller or removed should be automated before it is delegated. [L1515, 2019]
- Track time freedom as hours per week. Weekly working time fell from over 40 hours to under 20 hours after cutting responsibilities and automating tasks. [L1515, 2019]
- Money can buy time back in many ways, such as direct flights, ride-hailing, delivery, and cleaning services. Judge each time-saving purchase by comparing its price with the hourly value of your time. Otherwise convenience spending can quietly consume your income. [L1515, 2019]
- Count money and time together. A casual restaurant meal in a US city such as San Francisco can cost about 20-25 USD with tax and tip. Cooking at home also adds hidden time costs: grocery shopping, prep, cooking, cleanup and car errands. Compare both before choosing the cheaper-looking option. [L1515, 2019]
- Cost versus comfort, with examples:
  - Paying a few hundred dollars for a direct flight instead of a flight with two or three layovers is often worth it. A private jet at around 20,000 USD per hour is only worth it if your time is valued at that level. Compare the extra cost with the hours saved and the value you place on them. [L1515, 2019]
  - A traveler chose premium economy and paid cash for it instead of business class. The flight stayed comfortable and the large price difference went to investing. Choose a middle option when the top option costs more than the gain in comfort justifies. [L1515, 2019]
- Every purchase is a trade-off. You can have many things but not everything at once. Buying several major items can leave no cash for investing or emergencies, so decide deliberately which items deserve the money and time they consume. [L1515, 2019]
- Financing large purchases on long terms, such as 72- to 96-month car payments or leases and 30- to 40-year mortgages, can keep you paying for items long after you stop wanting them. Match the loan length to how long you realistically will keep the asset. [L1515, 2019]
- Possessions and downsizing: a single 60-liter carry-on duffel can hold everything a full-time traveler owns, including home, office and wardrobe. That minimalism means giving up items such as golf clubs or many pairs of shoes. Decide individually which possessions are essential before downsizing. [L1515, 2019]

### Location arbitrage and slow travel

- Living costs vary widely between cities and countries. Moving from a high-cost US city (about $4,000 to $6,000 per month in California) to a low-cost place (about $600 to $1,000 per month in Chiang Mai) can free roughly $3,000 per month for saving and investing. [L1126, 2018]
- Living costs of about 4,000-5,000 USD per month in a large US city fell to about 1,000-2,000 USD per month in places such as Chiang Mai, Eastern European cities or Prague. This roughly halves spending through location arbitrage. [L1515, 2019]
- Running a business from anywhere needs structure and routine. Location independence is built on a set daily rhythm, not on freedom alone. Slow travel and broadening your horizons are the other two elements that make this lifestyle sustainable. [L0926, 2018]
- Stability and uncertainty pull against each other, and frequent travelers often later buy a home base. A realistic plan combines slow travel with a stable base and chooses accommodation for comfort over months rather than the lowest nightly price. [L1515, 2019]
- Slow travel example: staying two to three months per location in monthly rentals of decent quality, with a couch, a TV and a separate bedroom, reduces logistics and fatigue compared with moving every two weeks. This costs more than the cheapest room but is chosen for comfort. [L1515, 2019]
- Living outside the US can make good private medical care affordable. It is often paid in cash at much lower cost than insurance-based care in the US. Include this when planning a move abroad. [L0827, 2019]

### Relocating with family

- A move abroad needs a strong personal reason. Copying others who went to the same place is not enough motivation to carry a family through difficulties. Every family member should agree to the move before it starts. [L1000, 2017]
- Relocating with children is harder when they lose close daily contact with grandparents and relatives. Before deciding, discuss the impact on each family member and agree how family back home will stay in touch. [L1000, 2017]
- Before travelling abroad with a family:
  1. Plan flights to the last detail. [L1000, 2017]
  2. Confirm visas and the exact dates you will be in each location. [L1000, 2017]
  3. Double-check those dates to avoid overstaying a visa, which can cause legal trouble. [L1000, 2017]
- Before arriving, research the new location thoroughly, including where the hospitals, shops and essentials are. A detailed destination guide with an interactive map helps. Local expat groups on Facebook are a good source of answers where no guide exists. [L1000, 2017]
- Check practical questions before committing to a location: nearby shops, how children will be cared for, daycare availability, the cost of a nanny, and seasons such as heavy rain or burning season that make living conditions poor. [L1000, 2017]
- A long-term base with several short retreats of two to three weeks suited one family better than living abroad permanently. Test a relocation before making it permanent, and expect to change plans if the first choice does not fit. [L1000, 2017]
- Case: Starting point was one online store, a part-time business run alongside a 9-to-5 job, and no team. The owner gambled on the business, quit the job, and within twelve months had three stores, two full-time virtual assistants, and a mastermind group. After the mastermind group formed at an industry retreat and the family sold its belongings, the family moved to Chiang Mai, Thailand, with a three-year-old daughter. The owner still answered store calls from about 4:30 a.m. Burning season made the air unlivable after about two months. The family moved on the spot to Phuket for about three weeks, found it too touristy, and returned to Australia to live with parents, about three months abroad in total. At this stage the owner hired a first virtual assistant, who managed the store day to day during a three-week trip to Bali, where the owner handled only minimal tasks. The result: three stores, two full-time assistants, and a home base with occasional mini-retirements. [L1000, 2017]
- Southeast Asia rules:
  - In Southeast Asia, food and water hygiene can be lower than in many home countries. Choose restaurants carefully. Do not drink tap water or brush teeth with it, and make sure children follow the same rule. [L1000, 2017]
  - Do not carry a family on a motor scooter in busy Southeast Asian towns. The risk to life is too high. Expect poor pavements, plan routes for pushing a stroller, and use safer transport for the whole family. [L1000, 2017]

### Income needed before quitting a job

- To find the income you need before leaving a job:
  1. Use the mindset and goal-setting lesson template to map the costs of your ideal life. [L0373, 2024+]
  2. Look up real rent and list every other expense. [L0373, 2024+]
  3. Add the totals to get the monthly income target. [L0373, 2024+]
- Quit a job only after at least three consecutive months in which net profit from the business, not gross sales, equals or exceeds the salary of the job or jobs you leave. Months spread apart do not count. [L0373, 2024+; L0471, 2024]
  - Source note: the 2024 version calls this true net profit and says to keep the job and build the store part time until the test is met. [L0471, 2024]
  - Source note: the 2020 version says to keep the day job until online business income matches job income for at least three months, without requiring consecutive months, and then to decide whether to quit, since you then have two incomes to choose from. [L0852, 2020]
- Example: if the jobs pay $3,000 per month and the store makes $3,000 net profit in each of three months in a row, the business has replaced that income. Total monthly income then reaches $6,000, and the owner can devote more time to growing the business. [L0373, 2024+]
- Quit when the business reliably earns what the job pays, and factor in how much time you need for the transition. [L0829, 2019]
- An accountant can give peace of mind, but is optional. Many founders run as a sole proprietor first and add an accountant once revenue arrives. [L0963, 2022]

### Net worth and progress measures

- Net worth equals savings plus investments minus debt. It shows whether income is actually kept and built into assets, which makes it a better progress measure than gross revenue or salary. [L1126, 2018]
- Judge your business and personal finances by the profit you keep after all costs, not by gross revenue. Retained money is what builds wealth. [L0823, 2018]
- The Cashflow Quadrant from *Rich Dad Poor Dad* sorts income into four types: employee, self-employed, business owner and investor. The lesson states that employees and self-employed people control about 5% of total US wealth while making up about 95% of the population. The aim is to move toward owning a system, then owning investments. [L1126, 2018]
- Use the cash the business generates to fund long-term passive income, such as real estate or shares in other companies. Set a longer-term vision, for example five years, that defines what the investments look like and sets the income goals for the business. [L1480, 2021-22]

### Pay yourself first

- Move a fixed share of income into savings or investments before covering other spending. This makes saving automatic rather than dependent on what is left over. Saving whatever remains after spending usually leaves little or nothing. [L0823, 2018; L1126, 2018]
- Even a small amount such as $300 per month should go into savings as soon as income arrives, before any spending. A small fixed amount beats waiting for a larger surplus that may never come. [L1126, 2018]

### Profit First

- Profit First is a cash management method from Mike Michalowicz's book *Profit First*. It splits incoming revenue into percentages sent to separate accounts before money is spent, so each dollar has a predetermined purpose. [L1200, 2021-22]
- Pay the owner a regular salary from a planned account. This reduces burnout risk and makes personal income predictable. Irregular withdrawals create uncertainty and anxiety for the owner and household. [L1200, 2021-22]

### Saving and investing basics

- Do not try to time the market by predicting short-term rises and drops and buying or selling on those predictions. [L0823, 2018]
- Use low-cost index funds as the core of a long-term portfolio. [L0823, 2018]
- Plan to hold investments long term rather than trading in and out of positions. [L0823, 2018]
- Dollar-cost averaging means investing the same amount at regular intervals regardless of price. Over time this spreads purchases across high and low prices and reduces the risk of buying everything at a peak. [L0823, 2018]
- Limit speculative investments to 0% to 5% of the total investment portfolio. The rest goes into core long-term holdings. [L0823, 2018]
- Compare investments by the time and responsibility they demand, not only by expected return. A passive fund or loan platform can be worth a lower return if it removes the work of tenants, repairs and property management. [L1126, 2018]
- Do not sell investments during market downturns. [L0823, 2018]
- Selling in panic during a downturn turns temporary paper losses into permanent ones and usually means missing the recovery. Long-term investors should expect downturns and stay invested. [L0823, 2018]
- Investing books for beginners:
  - *Unshakeable* by Tony Robbins is a shorter version of his longer book *Money Master the Game*. *The Only Investment Guide You Ever Need* by Andrew Tobias is a classic introduction to low-cost index investing. [L1126, 2018]
  - When reading investment books, skip any section that promotes a specific portfolio or company the author has a stake in. Keep the general principles on low fees and long-term holding. *Money Master the Game* has a portfolio section of this kind that should be ignored. [L1126, 2018]
- Personal finance reading list: *The Millionaire Next Door* (habits of wealthy people who live modestly), *Rich Dad Poor Dad* (buy assets, not liabilities), *The Total Money Makeover* by Dave Ramsey (for getting out of debt), and *The Millionaire Fastlane* (test whether an expensive lifestyle purchase is worth it). [L0823, 2018; L1126, 2018]

### Debt and emergency fund

- Compound interest works in both directions. The mechanism that grows investments also makes credit card and other high-interest debt grow fast. Clear high-interest debt before serious investing begins. [L1126, 2018]
- Start investing only after debt is cleared and an emergency fund is in place. Build the plan around a fixed monthly amount. Investing money still needed for debts or bills is a common reason people sell in a panic. [L1126, 2018]
- Case: an online store owner with $70,000 in debt paid all of it off first and became debt-free. Afterward, their dropshipping stores reached about $10,000 in profit per month. Clearing debt before building a passive-income system was the key step. [L1126, 2018]
- Keep a rainy-day fund in a separate bank account. Months of runway equal the fund divided by monthly costs. This shows how long the business can cover its costs if income drops to zero. [L1334, 2021-22]
- Credit mistake: assuming that no credit or bad credit blocks business cards for good is wrong. Start with a business debit card, then a secured card. This builds credit that leads to better cards within one to two years. [L0699, 2020-26]
