## Choose the kind of business and set honest expectations

### Decide the kind of business before anything else

- The mindset shift for scaling is moving from validating whether the business works to deciding how large you want it to become. [L0190, undated]
- Decide early what kind of business you want. The three options are:
  - One you run for 2 to 3 years, then sell.
  - One that gives you freedom with a small team.
  - One that scales to 15 to 30+ people with multiple stores.

  This choice shapes every hiring and process decision, and every team member must understand the company's direction. [L0263, 2022]
- Brand building is a long-term commitment that does not suit everyone, because it requires consistent content and patience instead of quick sales. Decide whether you are willing to invest that time before you start. [L0487, 2018]
- Brand building is a process of several years, not a short project. Plan for patience, because partnerships and supplier improvements take time. [L0492, 2023+]
- Building a high-ticket dropshipping business suits people who want to leave a job, gain control over their time and finances, and who are working long hours without a way out. [L1481, 2021-22]

### Choose your entry path

- Beginners should start with pump-and-dump products instead of supplements. Supplements compete against established brands that earn very large monthly revenues. Pump-and-dumps teach selling, testing and fast execution with less risk. [L0347, 2026]
- A pump-and-dump is a stepping stone, not a destination. Follow these steps to use it to build a long-term business: [L0347, 2026]
  1. Enter a pump-and-dump product to earn quick cash.
  2. Use the sales period to learn how ads work and how to test creatives.
  3. Reinvest the earned cash into a long-term brand.
  4. Level up by building the new brand with the skills and cash from the earlier product.
- Treating a pump-and-dump as a forever brand leads to getting stuck. Its role is to generate cash, teach advertising and fund a later brand. [L0347, 2026]
- Once a niche has produced a clear winner with strong revenue, it can be expanded into a brand with related products, such as adjacent fashion items. Expansion should be cautious because the niche may fade. [L1173, undated]
- A Swiss brand for a wearable blanket (the Fluffy brand) started as dropshipping and moved to stock once early tests justified it. The sequence was:
  - Early tests at about 200 to 300 USD in ads produced about 3,000 CHF in sales in Switzerland, which justified the move to stock. [L0492, 2023+]
  - In April 25 designs were bought, a shoot was held in mid-May and June, and about 500 units were ordered for the season at roughly 10,000 USD in product cost. [L0492, 2023+]
  - Offers were set at 30 off, free local shipping and 2 to 3 day delivery, a buy-two saving of 14, and a code for 5 off a single item. [L0492, 2023+]
  - Ads cost about 5,000 USD in winter for about 18,000 in November sales, roughly break-even overall. [L0492, 2023+]
  - The first stock sold out by the end of December with 410 orders and no refunds. After new stock arrived, customers kept ordering without ads, bringing about 1,000 USD in revenue. [L0492, 2023+]
  - Next steps were custom partner designs such as a football club logo for a one-time fee of about 10,000 USD for one year, Galaxus, and Germany and Austria. [L0492, 2023+]
- Use the opportunity of a physical retail location to get access to brands and to learn which products customers return. Sell online only the brands whose return rates and manufacturer policies make online sales viable. ** [L0801, 2021]

### Treat it as a business, not a side hustle

- Treat dropshipping as a real business that needs to be run, not as a side hustle. [L0631, 2026]
- Building a business takes months, not days. Be patient with the process and plan the timeline accordingly. [L0631, 2026]
- Each product test is part of running a real company with a revenue target in the six or seven figure range, not a quick product pump and dump. A disciplined process with patience is what separates a business from one-off wins. [L1124, 2026]
- Building a store is a long process that often takes weeks or months, not a few clicks. Consistent work over time is what produces results. ** [L1109, 2018]
- Because dropshipping is widely promoted as a fast-money method, many newcomers quit once they see the real work involved. Building a real business with a professional approach is what lasts. ** [L1069, 2019]
- Build for the long term rather than hoping for quick launches. A store can be built on the first launch, and results compound over the next decade. ** [L0429, 2022]
- Many e-commerce owners become so absorbed in the business process or in finding a product that they neglect the fundamentals. Without knowing where the business and its products stand in the market and which customers to attract, the rest of the work is built on an unclear base. ** [L0761, 2021-22]

### Build the first store before you expand

- Advice on expanding the business is best given after the first store works, because a new owner has no baseline to judge results. Start small, measure, and only then add stores, staff or agencies. ** [L0467, 2019]
- Before opening another store, make a store with no sales profitable: [L0470, 2023]
  1. Ask friends and family to browse the store as a potential customer and give honest feedback on trust and what stands out.
  2. Make improvements based on that feedback.
  3. Focus on optimizing the ads and generating profitable sales.
  4. Open a new store only after this one is profitable.
- Starting a high-ticket dropshipping business from zero is a split effort. Beginners set up and launch their first store, while people who already run one focus on growing it. Anyone starting from scratch should work through setup and launch before moving to growth and marketing systems. ** [L0438, 2021-22]
- Typical early challenges for new dropshippers are setting up the online store, finding reliable suppliers, and running profitable ads. Plan time and learning for each of these from the start. *(Older advice, 2019-22 source; undated in the course.)* [L0345, 2018]
- Dropshipping requires several skills at once: computer work, customer service and a business mindset, so the program covers more than a typical degree. Learning does not end, but the challenges change as the business grows. [L0832, 2019]
- Dropshipping requires several skills such as product finding, copywriting, video editing and Facebook ads, and the results move up and down over time. Expect setbacks and plan for them rather than assuming the business will be easy. [L1128, 2023]
- Dropshipping is simple but not easy at first. Each process becomes second nature with repetition, so early overwhelm is normal. [L0966, 2025]
- The first goal for beginners is experience and learning how to produce good creative content, not making money. Sales follow once the skills are developed. [L0820, undated]
- When starting out, focus on gaining experience in posting and making content rather than on immediate profit or finding a winning product. Learning to produce good content is the prerequisite for later profits. [L0821, undated]
- A new agency cannot reach 10 clients within two weeks. Early growth depends on building confidence and a portfolio first, often through free work for friends and family. [L0440, 2023+]

### Plan the exit deliberately

- Build to sell: store multiples are currently at their highest levels in 12+ years. Selling a profitable store generates capital for scaling or starting new ventures. ** [L0249, 2022]
- Start preparing for a sale early, because the sale process requires time whether or not a broker is used. Telling buyers that a trusted person runs the business and will stay is very valuable. [L0554, 2019]
- Expect buyers to push back on expenses and do not take this personally, because the store is a business deal rather than a personal project. Pushback on expenses is very common in negotiations. [L0554, 2019]
- Selling during a steep growth phase can feel like selling the farm, and some sellers regret it when the business doubles soon after. Choose the sale timing deliberately, based on the trend, rather than on emotion. [L1468, 2014]
- Choose the sale date by the revenue trend, not by emotion. If growth is still steep, a sale can leave money on the table, so waiting until the curve flattens may produce a better price. [L1468, 2014]
- A website is the output of a repeatable skill set, and that skill set is the real asset the operator keeps after a sale. Selling a single site while keeping the process allows the operator to build the next one. [L1468, 2014]
- Expect a business to change through growth stages. The main decisions at each stage are the ad budget, the number of products and the use of the existing audience. ** [L0854, 2022]

### Read the economic and market conditions

#### Recession and downturns

- High-ticket dropshipping (products priced $1,000+) remains viable even during economic downturns and recessions. Wealthy consumers continue purchasing premium goods, and niches with lower competition but higher margins can grow rapidly. ** [L0245, 2020]
- In the 2007-2008 economic collapse, the author built a high-ticket e-commerce business to multiple millions in revenue per year. Economic downturns create opportunity for those with online infrastructure while local competitors shut down. ** [L0245, 2020]

#### Inflation and costs

- Inflation and rising costs are permanent reality. Expect products to become more expensive and adapt pricing. Launch now rather than waiting; 13 years of business proves successful adaptation is possible regardless of economic conditions. ** [L0249, 2022]
- Plan for rising costs: inflation is expected to raise wholesale and retail prices, so keep a broad set of suppliers and monitor your margins regularly. ** [L1090, 2021]

#### Supply chain

- Supply chain challenges are real but affect different niches to different degrees. If you work with 10+ suppliers, enough alternatives exist to remain profitable. Continue contacting suppliers while sales go through existing partners. ** [L0249, 2022]
- A store owner used a supplier list of 20 to 25 approved brands, but a niche with only five suppliers was not enough. Broadening the niche one level brought the count to 20 or more, which allowed the store to move forward with a viable supplier base. [L0804, 2023]

#### Post-pandemic demand

- Post-pandemic, e-commerce adoption returned to its historical 20-year growth rate after the lockdown spike. Shopify hired for the spike rate (1,000 people added), then laid off 1,000 people (10% of workforce) on July 26, 2022 when growth normalized. This does not signal e-commerce decline; growth continues above pre-pandemic levels. ** [L0272, 2022]
- Government cash payments to consumers, such as the 2020 US stimulus checks, can produce sales spikes for stores selling higher-priced products, because buyers spend money they did not expect to have. Such payments are not guaranteed and have no set date, so a store plan should not depend on them. [L1049, 2025+]

#### Competition and market size

- Competition in dropshipping is rising slowly, not sharply, and many niches still have room. Businesses come and go, so a list of about 20 market ideas gives enough options to find a market with a suitable level of competition. ** [L0771, 2021-22]
- New drop shippers entering a niche rarely harm a well-run store, because many of them rely on weak methods such as cold social ads or supplier directories. Judge your store by its own metrics rather than by how many new competitors appear. [L0965, 2024]
- A single profitable store represents a tiny share of total online buying, so one store does not put other businesses out of business. The US alone has about 330 million people, most of them online shoppers. ** [L1085, 2018]

#### Effort and expectations

- Your timeline will differ from others, because your store, products, website and marketing style will be unique. The time needed to reach a goal is not a measure of success; what matters is whether you actually reach it. ** [L0419, 2021-22]
- Compare only your own progress, because build time varies by person. ** [L0963, 2022]
- Do not expect quick results. Dropshipping requires sustained effort over months and years before significant profits materialize. [L0280, undated]
- Dropshipping and other e-commerce businesses usually take months of consistent work before results appear. Expecting quick riches is a common reason people quit, so plan for sustained effort over a long period rather than fast returns. [L0444, undated]
- Building an e-commerce business requires long-term commitment. Even in the worst case, five years to $300,000 to $500,000 in profit is valuable and life-changing. [L0280, undated]
- High-ticket dropshipping is simple as a model but not easy to execute. The first one to two years usually demand long hours and many failed experiments. Expecting quick results without this workload leads to discouragement. ** [L1014, 2021-22]
- High-ticket dropshipping is not a quick fix or get-rich-quick plan. It requires sustained hard work, time and consistency, and it is not a fit for anyone who expects results in two weeks. ** [L0771, 2021-22]
- There are no get-rich-quick shortcuts, and business requires consistent work. Success depends mainly on following a process consistently until it works, and quitting early is the most common reason for failure. ** [L0458, 2021-22]
- There is no secret formula for dropshipping. Success comes from building basic skills, testing several products and offers, and scaling the one that wins. [L0519, 2020+]
- Expect the first real results from e-commerce to take around six to twelve months, with a plateau in between. The plateau is usually the hardest part, and continued consistent work is what gets a store past it. [L0819, 2020+]
- Low start-up cost does not mean easy. The low cost is offset by the work you need to put in. Business is not easy, and the people who succeed are the driven, focused, committed, and patient ones. ** [L1480, 2021-22]
- Dropshipping is simple but not easy at first, so early overwhelm is normal. [L0966, 2025]

### Never promise income

- Do not promise or expect a fixed income from the business model. Results vary widely based on niche, optimization, traffic and effort, so set short-term goals such as a net profit target over the next 90 days. ** [L0247, 2021]
- Income from this model varies widely, from zero to millions, depending on how you run the system, how fast you grow and how much you spend on ads as you scale. Do not plan around a fixed profit figure. ** [L0968, 2021]
- There is no reliable average for first-year sales or profit in dropshipping, because results vary widely depending on the work and optimization done. Plan with your own test numbers rather than industry averages. ** [L0551, 2020]
- Avoid promising revenue amounts by a fixed date, since launch timing depends on many variables such as account flags and optimization delays. Most stores need several weeks of setup and data before results can be predicted. [L0471, 2024]
- Revenue goals such as $1M are possible but never guaranteed, and income claims should not be made to customers or prospects. Set goals based on your own tracking, not promises. [L0808, 2026]
- A six-figure sales month is gross sales, not net profit. A two-person family store launched in 2023 reported its first six-figure sales month in May 2024, followed by six-figure months in June and July 2024. The owners had a steep learning curve before reaching them. [L0373, 2024+]

### Be skeptical of online claims

- Be skeptical of people claiming success online. Verify that they can actually prove their business makes money, because many large creators promote products they don't actually use or believe in. [L0280, undated]
- Do not trust online claims that promise a fixed income within a fixed time. Profit timing varies with the product, market, and the store's own setup, so such promises are usually unreliable. ** [L0438, 2021-22]
- Be sceptical about claims made online about suppliers refusing new retailers. Such claims often describe a specific period or industry, such as fitness products during the pandemic, that does not apply broadly. ** [L0962, 2021]
- Most people who start an online store never get to the stage of having traffic, and the odds of success improve a lot once a store gets its first real sales. Opening an empty Shopify store is a very small commitment, so an alleged 98% failure rate is not a reliable measure. ** [L1090, 2021]

### Use AI as leverage, not as passive income

- Traditional dropshipping relied on guesswork and hustle, while AI dropshipping relies on systems and leverage. The seller becomes an operator who uses technology to outpace competitors rather than only a seller. [L1459, 2026]
- AI amplifies entrepreneurs rather than replacing them. Sellers who use AI correctly are expected to outcompete those who do not. [L1459, 2026]
- This is not passive income. Passive income means money from investments such as rental property or dividends that needs no daily work, and a dropshipping business does not provide that on its own. It demands a lot of work early on. ** [L1480, 2021-22]
