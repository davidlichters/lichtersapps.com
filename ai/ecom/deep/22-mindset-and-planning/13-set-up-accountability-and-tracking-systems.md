### Daily accountability form

- Complete the form at the close of every day. The form is a mandatory part of the coaching program, so it must be submitted at the end of every single day with no exceptions. [L0507, 2026]
- The form is drafted in a Google Doc. Copy the finished form into your personal ticket in the program's Discord server, which is where the daily submission is made. [L0507, 2026]
- The Google Doc saves to Google Drive automatically while you edit it, so the status shows "Saved to Drive" after changes. [L0507, 2026]
- The daily progress check asks whether 100% of the day's planned tasks were completed. Anything below 100% requires a written explanation of what caused the shortfall. [L0507, 2026]
- Common reasons to log when tasks are not completed include distractions, poor time management, unclear priorities, and having to attend an appointment. [L0507, 2026]
- Productivity is self-rated daily on a scale from 1 (unproductive) to 10 (fully locked in). [L0507, 2026]
- Naming the specific obstacle that stopped you is a form of self-accountability. Acknowledging what happened, rather than hiding it, is what makes the daily review useful. [L0507, 2026]
- Daily self-reporting shows a coach where progress is stalling and how to help. The person must still take responsibility for their own results, because outside support has limits. [L0507, 2026]
- Skipping the daily form or leaving the "what stopped you" answer vague undermines the review, since the reasons for missed tasks are the information needed to fix them. [L0507, 2026]

Procedure for completing the form:

1. Open the daily accountability form document (Google Docs) and copy it. [L0507, 2026]
2. Open your ticket in the Discord server and paste the form into it. [L0507, 2026]
3. Enter today's date at the top of the form. [L0507, 2026]
4. Enter your name. [L0507, 2026]
5. Answer whether you completed 100% of today's tasks, with Yes or No. [L0507, 2026]
6. If you answered No, write what stopped you from completing everything. [L0507, 2026]
7. Fill in Tomorrow's Game Plan: your top 3 priorities for tomorrow and any other tasks. [L0507, 2026]
8. Fill in the Daily Reflection: a summary of today, what went well, what did not, and what you will improve tomorrow. [L0507, 2026]
9. Give a Productivity Rating from 1 (unproductive) to 10 (fully locked in). [L0507, 2026]
10. Write the Final Reflection and submit the form. [L0507, 2026]

Order of writing at the end of the day: list the top 3 priorities for tomorrow plus other tasks, then summarize today, note what went well, what did not, and what to improve tomorrow, before giving the productivity rating. [L0507, 2026]

### Daily habit tracker in Google Sheets

- A daily tracker holds you accountable for each day's planned actions. Writing down every day's plan and checking it off builds the habits needed for larger income goals. [L0456, 2026]
- Schedule each recurring habit as a fixed 30-minute block. Spend at least 30 minutes per day on each habit, and extend it to double, triple or quadruple time when possible. [L0456, 2026]
- Market research is tracked as a daily 30-minute habit. Three 30-minute habit blocks in one day add up to 90 minutes of planned daily work. [L0456, 2026]
- Three separate 30-minute blocks add up to 90 minutes of daily tasks. Each extra 30-minute block raises the planned daily total by 30 minutes. [L0456, 2026]
- A 30 Mins Development block can be filled with training, a course, a YouTube video or a book. Studying a losing ad and writing down why it failed and how to improve it also counts toward this habit. [L0456, 2026]
- The daily 30 Mins Development row is left blank on Sundays, so that habit runs on six days per week by default. [L0456, 2026]

Template layout:

| Element | Specification | IDs |
|---|---|---|
| Tabs | One tab per quarter: Q1 to Q4 | [L0456, 2026] |
| Blocks | One block per month within each tab | [L0456, 2026] |
| Rows | One row per habit | [L0456, 2026] |
| Daily marks | TRUE/FALSE per day in each habit row | [L0456, 2026] |
| Columns | Total, Goal, Complete, Reward | [L0456, 2026] |
| Day headers | M, T, W, T, F, S, S per week | [L0456, 2026] |

- Weekly habits such as New Idea To Growth Guide, Launch New Ad Creative and Attend Weekly Call are marked only once every seven days, always on the same weekday. All other days in those habit rows stay empty. [L0456, 2026]
- In the October block of the Q4 tab, the template replaces New Idea To Growth Guide and Launch New Ad Creative with Market Research and Go To The Gym. The November and December blocks in the same tab return to the standard set of habits. [L0456, 2026]

Daily tracking routine:

1. Make a copy of the habit tracker template (File > Make a copy) so you own your version. [L0456, 2026]
2. Schedule each habit as a 30-minute block in your day, at least 30 minutes per day for each habit. [L0456, 2026]
3. Each day, set the habit cell to TRUE if it was done and FALSE if it was not. [L0456, 2026]
4. For every missed habit, right-click the cell and add a note stating why it was not done. [L0456, 2026]
5. Check the Complete value for each habit and aim for at least 75%. [L0456, 2026]
6. Once a habit reaches 75% completion, give yourself a reward. [L0456, 2026]

- For a missed habit, right-click the cell in the Google Sheet and choose to add a note. Write the specific reason the task was not done, for example why the gym session was skipped. [L0456, 2026]
- Skipping a habit without documenting the reason removes the learning from the tracker. Vague excuses such as feeling sick are not useful; record the concrete cause in the cell note. [L0456, 2026]

### Whiteboard with checkboxes

- A whiteboard placed where you see it every day works as an accountability tool. It shows your goals and the tasks you must do, and each item has a box you tick once it is done. It works like a daily tracker but keeps your actions in front of you more constantly. [L0870, 2026]
- Writing goals and tasks down in a visible place outside your head makes them clearer and easier to follow through on consistently. [L0870, 2026]
- Board procedure:
  1. Write your goals on the whiteboard. [L0870, 2026]
  2. Draw a small empty box next to each goal. [L0870, 2026]
  3. Below the goals, write the tasks you need to do, each with its own box. [L0870, 2026]
  4. Look at the board every day and tick a box only if that goal or task was actually achieved. [L0870, 2026]
- Example: a daily goal written as a 10k day, with a box beside it that you tick yes or no at the end of the day depending on whether you reached that amount. [L0870, 2026]
- Write realistic, small goals that you can actually achieve within the time frame, not unrealistic dreams such as earning one million dollars. Goals you can check off are the ones that keep the board useful. [L0870, 2026]
- Small wins accumulate over time into large results, so consistent small achievements matter more than waiting for one big leap. [L0870, 2026]
- A visible unchecked box creates a feeling of guilt each time you see it, and this discomfort pushes you to act on the open task. The board works because the open box stays in front of you every day until you tick it. [L0870, 2026]
- Put on the board the tasks you are avoiding, not only the easy ones. A difficult task that stays unticked keeps pressing on you each day, which is the pressure the board is meant to create. [L0870, 2026]

- Place the whiteboard where you will see it first thing every morning, such as a wall you pass when you wake up. Daily visibility is what keeps unticked goals and tasks in front of you until they are done. [L0870, 2026]

### Diary of actions and logbook

- Track daily business actions in a diary (for example, "completed product research today") rather than measuring success only by money. Daily habits compound into long-term success. [L0280, undated]
- A logbook is a simple daily record of what you worked on, where you went and what you did. It does not need to be a detailed diary. Over several years, these short entries help you recall details and show how far your work has progressed. [L1343, undated]
- Wall calendar procedure:
  1. Buy a wall calendar that shows the whole year. [L1343, undated]
  2. Break your work into daily tasks that take a set amount of time. [L1343, undated]
  3. When each day's task is finished, mark a big X in that day's box. [L1343, undated]
  4. Aim only to fill each box and never break the chain of marked days. [L1343, undated]
- Logbook procedure:
  1. Keep a short daily logbook of what you worked on, where you went and what you did. [L1343, undated]
  2. Each evening, write down the best thing that happened that day, rather than only the problems. [L1343, undated]
  3. Review the entries after a few weeks or months to spot patterns in your work and energy. [L1343, undated]
- Finishing assigned tasks before the next follow-up call, without needing reminders, was linked to the fastest progress in a mentoring setting. Being accountable and self-directed moves a beginner forward more than advice alone. [L0819, 2020+]

### Milestones and celebration

- Celebrate small milestones along the way, such as the first supplier approval, the first recovered abandoned cart, or the first sale. Each one signals that the next level has been reached and makes the following step easier. [L0826, 2016]
- Celebrate milestones as they happen, including the first sale, the first profitable store, the first 10,000-dollar month and a 10,000-dollar day such as Cyber Monday. Celebration keeps the team motivated during slow periods. [L0832, 2019]
- After a first sale with about 100 dollars of profit, one operator spent about 500 dollars on a celebration and the whole profit was gone within seconds. Set a celebration budget that stays below the profit actually earned in that period. [L0832, 2019]

### Milestone rewards and leaderboards

- Milestone rewards can motivate students, for example a one-on-one call unlocked at 5k in sales or a bonus at 10k in sales. A community leaderboard that ranks members by engagement can add further motivation. [L1017, undated]

### Gamifying results and rewards

- Gamifying your business means attaching rewards to measurable numbers, so each win reinforces continued effort in the same way reward loops work in video games. Any business or life result that can be counted can be gamified. [L1039, 2019+]
- Small rewards for reaching a daily or early target include a massage, a nice dinner out, or a weekend getaway. Larger rewards for bigger monthly targets include a home upgrade, a car purchase, or a month working from another part of the world. [L1039, 2019+]
- Reward small wins promptly, since appreciating progress keeps effort going and stops you from taking results for granted. Rewards do not need to be expensive; their purpose is to show that your work is leading somewhere, and you should not spend all your earnings on them. [L1039, 2019+]
- Reward yourself for every measurable parameter of success, such as completing a task or reaching a milestone. Rewards trick the mind into taking action and keep the business moving forward. [L1043, undated]
- Gamify hard or boring work by timing how quickly it can be finished or setting a challenge, which makes progress feel like leveling up. [L1181, 2023]

### Measuring progress and celebrating wins

- Downplaying your wins makes you stop seeking what works. Measure progress, celebrate measurable gains, and then keep improving. [L0378, 2019+]
