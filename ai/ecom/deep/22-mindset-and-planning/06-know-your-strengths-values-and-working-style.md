## Know your strengths, values and working style

### Start with honest self-reflection

- Self-reflection exercises about work habits and thinking style have no right or wrong answers. Answering openly and honestly with yourself is what makes them useful. [L0802, 2021]
- Skipping self-reflection exercises because they feel uncomfortable reduces their value, since they are often the most useful part of personal business preparation. [L0802, 2021]
- Formal education, a strong academic record and exceptional intelligence are not prerequisites for building a profitable e-commerce business. Long-term persistence through slow or losing periods matters more than traditional credentials. [L0419, 2021-22]
- Persistence is the trait that carries successful sellers: checking the numbers every day, generating ideas, and running the team with genuine belief. Persistence does not mean stubbornly trying to solve everything alone. [L0780, 2024+]

### Review your past attempts honestly

- Reviewing past attempts at online business honestly, including what happened and why it ended, reveals recurring patterns such as quitting early or lacking capital. Address those patterns in the new plan rather than repeating them. [L1503, 2026]
- Answer each question of this self-assessment form in writing before starting an online business:

```
Personal self-assessment form to fill in honestly before starting an online business, answer each question in writing:
1. Name, age, and home country or city?
2. What do you currently do for work or study?
3. Why did you decide to start this business path now?
4. What is your strongest reason for wanting to succeed in ecommerce?
5. What is your main goal for the next 90 days?
6. What monthly or yearly income figure are you working toward?
7. Have you tried ecommerce, dropshipping, or any other online business before? If yes, what happened and why did it stop or fail?
8. What do you see as your biggest obstacle right now? Consider time, confidence, product research, ads, capital, and consistency.
9. What do you expect from a coach, course, or mentor, and how should they support you?
10. Imagine it is 12 months from today and everything worked out. Describe in concrete detail what your work, income, time, and daily life look like.
Commitment (sign or confirm): Guidance, support, and accountability can be provided by others, but results depend on taking consistent action yourself.
```
[L1503, 2026]

- Answering the planning questions quickly or vaguely undermines the whole plan, because the answers are the basis for setting realistic goals. Fill in each answer with specifics such as numbers, dates, and real constraints. [L1503, 2026]

### Map your personality and natural strengths

- Every person has a unique way of thinking and acting that works automatically and cannot be switched off. Trying to act against it drains energy, while working inside it makes tasks feel natural. [L0701, 2015]
- Myers-Briggs and DISC are personality assessments that help you understand how you think and deliver value. DISC is also used to read other people in one-on-one conversations so you can choose the right words. Free resources for both are available online. [L0701, 2015]
- The StrengthsFinder assessment from Gallup, described in Tom Rath's book, identifies your five natural strengths or talents. The Gallup study behind it reports that people operate about 80% of their lives from these five strengths, so work should be built around them. [L0701, 2015]
- Books alone rarely reveal your own strengths and working style. Get feedback from other people who know you well, and combine it with structured assessments such as Gallup's StrengthsFinder. [L0701, 2015]
- Many people stop using their natural way of working around ages 8 to 14, when they are told that what they do by default is not enough and that they must add unrelated skills to succeed. Rebuilding confidence starts with returning to that natural way of thinking and acting, not with adding more courses. [L0701, 2015]

### Define values, passion and purpose

- Values work as market selection: they decide which audience you want to serve and why. When your work fits your values, it becomes more enjoyable and marketing gets easier. [L0701, 2015]
- Problems that you notice in others, often before they notice them, are a strong starting point for a business. Those problems are usually linked to your natural way of seeing things. [L0701, 2015]

### Build in the right order

Work through these steps in sequence:

1. Understand your personality and how you process information. [L0701, 2015]
2. Identify your proficiencies, the five natural ways you think and act. [L0701, 2015]
3. Define your values, which determine who you want to work with and why. [L0701, 2015]
4. Find your passion, the drive to endure pain for the end result. [L0701, 2015]
5. Define your purpose, what you offer the world. [L0701, 2015]
6. Make a plan based on the steps above. [L0701, 2015]
7. Execute. [L0701, 2015]

- Plans that look complete on paper but collapse in execution, with about 50% failing right away, usually mean planning was done too early. Planning should come late, after personality, strengths, values and passion are clear. [L0701, 2015]
- Constantly second-guessing your marketing (target group, wording, message) usually signals that you do not yet know your own strengths and values well enough to speak clearly to customers. Fix the self-understanding first, then the messaging. [L0701, 2015]

### Check that your work fits your strengths

- Heavy work that produces small results usually means you are working outside your proficiencies and your natural working style. Re-check which tasks match your strengths before adding more effort. [L0701, 2015]

### Choose partners and direction carefully

- Repeated partnerships that do not match, where the cause is never clear, often come from unclear personal strengths and values rather than bad luck. Define both before entering a partnership. [L0701, 2015]
- Chasing every new opportunity (shiny button syndrome) keeps you from finishing anything and drains money and focus. Stay with one direction until it is defined by your strengths and values. [L0701, 2015]

### Name your obstacles

- Name your obstacles specifically in categories such as time, confidence, product research, ads, capital, and consistency, and rank the biggest one. Each named obstacle can then be matched to a concrete countermeasure. [L1503, 2026]

### Use the experience you already have

- Management experience from a corporate job transfers well to running a business, even when the person has no prior business ownership. Those skills in planning, team leadership and handling pressure apply directly to e-commerce. [L1014, 2021-22]
- Prior sales experience makes supplier contact much easier. Without it, expect the first calls to feel daunting, which is normal. [L1248, 2021-22]

#### Case: investing profits in an offline business (around 2009 to 2010)

Around 2009 to 2010, the owner invested profits from an e-commerce business into a digital signage company that installed displays in about 30 coffee shop locations in New York. The costs were very high because of computers, servers, screens, installation staff, and sales staff, and the business made no money. The owner sold it quickly, recovered only part of the cash, and concluded that offline businesses, managing many companies, and large teams were not a fit. [L1039, 2019+]

#### Case: a low-ticket store with China-shipped products (around 2011 to 2012)

Around 2011 to 2012, the owner built a low-ticket store selling products shipped from China, with an average order value of about $50, and drove traffic to it. Customers quickly complained about long shipping times, and no support systems existed for the volume of customers, so many were upset. The products were delivered, the website was shut down, and the lesson was that this model was not for them. Orders producing about $8 profit do not justify a large support team. [L1039, 2019+]

### Feelings of imposter syndrome

- Feelings of imposter syndrome are normal for entrepreneurs entering a new market. Push through them by taking small actions rather than waiting until you feel fully qualified. [L0453, 2021-22]
- The feeling of being a fraud at the start of a business usually comes from an unexplored personality. Clarifying your personality and the value you bring removes most of that feeling. [L0701, 2015]
- Feeling like an impostor is normal even for experienced creators, and nobody fully understands where their best ideas come from. Show up and do the work every day instead of waiting until you feel ready. [L1343, undated]

### Measure your confidence

Repeat this exercise to track how your confidence changes:

1. On a sheet of paper, rate from 1 to 10 how confident you are in your ability to do your job right now. [L0701, 2015]
2. Below the rating, list every task you have to do (for example marketing, setting up the store). [L0701, 2015]
3. Keep the sheet and repeat the 1 to 10 rating after the gratitude exercise to measure the change. [L0701, 2015]

## Commit your time, money and effort

### Written commitment and accountability

- Before you start a training program, write a concrete action plan. It states how many hours per day and how many days per week you will study and act, rather than just deciding to finish the material quickly. [L0444, undated]
- Your results depend on the actions you actually take. Without a written plan, the outcome is left to chance. Decide in advance which actions you will take and how often, so you shape your results instead of accepting whatever happens. [L0444, undated]
- Write a personal pledge in a notebook. Use this wording as the template:

```text
I will put in X hours each day and work on e-commerce X days every week.
I will keep this up until I have completed the course and tested at least 12 products.
Until I have tested 12 products, I will not doubt the method.
Regardless of circumstances, I will go through the entire course and test 12 products.
Date: [today's date]
Signature: [your signature]
```
[L0444, undated]

- Make the commitment official and accountable:
  1. Photograph or scan the handwritten, signed commitment. [L0444, undated]
  2. If you have a business partner, each of you signs a commitment and shows it to the other. [L0444, undated]
  3. If you work alone, send the signed commitment to the program's support contact by email so it is recorded and checked. [L0444, undated]
- Outside support such as coaching, mentoring or a course adds value only when you take consistent action. Results come from daily execution, so accountability is your responsibility, not the mentor's. [L1503, 2026]

### Realistic time minimums

- Put at least one hour per day into your business. Consistent daily effort produces measurable growth and results after one month, six months and one year. [L0280, undated]
- Avoid analysis paralysis by taking one concrete action every day toward your business, even while still learning. Combining action with learning is far more valuable than consuming information without acting. [L0280, undated]
- Set a realistic weekly time commitment for the program: a minimum of 2 hours per day on 6 days per week, which totals 12 hours per week. If you have fewer study days, compress the same 12 hours into fewer days, but do not go below 12 hours per week. [L0444, undated]
- Set a minimum effort that is realistic enough to produce results within the expected time. A very low, unrealistic minimum will probably not produce results, and a person who quits before the reasonable point never sees the outcome. [L0444, undated]
- Do not set an overly ambitious daily study target, such as 12 hours per day alongside a day job. Missing an unrealistic target leads to frustration, demotivation and the false belief that the training does not work. [L0444, undated]
- Consistency matters more than hours. Even 15 minutes per day adds up over time. [L0671, 2021]
- Consistency of daily effort matters more than the number of hours. Even one lesson plus its action item per day, for someone with only an evening free, can lead to a launched business within about a month, though it may take longer. [L1496, 2021-22]
- Squeezing study into very small, scattered time slots while pushing yourself hard is demotivating and raises the risk of dropping out. A daily time commitment you can sustain produces more progress. [L1496, 2021-22]
- Speed of progress does not determine long-run success. Anyone working with less time per day can be just as successful as someone working more, as long as they do the right things. [L0438, 2021-22] **

### Time for the day job

- Dropshipping can be run as a side business alongside a day job. Set aside time outside work hours to launch the store, then use outsourcing to reduce time spent once sales are coming in. [L0771, 2021-22] **
- A person with a full-time job can still build a business by committing a fixed daily block, for example half an hour at the same time every day. Use the same discipline you would apply in a workplace. [L0832, 2019]
- Scarce money can be offset by investing personal time. Working on a business before or after a day job and into the evening is a valid way to start with no capital. [L1041, 2023]
- Daily time after work can be used productively: keep testing new products, create content and build the store's traffic, and study marketing and design skills. [L1077, undated]
- If you still have a job, a mortgage or dependants, build the online business on the side. Leave the job only once the business produces steady income. Replacing the salary too early exposes the household to unnecessary risk. [L1014, 2021-22] **
- If you hold a full-time job while building a store, you cannot answer every call, email or chat live. Delayed replies are a normal limit of a side business, not a failure. [L0909, 2021-22] **
- Working on a business alongside a job lowers financial pressure but makes it easier to find excuses for skipping work. A stronger commitment comes from a clear personal reason and a fixed daily work schedule, whichever way the business is started. [L0826, 2016]
- Founders with no steady paycheck often push harder to make the business work. Commitment and drive matter more than free time when launching a store. [L0471, 2024] **
- Spreading time across too many projects at once is harmful. Start new ventures conservatively with a small share of time while keeping the established activity running. [L0805, 2024]

### Fixed routine and workplace

- Decide when in your week you will work on the business and keep that time. The business takes time to build before it produces revenue. [L0671, 2021]
- Set a fixed office location and fixed working hours, for example 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., and treat that time as work time. A kitchen table can work, but only if it is used for business at set hours and kept quiet. [L0832, 2019]
- An office does not have to be a room. A coffee shop works when traveling, as long as you sit down and set working hours. The office gives a defined space and start and end times for each goal. [L0832, 2019]
- Working from home alone without deadlines makes focus hard. Set self-imposed structure and discipline to keep working, especially when money is already coming in. [L0690, 2022+]
- Dropshipping can support location independence, but remote workers still need a fixed routine to stay productive. Working Monday to Friday with weekends fully off was the most effective rhythm for a remote operator. [L1441, 2018]
- The biggest reason beginners fail is not doing the work. Many people enroll but never log in, or log in only once. Commit to a regular schedule to make progress. [L0934, 2023]
- Do not stop partway through the setup. Without finishing the steps, no store can make a sale. [L0671, 2021]

### Starting capital and testing budget

- Use your own saved money, such as income from a job, savings or an inheritance, as the beginner testing budget. Avoid leveraged money such as bank loans. [L1394, 2017+]
- Do not fund beginner testing with credit cards. Using them at this stage is a major red flag. [L1394, 2017+]
- A large budget is not required to start. A small budget can push a beginner to work harder. Budget is rarely the reason a store fails. [L0934, 2023]
- Before launch, the main commitment in a dropshipping setup is time rather than money. Financial outlay is concentrated at the marketing stage that follows launch. [L0438, 2021-22] **
- Business costs for a new store can be a few hundred dollars if the owner does the setup work personally. Costs rise mainly when work such as logos, site build or uploads is outsourced. [L0801, 2021] **

### Working with limited capital

- When capital is limited, use other levers: skills, time and resources. If one lever is short, pull another, for example by learning Photoshop or Canva and filming products yourself. [L0561, 2023+]
- Assess your position on three levers: time, money and skill.
  1. If you have money but little time, outsource tasks. [L1041, 2023]
  2. If you have time but little money, do the work yourself. [L1041, 2023]
  3. Build skills in every case. When stuck, analyze your current situation to find a lever you can pull. [L1041, 2023]
- Starting capital example: about 300 pounds was enough to build a store and run a first Facebook ad of 45 pounds. Ads can be tested from as little as five pounds per day. [L0561, 2023+]

### Staffing and outsourcing in the early phase

- A store with a few suppliers still needs daily time for more approvals and uploads, even after launch. Expect this. [L0836, 2024] **
- When time is scarce, outsource product uploads and store build, and oversee the outsourced work. Consistent part-time work can keep a store moving. [L0471, 2024] **
- Without budget for staff, the founder does customer service, supplier contact and fulfilment personally at first. Calling suppliers before they close for the day, even very early in the morning, can be necessary when time zones are far apart. This phase ends when the store is profitable enough to hire help. [L0910, 2015]

### Buffers for the unknown and timelines

- Beginners underestimate the hurdles of a first business. Include a buffer for unknown difficulties in every time estimate, and plan slower than feels necessary to avoid burnout. [L0444, undated]
- Building a sustainable, profitable store can take months. The pace depends on how fast the owner works through tasks. A slower path still beats quitting, because stopping guarantees no results. Treat the work as delayed gratification: effort now produces returns later. [L1110, 2019] **
- A lead generation machine needs time in three parts:
  - 10 to 20 hours to write or outsource the lead magnet. [L1076, 2019]
  - 1 to 2 hours for the lead page. [L1076, 2019]
  - 5 to 10 hours to build the flow. [L1076, 2019]
  - Maximum total: about 20 to 30 hours. Let it run for one to two months before judging the results. [L1076, 2019]
- Stay with the commitment until the outcome is visible. Stay focused only on tasks that move the business forward, then commit to your goals and make a personal commitment to do whatever it takes to reach them. [L0419, 2021-22] **
- Apply what you learn every day, or whenever your schedule allows, even with a full-time job. Small steps count as long as you keep taking them consistently. [L0419, 2021-22] **
- The most common reason beginners fail is not doing the work. Many people enroll but never log in, or log in only once. A large budget is not needed to start, and a small budget can push a beginner to work harder. [L0934, 2023]
