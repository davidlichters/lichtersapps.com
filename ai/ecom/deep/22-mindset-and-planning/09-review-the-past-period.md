## Review the past period

### Timing and frequency

| Interval | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Annual, ideally in January | Review the past year before setting goals and a plan for the coming year. | [L0800, 2021] |
| Six-monthly | The same review can be repeated every six months. | [L0800, 2021] |
| Quarterly | The same review can be repeated every quarter. A quarterly review is also set when new Rocks are defined. | [L0800, 2021; L0441, 2021-22] |
| Shortest allowed interval | Do not review more often than once a quarter. | [L0800, 2021] |

Where the sources differ, the annual review is the baseline, and six-monthly or quarterly reviews are optional add-ons. The quarterly minimum applies to every interval. [L0800, 2021; L0441, 2021-22]

- Set a recurring calendar time for a quarterly review, held when new Rocks are defined, and for an annual review, when the business and its progress toward the yearly goals are assessed. [L0441, 2021-22]
- At the annual review, check whether the 10-year vision and the 3-year goals still fit, revise them if needed, then set the goals for the next year. [L0441, 2021-22]
- After the first 1-year plan, the next plan is a 2-year plan. [L0441, 2021-22]
- Quarterly reviews become more useful as the business grows and more changes happen in shorter periods. [L0800, 2021]
- Most growth actions need longer than a quarter to show results. Shorter review periods make it hard to judge what worked, and the time spent reviewing outweighs the benefit. [L0800, 2021]
- Start the review as soon as the period closes instead of postponing it to the end of the month. Complete it before you build the plan for the next period. [L0800, 2021]
- At the start of your planning period, review your business performance and results from the previous year, or from the months you were in business, to establish a baseline for improvement. [L0223, 2021]

### Preparing the review

- Use a spreadsheet annual review template, available as an Excel version for Windows or a Numbers version for Mac. [L0800, 2021]
- The template has a business half and a personal half. Each has the same seven questions: top goals, goals reached, what prevented goals, biggest weaknesses, unexploited opportunities, achievements you are proud of, and what you would have done differently. [L0800, 2021]
- The questions stay the same whatever the review interval. [L0800, 2021]
- Answer both the business and the personal section, since they reflect each other. Answers from one section can belong in the other, and that overlap is acceptable. [L0800, 2021]
- Limit each list to a maximum of five items, and never give one-word answers. Each line should be a complete statement with enough detail to explain what happened and why. [L0800, 2021]

### Mindset for the review

- Review the year objectively and without self-blame. Mistakes, failed experiments and missed goals are normal in every business, and the purpose of the review is to find what to change, not to judge yourself. [L0800, 2021]
- Learning systematically from past results is a defining trait of successful business owners, whether the previous year went well or poorly. The review turns past experience into the basis for changes in the next period. [L0800, 2021]
- Many people never change their situation because they do not analyze what they did and what did not work. Deliberately reviewing actions is what separates operators who improve from those who repeat the same mistakes. [L0800, 2021]
- Goals are targets to aim for, not a pass or fail test. Count progress toward a goal as a useful result even when the goal was not fully reached. [L0800, 2021]
- A missed goal does not make you a failure. It shows what to keep working on in the coming period. [L0800, 2021]
- Achievements can be listed even if you missed all your goals. Recognizing your wins is an important part of the review. [L0800, 2021]
- In a small or medium business, the owner's own performance strongly drives business results, so the review must cover both the business and the owner personally. Ignoring personal needs and personal performance puts the business at risk. [L0800, 2021]

### Business section

Procedure, in this order:

1. Write up to five top business goals you had for the past year, one answer per line, in complete sentences. Fewer than five is fine. [L0800, 2021]
2. List which of these business goals you reached. Leave the answer blank if you reached none. [L0800, 2021]
3. For each business goal, write one line on what prevented you from reaching it. Your own assessment may or may not be exactly right; the point is to think about the reason. [L0800, 2021]
4. List the biggest weaknesses in the business that stopped you from reaching your top goals. Write one line per weakness, up to five. [L0800, 2021]
5. List the unexploited business opportunities you identified during the year, even if they overlap with your weaknesses. [L0800, 2021]
6. List the business achievements you are most proud of in the past year. Include progress and firsts even if you missed your goals. [L0800, 2021]
7. List what you would have done differently in the business. Aim for as many items as you can think of, and revisit the list over several days. [L0800, 2021]

Notes on the business section:

- Examples of achievements worth listing: the first month with revenue above 100,000 in your currency; organic visits growing from about 500 to 2,500 per month; hiring the first virtual assistant; launching a first business; or taking consistent steps toward launching one. [L0800, 2021]
- List unexploited opportunities even if you never acted on them. They often reflect your weaknesses, so listing them alongside weaknesses is fine. [L0800, 2021]

### Personal section

Procedure, in this order:

1. Write up to five top personal goals for the past year. They can be non-business goals such as family time, fitness, health, or personal money targets, and they do not need to be recorded before. [L0800, 2021]
2. List which of these personal goals you reached. Leave the answer blank if you reached none. [L0800, 2021]
3. For each personal goal, write one line on what prevented you from reaching it. [L0800, 2021]
4. List your biggest personal weaknesses and bad habits, or the areas where you feel you could have performed better in life or in your work. Write up to five. [L0800, 2021]
5. List the unexploited personal opportunities you identified during the year. [L0800, 2021]
6. List the personal achievements you are most proud of, including anything reached such as weight targets, distance targets or new skills learned. [L0800, 2021]
7. List what you would have done differently personally in the past year. [L0800, 2021]

Notes on the personal section:

- Valid personal goals to review include spending time with family, fitness and health, starting a business, earning a personal income target from the business, buying a property, or investing in shares. [L0800, 2021]
- Personal weaknesses are often the hardest to identify, so give that question extra time. Negative findings in the review point to the opportunities for the coming year. [L0800, 2021]

### What you would have done differently (both sections)

- For this question, spend more than five minutes and list as many items as you can. [L0800, 2021]
- Include tried actions that failed and things you planned but never did. [L0800, 2021]
- Do this over several days, and come back after a break to find new items. [L0800, 2021]

### Turning the review into the next plan

- Use the answers from the review as the input for planning the next year, quarter or six-month period, because the weaknesses and opportunities identified here drive what gets planned. [L0800, 2021]
- Before building the plan, review the year behind you. List opportunities you have not fully exploited and identify your weaknesses. [L0447, 2021]
- Use these findings to set goals, and make sure at least one weakness is fixed within the six months. [L0447, 2021]
- Knowing your numbers is not enough by itself. Profit gains come only when you act on what the reports show, even on tasks that are not exciting. Set the next year's priorities from the analysis, not from sales targets alone. [L1046, 2021-22]
- Schedule a reset period, free of operational tasks, to review goals, business plans and new ideas from a high-level view. A virtual assistant who runs day-to-day tasks makes this possible. [L1000, 2017]

Turning results into routine changes:

1. After each result, decide whether it was a success or a weakness. [L1039, 2019+]
2. If an action worked, strengthen it so it becomes part of your routine. [L1039, 2019+]
3. If you spot a weakness, optimize that part of your routine before the next cycle. [L1039, 2019+]

## Write the 180-day plan

### Why a written plan matters

- Business goals are rarely reached by luck or disorganized action. A written plan gives daily to-do lists their direction and reduces wasted time, procrastination and lack of motivation. [L0447, 2021]
- Write down a business plan before starting, and treat it as a guide rather than a fixed rule. [L0829, 2019]
- Following scattered online marketing tips without a structured foundation leads to poor results, because tips do not show what matters first or how channels connect. A structured plan that sets numbers first and campaign types in order works better. [L1203, 2016]

### Planning horizon and timing

- Plan the next 180 days (about six months) rather than a full year, because a long horizon in an uncertain environment makes forecasts unreliable. Review the plan at the halfway point and adjust course if conditions have changed significantly. [L0447, 2021]
- Plan in January, after the year's review, when a high-ticket dropshipping business is often quieter. This makes it easier to look ahead and check whether the business is on track. [L0447, 2021]
- Time is the most valuable resource, so spend it on what makes the most money. Plan the next three months carefully, because they determine the following year. [L0334, 2017]
- In a compressed launch plan for a new branded dropshipping store, product research and validation can start in week 2 and overlap with store setup, because the research step can take only a few days. Ads and testing frameworks then begin around weeks 3 to 4, and the total timeline depends on how many hours per week the operator can give the project. [L0914, 2026]

### Setting priorities

- Limit goals and priorities to a maximum of five, because more than five are unlikely to be pursued seriously. Priorities should be major business changes for the year, not small recurring chores. [L0447, 2021]
- Typical six-month priorities include growing organic traffic, setting up an email marketing system, or getting 10 new suppliers approved and products uploaded. [L0447, 2021]
- Big business changes such as email marketing, SEO or widening the product range take a long time, so commit to them consistently even when distractions or new opportunities appear. [L0447, 2021]
- Outsourcing should be one of the priorities in a 180-day plan, and owners should check whether their plan already includes the tasks they need to hand off. [L1247, 2021]
- If you have fewer than five priorities, fill only the matching Priority task columns and leave the rest empty. [L0447, 2021]

### Task breakdown

- Example SEO task breakdown for six months: write 12 blog posts, promote those posts, run a link-building method on six of them, rewrite collection descriptions, run an SEO audit and fix duplicate content. [L0447, 2021]
- Use the plan as the daily reference: the top priority and its associated tasks become the day's to-do list, so you do not need to decide each morning what matters most. [L0447, 2021]

### Writing the plan

- Planning the 180 days can take ten minutes or a day or two. Finish it before starting the next task, and do not overthink it: get the goals and tasks onto the page first. [L0447, 2021]

### Planner sheet and tools

- The planning tool is a Google Sheets pair: a 180-day plan sheet and a goals and rocks tracker holding the quarter tabs. It follows the Course B structure and mirrors a 10-year, 3-year, 1-year and 90-day rocks planner. [L0447, 2021]
- The planner sheet has these sections and fields:

| Sheet | Fields |
|---|---|
| BSGR 180 Day Plan | Date (plan end and next review date); Revenue; Profit; 180 Day Goals/Priorities (no more than 5); Measurables; Paint The Picture; Priority 1 Tasks to Priority 5 Tasks |
| 90 DAY ROCKS - 1st Quarter | 90 Day Goals/Priorities; 90 Day Rocks (up to 7 rocks) |
| 90 DAY ROCKS - 2nd Quarter | 90 Day Goals/Priorities; 90 Day Rocks (up to 7 rocks) |

[L0447, 2021]

- For the second half of the year, duplicate the original planner and change its quarter tabs to third and fourth quarter instead of editing the first-half version. [L0447, 2021]

### Checking earlier plans

- Before writing a new 180-day plan, check any earlier long-range plan (10-year, 3-year, one-year and 90-day rocks). Copy across the items that still hold and revise the ones that no longer match the business. [L0447, 2021]

### Holding the plan

- Do not change the plan during its term. It stays fixed until the six-month end date, and only then is a new plan written. [L0447, 2021]
- Once a plan is set, follow it through to its end without changing it mid-way, unless a major personal event occurs. Change course only at the scheduled review. [L0447, 2021]

### Review date in the calendar

- Fix the review date now by scheduling a full day in the calendar roughly six months ahead to review the past period and decide what must change based on national or international conditions. [L0447, 2021]

### The planning cycle

1. Plan your business priorities and goals for the next 180 days (six months). [L0223, 2021]
2. Schedule a review date on your calendar for approximately six months ahead, around mid-year (June or July). [L0223, 2021]
3. On that date, review what you actually accomplished against your plan. [L0223, 2021]
4. Use the same planning tool to create your next 180-day plan based on the results and learnings from the previous period. [L0223, 2021]

## Set 90-day Rocks and measurables

### Why a 90-day plan matters

- Success in dropshipping comes from building systems and staying consistent, not from testing random products. A structured 90-day roadmap gives clarity and focus to move from beginner to consistent revenue. [L0199, 2026]
- Rocks are the priorities for the next 90 days and come from the goals set for the business. [L0441, 2021-22]
- Rocks are 90-day goals that break a one-year target into quarterly chunks. Each quarter, ask which goals must be hit to reach the one-year target. [L0679, 2021-22]
- Rocks give the business momentum and traction, so the owner feels progress even when growth is slower than hoped. Long periods without visible momentum lead to stagnation, self-doubt and dislike of the business, and recovering from that gets harder over time. Setting Rocks is therefore a proactive way to avoid that spiral. [L0679, 2021-22]

### Writing a valid Rock

- Every Rock must be specific, attainable and measurable, and it must be reviewed every 90 days. [L0679, 2021-22]
- A goal that is neither specific nor measurable, such as *more profit next quarter*, is not a valid Rock. [L0679, 2021-22]
- Example of a valid Rock: *Make an additional $60,000 profit in the next 90 days*. It is specific and measurable, so the result can be checked easily. A vague goal about more profit fails both tests. [L0679, 2021-22]
- Keep Rocks realistic for the 90-day window. People tend to overestimate what they can achieve in a short period and underestimate what they can achieve over a long period. A business with $100,000 in total sales to date should not set a Rock of $1,000,000 in one quarter. [L0679, 2021-22]
- Set no more than 7 Rocks per quarter, because more goals make focus difficult. [L0679, 2021-22]
- Use Rocks only for big-impact opportunities, such as:
  - a new product
  - promoting an existing bestseller
  - a new traffic source
  - an email marketing system
  - a hiring plan
  - a new business [L0679, 2021-22]
- Do not use Rocks for routine tasks, such as answering emails. [L0679, 2021-22]

### Breaking a one-year target into measurables

Older advice (2021-22). Worked chain:

1. One-year measurable: 600,000 extra organic search visitors. [L0679, 2021-22]
2. 90-day measurable: first publish 10 new pieces of content. [L0679, 2021-22]
3. Each content piece becomes a Rock, such as hiring a writer or writing one piece every two weeks. [L0679, 2021-22]
4. Weekly to-do lists carry these steps: in week one, write the full piece; in week two, find images and upload it. [L0679, 2021-22]

### Barrier issues

- Barrier issues are the obstacles that could derail a plan. Write them down next to the Rocks and measurables. [L0679, 2021-22]
- When a barrier appears, check whether other measurables or Rocks need to change to free up the resources it needs. For example, if there is not enough time for the content, adjust other commitments to make room. [L0679, 2021-22]
- If the owner does not know how to do a required task, that gap is a barrier issue. Solve it by making the acquisition of the missing skill a 90-day Rock, followed by a later Rock for putting that knowledge into practice. [L0679, 2021-22]
- Common mistake: do not schedule an entire Rock into a single week. A complex task such as hiring and training someone cannot be done properly within a week. Only a truly urgent item should be squeezed into one week. [L0679, 2021-22]

### Rolling 90-day window and quarterly review

- Set Rocks one quarter at a time, always working inside a rolling 90-day window. [L0679, 2021-22]
- Each Rock set is reviewed every 90 days, and a new set is defined every quarter. [L0441, 2021-22; L0679, 2021-22]
- The 90-day window can start at any time and does not have to match the start of a calendar quarter. Standard financial quarters (Q1 to Q4) are optional. [L0679, 2021-22]
- The first set of Rocks should cover the starting point toward the one-year plan. Build the 90-day sheet after the three-year picture and the one-year plan are filled in. [L0679, 2021-22]

### Linking Rocks to weekly and daily work

- Once the Rocks for the quarter are set, they define the weekly and daily to-do lists. [L0679, 2021-22]
- Worked example of this link: the content Rock is broken into weekly steps, with the full piece written in week one and images found and uploaded in week two. [L0679, 2021-22]

### First 90 days: the one source roadmap

**Overall sequence.** Run the six phases over 90 days in this order:

1. Days 1-14: Foundation [L0199, 2026]
2. Days 15-30: Product Research & Validation [L0199, 2026]
3. Days 31-45: Store Building [L0199, 2026]
4. Days 45-60: Creative Production [L0199, 2026]
5. Days 60-75: Launching Ads [L0199, 2026]
6. Days 75-90: Optimization & Scaling [L0199, 2026]

Note: as the source lists them, day 45 falls in both Phase 3 and Phase 4, and day 60 falls in both Phase 4 and Phase 5. [L0199, 2026]

**Phase 1 Foundation checklist (Days 1-14).**

1. Understand branded dropshipping as a business model. [L0199, 2026]
2. Set up Shopify with a custom domain and email address. [L0199, 2026]
3. Configure Facebook Business Manager and install your Pixel. [L0199, 2026]
4. Learn the product criteria for dropshipping. [L0199, 2026]
5. Learn advertising angles and how to identify testable products. [L0199, 2026]

Spend zero budget on ads in this phase. Focus entirely on building knowledge and technical infrastructure. [L0199, 2026]

**Rule for Phase 1.** Phase 1 requires zero ad spend. All effort and resources go into technical infrastructure and knowledge, so that Phase 5 ad spending becomes efficient rather than wasteful. [L0199, 2026]

**Mistake to avoid.** Starting Phase 5 (ad spend) before completing Phases 1-4 wastes budget on untested funnels and unrefined creatives. Each phase builds the knowledge and infrastructure the next one requires, and skipping steps multiplies risk. [L0199, 2026]
