### Cases

#### US supplier beating a Chinese price (case)

- A seller found a US supplier located in Minnesota that beat the price of an existing Chinese supplier. The Chinese price was about $4 per set of three books. The US supplier offered about $2 per set at higher order volumes and cut shipping cost and time. The seller planned to place orders slightly above $2 per set and expected prices to fall as order quantities grow. ** [L1153, 2016]

#### Fluffy brand: wearable blanket business in Switzerland (case)

- Brand name: Fluffy, a wearable blanket for women 40+, sold in Switzerland. Offers on the store (about 30 off, free Swiss shipping, 2 to 3 day delivery from Swiss stock, buy two save 14, single unit save $5 with a code): see 07-offers-pricing-and-bundles/11-pop-up-sms-and-email-codes.md. [L0492, 2023+]

- Best Dealer was the main supplier for the blanket stock. [L0492, 2023+]
- Custom designs require a minimum order quantity of 500 pieces from the supplier. [L0492, 2023+]
- The landed cost of one blanket was about 20 to 25 USD by air freight. It fell to about 7 to 10 USD with sea freight at large order volumes. [L0492, 2023+]
- The first batch of 500 blankets cost about $10,000 in product. Later packaging for a second batch cost about $500, bringing the total to about $20,000. [L0492, 2023+]
- Order the next season's stock early and ship it by sea to lower the per-unit cost. For a winter season, order in April and ship by sea. [L0492, 2023+]
- Supplier production paused over Chinese New Year. After the pause, plan for 7 to 15 business days of shipping from the supplier to the customer. [L0492, 2023+]

#### Australian fashion seller expanding to Switzerland (case)

- Starting point: an Australian fashion seller tested products and made most money in Australia with women's shoes and bags for women over 45. [L1212, undated]
- Actions: a product with custom branding was bulk-ordered, with a minimum of 500 pieces for custom logo hoodies, and shipped to a warehouse in Switzerland. Packaging was ordered too. The wearable blanket sold well in Switzerland for skiing and football fans. [L1212, undated]
- Plans: find a supplier in Europe, for example in Turkey, for cheaper delivery and customization. The seller was currently ordering from Best Dealer. [L1212, undated]
- Best Dealer is a supplier used for ordering a wearable blanket and hoodies with custom branding. [L1212, undated]

#### Sunglasses production time (case)

- The handmade sunglasses took about 90 days to produce per pair. Long production lead times like this must be included in inventory and promotion planning. [L0790, 2017]

- One operator paid an unknown private supplier, received silver jewelry instead of the ordered gold jewelry, and had to refund most customers. [L1017, undated]

### Validate Demand Before Buying Stock

- Placing a large upfront stock order before a product is validated can lock a store into one supplier and raise costs. Start with small test orders, compare the price with private agents, and only buy bulk after consistent sales. [L1315, 2023]
- Large bulk stock purchases before a sale create cash-flow and stock risk. Unsold stock locks up capital; in the lesson, about 10,000 units were still unsold. Sales are hard to forecast, so scale stock in step with proven demand. [L1363, 2023]
- Buying stock in advance carries risk: if one variant of a multi-variant product does not sell, the money tied up in that variant is lost. [L1440, 2021+]
- Build inventory through a repeated loop: order a small batch, sell it out, reinvest the revenue into a slightly larger reorder, and repeat. This keeps risk low while you learn what sells. [L1438, 2023+]
- Before buying a full range of designs from a supplier, order a single sample to judge quality, and use it for product photography. Only designs that look good in real life should be ordered in stock. [L1439, 2024+]
- Do not commit to selling products you cannot fulfil. Forecast demand and account for shipment times and production times, so orders can be delivered as promised. [L1438, 2023+]

### Minimum Order Quantities and Test Variations

- Most suppliers set a minimum order quantity of around 50 pieces per item. Look for suppliers who accept a lower minimum and offer more variations, such as three or four colours, so you can test which version sells best before reordering. [L1438, 2023+]

### Finding and Comparing Manufacturers

- Alibaba and AliExpress list factories with ratings, reviews and contact information, which helps you compare manufacturers. Many suppliers also list their email addresses and phone numbers on their own websites. [L1438, 2023+]
- When choosing a manufacturer, look for proof that they have made similar products before, good communication, and an established network of fabric suppliers, pattern cutters and pattern makers who can produce your design. [L1438, 2023+]
- Choose the manufacturing country by whether its factories have the fabric network and pattern-making skills your specific product needs, not by price alone. For this clothing brand, production was split between China and Pakistan, two regions used for apparel manufacturing. [L1438, 2023+]
- Switching manufacturers between repeat orders of the same garment can change its cut and shape, so returning customers receive an item that looks and fits differently and feel let down. Keep the same supplier for repeat designs so the product stays consistent. [L1438, 2023+]

### Apparel Development Workflow

Clothing development workflow:

1. Gather initial ideas from your own inspiration, other brands, street fashion and luxury runway shows.
2. Draw the designs (the founder used Photoshop) and make initial drawings.
3. Create a detailed tech pack covering measurements, fabrics, washes, colours and material weights.
4. Choose the fabrics together with the supplier.
5. Order a sample and wait for it to come back.
6. Note every change you want before the bulk order.
7. Place the bulk order.

[L1438, 2023+]

### Tech Packs and Sampling

- A detailed tech pack reduces production errors and misunderstandings, especially across language barriers. The more precise the specification, the smoother manufacturing runs for both you and the supplier. [L1438, 2023+]
- Plan for two or three sample rounds before the final product when starting with a new design or supplier. With an established supplier and the same design, you may only need to pay for one sample, with further adjustments made free of charge. [L1438, 2023+]

### Rush Production

- For products with long lead times, ask the supplier whether you can pay an overtime or labour fee to shorten production (rush production). Then charge that fee to the customer as a markup. [L1357, 2018]

### Private Label, Custom Labelling and Trademarks

- Custom labelling and private label products are not realistic for a beginner with a contract, because a single Chinese factory cannot be bound by contract to stay loyal unless it is a very large brand. A cheaper, more practical route is custom labelling, such as a logo on the product, custom packaging and boxing, combined with a better customer experience. [L1440, 2021+]
- A trademark or patent for a private-label product applies only in the country where it is registered, so a brand sold in several markets needs protection in each one. Factor the cost and time of these registrations into any private-label plan, along with sampling and contracts. [L1440, 2021+]

### US Forwarding Addresses and Warehousing

- Older advice: A US forwarding address with warehouse storage adds investment and operational complexity. Use it only if you are fully convinced the products will sell exceptionally well. [L1279, 2021]

#### Further details

- White label suppliers often have a minimum order quantity (MOQ) that the buyer must meet before the manufacturer will add their label to the product. [L1151, 2021-22]
