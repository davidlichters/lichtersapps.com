## Call Sequence and the First Conversation

**Goal of the first call**

- The goal of the first contact is only to reach the decision maker, not to close the deal. Keep the early conversation short and focused on introduction, your planned launch and your reasons for choosing them. [L0928, 2020-26]
- Keep calls short, maintain professionalism and focus on clarity and confidence. [L0126, undated]

**Full call procedure (17 steps)**

1. Research the supplier's website and company positioning to identify why their products fit your niche. [L0133, undated]
2. Find the supplier's phone number via Google search or directly on their website, looking for a toll-free number. [L0133, undated]
3. Call and ask for the person in charge of eCommerce accounts. If unavailable, request their email and/or direct phone number for a callback. [L0133, undated]
4. When you reach the eCommerce decision maker, begin by briefly explaining that you own an eCommerce store focused on their niche and want to add quality suppliers to your catalog. [L0133, undated]
5. Give a brief overview of your company without referring to any other websites you operate. [L0133, undated]
6. Explain that your team is finishing a major project in their niche that you are about to launch. [L0133, undated]
7. Share your excitement, noting that your team has extensively researched the niche and is confident you can dominate it with your marketing approach. [L0133, undated]
8. State that you are building out your catalog before launching a major marketing push on [specific date: 1-2 months from today]. [L0133, undated]
9. Reference specific findings from their website that show why their products are a perfect fit for your customers. [L0133, undated]
10. Express confidence that your company will become one of their top retailers within weeks of launching. [L0133, undated]
11. Emphasize building a long-term relationship, similar to partnerships you have developed with other suppliers in other niches. [L0133, undated]
12. Clearly state you are not looking for special payment terms, only a solid business relationship. [L0133, undated]
13. Ask directly how to apply for an eCommerce account. [L0133, undated]
14. If they say no or that they are not taking new retailers, explain how it is a shame they will miss out on the significant launch you are planning. [L0133, undated]
15. Ask who specifically needs to be involved to make the partnership happen. [L0133, undated]
16. Even if declined, understand they will remember this call and who you are, which positions you for future contact. [L0133, undated]
17. Document the conversation completely: the person's name, the date and all details discussed. [L0133, undated]

**Eight-step workflow (decision maker focus)**

1. Open the master supplier list and make sure each supplier has a URL, phone number and email. [L0928, 2020-26]
2. Call first whenever a phone number exists. Use email only when no phone is available. [L0928, 2020-26]
3. On the call, introduce yourself and your store, state the planned launch and why you like their products, and ask how to apply for a supplier account. [L0928, 2020-26]
4. Ask to be connected to the decision maker, who is the person who sends the approval form or grants access. [L0928, 2020-26]
5. Speak with the decision maker, reference something specific you learned from their website, and ask for the dealer application form to be emailed. [L0928, 2020-26]
6. As soon as they promise the form, give your email address, thank them and confirm you will return it right away. [L0928, 2020-26]
7. Fill out and return the form the same day you receive it. [L0928, 2020-26]
8. Log every contact in the tracking columns, then follow up on the agreed date. [L0928, 2020-26]

- When a supplier agrees to send an approval or dealer application form by email, give them your email address during the call, thank them, and confirm you will send it back right away. Then check your inbox, fill out the form and return it immediately. [L0928, 2020-26]
- Call the supplier and ask to reach the right decision maker during that same call. Ask them to email the approval form right after the call. If a promised callback or email does not arrive within a few days, follow up. Return the completed form as soon as possible. ** [L0853, 2021]
- Do not ask a supplier many detailed questions before they send their approval form, because many answers are in that form. Ask follow-up questions after the form is returned. ** [L0853, 2021]
- Trade account contact (2022): 1) Call and ask for the person in charge of opening new accounts. 2) If it is a small company, that person may answer directly; if larger, ask who handles it. 3) For email outreach, a short intro works: state you are interested in selling their products and ask who opens new accounts for internet retailers. ** [L0969, 2022]
- Getting a supplier approved by phone:
  1. Call the supplier rather than relying on email, since phone gives a faster answer. [L1090, 2021]
  2. Say you want to open an account as an online retailer and ask who handles new retailer accounts. [L1090, 2021]
  3. If nobody answers, ask how to email or whom to contact for a new account. [L1090, 2021]
  4. Follow up by email if no one calls back, because follow-up is part of the process. [L1090, 2021]
  5. If no phone number is listed, use supplier research tools to find other contact numbers. [L1090, 2021]
- Calling a supplier to ask about dropshipping stores (undated):
  1. Call the supplier and ask whether they work with dropshipping stores. [L0495, 2020-26]
  2. If they agree, ask them to send you the supplier application, and give them your email address so they can send it. [L0495, 2020-26]
  3. Fill in the application and return it to the supplier as soon as possible. [L0495, 2020-26]
  4. When the supplier sends you their product information, upload their products to your store right away. [L0495, 2020-26]
  5. Repeat these steps with more suppliers until at least five suppliers are approved and all their products are live on your store. [L0495, 2020-26]
- Trade account procedure (2020):
  1. Contact the brand by phone or email and ask for an account. [L0468, 2020]
  2. Ask which format they use for inventory updates and whether they can send a regular report. [L0468, 2020]
  3. Ask for the top three best-selling products with their weight and packaging size. [L0468, 2020]
  4. Record every answer, the reason for any rejection, and the date to follow up. [L0468, 2020]
  5. If the brand does not respond, follow up every few days. If it rejected you, follow up when the stated condition is met. [L0468, 2020]
- Approval workflow (2023):
  1. Call the supplier first and ask for the name and direct email of the person responsible for retailer relationships, even if they say to email. [L1094, 2023]
  2. Send a short, direct email to that contact that mentions the phone call, the store link and a request for the next steps. [L1094, 2023]
  3. If there is no reply after a few days, reply to your own first email with a short follow-up. [L1094, 2023]
  4. Keep applying to more suppliers until you have at least two approvals. [L1094, 2023]
- Outreach for approval (2023), in short form: call the supplier and ask for the name and direct email of the person who handles retailer relationships, even if they first say to email. Send a very short email to that contact that names the person you spoke with, includes the store link and asks for the next steps. If there is no reply after a few days, answer your own first email with a brief follow-up. Keep contacting more suppliers until at least two are approved before going live. [L1094, 2023]

**Call scripts and rules**

- Use phone scripts as conversation frameworks rather than reading them verbatim. Before calling, review the script and prepare to speak naturally. [L0126, undated]
- Do not present your business as established. Keep to what is true, and never claim anything you do not have. [L0365, 2020-26] *(See the honesty section below.)*
- Send Day 1 emails only if the supplier requests them. [L0126, undated]
- Do not bring up dropshipping, and do not say that you only want to resell their products (the opening pitch). [L0928, 2020-26]
- Note the conflict: [L0928, 2020-26] says not to bring up dropshipping in the pitch, while [L0495, 2020-26] has you ask whether the supplier works with dropshipping stores. Use the wording of the first pitch from [L0928, 2020-26]. Ask the dropshipping question in the step of [L0495, 2020-26] only if the supplier raises the subject or the step requires it. [L0928, 2020-26; L0495, 2020-26]

#### Opening script, phone (2023 version)

```
Hi, [name of person who answers], this is [your name] calling from [your company name]. We run a group of boutique e-commerce shops across several niches, and recently we spotted an opening in the [supplier's product niche] niche. We have just launched [store name, URL] and are looking for dependable suppliers who want to work with our shop. Based on our early research, my team and I believe our marketing will make this new venture our strongest yet. We are not asking for credit terms or anything similar; we simply want a sound working relationship. Could you tell me how we would go about applying for an account with [supplier name]? Once you have said this, stop and listen to their reply. Do they offer to help directly, or do they refer you to someone else? Write down the name of anyone they mention. Do not bring up dropshipping, and do not say that you only want to resell their products.
```
[L0928, 2020-26]

#### Pre-launch script, phone

```
Hi, [name of person who answers], this is [your name] calling from [your company name]. We are only a few [time frame, e.g. weeks or months] away from opening [store URL]. We are seeking reputable brands that want to supply our customers with the finest niche products, with the best [product type]. Given our research, we think your range would be a strong addition to our catalog, and we would be delighted to have you on board for our big launch. We are not requesting payment terms or anything of that nature; we just want to build a solid business relationship. Could you let me know how we can apply for an account with [supplier name]? Never claim to have existing stores or sales if you do not have them. Describe only the store you are actually building.
```

#### Phone script for a supplier who wants a dealer application

```
Hi, this is [your name] calling from [your store name]. We would love to sell your products because of [reason 1], [reason 2] and [reason 3]. Could you tell me the best way to get our dealer application approved?

If the supplier says they are not opening new e-commerce accounts right now, respond with:

That's too bad, because I know your products would fit well into our collection, and I know we would be able to promote them to our audience. Could you let me know when would be the best time to follow up?

When the supplier says it is not possible at this time, respond with:

Okay, we are moving forward with our opening date, and we would love to work with you in the future. When would be the best time to follow up?

Add the follow-up date and the supplier's answer to your notes list, and follow up on that date with your results.
```

## Email Outreach

- Copy email templates directly from the tool and customize them only slightly if needed. Send Day 1 emails only if the supplier requests them. Follow-up emails from Day 2 onward keep momentum without pressure; use them if the initial contact receives no response. [L0126, undated]
- Email procedure: 1. Email the supplier and introduce your store, including the store link. 2. Ask the supplier for product images for the description and for the prices. 3. Upload the products once the images and prices arrive; many suppliers reply yes right away. [L0832, 2019]


## Presenting Your Business Honestly

- Stay honest when contacting suppliers. There is no need to lie about who you are or what you have done to get a good supplier relationship. [L0365, 2020-26]
- Supplier sourcing templates and Q&A forms are available in the sourcing section to structure supplier outreach. Use them to present your business accurately. [L0365, 2020-26]
- Never claim a longer history than you have. If your store is not live yet, point to any earlier online or offline business experience you can honestly name. [L0933, 2020-26]
- When a supplier asks how long you have been in business, the question is about whether they can trust you to stay around and treat their brand with respect. [L0933, 2020-26]
- Established suppliers judge whether a new store will last mainly by its track record. A store that is not live yet should say so and point only to real earlier online or offline business experience, with no inflated history. [L0933, 2020-26]
- Our store is a new venture, and we are launching it now as part of our launch strategy. [If you have prior experience: Before this, we ran [previous business, for example an eBay store or an offline business] for [number of years].] [L0933, 2020-26]

#### Template: new venture with optional prior experience

```
Our store is a new venture, and we are launching it now as part of our launch strategy. [If you have prior experience: Before this, we ran [previous business, for example an eBay store or an offline business] for [number of years].]
```
[L0933, 2020-26]

**Using "I" and "we"**

- In outreach emails from a one-person store, writing "I" instead of "we" is acceptable, and a small mismatch like this is unlikely to end a supplier deal. Using the word "niche" in supplier communication is also fine. *(2023.)* [L0470, 2023]
- When speaking to a supplier, using "we" to refer to your business is acceptable as long as you do not claim anything untrue. The "we" refers to your company, not to a false claim about size or history. ** [L0960, 2019]

**Expertise in a technical niche**

1. Do not claim expertise you do not have. [L0967, 2020]
2. Study the niche and its product types at a surface level, for example through a few hours of video covering the basics. [L0967, 2020]
3. Tell the supplier honestly that you have a working knowledge and aim to become an expert. [L0967, 2020]
4. State that you want a good working relationship so you can educate customers and build a resource section on the store. [L0967, 2020]

## Demo Store, Launch Date and Existing Stores

**Demo store**

- When contacting suppliers, always have a demo store ready and mention it during approval conversations. Show it as a living example of how their products will appear on your store. ** [L0249, 2022]
- Always inform a supplier in advance that your store is in demo mode. This frame prevents suppliers from misinterpreting a limited product catalog or demo placeholder items as evidence of an unfinished or fraudulent operation. [L0294, 2025+]
- Sample wording for demo mode: "My name is [Your Name], I've been building e-commerce stores for [your years of experience], please visit this site to see how your products would look. The store is launching on [specific launch date]." [L0294, 2025+]
- During supplier review you can share your store template with a stated launch date, so the supplier can see how their products would look on it. Do not present the unfinished store as an active shop with products for sale. ** [L0834, 2021]
- Do not start with cold calls to request authorization, even when a demo store already exists. First determine why a supplier would choose you, and set up legitimacy signals beforehand. [L1063, 2020-26]
- Note the sources: [L0249, 2022] says always have the demo store ready and mention it during approval conversations. [L1063, 2020-26] says a demo store does not justify a cold call that asks for authorization. Use the demo store in the approval conversation, after you have established why the supplier should choose you. [L0249, 2022; L1063, 2020-26]

**Existing stores**

- If you already have successful online stores (dropshipping or otherwise), mention this when contacting suppliers. It helps with approval regardless of whether the stores are in the same niche. ** [L0249, 2022]
- Never claim to have existing stores or sales if you do not have them. Describe only the store you are actually building. [L0928, 2020-26]

**Launch date**

- When calling suppliers, specify a launch date 1-2 months in the future. Use an actual date you have set for your marketing launch, not a hypothetical timeline, so you can reference real results in follow-up calls and strengthen credibility. [L0133, undated]
- State a real planned launch date when speaking to a supplier, and keep it no more than two months in the future. A date further out makes the supplier treat you as not relevant now and ask you to come back later. [L0928, 2020-26]
- When giving a supplier a launch date, a window of about three to four weeks is typical, though a fixed date such as January 1 can also be given. [L1094, 2023]
- Describing your launch plan to a supplier works best when you have actually prepared it. [L0927, 2020-26]
- Use the launch strategy, not previous store history, to build excitement. Reference the launch date to set expectations. Include scripts.6 or Drop Ship Weekly training. ** [L0268, 2020]

**Launch date: sources differ**

| Source | Rule | Ids |
|---|---|---|
| Default | 1-2 months ahead, an actual date you have set | [L0133, undated] |
| Limit | Never more than two months ahead | [L0928, 2020-26] |
| Typical window | About three to four weeks, or a fixed date such as January 1 | [L1094, 2023] |

Use the 1-2 month window as the default. Keep the date within two months, and use the 3-4 week window when you need a short, realistic timeline.

- Use the launch date in the supplier pitch to ground the conversation. [L0133, undated; L0928, 2020-26]

## Traffic and Sales Questions

- During a launch, if you have no traffic yet, tell suppliers you plan to send hundreds of qualified leads to your website every day. Do not present current traffic numbers you do not have. [L0933, 2020-26]
- Suppliers want partners who add value, mainly through orders. Projected sales are essentially a guess, so do not invent a sales forecast. State a baseline instead: a brand must be able to reach at least 10,000 USD per month in sales through your store, otherwise it is not a good fit. [L0933, 2020-26]

#### Template: traffic and baseline sales answer

```
Right now we do not have traffic yet, but we are planning to send hundreds of qualified leads to our website every single day. I do not want to make up a sales number. What I can tell you is that if I cannot get you at least 10,000 USD a month in sales, I do not think it would be a good fit for us. So our baseline goal is at least 10,000 USD a month in sales for your brand on [your store domain].
```
[L0933, 2020-26]

- For traffic, use the statement from [L0933, 2020-26] and avoid any figure you cannot support. [L0933, 2020-26]
