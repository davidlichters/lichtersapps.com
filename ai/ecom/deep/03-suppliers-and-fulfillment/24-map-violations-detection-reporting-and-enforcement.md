### MAP violations: detection, reporting and enforcement

**Confirm and document the violation**

- Take a screenshot of every case where a competitor breaks the MAP policy, including sales on Amazon. Send the screenshots to your supplier's sales rep. Expect a quick fix if the supplier enforces the policy; a slow response shows how strictly it is enforced. [L0356, 2019]
- Take a screenshot of the competitor's product page showing the price that violates MAP, and confirm the price is live on several devices. (2025.) [L0837, 2025]
- To report a competitor selling a MAP product below the minimum advertised price, take a screenshot of the lower price. (2025.) [L0857, 2025]
- When a competitor sells below MAP and your ads face unfair price competition, take screenshots of the violating listings and send them to your supplier. (2023.) [L0470, 2023]
- Take screenshots of the below-MAP listing before contacting the supplier. (2026.) [L0808, 2026]

**Report to the supplier**

Procedure:

1. Contact your supplier representative.
2. Send screenshots of the violation.
3. See whether the supplier takes action.
4. Do not match the competitor's price yourself.

### Competitor pricing for non-MAP products

- For non-MAP supplier products, search for that brand on Google Shopping and check what prices competitors list the products at. (2025.) [L0255, 2025]
- Judge a supplier by its own price list before listing products. Two suppliers in one niche can offer different margins. [L0671, 2021]
- If competitors mark a product up only 10% or 15%, do not list it. This is a no-go. [L0671, 2021]
- Non-MAP supplier products: list the product on your site with free traffic only if competitors list at very low markup, around 5% margin. (2025.) [L0255, 2025]
- Non-MAP suppliers: do not advertise where competitors undercut your price on every listing. (2022.) [L0969, 2022]

### Negotiation and supplier handling checklist

Use this order when a new supplier is added:

1. Get the approved supplier's price list and upload its products. [L0941, 2022]
2. Confirm whether a MAP policy exists and whether the supplier enforces it. [L0466, 2018; L0941, 2022]
3. Calculate the margin per product including shipping, packaging, 3PL pick fees and payment fees. [L0433, 2025-26; L0222, 2025-26; L1046, 2021-22]
4. Compare the spread against the 50% rule and the 2x floor. [L0358, 2020; L0268, 2020; L0665, 2022]
5. Decide per product whether it is paid, free-only or dropped. [L0665, 2022; L0671, 2021; L0968, 2021]
6. Ask for better margins, incentive pricing and volume tiers. [L0934, 2023; L0941, 2022; L0967, 2020]
7. Once volume is proven, renegotiate. [L0939, 2020-26; L0780, 2024+]

### Priorities and sequencing

- In the early stage, focus optimisation efforts on conversions and product selection. Negotiating with suppliers gets easier once you have proven sales volume. [L0208, 2024+]
- Build steady demand before cost savings. Once steady sales are proven, negotiate supplier prices. [L0644, undated]
- Keep a backup supplier so the price can be justified, and only switch when the new supplier matches or beats price and service. [L0780, 2024+; L0644, undated]

### Checks before listing a product

#### Set up each supplier separately

- Pricing rules, margins and MAP requirements belong to each supplier, not to the niche. A niche is not disqualified by one low-margin supplier if others offer the 50%-of-MAP structure. [L1090, 2021]
- Margins are set per supplier and can differ within one store. Some suppliers offer 15%, others 20% or 30%, so track margin per supplier, not as one store-wide figure. [L1480, 2021-22]
- Individual products within one supplier can also have different margins. [L1090, 2021]
- MAP requirements differ from brand to brand. Run the 2x wholesale check for each supplier, not once across the catalog. If a brand's MAP is too close to its wholesale cost, negotiate better pricing before listing its products. [L1086, 2019]

#### Check the pricing rules before discounting

- Ask the supplier for its MAP policy before you discount. Many suppliers allow discounts, but some forbid coupon codes. An automatic coupon that pushes the advertised price below MAP can break the policy. [L1088, 2020]
- Extra discounts or coupon codes on top of MAP are allowed unless the supplier's retailer approval agreement forbids them. This restriction is rare. If a supplier has it, exclude that brand from promotions. [L1086, 2019]
- To find a brand's MAP without asking the supplier, search the brand name on Google Shopping and compare the prices sellers list for its products. Ask the supplier directly only if this does not reveal the price. [L1113, 2022]
- If a supplier's listed online price is below its MAP, ask the supplier about it immediately. Do not assume MAP is being enforced. [L1111, 2020]

#### The 50% rule (wholesale against MAP)

- Wholesale cost should be no more than 50% of MAP. Example: a MAP of 5,000 USD requires a wholesale cost of 2,500 USD or less. [L1090, 2021; L1111, 2020; L1112, 2021; L1277, 2020]
- The spread must cover shipping, payment fees, ad spend and profit. A wider spread leaves more room for these costs. [L1111, 2020; L1112, 2021; L1277, 2020]
- Exceptions are rare and only considered for very expensive items that sell consistently and easily. [L1090, 2021]
- MAP is often around 2x the wholesale cost. [L1274, 2018]

### Margin calculations

#### Build the full cost before committing

- Target a gross margin of about 25% to 30% of the selling price. Free shipping, the roughly 2.9% credit card fee and the ad cost to acquire each customer all come out of it. Build a full cost breakdown before committing to a product. [L1090, 2021]
- When researching, use the AliExpress product price plus shipping to your country as the initial COGS. Recalculate once you get real quotes from suppliers or fulfillment companies, which can be cheaper than AliExpress. (2026) [L1174, undated]
- Test the sourcing and shipping cost of a product through CJ or the supplier before you launch ads, especially for large or heavy packages where shipping can eat the margin. [L1176, 2023]
- Before pricing, ask the supplier for the product cost that applies to USA shipping. Do not reuse a cost from another market. The cost must cover the single unit and, for bundles, the total cost for each quantity. (2026) [L1182, 2026]
- Calculate the landed cost including shipping before choosing a heavy book for testing. Thick, heavy books have high cost of goods, and shipping to the US (fast or slow line) adds a large cost on top. (2026) [L1175, 2026]
- Before calculating margin on a heavy product, confirm whether the supplier ships directly to the end customer and what shipping costs for that destination. Shipping cost often decides whether a niche is viable, more than the product cost does. [L1109, 2018]
- Confirm whether a supplier's price list applies per unit or per pack or box before you publish a price. A price list for two units sold as one item led a store to sell four units at about half the intended price. [L1472, 2022]
- Two units were delivered in one box, and the supplier's price list showed the price for the two units together rather than per unit. The store sold four units at about half the intended price. The store later considered asking the supplier afterward to work on the price, instead of accepting zero profit. [L1472, 2022]
- Compare the sourcing price with comparable Amazon prices before committing to a product. Example: a vacuum sealer device listed at $60-80 on AliExpress was judged too expensive for what it does, while a vacuum canister sold for $20-40 on Amazon. [L1186, 2022]

#### Profit after product cost

Formula:

Profit after product cost = MAP − wholesale cost − average shipping cost − payment fees (2.9% for Shopify Payments) − other fees [L1111, 2020]

- Example: MAP $1,000, wholesale $650 and shipping $100 leave $250 before fees, and roughly $210 after the fees in the example, which may be lower. [L1111, 2020]
- Arithmetic check: 2.9% of $1,000 is $29, which alone would leave about $221 from $250. The source's roughly $210 implies further fees that it does not name. Use the full cost list in [L1090, 2021] and recalculate per product. [L1111, 2020; L1090, 2021]

#### Estimate COGS for bookkeeping

- Estimate the COGS percentage as 100% minus your gross margin, for example 80% for a 20% margin. Base the estimate on the last six months of your P&L and add a small buffer so the COGS account never runs dry. The result is an estimate, close enough to pay suppliers without checking each invoice to the cent. [L1200, 2021-22]

- The 3x markup in [L1183, undated] means a sale price of three times landed cost. That is a gross margin of about 67% of the selling price, which is well above the 25% to 30% gross margin target in [L1090, 2021]. The sources describe different measures, so check the base before applying a rule. [L1183, undated; L1090, 2021]
- The source describes [L1259, undated] as a product margin of at least 3x and does not define the base. Read it alongside [L1183, undated], which defines the base as landed cost.
- Competitors may sell at 3 times MAP. If so, a higher price than the 2.1x starting point is the right choice. [L1111, 2020]

### Net profit targets

- Target a net profit of 30 percent per sale, and never go below 20 percent. Accept 20 percent only for competitive products that need heavy ad spend, or for large bulk orders. [L1277, 2020]
- Reseller deals should leave at least 20% net profit after all costs as a minimum. The actual deal needs a larger margin than that, because ads and credit card processing fees come out of it. [L1274, 2018]
- Formula for net profit per sale:

  Net profit per sale = MAP − wholesale − shipping − ad spend per sale [L1277, 2020]

- Example with round numbers: 1,000 USD minus 500 USD wholesale, 100 USD free shipping and 100 USD ad spend leaves 300 USD net, which equals a 30 percent net margin. This is not the same as the markup over wholesale. [L1277, 2020]
- Use the gross margin target for early screening, before shipping, fees and ads. Use the net profit target for the decision on each sale. The sources give these as different measures and do not reconcile them. [L1090, 2021; L1277, 2020]

### Shipping in the margin

#### Judge shipping against price

- Shipping cost must be judged against the product's sale price and average order value, not against weight alone. A $800 shipping cost on a $5,000 product can still leave about $1,500 net profit, while the same $800 on a $1,200 product destroys the margin. [L1085, 2018]
- Example: two dining tables of the same size and weight cost $300 on average to ship. At $500 retail the $300 shipping makes the product unworkable, while at $1,500 retail the same shipping cost is absorbable. Shipping is therefore judged relative to the product price, even within one industry. [L1085, 2018]
- Example: a $1,000 product with about $500 gross margin and shipping of up to $300 leaves only about $200 for ads. Bringing shipping down to about $200 frees up enough money for ad costs and profit. Targeting customers in states close to the supplier is one way to lower shipping. [L1109, 2018]
- Shipping costs vary widely by destination. Remote states such as Montana or Idaho can be far more expensive to ship to. For a large item, a shipping cost of $450 instead of $250 can push a product into the danger zone. [L1148, 2021-22]

#### Estimate shipping without exact quotes

- Shipping costs depend on origin, destination, package size, weight and the carrier used, so they do not need precise calculation for every product at the start. If the shipping cost makes low-price items unprofitable, sell only the higher-priced items in that range. [L1085, 2018]
- Shipping can be quoted or estimated from the average shipping cost for each niche when those averages are known. Quotes are only needed when shipping costs are unknown. [L1111, 2020]
- Estimate shipping as follows [L1148, 2021-22]:
  1. Ask your supplier what it generally costs to ship an item from its location to your target regions. [L1148, 2021-22]
  2. Do not aim for exact figures, because the cost varies per order unless the supplier offers flat-rate shipping. [L1148, 2021-22]
  3. Estimate a typical shipping cost for each product, and compare item A with item B. [L1148, 2021-22]
  4. Enter the estimate in the supplier shipping cost field and adjust it once real orders come in. [L1148, 2021-22]

#### Shipping case and fix

Case: a seller had large, heavy products at about 9 x 57 x 36 inches and around 70 pounds. The cheapest shipping found was $198 (covering FedEx, UPS and LTL), while profit before shipping was $150, so each sale lost about $50. The advice was to ask the supplier how competitors ship at MAP and get a better carrier or wholesale rate, and if competitors are losing money too, either stop selling the item or raise the price until it is profitable. [L1085, 2018]

Procedure for a shipping cost that wipes out the margin on a product sold at MAP [L1085, 2018]:
1. Call the supplier and state the exact shipping cost per unit and the loss per sale at MAP. [L1085, 2018]
2. Tell the supplier which competitors sell the same product at MAP and ask which carrier they use. [L1085, 2018]
3. Ask whether the supplier can offer a better wholesale price or shipping rate. [L1085, 2018]
4. If no competitor can sell profitably, stop selling the product or raise the price until each sale is profitable. [L1085, 2018]

#### Lower shipping cost

- When rising shipping costs eat into margins, ask suppliers whether they offer a cheaper, slower shipping option or a shipping deal. Savings on shipping can protect margin before you raise retail prices. [L1112, 2021]
- Add shipping costs into the product cost used for pricing, so the selling price already covers delivery. Free shipping is only a sound offer if the supplier's alternative with free shipping is only a few dollars more expensive. Express shipping can be offered as an optional upgrade for customers who want it. [L1215, 2023+]
