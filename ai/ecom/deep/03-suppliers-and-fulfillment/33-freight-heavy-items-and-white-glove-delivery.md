## Freight, Heavy Items and White Glove Delivery

### Why heavy items change the margin

- Large and heavy products cost more to ship, which reduces the profit left per sale. When you compare a cheap product with an expensive one, include the shipping cost in both. [L0474, 2020-26]
- Large and heavy items need a separate shipping calculation, because their weight- or dimension-based cost can change a product's margin far more than it does for small items. [L0430, 2023]
- Large or bulky products such as home decor can be expensive to ship. Calculate the shipping cost against the ad price and margin before testing, because shipping can make a product that looks profitable in the ads unprofitable in practice. [L0638, 2023]
- Large and heavy products usually ship at freight rates rather than standard parcel rates. Confirm weight and dimension thresholds with carriers before you set the selling price. [L0675, 2025]
- Heavy and bulky products stay profitable only if the margin can absorb freight costs. Compare quotes from several companies and check the supplier's preferred carrier first. [L0967, 2020]
- Heavy items can cost more to ship than the margin allows. Example: a 60 lb item may cost 160 USD to ship. Calculate the average freight cost before you list and advertise the product. [L0836, 2024]
- Example of shipping-driven pricing: a 430-dollar shipping cost on an order that earns 90 dollars of margin makes the item unprofitable. [L0964, 2023]

### Check shipping cost before listing or advertising

#### Set a price floor by category

- For heavy or bulky items, ask suppliers for average shipping costs to the markets you plan to serve before choosing a product. Set a minimum price threshold so the margin covers shipping, and do not list cheaper items in that category. [L0357, 2019]
- Older (2019) example: If a heavy item such as a gun safe costs about $250 to ship, a $200 product cannot be sold profitably. If the lowest profitable price is $600, every item below $600 is excluded, and customers who want cheaper items are left to competitors. [L0357, 2019]
- If a brand's terms require a freight shipment on your account and the product margin works without MAP, freight is worth doing. Ask the supplier which freight company it recommends and work with that company. [L0358, 2020]
- A 30% markup on heavy products may not cover shipping costs. Shop freight quotes from multiple carriers instead of accepting the supplier's shipping quote. [L0276, 2024]
- For a large or heavy product with nationwide shipping of 100 to 150 dollars (up to 200 to 250 dollars for huge items), selling at a low price is not profitable with free shipping. [L0665, 2022]
- Carriers such as freight companies can cost around $150 per shipment, which makes items priced at $250 to $300 unprofitable. Ask each supplier which carrier it ships with. [L0663, 2019]

#### Estimate a whole range of products

Procedure: estimating shipping costs for many supplier products without quoting each one (2024)
1. For each product, calculate the spread as retail (MAP) minus wholesale cost in a spreadsheet, and sort from highest to lowest spread.
2. Look at the products with the smallest spread at the bottom of the sheet.
3. Ask the supplier for package size and weight for those 20 to 30 low-spread products.
4. Get free shipping quotes for those products from freightcenter.com.
5. Products where the shipping cost would consume the margin are the ones not to list.

[L0666, 2024]

Procedure: estimating shipping cost for heavy items before pricing (2024)
- Enter the supplier warehouse address and several made-up customer addresses across the country into freightcenter.com.
- Read the average shipping cost per item from the results.
- Set the selling price so the product stays profitable.

[L0836, 2024]

Procedure: estimating whether a large, heavy product is worth selling before you have supplier prices
1. Assume a wholesale cost, for example half of the expected selling price.
2. Ask the supplier candidates for the box size and weight of the product.
3. Get shipping quotes using these dimensions and weight (no account login needed).
4. Check whether the spread after shipping is big enough to leave a solid margin.

[L0854, 2022]

Procedure: get estimated freight rates before orders come in (2024)
1. Use a free instant freight quote site such as freightcenter.com.
2. Enter the supplier's zip code as origin.
3. Enter several customer zip codes in different regions, for example one near the supplier, one on the East Coast and one in the West or South.
4. Compare the quotes to estimate shipping costs.

Exact costs need both addresses and the package size. [L0806, 2024]

Procedure: average freight cost for free-shipping pricing
1. Get the supplier's typical product size and weight.
2. Use a freight quote tool such as freightcenter.com for freight shipments.
3. Set the ship-from address to the supplier's warehouse, for example New York.
4. Get quotes to ship-to addresses in several regions, for example New York, Florida, Texas and California.
5. Average the quotes and build that cost into the price of free shipping.

[L0801, 2021]

### Get freight quotes and compare them

#### Why quotes differ

- Freight brokers are approximately 50% cheaper than UPS or FedEx for large and heavy items. [L0251, 2023]
- Using only one freight carrier can lead to overpaying by about $100 to $200 per large shipment. Comparing quotes from several carriers can recover that difference. [L0854, 2022]
- Different quotes for the same shipment often differ by $100 to $300. [L0852, 2020]
- Freight prices can differ by hundreds of dollars between providers. Use several freight brokers for large freight. [L1086, 2019]
- Accepting the first freight quote is costly, because overpaying on freight can cost hundreds of dollars per shipment. Compare several brokers before shipping large goods. [L1086, 2019]
- The same carriers are quoted with different markups on different platforms. Compare the quotes from providers such as Freight Club and Freight Center on every order, because one can be cheaper by about $100. [L0834, 2021]
- Large, heavy products shipped from a single distribution point can vary by $150 or more between regions. Use separate shipping calculators for the supplier's location and average costs from several destinations, for example California, New York, Texas and Florida. [L0834, 2021]
- Freight quotes are free, and prices differ for the same shipment. Get quotes from several freight brokers. Freight Center is a common starting point, and Blue Freight Forwarder is another option members often use. [L0941, 2022]
- Freight quotes from brokers such as freightcenter.com and Red Hawk can differ. Collect quotes for the same shipment from several broker websites. [L0966, 2025]

#### Freight broker tools

- Freight Center (freightcenter.com) gives instant freight shipping quotes without binding you to a carrier. Enter package size, weight, origin ZIP and destination ZIPs to compare rates from multiple freight carriers and calculate actual shipping costs. [L0251, 2023]
- FreightCenter.com aggregates freight quotes from multiple LTL (less than truckload) carriers in real time, so you can compare prices before you pick a shipment method. Freight Club offers the same service with potentially better pricing. Use these when suppliers require you to arrange shipping on your own account for large or heavy items. [L0272, 2022]
- Use Freightcenter.com before launching ads to check that margins survive real freight costs. [L0276, 2024]
- Freightcenter.com provides free instant shipping quotes. Enter the supplier's warehouse zip code, package size and weight, and a few US destination zip codes to see real freight estimates. [L0666, 2024]
- Use Freight Center to get sample quotes before the first sale, and to check whether a supplier's freight quote is too high. [L0667, 2025]
- Freight Center is a freight shipping service for items too large or heavy for standard parcel carriers. Use it to get freight quotes when pricing bulky products. [L0675, 2025]
- Freight Center is a starting point for comparing large-shipment quotes. Ask your suppliers which freight companies their other retailers use, because this gives insider information on what works. [L1086, 2019]
- Freight Center is a resource for handling shipping and freight matters for dropshipped orders, especially bulky or heavy items. [L1089, 2021]
- Freight broker freightcenter.com lets you calculate freight rates by entering the supplier warehouse, the customer's warehouse or address, box size and weight. It is useful for heavy items where shipping costs are high. [L0969, 2022]
- FreightCenter is used when a supplier will not ship on its own account for large, heavy products. It collects less-than-truckload quotes from many freight carriers that compete for the shipment, which helps find the lowest price. [L0854, 2022]
- Freightcenter.com lets you get instant freight quotes from different companies and compare them with larger carriers. Freight carriers bid on shipments, so prices can vary widely. [L0835, 2023]
- Free online freight quote sites take one shipment's details, such as size, weight, origin and destination, and return prices from several carriers. These sites do not own trucks, so comparing their quotes shows the going rate for large or heavy items. [L0853, 2021]
- Freight Shipping Estimates is a listed resource in the December 2020 coaching call for estimating freight shipping costs. The lesson names it without describing how to use it. [L0426, 2020]

#### Procedures for getting quotes

Procedure: getting freight quotes through FreightCenter (Calculate freight rates, 2024)
1. Go to freightcenter.com.
2. Click 'Get Quote'.
3. Select 'Less Than Truckload'.
4. Enter your supplier's zip code.
5. Enter representative destination zip codes across the country (for example: New York, Florida, Texas, California).
6. Input estimated package weight and dimensions.
7. Collect freight quotes.
8. Calculate an average to estimate your typical shipping cost.

[L0276, 2024]

Procedure: getting freight rates on FreightCenter (2022)
1. Open freightcenter.com and click 'Calculate freight rates' at the top.
2. Select less than truckload (LTL), since the order does not fill a full truck.
3. Enter the supplier's warehouse location, the package size and weight.
4. Enter the customer's delivery address.
5. Compare the quotes from the carriers and choose the best price.

[L0854, 2022]

Procedure: using the Freight Center rate calculator
1. Open freightcenter.com and click Calculate freight rates.
2. Enter the supplier warehouse zip code as origin.
3. Enter destination zip codes around the country, such as New York, Florida, Texas and California.
4. Enter package sizes and weights for less-than-truckload shipments.
5. Click View my rates to see estimates.

[L0941, 2022]

Procedure: estimating freight rates with FreightCenter's 'Calculate Freight Weights'
1. Get the dimensions and weights of the product packages from the supplier.
2. Open FreightCenter.com and choose Calculate Freight Weights.
3. Enter the city or zip code where the supplier ships from as the origin.
4. Enter destination zip codes in the regions where your customers are, testing for example a New York, a California and a Florida zip code.
5. Enter the packaging details and box dimensions and weight given by the supplier.
6. Enter the freight class if known, otherwise leave it as unknown.
7. Click to get quotes and note the prices for each destination to build your averages.

[L0466, 2018]

Procedure: building an average rate from supplier products
1. Ask the supplier for the top three best-selling products, with weight and packaging dimensions.
2. Open a freight rate calculator and set the supplier's warehouse as the origin.
3. Enter the dimensions and weight of each product as the shipment.
4. Enter several destination addresses across different regions, for example one in the origin state, one in Florida, one in Texas and one in California.
5. Average the results to get a ballpark rate.
6. Repeat with more suppliers until the rates are known from experience.

[L0468, 2020]

Procedure: getting a freight quote through freightcenter.com for an item shipped from a supplier warehouse
- Step 1: Enter the supplier warehouse address as origin, the customer address as destination, and the package size and weight.
- Step 2: Compare the quotes and choose one.
- Step 3: Accept the quote to receive a bill of lading (BOL), which states the shipment route.
- Step 4: Send the BOL to the supplier, who attaches it to the package.
- Step 5: The freight carrier, often an LTL (less-than-truckload) company, picks up the goods and delivers them to the customer.

[L1090, 2021]

Procedure: getting shipping quotes for heavy or bulky items
1. Obtain box dimensions and weights from the supplier (not product dimensions).
2. Request quotes from UPS or FedEx, or from a freight broker such as freightcenter.com, by entering supplier warehouse, customer destination, box size and weight.
3. Compare the supplier's shipping charge with your own quotes. Suppliers shipping high volume may get better rates.

[L0969, 2022]

Procedure: getting freight quotes from brokers (2024)
1. Open a free account with a freight platform before you need quotes.
2. Get quotes from several freight brokers, and tell each one you are comparing quotes.
3. Ask brokers for a discount, since they have room to negotiate.
4. Use a free instant quote on freight platforms such as Freight Center, choosing the LTL service.

[L0855, 2023]

Procedure: contacting freight brokers for the best rate (2024)
1. Open a free account with a freight platform.
2. Email the platform with a competing quote if a supplier's shipping quote is cheaper.
3. Ask whether a better price can be offered.
4. Repeat with several quotes. Pricing usually improves after a few tries.

[L0855, 2023]

Procedure: contacting freight brokers (2024, the 'ask for a discount' steps)
1. Contact 4 to 5 freight brokers.
2. Provide each one with the item weight, dimensions, supplier zip code and destination zip code.
3. Mention that you are comparing rates for ongoing freight needs.
4. Collect and compare the quotes.
5. Negotiate rates or select the best pricing.
6. Repeat for different regions to determine average costs.

[L0276, 2024]

Procedure: shipping heavy or palletized freight (2025), for example 90 to 100 pounds on a pallet
- Do not open a UPS account, since parcel pricing is absurd for pallets.
- Use a freight broker such as freightcenter.com, which quotes several freight carriers at once.
- Enter the quantity as one pallet, the product weight, the supplier's ZIP code and the customer's delivery ZIP code, then compare the quotes.

#### Checking a supplier's freight quote

- Suppliers usually have the best shipping rates because they move high volume. To test a freight quote, send the supplier's shipping quote, with supplier details removed, to one or more freight brokers and ask them to beat it. [L0805, 2024]
- If a supplier will not ship on its own account, compare its freight carrier quote with a freight aggregator quote. Differences of $100 to $200 per shipment are possible and add up over time. [L0970, 2023]
- Freight aggregators collect quotes from many carriers for heavy items when you enter origin, destination, package size and weight. A quote that seems extremely high, such as over $1,000 for a 250-pound item crossing from Canada to the US west coast, should be checked before you accept it. [L0970, 2023]

Procedure: checking supplier freight quotes (2023)
1. For each new order, ask the supplier to send a quote from its freight carrier, giving the order number, product and customer shipping address.
2. Enter the pickup location, destination, package size and weight into a freight aggregator such as Freightcenter.com.
3. Compare the aggregator quotes from different carriers with the supplier's quote.
4. If the supplier's carrier is in line with the market, use it to save a step. If the aggregator is clearly cheaper, book that option.

[L0970, 2023]

Template to send to the supplier with each order (2023):

```
Hi [supplier contact], here is our next order: order number [number], product [product name], shipping to customer [customer name] at [full shipping address]. Please send a quote through your freight carrier so we can compare it with our own freight quotes before you ship.
```

[L0970, 2023]

- For a large list of products with different weights and sizes, quote one by one at the start. Once you have a relationship with a freight broker, send a spreadsheet of shipment data and group items with similar sizes and weights to get quotes faster. [L1090, 2021]
- Ask the supplier which freight company it prefers and whether it can introduce you. Different suppliers have different freight relationships, and this often gets the best price. [L0852, 2020]
- Shipping through the same freight company for years without comparing quotes can cost a lot of money. The lesson's example was thousands of shipments, with savings of $100 to $300 per shipment once quotes were compared. [L0852, 2020]
- Ask competitors who they ship with when they offer free shipping that seems unprofitable. Then approach their carriers or your own suppliers for better shipping rates or purchase order discounts. [L0834, 2021]
- Negotiate freight with brokers. Ask brokers for a discount, since they have room to negotiate. [L0855, 2023]

### Choose who books the freight: supplier account or your own

#### Supplier account

- Some suppliers allow you to ship on their account. They charge you for product and shipping together. Others require you to ship on your own account. [L0255, 2025]
- The ideal setup for freight: the supplier quotes the order, creates the shipment and the Bill of Lading (BOL), and charges shipping on its own carrier account. The store does not set up freight shipping in Shopify. [L0854, 2022]
- For large and heavy items such as furniture, it is normal for the supplier to ask the retailer to ship on the retailer's own shipping account. Expect this request when a supplier names a shipping account. [L0470, 2023]
- When suppliers ship on their own account, they typically get volume discounts on freight, so the shipping cost is better than you can negotiate alone. [L0255, 2025]

Procedure: freight shipping when the supplier ships on its own account (2024)
1. The supplier books the freight carrier and sends a tracking number when the shipment is ready.
2. You forward the tracking number to the customer.
3. The freight carrier contacts the customer to schedule delivery, for example on a specific day between 12 and 3 pm, since large heavy items are not dropped at the door.

[L0805, 2024]

Procedure: freight shipping with a supplier that ships only to you (2024)
1. Get quotes from a freight broker for the shipment.
2. Choose the price and carrier you want.
3. The broker issues a bill of lading (BOL).
4. Send the BOL to the supplier.
5. The supplier arranges with the trucking company to pick up the goods at the warehouse and deliver them to the customer.

[L0806, 2024]

#### Your own account

- Shipping on your own account makes you responsible for anything that happens in transit. Accept it only for large or heavy items when no other option exists, and do not write the supplier off immediately. [L0466, 2018]
- Some suppliers ship on their own account, and some require you to ship on yours. When you do, arrange the shipment as described below. [L0255, 2025]
- If a supplier says it will not ship on its own account, ask which of its top retailers use which carrier. Those retailers usually have the best rates. Contact that carrier to arrange pickup at the supplier's warehouse. [L0854, 2022]
- Ask the supplier to send a quote and to check whether its carrier is competitive. Use the supplier's carrier if it is in line with the market. [L0970, 2023]

Procedure: freight shipping when you ship on your own account (not UPS or FedEx) (2024)
1. Create the shipment through a freight broker such as freightcenter.com.
2. The broker generates a BOL (Bill of Lading), a document describing the shipment from the supplier warehouse to the customer address.
3. Send the BOL to the supplier.
4. The supplier arranges pickup at its warehouse with the freight shipper, who delivers to the customer.

[L0805, 2024]

Procedure: arranging freight shipping on your own account (2025)
1. Shop online for freight quotes from multiple carriers.
2. Compare pricing across different quotes.
3. Select the quote you prefer.
4. Pay for the shipment.
5. Receive a BOL (Bill of Lading), which shows pickup location (supplier warehouse) and delivery location.
6. Email the BOL to the supplier.
7. The freight company contacts the supplier to arrange pickup date and time.
8. The freight company picks up the order from the supplier warehouse.
9. The shipment gets a tracking number and delivers to the customer.
