## Approaching Suppliers: Scripts, Calls and Positioning

### Why Suppliers Approve Retailers

- Suppliers evaluate every potential retailer on three traits: a professional, functioning website already operating; a clear, defined strategy for generating sales; and demonstrated exceptional customer service. [L0133, undated]
- Show the three capabilities: a quality website that represents their brand well, a clear strategy for generating additional sales through your channel, and exceptional customer service that protects their reputation. [L0121, undated]
- Approval is not guaranteed, regardless of website quality, traffic level or business experience. Not every supplier will approve you even with professional preparation. Success depends on the right communication strategy, not external credentials alone. [L0133, undated]
- Suppliers accept a new retailer only when they believe it is a real, qualified business that will bring extra sales to their brand. Your job in the first contact is to make that belief clear. [L0927, 2020-26]
- Suppliers want retailers who prove themselves before approval, because a retailer represents their brand. They expect the retailer to honor policies such as MAP and to support customers after the sale. [L1094, 2023]
- Suppliers approve retailers they expect to care about customer service, run a lasting business and bring traffic that adds value to their brand. Show that intention in the approach call and in the store. [L0806, 2024]
- Suppliers generally want to work with new stores that are excited about their products. Clear enthusiasm and specific requests are usually well received. [L0804, 2023]
- Suppliers sell business to business, to physical and online retailers, so an online store is simply another retail customer for them. Approval is usually easy because you offer a sales channel that fits what they already do. ** [L0458, 2021-22]
- High-ticket suppliers usually do not sell to the public. They earn from selling to retailers, so they wait for serious businesses that will manage sales to customers. The approval call is a request for a partnership, not a favour. ** [L0771, 2021-22]
- The products already exist on the market and demand already exists. The store does not need to create demand; it taps into existing demand for these products. ** [L0771, 2021-22]
- Suppliers evaluate outreach from their side and accept a retailer only when they believe it is a real, qualified business. [L0927, 2020-26]
- Suppliers often ask the same questions when reviewing an approval request. Prepare answers in advance so the first conversation is not stalled. [L0590, 2020-26]
- Dropshipping brands usually require approval before a store may sell their products. Copying a product list from the web and listing it directly is not a valid route. Plan a contact and approval step for every supplier. [L0590, 2020-26]
- Suppliers are approached directly, and the retailer becomes an authorized retailer through approval, not by paying for supplier access or joining supplier directories. [L0506, 2020-26]
- Ready-made phone and email scripts are used to ask suppliers for approval as a retailer, so that approval is obtained without spending money. [L0506, 2020-26]
- Contacting suppliers and getting authorized to sell their products costs nothing. The first real spending starts with ads, so supplier approval should be requested before any budget is committed to traffic. [L0360, 2024]

### Positioning: How You Come Across

- Establish yourself as an attractive opportunity they would regret losing, not as a retailer who needs them to succeed. Make them wish they had accepted you sooner. [L0133, undated]
- Sounding desperate, or signaling that you need the supplier to succeed, damages your position. They should believe they would be making a mistake by passing on you, not that you depend on them. [L0133, undated]
- Position the relationship as the start of a long-term partnership, not a transaction. Suppliers want retailers who grow with them and generate reliable repeat revenue over time, not one-off buyers. [L0133, undated]
- The quality and content of each supplier conversation matter far more than call frequency. Make each call count rather than contacting suppliers repeatedly. [L0133, undated]
- Suppliers receive many calls about the same products, so they ask what sets you apart. Show that your business is long term, that customer service is your priority, and that you understand their brand and why it fits your store, rather than only asking to list their products. [L0933, 2020-26]
- Approach suppliers owner to owner, because suppliers need sellers who promote their products. Frame your outreach as giving them an opportunity to be sold through your store. [L0832, 2019]
- Describing your launch plan works best when you have actually prepared it. Position yourself as someone about to take a market share, and show that you understand the subject well enough to be believed. [L0927, 2020-26]
- Do not call a supplier with a generic request, such as asking whether you can drop ship their products. Research their brand first and explain specifically why your store is a good fit for them. [L0933, 2020-26]
- Suppliers judge retail partners by whether they will last. Show that you treat the relationship as lasting, not as a quick resale opportunity. [L0933, 2020-26]
- Some suppliers want to see your marketing plan before they approve you as a retailer. Before calling, prepare a short outline of how you will sell their products, such as through Google Shopping ads and retargeting. ** [L0771, 2021-22]
- Nervousness about calling is normal, since most suppliers are professional and welcome the contact. The discomfort decreases with practice, so use it as energy rather than trying to eliminate it before calling. ** [L0235, 2021-22]
- If you feel nervous, start with smaller, less visible suppliers to build confidence and practice the pitch. Approach the larger brands after you are comfortable with the conversation and have a few approvals behind you. [L0941, 2022]

### Phone First

- Always call suppliers first rather than emailing. Phone contact is the most efficient way to reach the right decision maker (the eCommerce account person) on the first attempt. [L0133, undated]
- Call every supplier with a phone number first. Use email or an online form as a follow-up or fallback. ** [L0771, 2021-22]
- Contact suppliers by phone first rather than email, because phone calls achieve higher success rates and lead to stronger business relationships. ** [L0235, 2021-22]
- Email is unreliable for initial supplier contact. Many recipients never read emails: inboxes get cluttered, unfamiliar senders are often ignored, and mailboxes reach capacity limits, so your opportunity depends on chance. ** [L0235, 2021-22]
- Contact suppliers by phone when a phone number is listed, because phone contact gets better results than email. Use email only when no phone number is available. ** [L0962, 2021]
- Calling suppliers gets better responses than emails. Emails are easily ignored, and a phone call creates a direct connection and a sense of urgency. *(2023.)* [L0970, 2023]
- Phone calls are the recommended way to reach suppliers. Once you reach the right person and describe what you want, the application usually takes 5 to 10 minutes to complete, and approval often follows right after it is returned. ** [L1086, 2019]
- Call suppliers instead of sending a long letter, because letters are easy to set aside or miss. A phone call reaches the right person quickly and gives a faster answer on approval. ** [L0960, 2019]
- Reseller approval requests to brand suppliers can be sent by phone, by email, or through the supplier's online application form. The first contact states that you want to sell their products through your business and asks for their approval. ** [L1060, 2021-22]
- Call suppliers rather than submitting only the trade or dealer intake forms. A supplier listing a trade or designer program does not stop you from getting approved, since those programs mainly serve larger corporate or B2B buyers. [L0941, 2022]
- Phone supplier contacts early in the morning, around 8:00 to 8:30 AM in the supplier's time zone, before their workday fills up. Choose the suppliers you call by time zone so the morning call makes sense for each. *(2023.)* [L0835, 2023]
- Calling suppliers early in the morning, around 8:30 to 9:00 AM, can get you through to decision makers before their workload builds up. If responses are poor at other times, test this window for a few days. *(2023.)* [L0970, 2023]
- Before calling, check the business or retail contact hours on the supplier's contact page, which may be listed in a specific time zone, and schedule calls within that window. Some manufacturers run a separate business line from their consumer support number. ** [L0937, 2021-22]
- Whether a supplier needs a personal meeting is rare, under 1% of supplier interactions. When a supplier wants to fly in, politely decline and suggest meeting at a trade show or local event instead, unless they are already nearby. *(2023.)* [L0964, 2023]

#### Online Application Forms: Submit, Wait 24 Hours, Then Call

- Exception to phone first: if the supplier's website asks you to submit an online application, submit it, wait 24 hours, and only if there is no response, make the phone call. This respects the process they set out on their site, but you still call. Open with: "Hi, it's [name] from [store]. I submitted an online application a day or so ago and just wanted to follow that up." Then ask whether they got it and say you would love to become a reseller of their products. ### Preparing Before You Call

**Research the supplier**

- Research each supplier's website and company positioning before calling to identify specific connection points. Reference these findings in the pitch to show genuine interest in the brand, not generic outreach to every supplier in the category. [L0133, undated]
- Research each supplier before calling, as you would prepare for a job interview. Suppliers approve retailers who show they understand and value the supplier's brand, and this improves approval rates. [L0928, 2020-26]
- Before a supplier call, learn its story: read its About and home pages, then open the conversation by saying what you like about the company and why you want to sell its products. ** [L0962, 2021]
- Pitch with something specific from their website, such as better materials or a virtual showroom, and explain how it fits your customers' expectations. Generic praise weakens the approval chance. [L0928, 2020-26]
- Evaluate the supplier before calling: its company background, history, products and how it sells. [L0927, 2020-26]

Supplier research method (2024 version, as written):

1. Google the brand or product category. [L0806, 2024]
2. Check that the brand sells through online stores and is not a dropshipper. [L0806, 2024]
3. Open the brand's About us page. [L0806, 2024]
4. Spend about three to five minutes reading what the brand is proud of and wants customers to know. [L0806, 2024]
5. Use those points in the supplier conversation, for example customer service focus, long history of the brand, or shared location. [L0806, 2024]

Supplier research method (undated version, 5 to 10 minutes per supplier):

1. Open the supplier's website from your research worksheet. [L0928, 2020-26]
2. Read the About Us section and homepage to find what the brand says about itself, such as its philosophy, materials, quality or differentiators. [L0928, 2020-26]
3. Check for extra content such as a virtual showroom or 360-degree tour, and browse it. [L0928, 2020-26]
4. Note what stands out and what the brand wants customers to know. [L0928, 2020-26]
5. Plan to reference one or two of these points on the call and explain why this brand is a better fit than other suppliers. Spend about 5 to 10 minutes per supplier. [L0928, 2020-26]

*Both research methods are current. Use the 3-5 minute reading of the About page (2024) for a quick first pass, and the full 5-10 minute method when you prepare a supplier you plan to call.* [L0806, 2024; L0928, 2020-26]

**Know what you bring**

- Know what you bring to the table: your store, your audience, your marketing channels and your sales plan. [L0927, 2020-26]
- Prepare answers to likely questions so you do not depend on improvising under pressure. [L0927, 2020-26]

**Prepare the list**

- Open your completed master supplier list. Make sure each supplier has a URL, phone number and email. [L0928, 2020-26]
- Have at least 20 suppliers researched and filled in before starting supplier calls. Work in batches, for example researching 20 suppliers one day and calling them the next. ** [L1068, 2019]
- Batch order: 1. Research the contact information for the first 20 suppliers. 2. On the next day, call those 20 suppliers. 3. On the following day, research the next 20 suppliers. 4. Repeat the cycle. ** [L1068, 2019]
- Calling one supplier and then researching the next one slows the process down. Expect voicemails and callbacks, so a prepared list should be ready before calling begins. ** [L1068, 2019]
- The scripts, emails and day-by-day timing in the supplier contact plan are starting points you can edit to fit your store and wording. Keep the overall order of calls and follow-ups intact, because the sequence is built to keep outreach going until a supplier gives a definite answer. [L0936, 2020-26]
- Prepare for the most likely supplier questions in advance. *(see [L0590, 2020-26] above)*

### Order and Pacing of Outreach

- Contact suppliers you expect to approve you first. Reach out to top-tier suppliers only after your business information is complete. Keep adding suppliers over time rather than waiting for every approval before uploading products. ** [L0968, 2021]
- Start at the top of the supplier list. Once approved, sign the agreement, upload the products, then move on to the next supplier. Have products from the first two approved brands live with traffic before you proceed. *(2023.)* [L0804, 2023]
- Silver suppliers come first: they are the majority of the list, and gold suppliers are left for later. Call each supplier, even when its website has a dealer form. Introduce yourself and your store, and say you are about to submit the form. When a dealer application arrives, fill it out and return it immediately. Stop contacting new suppliers while several applications are pending, so each is completed and approved before you move on. Once all silver suppliers are handled, contact the gold suppliers. ** [L0801, 2021]
- Order in a niche with many suppliers:
  1. On competitor category pages, note which suppliers' products appear first, since these pages sort by best-selling products. [L1090, 2021]
  2. Do not contact the top-ranked suppliers first. [L1090, 2021]
  3. Contact about 10 suppliers that rank lower but still carry products in the niche. [L1090, 2021]
  4. Get the first 5 to 10 approved and build comfort with the approval process. [L1090, 2021]
  5. Return to the top-ranked suppliers and seek approval from them. [L1090, 2021]
  6. Contact all remaining suppliers. [L1090, 2021]
- Credibility approach when a brand accepts a new reseller (2025): become an authorized seller, upload the supplier's products to the store, price them above the supplier's minimum price if margins are thin, and exclude them from paid campaigns. They can still be found through organic search and free Google Shopping listings, while paid ads go to proven sellers. Then reach out to more suppliers and mention the existing brand. [L0472, 2025]
