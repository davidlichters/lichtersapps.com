### Images, Descriptions and Content Rights

#### Get permission before using supplier content

- Use a supplier's product photos on your store only after the supplier has confirmed in writing, even when the photos come from the supplier's own website. Asking first avoids disputes. *Older advice (2025).* [L0972, 2025]
- You may use product images from a supplier's website after authorization. Email the supplier contact to confirm this is acceptable before uploading images to your store. *Older advice (2024).* [L0276, 2024]
- Before using a supplier's product descriptions or lifestyle photos, ask which content you may use. If unsure, email for a yes or no on each part. *Older advice (2023).* [L0934, 2023]
- Ask suppliers which product photos and descriptions you may use before copying them. Get a simple yes or no on each part of their content. *Older advice (2023).* [L0934, 2023]

Template for that request (*older, 2025*):

```
Hi [supplier contact name],

We are close to sending traffic to our product pages, and Google Shopping requires a main product photo on a white background for each product. We can see photos on your website that would work for this. Could you confirm in writing that we may use them for [product names]? If you have a white-background photo of each product, sending those would also be very helpful.

Thank you,
[your name]
[your store name]
```
[L0972, 2025]

- Do not use photos taken from other sellers. Ask the supplier for product photos or for the rights to use its images, and check its website, social pages and events for material. *Older advice (2018).* [L0466, 2018]
- Do not use product photos from Alibaba or AliExpress unless the supplier has given permission for them. Photos from non-approved sources should not be used. *Older advice (2022).* [L1113, 2022]
- Product photos and videos on AliExpress or Alibaba listings can be used for ads only after confirming with the supplier that the content is its own. Some sellers copy custom content from others, which creates copyright risk. [L0693, 2024]
- Product photos taken from AliExpress are hard to use on their own. A seller who shows the product in its own real setting and photos has a stronger store. [L0530, 2022]
- A product that is new on AliExpress with few sellers has few competitor listings to study. Product images may then need to come from the supplier listing. [L1194, undated]
- Ask a supplier for high-resolution product images through the sales rep or contact first, and also ask for older images, such as from trade shows. Do not take images from other websites. [L0359, 2020]
- Supplier spec sheets, lifestyle images or videos can replace the supplier's copy. When a supplier tells you to build product pages by copying its own website content, its operations are probably weak, but this is not a dealbreaker. Ask the sales rep for other content sources such as spec sheets, lifestyle images or videos, and use them to build product pages that go beyond the supplier's copy. *Older advice (2020).* [L0358, 2020]
- Installation guides, PDF manuals or videos from the supplier can be added to the product page as download links or embeds. Do not create such manuals yourself. *Older advice (2020).* [L0664, 2020]

#### Photo sourcing

- Do not take your own product photos. Use supplier or stock images, except for rare trade show photos. *Older advice (2022).* [L0969, 2022]
- For resellers and dropshippers without the physical product, photographing products at manufacturer or dealer showrooms creates unique images that competitors relying on stock photography or generic supplier images will not have. *Older advice (2021-22).* [L0301, 2021-22]
- Sources differ on taking your own photos. The 2022 rule allows only rare trade show photos ([L0969, 2022]). The 2021-22 example recommends showroom photos for resellers without the product ([L0301, 2021-22]). Use the 2022 rule as the baseline, and treat the showroom example as older.
- Once suppliers are connected, upload their product photos to Canva and crop them to the same size as the other store images. Uniform sizes keep the homepage collection tiles and banners looking consistent. *Older advice (no year given).* [L0342, 2020-26]
- When supplier images are unlabeled, identify the products in this order, *older advice (2025)*:
  1. Ask the supplier for a set of images where file names or SKU numbers match the products.
  2. If none exist, go to google.com, click the Search by image icon (Google Lens), upload the supplier photo, and click search.
  3. Use the matching product pages to identify the product. Do not copy images found on other sites because of copyright.

  [L0972, 2025]
- Steps to gather product images when the supplier sends too few or poor ones:
  1. After approval, request the product images together with the price list from the supplier.
  2. If the supplier sends only one photo, bad photos, or none, search Google Image Search by product name or SKU and collect every high-quality image you find.
  3. Ask your supplier contact whether it has attended trade shows and, if so, request photos of its best-selling products set up there.
  4. Upload all images as additional product images and mix them with the supplier's stock photos.

  [L1130, 2020-26]
- Trade show photos show products set up and displayed as a real range, which makes the offer look bigger and more professional. Mixing them with stock photos makes the product page stand out. [L1130, 2020-26]

### Reviews and Warranties

- Suppliers want you to make sales, so ask before displaying their product reviews on your site. Most suppliers agree, but never display reviews without asking first. *Older advice (2020).* [L0551, 2020]
- Ask suppliers for existing reviews and use them on the store only with the brand's explicit permission. These reviews come from the supplier, not from other retailers. *Older advice (2025).* [L0667, 2025]
- Procedure to get reviews for new product pages, *older advice (2020)*:
  1. Ask the brand's sales rep for reviews of the brand and its products.
  2. Check the supplier's own website for reviews first.
  3. If reviews exist, ask permission to use them.
  4. If none exist, ask whether the supplier has any reviews to share.

  [L0967, 2020]
- Brand names should be included on product pages, because brand names are search terms that drive traffic to the store. *Older advice (2020).* [L0967, 2020]
- A supplier can approve you without the store name appearing on pages. Naming brands on product pages helps the store rank for the brand names it sells. *Older advice (2020).* [L0967, 2020]
- Reviews from a supplier's brand may be used on your store only with the supplier's explicit permission. If the supplier agrees, the reviews can be used on your site, or on its Trustpilot page where they are published. If the supplier has no reviews or declines, rely on reviews from your own customers. [L1245, 2020-26]
- Procedure for using supplier reviews:
  1. Contact the brands you are already authorized to sell and ask whether they have product reviews you can use on your website.
  2. Continue only if the supplier gives explicit permission to use the reviews. Without it, do not use them.
  3. If permission is given, open the product page on your store as a customer would.
  4. Enter the supplier's review there, the same way a customer would enter a review.
  5. The review is published to the product page after submission.

  [L1245, 2020-26]
- Using reviews copied from a supplier's brand or website without permission is a mistake. Permission must be confirmed before any supplier review is placed on your store. [L1245, 2020-26]
- Show a supplier's warranty on the product pages of products from suppliers that offer one. *Older advice (2020).* [L1088, 2020]

### Sizing for Apparel and Footwear

#### Why sizes go wrong

- Chinese sizes are usually smaller than US and European sizes, so a size that fits a Chinese buyer may not fit a US or European buyer. Check the supplier's size charts against the target market before listing clothing or shoes. [L1128, 2023]
- Putting the size chart in the product description did not stop buyers from ordering the wrong size, even when the text was highlighted and buyers were told to order larger. Make size selection easier with a clear size chart, and expect fit problems with winter clothing and jackets. [L1128, 2023]
- For fashion products with sizing, verify the size chart with the supplier before selling. Size charts on competitor stores were often weak, so a better chart is an advantage. [L1161, 2024+]
- Trusting the supplier's size labels for shoes leads to wrong-size orders, refunds and complaints. The manual conversion work is slow at the start but is necessary. [L1219, undated]

#### Checks before listing

- Check after the ad validation step, before testing:
  1. Source the product and confirm a good price from the supplier.
  2. For fashion items, confirm correct sizing and a reliable size chart with the supplier, such as CJ or another dropshipping supplier.
  3. Only then start the test.

  [L1161, 2024+]
- For fashion products, CJ and Best Dealer are examples of suppliers from which to get a verified size chart before listing. Use the chart from the supplier that actually fulfills the order, since sizing differs between suppliers. [L1161, 2024+]
- Footwear sizing check before listing, *one source*:
  1. Ask the supplier to send the length in centimeters for each size.
  2. Compare each centimeter length with the size standard of the target market, for example EU sizes for Europe.
  3. If the supplier advertises a size that does not match its centimeters, sell it under the size that matches the measurement. For example, sell an EU 34 measurement as 34 rather than 36.
  4. Repeat this check for each target market.

  [L0661, undated]
- For footwear, ask the supplier for the real length of each size in centimeters, because supplier size labels do not match EU, UK or US sizing. Convert those centimeter values yourself for the target market instead of trusting the labels. [L1219, undated]
- Chinese shoe size labels usually run one to two sizes smaller than European sizes. The lesson's example is Chinese 34 corresponding to about EU 32. Convert sizes manually before you publish a size chart. [L1219, undated]
- Build a shoe size guide in this order:
  1. Get the centimeter length of every size from the supplier.
  2. Check in the target country which shoe size matches that length.
  3. Adjust the size numbers manually for that market.
  4. Publish the resulting size guide on the product page.

  [L1219, undated]
- Procedure to avoid size chargebacks when selling shoes across markets:
  1. Ask the supplier for its size chart with the exact length in centimeters for each size, for example size 6.
  2. Ask for the European, Australian (or other local market) and American size charts.
  3. Compare each size with the local market chart and adjust the sizes you list.
  4. Double-check before publishing, because the same size number can differ between suppliers and markets.

  [L1214, 2023+]

### Demo Products and Upload Sequencing

#### Demo products

- Start a new store with a handful of demo products, then remove them once the first supplier approval arrives. Continue working through the supplier list for more approvals. *Older advice (2020).* [L1111, 2020]
- Upload demo products to the store while waiting for supplier approval, then replace them with real brand products once approved. Do not leave products from low-quality suppliers on the store. *Older advice (2024).* [L0806, 2024]
- Remove demo products from the store as soon as the first live supplier products are uploaded. *Older advice (2020).* [L1088, 2020]
- Remove all demo products in Shopify after the first supplier approval, before uploading that supplier's products. *Older advice (2023).* [L0934, 2023]
- Upload only real, live products from approved suppliers, and remove demo products before turning on ads. Get at least a second supplier approval before running any traffic. *Older advice (2023).* [L1094, 2023]
- Upload only real products from approved suppliers and remove demo products before turning on ads. Secure a second supplier approval before buying any traffic. Do not open paid ad accounts until live products are on the store, because doing so early can be read as a red flag by Google. *Older advice (2023).* [L1094, 2023]
- Find 4 to 5 demo products from a store in another country, using a VPN, and upload them into the Shopify account the same way as shown before moving on to the next step. *Older advice (2021-22).* [L0452, 2021-22]
- Real supplier products will later be uploaded with the same method once suppliers have been contacted and their products are available, so this process is useful later as well. *Older advice (2021-22).* [L0452, 2021-22]

#### Upload timing

- Upload a supplier's products to the store immediately after approval and product information arrive, not in a batch later. Each delay between approval and upload leaves the store without products to sell. *Older advice (no year given).* [L0495, 2020-26]
- Start uploading products as soon as the first supplier is approved and has sent product data, without waiting for all supplier contacts to be made. Slow or unresponsive suppliers should not delay the upload work. *Older advice (2021-22).* [L1163, 2021-22]
- While waiting for approval from preferred suppliers, immediately upload products from authorized non-MAP suppliers to your store, even over a weekend. Non-MAP suppliers typically approve faster than MAP-enforcing brands. Although you will not run paid ads to non-MAP products, their presence significantly boosts store credibility. When a decision maker from a preferred supplier visits your site, they see 3+ brands with hundreds of products, which dramatically increases your odds of approval on subsequent outreach. Pricing non-MAP items for profit margins without ad spend ensures you capture revenue if customers buy organically while protecting cash flow if they don't. [L0294, 2025+]
- Keep a supplier's product pages live at launch only after approval. Before approval, a store may be shown unlocked without active purchase capability, and products are added only once real suppliers are approved. *Older advice (2021).* [L0834, 2021]
- Product uploads sequenced by the first-approval rule, *older advice (2023)*:
  1. Get approved by the first supplier.
  2. Remove all demo products in Shopify.
  3. Upload all products from that supplier.
  4. Contact the remaining suppliers on your list.

  [L0934, 2023]
- Sources differ on when to contact more suppliers. The 2023 advice says to contact the remaining suppliers after the first approval ([L0934, 2023]). The 2025 advice says to upload the first three approved suppliers' products and remove demo products before contacting more ([L0966, 2025]). Use the 2025 rule when you can reach three approvals, and the 2023 rule as the minimum.
- Get at least five approved suppliers before the module is complete. This creates an ongoing cycle of supplier outreach, approval, product integration, and store scaling. *Older advice (no year given).* [L0128, undated]
- Upload the top products first at launch, *older advice (2021)*:
  1. For each approved supplier, open the top three competitor stores in tabs.
  2. Search each brand name there and sort by best sellers.
  3. Upload the best sellers first at launch.
  4. Upload the remaining catalog over time.

  [L0834, 2021]
- Choose which products from each approved supplier to upload first, *older advice (2024)*:
  1. Find the top future competitors' stores.
  2. Sort their product lists by best sellers (or featured or recommended).
  3. Upload the top 20 percent of best sellers from each approved supplier first.
  4. Upload the remaining 80 percent in the background over time.
  5. Optionally, ask each supplier rep for their current best-selling SKUs.

  [L0856, 2024]
- Sources differ on the first upload: the 2021 procedure uploads the best sellers at launch and the rest over time, and the 2024 procedure uploads the top 20 percent first and the other 80 percent in the background. Both apply the same logic; use the 2024 split if you need a fixed ratio.
