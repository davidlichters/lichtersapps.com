## Follow-Up, Rejections and Re-Contact

### Tracking and records

- Record three things for every supplier: the date of first contact, the contact person's name, and detailed notes from each conversation with its outcome. This prevents accidental duplicate outreach, coordinates several supplier processes at once, and keeps follow-ups on time. [L0126, undated]
- Keep a Master Supplier list that tracks all outreach and contact attempts. For each supplier, record the contact person's name, the conversation date, the details discussed, and the outcome. [L0133, undated]
- Record the contact person and the date of first contact in your notes. A later follow-up can then address that person by name and refer to the earlier conversation. [L0938, 2020-26; L0936, 2020-26]
- Detailed notes matter because with many brands it is easy to forget what happened on an earlier call. Notes show exactly where you stand with each supplier. [L0936, 2020-26]
- Failing to log notes across a large list leads to repeated or inconsistent calls and emails. Record the outcome of every contact immediately. [L0936, 2020-26]
- Log every supplier response in the notes, including reasons for refusal and conditions such as "contact again in three months" or visitor and revenue thresholds. Schedule follow-ups from these notes instead of relying on memory. [L0938, 2020-26]
- Record every supplier response, including rejections, in a supplier tracking sheet and schedule a later follow-up date. [L0471, 2024]
- Log exact requirements, such as a monthly visitor minimum or a monthly sales level, together with a re-contact date such as 3-6 months. These notes decide when and how to follow up. [L0191, undated]
- Typical conditions to record, and the trigger each one sets:
  - "contact again in 3-6 months" [L0191, undated]
  - "requires 10,000 monthly visitors" [L0191, undated]
  - "only works with stores doing $20,000+ per month" [L0191, undated]
- Keep a supplier tracking spreadsheet with the exact date you must contact each supplier again. A supplier that is out of stock or asks you to come back later gets a dated entry, such as the end of Q3. [L0968, 2021]
- Use a supplier spreadsheet to track every supplier you contact, with a notes column for minimum requirements and the reason for each rejection. The spreadsheet from the Drop Ship Blueprint can serve as the starting template. [L0927, 2020-26]
- Record every supplier conversation with the contact person, the date, and the planned follow-up date. Circle back later, because a rejection in a new niche often means the supplier wants proof that you are a real store. [L1113, 2022]
- Track each conversation in a sheet with the date and what was said, and follow up within a set period. [L0803, 2022]
- Note the contact date, the person and the outcome in the supplier sourcing worksheet. [L0665, 2022]
- Record the requirements for each supplier next to its name in your supplier spreadsheet. [L0927, 2020-26]
- Record a refusal and its date in your supplier spreadsheet. [L0358, 2020]
- Record the stated reason for a rejection and the follow-up plan that goes with it. [L0835, 2023; L0468, 2020]
- Do not list an out-of-stock supplier's products yet, and record its status so you can return to it later. [L1090, 2021]

### First contact and the first follow-up

**Sequence for each supplier on the list**

1. Start with the first supplier on your list and work down one supplier at a time. [L0936, 2020-26]
2. Day 1: if the supplier has a phone number, call first. Ask for their application process and for the decision maker who handles new accounts. [L0936, 2020-26]
3. Day 1: if the supplier asks you to email during the call, send the day-one email from the contact plan. [L0936, 2020-26]
4. Day 2: if the day-one email gets no reply, send the follow-up email. It restates your earlier request, gives a near launch window, and asks for the application steps. [L0936, 2020-26]
5. Day 3: make another phone call. [L0936, 2020-26]
6. Days 4 to 7: continue the mixed calls and emails in the plan until you get a clear yes or no. [L0936, 2020-26]
7. Stop the sequence early once you reach the right person and get a clear answer. [L0936, 2020-26]

**Email or form requested by the supplier**

1. Send a short, simple email to the dealer or retailer contact address on the supplier's website. Introduce yourself and your store, and include a condensed version of your pitch on why they should work with you. [L0470, 2023]
2. If there is no reply by the next day, call in the morning. [L0470, 2023]
3. On the call, introduce yourself and the store, mention the email sent the day before, and say you want to confirm it arrived. [L0470, 2023]

Before ending the call, ask when you should expect a reply. Submit the email or form right after the call, and if nothing arrives within a couple of days, call the same contact again. [L0970, 2023]

- Ask: "Okay, perfect, I'll do that. Right after this call I'll send it. When should I expect a reply? Is that something you'll handle today, later this week, or at another time?" [L0970, 2023]

```
Okay, perfect, I'll do that. Right after this call I'll send it. When should I expect a reply? Is that something you'll handle today, later this week, or at another time?
```
[L0970, 2023]

- When following up after an email or form was requested, reference the date of the earlier conversation and the exact email or form you submitted. Confirm it arrived and ask where your application stands. [L0970, 2023]

```
Hi, this is [your name] from [your brand]. We spoke on [day of the week], and you asked me to [email [address] / fill out the form on your website]. I did that right after our call on [day]. I'm excited to continue the conversation and get things moving, and I just wanted to make sure it went through so you can follow up on your end. Could you let me know where things stand and whether you received it?
```
[L0970, 2023]

**Phone first, then email**

1. Call the supplier first, since a phone call reaches the right person and creates a first contact. [L0429, 2022]
2. Follow up by email afterward, because the supplier then expects the message. [L0429, 2022]
3. If there is still no reply, call weekly and record each follow-up in a supplier tracking spreadsheet. [L0429, 2022]

- Call the supplier the next day, in a friendly way, to confirm the application was received. Follow up sooner only if the supplier asked you to expect a longer wait. [L0835, 2023]
- Confirmation template for after an application:

```
Hi [Supplier contact name], this is [Your name] from [Store name]. I am following up on the application we sent on [date]. I just wanted to make sure you received it. Please let me know if you need anything else from us.
Thank you,
[Your name]
[Company name]
[Phone number]
```
[L0835, 2023]

- Application status check:

```
Hi [Supplier contact name], I just want to touch base and make sure my application was received. It does not need to be long or pushy. Could you let me know where it stands and what the next steps are? Thank you, [your name]
```
[L0247, 2021] **

- Confirmation of online dealer application:

```
I'm checking in to confirm you received my online application. We're very interested in becoming a reseller of your products.
```
[L0235, 2021-22] **

- Suppliers with online dealer applications expect the form to be filled out. If there is no reply after a couple of days, call to follow up. [L1112, 2021] **
- Timing after an online application, sources compared: submit, wait 24 hours, call only if there is no response [L0235, 2021-22, 04:50]; call the next day [L0835, 2023]; call if there is no reply after a couple of days [L1112, 2021]. The 24-hour rule and the newest source (2023) agree: about one day after submitting, if you have heard nothing, call. (Added 2026-11-07.)
- If a supplier does not reply within about two days after a promised follow-up, call or email again and stay in regular contact. [L0358, 2020] **
- Follow up with a supplier about three days after a call or email if you have not heard back. Keep the follow-up short, for example by confirming they saw your message. [L0941, 2022]
- If there is no response to an email or contact form, wait one day, then follow up. Suppliers often have no phone number listed. [L1277, 2020] **
- Email visibility: two to three days after the initial email, reply in the same thread. This creates two emails from you in the recipient's inbox on different days. [L0276, 2024]
- Do not sort or prioritize suppliers when reaching out. Contact all of them, keep notes on those that decline, and circle back later. [L0934, 2023]
- Start supplier outreach early, even before the store is ready. A supplier that is low on stock can still add you to a list for later. [L0803, 2022] **
- Starting outreach early in the autumn avoids the busy and slow periods described in the holiday section. [L0360, 2024]

**Warm leads first**

When suppliers say "email me" or "fill out our form", prioritize them, because a dialogue has started. [L0251, 2023]

Procedure for a warm-lead batch: [L0251, 2023] *(2023)*

1. Reach out to 20 or more suppliers as your minimum starting pool. [L0251, 2023]
2. Prioritize suppliers that said "email me" or "fill out our form". [L0251, 2023]
3. Follow up on these warm leads daily or every other day before contacting the remaining suppliers. [L0251, 2023]
4. Once a supplier sends approval documents, drop other priorities and complete the paperwork immediately. [L0251, 2023]
5. Upload their products and integrate them into your store. [L0251, 2023]
6. Return to contacting the remaining suppliers on your list. [L0251, 2023]

**Old onboarding forms**

Do not simply answer an old onboarding form or email that has sat unanswered for a long time. Contact that supplier again as if the relationship were new, using the channel you originally used (phone, contact form or email), because contacts and forms may have changed. [L0360, 2024] *(2024)*

### Follow-up intervals

**Which interval applies**

| General follow-up until a clear yes or no | Every 2-3 days, 2024 | [L0276, 2024] |
| Supplier list of 300 or more | Every 2-3 days with each supplier, 2024 | [L0276, 2024] |

- Conflict on follow-up frequency: 2019 advice set a weekly minimum and a 2-3 business-day gap after voicemail [L0357, 2019; L0243, 2019] **. Newer advice (2023-2025) uses every 2-3 days, every other day in active warm-lead phases, or the next business day after a missed call [L0276, 2024; L0251, 2023; L0807, 2025]. Use the tighter schedule while a supplier is active, and the longer intervals once a supplier has declined.
- Follow up with suppliers every two to three days until you get a clear yes or no. Persistence shows you are serious, and busy suppliers forget you if they are not reminded. [L0276, 2024]
- With a list of 300 or more, keep following up every 2-3 days. Persistent follow-up is not spam when it is backed by genuine business intent. [L0276, 2024]
- Keep following up until you receive a yes or a no. A clear no is better than no information. [L0357, 2019] **
- Keep following up by phone or email, because suppliers are busy and repeated contact often gets a response. Do not stop after one attempt. [L0835, 2023] *(2023)*
- Follow-ups are normal and expected. Suppliers receive many calls and do not treat a second attempt as a nuisance. [L0927, 2020-26]
- If a supplier does not answer within two to three days, follow up by phone. [L0471, 2024] *(2024)*
- Follow up after a supplier's stated timeline, not before. A supplier that says approval takes 4-8 weeks gets its reminder at 4 weeks. [L0294, 2025+]

**Gold-tier suppliers**

- Gold suppliers can take 3 months, 6 months, or more than a year to approve a store. Plan follow-ups on that timeline, while many other suppliers can be added immediately. [L0191, undated]
- You can contact gold suppliers early, but expect "not yet" until you reach a certain traffic or sales level. Use the time to build the store and track results so you can reapply later. [L0486, 2020-26]

**Procedure: supplier who said approval takes 4-8 weeks**

1. Document the contact person's name, the contact date, and the timeline in your supplier tracking spreadsheet. [L0294, 2025+]
2. Set a calendar reminder for exactly 4 weeks after initial contact. [L0294, 2025+]
3. Follow up with the supplier on the reminder date. [L0294, 2025+]
4. For email-only suppliers that have not responded after nearly 2 weeks of follow-ups every 2 days, stop following up on Fridays before long weekends, when staff take extended time off. [L0294, 2025+]
5. Resume follow-ups on Wednesday or Thursday of the following week, once decision makers are back in the office. [L0294, 2025+]

### Unresponsive suppliers

**Basic checks first**

- If no supplier responds, check the basics: that your email address receives mail, that your contact details on voicemails are correct, and that the store is not password-protected when you point suppliers to it. [L1002, 2019] **
- If suppliers do not respond despite outreach, follow up persistently and check whether the outreach strategy itself is failing before changing anything else. [L0969, 2022] **
- When many suppliers reject your outreach, the root cause is usually the approach: the wording of your message, the way you contact them, or the time of day you reach out. It is rarely the number of suppliers or the niche itself. [L1254, 2020-26]
- Switching niches or contacting many more suppliers after repeated rejections wastes time if you have not first diagnosed why suppliers say no. Review your message, contact method and timing first. [L1254, 2020-26]
- If hundreds of supplier applications produce only one approval, the application approach or the way suppliers are contacted is likely broken. Approval rates that low are not normal and must be investigated. [L0429, 2022] **

**Silence is not rejection**

- Suppliers are typically busy, and a lack of immediate response does not indicate rejection. Consistent follow-up through the entire 7-day schedule significantly increases approval rates. [L0126, undated]
- Do not interpret silence as rejection. Assume the supplier is busy with other matters and keep following up every day or every other day by email or phone. [L0251, 2023] *(2023)*
- Treat a silent supplier as a busy schedule. Follow up every few days rather than assuming rejection. [L0468, 2020] **
- Suppliers that go quiet while approval is pending are often busy, not hostile. Follow up on the same channel first. [L0243, 2019] **
- When suppliers stop responding after initial interest, it may mean they were not confident in the store's viability or have internal issues. Following up after getting the first two or three approvals from other suppliers often works better, because the store appears more established. [L0255, 2025] *(2025)*

**Procedure: unresponsive supplier, phone and email**

1. Send a follow-up email to the unresponsive supplier. [L0255, 2025]
2. Find a phone number on the supplier's website or through a Google search. [L0255, 2025]
3. Call the phone number. [L0255, 2025]
4. If the individual contact is unresponsive, email the main support address (support@, sales@, or info@ at the supplier's domain). [L0255, 2025]
5. Continue with other suppliers while you wait. You may retry after getting the first two or three approvals. [L0255, 2025]

**Procedure: follow-up after silence (2019 routine)**

1. Call the supplier rather than relying only on email. [L1002, 2019] **
2. If the call goes to voicemail, leave a message asking for a callback. [L1002, 2019] **
3. If nobody calls back within a week, call again. [L1002, 2019] **
4. Keep following up with calls and voicemails until you get a clear yes or no. [L1002, 2019] **

**Procedure: call at different times of day**

If suppliers consistently do not answer during your usual calling time, try other times of day: early morning (8:30 AM), mid-morning (10 AM), afternoon (2 PM), and late afternoon (4 PM). Small supplier companies often have aligned schedules, scheduled meetings, or lunch breaks that follow their time zone or company culture. [L0251, 2023] *(2023)*

- Call suppliers on business days. If a supplier does not answer, note it and call again on the next business day. [L0807, 2025]

**Procedure: LinkedIn fallback**

1. After following up by email on Wednesday or Thursday and receiving no response by Friday, switch to LinkedIn as an alternative contact method. [L0294, 2025+]
2. Search for the supplier company on LinkedIn. [L0294, 2025+]
3. Identify employees of that company and review their job titles. [L0294, 2025+]
4. If you do not have a LinkedIn account, create one and list yourself as owner of your company. [L0294, 2025+]
5. Send a direct message to an identified employee using this text: [L0294, 2025+]

```
Hi [Contact Name], I've been following up for a few weeks now, and we're really excited about listing for your brand. I haven't heard back yet and wanted to make sure my emails are getting through. Are you the right person to contact? If not, could you let me know who I should reach out to? Really appreciate your help, and thanks in advance.
```
[L0294, 2025+]

6. Keep a friendly, professional tone throughout, because friendliness increases the likelihood of a positive response. [L0294, 2025+]

**Procedure: unresponsive large supplier**

1. Before cutting ties, reply to an email from the contact person. Say that messages seem unanswered and ask whether someone else at the company can be contacted. [L0805, 2024] *(2024)*
2. Call the company again, ask to be connected to another person, and voice your concerns. [L0805, 2024]
3. If the new contact is just as unresponsive, stop selling that supplier's products. [L0805, 2024]

- A supplier whose sales contact does not answer emails or calls for weeks is a serious warning sign for later order processing. Move on, and revisit only after a long time, when the contact may have changed. [L0854, 2022] **
- If a supplier does not answer a sourcing request within a few days, escalate through the account manager on WhatsApp or the public community channel. Public escalation often gets a faster reply than a private message. [L0524, 2023] **
- If an order is delayed or a supplier does not respond, ask the supplier directly through its contact channels and keep your own record of the communication. Do not assume a supplier is reachable just because it is listed. [L0562, undated] **
- Approval timing from suppliers varies widely, from about an hour to many days. If a supplier does not respond, follow up consistently, since slow responders are often less reliable suppliers. [L0960, 2019] **

**Out-of-stock suppliers**

- If a supplier is out of stock, it will usually tell you during outreach. Do not list its products yet. Work with suppliers that have stock, and circle back to the out-of-stock supplier after a few months. [L1090, 2021] **
- A supplier that is out of stock or pressed by many inquiries: record the contact, ask when to follow up, and record that time. [L0665, 2022] **
