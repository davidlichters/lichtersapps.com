#### Building the policy

- Build a Shopify shipping policy for a high-ticket store with domestic suppliers in this order [L0541, undated]:
  1. Shipping Coverage Area
  2. Order Confirmation
  3. Order Processing Time
  4. Shipment Notification & Tracking
  5. Estimated Delivery Time
  6. Shipping Carriers
  7. Delays & Exceptions
  8. Contact Information
- A shipping policy for high-ticket products should stay between 300 and 500 words, use simple paragraphs and short section headers, and avoid hype and legal wording. Write it for real customers so it reads as a premium, established brand that takes logistics seriously [L0541, undated].
- A shipping policy pasted into Shopify must not contain bracketed placeholders, notes, or the word "dropshipping". Leftover template text or the word itself makes the store look unfinished and damages trust in a high-ticket purchase [L0541, undated].
- Starting values for the policy's delivery figures are in the delivery windows table above [L1048, 2020-26].
- Use the policy fields to state: the shipping region (continental United States only), handling time, transit time and total delivery time, each in business days [L1048, 2020-26; L0541, undated].

### Common Mistakes

- Counting only the advertised shipping days with a supplier underestimates delivery time [L0678, 2023].
- Long shipping times can kill viral momentum [L0706, 2020+].
- Shipping on your own UPS or FedEx account makes you liable for overage claims, and freight should not go through UPS or FedEx [L0971, 2024].
- Do not have a supplier ship products to your own address first and then to the customer. Ask the supplier to ship directly to the customer's address [L1111, 2020].
- Do not open a carrier account before contacting suppliers [L1090, 2021].
- Do not assume a group shipping discount is a good deal for everyone [L0942, 2022].
- Do not assume shipping on your own account is cheaper without comparing it with supplier quotes [L0551, 2020; L0835, 2023].

### Shipping Model: Supplier Account or Own Account

- Ship through the supplier first, since suppliers usually offer the best shipping rates. If the supplier marks up shipping, ask for better rates or another service, then compare quotes from several shipping companies using the same shipment details. [L1277, 2020]
- Choose products that suppliers can ship on their own account when they allow it. If an item is damaged or lost, the supplier sends the replacement and handles the insurance claim. [L1274, 2018]
- If you ship on your own account, you must file the insurance claim yourself and wait to be repaid by the carrier, which is slower. Damage is rare, but repeated damage from one supplier's products means that supplier is bad. [L1274, 2018]
- Suppliers ideally ship on their own accounts, but some will only ship on yours. This is common and should be checked with a test shipment before the first order. [L1279, 2021]
- Keep a shipping arrangement table with one row per supplier, as in the example below. Check it before placing any order, because it decides whether the supplier or your business books the shipment. [L1291, undated]

| Supplier | Shipping |
|---|---|
| Bobs Brown Chair | Supplier books shipping |
| Super Seats | We book shipping using UPS |
| Crazy Chairs | Supplier books shipping |

### Carriers and Who Books the Shipment

- Ask a supplier which carrier they use for their top retailers, such as FedEx, UPS, DHL or a freight broker, and send shipping details accordingly. Ship to the customer's address from the supplier's account. [L1111, 2020]
- When a supplier arranges shipping, it books pickup and delivery with its own nominated carrier after you place the order. You only need to pass the customer's delivery address and phone number to the supplier. [L1291, undated]
- For suppliers whose shipping you arrange yourself, ask the supplier for the package weight and dimensions before booking, because the carrier needs both. The supplier sends these details together with its invoice for the order. [L1291, undated]
- Shipping to the customer from a supplier with poor shipping and thin margins: test different warehouse locations and freight companies, and add more suppliers with better margins. Those products can then sit in the profitable group that the budget supports. [L1285, 2024]

### Free Shipping and Pricing It In

- Free shipping is the default offer in this dropshipping model because competitors offer it and customers expect it. The seller does not get shipping for free; the seller pays the supplier and absorbs that cost. [L1180, 2020-26]
- Free shipping is offered to customers, and the supplier is paid the actual shipping cost. The store does not calculate shipping through Shopify in this model. [L1111, 2020]
- Free shipping is offered only to the continental United States, not to Alaska, Hawaii or international destinations, because shipping there is very expensive. In other countries, international shipping is usually too costly to offer for free or to make available at all. [L1180, 2020-26]
- Free shipping is an offer many stores overlook. Shoppers who want to save money will choose a free-shipping store over a similar store that charges delivery, so the store can absorb shipping costs to win more customers and stand out from competitors. [L1400, undated]
- Shipping to Canada from US-based suppliers is usually fine with free shipping, but at the start put most ad budget into US sales, since those produce the highest profit. [L1277, 2020]
- Offering free shipping worldwide is only sustainable if the shipping cost is covered by the product margin or the supplier. Add the free shipping claim only after checking the cost per country. [L1250, 2026]
- Shipping cost limits, two sources, both apply to the same goal:
  - Keep shipping costs at a maximum of 10 dollars, or make shipping free by building it into the product price, especially for lower-margin products under 50 dollars. [L1260, undated]
  - For products under $50, keep shipping at a maximum of about 10% of the product price, or offer free shipping by including the shipping cost in the product price. (Under $50, 10% is at most $5, so the percentage is the tighter limit for those products. The $10 cap is the reference for higher-priced items.) [L1345, 2021+]
- Expedited shipping should cost no more than 15-20% of the product price, and high expedited fees should be avoided. [L1260, undated; L1345, 2021+]
- Expedited shipping can be marked up for profit, but only offer it with suppliers who respond very quickly. If your supplier usually takes a day or two to respond, a second-day delivery promise puts you in a bad position. [L1357, 2018]
- Free shipping and white glove can be communicated on the product page, at the product price and near the add-to-cart button. Showing the estimated arrival date next to free shipping builds trust at the point of purchase. [L1281, 2022]
- Ship trial or free-examination products from the warehouse or branch nearest the buyer, with shipping paid in advance. A shorter delivery distance shortens the time before the trial starts and keeps the buyer from losing interest. Prepaid shipping also removes a reason to delay. [L1369, undated]

### Shopify Setup: Locations, Zones, Profiles and Rates

- Shopify shipping settings must contain at least one shipping rate before launch, and a free shipping rate is the minimum required. [L1145, 2021-22]
- Shipping must be set up before launch, because a shipping zone with no rates stops customers in that zone from completing checkout. The zone shows 'No rates. Customers in this zone won't be able to complete checkout' until at least one rate is added. [L1167, 2026]
- Shopify splits shipping into a Domestic zone for your home country and an International zone that covers the other countries in your active markets. Each zone needs at least one shipping rate, because a zone without rates stops checkout for customers located there. [L1167, 2026]
- International shipping zones with no rates block checkout for those countries. Shipping rates and active markets must both be in place for each country you want to sell to. [L1167, 2026]
- Under Settings > Shipping and delivery, one shipping policy is created for the domestic country, United States, with shipping set to free. Free shipping then appears in checkout for US buyers. [L1306, 2020-26]
- The location list under Shipping and delivery should contain only the store's own billing address as the single location, with everything else left at default. [L1306, 2020-26]
- Supplier addresses should not be added as Shopify locations. Only the store's own business location belongs there. [L1306, 2020-26]
- Default domestic shipping rates in the example were $7.50 and $5.00. Delete these default rates under Settings > Shipping and delivery > General shipping rates before adding your own rates. [L1167, 2026]
- Procedure: Set up shipping rates in Shopify:
  1. Go to Settings > Shipping and delivery and click Manage on the shipping profile.
  2. Delete the default rates Shopify adds.
  3. Create a custom flat rate named 'Free shipping' with a price of 0.
  4. Add an express option, for example 'Expedited shipping' at $4.99.
  5. Repeat for the rest of the world with a suitable rate.
  6. Optionally create special zones such as ePacket or T4 for Canada, Australia, and the United Kingdom, with their own price.

  [L1345, 2021+]
- For a free shipping offer, create a flat rate named for the offer (for example 'Free shipping') with a shipping rate of 0 and apply it in the zone. If you offer free shipping to both rate types in the domestic zone, set both to 0. [L1167, 2026]
- For free shipping above a set order value, such as $50, use 'Add conditional pricing' on the rate instead of a flat 0 rate. Choose the rate type 'Use flat rate' and enter a custom rate name. [L1167, 2026]
- The default package is a 'Sample box' of 22 x 13.7 x 4.2 cm and 0 kg. It is used to calculate shipping rates at checkout and is pre-selected when buying labels, so replace it with your real package size and weight. [L1167, 2026]
- All products not assigned to another shipping profile go into the General profile, and newly created products are added there automatically. Create a new shipping profile only when certain products need different rates or destination restrictions. [L1167, 2026]
- Expected delivery date options are Shop Promise (automated delivery dates for eligible orders), Manual delivery dates (shown for orders without the Shop Promise badge), and Local delivery (off by default, with a Set up option). [L1167, 2026]
- Procedure: Setting up shipping zones (Settings > Shipping and delivery > General profile):
  1. Open the general profile for all products.
  2. Create separate zones for the top four markets: Canada, Australia, United States and United Kingdom.
  3. Put the remaining countries into a fifth zone (the lesson's example lists Germany, Europe, New Zealand and other countries).
  4. Add the countries to each zone.

  Lumping countries into one zone is acceptable, but separate zones are easier to read and manage. [L1327, 2026]
- Procedure: Settings > Shipping and delivery > General profile > Add shipping option for Express Shipping: choose the Express shipping option, set Rate type to Flat, set the price to $0.00 and enter the number of business days for delivery. The estimated dates at checkout are calculated from transit time and fulfillment time. [L1327, 2026]
- Express Free Shipping is always used: the shipping cost is not charged to the customer and is instead included in the product price, calculated from the average order value of the bundle sold. Shipping is therefore set as a flat $0.00 at checkout. [L1327, 2026]
- Shipping rules and shipping costs can be set up later and do not need to be finished when building the first product page. Shipping only becomes a detailed task when you add shipping rules or shipping costs. [L1331, 2025-26]

### Handling Times and Dispatch

- Separate processing time from delivery time in the shipping policy: processing covers how long until the order ships, delivery covers how long until it arrives. State both as business days. [L1250, 2026]
- For an in-stock order, a store can state that it ships within three business days of the order date and sends a shipping confirmation within 24 hours after the parcel leaves the warehouse. If the customer has no shipping confirmation within four business days, they can follow up with the store by email. These timings belong on the shipping page so customers know the expected window. [L1264, 2021-22]
- Some suppliers can dispatch quickly. CJ Dropshipping and similar suppliers can ship orders within about 24 hours when the product is in stock. A private agent holding local stock can also ship within 24 hours or less, so a fast dispatch promise can be stated on the store when the supplier is verified to be that quick. [L1259, undated]
- Suppliers such as CJ Dropshipping, or a private agent holding stock, can often dispatch within about 24 hours, which can support a 1-2 day dispatch promise on the product page. Confirm dispatch times with the supplier before making that promise. [L1260, undated]
- When the supplier ships within 24 hours, state dispatch time as 'We ship within one to two days'. Use the real dispatch time when writing shipping copy, for example CJ Dropshipping or a private stock agent typically ships within 24 hours or less. [L1345, 2021+]
- Dispatch times differ by source, so use the one your supplier confirms:
  - In-stock orders with a 3-business-day ship window, 24-hour confirmation and 4-business-day follow-up. [L1264, 2021-22]
  - Verified fast suppliers (CJ Dropshipping, private stock agents) at about 24 hours, supporting a 1-2 day promise. [L1259, undated; L1260, undated]
- one source's example template processes orders within 14 business days and moves orders placed after 12 PM PST, or on weekends or holidays, to the next business day. This is a template value and should be replaced with your supplier's real figure. [L1327, 2026]
- Orders are prepared within [X-X] business days, with weekends and public holidays not counted. A confirmation email with tracking details arrives once the parcel has left the store. [L1250, 2026]
- Before promising a shipping time or a return window in your policy, confirm that your supplier can meet it. A policy the supplier cannot fulfil creates refund requests and complaints. [L1250, 2026]
- Shipping policy text is expected to change after suppliers and products are in place, because delivery times and costs depend on the local shipping area and the country the store operates in. Adjust the shipping page at that point rather than treating the first version as final. [L1264, 2021-22]

### Delivery Estimates and Expectations

- Use the default product template for estimated delivery when all suppliers ship in a similar time, and create a separate template for each supplier whose delivery window differs, such as one that takes 7 to 14 days. [L1192, 2020-26]
- Show shipping times on the page, for example 7 to 14 days, and mention the express shipping option. Clear delivery times reduce doubt before the purchase. [L1194, undated]
- Advertising a delivery time you cannot meet leads to complaints and refunds. Test orders with China-based suppliers reportedly took about 20 days while quoted 4 to 9 days, so only advertise a delivery time you have measured. [L1214, 2023+]
- Delivery within about 10 days is generally acceptable, because customers wait when the product and service are good. A Prime-like 1-2 day service only matters at high daily volume from one country. [L1217, 2023+]
- Express carrier lines come in tiers: Priority typically delivers in about 3-4 days, Standard is the normal choice, and Economy can take 15-18 days. Delivery of 3-5 days is considered good, and even 5 days is acceptable. [L1217, 2023+]
- Domestic shipping from a local supplier typically takes two to four days, unless a product is on backorder, in which case it can take longer. [L1481, 2021-22]
- Enter the delivery window in business days based on the fulfillment provider's real transit time: a US 3PL uses 12 to 21 business days (or 10 to 14 business days for a faster setup), while a China 3PL needs a longer window because transit is longer. Do not promise shorter times than the fulfillment center can deliver. [L1327, 2026]
- Delivery windows in the sources differ by setup and are not interchangeable:
  - one source template: Standard 14 to 21 business days, Express 7 to 10 business days. [L1327, 2026]
  - one source copy template: delivery in 7 to 10 business days. [L1345, 2021+]
  - one source US 3PL: 12 to 21 business days, or 10 to 14 for a faster setup. [L1327, 2026]
  - Domestic local supplier: 2 to 4 days. [L1481, 2021-22]
- Logistics and customer service matter as much as finding a winning product, because shipping and service failures directly hurt sales and repeat purchases. Plan these areas before scaling ad spend. [L1251, 2024+]
- In dropshipping, the supplier ships each order directly to the customer, often from overseas such as China, so the store owner cannot control shipping speed. Because of this, delivery time and working tracking matter a lot for customer trust and repeat purchases. [L1251, 2024+]
- Shipping times can damage influencer partnerships, so choose suppliers with fast shipping before starting influencer marketing. [L1184, 2023+]
- Prefer light, non-fragile products when choosing a product. In one case, 4 kg items shipped to the UK, Australia and the US without customs problems. [L1217, 2023+]
- Set the customer-facing delivery window with a positive lead-in. Use this copy on the product page and in checkout, replacing bracketed placeholders:

```
Your order is on its way! We ship your item within [1-2] days, usually within 24 hours, from our [supplier or stock location]. Delivery takes [7-10] business days to your door, and you will receive your tracking number within [12-24] hours of shipping. Due to high demand, delivery times may vary slightly during peak periods, but we will keep you updated every step of the way.
```

  [L1345, 2021+]
- Rules for that copy: open with the positive shipping promise, never state a delay without a reassuring line, and use 'We pay for shipping' instead of 'free shipping' when shipping is free. Give the delivery window such as 7 to 10 business days with a positive lead-in and tracking within 12 to 24 hours. [L1345, 2021+]
- Phrase free shipping as 'We pay for shipping' on the store. [L1345, 2021+]
- For a holiday promotion that promises delivery before Christmas, the supplier must be able to deliver within about two weeks. Confirm this before running the promotion. [L1287, 2025]
- one source worldwide policy template gives both estimates in one line: Expected arrival: domestic orders [X-X days], international orders [X-X days]. Dispatch happens within [X] business days. When the parcel goes out, you receive a tracking number to follow it. Customs and weather can push back delivery dates. [L1250, 2026]

### Tracking

- To give customers a local tracking number instead of a China-origin number, follow this procedure:
  1. Ask the supplier or agent to ship with a local carrier such as USPS, Royal Mail or Australia Post, and to hide the origin information.
  2. Ask them to send the local number to Shopify automatically once the courier issues it.
  3. If no local number appears quickly, send the original tracking number and ask them to hide the origin.

  The local number often appears the same day or in a few hours for destinations like UK, Germany, Australia and New Zealand, but may only appear on arrival in the destination country for others. [L1315, 2023]
- A courier tracking number that is only the first leg (for example a China number) may not show delivery progress or days left. The local carrier number is needed for that, and tracking with a local carrier like FedEx or DHL shows the remaining days. [L1315, 2023]
- Once an order ships, the customer receives a tracking number by email so they can follow the package. [L1250, 2026]
- Procedure for Google Merchant Center shipping:
  1. Check the Google Merchant Center shipping settings.
  2. Locate the free shipping entry.
  3. Enter the average delivery timeframe for the whole catalog.

  The delivery time applies catalog-wide, not per product. [L1111, 2020]

### Multi-Supplier and Add-On Shipments

- Products sold as add-ons or upsells should come from the same supplier as the main product, so the order ships together without an extra shipping fee. Items from another source ship separately and need their own handling. [L1277, 2020]
- Use a separate delivery template for each supplier whose window differs from the default. [L1192, 2020-26]
- Each supplier's shipping arrangement must be recorded separately in the table above, because each one may book its own carrier. [L1291, undated]

### Shipping Insurance and Damage

- Shipping insurance should always be included on every shipment. When the supplier ships from its own account, insurance is typically the default and the seller should confirm it in advance. [L1281, 2022]
- Insurance and replacement responsibilities follow the account used: a supplier shipping on its own account handles replacements and claims, while a store shipping on its own account must file and wait for repayment. [L1274, 2018]
