### Recontacting after growth

**When to recontact**

- Suppliers who turned you down earlier can be contacted again once the store is live, real products are sold, other brands have approved you, and traffic systems are running. Name current supplier relationships when it fits the conversation. [L0191, undated]
- Recontact timing differs by source. Use the number the supplier gave you first:
  - Supplier-stated window: follow up on that date [L0927, 2020-26; L0801, 2021]
  - 3-6 months after going live, once you have traffic and results [L0470, 2023] *(2023)*
  - Every 3 months after a refusal with no criteria, with an update on growth [L0835, 2023] *(2023)*
  - Every 3-6 months, with the reason noted and concrete progress shared [L1279, 2021] **
  - 3 months after refusal, then again 3 months later if still refused [L0971, 2024] *(2024)*
- Track each declined supplier with its reason and a dated follow-up. Each rejection has a re-contact date such as 3-6 months. [L0191, undated]

**Procedure: recontact a supplier that declined the store (2020 routine)**

Procedure for a supplier that refuses a new dealer: [L0358, 2020] **

1. Accept the refusal as normal. [L0358, 2020]
2. Record the refusal and date in your supplier spreadsheet. [L0358, 2020]
3. Once you have traffic and sales from other brands, reapproach the supplier, which may take three to six months. [L0358, 2020]
4. Show your results and request approval again. [L0358, 2020]

**Procedure: reapproach an out-of-stock or busy supplier (2022)**

1. Note the contact date, person and outcome in the supplier sourcing worksheet. [L0665, 2022]
2. When they say they are not taking new dealers, ask when to follow up and record that time. [L0665, 2022]
3. On follow-up, call the same contact and state how the store launched, its sales progress, and the brands already sold, plus your desire to add them. [L0665, 2022]

**Follow-up call after launch (about 2-3 weeks after the date given)**

1. Plan the follow-up call for 2-3 weeks after your stated launch date. [L0133, undated]
2. Call and attempt to reach the same person from your initial call, using their name to remind them of your conversation. [L0133, undated]
3. Ask how their business has been, and bring up anything important from the first conversation. [L0133, undated]
4. Tell them your launch exceeded your team's expectations and you are even more excited about the project. [L0133, undated]
5. Mention that you wanted to call sooner but needed time to smooth out internal systems and processes. [L0133, undated]
6. Share that your initial results beat your projections. [L0133, undated]
7. Add that all your other supplier partners are equally thrilled with their product performance. [L0133, undated]
8. Inform them you are scaling up your advertising campaigns significantly. [L0133, undated]
9. Ask directly if they are ready to partner with you now. [L0133, undated]
10. If they decline, ask what you can do to make it happen, and show genuine interest in their concerns. [L0133, undated]
11. Highlight how this project is already benefiting your company, and explain the growth potential of adding their product line. [L0133, undated]
12. If they continue declining, ask for the real underlying reason, and state that you genuinely want to understand their hesitation. [L0133, undated]
13. On your next follow-up call, address their concerns with specific solutions or workarounds. [L0133, undated]

Send by email or messaging once the store is live with products and traffic.


- Follow-up with declined suppliers is an ongoing process, not a one-time task. Reference the previous conversation, name the brands you already sell, and ask what must happen before they can authorize you. [L0938, 2020-26]
- If a gold supplier turns down a new store for lack of traffic or sales, accept it, tell them you will reconnect once the business is established, and move on to silver suppliers in the same niche. [L1085, 2018] *(2018)*
- Suppliers that decline a new store can be approached again with related niches. Get the first five to ten brands live, then return to the target suppliers, since live brands add credibility. [L0667, 2025] *(2025)*
- Build credibility with the first live brands before going back: [L0969, 2022; L0667, 2025] **, expand the supplier list through trade show networks and consider a broader niche if outreach keeps failing.

- A supplier that is not accepting new accounts: note the date, return with sales results or traffic data at that time. [L0801, 2021] **

### Restarting with former suppliers

- When restarting with former approved suppliers, check for new products and price changes first, since approved accounts usually receive those updates by email. A separate "I'm back" message is often unnecessary. [L0804, 2023] *(2023)*
- Restart template:

```
Subject: Current inventory, products and pricing

Hi [Supplier contact name],

I hope the past couple of years have been good for you and your team. The last time we spoke, inventory was a big issue on your side, so I'd like to get up to date. Could you send me your current inventory list, your current product offerings and your current pricing?

I'm excited about working together this year.

Thank you,
[Your name]
[Store name]
[Phone] | [Email]
```

### Ongoing outreach routine

**Aim and pacing**

- The first goal is the first five suppliers to approve you. The real aim is approval from every supplier on your master list, including the gold-tier brands. [L0936, 2020-26]
- Do not sort or prioritize suppliers when reaching out. Contact all of them. [L0934, 2023]
- Upload a supplier's products to your store as soon as that supplier approves you, then return to outreach for the next supplier. Alternate between outreach and product uploads until every supplier on the list has been worked through. [L0936, 2020-26]
- When a supplier approves you, mark the lesson complete and move to the product upload lesson. Upload that supplier's live products to your store, then return to the contact plan and continue working down the list. [L0936, 2020-26]
- Approval rates differ by niche and store maturity; expect a partial approval rate with a new store. [L0966, 2025]
- Repeated contact with suppliers is part of the routine. Do not stop after one attempt. [L0835, 2023]

**Tools and planning**

  1. Log in to the members area. [L0808, 2026]
  2. Enter your name, company name (including LLC or corporation if applicable), store URL, phone number, and business email. [L0808, 2026]
  3. Enter the supplier's details from your supplier tracking sheet. [L0808, 2026]
  4. Click "Generate my contact plan". [L0808, 2026]
  5. Download the plan with calls and email follow-ups for each supplier. [L0808, 2026]
- Before a Black Friday launch, plan supplier outreach for the roughly two weeks before Black Friday. [L1097, 2025] *(2025)*
- Plan supplier outreach around holiday closures using the closure plan in the holiday section. [L0471, 2024]
- After submitting a supplier application, call the next day in a friendly way to confirm receipt. [L0835, 2023] *(2023)*
- Supplier approvals: call during normal business weeks, since the decision maker is available then. [L0803, 2022] **
- Keep the tracking spreadsheet and contact plan in sync so each supplier has a next date. [L0968, 2021] **
- Use the supplier list to decide who you call next; follow up each supplier on its own schedule. [L0936, 2020-26]

**Routine sequence for each day**

1. Check the tracking sheet for each supplier's next date. [L0126, undated; L0968, 2021]
2. Make the calls and send the emails scheduled for today, following the contact plan. [L0808, 2026; L0936, 2020-26]
3. Record every response and set the next follow-up date. [L0936, 2020-26; L0938, 2020-26]
4. If a supplier approves you, upload its products, mark the lesson complete, then return to the list. [L0936, 2020-26]
5. Follow up on warm leads before contacting the rest of the list. [L0251, 2023]
6. Keep the Master Supplier list current so no supplier falls out of the rotation. [L0133, undated]

**Supplier lists and the offline period**

- Expect slower replies around Black Friday through Christmas and New Year, when many suppliers close for a break. [L0807, 2025] *(2025)*
- Build a complete supplier contact list while offline, so you can start calling the moment you are back online. [L0941, 2022]

### Follow-up cadence

- Follow up at a steady pace until the supplier gives a clear answer, rather than treating a single unanswered message as the end. [L1287, 2025]
- Supplier relationship calls should not be outsourced, because relationships are central to this business. If time is short, send emails during the day with a scheduling tool such as the Boomerang Gmail extension so they arrive at a business-hours time. [L1279, 2021]
- Use this template to chase an application that is still pending (2024):

```
Hi, this is [Your name] from [Store name]. We spoke last week and you asked me to send an email, which I sent to the attention of [Contact name]. I have not heard back yet. Could you check with [Contact name] that it was received and let me know the status of our application?
```

### Unresponsive suppliers

- A supplier can go silent after an account setup or order, which is a form of ghosting. If a supplier does not respond, escalate to their sales or distribution contact early. [L1472, 2022]
- If a supplier stops answering after being responsive, keep following up instead of giving up. Silence often comes from busy periods, such as holiday weekends or back-to-school season. (2025.) [L1287, 2025]

### Holiday and seasonal timing

- Silence during holiday weekends and back-to-school season is often caused by busy periods, not by a refusal, so keep following up rather than closing the lead. [L1287, 2025]

### Objections and declines

- Expect that not every supplier will approve a new reseller account, so contact several suppliers in parallel. In one case, one supplier approved within two minutes while the next one refused. A refusal alone is not a reason to stop. [L1437, 2018]
- If one supplier declines, tell competing suppliers that demand for their products is high and that you have turned down sales for their brand. Once you have one supplier, word of mouth in the industry, especially in niche markets, often brings in other suppliers who reach out to you. [L1441, 2018]

### Paused supply and reasons for rejection

- When a supplier pauses new retailers because of inventory or supply chain problems, work through this procedure:
  1. Ask the supplier when its inventory is expected to stabilize and record the answer in your tracking sheet.
  2. Follow up on that date, or at the interval the supplier gives.
  3. Meanwhile, source backup suppliers from a trade show directory such as TSNN.com. [L1279, 2021]

### Re-contacting after growth

- A "we are not taking new retailers right now" answer usually means the supplier wants more authority, traffic, sales and a track record first. Return later with a live store and history, and ask again. (2023.) [L1283, 2023]
- Use this template when reopening a conversation that was declined earlier:

```
Hi [supplier contact name], this is [your name] from [store name]. We spoke on [date of last contact] and you mentioned you were not taking on new online retailers at that time. Since then we have [specific progress, e.g. grown traffic by X percent, added channel Y, reached Z email subscribers]. We are still very excited to list your products and would love to reopen the conversation. When would be a good time to talk, or when do you expect to take on new retailers?
```
[L1279, 2021]

### Tracking and ongoing outreach routine

- Record every supplier answer, including any expected restock or reopening date, in a tracking sheet, because the paused-supply procedure and the follow-up dates depend on it. [L1279, 2021]
- Keep supplier outreach as a recurring routine: follow up on the recorded dates, escalate silent contacts early, and keep parallel contacts open. [L1472, 2022; L1437, 2018; L1279, 2021]
