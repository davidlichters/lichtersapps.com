### Tracker and supplier list layouts

**Working rules**

- Keep one tracking sheet for every supplier you contact, recording each outreach date, response, approval status and notes. This makes it possible to work down the supplier list in order without losing track of follow-ups. [L0804, 2023]
- Store every supplier contact in one sheet with the contact date, the person spoken to and notes, so all outreach stays traceable. [L0542, undated]
- Track suppliers in a spreadsheet: Google Sheets with a supplier tracking template, with an Excel version as backup. Always make your own copy of the template before entering data so the original stays unedited. [L0937, 2021-22]
- Use Google Sheets with a copied template (never edit the original template) or the Excel equivalent. The later columns for call tracking (Phone Call + Date?, Call Comments, Paused) are filled only when suppliers are contacted. [L0932, 2021-22]
- During research only the first six columns are filled in: Supplier Name, Product Type, Email, Phone Number, Website and Online Application?. The remaining columns are used later, when you start contacting suppliers. [L0937, 2021-22]

**Per-manufacturer tab, one source 2021-22:** [L1396, 2021-22]

1. Manufacturer name, phone and email.
2. A separate contact person for orders, one for shipping, and one for warranties and claims, if the manufacturer has several departments.
3. Discount codes and the discounts each team member may apply.
4. The current price list.
5. The payment method, such as a card on file or a phone payment.
6. Shipping requirements for the brand.

Discounts, shipping policies and price lists often differ by brand, so keep each brand's information fully separate. Staff who prepare quotes need the item price, shipping requirements and bulk-order discount rules to work without asking the owner. [L1396, 2021-22]

**Approval status in the tracker, one source 2021-22:** [L0458, 2021-22]

1. Google the product type with words such as supplier, distributor or manufacturer, or check the brands sold on competitor sites.
2. Find each supplier's website and record its name, email address, phone number and website URL in a spreadsheet.
3. Add columns for the online application submitted (with date), the phone call (with date) and call comments.
4. Track every contact attempt, including email, phone and online form, and the response.
5. Mark each supplier approved only when the supplier has confirmed you can sell its products.

Ids for this procedure: [L0458, 2021-22]

**Supplier type and product type**

- Record the primary product type of each supplier, because you can then speak to the product the supplier actually makes. A brand can make more than one product type, so one supplier row may list several. [L0932, 2021-22]

### Common mistakes

- Guessing a domain, recording a retailer's page instead of the brand's own site, or confusing similarly named brands (for example Aeris and Aerus). Check each brand's official site and product match before writing anything into the spreadsheet. [L0937, 2021-22]
- Guessing generic dealer or vendor addresses such as dealers@ or vendors@. [L0931, 2020-26]
- Accepting the first result for an ambiguous name. [L0940, 2020-26]
- Marking N-A after one failed search. [L0940, 2020-26]
- Digging deep into Google results pages instead of trying a different search variant. [L0940, 2020-26]
- Copying phone numbers or emails from a ZoomInfo profile without checking industry and location. [L0940, 2020-26]

### Planning the time

- Finding phone, email and form details for over 40 suppliers took about 20 to 25 minutes in the worked example. Plan a comparable time block for a niche's supplier research. The brand extraction step itself is faster, at about 1 to 2 minutes per competitor. [L0931, 2020-26]
