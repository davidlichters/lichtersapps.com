### Minimum Orders

#### Principles

- (2023) Minimum purchase requirements from suppliers are not recommended for most people. Only consider them if you have proven niche knowledge, existing cash flow and high confidence in sales velocity. New sellers should avoid them. [L0270, 2021]
- Handle a supplier minimum purchase requirement by:
  1. Requesting that the supplier split the order into multiple charges if your credit card limit is low.
  2. Providing a debit card associated with your business checking account.
  3. Avoiding the supplier if you are new to business. [L0270, 2021]
- If a supplier's minimum order roughly equals the MAP of one product, list the products and place the first order only after a customer buys. If the minimum is larger, ask the supplier to let you test the products on your site for one month without the minimum commitment. [L0961, 2020]
- When a new account is held pending a first order of a set amount, check the terms and conditions for how the requirement is worded. If the supplier has already sent product information and images, upload the products and place the order. [L0853, 2021]

Email template for a new account held pending a first order:

```
Hi [Supplier contact name],

I saw your email about the [amount] minimum first order. We are happy to do business with you and send you many orders, but we are online-only and do not yet know which products will sell best. Could you send us your product information, product images, price sheet and descriptions? We will upload them, drive traffic to them, and expect orders within our first month.

Thank you,
[Your name]
[Store name]
```
[L0853, 2021]

- (undated) Never guarantee a minimum sales volume, and do not sign anything promising a minimum dollar amount. Sales cannot be guaranteed before the products are live on your site, visitors see them and buy them. [L0933, 2020-26]
- Refusal wording for a guaranteed minimum:

```
I would never agree to a guaranteed minimum. I think we are going to do great, which is exactly why we want to sell your products. But I cannot promise a sales amount, because nobody knows until your products are on our site, our visitors see them and they buy. So I am not going to sign anything promising a minimum dollar amount, and I think honesty is the right approach for both of us.
```

### Exclusivity and Territory

- (undated) Ask a supplier for exclusivity for a new product in one region for a limited time, such as six months, before testing it. This is typically only possible with a close relationship with the supplier. [L0334, 2017]
- Exclusive distribution offers are rare. Before accepting, ask why the supplier wants exclusivity and whether they expect your store to sell only their products. Then weigh the upsides against the downsides, such as obligations that limit how you promote other brands. [L0358, 2020]
- An exclusive retailer arrangement is rare and is usually not needed. It becomes realistic only when you already generate most of a supplier's revenue. A partnership with equity or payouts over time may then let you acquire part or all of the company. [L0469, 2021]
- Exclusive selling rights for a product in a niche are usually granted only after a very strong relationship and a high sales volume with the supplier. They are rarely possible from day one. [L1068, 2019]
- Confirm that you hold an exclusive distribution deal for a product in your market before promoting it. Exclusivity means you are the only retailer selling it locally, which lets you own its brand search traffic. [L0949, 2021-22]
- For an exclusive supplier, maximize sales while the exclusivity lasts. Forecast unit sales and stay in contact so the supplier keeps stock full. [L0963, 2022]
- (2023) Suppliers rarely require you to list all their products or specific ones. A rare exception is a multi-brand supplier that reserves some brands for physical stores. [L0934, 2023]
- (2024) Some suppliers will not revoke approval or ask you to remove products due to low sales volume, even if the products do not convert. This is standard in dropshipping relationships. [L0253, 2024]

### Conditions on Trade Accounts

- (2025) Some supplier trade accounts require verified dealer status before you can buy, and some brands set conditions on your sales. One brand allowed sales only within 100 miles of your address, and another required a guaranteed 50,000 dollars in sales per year. Check these conditions before you plan your sales territory, budget and outreach. [L0857, 2025]
- (2024) Trade program links on supplier websites usually mean the same as becoming an authorized dealer. Open the trade program page to confirm the terms. [L0856, 2024]

### Tax Documents and Sales Tax

#### Forms and Requests

- (2025) The W9 is a standard supplier requirement. It is an IRS form that shows your business entity information (LLC, sole proprietor, S corporation, etc.). [L0255, 2025]
- (2025) W9 steps:
  1. The supplier sends a W9 form request.
  2. Complete the W9 form with your business entity details.
  3. Send the completed W9 and contact person information to the supplier.
  4. Provide an email address and phone number as the contact person.
  5. The supplier responds with a price sheet containing product information, wholesale pricing, MAP and MSRP. [L0255, 2025]
- (2023) When a supplier asks for a state resale certificate, send the certificate for your home state and explain that you do not have economic nexus in the state the supplier is asking about. Some suppliers may also request a W-9 to confirm you are a business reselling the products. [L0835, 2023]

Email template for a resale certificate request:

```
Hi [Supplier contact name], our business is based in [Home state], and we have attached our [Home state] resale certificate. We do not currently have an economic nexus in [State the supplier asked about], so please let us know how you would handle orders shipping to that state.
[Your name]
[Company name]
```
[L0835, 2023]

- (2023) For a supplier that needs a resale or sales tax certificate:
  1. Search Google for the state name plus "reseller certificate" (or "sales tax certificate").
  2. Use the official.gov site and apply online.
  3. Send the resulting document to the supplier. [L0964, 2023]
- (2023) Expect a supplier to ask for a sales certificate, and be ready to provide buyer details when they request it. [L0964, 2023]
- (2024/2025) If a supplier that requires a reseller permit or tax ID is for a Canadian or non-US seller, search the community for members who have done this rather than relying on general advice. An EIN is not always necessary but should be checked. [L0665, 2022]

#### Sales Tax Charged by Suppliers

Two positions apply to different cases:

- (2025) Supplier agreements can require the reseller to pay sales tax. Accept this as a cost of credibility when a supplier requests it. [L0472, 2025]
- (2023) If a supplier charges sales tax because it has no resale certificate on file, provide the resale certificate or W-9 it asks for. Do not accept the charge until the supplier has confirmed it has the documents it needs. [L0835, 2023]

### Insurance

- If a supplier contract requires insurance, obtain it. A general liability policy from a large company with fixed rates is the recommended type. [L0963, 2022]
- Case: a policy sold through a broker with a low premium led to an insurance audit after revenue grew. The business owed more than 10,000 USD extra. Check that the rates are fixed before buying a policy. [L0963, 2022]

### EDI, FTP, Portals and Order Transmission

- (2025) Suppliers that use EDI or FTP run enterprise-style order integrations, and the Shopify apps for them are expensive. If a supplier asks for EDI, ask for email or an online ordering portal to submit orders instead. [L0667, 2025]
- (2023) EDI is a system for connecting directly with a supplier's inventory and order systems. Shopify apps that do this are expensive because developers must configure them for each supplier system. The answer is usually no. Ask for an alternative method of ordering and inventory checks. [L0835, 2023]
- If a supplier requires EDI integration over SPS and your business does not use EDI, say plainly that you are not set up for it. Then ask whether they will still work with you, since EDI may be a preference rather than a hard requirement. [L1112, 2021]
- Most suppliers do not need EDI. Around 95 percent of orders are sent to suppliers as automatic purchase orders by email, which is enough for most brands. [L1277, 2020]
- Automated electronic order transmission (EDI) to suppliers was not used in 2019. Some suppliers offered it, but for a small operation it was judged not worth building on Shopify. [L1002, 2019]
- (2023) Most suppliers do not accept orders through a portal. Typically the supplier agreement, usually a one- to two-page document, describes how to place orders, most often by email to an orders address. [L1283, 2023]
- (undated) When a supplier asks for a physical document or signature, you can handle it remotely:
  1. Forward their mail to a mailbox in your home country.
  2. Have it scanned to a PDF and emailed to you.
  3. Sign the PDF digitally and upload it to the mailbox service, which prints it and posts it to the supplier. [L1441, 2018]

### Marketplace and Channel Restrictions

#### Marketplaces

- (undated) Some suppliers forbid selling their products on third-party marketplaces such as Amazon, eBay or Walmart. This is common and can be ignored for the store itself, since no marketplace seller account is needed to follow the store setup. [L0360, 2024]
- (2024) Do not sell supplier products on third-party marketplaces such as Amazon or eBay, because most suppliers only authorize sales on the retailer's own store. [L1115, 2024]
- (2025) Most suppliers do not allow resale on third-party marketplaces such as Amazon or Walmart and expect sales only on your own website. The permitted sales channels are defined in the supplier agreement. [L0966, 2025]
- (undated) Most authorized-retailer supplier contracts forbid selling on third-party marketplaces such as eBay, Amazon and Walmart. True competitors are therefore online-only stores that sell the same brands, not marketplaces. [L0941, 2022]

#### Channel Permission and Ads

- (undated) Before listing a supplier's products on another sales channel, get the supplier's permission, because some suppliers may not be comfortable with it. Confirm this in writing or in the account agreement before you expand. [L1441, 2018]
- Some suppliers forbid paid ads using their brand name, including Google Ads. In that case, do not run paid campaigns on the brand. Rely on organic search and Bing traffic. [L1277, 2020]

#### Accessories and Flat-Rate Shipping

- (2023) Supplier distributor and channel rules apply. If a supplier prohibits selling accessories, or offers a flat-rate shipping cost that causes losses on accessories, sell accessories only as add-ons to a main product. [L1283, 2023]

### Trade Accounts, Affiliate Programs and Private-Supplier Setups

- (2023) Do not affiliate with a supplier's affiliate program. If a supplier offers an affiliate sign-up, ask instead whether you can become a retailer. [L0964, 2023]
- Working as an authorized retailer means the supplier ships without a supplier invoice, and the customer may see the shipping origin. This is normal for brand retailers and does not cause customer issues. [L0467, 2019]
- Buying products from third-party resellers, such as eBay sellers, and shipping them with their invoice causes problems. Authorized retailer status avoids this, because the supplier never includes an invoice. [L0467, 2019]
- (2024) Get approved with both distributors if a supplier is in stock on only one distributor and both are offered. Pricing and stock levels may differ, and approval costs nothing. [L0665, 2022]
- (2025) The authorized retailer agreement and its MAP and return terms apply to every sale of that brand. [L0667, 2025]
- Some suppliers: the affiliate, trade program and dealer routes all lead to the same outcome, so confirm which one you are on before you start. [L0856, 2024]
- (2024) Trade program links on supplier websites usually mean the same as becoming an authorized dealer. [L0856, 2024]
- (2024) Gold and private-supplier setups: Gold suppliers may reject a new store or ask to re-review it. [L0969, 2022]
- (2020, sole proprietor to LLC) Use this procedure when a sole proprietorship converts to an LLC:
  1. Fill out new vendor forms with the new business information and EIN.
  2. Email the same contact who received the original forms.
  3. Ask the supplier to update the business information on file to the new company name. [L0967, 2020]
- (2024) Resources such as the LLC setup document are shared in the supplier community channel and may require an LLC or UEN number to access. If you cannot get one, ask the community for help, and the supplier can arrange it. [L1167, 2026]

### Business Entity, Address and Account Setup

- (2024) To list a supplier with your business address, use the address from your Shopify settings:
  1. Contact the supplier as normal using your email and phone scripts.
  2. In Shopify settings, provide your official business address (for example, a Canadian address for a Canadian business).
  3. In the supplier approval forms, list your business address as it appears in your Shopify settings.
  4. The supplier will send its full product catalog regardless of how many products you mentioned.
  5. Upload only the products you want.
  6. This approach is standard and fully accepted by suppliers. [L0253, 2024]
- (2020) Ask in the initial paperwork whether the supplier ships on your account or theirs. If it is not stated, ask after approval and before orders start. [L1111, 2020]
- (undated) Some supplier approvals and documents can be handled remotely through a mailbox service, as described under EDI and portals. [L1441, 2018]
- (2025) Tax documents follow the entity type on the W9, so match the entity you register with the entity on the form. [L0255, 2025]

### Red Lines: What to Refuse

- (undated) If a supplier asks you to co-brand a website, to run their official site with your store content, or to take over advertising for their brand in exchange for an arrangement that sounds unusual, decline politely. Keep the current arrangement and do not agree to unclear terms. [L0941, 2022]
- Reject any brand that asks for an extra dropshipping fee on top of its wholesale price. Argue that the store brings new traffic, market reach and sales, so the brand gains without paying more. [L0962, 2021]
- If a new supplier asks the store to handle foreign certification, product liability or export logistics, decline unless the relationship is long-term and the extra work is worth the effort. Build such services only for established partners who rely on the store for most of their sales. [L0962, 2021]
- Never pay a supplier upfront to start a relationship. Refuse large credit card deposits, such as $4,000. [L0852, 2020; L1277, 2020]
- (2025) Avoid accepting a large initial minimum order, such as 2,500 USD, just to get listed. [L0966, 2025]
- (2021) Do not pay for a deposit or stock before a customer order. [L0270, 2021]
- (undated) Never guarantee a minimum sales volume. [L0933, 2020-26]

#### Further details

1. When the supplier responds with interest, they will email application forms.
2. These forms request standard business information: business name, address, phone number, and billing information.
3. Complete all sections of the forms completely and accurately immediately upon receipt.
4. Submit the completed forms back as quickly as possible-do not delay.
5. Maintaining momentum is critical; delayed submissions result in significantly lower approval rates.
6. An account manager will respond within a few days with approval, account number and contact details, or a request for additional information.
7. If additional information is requested, respond immediately with what's needed.
[L0133, undated]

- Before sending ID to a supplier for a credit card verification, confirm the company is legitimate by finding its products on other retailers' websites. A real brand's products normally appear in several places online. [L0468, 2020]
