## Shipping Setup, Policies and Delivery Expectations

### Who Ships the Order: Supplier Account First

- The preferred arrangement is for the supplier to ship on its own account, so you receive one invoice, and the supplier handles returns, loss and breakage [L0357, 2019].
- Ask suppliers to do this first, before you arrange any other shipping option [L0357, 2019].
- Always have suppliers ship on their own accounts when possible. They negotiate better rates through volume and can handle tracking issues. Provide your UPS or FedEx account only if the supplier explicitly cannot ship from its own [L0270, 2021].
- Ship through the supplier's own shipping account whenever possible, because suppliers ship high volumes and often receive bulk discounts that lower the cost (2025) [L0966, 2025].
- When possible, have the supplier ship from its own carrier account, because suppliers ship more volume and get better discounts. If a supplier will not ship on its account, the store must send bills of lading for freight shipments (2025) [L0667, 2025].
- Use the supplier's shipping account for shipping. Do not plan for freight carriers unless the supplier refuses to ship from its account [L1069, 2019].
- Ask suppliers to ship on their own shipping account whenever possible, because it reduces liability and simplifies operations. If a supplier refuses, using your own freight account through a freight broker is still a workable option [L1069, 2019].
- Prefer suppliers who ship on their own account, since they bill shipping on the invoice and handle damage claims and replacements. Take this option whenever it is available [L0466, 2018].
- Prefer suppliers who ship from their own accounts because it keeps operations simpler. Bill of lading shipments are still acceptable when a supplier requires them [L0469, 2021].
- Set shipments to go out on the supplier's account by default. If the supplier will not, get a freight quote and bill of lading, and have the shipment billed to the supplier [L0834, 2021].
- Ship orders on the supplier's shipping account whenever possible. The supplier then handles damage insurance claims and can send a replacement right away [L0663, 2019].
- Ship on the supplier's carrier account whenever possible, because suppliers ship high volume and usually get discounted rates. Use your own UPS, FedEx or freight accounts only when the supplier refuses [L0665, 2022].
- If a supplier ships on its own account, use that setup because it means less work. If it will not, ship on your own account, which is also common [L0803, 2022].
- Ship on the supplier's own account, with the supplier billing the store, whenever possible. Use this approach to keep operations simple [L1088, 2020].
- Ship on the supplier's own account when it offers published or discounted UPS rates, because that makes dropshipping easier and often cheaper. Ask the supplier whether it has a discounted rate sheet for its carrier account [L1090, 2021].
- Prefer suppliers with short shipping times, and ship on the supplier's own account whenever possible, since this is smoother for every order. Your shipping page can list all carriers you may use, and orders from different carriers are not a problem [L0968, 2021].
- Ship on the supplier's account by default and count the shipping fee as a cost per order. Offering free shipping to customers means the store pays this fee to the supplier, so it is not actually free for the business. In the example it was 100 USD per order [L0759, 2020-26].
- Suppliers either ship on their own account and charge you for shipping, or they ship on your account. Your preferred option is for the supplier to ship on its account and bill you for shipping. If it does not allow that, you ship on your own account [L0933, 2020-26].
- Shipping on the supplier's account makes it faster for the supplier to print labels and ship the same day. It also makes claims simpler if an item is damaged or lost in transit, because the supplier can file the claim and send a replacement quickly [L0933, 2020-26].
- Template for asking a supplier to ship on its account (no year):

```
We would prefer to ship on your account and simply pay you for shipping. If that is not something you do, we can arrange shipping on our account, and we will give you our UPS or FedEx account number when we process orders.
```
[L0933, 2020-26]

- Suppliers that refuse to ship on your account are not a deal breaker, but work out who pays the carrier charge before accepting the order arrangement [L0969, 2022].
- Ask suppliers which carriers their largest retailers use. Shipping on a supplier's business account usually gives bulk pricing [L0468, 2020].
- Suppliers should ship directly to customers, not drop-ship on the store's behalf for their own products. For a US store, the supplier should be in the US. A foreign manufacturer is acceptable if its products are warehoused in the US (2023) [L0964, 2023].
- Ship on the supplier's own account, and do not send products to your own address first and then to the customer. Ask the supplier to ship directly to the customer's address [L1111, 2020].
- Some suppliers, roughly 5 to 10 percent, require the seller to ship on their own account. In that case, ask for a UPS or FedEx account number to use for labels, or for a freight carrier the supplier prefers for freight orders [L1088, 2020].
- Most suppliers do not pay shipping. When the store offers free shipping, the store pays that cost itself [L1088, 2020].

#### Comparing the two account types

| Account used | Who bills the shipping | Invoice | Damage and replacement claims | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supplier's account | Supplier bills you for shipping, usually as one invoice with the product | One invoice (product plus shipping) | Supplier files the claim and sends a replacement quickly | [L0357, 2019; L0969, 2022; L0663, 2019; L0933, 2020-26] |
| Your own account | Supplier charges only the product; the carrier bills the shipping separately | Two bills: supplier for product, carrier for shipping | You are liable for overage claims, and in-transit damage must be claimed from the carrier | [L0969, 2022; L0971, 2024] |

- Shipping on the supplier's account keeps damage and replacement claims with the supplier, and large freight is not shipped via UPS or FedEx. Insure shipments yourself only if damage is rare with that supplier (2024) [L0971, 2024].
- Seller accounts with UPS or FedEx are free to open [L0969, 2022].
- Shipping on your own UPS or FedEx account makes you liable for overage claims, for example when declared measurements are wrong. In-transit damage must be claimed from the carrier rather than replaced free by the supplier (2024) [L0971, 2024].

### Using Your Own Carrier Account

#### Opening the account

- Do not open a shipping carrier account before contacting suppliers. Ask which carrier the supplier recommends, because suppliers know which carriers already collect at their warehouse at the best rates [L1090, 2021].
- For standard UPS or FedEx shipments, create your own account with each carrier and give the supplier your account numbers, so it ships on your account rather than its own [L0272, 2022].
- Create a free UPS or FedEx account online and share your account number with suppliers. They will use their own access to create shipping labels and charge the cost to your account. This requires almost no effort from you once set up [L0249, 2022].

**Setup for small parcels when a supplier will not ship on its own account (UPS or FedEx) [L0854, 2022]:**
1. Open a free account with ups.com or fedex.com.
2. Send your carrier account number to the supplier.
3. The supplier creates the shipping labels on your account for each order.

**Shipping options when a supplier does not ship on its own account [L0357, 2019]:**
1. Give the supplier a FedEx or UPS account number so it ships on your account (both can be opened free online).
2. If that is not possible, ask the supplier which freight carrier or freight broker it recommends.
3. Partner with the recommended carrier or broker for those shipments.
4. For each customer order, place the order with the supplier after the sale, then use the chosen shipping method.

**When a supplier will not ship on its own account [L0357, 2019]:**
1. Ask the supplier which shipping company or freight broker it recommends, since it already works with other retailers and knows which carrier handles most of its orders.
2. Contact the recommended carrier or broker.
3. Set up the account or partnership with that company for your shipments.

**Choosing a method with a supplier that does not ship on its account (2023) [L0251, 2023]:**
1. Contact the supplier and ask about its preferred shipping method.
2. If items are large and heavy, use a freight broker via freightcenter.com. The supplier may provide a freight account, or you create your own.
3. If items are standard parcel size, ask the supplier for its UPS or FedEx account number, or create accounts under your business name.
4. Communicate the chosen method clearly so both parties execute correctly.

- Ask suppliers who they recommend as a shipping company rather than asking whether they ship on their own account. Suppliers that do not ship themselves still know which carriers their other retailers use [L0357, 2019].
- Ask which carriers the supplier recommends and which large retailers it uses. Choose carriers from that list [L0247, 2021].
- Ask suppliers which carrier their largest retailers use, and open a business account in your own name [L0468, 2020].

**Opening a business account for a better rate [L0468, 2020]:**
1. Ask the supplier which carrier its top retailers use for shipping.
2. Contact that carrier and open a business account in your own name.
3. Tell the carrier where you ship from and that current pricing is not competitive.
4. Ask for a discount on the account.
5. Expect discounts to grow with shipping volume.

- Template for asking a carrier for a discount [L0468, 2020]:

```
Hi [carrier contact name],

This is [your name] with [business name]. We have opened a business account with [carrier name] and we ship our products from [ship-from city, state].

The current shipping pricing is not competitive for us. Is there any discount you can apply to our account? We expect our shipping volume to grow, so we would also like to know how discounts increase as we ship more.

Thank you,
[your name]
[business name]
[phone number]
```
[L0468, 2020]

- Shipping discounts from carriers are usually based on volume. Higher volumes through a carrier, or shipping through a supplier who ships a lot on its own account, generally produce the best rates (2023) [L0835, 2023].
- Shipping services that charge a fee on top of carrier rates are only useful for stores with their own warehouse and high shipping volume. For drop-ship stores they are generally not worth the cost [L0468, 2020].
- Before accepting a third-party group shipping discount, compare its rates against your actual shipping lanes. Some lanes may get better rates while others get worse. Do not assume a group deal is a good deal for everyone [L0942, 2022].

### Choosing Carriers and Comparing Prices

- For items that are not large, heavy or freight, use the carrier the supplier prefers, usually UPS or FedEx. Large, heavy items need a different carrier (2025) [L0857, 2025].
- Carrier choice for small parcels in Australia: Couriers Please, Sendle and eParcel were recommended. Compare their rates and delivery times before choosing one for a store [L1087, 2020].
- In Australia, many brands rely on Australia Post because it is government-mandated to deliver nationwide. A hybrid setup with StarTrack, which Australia Post owns, can offer a branded tracking page on top of the carrier [L0955, 2025-26].
- Pirate Ship is mentioned as a good option for small parcels. The lesson presenter recommended it from experience but had not used it personally (2025) [L0667, 2025].
- Pirate Ship is reported to be cheaper than UPS for small parcels, with insurance provided through a third party. Compare its rates against your own UPS quote before switching (2023) [L0835, 2023].
- Check shipping costs with a supplier's shipping calculator before committing, since international UPS shipping is typically very expensive. If the cost looks unreasonable to you, customers will likely see it the same way [L0968, 2021].
- Sales reps at supplier brands are the best source for shipping advice. Ask which carrier they prefer, what pricing they get, and how their largest online retailers ship [L0967, 2020].
- Before you use the same carrier as a supplier's other retailers, check its label status. If a supplier has a label pending status with FedEx for more than about 24 hours, call the carrier to check whether the delay is on the supplier or the carrier side [L0967, 2020].
- If a supplier marks up shipping above its real cost, ship on your own account and do not pay the markup [L0359, 2020].
- If a supplier offers a better price when you handle shipping yourself, ask whether it means you buy the stock in advance or pay for shipping. If you ship with your own UPS or FedEx account, or through a freight broker's bill of lading, this can save money [L0551, 2020].
- Ask the supplier whether it can use the same shipping method you select. If the price still does not match, shipping on your own account is the next step (2023) [L0835, 2023].

**Matching the carrier price with your own UPS account (2023) [L0835, 2023]:**
1. Ask the supplier to ship with the same shipping method you select, so the price matches your quote.
2. If it cannot match it, open a free UPS account for your business.
3. Give the supplier your UPS account number.
4. The supplier creates each shipment in UPS and enters your account number in the billing section.
5. UPS bills you for the shipping, and you pay the supplier only for the cost of the goods.

- Template for querying a supplier's higher shipping price (2023) [L0835, 2023]:

```
Hi [Supplier contact name], we were reviewing the quotes for our shipments and noticed yours are substantially higher than the ones we get from UPS. Just wanted to make sure you are choosing the same shipping method, or are we missing something here? Could you check whether you can use the shipping method we select so the price matches? If not, we would like to ship on our own UPS account, so please use our account number [UPS account number] when you create shipments.
[Your name]
[Company name]
```
[L0835, 2023]

- Supplier surcharges for shipping on their own account (for example a 5 dollar surcharge on regular items and 25 dollars on large items) are a reason to compare with self-arranged carrier quotes before accepting [L0969, 2022].
- Shipping on the supplier's account versus on the seller's account: when the supplier ships on its own account, it charges the product plus shipping in one invoice. When the seller ships on its own UPS or FedEx account, the supplier charges only the product, and the carrier bills the shipping separately [L0969, 2022].

**Negotiating shipping costs with a supplier [L0852, 2020]:**
1. Bring real numbers to the supplier, including the number of sales, where orders shipped, shipping costs and the margin you are left with.
2. Ask whether the supplier can ship through its preferred carriers on its own account, which often gives better rates.
3. Ask for incentivized pricing or a wholesale discount so the product becomes profitable for both sides.
4. If the supplier ships through freight, get separate freight quotes from freight brokers and compare them with the supplier's rate.

- Template for asking a supplier to cover shipping on its account and cut costs [L1046, 2021-22]:

```
Hi [Supplier name], last year we spent about [98,000] with you on shipping and fulfillment. Your shipping volume is likely higher than ours, so could you check whether your freight accounts or carrier rates can cover our orders at a lower cost? Our target for next year is about [75,000], and I would like to agree on a plan to reach it. Could we set up a call this week to review the rates? Thank you, [Your name].
```
[L1046, 2021-22]

- Some suppliers do not coordinate shipping, or their shipping is too expensive, so the store may use a separate freight provider such as UPS. In that case product cost and shipping cost must be tracked separately, and both need to be compared against the supplier's pricing [L1046, 2021-22].
- Record each supplier's shipping and fulfilment details so an assistant can arrange pickups, including supplier pickup times, warehouse hours, the contact person, and which carriers collect from the supplier's area [L1000, 2017].

### Insurance, Damage and Claims

- Insure every shipment. Insurance is the baseline protection, regardless of which carrier is used, and it stops occasional damage from costing the store money [L0853, 2021].
- Insure every shipment, whether the supplier ships it or you ship from your own shipping account, so damaged or lost goods can be replaced without an out-of-pocket loss. Insurance is selected as an option when the shipment is created (2025) [L0966, 2025].
- Shipments sent to customers should be insured against loss and transit damage. Suppliers usually insure shipments made on their own accounts [L1088, 2020].
- Insure shipments yourself only if damage is rare with that supplier. If the supplier ships on its own account, it handles claims (2024) [L0971, 2024].
- Before paying extra for specialized shipping on fragile goods, check each supplier's products for complaints [L0853, 2021]:
  1. Search Google for the supplier's name and product names together with "reviews".
  2. Look for repeated mentions of returns, refunds or damage from other stores' customers.
  3. If no clear pattern appears, ship with a recommended carrier and insure every shipment.
  4. Only use costlier specialized shippers if damage remains a recurring problem.
- Check the carrier's insurance terms before relying on insurance. Some policies exclude parcels shipped in manufacturer boxes printed with product names or SKUs, which can lead to denied damage claims [L0247, 2021].
- Insurance is covered by the supplier when it ships on its account, so the store can focus on tracking the claim and the replacement [L0663, 2019].

### Tracking and Multi-Supplier Shipments

- Require suppliers to send a tracking number when an order ships. A supplier that offers no tracking at all is a risk, so ask it to at least reply to purchase orders with tracking numbers [L0247, 2021].
- In-stock orders should have tracking information sent within 24 hours of the order leaving the warehouse. If the customer has none after four business days, they should follow up by email [L0556, 2021-22].
- Customers accept multiple packages if they receive tracking numbers. When customers order products that ship from multiple suppliers, send tracking information for each shipment as it ships separately (2024) [L0276, 2024].
- Handling a multi-item order shipped from different suppliers [L0852, 2020]:
  1. Expect the items to ship separately from different warehouses, and tell the customer this is normal.
  2. When part of an order ships, enter the tracking number in the Shopify order backend for only the products that shipped.
  3. Keep the customer updated so each separate shipment is expected and understood.
- When a store's products come from several suppliers, supplier-specific shipping costs can make some products unprofitable. Check shipping cost and the supplier's location before deciding where to advertise [L0967, 2020].
- Free tracked shipping instead of standard shipping can increase trust and reduce checkout drop-off [L0640, 2023].
- Weekly shipments for bulk ordering [L0324, 2025+]:
  1. Collect all orders placed Monday to Friday.
  2. On Friday, batch-ship all week's orders to DHL in one consolidated shipment rather than individual drops.

- Regional assignment of suppliers reduces shipping times. Instead of one centralized warehouse, assign suppliers by region: Northeast suppliers handle Northeast orders, Southeast suppliers handle Southeast orders. This speeds delivery without increasing costs (2025) [L0278, 2025].
