## Finding Suppliers: Competitor Research and Brand Lists

### Set up the working sheets

Work inside the Niche Research Worksheet, in the tab "Niche Master Supplier List." [L0056, undated]

To create the niche's own list, follow these steps:

1. Right-click the "Niche Master Supplier List" tab.
2. Select Duplicate.
3. Rename the tab with your niche name, for example "Outdoor Kitchens, Master Supplier List."
4. Position the new tab next to your Future Competitors sheet. [L0056, undated]

A second build procedure gives the same sequence in more detail:

1. Open the niche's master supplier list template, a sheet named "niche master supplier list."
2. Right-click it, choose Duplicate, then right-click the copy and choose Rename.
3. Name the copy after the niche, for example "Chandelier Master Supplier List."
4. Drag each copy next to the matching future competitor list so the sheets stay in order.
5. Work only on column A (supplier names) in this step.
6. Open the first future competitor website in a split-screen view next to the spreadsheet.
7. Find the brand list: search the niche category, then open the filters and select Brand. On some sites the brand list is in the footer under a "shop all brands" link.
8. Before copying a brand, open its brand page and confirm it sells products in your niche.
9. Type each qualifying brand name into column A, skipping brands already on the list.
10. Repeat for every future competitor website, then repeat the full process for every other niche that is still a winner. [L0930, 2020-26]

Both build procedures are kept in full. The fuller one is in the section on collecting brands below. [L0930, 2020-26; L0931, 2020-26]

Which columns to fill depends on the stage:

- **Name collection stage:** Only column A (supplier names) is filled. URL, phone, email, date of contact, contact person and notes stay empty until the supplier-contact stage. [L0930, 2020-26]
- **Full build:** Columns A to D are completed in one pass: Supplier Name, Supplier Website, Supplier Phone Number, and Supplier Email or Contact Form. Columns E, F and G are covered in later modules. [L0056, undated; L0931, 2020-26]
- **Other template:** A Master Supplier List spreadsheet can use four required columns: Supplier Name, Supplier URL, Supplier #, and Supplier Email. [L0116, undated]

A Future Competitors sheet is created in the Niche Research Worksheet. It has these columns: [L0062, undated]

- Supplier Name
- Supplier URL
- Supplier Phone Number
- Supplier Email
- Date of Contact
- Contact Person
- Notes

To fill it:

1. Open your Niche Research Worksheet.
2. Navigate to the [Niche] Future Competitors sheet.
3. Examine all products your future competitors sell.
4. Identify which suppliers provide each product.
5. Enter each supplier with all available information in the tracking columns.
6. Ensure you record every supplier you discover from your competitors. [L0062, undated]

A one source tracker uses different columns. Its name-stage fields are Supplier Name, Product Type, Email, Phone Number, Website and Online Application. Phone Call + Date?, Call Comments and Paused come later, in the outreach phase. [L0932, 2021-22]

Before outreach begins, keep the list ready. For each supplier, gather the website URL, phone number and email address, and put all of it in one document or spreadsheet. Aim for 20 or more potential suppliers in the niche. [L0278, 2025]

### Collect brands from future competitor websites

Before you start, keep in mind that the approach of this section is to take brands from competitors, not from supplier directories. Build from online-only competitors and from competitors known to dropship. [L0930, 2020-26; L0459, 2020-26]

Use this procedure for a single competitor:

1. Open the category page for the niche, for example CNC machines or outdoor kitchens.
2. Find the brand list in the sidebar filter.
3. Click show more to expand the full list.
4. Count every brand. This total is the estimated supplier number that competitor contributes. [L0459, 2020-26]

Use the building procedure to add brands to the list:

1. Duplicate the master supplier list template, rename the copy "<niche> master supplier list," and place it next to the niche's future competitor sheet.
2. Open the research sheet on one side of the screen and the competitor's website on the other (split screen).
3. On the competitor's site, open the exact niche category, not a parent or neighbouring category.
4. Scroll to the brand section, click "show more," and copy every brand name into column A.
5. Repeat for each future competitor, adding new brands to the bottom of column A.
6. Sort column A from A to Z and delete repeated brand rows.
7. If fewer than 20 brands remain, go back, find more future competitors, and extract their brands.
8. Google each brand name; add a niche keyword (for example "kitchen") if the results are unclear.
9. If the top result is the competitor itself, the brand is a private label: delete its row.
10. Copy the supplier website URL, remove any trailing code after it, and paste it into column B.
11. Find the phone number, reformat it to hyphenated US style, and paste it into column C.
12. Find the email address on the supplier's contact page and paste it into column D. If there is none, paste the contact form link instead.
13. If a supplier's site does not load, leave its fields blank and continue with the next brand.
14. Write any add-on or accessory products in the notes column.
15. Validate the niche: at least one competitor needs a traffic rank under 100,000, and at least 20 brands need contact information. [L0931, 2020-26]

Brand lookup on competitor sites:

- **Where to look:** category page, then Filters (or More filters), then Brand. [L0930, 2020-26]
- **Where there is no brand filter:** check the footer for a "Shop all brands" or brand page link and open that list instead. [L0930, 2020-26]
- **Brand section:** the brands sit in the 'brand' section of the category page, which appears after clicking show more. The competitor's category item count (for example 407 items) shows how big the section is. Zooming out in the browser makes the list easier to read while copying. [L0931, 2020-26]
- **Where to look for headings:** brand lists may sit under headings such as "Brands," "Brands we stock" or "Our Supplier Brands," or in the main navigation, footer or a small finder widget. If none exists, brand names can still appear in the sidebar or in product category links, so keep scanning before deciding a site has no brand list. [L1484, 2021-22]
- **Menu first:** if the site has a Brands item in the main menu, use it. If not, open the main product category page and scroll down for a "best brands" section, a brand list in the sidebar, or a brand filter. [L0937, 2021-22; L0932, 2021-22]
- **Other categories:** browse the other product categories the site sells (for example dehumidifiers next to air purifiers), add brands not yet listed, and record each supplier's primary product type. A brand covering two product types can stay in one row with both types listed. [L0937, 2021-22; L0932, 2021-22]
- **Site search:** use a competitor's own site search with the niche keyword, such as CNC or outdoor kitchens, to see the full range. Brands that the category menus do not show can be found this way and added to the supplier count. [L0459, 2020-26]
- **Best sellers first:** use the brands that top the Best Sellers or Recommended sorting on future competitor category pages first, because stores that know what they are doing place their best sellers at the top. [L1113, 2022]
- **Default sort:** to find the most popular brands on a large marketplace, open the category and read the brand names in the default sort, which is bestsellers first. [L0803, 2022]

Brand selection rules:

- Add a supplier only if it actually makes products in your target niche. A brand that sells mainly furniture or decor should be skipped even when it appears on a competitor's brand filter, because a competitor selling a mixed catalogue does not mean the brand makes the niche product. [L0930, 2020-26]
- Use the exact niche category on a competitor's site, because competitors often sell in several niches, and brands from other niches would be wrong suppliers. Clicking back to the parent category by mistake leads to products outside the target niche. Always confirm the category name before copying brands. [L0931, 2020-26]
- Before copying a brand, open its brand page and confirm it sells products in your niche. [L0930, 2020-26]
- Each brand a reseller lists is usually a manufacturer or company that makes its own product, so every brand you count is a possible direct supplier to contact later. The brand still counts even if the reseller does not carry its full range. [L1484, 2021-22]
- Competitor brand filters are a practical way to discover suppliers, because competitors have already vetted which brands they sell. Verifying the category on the brand's own page before adding it keeps the list relevant. [L0930, 2020-26]
- Retailers and e-commerce sites that sell several brands are the source of supplier names, not suppliers themselves. A retailer is a competitor and is not added as a supplier. An own-brand site that sells only its own products can be added to the list but is not the primary target. [L0932, 2021-22; L0937, 2021-22]
- Do not enter a retailer's own website as a supplier, and do not confuse the retailer's product list with a brand list. A combined listing, such as two brand names in one entry, should be split by checking each brand separately. [L0932, 2021-22]
- Add each brand name as its own row, noting its primary product type. Record names only at this stage. [L0932, 2021-22; L0937, 2021-22]
- Older example: on an allergy-focused retailer's air purifier category, the category page had no brand menu. Brands were found by scrolling below the collection links to a "best brands" section and a brand list in the sidebar filter. That single site yielded about 16 to 17 manufacturer brands, which were added one per row: Airfree, Aerus, Airmega, Airpura, Alen, Austin Air, Blueair, Coway, Germ Guardian, LiteAir, PowerPure, QuietPure, Winix, and others. Checking the same retailer's dehumidifier category added further brands that were not on the air purifier list, so both categories were recorded. [L0937, 2021-22; L0932, 2021-22]

Use the following procedure for a retailer-based build:

1. Build a tracking spreadsheet (Google Sheets: make your own copy of the template; Excel version also available) with columns Supplier Name, Product Type, Email, Phone Number, Website, Online Application? and, later, Phone Call + Date?, Call Comments, Paused. For this phase only fill the first six: Supplier Name, Product Type, Email, Phone Number, Website, Online Application.
2. Search Google for your main product type (for example "air purifiers") and open retailer or e-commerce sites that sell many brands in your market, not only their own label.
3. On each retailer site, look for a brands filter or brand list in the main menu or sidebar. If none exists, open the category pages and scroll for a "best brands" section or a brand list in the sidebar navigation. Record each brand name as its own row in the Supplier Name column, noting its primary product type.
4. Check several retailer sites (at least 4 to 6) and across other product categories in the niche (for example humidifiers, dehumidifiers), since the same brand often appears on many sites and new brands appear in other categories.
5. Aim for at least 20 suppliers. Do not spend weeks on this step.
6. For each brand, search its name on Google (do not guess the URL) and open its official website.
7. Record the brand website URL, a public email address if available, and the phone number (often in the footer).
8. Look for a "contact us" page and footer links. If no email is public, record "Website Contact Form" in the Email field to show no email is known yet.
9. Look for dealer or retailer links such as "Become a dealer," "Become an [brand] dealer," or "Become a retailer" (often in the header or footer). If found, record the online application page URL in the Online Application column and follow that page's instructed contact method.
10. Do not submit applications yet. Only record the page. Contacting suppliers and seeking approval come in a later step, after the website is built. [L0932, 2021-22]

Older example (2021-22): a second site can add brands missing from the first. On an Australian 4x4 accessories reseller's brands page, the visible names included Eezi-Awn, Direction Plus, Thunder Auto, Harrop, GME, CTEK, Hema, Roadsafe, Tough Dog, Warn and Ultra-Vision, so that single site already showed at least 20 brands. Brand names such as Clearview, Boss and Bushranger also appeared in another reseller's product category links. [L1484, 2021-22]

Stop at one site only when you have a reason to. Do not stop at one competitor site when checking supplier counts, because a single site may show a list that is incomplete or only partly relevant. [L1484, 2021-22]

Start the brand count with the competitor e-commerce sites you already checked for average price and shipping, instead of starting a new search from scratch. Reuse those sites first, then add further stores found through Google results that sell products matching your market idea. [L1484, 2021-22]

Keep the online-only, multi-brand competitor websites open, because they will be reused later to find more brands and to track future competitors. Do not close a competitor site until the niche has been fully evaluated. [L0459, 2020-26]

### Clean and de-duplicate the list

Sort the list and remove repeats as follows:

1. Right-click Column A.
2. Sort A to Z.
3. Identify duplicate brand entries.
4. Delete duplicate rows.
5. Keep one entry per unique brand. [L0056, undated]

Other rules for duplicates:

- Duplicate brand names across competitor websites are expected and normal. Do not delete duplicates until you have finished collecting data from all competitor websites. [L0056, undated]
- Brands sold by several competitors appear more than once, so duplicates are expected and must be removed before counting. Sorting column A alphabetically makes repeated names easy to spot. Counting only unique brands keeps the number of real suppliers accurate. [L0931, 2020-26]
- Do not add a supplier twice. Brands that appear on several competitor websites should be entered once, so before adding a name, check whether it is already in the list. Overlap between competitors is normal. In one example, three or four brands from the first competitor also appeared on the second. [L0930, 2020-26]
- Cross-check across four to six competitor sites. Many brands appear on several sites, so add each brand only once. [L0937, 2021-22]

### Verify each brand before it stays on the list

- **Private label check:** if a brand's top Google result is the competitor's own site, it is that competitor's private label and cannot be sourced directly, so its row is deleted. Marketplaces such as Wayfair private-label their own brands while still dropshipping them. Private label brands are excluded from the supplier list. [L0931, 2020-26]
- **Rare case:** if a brand's own website cannot be found, the product may be a private label owned by a retailer such as Wayfair. This is rare and should not be the default assumption. [L0967, 2020]
- **Verify the company:** when you search for a supplier's website, make sure you reach the brand's own company site, not a retailer page that only contains the brand name in its URL. A master brand may sell under several sub-brands in the same niche, so verify the company before outreach and avoid contacting a competitor that already sells for that brand. [L0834, 2021]
- **Parent companies:** a brand that appears only on one large retailer may be a white-label product made for that retailer. If the same brand shows up on other sites, it likely belongs to a parent company with sister or daughter brands, and approval with the parent can open those brands too. [L0803, 2022]
- **Search each brand:** search each brand name on Google and check its website to qualify it before you reach out. [L0942, 2022]
- **Judging brand quality:** when no quality signal is given, use this procedure:
  1. List the brands on the first page of competitor listings.
  2. If price is used as a quality proxy, sort by price.
  3. Check that at least about 20 brands exist at the chosen price level.
  4. Read the reviews for each brand.
  5. Search each brand name together with problem-related words to see whether a recurring complaint shows up. [L0969, 2022]
- **Brand with an unlabelled product:** to identify the real brand behind a product with no brand name, use this procedure:
  1. Search the product name and any SKU or identifier on Google.
  2. In Google Chrome, right-click the product image and choose Search image with Google Lens.
  3. Check the other sites that list the product for a brand name or identifier. [L0803, 2022]
- **Nothing published stays current:** no published list of drop-ship suppliers stays accurate for long, because brands and companies appear and close constantly. The most current supplier list for a niche comes from researching that niche yourself with the same method each time. [L0801, 2021]
- **Directories do not replace research:** supplier directories and lists are not a substitute for researching brands yourself. Direct research uncovers more current and higher-quality suppliers than any directory. [L1279, 2021]

### Identify the brand behind a competitor's product

Reverse image search is a common way to find a supplier behind a competitor's product. Use the following procedures.

Procedure 1:

1. Use Google Chrome.
2. Right-click the product image on the competitor site.
3. Select Search Google for image.
4. Review the sites that show the same image. If only the competitor appears, the product may be exclusive to them.
5. Open other retailers that list the same product and check whether they show the brand name. [L0967, 2020]

Procedure 2, for finding the product name and brand:

1. Find product name and description from the competitor site.
2. Copy the product name into Google search to find which other retailers stock it.
3. Check which retailers display the brand name on the product page.
4. Right-click on the competitor product image, select "Search Google for image," and check visually similar results for brand names and other retailers selling the same product. [L0268, 2020]

Setting: in Google Chrome, right-click the product image on a competitor's product page and select Search Google for image. This returns every web page where the image appears, which can reveal the supplier's website. [L0801, 2021]

Image search on AliExpress: use the AliExpress image search to find the exact product, or the closest match seen on a competitor's store, and check whether it is listed there. This is a verification step, not a sourcing recommendation. For the status of AliExpress as a supplier source, see the channel section below. [L0549, undated]

Products from Wayfair: many Wayfair products are dropshipped, so suppliers can be sourced from Wayfair product listings by identifying the brand behind them. [L0967, 2020]

### Channel fit: online-only versus brick-and-mortar

Core principle: suppliers that already sell to online-only retailers are more likely to approve a store without a physical location. Source from online-only competitors first. [L0664, 2020; L0854, 2022; L0969, 2022; L0808, 2026]

- **Online-only competitors:** a competitor that sells online only, with no warehouse, store or showroom, is a source of suppliers that already work with online-only retailers. [L0854, 2022]
- **Future competitors:** an online-only store that sells in your niche is a future competitor, and the brands it sells are suppliers you may want. A manufacturer that sells many other brands online is probably focused on e-commerce, so its products are usually not a fit to list. [L0470, 2023]
- **Brick-and-mortar niches:** when a niche has brick-and-mortar competitors, build the supplier list from brands sold by online-only competitors. Those are your true future competitors. [L0803, 2022]
- **Preferred route:** finding suppliers through the websites of online-only future competitors is preferred, because those competitors already work with online-only retailers. Brands that only work with brick-and-mortar stores are not worth pursuing when the goal is an online-only store. [L0666, 2024]
- **Grandfathering:** suppliers that have online-only competitor stores, or brick-and-mortar stores with long-held brands, may still be worth contacting, as they can be grandfathered into brands. Do not build the initial list of 20 brands from them. [L0666, 2024]
- **Online-only sellers:** suppliers that do not require a physical storefront are the best supplier source. Find online-only competitors and reach out to the brands they carry, rather than sources that own physical stores. [L0969, 2022]
- **Find through online-only stores:** a supplier that also sells in physical stores is acceptable if you first found it through an online-only store. [L0808, 2026]
- **Online supplier that also sells in stores:** still reach out, get the price list and consider approval. Do not remove them from the master list automatically. [L0665, 2022]
- **Showroom competitors:** competitors that take a showroom approach can still be checked for new brands. Get approved and look at their price lists before deciding if they belong in the store. [L1281, 2022]
- **Physical retailers add markup:** buying from physical retailers adds their markup, which leaves small margins. Find online-only retailers in your country that sell the brands, then contact those brands directly, since their distribution already exists. [L0471, 2024]
- **Brands with a dominant chain channel:** to source from a brand dominated by large chains:
  1. Find online-only stores that sell new products in the niche.
  2. Note which brands they carry.
  3. Contact those brands directly to become an authorized retailer, not through a distributor or middleman. [L0857, 2025]
- **Partner pages:** a brand's partner or "our partners" page can name the online retailers that already stock it. This helps you identify the brand's distribution channels. [L0940, 2020-26]
- **Big box stores:** brands sold at big box stores such as Home Depot or Lowe's should still be added and contacted. A brand may list only a small share of its range there (for example 10 of 300 products), leaving the rest open for your channel. [L0940, 2020-26]
- **Big brands:** big brand suppliers generally do not drop ship, so do not contact them. [L1088, 2020]
- **Older conflict on big brands (2020 against 2024):** [L1088, 2020] (2020) says big brand suppliers generally do not drop ship, while [L0940, 2020-26] says big box brands should still be added and contacted, since they can open a share of the range. Both apply at different levels: the first describes the brand as a whole, the second the part of its range that is not in the big box channel. [L1088, 2020; L0940, 2020-26]
- **Mixed cases:** sources that sell both online and in stores are not automatically excluded, as described in the rules above. [L0808, 2026; L0665, 2022]

suppliers whose own distribution is online-only are the highest-yield starting point. Also, from 2020: suppliers that already sell to online-only stores are much more likely to approve you than suppliers that only work with brick-and-mortar retailers. [L0664, 2020]

suppliers that sell only online already work with online retailers. If a supplier refuses new stores or asks for a brick-and-mortar location, set it aside, first get approved by others, and return to it once you have traction. [L0663, 2019]
