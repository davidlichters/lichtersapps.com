## Preparing the Business and Store Before Outreach

### Why suppliers screen before they approve

- Trust is the main factor in supplier approval. Suppliers share their full catalog with authorized retailers and depend on the retailer to represent the brand to customers, so approval is a judgment about whether you are legitimate and will act responsibly. Brands put their reputation in the retailer's hands and need confidence that the retailer will look after customers and is not a scam. [L0108, undated; L0109, undated; L1063, 2020-26]
- The suppliers' worst case is a retailer who takes orders but never fulfils them, or who takes the supplier's full catalog, photos, names and descriptions, collects payments, and never forwards orders or ships products. Customers then see the brand as a scam. Suppliers therefore vet retailers carefully and avoid those who show this risk. [L0109, undated; L1063, 2020-26]
- Suppliers do not refuse retailers in principle, since retailer sales are how they earn revenue. Each supplier you contact needs proof that you are a real business that will treat customers properly. [L1063, 2020-26]
- Good suppliers screen applicants and approve only professional sellers who can explain their business clearly. Presenting the store professionally during the application improves approval chances. [L1069, 2019]
- Brand owners want resellers who protect brand value by presenting products professionally on a functional, credible site, so a finished store is evidence that makes approval more likely. [L1060, 2021-22]
- Suppliers expect an applicant to have already invested time and some money in the business. A visible setup, such as a working site and business emails, signals commitment before any approval. [L1060, 2021-22]
- Established brands can be worth millions, so brand owners screen applicants to avoid scammers and unvetted resellers. A working website, business emails and consistent branding give them evidence that you can be trusted with their products. [L1060, 2021-22]
- Cold outreach from someone with no website, brand or email looks unprofessional and wastes the supplier's time. Contact suppliers for reseller approval only after a real business presence exists. [L1060, 2021-22]
- Removing red flags before you contact suppliers significantly increases approval rates, because it shows you are a professional, trustworthy business. [L0109, undated]
- Suppliers judge store quality and professionalism when deciding on a partnership. A polished, complete store signals a legitimate business. [L0123, undated]
- Suppliers check a dropshipping store for trust and completeness before approving it. [L0767, 2020-26]

### Sequencing: store, supplier list and outreach

- Build and launch the store before contacting suppliers. No legitimate supplier will approve you or accept orders without first reviewing a completed, professional storefront. [L0070, undated]
- Suppliers are more likely to approve a retailer that shows a professional, operational storefront rather than an early-stage idea. A finished demo store means design and technical work is complete before outreach, which shortens approval timelines and reduces back-and-forth. [L0069, undated]
- The store must exist before approaching preferred suppliers, because they usually will not approve a retailer with no store yet. [L0506, 2020-26]
- Build the website first, then apply for supplier approvals, because suppliers approve stores that already exist. [L1224, 2025+]
- Before a supplier discusses approval, it usually asks where you sell, so have a live, high-quality website ready to show. The site lets the supplier picture its own products on your store. [L1422, 2020-26]
- Silver-tier suppliers approve new retailers through their own approval form and review the applicant's live store, so the store must be fully built and online before you apply. [L0711, 2020-26]
- Build the store before contacting suppliers, because suppliers review the site and it must look sufficient for your approval rate. [L0852, 2020]
- A finished website shows suppliers how their products will look and that you are serious. Lesson figures state that approval rates are significantly higher when you have a website to show. [L0458, 2021-22]
- Before the supplier review, the Shopify demo store should be fully set up and look finished, since suppliers review it during the approval conversation. A store that is not ready weakens the approval request. [L0590, 2020-26]
- A professional store and business setup make suppliers respond more positively, so prepare both before the first outreach. [L0590, 2020-26]
- Store approvals are easier when the store looks live, even with a few demo products. [L0853, 2021]

**Timing conflict: store first or minimum essentials first**

The sources give three positions. The store-first position is the one repeated across most units above. Two other positions appear:

- Minimum essentials: do not wait for the demo store to be perfect. Complete the minimum essential elements by end of day, then immediately email the supplier with your store link and request next steps, because timing matters more than completeness. [L0245, 2020]
- Start early (2023): do not wait until everything on the store is perfect. Supplier approval takes time and delays add up, so start outreach as soon as the niche is chosen. [L1094, 2023]
- Split sequence: in a high-ticket dropshipping model, the supplier list is built before the website is finished. The list is parked until the store is built and set up, and approval to sell the products on your site is sought in a later stage. Finish the supplier research now, set the list aside until the website is built, and contact suppliers only after the store is set up. [L0932, 2021-22; L0937, 2021-22]

When each applies: store first is required where the supplier reviews a live store, as with silver-tier and preferred suppliers. [L0711, 2020-26; L0506, 2020-26] Minimum-essentials outreach is the 2020 approach, which depends on the basics being in place before you send the store link. [L0245, 2020] Early outreach from the 2023 source starts from niche selection, so research and list building can begin immediately while the first approval request waits for the store to be presentable. [L1094, 2023]

**Supplier list and target**

- Add each supplier's website, phone number and email to a master supplier list. [L0393, undated]
- Keep all supplier contact details in one Master Supplier List. [L0532, undated]
- The target for the first store is approval from five suppliers. [L0393, undated]

**Sourcing and approval order**

1. Add each supplier's website, phone number and email to a master supplier list. [L0393, undated]
2. Finalize the master supplier list. [L0393, undated]
3. Set up a business email address, business phone number and mailing address. [L0393, undated]
4. Get approved by your first five suppliers. [L0393, undated]

**Legitimacy build order**

1. Register the business as an official business entity. [L1063, 2020-26]
2. Set up official business email addresses. [L1063, 2020-26]
3. Set up a business phone number separate from your personal phone. [L1063, 2020-26]
4. Prepare a supplier contact plan. [L1063, 2020-26]
5. After approval, upload the live products you are authorized to sell. [L1063, 2020-26]

### Business entity and tax identity

- Register the business entity before contacting suppliers if you are committed to the venture. Switching from a sole proprietorship to an LLC or corporation later requires resubmitting all documents to each supplier. [L1283, 2023]
- Before approaching silver-tier suppliers, have a registered business entity, a business email address and a business phone number in place. Suppliers check these during review. [L0486, 2020-26]
- A registered entity, business email, dedicated business phone, and polished demo store signal trustworthiness during silver approval review. [L0110, undated]
- Roughly 90% of suppliers ask for a sales tax certificate, and suppliers expect a business EIN rather than a personal Social Security number. An LLC name, EIN and sales tax certificate together are what is needed to start securing suppliers. [L0695, 2020-26]
- Many suppliers ask for your EIN when you start working with them, so have it ready before you contact suppliers. [L1344, 2020-26]
- Some suppliers require an EIN before approval and others do not. If the EIN is still pending, tell the supplier it is pending and keep reaching out to suppliers that do not require it. [L0967, 2020]
- Suppliers usually ask for the business tax number during account approval, such as the ABN or ACN in Australia, so have it ready before contacting them. [L0702, 2021-22]

### Business address

- Use a dedicated business address instead of your home address on supplier applications, your store website and other business accounts, so private information is not published online. [L0696, 2020-26]
- Publishing a home address on a store website exposes private information to anyone online and should be avoided from the start. [L0696, 2020-26]
- Using a virtual or mailing address for public business information is the approach. The speaker saw no evidence that a home address on a Google account causes suspension. [L1088, 2020]
- Get the business address set up before contacting suppliers, because supplier approval forms ask for a business name and address. [L0696, 2020-26]
- A virtual business address is optional and separates your home address from business documents. A mail service holds the mail and either forwards it on a schedule or waits for pickup. [L0964, 2023]
- A supplier that asks for a business address can be given the virtual address, which is used for bank statements, credit cards and suppliers. [L0964, 2023]
- The virtual address matters only if the supplier requires one. A standard business address is the usual choice. [L0964, 2023]
- If a supplier needs a US mailing address and the seller is based abroad, use a US mailing address such as a UPS Store location in the seller's US shipping area. [L1088, 2020]
- A P.O. Box looks less professional than a business address with a suite number. In the United States, a USPS P.O. Box must be listed as a P.O. Box, which signals a small box inside a post office. A UPS Store address does not require the P.O. Box label. [L0696, 2020-26]
- The source's speaker prefers a suite or unit style address, because the address is printed on the store. A P.O. Box can also serve as a virtual address. [L0964, 2023]
- UPS Store locations are widespread in the United States. A New York City ZIP code search returned more than 28 locations. [L0696, 2020-26]
- The cheapest UPS Store option is a folder in a cabinet behind the counter, where mail is stored for pickup or forwarding. Prices vary by location because UPS Store locations are franchises. [L0696, 2020-26]
- Some UPS Store locations offer a mailbox you open with a key. Others offer the folder-in-cabinet option. Ask the specific location which option it offers before you sign up. [L0696, 2020-26]
- Request the smallest size available and ask for pricing to keep monthly costs low for a business address. [L0249, 2022]
- Post Scan Mail receives your post at an address you choose a plan for. For an extra fee it can open and scan paper mail and email you a copy. Its starter plan costs $15 per month in the United States. [L0696, 2020-26]
- Where possible, have business correspondence delivered electronically by email. Only mail that must arrive on paper needs a physical address or a scanning service. [L0696, 2020-26]
- Keep the exact same business address across the website, Google Merchant Center and Google Ads, and resubmit after any correction. Even a small mismatch can trigger a suspension or delay the account's reinstatement. [L0964, 2023]

**Procedure: set up a UPS Store business address in the United States**

1. Open the UPS Store website. [L0696, 2020-26]
2. Click Find a Store. [L0696, 2020-26]
3. Enter the ZIP code where you want your business address. [L0696, 2020-26]
4. Review the listed locations and click on the stores to check them. [L0696, 2020-26]
5. Visit the store in that area or call it. [L0696, 2020-26]
6. Ask what the mailbox costs, request the smallest size, and ask for the folder-in-cabinet option, which is the cheapest. [L0696, 2020-26]
7. Choose whether you pick up your mail in person or pay the store to forward it to you. [L0696, 2020-26]

### Business email

- Set up a business email address, phone number and mailing address before applying to suppliers, since suppliers are approached as a business. [L0532, undated]
- Contacting suppliers without a business email address, business phone number and mailing address can lead to rejection, because suppliers expect a verifiable business. [L0393, undated]
- The target for the first store is approval from five suppliers, and the business email, phone number and mailing address are needed before contacting them, since they need a professional business identity to approve you. [L0393, undated]
- Contact suppliers from the store's own email address rather than an email tied to the business entity. [L1088, 2020]
- Send supplier approval emails from a business address on your own domain, not a free personal webmail account such as Gmail or Yahoo. Free addresses look unprofessional and can slow supplier approval. [L1196, 2020-26]
- Use the same business address on the website, for customer replies and for supplier communication in the long term. [L1196, 2020-26]
- Use one primary login address, such as sales@yourdomain.com, for the Google Workspace account, and add further addresses as aliases. Aliases such as returns@ and a personal first-name address deliver to the same inbox, so one login manages all of them. Adding an alias does not require a separate paid account. [L1196, 2020-26]
- Use a personal alias with first and last name for supplier approval emails, so the supplier sees a real person from the business. Use the sales alias for general sales contact and the returns alias for return or refund communication. Separating addresses by purpose makes replies easier to sort. [L1196, 2020-26]
- Suppliers see the sender's name and address before approving a business account. A professional address with the first and last name builds more trust than a personal email. Use the firstname@ alias with the full name for supplier contact. [L1197, 2020-26]
- In supplier forms' marketing, purchasing, accounts payable or sales contact fields, a generic support or email address at the store's domain is acceptable when no real contact exists. [L0964, 2023]

### Business phone number

- Create a dedicated business phone number before contacting suppliers. [L0127, undated]
- A store's public phone number should not be a personal line, because supplier callbacks then get mixed up with spam and unknown calls. A separate business number lets you answer supplier and customer calls without guessing who is calling. [L0338, 2020-26]
- OpenPhone and Google Voice are available tools for creating business phone numbers. [L0127, undated]
- Use a US business phone number even if you run the business from outside the US, because suppliers ask for phone numbers and US-based stores usually need one anyway. Many members operate from abroad without problems. (2023.) [L0934, 2023]
- Purchase a local Skype phone number for your target market, for example a US number if you are calling from overseas. Suppliers see your local number rather than an international one, which improves answer rates. [L0235, 2021-22]
- Avoid collecting customer phone numbers before launch unless the supplier asks for them, because pre-launch sign-ups cannot find you before the store goes live. [L0934, 2023]

**Procedure: business phone setup**

1. Decide whether the personal phone number is acceptable for now, or sign up for Quo's Starter plan to get a business number. [L0338, 2020-26]
2. If using Quo, install the app and log in so calls work from the phone. [L0338, 2020-26]
3. Add the business phone number to the header and to the Contact us section of the footer in the Shopify theme. [L0338, 2020-26]
4. Click Save. [L0338, 2020-26]
5. Mark the lesson complete. The app must be ready before supplier calls begin. [L0338, 2020-26]

### Demo store setup

- Use 5 demo products, which is sufficient to show suppliers what the store looks like and how their products would be presented. [L0268, 2020]
- A working homepage, one collection page and product pages are enough to show the supplier what their products will look like. [L0853, 2021]
- A newer store can list a few products from a supplier at a higher markup to build credibility when approaching more brands. Choose the products with the best margins and do not include them in paid ads. Uploading the full catalog is not required. (2025.) [L0837, 2025]
- Do not use Oberlo for demo products. Build them manually following blueprint module 3 guidance. Demo products do not need real specs, materials or dimensions, since generic descriptions are sufficient when the products are not for sale. [L0268, 2020]
- Do not use dropshipping apps to fill the store before supplier approval. Use stock images to build the site for suppliers to review. [L0834, 2021]
- Build the demo store with stock or generic products so the supplier can picture how its products would look on your site. The demo is not meant to mislead them. [L1113, 2022]
- Do not display one supplier's branded products on a demo store to pitch a different supplier. The first brand may find them through Google alerts and state that the store is not authorized to sell its products. [L0623, 2020-26]
- A good-looking live store that already sells other brands makes suppliers more willing to work with you. Show your site when you approach a supplier. [L0832, 2019]
- Well-organized filters and tags on the storefront show future suppliers how the website functions. Additional tag lists can be added over time as more suppliers are onboarded. [L0890, 2020-26]
- The store must show a logo, favicon, consistent colors, clean collections with images, working links and a visible email signup. [L1309, 2020-26]
- A buyer's guide on the store is optional before contacting suppliers. It adds credibility and trust. [L1285, 2024]
- Link the domain to Shopify before suppliers review the store. [L1283, 2023]
- Before you contact suppliers, a few days of study of store-building and traffic basics makes it easier to explain your business and tends to increase approvals. You do not need to implement those lessons yet. [L0854, 2022]

**Password removal and purchase blocking**

- The storefront must be publicly viewable before you contact suppliers, because they check the URL themselves. Removing the password protection makes this possible. [L1294, 2020-26]
- The site must be supplier ready: the domain is linked to Shopify and the storefront password has been removed. Suppliers should see the store only once it is fully ready. [L1283, 2023]
- Do not set a store password when showing demos to suppliers. [L0856, 2024]
- To prevent purchases on a demo store shown to suppliers, disable all payment options, or leave the shipping profile with no selected zones so no one can check out. [L0856, 2024]

### Demo-mode disclosure

- If a supplier sees its products on your store before launch, explain that they are only demonstration placements, so the supplier can see how its products will look, and that you are not selling them yet. This also shows you can build and maintain a website. [L0929, 2021-22]
- Do not present a demo as a live shop. The demo is for showing layout and presentation, not for selling, and it is not meant to mislead the supplier. [L1113, 2022]

### Trust signals and pre-outreach checks

- Before contacting suppliers, the store must look trustworthy: links must work, there must be no typos or errors, and trust signals must be in place. Conversion optimization can wait until after the supplier approves you. (2024.) [L0806, 2024]
- Pre-outreach checks before contacting suppliers:
  1. Make sure the website is not locked behind a password. [L1114, 2023]
  2. Click every link to confirm it works. [L1114, 2023]
  3. Spell-check the whole site, including collection names and the About Us page, for obvious errors. [L1114, 2023]
  4. Confirm the basic structure is complete before sending supplier applications. [L1114, 2023]

### Credibility building

- A social media presence is not make-or-break for supplier approval, but suppliers often check that a company is legitimate by looking for active pages under the company name. [L1112, 2021]
- A newer store can build credibility by listing a few supplier products at a higher markup, as described above, rather than by uploading a full catalog. [L0837, 2025]
- Presenting the store and business setup professionally is part of the approval case, together with the entity, email and phone already in place. [L0110, undated; L1069, 2019]

### Readiness checklist

Before supplier calls, prepare the following:

1. A live, high-quality demo store that suppliers can review. [L1422, 2020-26]
2. A clear plan for generating additional sales for their products. [L1422, 2020-26]
3. An explanation that your focus as a business owner is exceptional customer service. [L1422, 2020-26]

Before the first supplier contact, confirm:

- Registered business entity, business email, business phone and business address are in place. [L0486, 2020-26; L0393, undated; L1063, 2020-26]
- EIN, sales tax certificate and any state certificate that suppliers require are ready to send, and the EIN status is known if it is still pending. [L0695, 2020-26; L0967, 2020; L1115, 2024]
- The store is live without a password, every link works, the text is checked, and the demo products are in place. [L1114, 2023; L1294, 2020-26; L0268, 2020]
- The master supplier list is finalized. [L0393, undated]
- The first outreach aims at approval from five suppliers. [L0393, undated]
