### Wayfair, Overstock and other large online retailers

Large online retailers such as Wayfair and Overstock are not treated as competitors. Their catalogs can be used to find brands to contact. Searching for those brands on Google usually reveals other online stores selling them. [L0468, 2020]

The share of dropship items on these platforms is reported differently by source. The figures are:

| Source | Year | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wayfair drop-ships | 2021 | more than 95% of what it sells | suppliers found there are strong candidates [L0469, 2021] |
| Large drop-shipping retailers such as Wayfair | 2024 | about 90 percent sourced directly from brands | a useful source of supplier leads in niches they already sell in; their pop-up stores hold only a small part of their range [L0806, 2024] |

Use the 2021 figure for Wayfair specifically and the 2024 figure for large drop-ship retailers in general. Both show that Wayfair's brand list is a strong source of leads. [L0469, 2021; L0806, 2024]

Build the supplier list from the following:

- Wayfair suppliers.
- Two to three other popular online retailers that have no physical stores or warehouses and publicly list the brands they sell for. [L0469, 2021]

Wayfair sellers are almost all dropshippers, so treat them as dropshipping competitors. Source supplier brands from Wayfair listings where prices are equal to or lower than Amazon, and do not source suppliers from Amazon. [L0551, 2020]

Amazon and eBay are the wrong places to find dropshipping suppliers. Look for brands and approved suppliers who sell directly. [L0466, 2018]

Walmart: large US retailers such as Walmart also use drop shipping and allow third-party sellers to list products on their platform. The fact that a competitor drop ships does not decide whether it counts as a direct competitor. What matters is that it resells other brands in your niche. [L0896, 2021-22]

### Google Shopping and search results

Google Shopping, local procedure:

1. Build a list of brands and suppliers that sell the products in the chosen market, using Google Shopping listings and searches such as "electric bike manufacturers," "electric bike suppliers" or "electric bike distributors."
2. For each brand, find the website, the phone number, the email address and any online dealer or retailer application form.
3. Record each supplier in a tracker with the product type and contact details.
4. Call each supplier and introduce your business, then ask whether you can sell their products through your website.
5. If the call does not produce a clear result, send an email or submit the online dealer application, and record the date.
6. Log each call with the date, the person spoken to and the outcome.
7. After approval, the supplier shares product data, pricing and images, which you upload to your website so the products go live. [L0771, 2021-22]

Contacting suppliers from the most visible Google Shopping results is a valid starting point. Starting with the least popular suppliers can reduce fear of rejection and make early outreach easier. [L0803, 2022]

### Trade shows and exhibitor lists

Trade shows let you see many products in person in a short time and connect with brands face to face. They can also help get approved by brands that had put you on a waiting list. [L0467, 2019]

Trade-show sources:

- **TSNN.com (Trade Show News Network):** lists trade shows by industry keyword. It is a worldwide trade-show directory and lets you find upcoming shows by country and industry keyword. [L0804, 2023; L0935, 2022+]
- **Trade Show Network:** lists trade shows for almost any industry. Open each show's website, go to its exhibitors list, and extract the brand names, and where available the URL, contact name, phone and email, for a supplier list. [L1068, 2019]
- **Search options:** you can search TSNN.com by industry keyword or by geographic location to find in-person events for your industry or a group of related industries. [L0942, 2022]
- **Exhibitors:** exhibitors are the companies that pay to show their products at trade shows, and they are often looking for new retailers. Their exhibitor lists are therefore a direct source of supplier leads. [L0935, 2022+]
- **Directory of shows:** a trade show directory lists trade shows across industries. Each show's exhibitor list gives companies you can contact as potential suppliers. [L0663, 2019]
- **Source link:** the source for the directory address reads tsn.com in one unit and tsnn.com in others. The name used most often in the course is TSNN.com. [L0666, 2024; L0804, 2023]

Setup procedure, for finding suppliers through trade shows:

1. Open tsnn.com and use the search box.
2. Select the country where you plan to do business.
3. Enter a basic industry keyword, such as furniture.
4. Review the list of upcoming trade shows in that country.
5. Check whether each show is actually taking place, since events can be cancelled or postponed.
6. Open the website of a relevant show and find its exhibitor list.
7. Contact the exhibitors directly to build your list of at least 20 suppliers in the niche. [L0935, 2022+]

Sidebar procedure, when only a few suppliers approve:

1. Open the trade show news network website (tsnn.com).
2. Use the sidebar to enter your niche and find trade shows.
3. Open the exhibitor lists, which show companies exhibiting their products.
4. Contact those companies as potential suppliers. [L0834, 2021]

Trade show attendance in person (procedure at the event):

1. Search TSNN.com by industry keyword or location to find upcoming shows.
2. Attend the show, which is typically free or cheap, and walk the hall, where there are often hundreds of companies you could sell for.
3. Bring business cards, introduce yourself to exhibitors, collect their cards, and tell them you will email them.
4. A few days later, follow up by email and reference the expo where you met. [L0942, 2022]

Meeting a supplier in person makes approval easier than cold emails alone, because the contact is a face and a name and the supplier sees you as more serious. Personal contact also builds trust faster than a mass email. [L0942, 2022]

Trade shows typically take place several times a year, often coast to coast in the USA, so a new supplier search can be timed to upcoming events. [L0942, 2022]

Trade shows take place every year and new brands appear regularly. Repeat exhibitor list research regularly, even for established stores. [L1068, 2019]

Procedure without an upcoming trade show:

1. Open the trade show's own website and go to its Exhibitors section, which lists every company attending.
2. Copy the exhibitor names.
3. Search each name on Google and open their websites.
4. Contact them directly, the same way you do normal supplier research. [L0942, 2022]

Filtering: trade show exhibitor lists can be filtered for companies that offer drop shipping and used as an extra source of supplier leads when your normal supplier search runs short. [L0962, 2021]

Backup source: TSNN.com lists trade shows by niche and country. Open the exhibitors list of relevant shows in your country of business to find additional brands to contact, and use it as a backup source when suppliers are out of stock or not onboarding. [L1279, 2021]

Stock shortages: when suppliers in a niche stop taking new retailers because of stock shortages, the TSNN.com trade show network can reveal suppliers that are still approving retailers and have inventory. Start with those approvals and add the shortage-hit suppliers later, once they restock. [L1112, 2021]

Slow supplier: when a popular supplier has delayed inventory or long lead times, look for similar alternatives that can ship fast. Trade show exhibitor lists from TSNN.com are a source for new brands. [L0803, 2022]

Doubling the product count: TSNN.com is a source for finding suppliers. Also search for your niche with "trade show" and look at exhibitors, then contact those brands. The aim is to double the product count so more products can ship quickly. [L0854, 2022]

Trade shows as a later stage: advanced supplier strategies can require attending trade shows. Treat them as a later-stage option, not a starting step. [L0544, 2026+]

Trade shows as optional: attending trade shows in person is optional. Trade-show directories and exhibitor lists should be used as a supplementary way to find suppliers alongside other sourcing methods. [L0935, 2022+]

Note on the trade show sequence: the course gives three positions on trade shows. [L0544, 2026+] treats them as a later-stage option, [L0935, 2022+] as supplementary, and [L0942, 2022] describes attending as typically free or cheap. Use the later-stage position when the list already has enough suppliers, and use exhibitor lists earlier if few suppliers approve or stock is short. [L0544, 2026+; L0935, 2022+; L0942, 2022; L0663, 2019; L1112, 2021]

if you are very limited, look up trade shows in your niche in a trade show directory and contact exhibitors from the exhibitor lists. Return to the suppliers who declined once you have traction. [L0663, 2019]

exhibitor lists keep giving new brands. Repeat the search with a new trade show keyword in another year. [L1068, 2019]

### Competitor directories, Thomasnet and other external sources

- **Thomasnet:** lists many brands useful for finding extra suppliers. Its listings do not show whether a brand works with dropshipping, so each brand must be contacted to confirm. [L1092, 2022]
- **Supplier directory blogs:** these list suppliers by country and niche. Use them to build a shortlist of non-China suppliers before testing each one. [L1214, 2023+]
- **Online supplier research tools:** these can show which suppliers larger brands in a niche use, mainly in the US. This is a faster route than a blind search for reverse sourcing. [L1225, 2025]
- **AI-generated lists:** supplier lists generated by AI prompts are acceptable only after you check that each brand is drop-ship friendly. Use them to expand a list over time after the main sourcing methods have been used up, not as the starting list (2024). [L0965, 2024]
- **Middlemen platforms:** build supplier lists mainly from websites that already drop ship, and avoid middlemen platforms such as Doba. Middlemen mark up products and leave almost no profit margin (2024). [L0965, 2024]
- **Alibaba and AliExpress:** these are not recommended as supplier sources for these stores. For more brands, search trade show exhibitor lists on TSNN.com for your niche in the country where you do business, then contact the exhibitors directly. [L0429, 2022]
- **Community forum:** to find suppliers in your own country, ask experienced sellers in a community forum for your region which suppliers they have approved and how they got them. [L0663, 2019]
- **Crowdfunding:** search Kickstarter and Indiegogo for projects from five years ago that became legitimate brands. Many crowdfunded projects that succeeded are now offering wholesale to retailers. Never pre-sell products that are currently in crowdfunding development. [L0249, 2022]
- **Competitor brand pages:** competitor brand pages on sites in your niche show which suppliers they sell, which is an alternative source of supplier leads to trade show exhibitor lists. [L0942, 2022]
- **Suppliers from many sources:** suppliers can be sourced from many different types of lists and products. Before accepting a supplier, check the margin and shipping cost, because both determine profitability. [L0967, 2020]

### Local, international and special-case suppliers

Local suppliers are approved using the Google Shopping procedure above. For a local market, use the same steps to find and approve local suppliers. [L0771, 2021-22]

International suppliers found through competitor stores often have stock or fulfilment in the target country. Calling them via Google Voice or Skype, or emailing them, is fine. [L1112, 2021]

Free bonus product: first decide which product to give away, then source suppliers for it the same way as for the main products. Get their wholesale price and use them to fulfil the bonus orders. [L1112, 2021]

Outside the standard criteria, in order:

1. Exhaust standard supplier research first.
2. In a niche with many product variations, check whether the top two or three suppliers make most of the sales, since deeper research often reveals more sellers.
3. Search marketplaces such as Etsy for unique products from sellers with many reviews, which shows they have proven sales.
4. Contact those sellers and ask to become an authorized retailer for their products.
5. Ask for wholesale pricing and confirm the numbers make sense before committing.
6. Look for independent craftspeople who sell through eBay, Facebook Marketplace, or Etsy and can make custom designs, colours, or patterns. [L1125, 2020-26]

In niches with very high product variety, such as wallpaper or horse saddles, the number of patterns, colours and textures is nearly unlimited. Buyers often search for exact names of patterns or materials. Small craftspeople and niche sellers can add many products and long-tail keyword traffic beyond what the top brands offer. [L1125, 2020-26]

Note on the US spelling in the course text: the outside-criteria unit uses "colors" and "patterns" in the same way. The source text is kept in full. [L1125, 2020-26]

### Prioritisation and choosing which brands to contact first

Use this order:

- **Gold, silver and bronze:** classify each brand as gold, silver or bronze based on its reputation and reseller stance. Contact gold and silver brands first and log every response in the notes column. Follow up on each brand according to the reason and timing given. [L0468, 2020]
- **Gold suppliers on the list:** gold suppliers are often already on your research list from the start. Keep these names in your list, because they become realistic partners once you have traffic and sales. [L0486, 2020-26]
- **Best sellers first:** use the brands that top the Best Sellers or Recommended sorting on future competitor category pages first, because stores that know what they are doing place their best sellers at the top. [L1113, 2022]
- **Lesser-known suppliers:** large, well-known suppliers often have thin margins, while lesser-known suppliers with less competition can be far more profitable. Look past the familiar names when choosing suppliers. [L0671, 2021]
- **Newer brands in a competitive niche:** in a competitive niche, break in by approaching newer or up-and-coming brands that the largest competitors are not focusing on. Still apply to the established brands, but do not rely on them to get started. [L0803, 2022]
- **Margin and shipping check:** before accepting a supplier, check the margin and shipping cost, because both determine profitability. [L0967, 2020]
- **Start with approvals:** start sales and traffic with suppliers who approve you. [L0663, 2019]
- **Online-only sources first:** source from companies that sell only online, since they already work with online retailers. [L0663, 2019]
- **Competitor count versus quality:** a site having 20 brands is not proof of a market, unless the brands are actually distinct and sell products in your niche. [L1484, 2021-22]
- **Demand validation:** nearly 3,000 ratings with four stars on Amazon for a comparable product type indicate proven demand and can be used as a validation signal before testing. This is a demand signal, not a supplier source. [L1186, 2022]
- **Completeness versus shortlist:** the master supplier list should contain every supplier in the niche that works on the dropship model, not a selected top group. Stopping at a handful of suppliers limits later sourcing and comparison options. [L0930, 2020-26]
- **Earlier sourcing and the quality check:** the list must hold all qualifying suppliers, while outreach order is set by the classes above. These are two different stages. [L0930, 2020-26; L0468, 2020]

Older note (2020 and 2022): a supplier's large size does not mean good margin, and a list of 20 brands at a chosen price level remains the threshold for the quality check. [L0671, 2021; L0969, 2022]

### Size, validation and source mix

The number of suppliers the course asks for is stated in several places:

| Source | Year | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| one source, supplier-contact step | 2025 | identify 20 or more potential suppliers in your niche [L0278, 2025] |
| one source, master list validation | current | at least 20 brands need contact information [L0931, 2020-26] |
| one source, source mix | 2022 | a list should contain two or three sources; a single source with 20 or more brands plus a second with 10 or more brands is enough [L0665, 2022] |
| one source, retailer build | 2021-22 | at least 20 suppliers, do not spend weeks on this step [L0932, 2021-22] |
| one source, competitor build | 2021-22 | keep going until the list holds at least 20 supplier names [L0937, 2021-22] |
| one source, trade show step | current | at least 20 suppliers in the niche [L0935, 2022+] |

These figures apply at different stages:

- 20 is the threshold for completing the first list.
- The 2022 two-source rule is the sufficiency rule for a list that is thin.
- The 2025 step is the minimum for the supplier-contact stage.

[L0278, 2025; L0931, 2020-26; L0665, 2022; L0932, 2021-22; L0937, 2021-22; L0935, 2022+]

Size depends on the niche. A niche like chandeliers may hold 500 to 1,000 suppliers across all competitors. A smaller niche may hold 10, 20 or even 2,000. The aim is to find every supplier in the niche that works on dropship terms, not a shortlist of the best ones. [L0930, 2020-26]

Example counts from the course:

- **Chandeliers:** the first competitor, a dropshipping store, yielded 32 supplier names. The second added about 20 more after duplicates were removed. [L0930, 2020-26]
- **Outdoor kitchens:** starting point was three future competitors: Wayfair, Zorro and Home Outlet Direct.
  - Wayfair's brand list in the outdoor kitchen category gave 29 brands in about 2 minutes.
  - Zorro's category, with 407 items, raised the total to 46 in about 1 minute.
  - Home Outlet Direct brought it to 54 in about 7 to 8 minutes overall.
  - After sorting and deleting duplicates, 52 brands remained. [L0931, 2020-26]
- **Air purifiers:** about 16 to 17 manufacturer brands from one retailer, as listed in the research section above. [L0937, 2021-22]
- **Australian 4x4:** at least 20 brands from one reseller's brands page. [L1484, 2021-22]

A single site with 20 brands is a starting point. It does not prove a market on its own, so check the brands across several sites. [L1484, 2021-22]

Validation checks for the niche:

- At least one competitor needs a traffic rank under 100,000.
- At least 20 brands need contact information. [L0931, 2020-26]

Do not build the initial list of 20 brands from grandfathered suppliers, as described in the channel section above. [L0666, 2024]

Keep the list large enough before building the store. Make sure the list holds enough brands before the store is built, since many brands may refuse online retailers. [L0460, 2020-26]

Two to three sources: a master supplier list should contain two or three sources; a single source with 20+ brands plus a second with 10+ brands is enough. To grow a thin list, look at non-competing sites that sell similar products and check which brands they carry in the niche's subcategory. [L0665, 2022]

Keep the research list as a source of future options: each source counts as a source for more brands. Track future competitors and add every brand found to the master list, and continue beyond the first few brands, because the list grows as more sellers are checked. [L0459, 2020-26]

### Ongoing tracking and refresh

- **Keep tracking:** after a niche passes the dropship friendly test, keep tracking every future competitor and add every brand found to a master supplier list. Tracking should continue beyond the first few brands, because the list grows as more sellers are checked. [L0459, 2020-26]
- **Revisit the competitor list:** revisit the list of future competitors and check their current brand lists for new brands to contact. [L0666, 2024]
- **Repeat regularly:** trade shows take place every year and new brands appear regularly. Repeat exhibitor list research regularly, even for established stores. [L1068, 2019]
- **Subscribe to newsletters:** subscribe to the email lists of competitors, suppliers and industry trade shows to see which publications and newsletters curate content. Watch what they cover so you know which outlets are relevant for your content. This serves content research, not supplier sourcing. [L0899, 2020-26]
- **Competitor pages stay open:** do not close a competitor site until the niche has been fully evaluated. [L0459, 2020-26]

### Handover to outreach

Order of steps before outreach (2025):

1. Add websites, phone numbers and emails of potential suppliers to the Master Supplier List.
2. Finalize the Master Supplier List.
3. Set up a business email address, phone number and mailing address.
4. Get approved with the first 5 suppliers. [L0532, undated]

Build the master supplier list with brand names and contact details before the store is delivered, so suppliers can be contacted as soon as the store is ready (2025). [L1116, 2025]

Research stage only: at the research stage you only identify suppliers and record their details. Asking suppliers for approval to sell through your store comes later, so do not submit applications yet. [L0937, 2021-22]

Do not submit applications yet. Only record the online application page. Contacting suppliers and seeking approval come in a later step, after the website is built. [L0932, 2021-22]

Outreach procedure, including the approval email and follow-up:

1. Research suppliers using store directories.
2. Send initial approval email.
3. If no response, follow up via phone.
4. Build relationships by managing communication professionally.
5. Take notes on interactions.
6. Continue adding suppliers even after initial launches. [L0270, 2021]

If only a few suppliers approve, use the trade show sidebar procedure above, then contact the exhibitor companies. [L0834, 2021]

Keep the list in a structure that is ready for outreach. Gather the website URL, phone number and email for each supplier, and keep the list ready before starting supplier outreach. [L0278, 2025]
