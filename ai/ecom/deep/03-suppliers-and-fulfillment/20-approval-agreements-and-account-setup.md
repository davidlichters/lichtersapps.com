## Approval, Agreements and Account Setup

### Approval, Forms and First Contact

- Expect the supplier's account manager to reply within a few days after you submit the application forms, with approval, an account number and contact details, or a request for more information. [L0133, undated]
- Delaying the return of supplier forms significantly lowers your approval rate, so complete and submit them as soon as they arrive. [L0133, undated]
- Fill out supplier authorized-retailer forms as soon as they arrive and return them right away, so products can go live without downtime. [L1092, 2022]
- Letting an application or product information sit unanswered slows the whole sourcing process. Return applications and upload product data as fast as possible so you do not lose time between suppliers. [L0495, 2020-26]
- A dealer or retailer application on a supplier's website is the same process as the formal approval paperwork, so fill it out the same way. [L1111, 2020]
- A brand's dealer application typically asks for first and last name, email, phone number and a business street address, and a sales representative then calls back. Use the same details and phone number you plan to use when you call the supplier. [L0932, 2021-22]
- To find the right person:
  1. Call the supplier's main contact numbers and ask who handles retailer approvals.
  2. Submit the approval paperwork.
  3. Ask whether they ship on your account or their own. [L1111, 2020]
- Approval reveals the supplier's actual price list, which shows your real margin and what competitors earn per sale, so you can skip any product with weak margins. Getting approved costs nothing and does not oblige you to list any product. [L1146, 2020-26]
- Some suppliers do not approve a new store immediately. Gold suppliers commonly reject or ask to re-review once a store has more products. Application fields asking for Facebook or Instagram accounts can be left blank until those accounts exist. [L0969, 2022]
- You can contact suppliers and get approved while your business bank account or DBA is still pending. You can request price lists and CSVs and prepare the site, and simply hold orders until the account is ready. [L0961, 2020]
- If a supplier that owns several brands approves the account, get approved with all of its brands rather than just the first one. [L0967, 2020]
- If you have not been approved by a brand yet:
  1. Contact the brand and go through its normal approval process.
  2. Once approved, request its price lists.
  3. Compare the wholesale price, the retail price and the shipping cost for each item.
  4. Approval does not require listing every product the brand sells, so list only the items whose margin works. [L0357, 2019]
- Some approved suppliers do not require a contract and only send specifications, prices and photos. This is enough to start, and products should be uploaded and traffic set up as soon as possible. [L0466, 2018]
- Secure supplier approval before listing their products on your store, because suppliers must agree to let you sell their products through your website. [L0912, 2021-22]
- Approval steps once a supplier moves toward approval:
  1. Stop other tasks and prioritize the supplier while the conversation is moving forward.
  2. Send the documentation the supplier requests and sign the MAP agreement.
  3. Get approved, then receive the product CSV and pricing sheets.
  4. Check that your selling price can be competitive and still profitable against the MSRP and MAP.
  5. Upload the supplier's products to the store. [L0467, 2019]
- Approval order (2025):
  1. After approval, the supplier sends a simple agreement to sign and return.
  2. After they acknowledge the signed agreement, they send product information.
  3. If product information does not arrive, follow up with every approved supplier and state your launch date. [L0972, 2025]
- Once authorized as a retailer, repeat this cycle for as long as you keep adding brands:
  1. When a supplier agrees to work with you, immediately send the authorized retailer application form it requests.
  2. Once authorized, upload that supplier's products to your store.
  3. Return to your master supplier list and contact the next brand.
  4. Repeat getting approved, uploading products and moving to the next supplier. [L0938, 2020-26]

### Product Data and First Uploads After Approval

- After approval, ask the supplier for its top three selling products and how many units of each it sells per month. The monthly unit count gives you a baseline for how much of that demand you can capture. [L1274, 2018]
- When a supplier approves you:
  1. Ask for the top three bestselling products and write them down, since these are the priority for early work and conversion optimization.
  2. Ask how many units of each sell per month.
  3. Upload the approved products, then move to the next supplier. [L0803, 2022]
- If a supplier agreement requires you to upload the whole catalog, ask the supplier by email to confirm that you only plan to list selected products before you rely on that. [L1113, 2022]
- Upload only the products you want. You do not need to upload everything. [L0253, 2024]
- If a supplier requires a product purchase or deposit for a product list, a common workaround is to ask them to upload the catalog first, then pre-purchase only the proven bestsellers. For custom products, you can offer to forward each order to the supplier once it arrives. [L0801, 2021]

### Agreements and Terms to Check

- Supplier agreements are usually short and cover payment, business information, shipping method and the price policy (MAP). Review them carefully. A lawyer review was not needed for typical supplier agreements in the lesson's experience. [L0468, 2020]
- Supplier agreements usually cover the MAP policy, brand representation terms, the contact details for both sides, payment terms and the return policy. Use a signed agreement from a supplier you already work with as a template when a new supplier asks what terms you propose. [L1112, 2021]
- The authorized retailer agreement you sign with a brand contains its MAP policy and return policy. Read these terms before selling, because they decide whether discounts and returns are allowed. [L0667, 2025]
- A supplier can require approval before you offer discount codes. When signing a MAP agreement, check whether the supplier prohibits discount codes. This is rare, around 5% of cases, and if it applies, do not offer discounts on that brand. [L1090, 2021]
- Supplier agreements can require a supplier to contain the order process. Read each agreement and document how orders are processed, by email or through a dealer portal, so the process can later be handed to a team. [L0856, 2024]
- Supplier agreements typically describe how orders are processed, by email or through a dealer portal. Read each supplier's agreement and document its order process, so the process can later be handed to a team. [L0856, 2024]
- If a supplier's application or contract contradicts itself, for example about who arranges freight, call them before submitting and ask which term applies. A professional supplier should be able to clarify the wording and send a corrected version. [L0853, 2021]
- If a supplier has no standard terms for online retailers, define them yourself:
  1. Expect that the supplier has no ready dropship terms and will need to be guided.
  2. Take the terms from your existing supplier agreements. These cover the MAP pricing formula (for example 2x or 2.5x wholesale), who ships and bills the order, and the length of the return window.
  3. Copy these terms into a Word or Google document and edit them to fit the new supplier.
  4. Save the document as a PDF and send it to the supplier, stating that these are the terms you are looking for. [L0626, 2025+]
- If a supplier has no drop shipping agreement, take the clearest existing supplier agreement from another brand and adapt it into your own template. [L1113, 2022]
- When a supplier asks you for a contract, it often means they have little experience with online retailers. A contract for this purpose can fit on one page and list the basic terms as bullet points. [L0968, 2021]
- If a supplier has no dropship agreement, create a simple one-page agreement in Google Docs. Include how to place orders, the return policy, who to contact and any warranties. Base it on an agreement from another supplier, then save it as a PDF. Suppliers rarely lack such policies, so this should be uncommon. [L0666, 2024]

- Supplier agreements typically forbid selling their products on marketplaces such as eBay and Amazon. Check the agreement before listing a supplier product there. [L0803, 2022]

### Payment on Card File and Prepay

- Most supplier relationships run on a credit card kept on file: the supplier charges it when an order is sent, so the item ships right away. A debit card on file can work too. [L0970, 2023]
- Many suppliers keep the operator's card on file so an order can be charged and shipped right away when it is submitted. The card is used for orders the operator places with that supplier and is part of the supplier's online vendor account setup. [L1114, 2023]
- When a supplier approves an online vendor account, it may ask for a card on file, a preferred courier and the store's account number so orders can be charged and shipped quickly. It may also send a spec sheet with dimensions. Use the supplier's own data file where it is usable, and fill in the missing details yourself before uploading. [L1114, 2023]
- Standard supplier approval paperwork often includes a credit card authorization form with the card number and a signature, so the supplier can process orders quickly. Some sellers also send a card photo for verification. [L1113, 2022]
- Providing credit card details to approved suppliers is standard practice for pre-pay arrangements. Suppliers charge the card to fulfill orders after customer purchases. [L0270, 2021]
- To pay suppliers:
  1. Keep your company credit card on file with the supplier.
  2. Send the order. The supplier charges the card and ships the product.
  3. You receive the tracking number and invoice by email.
  Some suppliers instead require payment through a dealer portal on their website. [L0247, 2021]
- Keep a credit card on file with suppliers so they can charge it immediately when you submit an order, rather than asking for payment each time. Pay the card off the same or next day, and ask the card company to raise the limit as sales grow. [L0852, 2020]
- A supplier can be paid per order by debit card or PayPal if no card is kept on file. A stored card lets the supplier charge immediately and dispatch sooner, so it is the faster option. [L0852, 2020]
- Products from a supplier can be paid with a debit card, as suppliers generally accept it. [L1111, 2020]
- Do not ask suppliers for credit terms. Instead, leave a credit or debit card on file that they charge when each order is submitted. [L0834, 2021]
- (2023) Keep all purchases on your card-on-file with a supplier as prepaid orders. When describing this to a supplier, use the term "prepay for orders", because you pay before they ship. [L0835, 2023]
- (2023) Prepay wording for a supplier application that asks for credit terms: write "prepay terms" over the request. Because the store pays the supplier before it ships, credit references are not needed. [L0964, 2023]
- (2021) Credit-application forms from suppliers often ask for credit and trade references. You can cross those sections out, mark the application as wanting prepaid terms, and send it back. [L1279, 2021]
- (undated) Suppliers may offer net payment terms such as 30, 60 or 90 days, which means they extend credit and ship inventory before being paid. Asking for prepay instead means you pay by card for each order before the supplier ships it. This takes the credit risk away from them and speeds up approval. [L0933, 2020-26]
- (undated) Prepay wording to use with a supplier:

```
We are not looking for any credit terms. We would like to prepay: we will keep our credit card on file, and when we send you an order, you can charge our card and ship the order to our customer. [If you have no credit yet: We will start with a debit card on file and switch to a credit card once our credit has built up.]
```

- (2023) Some suppliers accept bank transfers, but many prefer payment by credit or debit card for orders. A business card is therefore the more practical way to pay suppliers, even though transfers work with some of them. [L1405, 2021-22]

### Credit Limits and Building Credit

- Minimum credit card limit: $1,000 covers a typical first order (COGS $500 + shipping $100). A debit card is an acceptable alternative. [L0268, 2020]
- To grow the limit:
  1. Pay off the credit card balance daily after orders go through.
  2. Call the card issuer after the first few weeks and request a limit increase based on the demonstrated daily payoff history.
  3. Repeat monthly or bimonthly. Limit increases are granted based on usage pattern, not waiting period. [L0268, 2020]
- Build credit limit by paying off the supplier card every day. This keeps it near its limit while showing the bank consistent use. Then ask the bank for limit increases, since the payment history supports larger limits over time. [L0970, 2023]
- A new seller does not need a high credit card limit at the start. Limits can grow over time as you repay the card each day and request increases from the bank. [L0852, 2020]
- Build credit in stages: low limit, then increased limit over time, then a secured credit card backed by a $5,000+ deposit. [L0270, 2021]

### Credit Terms and Net Payment

Sources differ on when net terms are possible. Use this order:

| Stage | Position | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Start (new store) | Prepay each order by card on file. Net terms are not available at the start. | [L1480, 2021-22]; [L0963, 2022] |
| Start, general | Net terms are uncommon and depend on the supplier. Most suppliers expect prepay. | [L1112, 2021] |
| Start, preference | A supplier that offers credit card terms is preferred. Net terms and wire transfer per order are not recommended for a new dropshipper: net terms need many trade references, and wires slow each order. | [L0665, 2022] |
| Later | After about ten orders over two months, ask for net 30 terms. | [L1113, 2022] |

- Suppliers often offer 30-day, 60-day or 90-day net terms. Avoid applying for credit terms and prepay each order by card instead. [L0963, 2022]
- Suppliers offering credit terms may request credit references, bank references or a personal credit report. Prepaying by card on file avoids the credit check. [L0963, 2022]
- Net 30 is available only after about ten orders over two months. Switch to ACH transfer at that point if needed, as described under card fees. [L1113, 2022]
- Supplier credit accounts, which allow you to pay invoices later, are not available at the start. Expect to pay each order in full when it is placed. Treat a credit account as a later-stage option once the relationship is established. [L1480, 2021-22]

### Deposits, Stock and Pre-Purchase

- Never pay a deposit to a supplier before a customer order exists, so the cash outlay is zero until sales come in. Refuse requests for large credit card deposits, such as $4,000, because they are uncommon and risky before you have a relationship with the supplier. [L0852, 2020]
- Only pay a supplier deposit or opening order from profits your store has already earned with other suppliers. Do not pay a deposit or stock inventory before you know the best sellers. Commit to larger inventory only when the opportunity is clearly massive. [L0801, 2021]
- Never pay a supplier upfront or buy inventory in advance to start a relationship. Propose a 30-day trial run of listing their products instead. [L1277, 2020]
- Do not send stock to a fulfillment center to meet a supplier minimum or to handle a first order. It is not necessary in this situation. [L1109, 2018]
- If a supplier requires a product purchase or deposit for a product list, ask them to upload the catalog first, then pre-purchase only the proven bestsellers. [L0801, 2021]
