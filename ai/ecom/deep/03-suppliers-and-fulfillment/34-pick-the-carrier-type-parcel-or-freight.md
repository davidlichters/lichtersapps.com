### Pick the carrier type: parcel or freight

#### Parcel carriers for small items

- For small parcels, UPS and FedEx usually have the best rates. Their freight rates for large items are often well above what freight brokers quote, so for freight use freight brokers and ask them about discounts. [L0835, 2023]
- For normal-size parcels, UPS and FedEx are reasonable. [L0972, 2025]
- Small parcels with UPS, FedEx or USPS usually cost about the same. The freight-broker advice in this section applies only to freight. [L1086, 2019]
- Ask each supplier which carrier it ships with. UPS, FedEx or DHL keep shipping affordable for compact products. [L0663, 2019]

#### Freight for large and heavy items

- Large and heavy items are shipped by LTL (less than truckload) freight from the supplier's warehouse to the customer, not with UPS or FedEx. [L0855, 2023]
- Large and heavy items are far too expensive to ship with UPS, FedEx or DHL as parcels, so they move through freight brokers instead. Parcel carriers are only used for smaller products. [L0854, 2022]
- For products above a certain size and weight, shipping with UPS or FedEx becomes too expensive, so you ship with a freight carrier. Freight is the standard option for large and heavy items. [L0961, 2020]
- For large and heavy items such as 9 x 57 x 36 inches and about 70 pounds, use LTL freight shipping instead of parcel carriers like FedEx and UPS. [L1085, 2018]
- Heavy items of 300 to 400 pounds usually ship as LTL freight, which is expensive. Get freight quotes from a site such as freightcenter.com by entering the supplier warehouse, package size, weight and destination. Negotiate rates, since there is often room. [L0804, 2023]
- Freight-shipped items for a product of about 100 to 350 pounds can be quoted up to $600 per shipment as LTL. Compare quotes from several freight companies before setting shipping prices. [L0667, 2025]
- Less-than-truckload freight quotes for heavy or long-distance orders can be very high, for example 500 to 600 USD per shipment. Check whether a product can still be sold profitably before advertising it. [L0966, 2025]
- For large and heavy furniture and similar bulky products, freight shipping is usually handled as LTL freight. Aggregator sites that handle heavy shipping cover this category. [L0970, 2023]
- Use the 'UPS or FedEx for normal parcels, freight for large' split: for large and heavy items, use traditional freight shippers, since the freight rates of UPS and FedEx are far higher. [L0972, 2025]

#### UPS and FedEx freight

- Never use UPS Freight or FedEx Freight for freight items. Their rates are far too expensive for heavy or large freight orders. [L0806, 2024] (2024)
- UPS and FedEx are not used for freight shipments because they are too expensive. For freight, the shipment is created through a freight broker and a BOL is generated. [L0805, 2024]
- UPS and FedEx freight for large or heavy items costs about four times more than other freight companies. Use them only for very small items. [L0855, 2023]
- For large and heavy items, UPS and FedEx freight rates are usually far above traditional freight shipping rates. Get quotes from traditional freight shippers before you set shipping prices. [L0972, 2025]
- Parcel carriers such as UPS, FedEx and USPS are usually expensive for heavy or bulky items. Use a freight broker or freight company for those shipments and compare quotes. [L0468, 2020]

- UPS or FedEx rates for small parcels are usually the best. For large items, their freight rates are often well above what brokers quote. [L0835, 2023] (2023)
- Use freight brokers for large or heavy items. Use UPS or FedEx for small parcels. [L0835, 2023; L0855, 2023; L0251, 2023]

### Find carriers through top retailers

#### Ask suppliers for their carriers

- Ask each supplier which carrier its largest retailers use for shipments of the same size and weight. Those retailers usually have the best rates. [L0854, 2022; L0835, 2023]
- Ask a supplier which freight companies its top retailers use, then request quotes from those companies, since they usually offer the best rate for that product size and weight. [L0972, 2025]
- Ask suppliers which companies their other retailers use, since this gives insider information on what works. [L1086, 2019]
- Ask the supplier which carriers its biggest retailers use. Get quotes from those carriers directly. Compare with freight quotes from FreightCenter.com, which is commonly used for LTL and white-glove freight. [L0803, 2022]
- Use freight brokers such as FreightCenter.com for freight quotes rather than contacting carriers directly. The best carrier depends on origin and destination, so a broker usually finds the best prices. [L0803, 2022]
- Asking a supplier's top retailers for their freight company, then contacting that company directly, can save around 100 to 200 USD per shipment. [L0966, 2025]

Procedure: finding a freight shipper for a product (2025)
1. Email your supplier contact and ask which companies their top retailers use to ship.
2. Ask for the names of those shipping companies.
3. Request quotes from those companies, since they usually offer the best rate for that product size and weight.

[L0972, 2025]

Procedure: getting a freight quote from the carriers your supplier's top retailers use (2023)
1. Ask the supplier's contact which freight companies their largest retailers use for shipments of the same size and weight.
2. Get instant quotes from several freight companies, for example at freightcenter.com.
3. Email the freight companies asking whether any discounts are available on the quotes.
4. Choose a carrier and pay it. Choose pickup at the supplier or warehouse when that applies.
5. Receive the bill of lading (BOL), which works as the freight shipping label.
6. Send the BOL to the supplier. The freight company picks up the order and arranges with the warehouse, and tracking information goes to the customer.
7. Start the shipment as soon as possible, since the BOL and pickup are arranged quickly.

[L0835, 2023]

Procedure: negotiating with brokers and your supplier's benchmark (2025)
1. Collect shipping quotes for the same shipment from freight broker websites such as freightcenter.com and Red Hawk.
2. If one broker quotes a lower price, send that quote to the other broker and ask them to beat it.
3. Use your supplier's quote for a shipment as a benchmark when negotiating with brokers.
4. Ask your suppliers which freight companies their top retailers use, then contact those companies directly for better rates.

[L0966, 2025]

Procedure: getting freight quotes from a supplier's top retailers (2020)
1. Ask each supplier which freight company it prefers and whether it can introduce you. Different suppliers have different freight relationships, and this often gets the best price.
2. If that fails, get quotes from a freight broker such as freightcenter.com.
3. Compare the quotes. Different quotes for the same shipment often differ by $100 to $300.

[L0852, 2020]

Example for carrier search (2025): ask the supplier which top retailers ship the item. Give the carrier the item's exact size and weight, for example 57 x 3 x 26 inches and 11 pounds. [L0857, 2025]

- Ask each supplier which freight company it prefers before you assume a broker's quote is the best. [L0852, 2020]
- A supplier that will not ship on its own account can be served by the carrier its top retailers use, as long as that carrier will pick up at the supplier's warehouse. [L0854, 2022]

#### White-glove carriers through top retailers

- For white-glove delivery, ask suppliers which white glove company they use or recommend for their product line, rather than finding a random local service. Suppliers already know which providers deliver their products well. [L0249, 2022]
- For large and heavy items, suppliers recommend white glove partners. Contact them to learn their established process and which delivery companies other retailers use. [L0245, 2020]
- Ask suppliers which delivery companies they recommend. White glove pricing varies widely, so compare several providers and choose the one with the best price and reviews. [L0664, 2020]
- For white glove with assembly, ask the supplier which top retailers it uses for that service. Those retailers likely already know the products and have set pricing. [L0970, 2023]

### Book the shipment and the bill of lading

#### What the BOL is

- A bill of lading (BOL) is the freight equivalent of a parcel shipping label. The freight company provides it, and you send it to the supplier so the freight company can pick up the order. [L0835, 2023]
- BOL stands for Bill of Lading. It shows the pickup location (supplier warehouse) and the delivery location (customer address) for freight shipments. [L0255, 2025]
- Freight transit estimates, such as four to six business days, are not a fixed promise until a delivery window is confirmed, so delays can happen before that point. [L0470, 2023]

#### Procedure: booking with a broker and the BOL

Procedure: arranging a bill of lading shipment via FreightCenter (2021)
1. Open the calculate freight rates tool on FreightCenter.
2. Enter the supplier's address or ZIP code and the customer's ZIP code.
3. Enter the package size and weight.
4. Compare the quotes from freight carriers and book the one you choose.
5. Download the bill of lading and send it to the supplier, whose truck then picks up the shipment.

[L0469, 2021]

Procedure: shipping through a freight broker, with the BOL sent to the supplier
1. Place your order with the supplier.
2. Wait for the supplier to confirm that the item is ready for pickup.
3. Obtain a bill of lading (BOL) from a freight broker, a freight shipper, UPS, or FedEx.
4. Send the BOL to the supplier so the item is prepared for pickup.
5. The shipping company picks up the item from the supplier and delivers it to your customer.

[L0466, 2018]

#### Freight shipment for a cost-conscious owner

- Ask the supplier which freight company it prefers and get quotes from a broker, then book the cheaper option. Pay attention to delivery windows, since large heavy items are not dropped at the door. [L0852, 2020; L0805, 2024]
- For large freight, start the shipment as soon as possible, since the BOL and pickup are arranged quickly. [L0835, 2023]

#### Procedure for a supplier warehouse pickup

- Get the BOL from the broker. [L0805, 2024]
- Send it to the supplier. [L0805, 2024]
- Let the supplier arrange pickup. [L0805, 2024]

### Delivery levels: curbside, liftgate, white glove

#### Curbside and freight defaults

- Freight shipments should include free curbside delivery by default, with white glove service offered as a paid upgrade. [L0834, 2021]
- Standard freight delivery for heavy, high-ticket items is curbside delivery, where the carrier leaves the box outside the door. White glove upgrades this so the crew brings the item inside, unpacks it and removes the packaging. It can be sold as a paid upsell to buyers of items costing thousands of dollars. [L0923, 2015]
- Large items shipped curbside: a PDF explaining the delivery process on each product page is sufficient at the start. Later, ask suppliers whether they offer white glove delivery and consider a markup for it. [L0856, 2024] (2024)
- Freight options include lift gate, inside delivery and white glove delivery, and each can be charged as a premium. [L0920, 2018]

#### Table of delivery levels (2020)

| Level | What the carrier does |
|---|---|
| Curbside | Default freight delivery. The product is left at the curb. |
| Lift gate | The truck lowers the product with a lift gate. The product is still not brought inside or to the door. Choose it over curbside when prices are comparable. |
| White glove | The freight carrier delivers to a local white glove company, which brings the product inside to the room the customer chooses, unboxes it, removes packaging debris and can assemble it. |

[L0961, 2020]

- White glove delivery comes in levels: delivery into the home, unboxing and removing packaging, or assembly and carrying up stairs with packaging removed. Ask each supplier which delivery and assembly partners its top retailers use for the product type. [L0469, 2021]
- Two tiers of white glove delivery: the Standard tier (free or freight shipping) delivers to the curbside, and the customer is responsible for unboxing and setup. The Premium tier (at upcharge) includes professional installation, unboxing, assembly and removal of all packaging. [L0245, 2020]
- White glove delivery means the product is brought inside the customer's home and can be installed or set up. It is the usual meaning of installation services in a niche. [L0856, 2024]

#### Levels of service for the freight carrier

- Freight delivery levels: the product is delivered by a lift gate when the customer's site has no loading dock. [L0471, 2024]
- White glove delivery typically costs 100 to 300 USD extra, depending on the product and whether assembly is included. Confirm the price before you offer it. [L0961, 2020]

### Liftgate, residential and terminal fees

#### Definitions

- A liftgate is a hydraulic platform on a truck that lowers heavy goods to ground level. Residential delivery is a fee for delivering to a home rather than a business. A freight terminal delivery sends goods to a freight hub instead of the customer's door. [L0471, 2024]
- Liftgate is a hydraulic platform on a truck that lowers heavy goods to ground level. [L0471, 2024]

#### Include liftgate or charge it as an option

- Include liftgate services by default on heavy freight shipments to prevent unexpected fees. If a customer's location triggers additional charges (residential fees, overhanging issues), send an invoice for those location-specific costs. [L0276, 2024]
- Decide on lift gate and white glove charges by checking the top two or three competitors in the niche. Some competitors offer free curbside delivery, some include a free lift gate, and some charge extra for it. [L0468, 2020]
- When booking a freight shipment, select the lift gate or white glove option through the checkbox in the freight booking. If the shipment is on the supplier's account, ask the supplier to add the option to the shipment. [L0468, 2020]
- A single freight carrier quoted $200 to $250 for liftgate service on one shipment. A flat liftgate fee on every order can therefore be costly, so offer it as an option rather than a default charge. [L0667, 2025]

Procedure: adding liftgate as a paid option (2025)
1. Check whether competitors include liftgate service on their product or shipping pages.
2. When booking a freight shipment, add liftgate to the delivery if the carrier offers it.
3. If liftgate is not included by default, send the order confirmation first, saying the product is in stock and ready to ship.
4. Offer the liftgate upgrade and ask the customer to reply if they want it.
5. Get a carrier quote, and if the customer accepts, create a custom invoice in Shopify and send it.

[L0667, 2025]

```
Thank you for your order. Your product is in stock and ready to ship. We also offer an upgrade to liftgate delivery service, which helps with delivery to locations without a loading dock. If you are interested, please reply to this email and let us know, and we will send you a quote for the upgrade.
```

### White glove and installation partners

#### Ask the supplier first

- Ask the supplier directly whether it offers white glove delivery and at what price. If not, ask which delivery company the supplier's largest retailers use. Work with the same companies, since they will give the best rates. [L0964, 2023] (2023)
- Ask a supplier to organize white glove delivery or installation for your customers instead of building your own service. Get the supplier's preferred provider and pricing, then offer it as an add-on. If the supplier declines, contact the top retailers' providers directly. [L0934, 2023] (2023)
- Products that need assembly or adjustment on arrival can work if the supplier already has a delivery and setup process. Verify who delivers, assembles and adjusts the product by asking future competitors which companies they use. [L0962, 2021]
- For suppliers that already work with white glove partners: white glove is the carrier-based service the supplier's partners perform. [L0245, 2020]

Procedure: offering white glove delivery through a supplier (2024)
1. Ask the supplier whether white glove service is available through their shipping carrier, not through the supplier itself.
2. Ask what the upcharge is for it.
3. If the supplier says no, ask which other top retailers they work with that offer white glove shipping and which providers those retailers use.

[L0666, 2024]

Procedure: setting up white glove delivery (undated)

2. Use those same carriers, since they already pick up from that warehouse and have pricing.
3. Set the price per product based on carrier cost and margin.

Procedure: arranging white glove delivery (delivery into the home, unboxing, assembly and removal of packaging)

2. Ask for a contact name, phone number and email for the recommended company.
3. Contact those companies directly.

- White glove delivery can be offered for high-priced items either as a paid add-on or as a bonus. [L0967, 2020]

### Assembly and installation

#### Which products need a partner

- Products that need site work or a trade installer, such as Solar Panels, Wind Turbines, Garage Doors, Well Pumps, Hot Tubs, Saunas, Above Ground Pools, Fireplaces, Wood & Pellet Stoves and Walk-in Refrigerators & Freezers, need an installation partner or local service network. Without one, the sale ends at delivery and the return and complaint risk rises. [L0546, undated]
- For products that need assembly of four or more hours, no national service was found. Ask the supplier for a recommended local installer, since many large retailers subcontract installation to local companies. [L1090, 2021]
- Assembly services are seldom offered by suppliers. When they are, present them as an upsell rather than an included feature. [L0934, 2023] (2023)

#### Procedure: assembly on large items

Procedure: offering assembly on large items (older, 2021; the same procedure appears in two units)
1. Check competitor listings on Google Shopping. If they include assembly, offer it too.
2. Ask the supplier which retailers it uses for white glove delivery.
3. Contact those companies and build a relationship with them.

[L0671, 2021]

Procedure: finding a competitor's assembly partner
1. Check the competitor's website for a named assembly or service partner.
2. If none is named, contact the competitor from a personal email address (not your business email) and ask which company assembles the product.
3. Contact that assembly company directly to set up your own partnership.

#### Assembly as an upsell

- An upsell for assembly can appear at checkout as a pop-up asking whether the buyer wants one of the brand's specialists to set the product up at home. When the shopper says yes, the store sends a nearby professional, or sources one through Craigslist, and keeps the markup. [L0923, 2015]
- Assembly as an upsell can be handled by hiring a local contractor per order, which means manual coordination. Alternatively, use a nationwide white-glove service that delivers and assembles in one step. The second option keeps the margin without extra coordination. [L0970, 2023] (2023)
- Do not buy from a supplier and hire local installers on your own unless no supplier process exists. Doing it yourself adds work and risk. [L0962, 2021]

### Geo-targeting heavy products in ads

#### The principle

- For heavy items that are expensive to ship over long distances, sell nationally but run paid ads only in the area near the supplier's warehouse. Geo-targeting keeps shipping costs low for paid orders. [L0853, 2021]
- Products from a heavy, large supplier may be sold with a geo-targeted campaign around the warehouse, so that orders ship only from a short distance and shipping cost stays low. For most campaigns, geo-targeting should be off. [L0964, 2023]
- To keep expensive heavy items profitable in ads, split suppliers into separate campaigns with geo-targeting. Put the heavy, low-margin supplier products into their own campaigns targeted within a 50-mile radius of the warehouse, so shipping costs stay low. [L0941, 2022]

#### Radius

- Source conflict: a 50-mile radius around the warehouse ([L0941, 2022]), against a radius of about 100 to 200 miles around the warehouse, with the 100-mile radius given as the example ([L0964, 2023]). [L0941, 2022; L0964, 2023]

#### Campaign split in Google Ads

Setting: campaign split for an expensive-to-ship supplier in Google Ads (2023)
- Make Alpha 1 and Beta 1 exclude that supplier's products.
- Create Alpha 2 and Beta 2 that contain only that supplier's products.
- Apply geo-targeting to Alpha 2 and Beta 2 around the supplier's warehouse.

#### Shipping-driven example

- Example of shipping-driven pricing: a 430-dollar shipping cost on an order that earns only 90 dollars of margin makes the item unprofitable. The fix is to split the campaigns so only that supplier's products run in a geo-targeted campaign around its warehouse. [L0964, 2023]
