## Qualifying Suppliers: Location, Stock, MAP, Direct Brands vs. Distributors

### Supplier criteria at a glance

| Criterion | Requirement | Ids |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Supplier is in the same country as your store (for a US store, a US-based supplier) | [L0247, 2021] |
| Stock | Supplier holds the product in stock in the country or region where you sell | [L0962, 2021; L0994, 2021-22] |
| Origin of manufacture | Does not matter if stock is local; matters if everything ships from abroad | [L0466, 2018; L0941, 2022; L1085, 2018] |
| Relationship | Brand or importer that owns the brand name, approved directly | [L0276, 2024; L0359, 2020; L0665, 2022] |
| MAP | Enforced by the supplier, or a documented reason to accept a non-MAP supplier | [L0110, undated; L0472, 2025; L1069, 2019] |
| Direct-to-consumer | Sells at MSRP while you sell at MAP, or its own store is weak | [L0247, 2021; L0249, 2022; L0803, 2022] |
| Channel | Allows you to sell on the channels you need, and ships on your account | [L0835, 2023; L0967, 2020] |
| Responsiveness | Answers phone and email before approval | [L1111, 2020] |

### Order of checks before you contact a supplier

1. Check dropship friendliness of the niche before you build the store, because suppliers may refuse retailers that are not physical stores or do not hold their own stock. [L0460, 2020-26]
2. Check how many online retailers already sell each brand, and prefer brands with fewer competing sellers, especially when the business depends on paid ads. [L0711, 2020-26]
3. Check whether the supplier's products are already sold by other online-only stores, because that shows dropship friendliness. Not every supplier will approve, but enough approvals are likely when every criterion is followed. [L0359, 2020]
4. Check whether competitors have a retail store, showroom or warehouse, because suppliers with retail presence often refuse drop shipping. [L0671, 2021]
5. Verify the stock location (see the stock verification section below).
6. Identify whether the seller is the brand, an importer, a manufacturer or a distributor (see the distributor section below).
7. Check MAP policy and enforcement (see the MAP section below).
8. Check the direct-to-consumer conflict (see the direct-to-consumer section below).
9. Check marketplace and channel restrictions, and product shipping restrictions.

### Stock location: the first filter

- Source from domestic suppliers in the market where the store operates. For a US store, that means US suppliers. [L0247, 2021; L1069, 2019]
- A supplier's location (warehouse or showroom) is treated the same way when deciding whether it is a valid source for a store. The decision is based on the same data for each. [L0964, 2023]
- Choose suppliers that hold the product in stock in the country or region where you sell. Overseas suppliers shipping to customers are a very small exception and are not recommended. [L0962, 2021]
- Most of the supplier base should be domestic. Foreign suppliers should be used only where they offer unique products and good quality. [L1086, 2019]
- Domestic suppliers are preferred because shipping is faster and cheaper and customers are happier. [L0962, 2021]
- When a domestic supplier with short lead times is available, prefer it over an overseas middleman with long lead times, even if the unit price is slightly higher. Long lead times create customer cancellations and backorder handling. [L0852, 2020]
- Where possible, find a supplier of the same product located in the target country, such as the US, instead of shipping from CJ. In the US, the number of warehouses made little difference in the session. [L1108, 2023]
- Products made overseas but stored in US warehouses are fine, since they ship quickly. [L0941, 2022]
- Suppliers outside the US can still be a good fit if their products are stored in US warehouses and can ship quickly and efficiently. Warehouses close to your customers make shipping easier. [L0960, 2019]
- Suppliers with warehouses in the country you sell in are a positive sign. [L0963, 2022]
- Suppliers located outside the US can be used for US sales if they already have US distribution. In that case the sourcing process is the same as with a US-based supplier. [L0971, 2024]
- Suppliers located in Canada or Mexico are fine to contact for a US store. Competitors selling for them often already use US distribution, a US fulfillment center or a US warehouse. [L0856, 2024]
- Suppliers headquartered abroad that also have a US office may stock their inventory in the United States. Ask where a product is stocked before excluding an international supplier. [L0834, 2021]
- Many large foreign brands already serve the US market through US distribution, a US fulfillment center or a US warehouse, and can be sold to US customers. [L0806, 2024]
- A supplier with a foreign domain (for example.ca) that is listed on a US retail site probably also has US distribution. Record its contact details and check its contact page for a US option. [L0940, 2020-26]
- North American brands can supply a US store. Ask them which carriers they use and which other companies already ship to the US, then reverse engineer what competitors do. [L0671, 2021]
- Start with US-only shipping. Suppliers in Canada that sell to the US may already hold stock in US warehouses or fulfillment centers, so reach out to them if they appear on competitor sites. [L0470, 2023]
- Prefer suppliers that hold inventory where you operate and where your customers are. [L0469, 2021]
- Working with suppliers that hold stock in your target country is the standard. [L0671, 2021]
- Suppliers outside the US may hold stock in US warehouses. Add them to your supplier list and ask whether they ship from a US warehouse and how stock reaches customers. [L0469, 2021]
- Suppliers based in Europe: ask whether they have stock or distribution centers in the United States, because local stock speeds up delivery. [L1002, 2019]
- Shipping to a different country from the sourcing market is possible, for example selling a product that performs well in the US in Australia, which is in winter when the US product has been popular. Sourcing a product from one market and selling it in another is a common way to test products. [L0678, 2023]
- Ship international orders only through the supplier's own shipping arrangements. If a supplier does not ship to a given country such as Canada or the US, do not sell its products there. [L0934, 2023]

#### Delivery benchmarks

| Scenario | Delivery time | Ids |
|---|---|---|
| US-based supplier with US stock, for US customers | 2 to 3 days | [L0233, undated] |
| High-ticket product from a US manufacturer that stocks in the US | 2 to 7 days | [L0458, 2021-22] |
| Low-ticket items shipped from China to US customers | 1 to 4 weeks | [L0458, 2021-22] |
| International dropshipping (supplier abroad) | 21 to 30 days | [L0233, undated] |
| Overseas supplier with no stock in your market | 2 to 4 weeks | [L0360, 2024] |
| Repeat orders from supplier with local stock | 4 to 10 days (a first order taking 8 to 10 weeks is unusual) | [L1111, 2020] |

- Customers in a country expect delivery within about a week, so local stock matches what they already expect. [L0458, 2021-22]
- Faster fulfillment reduces perceived time delay and effort/sacrifice, which multiplies the value of the same product. [L0233, undated]
- Shipping time is a factor to review before choosing a supplier. Slow suppliers with long shipping times can hurt the customer experience and should be weighed against product, price and margin. [L0440, 2023+]

#### Stock verification procedures

**Find where a supplier is based when you only know its product from another store:**

1. Find the supplier's product listed on a competitor's store. [L0360, 2024]
2. Google the supplier's name to find its own website and the country it operates from. [L0360, 2024]
3. Contact the supplier and ask whether it holds stock or uses a fulfillment center in the target market. [L0360, 2024]

**Check a foreign supplier's US stock before building a store:**

1. Find US retailers that sell the supplier's products. [L0854, 2022]
2. Contact them as a normal customer and ask whether the product is in stock and where it ships from. [L0854, 2022]
3. Alternatively, ask the supplier directly whether it has US distribution or a fulfillment center that ships to US customers. [L0854, 2022]

**Ask a supplier about its stock location by phone or email:**

1. Contact the company by phone, email, live chat, or Facebook message. [L0466, 2018]
2. Ask whether they keep the specific product in stock themselves and, if not, where it ships from. [L0466, 2018]
3. Phrase the question as a shopper's question, not as a competitor's question. [L0466, 2018]
4. If other sites seem to ship the same product from one location, mention it and ask whether they do the same. [L0466, 2018]
- Do not tell a company you are building a competing store when you ask whether they dropship; it makes the answer less reliable and can end the relationship. [L0466, 2018]
- If a supplier's location is unclear, check it directly rather than assume it holds stock. Most dropshipping websites can be identified from their site with about 80 percent certainty. [L0466, 2018]
- When contacting a foreign supplier, it is professional and inoffensive to ask whether they have a U.S. warehouse or fulfillment center before requesting authorization to sell their products. [L0276, 2024]
- A lead-time gap matters: contact the supplier, ask for its shipping and fulfillment location, and check whether it holds stock in your country. Overseas phone numbers do not always mean stock is overseas, so ask directly before deciding. [L0801, 2021]
- For a foreign-based supplier selling into a market, confirm that it stocks inventory in that country, for example through a local distribution center. Ask directly by phone or email, since this is common among international brands. [L1069, 2019]
- A store that says products from certain brands may not ship the same day is usually dropshipping those items from third-party warehouses it does not control. Only the brands it ships itself are stocked in its own warehouse, and those are the exceptions in the notice. [L0459, 2020-26]
- Suppliers may hand out an international product catalog without stock in your market. Check whether they have stock locally before committing to them. [L1111, 2020]
- A first order that takes 8 to 10 weeks while repeat orders ship within 4 to 10 days is unusual. Question the supplier about the stock location before accepting the arrangement. [L1111, 2020]
- If a supplier says it is US-based but ships from overseas, drop-shipping from there will cause slow shipping and is a problem. [L0941, 2022]

### Overseas and China sourcing

#### China

- Do not dropship from China. Long delivery times, inconsistent quality and unexpected import charges make customer experience poor; work with domestic suppliers instead. [L0359, 2020]
- Do not build a long-term model on drop-shipping from China. It creates more problems than it is worth. [L0469, 2021]
- Do not drop ship from China. Work with suppliers that hold stock in your target country. [L0671, 2021]
- Do not approve suppliers that only source from China or that produce products bearing company names or logos. Approve suppliers based in the US or other countries with brand-authorized inventory. [L0253, 2024]
- Do not dropship from AliExpress. [L1069, 2019]
- Products that are made in China or India can still be acceptable when the brand or importer holds stock locally. A seller should prefer those brands or importers. [L0852, 2020]
- Products made overseas but stored in US warehouses are fine. Avoid drop shipping from overseas, such as China, when stock is shipped from there. [L0941, 2022; L0963, 2022]
- Exception: a custom, high-demand item from a single overseas maker, where production and shipping time are acceptable to customers and the margin still works after all costs. Do not actively seek out overseas suppliers as a general sourcing strategy. [L0358, 2020]
- Brands that manufacture outside the US often work only through approved master distributors. Expect to apply to those distributors, and plan for smaller margins when buying through them. [L1068, 2019]

#### Other overseas suppliers

- Overseas suppliers are usually a poor fit for dropshipping because transit takes weeks, and freight on large or heavy products can approach the product price itself. [L0358, 2020]
- Shipping from a supplier abroad often costs about as much as the product, so international dropshipping is rarely worth it. It can only make sense for very small, light items that ship fast. [L0468, 2020]
- Do not drop-ship from other countries as a general rule, because shipping times and fees are often too high. Only consider it when the product is clearly in demand and customers accept long waits and extra cost. [L0834, 2021]
- Avoid suppliers who ship from overseas to the target market when the product also has US stock. Long international delivery times and high shipping costs erode both margin and customer trust. [L1111, 2020]
- Avoid dropshipping from Europe to North America or vice versa. Shipping costs are too high and reduce margins too much. If selling in Europe, create a separate store and source suppliers with inventory stocked in Europe. [L0270, 2021]
- Shipping from Europe to the US is often not cost-effective, especially for large or heavy goods. Check the shipping cost against the selling price before deciding; a long delivery time can be shown as a benefit if the margin still works. [L0357, 2019]
- Suppliers outside the US are acceptable when the products are only available there and quality is good. The risk to avoid is cheap, slow marketplace dropshipping, where delivery takes weeks, quality disappoints and customers keep asking where their order is. [L1086, 2019]
- Suppliers in nearby countries such as Europe can work if shipping remains affordable and no high import taxes apply. [L0962, 2021]
- Foreign-manufactured products are normal. Avoid suppliers that dropship from another country and look for stock in your country of business instead. [L0466, 2018]
- Some suppliers are shipped from abroad under FOB or online-only landing prices. These import terms are rarely used in this model. Confirm that the supplier already has stock in the country where you sell. [L0468, 2020]
- If a manufacturer is abroad, use a distributor only when the product is stocked in your country. Ask the manufacturer which distributor or local warehouse holds stock. [L0468, 2020]
- If a supplier abroad has no stock in your target country, be hesitant, because shipping costs and delivery times will be high. [L0671, 2021]
- Before selling a foreign supplier's products to customers in another country, ask whether the supplier has its own warehouse or a fulfillment center there with stock. If everything ships from abroad, shipping can be expensive and take two to four weeks. Such a supplier is usually not worth it unless it is a top-tier supplier. In that case, the origin and shipping time must be stated on every product page. [L0360, 2024]
- Foreign supplier and sale in a second market: a separate store that only targets that region, loaded only with products that should sell there at the needed prices, with traffic only to that region. [L0853, 2021]
- Use a matching country domain for a non-US market. A supplier's warehouse location affects lead times and costs. If suppliers are based in Europe and you sell from Europe, use a matching domain such as a.com.eu domain. [L0968, 2021]

#### Canada

- A US business address is not required to sell to US customers from Canada. If you display an address on your website, a forwarding address from a US location (that forwards mail to Canada) is recommended for legitimacy. [L0253, 2024]
- Many suppliers in Canada and other countries have a distribution center in the US, so ask for one before assuming high international shipping costs. Costs differ for each supplier and must be checked one by one. [L0357, 2019]
- Procedure for a supplier in Canada (or another country) that sells to US customers:
  1. Contact the supplier individually. [L0357, 2019]
  2. Introduce yourself and your location. [L0357, 2019]
  3. State that you sell in the US. [L0357, 2019]
  4. Ask whether the supplier has a US distribution center and whether there are extra fees or shipping charges for US delivery. [L0357, 2019]
- For Canada-based suppliers, first check whether they hold US inventory. If not, ask which carrier they use to ship to US customers and how they avoid border delays. [L0469, 2021]
- Ask suppliers if they ship to Canada before you add Canada to your store, and whether they already use a go-to shipper for it. Start with the US market first because it is the bigger market. [L0804, 2023]
- If products are already warehoused in the US, they can be sold to both the US and Canada. Choose Canadian suppliers only when the business sells only in Canada. [L0963, 2022]

#### UK, Europe and other markets

- Products from UK suppliers are acceptable only when no US source exists or US sellers all source from the UK. Product pages must state clearly that the products are made and shipped from the UK, with the estimated shipping time to the US. [L0467, 2019]
- Brexit and similar trade barriers make supplying UK customers from European warehouses more complicated. Check current import and export rules before sourcing across that border. [L0781, 2021-22]
- Operators in the UK may need to adjust the standard drop-ship model and explain the process to local suppliers who are not familiar with it. Successful UK sellers have documented those adjustments and shared them with their supplier partners. [L1068, 2019]
- The same MAP reseller rules are described as applying in the UK, Germany, Australia and the US. If a market appears to have no MAP for a brand, research how experienced resellers in that market handle the brand rather than assuming it cannot be sold. [L0663, 2019]
