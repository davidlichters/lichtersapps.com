## Researching Supplier Contact Details

### Where contact research fits in the workflow

- Contact research comes after market research. Once the supplier list is built, the next step is to find each supplier's contact details before reaching out for approval. [L0590, 2020-26]
- The supplier list holds brands found while studying future competitors. Collecting contact details in advance saves time at the approval stage. [L0940, 2020-26]
- Contact research only collects contact data. Do not submit applications, sell-with-us forms or sales inquiries during this phase. Approval requests come later. [L0940, 2020-26]
- At this stage, fill only the URL, phone and email columns. Contact date, contact person and notes are completed in a later step. [L0940, 2020-26]

### Building the first list

- Give each niche its own master supplier list, named after that niche, so suppliers from different niches never mix. [L0931, 2020-26]
- Fill columns A to D first. Columns E to G stay empty at this stage and are completed later. [L0931, 2020-26]
- Stage-one layout:

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| A | Supplier / brand name |
| B | Supplier website URL |
| C | Supplier phone number |
| D | Supplier email address, or contact form link if no email exists |
| Notes | Add-on or accessory products useful for cross-selling (usually blank for core-product suppliers) |

[L0931, 2020-26]

- The Notes column is optional. Use it for add-on products, upsell and cross-sell opportunities, or links to dealer or reseller application pages. [L0056, undated]
- Record cross-sell items in Notes, for example a supplier selling knife sets for add-on or upsell offers. Most Notes stay blank for suppliers of core products, since those are the suppliers you would order the main products from. Add-on sellers are noted but are not treated as core suppliers. [L0931, 2020-26]
- Brands that do not sell the core product can still be recorded in Notes, but they are not treated as core suppliers. [L0931, 2020-26]
- A brand with no working contact information does not count toward the 20-brand minimum. [L0931, 2020-26]

### Per-supplier research routine

**one source procedure, per supplier, in this order:** [L0940, 2020-26]

1. Open the master supplier list for your chosen niche and place it side by side with a browser window (split screen or a second tab).
2. Search Google for the exact supplier or brand name, starting at the top of your list.
3. Open the brand's own website (not a reseller or article) and copy its URL into column B (Supplier URL).
4. Open the site's Contact page (usually via the header menu, or the footer if missing there), copy the phone number and paste it into column C (Supplier Phone).
5. Copy a direct email into column D (Supplier Email); if only a contact form exists, copy the form's link instead.
6. If no site or contact channel appears, run the alternative searches (brand + niche + website; brand + niche; brand + customer service; brand + contact number; brand + Zoominfo).
7. If still nothing is found, enter N-A (not available) in the missing cells.

**one source procedure:** [L0937, 2021-22]

1. Search the brand name on Google and open the official website from the organic results, not from ads. Do not rely on a guessed domain, and check the exact spelling, since similar names can belong to different companies.
2. Confirm that the site sells your product type and that the company is the manufacturer or brand owner, not a retailer.
3. Open the Contact Us page from the top navigation first, since it is the most likely place for contact details.
4. Check the footer for a phone number and any support or customer service page.
5. Copy the website URL, the email address (if one is published) and the phone number into the spreadsheet.
6. If there is no public email address, type 'Website contact form' in the Email column and still record the phone number.
7. Look in the header and footer for links such as 'Become a Dealer', 'Become a Retailer' or 'Become a [brand] dealer'. If one exists, note its page URL in the Online Application column and do not submit anything yet.
8. If a dealer page gives specific contact instructions (phone, form, mailing address), record that channel as the way to reach them later.
9. Move on to the next brand.

**one source 2021-22:** the research routine above is a copy-and-record process, so the research is done in a single pass per brand: search, verify, record, then move on. [L0937, 2021-22]

### Finding the official website

- For an exact brand name, the first organic Google result is often the official website. Always verify that it is the brand's own domain before copying it. [L0940, 2020-26]
- A Google search for a brand name returns its official site and often identifies its real domain, which is more reliable than guessing the URL. Skip ads at the top of results. [L0932, 2021-22]
- Choose the brand's own website over marketplaces and retail sites such as Walmart, Amazon, Houzz, Target or Wayfair. Those sites show who sells the products, not how to contact the supplier. [L0940, 2020-26]
- A marketplace or retailer listing is not the brand's website. A brand that only appears on marketplaces may still be reachable through other search variants. [L0940, 2020-26]
- Do not guess supplier website addresses from the brand name. Search the brand on Google first, because some brands use unexpected domains or share a name with unrelated companies. [L0932, 2021-22]
- Do not record a retailer's page instead of the brand's own site. [L0937, 2021-22]
- Confirm the company is the manufacturer or brand owner, not a retailer. [L0937, 2021-22]
- If a brand search returns many unrelated websites, add the niche keyword (for example 'kitchen') to the brand name and check the products section of the result. This narrows the results to the brand's own product website. [L0931, 2020-26]
- For a generic or shared brand name, add a niche word such as lighting, health or furniture. Adding a product word such as chandelier can also help separate the right result. [L0940, 2020-26]
- Do not dig deep into Google results pages. After the first few results they are mostly unrelated. Try a different search variant instead. [L0940, 2020-26]
- Copy the official website URL into the Supplier Website column and remove any tracking parameters or unnecessary URL elements. [L0056, undated]
- If a supplier's website does not load, leave its row blank for now and move on. Come back to it later rather than spending time on a broken site. [L0931, 2020-26]

### Finding the phone number

- Phone numbers are usually under a Contact link in the header menu. If the header has none, check the footer menu. [L0940, 2020-26]
- If a brand's header and footer have no Contact link, check the sidebar menu of its website for a Contact entry before concluding that no contact page exists. [L0940, 2020-26]
- Phone numbers can appear in odd formats or as international numbers. Check that the number has enough digits and reformat it to US style with hyphens (3-3-4 digits, e.g. 555-010-0199). [L0931, 2020-26]
- If the number shown is international, look for the contact page number instead. [L0931, 2020-26]
- If several phone numbers are listed (for example toll-free and local), they usually ring the same place, so any one of them can be used. [L0940, 2020-26]
- Record the supplier's main contact phone number in the Supplier Phone Number column. [L0056, undated]
- For a toll-free number, call it and ask for the person in charge of eCommerce accounts. Request their direct email address for future communication if you prefer to follow up that way. [L0133, undated]
- A supplier can have an email but no phone number. In that case enter N-A in the phone column and keep the email. [L0940, 2020-26]
- Blank versus N-A for a missing phone: the one source column rule says leave the cell blank if no number is listed [L0056, undated], while the step-by-step routine says enter N-A once all search variants have failed [L0940, 2020-26]. Use blank when the supplier lists no number at all and you are filling the column; use N-A only after the alternative searches have also failed.

### Finding the email address or contact form

- Take the supplier's email address from its contact page. [L0931, 2020-26]
- When both a direct email and a contact form are offered, prefer the direct email. [L0940, 2020-26]
- When a contact page lists several emails for different distribution centres (for example USA and Canada) or offices and they all share one address, use that address. Otherwise choose the entry that matches the market you sell in. [L0940, 2020-26]
- When a brand lists both a sales inquiries email and a parts inquiries email, use the sales inquiries address. [L0940, 2020-26]
- When a contact page lists several locations or departments, choose the e-commerce or online sales entry, since that is the contact point for online sellers. [L0940, 2020-26]
- Enter the published email address in the Supplier Email column. If none is published, enter the URL of the contact form instead. [L0056, undated]
- If a supplier has only a contact form and no direct email, paste the URL of the contact form into the email column. [L0940, 2020-26; L0931, 2020-26]
- Some suppliers have only a contact form for drop-shipping requests and no direct email. Enter that form in the email column so the supplier can still be contacted later. Record every supplier with a form this way. [L0931, 2020-26]
- Brand inbox addresses often follow the pattern info@[brand].com. Treat this only as a hint and confirm the address on the brand's website before recording it. [L0937, 2021-22]
- Do not guess generic dealer or vendor addresses such as dealers@ or vendors@ and assume they exist. The lesson's test with this kind of address did not work for the brand checked. Look for named dealer or vendor contacts on the contact page instead. [L0931, 2020-26]
- Many brands sell only through resellers and publish no public email address, because they do not want consumers emailing them or receiving spam. A website contact form or a phone number is then a valid route, so record it as the contact method. [L0937, 2021-22]
- Brands often do not sell directly to the public and prefer to supply resellers, so most will not publish a public email address. Missing public email is normal and is not a reason to drop a supplier. A phone number or contact form is enough to start. [L0932, 2021-22]
- Brand contact forms often ask for name, relation to the company, street address, city, state, ZIP code, email, whether the company may contact you, preferred contact method, phone number, topic, subject and message, usually with a CAPTCHA check. Fill these in only when you actually contact the supplier, not during research. [L0937, 2021-22]
- Form-label convention differs by source. one source's sheet uses the text 'Website contact form' in the Email column when there is no public email [L0937, 2021-22; L0932, 2021-22]. one source's sheet uses the form's URL [L0931, 2020-26; L0940, 2020-26]. Use the URL when you need to reach the supplier through that form later; use the label when the form is the only route and the URL is not needed.

**Examples:**

- **Airfree:** the brand was found on Google, its official site showed a phone number in the footer, and no public email existed. The row got the phone number, the URL, and 'Website Contact Form' in the Email column, since the brand's contact form was its only public email route. [L0932, 2021-22]
- **Aerus:** the official site had a contact form and a phone number at the bottom, so the row was completed with both, and the form was noted as the contact route. [L0932, 2021-22]
- **Coway/Airmega:** the contact page listed a support email, a toll-free number and opening hours, plus a separate business and retail contact section. The business and retail contact was recorded. [L0932, 2021-22; L0937, 2021-22]
- Some manufacturers publish a separate Business and Retail contact path next to their consumer support. Use that business channel for supplier and wholesale inquiries and record the business contact rather than the consumer support line. [L0937, 2021-22]

### Contact labels and routes

- Contact pages may use labels such as Contact Us, Customer Support, Customer Service, Dealer Contact or Get in Touch. Use the label that matches your goal, for example dealer or vendor pages when you want to become a dealer or vendor. [L0940, 2020-26]
- Brand websites often split contact routes by purpose, such as General Inquiry, Government and Volume orders, and Become a Dealer. Choose the route that matches your request. A dealer or wholesale request should go through the dealer page rather than a general contact form. [L0932, 2021-22]

### Dealer portals and application pages

- A footer link to a dealer portal with a 'request access' option means the brand has a dealer program. Speak to the supplier first, by phone or email, before filling in the request form. Record the phone number and email you find for it in the usual columns. [L0931, 2020-26]
- When a brand publishes a dealer or retailer application page, the brand is signalling the first step it wants resellers to take. Follow the process it describes when contacting it later. [L0932, 2021-22]
- If a brand offers an online application, use it as the first contact step, since putting it on the site signals that this is the route they want. If no application exists, that is normal and not a problem. [L0937, 2021-22]
- Dealer pages often name a specific route for applicants, such as a phone number for quick help, a form that a sales representative answers, and a postal address marked for dealer inquiries. Follow the route the page specifies rather than a generic contact method. [L0937, 2021-22]
- Example (Austin Air, one source 2021-22, older): the site had a 'Become an Austin Air Dealer' page with a phone line for immediate assistance and an online dealer application form. The application page URL was recorded in the Online Application column, and the phone number was noted for contact. [L0932, 2021-22]
- A wholesale-only online store that hides prices behind a dealer login is still a valid supplier. Use its vendor or dealer contact page, because prices only become visible after approval. [L0940, 2020-26]

**Conflict on first contact:** one source says to use the online application first if one exists [L0937, 2021-22]. one source says to speak to the supplier by phone or email before filling in a request form [L0931, 2020-26]. Both fit the research rule not to submit anything during research [L0940, 2020-26]. Use the application first where the page clearly names it as the route; use a phone or email call first where the supplier has a dealer-portal request form. After submitting, wait 24 hours and call if there is no response (page 14, "Online Application Forms: Submit, Wait 24 Hours, Then Call"). [L0235, 2021-22]

### Verifying the address and the seller

- Verify a listed address before you rule a seller out or in: [L0459, 2020-26]
  1. Copy the address from the footer or contact page.
  2. Search it on Google.
  3. Open Street View in Google Maps to see whether the building is a store, a warehouse, an office or a training site.
  - An office or a learning centre does not count as a physical store, but it also does not prove the seller is online only. [L0459, 2020-26]
- When a brand name is searched with the word ZoomInfo, the results can show a company profile for a different business with a similar name. Check that the profile's industry and location match the brand before copying any phone number or email from it. [L0940, 2020-26]
- Unrelated businesses can share a brand name, such as a bicycle shop, a home care company or a Facebook page. Do not accept the first search result for an ambiguous name without checking it. [L0940, 2020-26]

### When the first search fails

**Alternative search sequence, one source (when the brand's own site does not appear):** [L0940, 2020-26]

1. Brand name + niche + 'website' (for example Brand Name Furniture Website).
2. Brand name + niche only (for example Brand Name Lighting).
3. Brand name + customer service.
4. Brand name + contact number.
5. Brand name + Zoominfo (written as one word).

**Alternative search sequence, one source (order of combinations):** [L0116, undated]

1. [Brand Name] + Niche
2. [Brand Name] + Website
3. [Brand Name] + Customer Service
4. [Brand Name] + Phone Number
5. [Brand Name] + zoominfo

Both sequences cover the same search terms. Run them in the order given in the list you are working from; [L0940, 2020-26] is the sequence used in the per-supplier routine [L0940, 2020-26].

- Do not mark a supplier N-A after a single failed search. Enter N-A only after the alternative search variants have also failed. [L0940, 2020-26]
- Reverse image search: [L0801, 2021]
  1. Search the exact supplier company name on Google, putting the full name in quotation marks so only exact matches appear.
  2. If that fails, open a competitor's product page in Google Chrome.
  3. Right-click the product image and choose the option to search Google for the image.
  4. Review the pages that contain the same image, since they may show the supplier's website or identifying information.
  5. Use that information to find the supplier.
- A brand without its own website may be a sister brand of another supplier. If the parent supplier is approved later, it may allow you to sell the sister brand too, so keep the entry and recheck it later. [L0940, 2020-26]

### Reaching a person

**Outreach sequence, one source (2024):** [L0836, 2024]

1. Call suppliers by phone whenever a number is available, since this is the fastest way to reach the right person and get a yes or no.
2. If no phone number is listed, find contacts through LinkedIn by searching the company name.
3. If nothing works, message the company's Facebook page or Instagram account if it is active.
4. Start with the less well-known suppliers that rank lower on future competitor stores. They are easier to get approved with and help build phone confidence before calling the key brands.

- Avoid sending physical mail to suppliers to get contacts, because mail is unlikely to reach the right person. [L0836, 2024]
- Email is acceptable as a first contact method only when a supplier has not listed a phone number on their website. [L0235, 2021-22]
- When a supplier asks you to email them, ask for the name of the right person, their direct email and a phone number. Writing that person down lets you follow up with a specific contact instead of a general inbox. [L1285, 2024]
- If the first contact has outdated information: [L1287, 2025]
  1. Ask the supplier to confirm or provide a different contact person.
  2. If calls keep reaching the same person, look up the contact on LinkedIn and send a message.
  3. If the contact has a Facebook profile, send a Facebook message.
  4. Try email as well, and keep following up with the new contact.
