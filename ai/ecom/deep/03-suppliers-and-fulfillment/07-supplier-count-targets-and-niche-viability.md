## Supplier Count Targets and Niche Viability

### Why the Supplier Count Matters

- Multiple suppliers reduce business risk by avoiding dependence on one vendor, increase buyer confidence through brand variety, and build store credibility. Establish this foundation first, because a stronger product base makes conversion optimization work significantly more effective. [L0132, undated]
- Supplier depth is one of the two primary levers for growing revenue, so a dropshipping business should secure as many suppliers as possible within its chosen niche. [L0512, 2020-26]
- Supplier count matters because not every brand you contact will approve you. You need enough brands that some saying no still leaves enough products on the site for the market to be viable. [L1484, 2021-22]
- Multiple supply sources reduce risk, because a supplier can close, have an owner stroke, or move on to another project. [L0801, 2021]
- Expect the 80/20 pattern, where about 80 percent of sales come from about 20 percent of suppliers, so keep approaching many suppliers and following up over time. [L1113, 2022]

### Minimum Supplier Count per Niche

- Set a minimum of 20 suppliers per niche before committing to it. Supplier availability is checked before the product count, so the niche can still be widened if the count is short. [L0356, 2019]
- Before committing to a niche, confirm that at least 20 suppliers are available for it. [L1080, 2020-26]
- Before choosing a niche, identify at least 20 potential suppliers in your research. [L1113, 2022]
- Aim for at least 20 different brands or suppliers per niche. If a niche cannot reach 20, niche up a level and add related products to the range. [L0359, 2020]
- Build a master supplier list of at least 20 brands before outreach begins, then aim to become authorized with every viable brand on that list over the long term. [L0191, undated]
- Research at least 20 suppliers before starting outreach, because approval rates from day one are low for new stores. [L0928, 2020-26]
- Build a list of at least 20 different suppliers before you start calling. If one call goes badly, you still have 19 others to approach, so a weak first attempt does not end sourcing. [L0927, 2020-26]
- During niche research, find at least 20 different brands you could work with. Not all will approve on day one, which is acceptable. [L1279, 2021]
- For the first store, find at least 20 brands or suppliers. Many will not approve, some will have low margins or no MAP, and some may charge for access, so the extra count is needed for a working store. [L0967, 2020]
- Research should yield at least 20 suppliers, and ideally 50 to 100 or more, to work through. [L0938, 2020-26]
- Expect about 20 suppliers per niche as a minimum target, and as many suppliers as meet the criteria if there are more. [L0964, 2023]
- Plan to find at least 20 suppliers before launching a first store, so enough remain when many of them decline. [L1069, 2019]
- Find at least 20 potential brands per niche before launching, and confirm they sell through online-only stores without warehouses so the niche is drop-ship friendly. Brands that sell through distributors with warehouses usually do not drop ship. (2023) [L1094, 2023]
- Require at least 20 approved brands and at least 100 products per store as a minimum. Expect some suppliers to reject the application, so aim for a cushion. [L1111, 2020]
- A store needs at least 100 unique products from at least 20 different suppliers. Each supplier should contribute several products rather than just one. [L0663, 2019]
- Aim for at least 20 supplier candidates early, because some rejections are expected. That gives enough suppliers to launch, build traffic and make sales before the rest are approved. [L0469, 2021]
- Aim for a supplier count of at least 20 brands in a niche during research. [L0834, 2021]
- Contact at least 20 suppliers, because not every supplier approves a new seller on the first application. Approval standards have not become easier or harder over time. [L1088, 2020]
- Before testing a niche's competition, source at least 20 suppliers. More suppliers are always better, and 20 is the minimum for the supplier competition test. (2024) [L0971, 2024]
- Contact at least 20 suppliers to see the range of margin differences, because some will refuse and others will offer better margins. (2024) [L0666, 2024]
- Aim to get approved with at least 20 suppliers in a niche, then put paid ad budget only on products from suppliers that have real margins. [L0671, 2021]
- A competitor site listing 20 or more brands and at least 100 products shows enough variety, even if you do not recognize the brands. Use competitor catalogues to gauge this. (2023) [L0934, 2023]
- Working toward about 20 suppliers on your list runs alongside the first ads, so keep the list growing while traffic runs. (2023) [L0855, 2023]

### Market-Specific Minimums

one source's market thresholds are lower than one source's 20-supplier standard. Both values apply to different situations.

| Market | Minimum brands or suppliers | When it applies | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| USA | 20 or more | Standard US target; 20 is the threshold, 100 or more is fine | [L1484, 2021-22] |
| Australia | More than 10 for the niche; 18 is acceptable | Smaller market with fewer suppliers and customers, so lower competition | [L1484, 2021-22; L0490, 2021-22] |
| UK | 10 or more | Smaller market | [L1484, 2021-22] |
| Any other market outside the USA | 10 or more | Non-US markets generally | [L1484, 2021-22] |
| Smaller countries such as the UK, Australia or Canada  | At least 20 suppliers you can sell from before launching the niche | Standard target; fewer is acceptable only as a temporary stage | [L0935, 2022+] |

- For a US niche, 18 suppliers would be low; the same count is acceptable for Australia. [L0490, 2021-22]
- Smaller markets have fewer suppliers and fewer customers, so a lower brand threshold of 10 is accepted because competition is also lower. [L1484, 2021-22]
- Some operators have built stores abroad with well under 20 suppliers. This carries more risk and should be treated as an exception rather than a plan, so the 20-supplier target remains the standard even when it requires a broader niche. [L0935, 2022+]

### Market Size and Warehouse-Heavy Markets

- Supplier counts scale with population. The US, with roughly 330 to 350 million people, has far more suppliers per niche than smaller markets. Expect to find fewer suppliers for the same product in a small country and plan research accordingly. [L0935, 2022+]
- Aim for at least 20 suppliers that sell into the country where you do business. Finding 60 suppliers in the US is more than enough for a niche. [L0963, 2022]
- In markets where most suppliers keep a warehouse, expect lower approval rates. Contact a larger number of potential suppliers (for example 50 to 60 instead of 20) in the niche, and if too few suppliers exist, consider a broader niche to get enough products. [L0969, 2022]
- For a store in a country outside the US, the main constraint is usually supplier availability. Check whether enough suppliers exist locally or whether suppliers in another country will ship into it. [L0905, 2021-22]
- US suppliers shipping into Canada is a workable option, and Canadian suppliers are the ideal case because they avoid cross-border shipping. [L0905, 2021-22]

### Building the Candidate List and Expected Approval Rates

- Build the list of potential suppliers in this order:
  1. Research and compile a master supplier list for all potential suppliers in the niche. [L0115, undated]
  2. Complete the approval process with the first five suppliers. [L0115, undated]
- Keep the list growing, but limit time spent on research. The goal at this stage is enough suppliers to launch the store. More suppliers can be discovered later, sometimes when brands contact the seller after seeing it sell in their market. [L0932, 2021-22]
- Do not spend weeks perfecting the supplier list. At the start, you only need enough suppliers to stock the site so you can launch and begin growing sales. [L0937, 2021-22]
- Aim for at least 20 suppliers in the list before moving on to the next stage. Once the store is running, more suppliers usually surface on their own, and some contact you after seeing you sell their products. [L0937, 2021-22]
- Approval rates to plan around:

| Reference point | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Typical first-stage approval | About 50 percent at first, improving over time, with at least 20 suppliers contacted | [L0803, 2022] |
| Decent approval including follow-ups | 50 percent is considered decent | [L0834, 2021] |
| New store calling suppliers | Just over 60 percent of suppliers called | [L0771, 2021-22] |
| Warning level | Approval rates well below about 5 percent signal a problem with the outreach method, not just bad luck | [L0834, 2021] |

- Approval is a numbers game. Expect many rejections, and document every "reach out again" answer to follow up in three to six months, sometimes a year. [L0834, 2021]
- Example from a bike, e-scooter and e-skateboard niche: a list of 30 brands was built from Google Shopping listings, and each brand was tracked with its phone, email and website. The owner needed only about 10 approved suppliers for a viable start. With about 60 percent approval expected from calls, that meant roughly 19 approvals from 31 suppliers. [L0771, 2021-22]
- A single approval is enough to start, so it is worth calling many suppliers rather than a few. [L0771, 2021-22]
- A new store may land about 10 suppliers in its first week when outreach is active. Over about six months, one partner added around 30 more suppliers for the first store. [L0832, 2019]
- Suppliers unfamiliar with dropshipping can take a year or more to approve a new seller. The first months of a store need enough runway to wait for approvals, so plan the launch and budget around slow approval rather than assuming the first application will succeed. [L1085, 2018]
- Approval is an ongoing process, so keep prospecting and following up while the first ads run. [L0664, 2020]
- Keep contacting suppliers while the store is built and running traffic. Store building and supplier outreach can run in parallel, so do not wait for a full supplier list before launching. [L0853, 2021]
- Do not stop supplier approvals when ads start. Get the first two suppliers live, switch on Google ads to create data and sales, and keep adding suppliers and products. [L0359, 2020]
- Apply for approval with every brand in the niche, including the lower-tier brands, and do not stop after the first one is approved. More approved brands give more products and more options when some are out of stock. [L0358, 2020]
- Get approved with every possible supplier in the niche, then identify standout brands by margin strength and willingness to enforce MAP. [L0249, 2022]
- Approvals from several brands, even ones that are not the top sellers, add to store credibility. A store with more brands also makes it easier to approach larger brands in the niche later. (2023) [L0470, 2023]
- Aim to get approved with every supplier and carry every available product in the niche over time. [L0852, 2020]
