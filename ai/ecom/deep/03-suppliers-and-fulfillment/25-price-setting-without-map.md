### Price setting without MAP

#### Procedure for suppliers without MAP

Procedure (undated) [L1146, 2020-26]:
1. Apply for approval with each supplier. [L1146, 2020-26]
2. After approval, receive the supplier's price list. [L1146, 2020-26]
3. Build your own price list by adding a third column for your sale price next to the wholesale cost and MSRP columns. [L1146, 2020-26]
4. Set the sale price using the 2x wholesale minimum, and go higher if competitors sell above that level. [L1146, 2020-26]
5. Review your paid advertising KPIs every week. [L1146, 2020-26]

Structure of your own price list (supplier lists usually contain only the first two columns) [L1146, 2020-26]:

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Wholesale cost | What you pay the supplier per unit (your cost of goods) |
| MSRP | Manufacturer's Suggested Retail Price, the selling price the manufacturer recommends |
| Sale price | Your own selling price, added by you because no MAP sets it outside MAP markets |

#### Check the starting price against competitors

- For non-MAP suppliers, set a starting price of roughly 2.1 times the wholesale cost, then compare with competitor prices on Google Shopping. Competitors may be selling at 3 times MAP, which would make a higher price the right choice. [L1111, 2020]
- Check the supplier's listed MAP-free wholesale prices and the prices two to three stores advertise them for [L1279, 2021]:
  1. Check the supplier's listed MAP-free wholesale prices and the prices two to three stores advertise them for. [L1279, 2021]
  2. If those stores sell at at least 2x wholesale, list the product at that price without undercutting them. [L1279, 2021]
  3. Exclude these products from your price match guarantee to avoid losses. [L1279, 2021]
- Products of the same type can be offered by different companies at very different price levels, for example about $700 from one company and about $3,500 from another. A store that combines both price levels for a high-end supplier did not face pushback from that supplier. [L1111, 2020]

#### Raise margin without negotiating

- Dropshipping an accessory from the same manufacturer you already use solves the logistics side, which makes upsells and downsells of existing brand accessories one of the easiest ways to raise margins in a store. [L1152, 2021-22]
- Case: a dog toy was sourced for 6 USD from suppliers while competitors sold it for 9.99 USD. Competitors likely either bought in bulk or used slow AliExpress fulfillment, and they accepted lower margins to win volume. A beginner should aim for higher margins to reinvest profits, so the choice was between raising the price or adding a shipping fee. [L1176, 2023]

### Negotiating better terms

#### Ask early and track volume

- Ask suppliers early about volume-based pricing tiers and incentive pricing. Revisit negotiation when a product shows no margin or when the store brings a large volume of sales to that supplier. [L1069, 2019]
- Once you have proven sales volume with a supplier, you can ask for better pricing terms. If margins do not work from day one, ask the supplier for better pricing at the start. [L1277, 2020]
- A processing fee of about $6 to $25 per order is uncommon. It is acceptable when the order value is high (for example $200 to $300), and it can be negotiated later. [L1069, 2019]

Template for volume-based pricing tiers [L1069, 2019]:

```
Hi [Supplier name],

We have just started selling your [brand or product line] on our website and want to grow this partnership. Do you have volume-based pricing tiers or discounts that unlock after a certain number of orders? Do you also have any incentive pricing we should know about for new retail partners?

Thank you,
[Your name]
[Company name]
```

Template for a margin-based request [L1069, 2019]:

```
Hi [Supplier name],

We have worked together for [time period] and we want the partnership to be profitable for both sides. Right now our profit margin on your products is only about [X]% after shipping. We have seen competitors [Competitor A, B, C] selling the same products at [price] with free shipping.

Are there better wholesale prices, better shipping rates or volume tiers we can access? Could you also review our invoices for discounts we might be missing? We would like to make these products work for everyone.

Thank you,
[Your name]
[Company name]
```

#### Show the competitor prices

Template for a request on a product with too little margin [L1068, 2019]:

```
Hi [CONTACT NAME], I was searching on Google and found [STORE 1], [STORE 2] and [STORE 3] selling [PRODUCT A], [PRODUCT B] and [PRODUCT C] at [COMPETITOR PRICE]. I checked our price list and our cost is [OUR COST] with shipping at [SHIPPING COST]. Is there any pricing incentive we could work on, such as a discount on the price list, a different tiered price list or better shipping rates? We would love to promote these products and bring in sales, but there is simply not enough margin with our current price list. Could we set up a short call to go over this? Thank you, [YOUR NAME], [COMPANY], [PHONE].
```

Use: send it as written, then show the supplier the competitor prices you found and let them propose options. [L1068, 2019]

Template for a product with negative net after shipping (2024) [L1285, 2024]:

```
Hi [Supplier contact name], we have recently been running our numbers. To run ads to your products profitably, we cannot sell at MAP and still keep a profit. Is there any incentive or deal you could offer on the wholesale price for [product or SKU names]? Thank you, [Your name], [Store name].
```

### Low-margin handling

#### Decide what to do with a thin product

Procedure when a supplier's margin is too low to fund ads (2023) [L1283, 2023]:
1. Ask the supplier if it has pricing incentives or different pricing tiers. [L1283, 2023]
2. If nothing changes, list the products on the store without running ads for them and rely on organic traffic. [L1283, 2023]
3. Only keep them if the margin is enough for the products to be worth the time. [L1283, 2023]

Procedure when a product has negative net after shipping (2024) [L1285, 2024]:
1. Ask the supplier for a better wholesale price. [L1285, 2024]
2. If the supplier says this is their best price, exclude that product from paid ads. [L1285, 2024]
3. Add other suppliers who can offer better margins so the budget goes to winning products. [L1285, 2024]

Procedure when a supplier's product is priced below your margin needs (2021) [L1112, 2021]:
1. Ask for better wholesale pricing first. [L1112, 2021]
2. If they refuse and the margin is too thin for ads, keep the product at MAP and rely on organic traffic without including it in Shopping, or drop it. [L1112, 2021]

- If a supplier gives a pricing incentive, the question is whether margins support ad spend. If a supplier refuses any change in price structure and competitors list at a 10 percent markup over the supplier price, it is often not worth listing. (2023) [L1283, 2023]
- For a very poor supplier where little margin remains, either skip the products entirely or list them above MAP with no paid ads. Paid ads should be reserved for products with enough margin to cover the ad cost. [L1111, 2020]
- Case: a seller had large, heavy products whose shipping cost wiped out the margin at MAP. The case and fix are in the shipping section above. [L1085, 2018]

### Ads or free traffic

- If the remaining margin covers a sale at MAP but not paid ads, keep the products listed and exclude them from Google Ads. They can still earn free Google product listing and organic traffic. [L1111, 2020]
- Reserve paid ads for products with enough margin to cover the ad cost. [L1111, 2020]
- Before you run ads on a product, confirm that the target price covers the average cost per purchase. For TikTok ads, the source sets 3x landed cost as the minimum sale price. [L1183, undated]
- Target product margin before running ads, so that sales are profitable and scalable. [L1259, undated]
- When the supplier's margin is too low, the order of action is: ask the supplier for incentives, then list without ads for organic traffic, then keep only if the margin is worth the time. [L1283, 2023]
- If MAP enforcement fails after escalating up the supplier's chain of command, stop paid ads for that brand. Keep the products on the store for organic traffic only if the MAP policy allows it. [L1112, 2021]
- If a product has negative net after shipping and the supplier cannot improve the price, exclude it from paid ads and move the budget to suppliers with better margins. [L1285, 2024]
- Before pausing ads on a product whose competitor is violating MAP, search the competitor's name online and read the reviews. If reviews show poor customer service, late shipping or payment problems, the violator is likely to lose customers and it can be worth keeping ads running. (2023) [L1094, 2023]

### Direct-to-consumer undercutting and competitor prices

- Supplier sales and temporary discounts can be listed on the store, and there is no need to worry about undercutting other suppliers. Contact brands around the holidays to ask if they will offer deals, and the store can promote those discounts with graphics and emails. (2023) [L1114, 2023]
- Clearance items are typically exempt from MAP compliance. A competitor selling clearance stock below MAP is therefore not automatically a violation. [L1275, 2019]
- If a supplier starts selling its best-selling products below your dealer cost or discounts them heavily on its own site, pause those products and do not spend ad money on them until the supplier explains and corrects the pricing. [L1090, 2021]
- Some suppliers sell at a price so close to cost that a MAP violator cannot sustain it. Such retailers tend to get bad reputations, so their reviews and customer complaints should be checked before reacting to their price. (2023) [L1094, 2023]
- A retailer that sells below wholesale price is very likely a scam or burner store that never ships orders. Do not match such prices, and build trust with your own store through reviews instead. (2024) [L1115, 2024]

### MAP violation monitoring and reporting

#### Find the violation

- Testing whether a competitor violates MAP on Google Shopping (2023) [L1094, 2023]:
  1. Search the product on Google Shopping. [L1094, 2023]
  2. Read the prices shown by the listings, including the free listings from three stores. [L1094, 2023]
  3. If most listings show one price and one or two show a much lower price, the most common price is probably MAP and the lower one is a violation. [L1094, 2023]
- Search the brand name and SKU in Google to find competitors who are selling below the minimum advertised price. Copy the links to the competitor listings. (undated) [L1472, 2022]
- Expect manufacturers to use software to monitor MAP compliance, and note that large retailers are less often penalized than smaller ones. (undated) [L1472, 2022]
- A distribution center that warns you about not updating prices on time is a good sign, because it means they care about you maximizing profit. Keep product prices current in your store to avoid these warnings. (undated) [L1472, 2022]

#### Report the violation

- When a supplier's MAP is known and a competitor sells below it, report the violation to the supplier right away with a screenshot. Ask whether the competitor has a special deal or whether the supplier will contact them. Good suppliers enforce MAP and may remove an unauthorized retailer as an authorized dealer. (2024) [L1115, 2024]
- Handling a MAP violation by a competitor (2021) [L1090, 2021]:
  1. Screenshot the competitor's product page showing the low price. [L1090, 2021]
  2. Search the product in Google Shopping and screenshot the listing showing the same lower price. [L1090, 2021]
  3. Email the supplier contact with the competitor URL, both screenshots and a request to correct the pricing. [L1090, 2021]
  4. If the price is not updated within a few days, reply to the same thread. Most suppliers fix pricing quickly. Amazon and Walmart listings below MAP can be reported to the supplier the same way. [L1090, 2021]
- Reporting procedure [L1275, 2019]:
  1. Report every MAP violator to the supplier. [L1275, 2019]
  2. Follow up with the supplier regularly. [L1275, 2019]
  3. If nothing changes after about one to two months, stop selling that supplier's products and stop spending ad budget on them. [L1275, 2019]
  4. Take screenshots of competitors selling below your cost, send them to the supplier, and ask whether a better pricing tier or incentive is available. [L1275, 2019]
- Send the supplier screenshots of the Google Shopping listing and the competitor's website. A good supplier enforces MAP and responds within the number of days in the MAP policy. If nothing changes, stop paid ads for that brand, keep listings at MAP for organic traffic, or remove the supplier. [L1112, 2021]
- Send the supplier links to the competitor listings. The supplier can then contact the violating retailer through its representatives. (undated) [L1472, 2022]

#### Templates for MAP violation emails

Template for the first report [L1090, 2021]:

```
Subject: MAP violation for [product name]. Hi [supplier contact name], we noticed that [competitor website] is currently selling [product name] for [price], which is below your MAP policy. Here is the product page link: [competitor product URL]. I have attached a screenshot of the product page and a screenshot of the same listing in Google Shopping. Could you please contact them and ask them to update their pricing? Thank you, [your name]
```

Template for the follow-up [L1090, 2021]:

```
Subject: Follow-up on MAP violation for [product name]. Hi [supplier contact name], I am following up on my email from [date] about [competitor website] selling [product name] below MAP. The price has not changed yet. Could you please check on this again? Thank you, [your name]
```

Template for asking about wholesale cost changes [L1090, 2021]:

```
Subject: Question about wholesale costs. Hi [supplier contact name], I noticed on your website that [product 1] is listed at [price 1] and [product 2] at [price 2]. Is there an update to our wholesale costs that I should know about? I would appreciate a quick reply so I can plan my pricing. Thank you, [your name]
```

Template for a follow-up when a competitor is below MAP and the supplier has a special deal question (2024) [L1115, 2024]:

```
Hi [supplier contact name], thanks for sending the link to [competitor store URL]. I checked the site and it looks good. I noticed that the MAP on [product name or SKU] is [MAP price], but they are listing it for [competitor listed price]. Could you let me know whether they have a special deal, or whether you will reach out to them to adjust their pricing? I have attached a screenshot for reference. Thank you, [your name]
```

#### Escalation and when to stop

- If MAP enforcement fails after escalating up the supplier's chain of command, stop paid ads for that brand. Keep the products on the store for organic traffic only if the MAP policy allows it. [L1112, 2021]
- Sources differ on timing. The 2019 procedure stops selling after about one to two months without change [L1275, 2019]. The 2021 procedure responds to the number of days in the MAP policy and then stops paid ads [L1112, 2021]. Use the timing in the supplier's MAP policy where it exists, and the one to two month limit where it does not. [L1275, 2019; L1112, 2021]

### Supplier invoices and cash reserve

- Not tracking exactly what is owed to suppliers for each order leads to unexpected supplier bills. Allocating a COGS percentage of every sale into a dedicated account gives a reserve that covers these invoices. [L1200, 2021-22]
- Case: a high-ticket dropshipper had just started out and was beginning to see sales and profit rise, believing the business was close to breaking even. After several months, a supplier sent an unexpected bill of 10,000 to 15,000 dollars. The dropshipper had believed all supplier orders were paid, but they were not, so the owner had to borrow money from friends and family to cover the bill. [L1200, 2021-22]
- Allocate the COGS percentage from [L1200, 2021-22] into a dedicated account from each sale, so that supplier invoices are covered. [L1200, 2021-22]
