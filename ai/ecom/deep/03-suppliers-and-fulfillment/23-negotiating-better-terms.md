### Negotiating better terms

**Timing: when negotiation works**

- In the early stage, with minimal sales history, you have little negotiating power with suppliers for better rates or terms. Focus optimisation on conversions and product selection. Negotiate more heavily once you have proven sales volume. [L0208, 2024+]
- Wholesale cost is usually not negotiable for a brand-new retailer, even one with a track record in other businesses. Suppliers typically want proof of sales first. Wholesale cost becomes negotiable as your sales volume with that supplier grows. [L0939, 2020-26]
- Once a relationship is established, a supplier may give about 10% off wholesale cost. Some suppliers also offer special holiday pricing so the retailer can sell more of their products. [L0939, 2020-26]
- Demand comes before cost. Build consistent demand first, for example 20 sales per day consistently and later 50 per day. Inconsistent demand is a bigger problem than supplier cost at an early stage. [L0644, undated]
- Validate first, then negotiate once you know the product will scale. Keep a second backup supplier so you can justify the price. [L0780, 2024+]

**Basic sourcing sequence**

1. Start with CJ Dropshipping for convenience, since it is easy to set up and offers acceptable prices and shipping.
2. Build consistent demand first, for example 20 sales per day consistently and later 50 per day.
3. Once demand is steady, contact other suppliers, state your sales volume and ask whether they can beat your current price.
4. Move the business only if the new supplier matches or beats the price and service.

[L0644, undated]

**Negotiation procedures**

1. Ask your current supplier, such as CJ, whether they can lower the price.
2. Request quotes from several other suppliers for the same product.
3. Compare the quotes and choose the supplier that gives the best margin and delivery terms.

[L0661, undated]

- In the shoe example, fulfilment cost per pair was about $13 to $15 after negotiation. That cost still left a healthy margin on a selling price of $40. [L0661, undated]
- A jewelry product with a fulfilment cost of about $7 per item allowed a selling price of $30 with a comfortable margin. The margin came from low cost of goods rather than from a high selling price. [L0661, undated]
- Check your supplier's best price rather than accepting the first offer, and ask whether the price can go lower once you have scale. [L0780, 2024+]

**Negotiation procedure with a volume threshold**

1. Obtain the supplier's price list showing wholesale and MAP.
2. If MAP is not about double the wholesale price, state that the price must be at that level to sell and that otherwise the brand will not be sold.
3. Ask immediately whether the supplier offers volume-based pricing incentives, for example a discount once a monthly dollar or unit threshold is reached.
4. Track monthly volume and follow up for the discount once the threshold is reached.

[L0967, 2020]

**Incentive pricing after approval**

1. Once you are approved, get their price list and upload their products right away.
2. Ask whether they offer incentive pricing for selling a set number of units per month or for reaching revenue targets.
3. Ask whether they give discounts off wholesale prices for your account.
4. Before the busy season (Black Friday, Cyber Monday through Christmas), contact them again to negotiate special seasonal pricing.

**Requesting better margins**

- Ask suppliers for a tiered retailer discount structure early in the process. Standard options include a percentage off wholesale or discounts at set sales volumes. [L0934, 2023]
- Ask whether the supplier offers tiered retailer pricing, such as 20% off wholesale. Ask for discounts at set sales volumes, such as a number of units or sales. Decide to list or advertise the products only after you see the final margins. [L0934, 2023]
- A supplier can be asked for better margins with a specific framing: say you want to promote their products and spend more on ads, and ask about incentive pricing. Start with friendly questions rather than an immediate request for discounts. [L0835, 2023]
- Before running ads on a supplier offering a 15-20% discount off retail, ask for better margins first. Some stores have negotiated better margins with suppliers, so a low spread can sometimes be improved. [L0666, 2024]
- When a supplier adds a drop-ship fee, shipping and taxes that erode your margin, negotiate first. If prices cannot change, exclude that supplier from paid ads, because organic sales still earn margin. Remove the supplier if the margin is still too small. [L0469, 2021]
- Ask whether a supplier offers a value-for-volume deal. Ask for better wholesale prices, incentive or volume programmes, and whether a retailer hitting a set number of units or sales unlocks lower wholesale prices. [L0970, 2023]
- Add the question to your early calls: ask suppliers for their tiered retailer discount structure, including volume discounts. Ask for 5 or 10 percent off at set sales levels. [L0359, 2020]

**Excess stock and special offers**

- Suppliers often hold excess or discontinued stock they want to clear quickly, and offer discounts of up to 40% for sellers who promote it. A store that already has traffic can start moving that stock almost immediately by featuring the products or running new ads on them. A brick-and-mortar retailer must place a bulk order, wait for shelf space and then handle the stock before it can sell. [L0929, 2021-22]
- When a service provider such as a finisher or waterproofing shop has to market its services itself, you can ask for a discount if you can line up a steady group of customers who need the same service. The provider accepts a lower price because you reduce its acquisition effort and time. [L0701, 2015]

**Using the list of suppliers as leverage**

- When competitors sell the same product for less than your supplier price list allows, they most likely have better supplier pricing or better shipping rates. They are not usually selling at a loss. [L1068, 2019]
- Ask suppliers about the product's price when a competitor is undercutting you. Possible answers include a cheaper carrier or a lower wholesale tier. [L1002, 2019]
- Email the supplier with your wholesale price, the competitor's price with free shipping and your shipping cost. Ask whether they can offer better pricing or cheaper shipping. [L1002, 2019]

Raising prices above MAP, adding a large shipping fee or dropping the brand were rejected, because sales would fall and ad spend would be wasted. [L0852, 2020]

### Low-margin handling

**Options when the spread is too thin**

1. Contact the supplier to negotiate better wholesale pricing.
2. If the margin is still acceptable but ad spend is unaffordable, keep the product and rely on organic traffic only.
3. Otherwise drop the product.

[L0665, 2022]

- Low-margin products can still be listed without being run in paid campaigns. Keep them in free Google product listings for organic traffic, and keep paid campaigns focused on products with the largest spread. [L0666, 2024]
- If a supplier's margin is too thin, either do not list its products or list them without including them in any paid ads, so only free traffic reaches them. [L0671, 2021]
- Products with too small a margin to advertise can still be listed on the store and in the free listings section of Google Shopping, without any ad budget. [L0836, 2024]
- Spend paid traffic only on products from suppliers with healthy margins. Send the full catalogue to free Google product listings, and add suppliers to paid campaigns only after their margins are proven. [L0968, 2021]
- Non-MAP suppliers: do not run Google Shopping ads for products where competitors undercut your price on every listing. Invest in free traffic sources instead, and do not expose the products to price-match guarantees. [L0969, 2022]
- When margins are too thin, ask suppliers whether they have incentive or volume programmes that lower wholesale prices once a retailer hits a set number of units or sales volume. If the answer is no, decide whether to list the brand with no ad spend or skip it entirely. [L0970, 2023]
- Do not match prices of non-MAP competitors when no margin is available. If the supplier enforces MAP, ask it to contact the competitor. Otherwise keep your price where you can make money and do not run Google Shopping ads on those products. [L0834, 2021]
- Do not sell a product at a price below MAP through advertising. [L0963, 2022]
- If a supplier that does not enforce MAP sells the same product elsewhere, check it has no price-match policy before approaching. If margins are too thin, exclude those products from paid ads and rely on free Google Shopping listings. [L0941, 2022]

**Thin margin: a shipping example**

1. Calculate the margin: retail price minus wholesale cost minus shipping. Example: $699 - $530 = $169, but $200 shipping would leave a loss.
2. Email the supplier with your wholesale price, the competitor's price with free shipping and your shipping cost, and ask whether they can offer better pricing or cheaper shipping.
3. If the supplier refuses, still list the product if people search for it, but exclude it from paid traffic.
4. Price the product with your own margin, and exclude it from your price-match guarantee.
5. Add perceived value with bonuses and shipping upgrades, such as white-glove delivery.

[L1002, 2019]

- A supplier's cost should be checked against its MAP before it is listed. A thin spread should be reconsidered or negotiated (see the 2x and 50% benchmarks above). [L0665, 2022]
- If a supplier adds a drop-ship fee or tax that erodes margin, first negotiate. If prices cannot change, keep the product on free traffic only. [L0469, 2021]
- Do not sell at very low margins, and do not advertise products whose spread has become too thin. [L0835, 2023]

### Ad versus free-traffic decisions

**Paid versus free listing by margin**

- Spend paid traffic only on products from suppliers with healthy margins. Send the full catalogue to free Google product listings. [L0968, 2021]
- Keep the paid campaigns focused on products with the largest spread. Products with thin spreads stay in free listings. [L0666, 2024]
- Products from suppliers with weak sales can remain in free Google Shopping listings if they still show margin, so they earn traffic without ad spend. [L0967, 2020]
- Keep products with thin margins in free listings only, so that organic traffic can still reach them. [L0836, 2024]

**Checking whether a supplier's product is working**

- When a supplier's products get more than 100 unique visitors from paid ads without any sale, shoppers are most likely buying from the supplier directly. Check unique visitors per supplier product before deciding whether to keep advertising it. (2023.) [L0934, 2023]
- If big retailers ignore a supplier's MAP enforcement, check that supplier's SKUs one by one. Advertise only products where big retailers are not selling at a lower price. Filtering this way keeps ad spend on products you can actually sell at MAP. (2023.) [L0934, 2023]

**Products that do not sell**

1. First optimise the product pages and the ads for those products.
2. If they still do not sell, lower the target cost per click for them in Google Shopping, so they still show but receive less budget.
3. If that fails, remove them from the paid alpha and beta campaigns and let them sell through free Google product listings and organic search.

[L0854, 2022]

**Competitor price-cutting in paid campaigns**

1. Take screenshots of the violating listings and send them to your supplier.
2. Watch how the supplier responds. A supplier that acts quickly is the first step.
3. If the supplier is not helpful, check how your ads convert for those specific products.
4. If they do not convert and the spend looks like unfair competition, drastically lower the bids for those products rather than removing them from the feed.

[L0470, 2023] (2023.)

- Stop paid ads for a supplier's products when the supplier's margin does not hold after shipping and ad costs. Keep the products on the store for organic traffic. [L0807, 2025]

**Ad spend after a supplier discount**

- A supplier discount of 30-35% is not enough if you still pay shipping and must fund customer acquisition. Ask suppliers for better margins, check whether they offer pricing tiers, and review their terms, exclusivity and pricing before deciding which products to fund with paid traffic. (2025.) [L0807, 2025]
- Products where a 2x MAP leaves room are run in ads only if the remaining profit covers acquisition. A 2x MAP at 10% customer acquisition still needs the rest of the costs covered. [L0268, 2020]

**Ad decision by supplier type**

- Suppliers that sell direct to consumers and undercut your price while running ads are hard to beat. Keep them on the store without ads and rely on organic traffic, while advertising products where your store adds value. (2023.) [L0855, 2023]
- For a product where competitors undercut your price, bid lower and run no ads on those products instead of matching their price. [L0854, 2022]

### Direct-to-consumer undercutting

- A brand selling direct to consumers is a good partner when it sells at MSRP and lets retailers sell at MAP. Avoid brands that sell at MAP themselves or below MAP, because this removes your price advantage. [L0961, 2020]
- If a brand sells direct at MAP, look for a unique value proposition, such as better presentation, trust or customer experience. Skip the brand if its own site already sells well and it expects you to match or beat its price. [L0961, 2020]
- If a supplier sells direct to consumers at the same price and advertises in the same channels as you, stop paid ads to its product pages. Keep those products only in free Google Shopping listings, where organic traffic can still arrive. (2023.) [L0934, 2023]
- Once an approved supplier is strong direct-to-consumer, keep it on your store but stop spending on it:

  1. Remove its products from the Google Shopping product feed.
  2. Keep the products live for organic traffic.
  3. Put ad budget into products from suppliers you have not yet onboarded.

  [L0804, 2023] (2023.)

- If a supplier sells direct and runs its own paid ads, do not include its products in your paid ads. Listing them can still add credibility. (2025.) [L0837, 2025]

### Price-match guarantees, coupons and advertised price

- Coupon codes that reduce price below MAP are generally acceptable, because MAP is the minimum advertised price, not the minimum selling price. Check individual supplier agreements for language forbidding promotional codes. [L0249, 2022]
- The advertised price, meaning the price shown in ads and emails, must not go below MAP. A coupon code applied at checkout can lower the price the customer pays, because the advertised price stays at MAP. Product-specific discounts that only apply at checkout are generally allowed unless a supplier forbids them. (2023.) [L0964, 2023]
- Clear coupon mechanisms that only apply at checkout are the only standard exception to not selling below MAP through advertising. [L0963, 2022]
- MAP is a minimum advertised price, so you may advertise a product above it but not below it. A retailer below MAP is in violation of the supplier's policy. (2025.) [L0837, 2025]
- Ask whether the supplier has a MAP policy after approval. If it does not, the site should not carry a price guarantee, because it can only be honoured when MAP applies. [L0466, 2018]
- Do not offer a price-match guarantee on a supplier's product pages if that supplier does not enforce a minimum advertised price. Otherwise a competitor willing to sell at a loss can force you to match and lose money on every sale. [L0664, 2020]
- Do not work through a supplier's MAP with a price-match guarantee if the supplier does not use MAP. A price guarantee promises to match a lower price from an authorised retailer, which only fits MAP-enforced products. [L0853, 2021]
- Products from non-MAP suppliers do not require price-match policies. (2025.) [L0255, 2025]
- Before approaching a non-MAP supplier, confirm that it has no price-match policy. [L0941, 2022]
