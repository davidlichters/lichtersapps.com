## 02. Niche and Product Research

### Product Research V3 with Claude (Lesson 031), claude-ai-fuer-produktsuche-mit-calodata-nutzen-566db.md

- [08:31] After Claude names a winning title, search it in Kalodata, open it, and create several variations of the book (the coach tested 6 to 8 variations; only 7 were available), put them on the Shopify product page, launch the ads and watch demand for the next 2 to 3 days.
- [02:16] The coach started the screenshot run at page 20 of the Kalodata ranking; the full ranking in the demo had 42 pages, and he only did 6 to save time.

### Case Study: cozy socks pump and dump (Lesson 036), case-study-winning-pump-and-dump-mit-cozy-socks-9a06d.md

- [02:41] The competitor also ran some type of subscription on the socks; on a seasonal product that brings a lot of chargebacks, because when it gets hot nobody wants more socks arriving.
- [04:40] The coach's ads mixed and matched clips: for example a video of someone surprised by the product switching over to B-roll, several clips mashed together to fit the fall vibe. His conversion rate was "crazy".
- [05:03] On the competitor's ad list, ads with a low impression count are the losers; the winner ads simply show the product. One probable winner only shows the socks with the text "cozy socks".
- [06:38] Visual products like this go straight to the product page: no listicle, no advertorial, nothing to explain (unlike supplements).
- [07:51] Seasonality can be flipped by hemisphere: sell it in Australia when it is cold there while it is hot in the US.
- [09:14] Beginner mistake: trying to brand a pump and dump. At most make it look like a semi-brand; the competitor did not even put a brand name on the store, just name, images, FAQ, pictures, videos and a simple offer.
- [10:18] "I never look at the ad library when I test unless it's something I just want to take inspo from"; do not stress about saturation on a broad product. (This differs from the site's rule to re-check active ads in Meta Ad Library before testing a shared-list product.)
- [10:48] "If you treat pump and dump like a forever brand you'll get stuck": come in, get your money, leave, reinvest and level up to a long-term brand. Once you master this, building real brands is "100 times easier"; the coach would start here rather than with supplements.

### Ad and Competitor Research (konkurrenz-und-anzeigenforschung-mit-kalodata-c5336.md, lesson 038)

Tool shown is WinningHunter (the file name says Kalodata).

- [00:27] "Find Shopify competitors with AI" works by reverse image search over saved images of almost every Shopify store. Example result: a similar product sold by a Hong Kong shop with 300k monthly visits ("a pretty good shop").
- [01:34] Rip check rule: if nobody ripped the product, "you're basically in the clear to test it"; if too many people ripped it or are running it right now, skip it and save $20 to $100 in ad spend. A branded product is "something you wouldn't rip".
- [02:07] The Brands feature is compared to Atria ("basically the same").
- [07:35] On the TikTok Shop section, all videos can be downloaded with one button.
- [09:19] Ad library filters also allow filtering out by monthly visits in one go and by niche (for example only supplements).
- [09:49] All-in-one positioning: Brands replaces Atria, Meta Ad Library is their own, TikTok Shop replaces Kalodata, the store tracker replaces Shop Hunter ("nobody uses it really anymore"); Magic AI is described as unique.

### How to validate a product, Winning Hunter (produkt-validierung-mit-brand-tracker-und-store-analyse-ce25c.md)

- [02:47-04:27] Reading the 1 to 3 star Trustpilot reviews of the example brand: most complaints were about the cancellation system. Conclusion in the lesson: the product is not the main issue, it is a service issue and a "subscription trap" (the product page does not clearly say the product is on subscription, so buyers do not know they subscribed). Treat this kind of gap as an opening: compete with an honest, clearly disclosed subscription and better service.

### Prod Research V2, Kalodata books (produktforschung-mit-calo-data-gewinnbare-buecher-finden-28ca0.md)

- [06:59-07:32] A book at about $10 is hard to scale, because the ad cost to acquire a customer needs a higher order value; aim for a slightly higher average unit price.
- [14:19] For a thick, heavy book, if the cost is about $20 including shipping to the US, the AOV has to be set "way higher" to stay profitable.

### Product research V1: health winners (produktrecherche-v1-gewinner-produkte-im-health-segment-43191.md)

- [08:44-09:12] Speed versus customizing: if you want speed, test the ripped product first and customize once it works; the course still recommends customizing from the start, which delays launch by about one or two weeks compared with ripping a product 1:1.

### Right Keywords To Find Competitors (richtige-keywords-zum-konkurrenten-sourcing-2a54e.md)

- [00:46] Wrong method: copy and paste the full product title from the store into the Ad Library search, "which seriously is not going to work". The Ad Library searches words spoken in the video.
- [02:26] Example of searching the product name directly: "peel shot", the product name mentioned in the ads, returned far more competitor ads than the side keyword.
- [02:58] Example with no unique keyword in the ad: for a "yerba mate instant tea powder" product, search the plain product type "yerba mate", which is certainly mentioned in the video. This surfaced competitors immediately (for example a "nature zempic" store).

### Must Need Shopify Apps (Lesson 042), shopify-apps-fuer-store-grundausruestung-211d6.md
