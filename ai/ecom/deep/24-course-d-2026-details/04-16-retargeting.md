## 16. Retargeting

### Call Walk-Through, BOF ads (Lesson 085), bottom-of-funnel-anzeigen-billig-testen-und-mit-videos-skali-95cc3.md

- [01:02] A super unique solution to a problem no one is aware of will not work as BOF from the start: ad relevance is very low, so you get high CPMs, high CPCs and no buyers.
- [01:49] BOF CPMs depend on niche: often cheap, but in supplements they can still be quite high.
- [04:37] The coach also counts as BOF warm audience: people who have researched the same solution from other brands but have not bought yet (for example someone researching phone cases for a few days).
- [05:33] Nothing to set up for audiences: put videos in one campaign and BOF image ads in another; Facebook shows the BOF images to people when it deems they are likely to buy. Manual audience buckets (site visitors, video viewers campaigns) were relevant 5 to 7 years ago, not now.
- [07:01] If BOF ads sell a product whose Kalo rips were too saturated, that validation can justify making custom video ads with AI.
- [12:32] When videos run alongside BOF, the BOF ads fatigue much more slowly. When you test with BOF alone, your entire audience runs through the BOF ads, so they fatigue quickly and need new ads regularly.
- [13:36] With a strong video campaign you do not need to refresh the BOF campaign often. When you do add new ads, always put them in a new ad set with 4 to 8 ads.
- [14:33] Without BOF, customers who come out of the bottom of the cold funnel have nothing retargeting them, so you lose customers and money.
- [14:58] The coach runs no middle-of-funnel ads: only videos (cold) and images (BOF).
- [16:32] On a straightforward product, a BOF test can be up and running within about 24 hours. For a more niche product (for example a really specific supplement), start with strong videos first.
- [17:35] "It takes one BOF ad to just make you a profit"; if you are already profitable, everyone should be trying BOF.
- [18:29] "Ugly ads print": weird-looking ads can win because they make more people stop scrolling.
- [19:00] The coach's own BOF layout: headline at the top, product in the middle, benefits, and maybe a 50% discount. He never includes the price in the ad. Instead of a price, use for example "50% off today only" and "Claim yours".
- [20:27] Benefit ad pattern he would run: headline at the top, benefits listed, scarcity with "20% off", no price. A viewer who saw a video explaining the product and sees this image about two days later often just buys.
- [20:54] The Canva re-download step applies to all AI image tools (Higgsfield was asked about); it costs about two extra minutes and may lower CPM, so there is no downside.
- [21:20] There is no threshold before using BOF: your very first ads can be BOF, for example launch 8 Kalo rips and a BOF campaign at the same time. BOF ads are uploaded as images.
- [24:02] Meta settings for BOF: just a CBO, same format and settings as the Kalo rips, with more emphasis on a decent primary text. Prompt: "Hey, based on my customer research, make me a primary text for a bottom of funnel ad." Or copy a competitor's primary text into Claude and ask "Make a variation of this."
- [26:33] Migraine-cap ad set the coach shared: review-style ads, a mixed ad (headline, three benefits, plus "50K relieved and relaxed users" with five stars and "click below to get natural relief 25% off" as CTA), and a "drug-free migraine relief, proudly Aussie owned" headline for buyers who care where they buy from.
- [27:02] There is no course BOF prompt. Find a good-looking competitor BOF ad in any niche (Facebook Ad Library, Kalodata or Winning Hunter), drop it into Claude and ask: what's good about this ad, why is it working, make me a prompt so I can make ads like this for my niche.
- [27:25] Custom books BOF test: started at 20 AUD per day (about 15 USD) and gave it 5, 6 or 7 days to bring in data slowly as a very controlled test. "Generally speaking, the lower your budget, the higher the ROAS."
- [28:30] Books version of the RYZE supplement ad: put the book where the supplement is, replace the ingredient arrows with benefit arrows (why you need this book), and replace "No more sleepless nights" with a headline about the problem the book solves.
- [28:55] Scaling a winning BOF ad: not to the level of the ripped Kalo ads; maybe about 30% of what the videos spend (not a fixed rule). Example: breakeven ROAS 1.5, comfortable running at 2.5 ROAS; if scaling drops it to 2 ROAS, scale back a bit. Do not overscale.
- [29:56] While videos keep working and are not fatiguing, the BOF audience stays refilled, so keep the BOF budget fairly static, provided new ads come into the account weekly.
- [33:58] Side BOF test next to the Kalo rips: at $50 a day give it 1 to 2 days and analyse; if budget is tight, run a $15 a day BOF campaign alongside the videos. Natives never on day one, only from week 2 if profitable. Kalo rips are the priority because they are the validation phase.
- [36:07] Do not try to run the ice cap (migraine cap) product: the coach tried it twice at different points of his e-com career and it completely flopped.
- [37:25] BOF can "scoop up" customers that other brands' video ads warmed up to the same solution but who did not buy from them ("they did half the work for you").
- [39:35] When ripped ads at $50 are stuck: first add new ads and keep the budget the same, let them spend a couple of days. "If you're not profitable at $50, what's the point in de-scaling?" Adding beats reducing; if you keep reducing you are in survival mode. With a correct strategy, new ads should hit winners about 20 to 30% of the time.
- [43:44] "Ads are like 95% of the game" for scaling; about 90% of the call's problems are ads. The coach's main brand plateaued for two years on mostly image ads; in the last 30 days, after introducing AI UGC, monthly revenue went 5X.
- [44:50] Rips from Kalo can sometimes carry you to about 10K a day, but eventually there are no more rips and you need your own ideas; BOF is an easy way to generate winning ads without much video time. "The difference between $200 a day and $300 a day could be BOF ads."

### BOF PDF Guide (Lesson 086), bottom-of-funnel-ads-warm-audiences-ernten-378d9.md

- Urgency concept wording in the PDF is "Only 43 left in stock. Sale ends Sunday at midnight." (the site quotes "Only 45").
- The ceiling rule, verbatim: "For solution-aware products (phone case, posture belt, water bottle) you can run BOF alone for years and stay profitable. But small audience means small budget means real scale ceiling. BOF harvests. It doesn't build to $1M months."
- Six takeaways as listed: 01 Separate campaign from cold, 02 One ad set per concept, 03 4–8 ads per concept, 04 Video feeds the pool, 05 Best for cheap tests, 06 Harvest, don't scale.

### BOF Example Ads (Lesson 089), bof-beispielanzeigen-fuer-dropshipping-39cb5.md

- Ice Cap long-testimonial ad: headline "SAY NO TO MIGRAINES" over a full customer story ("After years of losing days to crippling migraines ... this wearable cap has given me control again. No meds, no side effects, just instant, soothing relief"), signed with a first name and initial (Steph J.).

- Ice Cap sale ad: "Drug-Free Migraine Relief Anytime." + "SALE DISCOUNT 25%" + "+ FREE SHIPPING" + "SHOP NOW".

## 17. Customer Service Returns and Chargebacks

### Avoid Chargeback (chargebacks-und-reserve-payout-vermeiden-2f62a.md)

- [00:00] Case context: the speaker made about 200k in one month on a single book, with custom ads, then got a DMCA in both Meta and Shopify.
- [04:06] Chargeback tools named: Chargeback IO, Disputely and Dispute Fire. The speaker uses Disputely because it is one of the cheapest.
- [07:20] The speaker paid close to 2,000 in chargebacks. With the RDR auto refunds he lost about 4 to 5K USD in total.
- [07:20] Some mentees lost their whole Shopify payout because they did not fulfil orders fast enough.
- [09:40] The speaker's own reserve held his payment for four months, about 30k (Singapore dollars), or about 32 to 33k including more than 30 chargebacks.
- [10:11] A mentee who did not fulfil for close to two months had Shopify Payments banned on the whole account.
- [10:11] "E-commerce is all about cash flow". You need the cash flow to pay MOQ orders.
- [10:46] Kuro basic membership: you deposit a little money every day, up to $5,000 a month in spend. If you spend more, upgrade to the higher tier.
- [11:50] After the DMCA, every new Business Manager the speaker created with his credit card got banned, because the record was tied to his IP in Singapore.
- [12:53] In Singapore, ad spend carries 9% GST. Example: 100K USD of spend (about 100K SGD) costs about 9,000 SGD in GST alone. The speaker spent 60K USD on a personal account and paid GST on all of it.
- [13:30] A credit card gives miles. With KrisFlyer miles you can fly business class. In Southeast Asia a flight costs about 30 to 50K miles.
- [14:35] If your credit card limit is 40K, 50K or 60K, use it. Card billing gives about a 30-day window. If your third-party agent gives net terms of two weeks or 20 days, that is even better for scaling on cash flow, because Shopify payouts may not clear fast enough to pay suppliers.
- [16:17] Custom ads, a custom cover and custom internal pages are the next step, usually once you make over 1K a day or consistent profit.

## 18. Legal Tax Banking and Payments

### E-Digital Sales, DMCA Risks, Kachings Cart & Checkout Setup (e-books-mit-kaching-cart-und-dmca-schutz-e185d.md)

- [01:04] The speaker judges leftover ready-stock books without MOQ that have not been tested before as "most likely" winners, "quite a high chance".
- [02:28] Running a book is "technically a very gray area". If it is a big author's book, you can get a DMCA on your ad agency account or your personal account. The speaker got a DMCA in both Shopify and Meta while running a big main author's book.
- [03:05] Only customize once the concept is proven and the P&L is very strong every day, starting around day six or day seven. Customize the UGC/AI ads, the cover page and the internal pages of the book.
- [03:05] To avoid plagiarism, turn the hardcopy into a soft copy and rewrite it with Claude. The result: different words, the same formatting, the same meaning, but a totally different author and title, with the small images changed.
- [05:25] Promotion progress bar example: set a threshold such as $50 that unlocks 5% off. Set it high enough that the buyer must buy two units to get the 5%.
- [05:25] Product upsell and products in cart are normally not used in Kaching Cart. Skip them.
- [06:01] When scaling, add shipping protection as an optional choice for $3. The image size can be set to larger.
- [06:01] Payment methods in Kaching Cart: select all the cards.

### Running books: customization rule (running-books-die-customization-regel-35479.md)

- [01:06] Second reason to customize: ownership. With your own spin the product "genuinely becomes yours", and competitors who want to rip it cannot do so as easily as with a repackaged template.
- [02:08] Exact customization example: a book titled "How to Cure Gut Health" is retitled "Gut Health 101 for Mothers", and a blue cover is changed to dark blue, so it becomes a different variation. You can also make it a whole different color, "everything different".
- [02:41] Wording of the rule: "100% will always recommend you guys to customize your books". Framed as best practice, "not a hard rule".

## 19. Metrics Finance and Tracking

### Fix Columns and customization decisions (lesson 078): meta-spalten-einrichten-und-customization-entscheidungen-tre-ff1f4.md

- [02:23] Use the same standardized column view so coaches can read your soft metrics at a glance and judge whether the product is worth scaling or worth a budget increase.
- [08:08] Custom UGC AI videos were made with tools the teacher calls "C-Dance" (most likely Seedance) or "King 3.0" (most likely Kling 3.0). Customization needs UGC credits and money for each video.
- [09:48] For a P&L that fluctuates with only about 100+ profit: "I will just cut and move on and move to another book." Reasons given: there are so many books that holding one wastes opportunity cost, and one red day wipes out the profit.

### Metric Deep Dive (kennzahlen-deep-dive-cpc-cpm-und-ctr-428bb.md, lesson 080)

- [00:00] Framing: "pretend that your campaign is like a brain"; it needs resources (spend) to learn. Recommended amount $50 so Meta has enough data to optimize; the more you spend, the more time it has to learn and find your preferred audience.
- [00:28] On-screen rule: "Rule of thumb: 3 days ads spend" for judging a campaign.
- [01:00] After you finish spending your first 24 hours, send your metrics to the coaches so they can read them; at [04:14] for borderline CTR, "send a screenshot of your matrix to your coaches".
- [01:34] How to combat a bad CPC ($2 and above): (1) alternative target audience: instead of only the United States, target a broader range, for example "the top five countries, UK, New Zealand, Canada"; (2) better hooks (on screen: "Better Hooks (3 second)") to captivate attention; (3) high quality ads.
- [03:23] Combating a high CPM uses the same three levers as CPC.
- [04:14] "The only way to combat CTR is you need better hooks."
- [04:49] Add-to-carts mean interest: if all of CPC, CPM and CTR are bad but there are add-to-carts, run another day.
- [05:18] Add-to-cart but no conversion, second cause: the offer "could be terrible"; look at competitors for reference or ask the coaches how to improve the offer. Case: a Discord student had many add-to-carts and no buyers; his checkout button was broken, and after fixing it people bought.

### Daily Reporting (Lesson 081), taegliches-reporting-die-zweite-regel-28e5b.md

- [00:00] Called "Probably the most important rule". Screen rule card: "RULE #2: DAILY AD REPORTS", "Day 1 report within 24 hours", "Day 2 report within 24 hours", "Day 3 report within 24 hours", "Continue daily until told otherwise", "Missing reports = You lose money & we lose control to help", "SUBMIT EVERYTHING BEFORE PROCEEDING". Rule #1 on the same card is about competitors.
- [00:34] Some students lost "a few hundred dollars" by not reporting that their ads were live; this happens "a few times a week".
- [01:29] Reporting starts after you make your website, find your videos and launch your campaign.

## 20. Scaling Team and Processes

### The Reason Why You're Not Scaling (L097, warum-du-nicht-skalierst-das-1-problem-b1829.md)

- [01:56] Theme is not the main thing: one of the coach's main brands runs on "the simple version of Shrine" with some custom-coded parts, so it looks different from the average Shrine store. "Either Shrine or Elixir, that's not the main thing. The main thing is to get it up fast."
- [02:19] Creatives matter more than the website: "our creatives are good, but our website is not good ... it's converting due to the creatives being good."
- [04:12] Gadget example: a Bitcoin-farm gadget seen on X was doing about 5K days. Look for gadgets and "stuff that no one else is looking at" instead of supplements.
- [05:27] Plan for zero to 1K days in under a month (for someone who can already build an Elixir store, rip ads and run Ads Manager/BM): "set up a proper system of testing", launching two or three times a week, and get that done every week.
- [05:27] Ask questions you cannot figure out yourself; operators at 500+ days ask better questions than people going from zero to 500 ("do I click yes on this notification?" is the wrong kind). "Just execute on what you know already."
- [06:23] "Going from zero to 500 is way harder than 500 to like 50K months." Example student (Parcival): now at about 8K days; the hardest part was zero to his first 1K day; after 1K "it was just up in the budget, more ads".
