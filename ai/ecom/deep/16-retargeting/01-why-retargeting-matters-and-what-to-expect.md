## Why Retargeting Matters and What to Expect

### Why Most Visitors Leave Without Buying

- A conversion rate is never 100 percent, so most visitors leave without buying, and remarketing ads bring those visitors back. [L0586, 2020-26]
- Plan on most visitors not buying on their first visit, even if the store is well designed, converts well, and has strong products. The purpose of remarketing is to get them to take the first purchase step. [L0676, 2020-26]
- Most first-time visitors leave a store without buying. Retargeting ads bring them back to the products they already viewed, so the brand stays in front of the people who are closest to ordering. [L0733, 2020-26]
- About 97.7 percent of visitors leave a store without buying on their first visit. Retargeting and Facebook dynamic product ads (DPA) are used to bring these visitors back. [L1031, 2018]
- Typical e-commerce conversion rates are around 1 percent, 2 percent or 0.5 percent, so most visitors leave without buying on that visit. Retargeting aims to keep these non-buying visitors engaged so they can return and complete a purchase. [L1240, 2021-22]
- In an example store with about 500,000 dollars of monthly sales, the conversion rate was below 1 percent. Since no site reaches a 100 percent conversion rate, most visitors leave without buying, and retargeting them has real value. [L1203, 2016]
- Visitors who buy on their first visit are a minority, so retargeting covers the rest. [L0771, 2021-22]
- Shoppers forget a store quickly, even with a strong domain name, so remarketing is needed to bring them back. ** [L0671, 2021]
- Skipping retargeting means leaving sales on the table, because many visitors leave without buying and need repeated exposure to convert. Treat retargeting as a standard part of the setup, not an optional extra. [L0920, 2018]

### The Case for Retargeting

#### Reaching People Who Already Showed Interest

- Retargeting is effective because it reaches people who have already shown interest in the product. It works best running alongside cold prospecting ads rather than replacing them. [L0561, 2023+]
- Remarketing targets people who already showed interest, and it brings previous website visitors back to the store to complete their orders. [L0597, 2020-26]
- Remarketing recaptures sales from first-visit non-converters by re-engaging visitors who demonstrated interest in specific products. [L0163, undated]
- Remarketing works by showing ads for the exact products a visitor viewed to people who left the site without buying. Clicking the ad returns them to those product pages so they can complete the purchase. [L1029, 2020-26]
- Retargeting, also called remarketing, shows ads to people who have already visited your website. [L0720, 2020-26]
- Retargeting ads show specific ads to people who have already been on the store and viewed products, to bring them back to finish a purchase. ** [L0771, 2021-22]
- Retargeting shows ads to people who already visited the website and took an action such as adding to cart, viewing a product page, viewing a collection page or viewing a content page. Its purpose is to bring those visitors back to complete a purchase. ** [L1239, 2021-22]
- Retargeting means showing specific ads to people who already interacted with your store, for example by adding a product to cart, viewing a product page, a collection page or a content page. The audience is built from these on-site actions. ** [L1240, 2021-22]

#### Higher Conversion Than Cold Traffic

- Retargeting conversion rates are 2 to 10 times higher than cold traffic conversion rates. [L0257, 2020-26]
- People who have already visited the store are expected to convert at a higher rate than cold traffic, which is why retargeting is usually the stronger bottom-of-funnel layer. The lesson claims this holds for about 99 percent of e-commerce stores, but this is a rule of thumb and not a verified statistic. Check it against your own account data. [L0867, 2020-26]
- Retargeting lists convert better than cold traffic, so you can bid more aggressively for these users or offer them a coupon. [L0720, 2020-26]
- Retargeting ads lifted conversion rate by about 26 percent on average. For example, a 1 percent conversion rate becomes 1.26 percent after adding Facebook dynamic retargeting ads. [L0510, 2018]
- A cited figure claims retargeting raises the chance that a visitor converts by 70 percent. This only holds when the original traffic was well targeted, so do not apply the figure to poorly targeted campaigns. [L1031, 2018]

#### Lower Cost of Reach

- Converting existing buyers or visitors is said to cost about seven times less than acquiring new customers, so retargeting should carry real budget and attention. This comparison is cited as a general claim, not as a verified benchmark. [L1243, 2017]
- Retargeting ads show to people who already visited the store, which allows a small budget to bring back visitors who are likely to buy. This is the main way to spend little on paid ads early. ** [L1281, 2022]

#### Several Touchpoints Before Purchase

- On average across e-commerce, a customer needs about seven touchpoints with a brand before feeling ready to buy. This is an average, so some customers buy after fewer and others after more contacts. ** [L1239, 2021-22]
- Across the e-commerce industry, a customer needs on average about seven touchpoints with a business before buying. Some customers need fewer and some more, so seven is a benchmark, not a fixed rule. ** [L1240, 2021-22]
- Because customers need several brand interactions before buying, the goal is to reach them as quickly as possible and keep the brand within their attention until they are ready to purchase. ** [L1239, 2021-22]
- Because customers need several brand interactions before buying, the goal is to fit those touchpoints into as short a time as possible. Staying within the customer's attention until they are ready to buy is the purpose of retargeting. ** [L1240, 2021-22]
- Retargeting keeps the brand top of mind during the period before purchase, so that when the customer is ready to buy they think of this store rather than competitors who are also targeting them. ** [L1239, 2021-22]
- Retargeting is also meant to keep your brand top of mind, so that when a visitor is ready to buy, they choose your store and not a competitor. Direct conversions from these ads are a welcome bonus, not the only goal. ** [L1240, 2021-22]

#### Competition and Brand Choice

- The main remarketing goal is to make sure no lead or customer is lost, because many competitors sell similar products, and the customer should choose the brand when ready to buy. Abandoned cart emails and dynamic product ads are the basic must-have tactics. [L1076, 2019]

#### Platform Advantage

- A Shopify store lets the seller run retargeting for visitors who did not buy, which is hard to do on Amazon. Retargeting works best when the traffic is sent to a store focused on the seller's own products. [L1153, 2016]

### Retargeting Setup and Process

#### Audiences and Ad Creative

- Remarketing targets website visitors and past buyers who did not complete a purchase or made a one-time purchase. It uses abandoned cart emails, custom audiences on Facebook and Google, and dynamic or static ad creative to re-engage them. [L0240, 2019]
- Facebook dynamic product ads (DPA) are used to bring back visitors who left without buying. [L1031, 2018]
- The ideal return point is the product page the visitor was already viewing, because that is where the first purchase action is most likely. [L0676, 2020-26]
- Missing follow-up such as retargeting or email lets visitors who leave without buying disappear, which pulls the conversion rate down. Retargeting must be planned as part of the traffic review, because it runs on the same traffic platforms. ** [L0473, 2021-22]

#### Audience Window and Budget

- Facebook retargeting ROAS depends on the audience window. A 4-day audience usually performs much better than a 30- or 60-day audience. With small budgets and a good traffic source, a 30 to 50 times return on ad spend is realistic. [L0971, 2024]

#### Example Sequence

**

1. Send visitors to the store from Google Shopping ads and search traffic.
2. Track the visitors who did not buy on their first visit.
3. Retarget those visitors with email marketing, Facebook ads and Google ads.
4. Keep showing products until the visitor is ready to buy, then move them to purchase.
5. Repeat the cycle with new first-time search traffic.

[L0458, 2021-22]

#### Example From one source

- Retargeting drove most sales for this store. Visitors often leave on their first visit, then return after retargeting and are pushed to buy by a compelling offer. Plan retargeting ads for visitors who did not purchase. [L1077, undated]

### Do Not Judge Retargeting Only by Direct Conversions

#### Direct Conversion Varies by Ticket Size

- Retargeting should be seen as more than a direct conversion tool, because many visitors do not convert immediately but still benefit from repeated exposure to the brand. ** [L1239, 2021-22]
- Retargeting ads should not be judged only by direct conversions. Low-ticket products often show high conversion rates from retargeting, while high-ticket products often show no directly attributable conversions even though the ads still influence buyers. ** [L1240, 2021-22]
- On low-ticket products, retargeting ads often show a high, directly measurable conversion rate. On high-ticket products, retargeting often has no clearly attributable conversion rate, yet it still serves a purpose and should not be judged only by direct sales. ** [L1239, 2021-22]

#### Check the Role Before Cutting

- Retargeting campaigns that generate clicks and cost money but no attributed sales are not automatically failing, especially for higher-ticket products. Before cutting them, check their role in keeping the brand in front of the customer. ** [L1240, 2021-22]

#### Further details

- At any time only about 10% of the total available market is ready to buy, which is the bottom-of-funnel segment that needs little extra effort to convert; growing traffic increases the share of audience that needs education and support. [L0414, 2021-22]

## When to Start and How Much to Spend

### Timing: Launch Day or After Traffic and Sales

Sources differ on when paid retargeting should start. Launch-day setup is recommended in most sources. Several sources also require store traffic and sales before paid retargeting is funded.

**Set up at launch**

- Set up Facebook remarketing from the first day, alongside Google Ads, because most first-time visitors from search do not buy on arrival (figure cited: about 98%), so they need to be brought back. [L0663, 2019]
- Facebook ads can be set up from day one, but only for remarketing to people who already visited the store, not for cold prospecting. [L0356, 2019]
- Set up remarketing campaigns on Google, Bing and Facebook at launch, so visitors who leave without buying can be shown the products again. [L0393, undated]
- Start remarketing campaigns on Google and Facebook early in the launch, so visitors who did not buy can be reached again. [L0532, undated]
- Set up Facebook remarketing campaigns to start as soon as the first store traffic arrives, not later, so retargeting can convert from the launch day. [L0671, 2021]
- Even one extra remarketing sale every few days can be very profitable early on. This is why remarketing should be live at launch. [L0671, 2021]
- Run small remarketing campaigns on both Google and Meta from the start, alongside Shopping (2025). [L0667, 2025]
- On Facebook, start with remarketing only. Do not spend on cold audiences or brand awareness initially. [L0245, 2020]

**Wait for traffic, sales and performance**

- Retargeting requires store traffic and sales first, because the audiences are built from visitors and buyers. Without early sales there is nothing to retarget. [L0661, undated]
- Abandoned cart emails and exit-intent capture are the first retargeting layer. Paid remarketing (Google and Facebook) comes after the store has traffic. [L0468, 2020]
- Start retargeting after about two weeks of consistent performance from the product. Before that, the audiences are too small and the data too thin. [L0648, undated]

**How to read the conflict**

- The launch-day sources describe setting up campaigns and capturing the first visitors. [L0663, 2019; L0393, undated; L0532, undated; L0671, 2021]
- The traffic-first sources describe when paid budget should be funded and grown. [L0661, undated; L0468, 2020; L0648, undated]
- The only dividing line given in the sources is the two-week performance rule. [L0648, undated]

### Warning Signs of Over-Weighting Retargeting

- Putting most of the budget into remarketing is a warning sign. It means the store is not finding new prospects. [L0961, 2020]
- Response: shift the budget back toward Google Ads for new visitors. [L0961, 2020]
- Keep the remarketing share closer to 10% in the early phase. [L0961, 2020]
- The warning sign applies while the store is still finding its first buyers. one source's growing share describes a later stage, once returning visitors and buyers exist. No threshold is given in the sources. [L0961, 2020; L0563, 2021-22]

### When to Scale

**Conditions before scaling**

- Retargeting starts after about two weeks of consistent performance from the product. [L0648, undated]
- Scale only once traffic and sales are growing. [L0733, 2020-26]
- Increase the budget only once it brings sales. [L0468, 2020]

**Scaling steps**

1. Confirm about two weeks of consistent product performance. [L0648, undated]
2. Confirm that traffic and sales are growing. [L0733, 2020-26]
3. Duplicate the campaign to target only 7-day website visitors. This audience usually converts best because those visitors were on the store recently. [L0733, 2020-26]
4. Increase the budget only where it brings sales. [L0468, 2020]
5. Add longer audiences, such as the 180-day audience, later for promotions. [L0733, 2020-26]

## Tracking, Pixels and Data Prerequisites

### Prerequisites for Tracking and Data

#### Setting Up Pixels and Tags

- Retargeting pixels and tracking codes from Google Ads, Facebook and Google Analytics must be installed on all content pages of the site. [L0493, 2021-22]
- Retargeting only works after tracking is set up on each ad network you plan to use. Cookies placed on visitors who view pages or take actions on your site become the basis for the audiences you target. [L1390, 2021-22]
- The Google Ads retargeting pixel must be installed on the Shopify store before a website visitor audience can fill with visitors. Without it the audience stays empty. [L1242, 2020-26]
- Add the remarketing tag through the Feeds for Google Shopping app before launch so that visitors are tracked from their first visit. The remarketing campaigns themselves are set up after the store goes live. [L1145, 2021-22]
- Every visitor should be pixeled on every store you own, so you can retarget them on Facebook and Google later. Stores you own can carry the pixel because you control their code, but you cannot track visitors through affiliate links. [L0923, 2015]

#### Avoiding Duplicate Installs

- Install each tag or pixel only once, because duplicate installs of the same tag distort tracking. [L0493, 2021-22]
- Installing the Google Shopping feed app's tracking events twice creates duplicate events with the same names in the tag list. Choose the one labeled 'purchase in category' and keep only one copy of each event in the account. [L0420, 2020-26]

### Verifying Firing

- Pixels and tracking codes often exist in an existing store but are set up incorrectly or not firing, so verify that they register site visits. [L0493, 2021-22]

### Data Sharing Settings

- Since 2023, GA4 remarketing audiences require data sharing settings to be enabled, or audiences show a 'not eligible' status. [L0274, 2023]
- Enable the following settings:
  1. Granular location and device data
  2. Google insights
  3. Ads personalization
  4. Acknowledgement of user data collection [L0274, 2023]

### Privacy and Ad-Blocker Losses

- A remarketing audience can show zero impressions even with daily clicks, because ad blockers and browser privacy prevent tracking of part of the visitors. Estimate the trackable share first; for example, about 40 of 50 daily clicks may be trackable. [L0470, 2023]
- Visitors who block tracking in their browser are excluded from Google and Meta remarketing audiences, and repeat visits from the same person count only once. [L1283, 2023]
- Remarketing audiences built on advanced privacy blocking still work using platform-owned data such as link clicks, comments and shares. Platforms with their own data, such as Google-owned YouTube, can still target viewers. [L1112, 2021]

### Traffic Minimums

- Each advertising platform requires a minimum amount of traffic, usually within a 30-day window, before a retargeting audience can be built and activated. [L0493, 2021-22]
- A low-traffic store can keep its audiences below the 100-person minimum for weeks, so check audience size before changing bids or budgets. [L1283, 2023]
- A new store with low traffic can still create audiences in Google Ads and Facebook ahead of time. Such an audience stays inactive until enough visitors fill the pool, then it can be used straight away and ads can run on it automatically. [L0493, 2021-22]

### Filtering Low-Quality Visits

- Add a minimum session duration rule to each audience, or delay pixel firing with a trigger rule in Google Tag Manager. Set the delay to 30 seconds so bouncing visitors stay out of the cookie pool. [L1390, 2021-22]
