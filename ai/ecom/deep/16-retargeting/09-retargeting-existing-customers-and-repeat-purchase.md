## Retargeting Existing Customers and Repeat Purchases

### Why Existing Customers Are Worth Targeting

- Not retargeting existing customers is a major missed opportunity, because selling to an existing customer is much easier than acquiring a new one. Relevant upsells and resells should be promoted through ads to past buyers. [L0563, 2021-22]
- After a first sale, retargeting and repeat email campaigns bring the same customer back for second and third purchases. Repeat sales are cheaper to win than new ones, so their profit margins are usually higher. [L0771, 2021-22]
- Second and third purchases from existing customers usually generate more profit than the first sale, which makes existing-customer retargeting a key driver of customer lifetime value. Combine it with email marketing for the best effect. [L0563, 2021-22]
- Do not assume existing customers will never buy again. Not all of them will, but some will, so test repeat offers rather than dropping them from campaigns. [L0563, 2021-22]
- People who already use the product have already decided and rarely read acquisition ads, so repeated acquisition ads for current customers waste budget. [L1261, undated]

### Building Customer Audiences

- Leaving existing customers out of retargeting means missing repeat sales. Build a separate customer audience and give it its own campaigns. [L1390, 2021-22]
- Build a customer list from customer email addresses and names. Upload the list to the ad network, which matches it to users on its platform. [L0563, 2021-22]
- Wait until the list reaches the network's minimum size before using it. Google Display needs 100 people. [L0563, 2021-22]
- Show ads to this audience only when you have a relevant offer or content for these customers. [L0563, 2021-22]

**Customer audience procedure:**

1. Build a customer list from customer email addresses and names. [L0563, 2021-22]
2. Upload the list to the ad network, which matches it to users on its platform. [L0563, 2021-22]
3. Wait until the list reaches the network's minimum size before using it; Google Display needs 100 people. [L0563, 2021-22]
4. Show ads only when you have a relevant offer or content for these customers. [L0563, 2021-22]

### Excluding Customers from Acquisition

- If you have enough volume, build a customer audience and exclude it from new-customer acquisition campaigns. Customers also need their own retargeting campaigns matched to their stage in the customer journey, not first-purchase offers. [L1390, 2021-22]
- Showing first-purchase retargeting ads to existing customers wastes spend, because they rarely buy the same product right away. Serve customers separate messages based on where they are in the journey. [L1390, 2021-22]
- Target only first-time buyers by excluding customers with a purchase frequency above one. [L1243, 2017]
- Use the same exclusion for the thank-you video so repeat buyers do not receive it again. [L1243, 2017]

### Timing After Delivery

- Timing for existing-customer ads can range from soon after the first purchase to six months later or longer. The right timing depends on the offer, such as an upsell or a reorder. [L0563, 2021-22]
- The repeat-purchase funnel must start only after the customer has received the product, not when the order is placed. [L1243, 2017]
- If products have different shipping times, separate the audiences by brand or product so each one is triggered by its own delivery. [L1243, 2017]

### Goodwill and Thank-You Videos

- Recent buyers get a goodwill video ad on reach and frequency, at around 2 impressions per day for 7 days. The goal is to be seen, not to sell, so conversion objectives are the wrong choice here. [L0649, 2019]
- The thank-you video ad for customers in the first 7 days is set to low frequency, roughly twice a day per person. [L1076, 2019]
- Customers from the last 180 days see the same content and promotions over time. [L1076, 2019]
- Goodwill thank-you ad pattern: a short personal video that thanks the buyer, names the store, and says what to expect next. It works because it builds the relationship after purchase and gets high engagement. [L0649, 2019]
  - Example one: "Thank you for ordering from us, here is what happens next and how we will look after you." [L0649, 2019]
  - Example two: "Welcome to the family, here is what to expect from your first week with us." [L0649, 2019]

**Thank-you video script for first-time buyers.** Recorded by the store owner on camera and posted as a Facebook ad after the product has been delivered. Target only people who have bought once, by excluding purchase frequency above one, so repeat buyers do not receive the thank-you message again. [L1243, 2017]

```
Hi {first name}, I wanted to take a minute to thank you for your order of {product name}. Customers like you are the reason this store exists, and I hope you love using it. Keep an eye on your inbox, because I have a discount for your next order.
```

### Repeat-Purchase Funnel

- Repeat buyers were cited as converting at 60% to 70%, compared with an industry average of 2% to 3% for new customers. Targeting past buyers is therefore presented as the main source of repeat sales. [L1243, 2017]

**Repeat-purchase funnel procedure:**

1. **Step 1, after delivery:** Have the owner send a short thank-you video through Facebook, then follow up with an email containing a coupon code for the next purchase. Target only first-time buyers by excluding customers with a purchase frequency above one. [L1243, 2017]
2. **Step 2, 7 to 14 days after delivery:** Run a cross-sell campaign with dynamic product ads. Set up pairings in the product catalog beforehand, such as when a customer buys product X, show product Y. Then send an email that recalls the coupon code from step 1. [L1243, 2017]

### Cross-Sell and Upgrade Ads

- Set up cross-sell pairings in the product catalog before launch, so that a purchase of product X triggers product Y (older, 2021-22 pairing logic is the same as the current funnel). [L1243, 2017]
- Example: customers who bought a product a year ago are shown an ad for a newer model, presented as an upgrade to the product they already like. Some of them will buy again, so the offer is worth testing. [L0563, 2021-22]

### Channel Split: Acquisition vs. Retention

- Use paid ads to acquire new customers, and use SMS and email to retarget existing buyers with new products or better offers. [L1431, 2020+]
- Retargeting existing customers avoids paying again to reach them on Facebook. [L1431, 2020+]
- Use retention and reactivation messages separately from the acquisition campaign. [L1261, undated]

## Retargeting for Long Buying Cycles and Competitive Niches

### Retargeting for High-Ticket Products with Long Buying Cycles
- High-ticket products usually have longer buying journeys, so follow-up must cover a longer period. Without follow-up for first-time visitors, many will leave and buy elsewhere before they are ready. [L0473, 2021-22]
- High-ticket buyers, such as people furnishing a home, may research products up to six months before they buy. Keep paid traffic running in slow periods so these early planners enter your retargeting list and recall your store when they are ready to purchase. [L0949, 2021-22]
- For some high-ticket products, customers shop months before they intend to buy, so retargeting must keep your brand present through that whole consideration window until they are ready to pay. [L1239, 2021-22; L1240, 2021-22]

### Keeping Paid Traffic Running in Slow Periods
- For high-ticket products with long buying cycles, keep paid traffic running during slow periods. Customers may plan purchases months ahead, and retargeting lists keep your store in front of them until they buy. [L0949, 2021-22]
- The right level of paid traffic depends on your risk tolerance and long-term view. Some owners cut traffic in slow periods to save money, while others keep it running to build audiences for later. [L0949, 2021-22]
- Remarketing audiences built during slow economic periods compound in value. Maintaining consistent remarketing metrics during downturns means a higher audience size and message frequency when the economy recovers, which multiplies campaign effectiveness. [L0245, 2020]

### Building Audiences in Slow Periods, Then Activating Them

1. Collect customer emails through opt-in forms. [L0245, 2020]
2. Create Google audiences of all website visitors (website visits). [L0245, 2020]
3. Create Facebook audiences of website visitors and page engagement. [L0245, 2020]
4. During periods of low purchase intent (economic downturns), build these remarketing lists without aggressive selling. [L0245, 2020]
5. Once consumer confidence recovers, execute heavy remarketing campaigns to these built audiences via email, Google, and Facebook. [L0245, 2020]

### Competitor-Aware Retargeting for Resellers
- Resellers who sell other brands' products face competitors selling the same items. Prospects who research in advance see several sellers, and in long consideration periods the seller a prospect saw most during research often wins the sale, not only the seller with the best offer. Retargeting therefore gives a competitive advantage. [L1239, 2021-22; L1240, 2021-22]
- Retargeting brings back visitors who did not buy yet. It is one of the reasons a store wins repeat sales when competitors offer the same MAP price. [L0966, 2025]

### Retargeting in Ultra-Competitive Niches
- In an ultra-competitive niche, retarget visitors on Google, Facebook, Instagram, Bing and Yahoo with higher bids. Buyers often return days or weeks later, forget which store they researched, and buy from a competitor, so repeated exposure keeps your store in front of them. [L1357, 2018]

### Private-Label Products
- Remarketing to your own visitors is a main traffic source for newly private-labeled products that Google Shopping does not reach, because shoppers do not search for them. [L0968, 2021]

## Channel Selection and Priorities

### Start with search, then retarget
- Search-based campaigns come before retargeting. Google Search, Google Shopping and Bing are the preferred sources of store visitors because people actively look for the product there. [L1020, 2020-26; L1243, 2017]
- Facebook retargeting starts only after Google Search, Google Shopping and Bing are maxed out. Facebook is a discovery platform, not a search platform, so it is the next channel to add, not the first. [L1243, 2017]
- Cold Google campaigns target people searching for the product, while remarketing runs on both Google and Meta. This does not change based on whether your audience is more active on Google or Facebook. [L0471, 2024]
- Retargeting should run across several networks with the same core messages. Using only one channel loses customers whose attention is split across platforms. [L0563, 2021-22]
- Aim for a subtle, omnipresent presence across relevant networks so visitors meet your ads wherever they go without feeling followed. Reach cold traffic this way, and retarget warm and hot audiences across the same networks so competitors do not dominate attention. [L0563, 2021-22]
- Scale retargeting to your traffic volume. [L1251, 2024+]

### Channel order
1. Start with Google retargeting, which has the highest priority. Set up dynamic remarketing first, then standard remarketing, then video/YouTube remarketing. Build these audiences before any other retargeting channel. [L0920, 2018]
2. Add Facebook, Instagram and Bing next, using the same audience logic on each platform rather than separate strategies. [L0920, 2018]
3. Add the next bottom-of-funnel campaigns: website retargeting on the Google Display Network and retargeting on Shopping. Both target people who have already visited the store. [L0867, 2020-26]
4. Keep YouTube low priority until other channels are fully used. [L0247, 2021; L0962, 2021]
5. Test TikTok last, and only after the Meta and Google remarketing budgets are fully used. It is not a main revenue source. (2025) [L0837, 2025]

**Conflicting advice on Facebook dynamic remarketing versus Google remarketing.** The 2021 advice gives Facebook dynamic remarketing ads the remarketing budget before Google remarketing or YouTube placement ads. It says Google remarketing is not worth the added complexity until monthly sales reach the high five or six figures. The undated one source advice puts Google first without a sales threshold. [L1112, 2021; L0920, 2018]

- Google Ads offers several retargeting options, and Facebook (Meta) is the second main retargeting platform. [L1239, 2021-22; L1240, 2021-22]
- Run retargeting natively inside Google Ads and do not pay for third-party retargeting apps. [L0803, 2022]

- Remarketing ads bring back visitors from all traffic sources so they can be converted into sales. [L1393, 2020-26]

### Meta: Facebook and Instagram
- Use Meta for remarketing to people who have already visited the store. [L1116, 2025]
- Use Facebook for remarketing rather than prospecting new audiences. Remarketing is the part of the Facebook budget that works best. [L1090, 2021]
- Use Facebook ads to re-engage visitors after they leave the store, because most of the profit comes from this once Google brings in the first traffic. [L1069, 2019]
- Facebook retargeting showed a slightly higher return on ad spend than Google retargeting in 2020, though not by much. Run both rather than choosing one. [L1277, 2020]
- Remarketing is a high priority and should run on both Google and Meta. A good audience can help conversion and return on ad spend, especially for high-ticket products. (2023) [L1094, 2023]
- Use Google remarketing alongside Meta, because some visitors never use Facebook or Instagram. Audience Network also shows ads on partner websites and apps. [L0649, 2019]
- Facebook dynamic remarketing ads get the remarketing budget before Google remarketing when monthly sales are below the high five or six figures. [L1112, 2021]
- Use carousel ads for the product in Facebook remarketing. [L1251, 2024+]
- Remarketing to non-buyers runs through Google Ads, Facebook, Instagram and email. Each channel can restart the funnel from the store visit. [L0676, 2020-26]

**If your target audience has low Facebook usage,** use retargeting campaigns on Google, Instagram or other advertising networks instead of relying solely on Facebook. [L0238, 2019]

### When Meta is added
| Condition | Action | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Google Search, Google Shopping and Bing are maxed out | Add Facebook retargeting as the next channel | [L1243, 2017] |
| Google has brought in the first traffic | Use Facebook to re-engage visitors who left | [L1069, 2019] |
| Visitors have left without buying | Use Meta to win them back, not to prospect | [L1020, 2020-26; L1116, 2025] |

### YouTube and video
- YouTube ads work well for remarketing. Target website visitors from the last seven days and exclude customers. Video production is a barrier, so YouTube is generally lower priority than Google Shopping. [L0247, 2021]
- A short video, filmed as a selfie or recorded as a screen share, can remarket to visitors who viewed the store in the past seven days without buying. It works well on small budgets. Keep YouTube ads low priority until other channels are fully used. [L0962, 2021]
- Video/YouTube remarketing comes third in the Google priority order, after dynamic and standard remarketing. [L0920, 2018]

### TikTok
- Test TikTok remarketing only after the Meta and Google remarketing budgets are fully used. It is not a main source of revenue. (2025) [L0837, 2025]
- A separate TikTok retargeting campaign is generally unnecessary, because prospecting already reaches people who saw the product. Retarget only with a specific creative built for existing brand audiences, such as a discount-code offer. [L1374, undated]
- Adding Google ads to Facebook and TikTok campaigns lets you retarget visitors from those channels who did not buy. This recovers customers who would otherwise never return. [L1213, 2023]

### Bing
- Retarget on Bing after Google, using the same audience logic as on Facebook and Instagram. [L0920, 2018]
- Bing search campaigns belong with Google Search and Google Shopping as search-based campaigns to max out before Facebook retargeting starts. [L1243, 2017]
- Bing is a preferred search source of store visitors. [L1020, 2020-26]

### Pinterest
- Pinterest remarketing can add some revenue if your target audience uses Pinterest. It is unlikely to double a business on its own. [L1111, 2020]

### Running Google and Meta as one sequence
1. A visitor clicks a Google Shopping product listing ad and visits the store. [L1031, 2018]
2. The visitor leaves without buying. [L1031, 2018]
3. The visitor sees Facebook retargeting ads for those products. [L1031, 2018]
4. Plan Google and Facebook campaigns together as one sequence, not as separate projects. [L1031, 2018]

- Use Google ads after TikTok or Facebook ads to reach people who visited the site but did not buy, across channels such as YouTube and Google. This creates an omnichannel presence. [L1436, undated]
