## Planning Audiences: Funnel Stages, Segmentation and Windows

### Audience Foundations

- An audience is any group of people who took a specific action on your website or social media accounts, such as visiting a page or engaging with a post. Audiences are the basis for every later ad and email campaign. [L0757, 2022]
- Build audiences so you can promote current and future offers to them, aiming for the highest possible ROAS. The same audiences are used for search ads and audience ads. [L0757, 2022]
- A remarketing audience is a group of people who visited your website, identified through tracking cookies. Showing Google or Facebook ads to those visitors is remarketing, and it builds the audience for later promotions. [L0801, 2021] **
- Remarketing audiences grow from paid ads, organic search and direct visits. Each visitor who lands on the store adds to the audience for future campaigns. [L0801, 2021] **
- Retargeting needs no prior sales. People who viewed products on the site without buying are enough to fill the remarketing audience. [L1086, 2019] **
- Do not target people who have never visited the store or do not know the brand with Meta remarketing ads. Remarketing is only for visitors who already showed interest by viewing products on the store. [L1020, 2020-26]
- Audiences are what make retargeting work, because they turn the cookie pool into groups that receive more personal and relevant ads. Without segmenting them, most of the benefit of retargeting is lost. [L1390, 2021-22] **
- Retention is the number of days a person stays in an audience after the triggering action. Visitor and purchase audiences are dynamic and refresh automatically. A person who visited within the last 7 days is in the 7-day audience and drops out on day 8 if they do not return. [L1023, 2020-26]
- Build dynamic audiences in Meta and Google before launch, such as 180-day website visitor audiences. They start empty but fill automatically as people visit, and each person drops out after the window ends. [L0934, 2023]
- The three main audience types are:
  - retargeting and customer match (people who already interacted with the brand or are on your customer list);
  - search intent (people actively searching for relevant terms);
  - lookalikes (people who resemble existing customers).

  Lookalike and search-intent audiences are central to cold traffic campaigns. The lesson gives the first two the most attention. [L1386, 2020-26]

### Growing the Audience Pool

- Fill your email list and your invisible audiences using both free (organic) and paid traffic. The aim is to grow every list in the audience table, not only the email list. [L0757, 2022]
- Lists to fill, by channel:

| Channel | Lists to fill |
|---|---|
| Email | Broadcast |
| Google | Website Visitors - 180 Days; Website Visitors - 30 Days; Website Visitors - 7 Days; Purchased - 180 Days; Purchased - 7 Days |
| Facebook | Website Visitors - 180 Days; Website Visitors - 30 Days; Website Visitors - 7 Days; Purchased - 180 Days; Purchased - 7 Days; FB Engagement - 180 Days; Insta Engagement - 180 Days |

  [L0757, 2022]
- Start small and expand as traffic grows. Build retargeting audiences for the products and content that matter to the business. More audience opportunities appear as traffic grows over time. [L0493, 2021-22] **

### Audience Types by Funnel Stage

- The units define middle-of-funnel (MOF) and bottom-of-funnel (BOF) audiences. They do not define top-of-funnel retargeting audiences. For cold traffic, the units point to search-intent and lookalike audiences. [L1386, 2020-26]
- Facebook remarketing can follow visitors who viewed low-margin product pages, even when those products are excluded from cold Google traffic. This builds brand association without buying cold traffic. [L1068, 2019] **

**Middle of funnel (MOF)**

- Facebook page engagement and Instagram engagement from the last 7, 14 or 30 days, and video viewers who watched 50-95 percent of a video. Create them in the custom audiences section of Business Manager. [L0648, undated]
- Sources differ on engagement and video windows:

| Audience | Source value | Context |
|---|---|---|
| Page and Instagram engagers | 7, 14 or 30 days | one source's narrow MOF pool [L0648, undated] |
| Page and Instagram engagers | 90 days | one source' look-back for this audience [L0315, 2026] |
| Facebook and Instagram engagement | 180 days | Ecom list-building for the dynamic audience set [L0757, 2022] |
| Video viewers | 50-95% watched | one source's MOF definition [L0648, undated] |
| Video viewers | 25%, 50% or 75% watched | one source' BOF table [L0315, 2026] |

  The units do not say which value is correct. Use the 7-30 day MOF pool when you want a narrow, recent audience, and the longer values when a bigger pool matters more than recency.

**Bottom of funnel (BOF)**

- BOF audiences are people who added to cart, started checkout, or purchased. [L0648, undated]
- Always exclude purchasers from retargeting campaigns, unless you are promoting another product to them. [L0648, undated]
- Past buyers and non-buyers should have separate remarketing audiences, with date ranges that vary across campaigns. The lists of people who bought and people who did not buy are the basis for this. [L0247, 2021] **

### Segmentation by Pages and Actions

- Segment retargeting audiences by how each visitor interacted with the store, such as which pages they viewed or whether they added to cart. Each segment should receive ads matched to that behaviour. [L1240, 2021-22] **
- Putting all visitors into one or very few retargeting segments gives generic ads that convert poorly. Split the cookie pool into several audiences with different intent levels. [L1390, 2021-22] **
- Using one broad audience, such as everyone who viewed a product in the last 30 days, misses opportunities. Segment audiences by funnel stage so each group receives more relevant content. [L0563, 2021-22] **
- Most people start by retargeting everyone who viewed a product page. That can be enough at very low traffic. As traffic grows, segment the cookie pool further so ads match each group's stage and interest. [L1390, 2021-22] **
- Audience types to consider (not an exhaustive list):

| Audience |
|---|
| Viewers of a specific product page |
| Viewers of all product pages |
| Viewers of a specific content page |
| Viewers of all content pages |
| Viewers of a content page but not a product page |
| Viewers of a collection page but not a product page |
| Viewers of a specific collection page |
| Viewers of the cart page (cart abandoners) |
| All website visitors |
| All blog readers |
| Users by location |
| Users by time on site |
| YouTube viewers (if you have a channel) |
| Video viewers (if you run Facebook Ads) |
| People who interact with the Facebook page |
| Previous customers |

  [L1390, 2021-22] **

**Procedure: building segmented audiences and ads**

1. Build a range of retargeting audiences tailored to specific pages and actions on the website. [L0414, 2021-22] **
2. Run a suite of retargeting ads that remind, build trust, educate, recommend and show why to buy from this business. [L0414, 2021-22] **
3. Vary the angle to reduce ad fatigue and match each prospect's stage. [L0414, 2021-22] **

**Procedure: starting from high-value visits**

1. Start by retargeting the highest-value visits, such as longer sessions and product or content engagement. [L0563, 2021-22] **
2. Use Analytics Audiences to build highly segmented retargeting audiences. [L0563, 2021-22] **
3. Deliver a different message to each segment based on its stage in the funnel. [L0563, 2021-22] **

### Quality Filters: Time on Site

- A standard retargeting pixel adds every visitor to the audience, including people who stay only 10 seconds on a content or product page. These visitors are very unlikely to buy, yet they are shown ads for 30 to 60 days. This wastes spend and lowers click-through rate. [L0563, 2021-22] **
- Focus retargeting on your most valuable potential customers: visitors who spend more time on the site and engage seriously with products and content. This tightens ad spend and improves conversion rate. [L0563, 2021-22] **
- A narrower product page audience can be limited to visitors who stayed on the product page for longer than 30 seconds. These visitors are further down the funnel than people who only glanced at the page. [L1169, 2021-22] **
- Visitors who stay about 10 seconds are rarely serious buyers. People who spend minutes on the site or on one page are considering a purchase. Filtering by duration focuses the budget on that group and raises click-through rate. [L1390, 2021-22] **
- Time on site is one of the audience types in the segmentation list ("Users by time on site"). [L1390, 2021-22] **

### Lookback Windows by Price and Traffic

**Standard windows for website visitors**

- Website-visitor audiences are commonly built at 7, 30, 60, 90 and 180 days. The right window depends on traffic volume. A store with heavy traffic can use a short 7-day audience as its main pool. A smaller store may need a 30- or 60-day window. [L0623, 2020-26]
- One lesson sets the remarketing audience window at 30 days: target only website visitors from the last 30 days. [L0189, undated]
- For each audience, create time-based versions covering the last 7, 14 and 30 days. [L1390, 2021-22] **

**Recency groups**

- Create separate audiences for people who visited in the last 7 days, 7-30 days, 30-60 days, and 60+ days. This lets you adjust messaging frequency and offer intensity for each recency group. [L0240, 2019]
- Three segment names to set up for remarketing: Website Visitors exc. Customers, 7 Days, Website Visitors exc. Customers, 30 Days, and Website Visitors exc. Customers, 180 Days. [L0165, undated]

**Price-based duration**

- Retargeting audience duration by product price (lesson guideline):

| Product price | Retargeting audience duration |
|---|---|
| Under $100 | 7 days |
| $100-$500 | 14 days |
| $500-$1,000 | 30 days |
| Over $1,000 | 60-90 days |

  [L1031, 2018]
- Higher-priced products get a longer audience window, because buyers usually take longer to decide on a larger purchase. Lower-priced products get a shorter window. [L1031, 2018]
- Shoppers typically make their purchase decision within two to five days of first visiting the store. Plan remarketing windows around that period. [L0972, 2025]

**Look-back periods by BOF audience**

| BOF Audience | Look-back Period |
|---|---|
| Website visitors | 7 days, 14 days, or 30 days |
| Video viewers | 25% watched, 50% watched, or 75% watched |
| Add-to-cart without purchase | 30 days |
| Checkout initiated without purchase | 30 days |
| Instagram/Facebook page engagers | 90 days |
| Email list (uploaded custom audience) | No expiration |

[L0315, 2026]

**Customer lists**

- Customer lists can use longer windows, such as three to four months. [L1390, 2021-22] **
- The uploaded email list has no expiration. [L0315, 2026] Use the three-to-four-month window for time-based customer audiences and the no-expiration setting for an uploaded custom audience.

**Shorter or longer windows**

- Shorter remarketing windows usually have a better return on ad spend. A 60-day window is still better than none if the 30-day audience is too small. [L1094, 2023]

### Narrow-Window Audiences

- Narrow window audience = longer audience minus shorter audience. Example: 14-day product viewers minus 7-day product viewers equals people who entered the audience between day 7 and day 14. Ad networks often cannot set this day range directly, so exclusion rules create it. [L1390, 2021-22] **
- A retargeting ad can be limited to visitors who viewed a product 7 to 14 days ago by targeting the 14-day product viewer audience and excluding the 7-day product viewer audience. This keeps the ads away from people who just visited and stops them once the visit is older than 14 days. [L1342, 2021-22] **
- A 7 to 14 day window reserves the ads for people who are still considering the product. Reaching them too early risks ads that feel pushy. This is a starting setup for the Google and Facebook examples and can be changed based on results. [L1342, 2021-22] **

**Procedure: building the remarketing window structure**

1. Create website visitor audiences for 180, 90, 60, 30, 14, 7 and 2 days, if each audience is big enough. [L1094, 2023]
2. Create one remarketing campaign per audience. [L1094, 2023]
3. In each campaign, target recent website visitors for that window and exclude visitors from the next shorter window: 180 excludes 90, 90 excludes 60, 60 excludes 30, 30 excludes 14, 14 excludes 7, 7 excludes 2, and 2 excludes nobody. [L1094, 2023]
4. Exclude recent customers in every campaign. [L1094, 2023]
5. Set the budget for each campaign separately. [L1094, 2023]

### Stacking

- Retargeting audience stacking layers a second targeting method on top of the website visitor audience to reach people actively researching. Example: YouTube content in the same category as the business, such as furniture for an office furniture store. [L1510, 2020-26]
- Audience size rule for stacking targeting filters:
  - under 5,000 users: do not add an extra topic or placement filter;
  - over 5,000 users: add a filter to target people actively searching the topic.

  [L1510, 2020-26]

### Retargeting Only Valuable Visitors

- Two sources give different defaults for the audience pool:
  - retarget every visitor who leaves the store without buying, using Google and Facebook remarketing from the start. This applies to all visitors, not only those who added items to the cart. [L1085, 2018]
  - Newer (2021-22): focus on the most valuable visitors, meaning those who spend more time and engage seriously. This tightens spend and raises conversion rate. [L0563, 2021-22] **
- Use the focused approach as the default. Reach every product viewer only when traffic is too low to segment, as the 2021-22 guidance allows. [L1390, 2021-22] **
- Filter by duration to keep the budget on visitors who are considering a purchase. [L1390, 2021-22] **
- Do not target people who have never visited the store. [L1020, 2020-26]

## Building Google Audiences (Google Ads and GA4)

### Account structure and setup order

- Google Ads and GA4 must be linked before remarketing audiences can be built from website data. Setup order: (1) link Google Ads to Google Analytics, (2) set up data settings in Google Analytics, (3) update data retention in Google Analytics, (4) create remarketing audiences. [L0165, undated]
- Create remarketing audiences early, even before any remarketing campaign is running, because they keep filling with users over time. An audience created later starts empty. [L1520, 2020-26]
- Google Ads Audience manager builds remarketing audiences from site visitors, app users, YouTube users and customer lists, and shows where each audience can be applied. Google also auto-creates some audiences there. [L1242, 2020-26]

### Google Ads: data segments in Audience manager

#### Concepts

- A data segment is a group of people who completed an action on the website, such as visiting a page or buying. Saving them as segments lets you target each group separately later. [L0420, 2020-26]
- A newly created audience does not show its size immediately. It can take several hours to populate. Only then does Google Ads show how many people it contains and can be targeted with remarketing ads. [L0420, 2020-26]
- The segment name is only for the account owner to recognise the audience later. It does not need to be perfect; choose a clear name that shows what the segment contains. [L0420, 2020-26]

#### Naming

- Name the website visitor segment so it identifies the store, the audience and the time window, for example "Store name, visitors 30 days". [L0420, 2020-26]
- Name the buyer segment in the same pattern, for example "Store name, purchase 30 days". [L0420, 2020-26]
- Use names that show the lookback window and the purpose, for example "540 day retargeting list for search". [L1242, 2020-26]

#### Step-by-step: create the two data segments

1. Sign in to Google Ads. [L0420, 2020-26]
2. Open **Tools** from the left-hand menu. [L0420, 2020-26]
3. Select **Shared library**, then **Audience manager**. If Shared library is collapsed, expand it with the caret beside it. [L0420, 2020-26]
4. Under **Data segments**, press the plus icon to start a new remarketing list. [L0420, 2020-26]
5. Choose **Website visitors**. [L0420, 2020-26]
6. Type a name for the visitor audience, such as "Store name, visitors 30 days". [L0420, 2020-26]
7. Leave **Segment members** set to *anyone who viewed a webpage in the last 30 days*. [L0420, 2020-26]
8. Keep the pre-fill option switched on, which adds people who visited in the past 30 days, so the segment starts with existing visitors. [L0420, 2020-26]
9. Click **Create segment** and allow several hours for the audience to fill. [L0420, 2020-26]
10. Press the plus icon again and select **Website visitors**. [L0420, 2020-26]
11. Name the buyer audience, for example "Store name, purchase 30 days". [L0420, 2020-26]
12. Change **Segment members** from *Visitors of webpages* to *Visitors of a page with specific tags*. [L0420, 2020-26]
13. Search for **Google Shopping** and confirm that *add to cart*, *begin checkout* and *purchase* are all listed. These events are installed through the Google Shopping feed app. [L0420, 2020-26]
14. Select the purchase event labelled **"purchase in category"**. [L0420, 2020-26]
15. Set the membership duration to **30 days**. [L0420, 2020-26]
16. Click **Create segment**. [L0420, 2020-26]
17. Check that both data segments appear in the list. [L0420, 2020-26]

#### Finding the audience

- To find the remarketing audience under Audience segments, search for the customer exclusion audience name, then scroll to the bottom of the list. The three most recently created audiences appear there, and the 30-day website visitor audience is the middle one of the three. [L0733, 2020-26]

### Google Ads: website visitor audiences by URL rule

#### Create the audience

1. Open Audience manager by typing "audience manager" into the search bar at the top right of Google Ads. [L1242, 2020-26]
2. The Remarketing tab opens by default. Click the plus sign. [L1242, 2020-26]
3. Select **Website visitors**. The other options are app users, YouTube users and customer list. [L1242, 2020-26]
4. Enter an audience name. [L1242, 2020-26]
5. Choose a visitor rule: [L1242, 2020-26]

| Visitor rule | Restriction options |
|---|---|
| Visitors of a page | URL match type only |
| Visitors of a page who also visited another page | Can be restricted to specific dates or tags |
| Visitors of a page who did not visit another page | Can be restricted to specific dates or tags |

6. For "Visitors of a page", pick the URL match type and enter the URL. The options are Contains, Equals, Starts with and Does not contain. [L1242, 2020-26]
7. Turn on the option to include people from the previous 30 days. [L1242, 2020-26]
8. Set the membership duration to **540 days**. [L1242, 2020-26]
9. Add a description. Copying the audience name works. [L1242, 2020-26]
10. Create the audience. [L1242, 2020-26]

#### Rule warning

- A single slash (/) as the URL rule matches all visitors to the site. Use it only when a broad audience is intended, and use specific URLs for narrower audiences. [L1242, 2020-26]

### GA4: audiences for non-purchasers and exclusions

#### Concepts

- GA4 creates two default audiences, **All users** and **Purchasers**. They can stay in place and do not need to be deleted to create new audiences. [L1520, 2020-26]
- Audiences in Google Analytics are segments of website users defined by past behaviour, such as visitors in the last 30 days or buyers in the last 7 days. They are created and managed in GA4 and later used to show remarketing ads. [L1520, 2020-26]
- Include and exclude settings are now combined in one GA4 audience. No separate exclusion setting is needed: edit the audience and set include (website visitors) and exclude (customers) together. [L0274, 2023]

#### Step-by-step: create the three non-purchaser audiences

1. Open Audiences from the left-hand navigation, or via Admin (gear icon, bottom left) and then Audiences in the property column. [L1520, 2020-26]
2. Click the blue **New audience** button. [L1520, 2020-26]
3. Choose **Non-purchasers (users that have not made a purchase)** as the audience basis. [L1520, 2020-26]
4. Rename the audience, for example **Website visitors exclude customers 7 days**. The default name is "Non-purchasers". [L1520, 2020-26]
5. Set the **Membership duration** to **7 days**. The form defaults to 30 days. [L1520, 2020-26]
6. Review the exclusions, which include anyone who has made a purchase, and the estimated audience size. [L1520, 2020-26]
7. Click **Save**. [L1520, 2020-26]
8. Click **New audience** again, choose Non-purchasers, rename it **Website visitors exclude customers 30 days** and keep the Membership duration at 30 days. Click **Save**. [L1520, 2020-26]
9. Click **New audience** again, choose Non-purchasers, rename it **Website visitors exclude customers 180 days** and change the Membership duration from 30 to 180 days. Click **Save**. [L1520, 2020-26]

#### Reference: names and durations

| Audience name | Membership duration |
|---|---|
| Website visitors exclude customers 7 days | 7 days |
| Website visitors exclude customers 30 days | 30 days |
| Website visitors exclude customers 180 days | 180 days |

[L1520, 2020-26]

#### Form layout

- In the GA4 new audience form, the Membership duration field, the audience name field and the Save button are all at the top of the form. [L1520, 2020-26]
- For each of the three non-purchaser audiences, only the name and the Membership duration change. All other settings stay as they are. [L1520, 2020-26]

#### How membership duration works (7-day example)

- A user who visited without buying stays in the audience for 7 days. They drop out on day 8 if they do not return. [L1520, 2020-26]
- A new visit restarts the 7-day count. [L1520, 2020-26]
- A purchase removes the user from the audience. [L1520, 2020-26]

### GA4: purchasers exclusion audience

#### Step-by-step: exclude existing customers from a 30-day visitor audience

1. In GA4, go to the Audiences list. [L0274, 2023]
2. Click the three dots on the audience, for example **30 Day Website Visitors**. [L0274, 2023]
3. Click **Edit**. [L0274, 2023]
4. Set the membership duration to **30 days**. [L0274, 2023]
5. Click **Include** and choose **website visitors, 30 days**. [L0274, 2023]
6. Click **Exclude** and choose **users with purchase**. [L0274, 2023]
7. Click **Save**. [L0274, 2023]

- Excluding purchasers prevents wasting spend on existing customers. [L0274, 2023]

### Data sources for audience building

#### Google Analytics page conditions

- Google Analytics page conditions in the Audience Builder offer the operators *contains*, *exactly matches*, *starts with* and *ends with*. [L1390, 2021-22]
- Facebook website URL rules offer *contains*, *equals*, *doesn't contain* and *at least one contains*. [L1390, 2021-22]
- Use *contains* with a short URL fragment unless you need one exact page. [L1390, 2021-22]
- Audiences built in Google Analytics can also be used with Google Optimize 360 to target users who return to the site. This is an additional use beyond Google Ads and Display remarketing. [L1390, 2021-22]
