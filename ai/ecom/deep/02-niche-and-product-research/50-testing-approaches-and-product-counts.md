### Testing Approaches and Product Counts

#### Choosing an Approach

- Product testing falls into three approaches: [L1158, 2023]
  - Spam testing: many products tested quickly with little thought.
  - Continuous testing: a steady flow of products checked against fixed rules.
  - Branded testing: building a brand around one product with custom content and quality control.
  - Spam testing is not recommended, because it depends on luck and needs a large budget to test 50 to 60 products. [L1158, 2023]
- Spam testing means testing roughly 3 to 5 products per day or 10 to 15 per week, with a hit rate of about 2 to 3 winners out of 10 tested products. [L1158, 2023]
- Continuous testing with rule sets means testing about 1 to 2 products per week against defined criteria, rather than being tied to a single product or niche. [L1158, 2023]
- The recommended approach combines rule-based continuous testing with a light branded approach, adding brand elements without investing heavily upfront. [L1158, 2023]
- Product tests should be good enough rather than perfect, and should not consume much time or money, since a test can fail and the time spent should still produce learning. [L0349, 2024]
- Early on, test products aggressively, since you cannot yet tell which products are good. Once you have a viral win and know how to create virality, shift to strict product selection. [L0372, 2022]
- Early tests on weak products are useful for building the skill of going viral. Before investing heavily in a product, confirm it can produce sales. [L1429, 2022]
- Product research has no shortcut. A product's potential is only known after spending money on it. Learn audience research as the core skill rather than relying on hiring freelancers for research. [L0690, 2022+]
- No single product research method is perfect, so a researcher should adapt a method that works for them rather than follow one rigidly. A method is worth keeping only when it has produced winners for that person. [L1382, undated]
- Product testing is about spending time and money efficiently. [L0577, 2023]

#### How Many Products to Test

| Count | Context | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 20 to 30 products on Facebook to find at least one winner; three winners is a better result | Beginners on Facebook; if no winner after about 30 products, revise the approach | [L0349, 2024] |
| 10 to 20 products before expecting a winner | Beginner; two tested products is very early | [L0361, 2024] |
| About 6 to 7 products before a hit | Referenced creator; keep testing new products continuously | [L0821, undated] |
| 5 to 6 products, about 300 USD each | Budget-limited batch before expecting a winner | [L1017, undated] |
| 3 to 5 products before the next winner | Planning expectation: several failures are normal | [L0388, 2024+] |
| 6 to 7 products from a list of 10 to 15 | Beginner test limit; later narrow to about 3 to 6 | [L0708, undated] |
| About 3 products at once | When budget and time allow | [L0995, 2023] |
| About 3 segments or marketing messages | Limit to what budget and time can support | [L0999, 2023] |

- The counts above differ because they answer different questions. The 20 to 30 figure is for beginners testing on Facebook, where three winners is the goal. The 10 to 20 and 5 to 6 figures are for budget-limited testing. The 6 to 7 figure from the referenced creator and the 6 to 7 test limit in the beginner list both describe a batch of tests, not a total lifetime count. Use the 20 to 30 range as the upper planning frame for a beginner, and the 5 to 6 batch at about 300 USD each when the budget is the constraint. [L0349, 2024; L0361, 2024; L0821, undated; L1017, undated; L0708, undated]
- Test many products rather than a few. Two tested products is very early, and a beginner should aim to test about 10 to 20 products before expecting a winner. [L0361, 2024]
- Limit the number of product tests so each product gets enough effort and budget. Dividing effort across too many products dilutes each one. [L0995, 2023]
- Limit the number of products or segments to what the budget and time can support. Testing about three product segments or marketing messages is a reasonable number, since spreading effort too thin means none gets enough attention to land. [L0999, 2023]
- Expect several failed product tests before finding a winner. Plan for testing about three to five products before finding the next one. [L0388, 2024+]
- Expect to test several products before one becomes a winner. The referenced creator needed about 6 to 7 different products before finding a hit. Keep testing new products continuously, because a winner cannot be predicted in advance. [L0821, undated]
- Testing more than ten products in about six months without scaling any single winner tends to leave a store at break-even. Concentrate budget on fewer products that show clear data before moving on. [L1172, 2023+]
- A beginner's first product was a beauty product, eyelashes, which failed because the store and brand were over-built before any sales. The seller scrapped it and tested about 10 products over seven to eight months, spending about two weeks on each product. Later the rule became: if the first three days show no views, drop the product. [L0820, undated]
- For one product test, choose one niche you genuinely like and pick five products within it. The same pixel and page system can then serve all five products. [L1194, undated]
- Test a different product every few days when testing fashion items. The example given is testing a new pair of shoes every third day in Australia until a winner appears. [L1316, 2024+]
- The product funnel narrows through validation. Starting from 10 to 15 candidates, a list typically shrinks to about 3 to 6 products after the quality and market checks. Only these remaining products should be tested with a landing page and ads. [L0708, undated]
- Keep testing new products continuously, and research should begin before the current winner is exhausted. [L0821, undated; L1184, 2023+]
- Research is a continuous task that should be done even while a winning product is already running, so a tested next product is always ready. Research should start from broad niches and narrow down to specific products. [L1184, 2023+]

#### Working With Sales Targets in Each Approach

- Products that sell at least one order a day can be reviewed weekly, while the rest of the list is refreshed. [L1158, 2023]

### Test Budgets and Loss Thresholds

#### Budget Anchors

| Budget | Context | Source |
|---|---|---|
| About 200 USD | First test of a product selected with the Peak method | [L0577, 2023] |
| About 300 USD per product | Test of 5 to 6 products before expecting a winner | [L1017, undated] |
| About 300 USD across several offers, creatives and audiences | Ad with good CTR but no sales; move on when no longer good | [L0650, undated] |
| Ad loss around 300 USD | Stop threshold; depends on how much has been spent | [L1127, 2024] |
| About 2,500 GBP over two weeks | Owner's early testing to find what worked | [L1077, undated] |
| 5,000 names instead of 50,000 | Small mailing test before committing a large budget | [L1366, undated] |

- Test a product until the ad loss reaches about $300, then stop. The threshold depends on how much has been spent, so check the Facebook and Shopify metrics before deciding to test a few more days. [L1127, 2024]
- Many products must be tested before committing. In the owner's early testing, about £2,500 was spent over two weeks on ads to find what worked, and the first product lost about £150 before it broke even and turned profitable. [L1077, undated]
- Test a new mailing with a small list before committing a large budget, such as 5,000 names instead of 50,000. A small test reveals whether the offer, the copy and the list work before the full cost is at risk. [L1366, undated]
- Do not spend heavily on a product before it has produced sales. Confirm it can sell first. [L1429, 2022]

#### Time Budgets for Research

- Spend about 6 to 8 hours on initial market research per product before testing. This is considered a reasonable investment. [L0363, 2022+] **
- Budget time per niche: about one hour at most, not days for the whole sheet. Doing the full check on an outdoor kitchen niche took about 15 minutes using this method. [L0459, 2020-26] **
- Spend only the research time that a niche needs. After the first few niches, you start to recognize patterns and can drop weak niches quickly, while the niches that need more work get extra time. [L0667, 2025] **
- The two figures measure different units: 6 to 8 hours covers one product in depth, while one hour covers a whole niche screen. Use the hour for screening niches and the 6 to 8 hours for products you are about to test. [L0363, 2022+; L0459, 2020-26]

### Test Duration and Stop Rules

#### Duration Windows

| Window | Context | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Three days per offer or interest | Paid ad testing; fixed period to avoid endless testing | [L0650, undated] |
| Two to three days | Check whether demand holds for a book product after launching ads | [L0354, 2026] |
| Three to four days of ad data | Give a new product this long before judging; if still weak, replace the page | [L1412, 2023] |
| First three days with no views | TikTok or organic content; drop the product | [L0820, undated] |
| About two weeks | TikTok product testing window: many views but no sales means drop | [L0819, 2020+] |
| Two weeks plus one more week | Organic case where sales began the day after an extra week of posting | [L0820, undated] |
| Last 30 days | Revenue check before committing to a product | [L1479, 2026] |

- Interest and product testing should be done over a fixed period of three days per offer or interest, to give enough time for data without losing money to endless testing. [L0650, undated]
- Test a product for two to three days after launching ads to see whether demand holds before going further. [L0354, 2026]
- Give a new product about three to four days of ad data before judging it. If it still underperforms after that, replace it with a new product page and test the next product. [L1412, 2023]
- A product testing window of about two weeks was used on TikTok. If a product has many views but no sales after two weeks, it is dropped in favor of another product or account rather than tested for weeks more. [L0819, 2020+]
- Do not judge a product by a two-week test alone. Another seller's version of a product was reported to go viral about a month after the first seller gave up, so combine views, store sessions and sales before deciding to quit. [L1444, 2022]
- A winning product was found after a series of tests. After two weeks the seller wanted to quit, but one more week of posting produced sales starting the next day. Daily sales rose from around 5,000 to 20,000, and the follower count grew from 10,000 to 20,000 and beyond 100,000. [L0820, undated]
- Creative tests should use at least 12 videos taken from the last 30 days. The range for a test is 12 to 24 videos. [L0704, 2026]

#### Stop Rules

- If an ad has a good CTR but no sales after spending a lot (about $300 spent across several offers, creatives, and audiences), move on to the next product, since the data is no longer good. Continue only when the data is good. [L0650, undated]
- When a store has a good CTR but poor sales, test a different offer before moving on to another product. A good CTR means the ad is doing its job, and the offer is the likely problem. [L0650, undated]
- A loss of around $300 with sales still coming in can justify stopping, while a good CTR with few offers tested means more offers should be tested first. [L0650, undated]
- If the first two or three sales come in, stop or scale based on the results. [L0552, 2023]
- If a product generates some sales but cannot be scaled, stop it rather than forcing it, and move the budget to the next product test. Scaling potential matters as much as early sales when choosing which product to keep. [L0349, 2024]
- Use a rule set for every product: if the CTR is good, continue; if it is bad, try new creatives; if it is still bad after new creatives, move to the next product. [L0502, 2020+]
- Switch immediately to the next product when the CTR is bad. [L1184, 2023+]
- Products that miss criteria are dropped, not tested further. [L1194, undated]

#### Procedures With Stop Rules

- Saturation and trend procedure for a product:
  1. Check when the first ad for the product started running. [L0502, 2020+]
  2. Look for viral ads already run for the product; if there are many, the market may be saturated. [L0502, 2020+]
  3. Search TikTok for old trending videos; if trends from several years ago are still circulating, the trend has likely passed. [L0502, 2020+]
  4. Check the product in ad-spy apps for old ads. [L0502, 2020+]
  5. Test it with ads and watch the CTR: if the CTR is good, continue; if it is bad, try new creatives; if it is still bad after new creatives, move to the next product. [L0502, 2020+]
- Women's product test:
  1. Pick a niche from Jungle Scout or similar tools. [L1184, 2023+]
  2. Search women's (or target-audience) products on AliExpress. [L1184, 2023+]
  3. Sort the results by likes or orders. [L1184, 2023+]
  4. Choose the designs you like most. [L1184, 2023+]
  5. Run carousel ads for them. [L1184, 2023+]
  6. Check the CTR: if it is good, continue with the product; if it is bad, switch immediately to the next product. [L1184, 2023+]

#### Sales Proof

- A common product research guideline is a minimum of 300 orders before a product is considered proven. Treat this as a guideline and not an absolute rule, since some products with strong potential sell below this level. [L0644, undated]
- The 300-order guideline is for judging a product as proven. Early stages need only a few sales to justify continuing, as set out in the stop rules above. [L0644, undated; L0552, 2023; L1219, undated]

### Diagnosing a Test: What to Change

- Check a strong CTR before changing anything else. If a store has a good CTR and poor sales, test a different offer before moving on, because the ad is doing its job. [L0650, undated]
- Diagnose the audience, product, videos or a mix before changing everything. [L0764, 2022]
- Test many products rather than a few, but change one variable at a time when diagnosing a test so you know what caused the result. [L0764, 2022; L0361, 2024]
- Keep one fixed core audience, such as women over 40, and test every product against it, so the ad data stays comparable from product to product. Consistent audience data makes it faster to tell which products work. [L1184, 2023+]
- Test with ripped creatives: the test measures the product and the offer, not the quality of the creative. Creative quality is improved only later. [L1252, 2026]
- A creative test uses at least 12 videos from the last 30 days, with a range of 12 to 24 videos. [L0704, 2026]
- For a collection of many similar products, test sending ads to the collection page or a landing page built from it, not only to individual product pages. A collection landing page can add a hero section, testimonials and a product list. [L1315, 2023]
- Test several designs or products, and send traffic both to collection pages and to individual hero products. [L1217, 2023+]
- Give a product new ad data before changing it. A product that does not perform after three to four days is replaced with a new product page. [L1412, 2023]

#### Google Shopping Diagnosis

- Diagnosing high impressions with low clicks on Google Shopping: [L1085, 2018] **
  1. In Google Ads, check which search terms and keywords trigger your ads. [L1085, 2018]
  2. If the keywords are very broad, accept a low click-through rate and add negative keywords to cut irrelevant searches. [L1085, 2018]
  3. Compare your price, promotions (such as free shipping), product images and ad position with the competitors showing for the same keywords. [L1085, 2018]
  4. Search the keyword yourself as a customer and fix what makes your listing less clickable. [L1085, 2018]
- Diagnosing a 50% impression rate with only a few clicks per day: [L1085, 2018] **
  1. Open Google Keyword Planner and check the monthly search volume of your keyword. [L1085, 2018]
  2. If search volume is low, people are not searching for the product, so reconsider the keyword or product. [L1085, 2018]
  3. If search volume is high, search the keyword on Google as a customer and compare your listing with the others. [L1085, 2018]
  4. Check whether your price, your image or the listing itself makes people skip your product. [L1085, 2018]
- Google tests are for established products, while new product tests run on Facebook and TikTok. [L0577, 2023; L0920, 2018]

### Scaling, Stock and Commitment

- Track clicks and early traction on a product to decide whether to scale it, with profitability checked alongside demand. A trending product is only useful if the margin can still work at higher volume. [L1508, 2022+]
- A trending product list is used this way: [L1508, 2022+]
  1. Research which products are trending in the niche. [L1508, 2022+]
  2. List products that are not your own to test whether they get clicks. [L1508, 2022+]
  3. If the products get clicks and traction, adjust the pricing. [L1508, 2022+]
  4. Check that the product can be profitable at scale, for example by lowering cost through bulk shipping or a faster shipping method. [L1508, 2022+]
  5. Go back to the manufacturers, replicate the winning product, and expand into its category. [L1508, 2022+]
- Once sales or sign-ups are coming in, you have proof the product sells, so you can justify the supplier order. [L1017, undated]
- For a new product, run ads and collect sales or pre-order sign-ups. [L1017, undated]
- If the product does not ship on time, email the buyers, explain the situation, and either refund them or give them the expected delivery window if they are willing to wait. [L1017, undated]
- Stock procedure for confirmed winners:
  1. Test several designs or products and send traffic both to collection pages and to individual hero products. [L1217, 2023+]
  2. Check at least two weeks of sales data to find the top sellers and the best-selling variant of each. [L1217, 2023+]
  3. Confirm that the best seller keeps selling consistently for about three weeks. [L1217, 2023+]
  4. Order stock for about one month of sales for each confirmed top product. [L1217, 2023+]
  5. During the stock order, keep ad parameters and performance unchanged, and watch sales for the next month. [L1217, 2023+]
  6. If performance stays consistent or improves, scale the ad budget and order stock in proportion to the increase. [L1217, 2023+]
- Scaling potential matters as much as early sales when choosing which product to keep. [L0349, 2024]
- Only scale after validation shows the product sells consistently. [L0914, 2026]
- Spot-check your top 10 to 20 selling products regularly. For each product, check the Google Ads results, the Bing Ads results, and the top organic search results. [L0920, 2018] **
- Optimize the top products rather than the whole catalog. A supplier catalog with about 1,000 SKUs is updated first on the top 20%, which can be identified with the 80-20 rule. [L0272, 2022] **
- In a catalog of about 2,000 products, typically 200 to 300 products are the best sellers, so use a 200-product group as the starting point for paid campaigns. [L0471, 2024] **

#### Catalog Optimization Procedure

- Upload the supplier's full product catalog (e.g., 1,000 SKUs) with only SEO modifications: product name and meta descriptions. Do not rewrite product descriptions. [L0272, 2022] **
- Monitor Google Analytics and Google Ads data for 3+ months to identify which products gain traction. [L0272, 2022]
- Apply the 80-20 rule: identify the top 20% of products (200 from 1,000) generating impressions and clicks. [L0272, 2022]
- Further optimize only those top 200 by improving descriptions, enhancing pages, and increasing ad spend, while leaving others as they are. [L0272, 2022]
- Continue until performance plateaus, then repeat for the next product tier. [L0272, 2022]

### Relaunching and Revisiting Products

- Do not abandon a niche that already shows sales and buyer trust, such as about 11,000 USD in two months. First optimize the existing ads and products within it. [L0836, 2024] **
- Expect the plan and the niche to change as sales data comes in. Adjust rather than hold to the first idea, and revisit projected sales regularly to see which direction works. [L0832, 2019] **
- If a niche was chosen using the blueprint criteria and is not profitable, do not switch niches right away. Troubleshoot the supplier, product pages, traffic and conversion elements first, because a new niche will often show the same problems. [L1086, 2019] **
- After passing the brand and product count test, continue with the evergreen demand test and price point tests. A niche that passes the count test still needs demand over time and workable price points. [L0357, 2019] **
- Systematic process for finding more products after a winner: [L1440, 2021+]
  1. After every test, review each funnel stage: the ad, the store and the product, and find the weak point. [L1440, 2021+]
  2. Use a strong click-through rate as proof that an ad angle works, and replicate that angle with other products. [L1440, 2021+]
  3. Research products in the same niche continuously. [L1440, 2021+]
  4. Study competitors: what they sell and which marketing angles they use. [L1440, 2021+]
  5. Look for angles others are not using, for example in Amazon reviews. [L1440, 2021+]
  6. Revive older products with new marketing angles. [L1440, 2021+]
  7. Use customer reviews and feedback to improve the product, working with agents and manufacturers if needed; this improvement took about one to two months. [L1440, 2021+]
- Revisit a product once a new marketing angle or a new product page is ready. A product that failed a test can be retried with a new page, as set out in the three-to-four-day rule. [L1412, 2023; L1440, 2021+]
- Lessons from each failed product test should carry into the next test, so product page, copy and creatives improve from test to test instead of starting from zero each time. Product selection should become stricter as the operator gains experience. [L0349, 2024]
- If a product that does not perform is revived, keep the same core audience so the data stays comparable. [L1184, 2023+]
