### Brand loyalty: categories that pass

- Persian rugs show low brand loyalty. Customers care about quality and style, not the manufacturer, so the niche is acceptable even though rugs are sold in big-box stores. [L0021, undated]
- Stand-up paddle boards have no dominant brand equivalent like Apple or Bose. Customers buy on appearance and price fit, with no requirement for brand loyalty. [L0319, 2020-26]
- Stand-up paddle boarding is a product niche with low brand loyalty and a good fit for a niche list. Its buyers ask for the product itself, not a specific manufacturer. [L0988, 2020-26]
- Chandeliers are expensive products without brand loyalty that appeal to upper-middle-class customers. Numerous variations in style, size, color and light count make them suitable for online dropshipping. [L0319, 2020-26]
- Entryway chandeliers work well online because customers search by specific style and dimensions rather than manufacturer. A homeowner who cannot find a preferred 30" x 30" design locally will buy online when they find an exact match, regardless of brand. Upper-middle-class homeowners renovating create sustained demand. [L0022, undated]
- Wall lighting fixtures are an effective niche and a strong cross-sell product for chandelier stores, because customers do not require specific brands. [L0319, 2020-26]
- Table lamps sell well because customers seek appearance and design, not brand identity. [L0319, 2020-26]
- Crown molding is suitable for dropshipping because it comes in many styles and designs. Customers choose on aesthetic fit, not brand. [L0319, 2020-26]
- Wallpaper is brand-generic. Customers buy multiple rolls for whole houses, generating multi-thousand-dollar orders, because style and pattern, not manufacturer, drive the purchase. [L0319, 2020-26]
- Interior designers are a key buyer segment in wallpaper-type niches. They buy for clients and search for exact pattern, color and texture names. Products that match these specific searches can attract buyers who would not find them at large retailers. [L1125, 2020-26]
- Mirrors and decorative vases sell effectively at appropriate price points. Customers do not prioritize brand when these products match their aesthetic vision. [L0319, 2020-26]
- Wooden flooring customers select by color, width and pattern. Brand identity does not influence the decision, which makes wooden flooring suitable for dropshipping. [L0319, 2020-26]
- Coffee tables, daybeds, fireplaces, chairs, shelving and other furniture items are suitable for dropshipping because customers do not require specific brands. [L0319, 2020-26]
- Low brand loyalty home and décor items with multiple manufacturers include stand-up paddle boards, chandeliers, wall lighting, table lamps, crown molding, wallpaper, coffee tables, sofas, fireplaces, mirrors, vases, bookshelves, dining tables and chairs, and wood flooring. Customers focus on style, function and price rather than brand. [L0021, undated]
- Standup desks show wide manufacturer variation and are marked no for brand loyalty. [L0455, 2020-26]

### Brand loyalty test with Google Shopping

- Google Shopping is the data check for brand loyalty. Reach it by searching the niche keyword on google.com and opening the Shopping results. It shows which brands appear on the first results page. [L0455, 2020-26]

**Procedure: brand loyalty test for each niche**

1. Ask yourself whether any brand comes to mind immediately when you imagine buying a product in the niche. If none does, this points toward no. [L0455, 2020-26]
2. Copy the niche keyword, for example "chandelier", and search for it on google.com. [L0455, 2020-26]
3. Open the Shopping results. [L0455, 2020-26]
4. Review the brands shown on the first results page, including the brand list in the sidebar. [L0455, 2020-26]
5. Check the product names for manufacturer names. [L0455, 2020-26]
6. Decide: if the same few brand names keep repeating, mark yes; if many different and unfamiliar companies appear, mark no. [L0455, 2020-26]
7. If you marked yes, move the niche to the bottom of the list and continue researching the other niches. If you marked no, the niche can stay at the top as a candidate. [L0455, 2020-26]

- Judge the companies that manufacture the products, not the online stores that sell them. Ignore store names on Google Shopping when judging brand loyalty. [L0455, 2020-26]
- Do not confuse the retailer selling a product with the brand that makes it. Counting store names as brands gives a false picture of brand loyalty. [L0455, 2020-26]
- The sidebar on Google Shopping can list both retailers and brands. For standup desks, retailers such as Standup Desk Store, Vivo, Luxor, Simple Stuff and Costway appear alongside brands, so retailer names must be filtered out. [L0455, 2020-26]
- There is no fixed number of repeated brand names that defines brand loyalty on Google Shopping. The judgement rests on whether the first results page repeats the same brands or shows wide variation across many companies. [L0455, 2020-26]
- The brand loyalty column on the niche research worksheet takes only a yes or no answer. Mark yes when the same brands dominate the results and no when there is wide variation. [L0455, 2020-26]
- Switch Google Shopping from list view to grid view to scan many brand names quickly on the first results page. [L0455, 2020-26]

### Retailers and big-box stores

- A product sold in big-box stores is acceptable for niche selection. A niche controlled by a few powerful brands is not. [L0021, undated]
- Being sold in big-box stores does not disqualify a niche by itself. Persian rugs sold at Home Depot or Lowe's remain acceptable because buyers ask for the rug, not a brand. [L0988, 2020-26]
- Treating any product sold in big-box stores as a bad niche is a mistake. Look at whether buyers ask for a specific brand, not at where the product is sold. [L0988, 2020-26]
- Large retailers selling a product category do not disqualify it, because individual brands are often sold in only a few stores. [L1069, 2019]
- Niche validity is judged per product. A niche with many brands but few stores selling them is fine. If few advertisers are in Google Merchant Center or the free listings, that is an advantage. (2024) [L0856, 2024]
- Exclude a niche where big established retailers sell most of the brands. Focus on the brands those large retailers do not sell. [L0961, 2020]
- Avoid brands that are mainly sold by large retailers. Choose suppliers among the many makers of a product type rather than the few brands big retailers stock. [L0853, 2021]
- Local stores that stock only a few top brands do not rule out an online niche. If online-only retailers in the niche collectively list hundreds of brands that cannot be bought locally, there is enough variety to support online demand. [L0834, 2021]

**How the sources fit together**

- The 2019 units say big-box presence and large retailers do not disqualify a niche. The 2020 and 2021 units say to exclude a niche where big retailers sell most of the brands, and to avoid brands mainly stocked by large retailers. Apply the niche-level rule (big-box presence is acceptable) and the supplier-level rule (choose brands the large retailers do not carry). [L1069, 2019; L0357, 2019; L0961, 2020; L0853, 2021; L0021, undated; L0988, 2020-26]

### Variety, competition and failed tests

- Before choosing a niche, verify it is not dominated by a few large brands. Tablet buyers go directly to Apple.com or Costco, so the market has little variety. Good niches have many products and brands, so customers search broadly within the category. [L0270, 2021]
- A niche where only big brands sell and no dropship-friendly competitors exist is a failed test. Niche up, sideways or pivot. (2023) [L0855, 2023]

### Validating how people buy

- Validate a niche by checking whether people buy out of pocket or through third parties. Hospital beds and mobility equipment are often purchased through insurance companies, which reduces profitability. Ask suppliers or competitors to understand actual purchase behavior. [L0270, 2021]
- Medical and mobility items such as stairlifts, lift chairs, CPAP machines and mobility scooters are often bought by older adults or caregivers. Health or medical claims in ads may face platform or regulatory limits in some markets. [L0546, undated]

### Consumer and business buyers

- Research B2B niches the same way as B2C: check competitor traffic, Google Shopping competition and monthly search volume. Buyers still search and buy online, even if product names return few results. (2024) [L0971, 2024]
- Whether a niche has business buyers should not be a factor in niche selection. Consumer-only and mixed markets can both be profitable, so judge a niche on its other criteria. (2025) [L0972, 2025]
- The upper middle class label does not apply to business buyers, who are used to spending money. [L1109, 2018]
- Business buyers should not be ruled out by the demographic label; the 2025 approach judges by other criteria, while the 2018 label is a service-level description only. [L0972, 2025; L1109, 2018]

### Repeat purchases and cross-sell

- Repeat purchases are rare in most niches, and most traffic comes from first-time visitors. Use upsells, cross-sells, referral discounts for family and friends, and affiliate content for indirectly related products to get additional value. (2023) [L1283, 2023]
- Wall lighting is a strong cross-sell for chandelier stores. [L0319, 2020-26]

### Audience segments beyond income

- Track buyer demographics in the sales data of winning products. In the example, the winning products mostly sold to women aged 45 and older, which helps refine audiences. [L1131, 2018+]
- Find the customer segment that converts best in your past stores and write the research brief around its needs. In one tested case, the best audience was women over 45, so every product search was framed around their needs. [L1358, undated]
- A dropshipping owner who has tested products, niches and countries over several years may find that one audience clearly outperforms the others, such as women over 45 in one documented case. Product research can then be filtered to that audience's needs, which gives a team member a precise target. [L1360, undated]
- Women over 35 were the most profitable audience for fashion, jewelry, home decor and fitness items, while products aimed at men performed worse. Test this audience first when researching products for Germany and Australia. [L1431, 2020+]
- Some sellers found that men aged roughly 35 and above were the easiest customers, generating few support emails, while women over 45 were another reliable group. The choice depends on which audience your own sales data shows. [L1432, undated]
- If a product has two different buyer groups, such as partners buying gifts and parents buying for children, reach each group through creators whose followers belong to that group. Look at the followers of creators who already post content with that buyer type. [L1379, 2020+]
- For Valentine's Day, focusing only on women as buyers is a common error. Men and parents also buy gifts on that day. [L1469, 2023+]
- A jewelry store offered only one product at a time to organic traffic. About 30 percent of its viewers turned out to be men, so men's products were added to the store and purchases from men increased. [L1469, 2023+]
- A product can be repositioned by changing who buys it. A product first aimed at women was marketed to men as a gift, and that switch in buyer and angle opened new demand. Test different buyer groups with the same product before dropping it. [L0552, 2023]
- A food vacuum sealer is more likely to sell to people storing dry goods in bulk, such as nuts, oats or coffee beans, than to daily cooks who can use jars. Target the bulk-storage buyer and check whether these buyers would need several units. [L1186, 2022]
- A product with a niche audience, such as alcohol-related items, can be limiting and may need a broader angle or a different target group to scale. [L0656, undated]
- A product tied to a cultural or national identity, such as a map necklace of a home region, can reach a large audience of people who feel connected to it. Target that diaspora in several Western countries such as the US, UK and France. Offer a second item the buyer chooses for their home country as a free gift to raise the perceived value of the order. [L0661, undated]

### Broad targeting and narrowing

- Start with broad interests and a broad audience. Narrow to the age, gender and interest that win once a winning audience, age, gender and interest are found, and do not niche down before a winner exists. [L0773, 2023; L0774, 2023]

### Enthusiast markets

- An enthusiast market is one where people buy because they want the products and enjoy them, not only because they need them. Enthusiast markets are easier and cheaper to market than need-based ones, such as fridges, which the lesson treats as not enthusiast. [L0458, 2021-22]
- A niche is an enthusiast market when buyers want the products for a hobby or recreation rather than need them. Products people buy to create things always count as enthusiast. [L0490, 2021-22]
- Survival and off-grid gear is classed as a want, not a need, because buyers can still live on the grid. For this reason it counts as an enthusiast market. [L0490, 2021-22]
- Target an enthusiast market: customers who buy products for a hobby, passion or activity they choose, not something they must do daily. Examples include surfboards, stand-up paddle boards and kiteboarding gear. [L0994, 2021-22]
- Enthusiast buyers usually have more disposable income and want the product, so a high price is less of a barrier. They also haggle less, which makes customer service and marketing easier. [L0994, 2021-22]
- Fitness equipment such as exercise bikes and cardio machines is a good fit: enthusiasts recognize several brands from gyms and fitness media, while outsiders do not. If you already recognize every brand in a niche without researching it, the niche is probably too mainstream. [L0994, 2021-22]

#### Further details

- If uncertain whether a niche has high or low brand loyalty, do not delete it yet. the niche check step software automatically analyzes brand loyalty for all niches and identifies which ones fail this test. [L0021, undated]

- Launch your niche research worksheet. Survey your niche list and note which products instantly call to mind specific brand names. Demote or remove niches that are plainly dominated by one or two major brands. Retain niches where dominance is unclear-they're candidates for the niche check step testing. Finalize and check off your list. [L0021, undated]

- Remove niches where a few big brands dominate and shoppers pick a specific brand by default, such as tablets, headphones, digital cameras, gaming consoles, laptops and speaker systems. These niches usually pass other product filters but fail the brand loyalty test. [L0988, 2020-26]

Mark this lesson step as complete and move on to the next task. [L0988, 2020-26]

- Deleting niches you are not sure about wastes good options early on. Only remove niches that are clearly dominated by a few brands, and let a later check decide the rest. [L0988, 2020-26]
