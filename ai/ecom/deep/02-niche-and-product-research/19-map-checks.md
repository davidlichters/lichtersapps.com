### MAP Checks

- MAP (minimum advertised price) enforcement: check whether a supplier enforces MAP before contacting them.
  1. Find the supplier's bestselling product.
  2. Search for that product on Google Shopping.
  3. Check whether all sellers show the same price. If they do, the supplier likely enforces MAP. [L0804, 2023]
- Check MAP quickly with Google Shopping:
  1. Take the most popular products from each brand on your list.
  2. Search each product name on Google and click Shopping.
  3. Compare the prices of the stores. Example: four stores at 1,000 and one at 1,100 means MAP is likely 1,000. Five stores where one sells at 800 means a MAP violation or no MAP. [L0963, 2022]
- Check whether a supplier enforces MAP before approval by searching several of its products on Google Shopping and comparing what stores advertise. If prices are nearly identical, the supplier is likely MAP-enforcing. If some sellers undercut the others substantially, it likely is not. [L0964, 2023]
- Test whether a competitor is violating MAP on Google Shopping:
  1. Search the product and read the prices of all listings, including free ones, from three or more stores.
  2. Check the seller of each listing.
  3. If most sellers show one price and one or two show a much lower price, the common price is likely the MAP and the lower one is a violation.
  4. Check the price against the supplier's MAP policy before acting on it. [L1094, 2023]
- MAP enforcement reality: [L0856, 2024]
  - A store with supplier approvals from a MAP-enforcing supplier can still work if the niche has many shops.
  - For non-MAP suppliers, most traffic must come from organic sources. Paid traffic is harder when competitors undercut price.
  - A store with only non-MAP suppliers should sell mostly through organic traffic and should seek MAP-enforcing suppliers as the main fix.
- Non-MAP suppliers can be used only with caution. Check the linked video on profiting with non-MAP suppliers before proceeding. [L0964, 2023]
- Non-MAP suppliers sell products that competitors can undercut. [L0856, 2024]
- Be cautious with some baby product sub-niches, such as strollers, where suppliers may give large retailers lower MAP tiers. That makes smaller stores undercut each other or fall below expected prices. Verify MAP is uniform before building. [L0469, 2021]
- Before committing to baby strollers, check the pricing structure. Hearsay suggests major brands give retail stores better wholesale and MAP pricing than online-only sellers, and online-only margins can disappear under such pricing. [L0960, 2019]
- In some niches, such as car seats, physical stores get preferred wholesale pricing and can sell at a different MAP than online-only sellers. If a competitor sells a brand well below the others on Google Shopping, check that brand's MAP policy before advertising. [L0968, 2021]
- Inventory delays from port backlogs affect only a minority of brands, but the risk is real. Sell for many brands rather than two or three, so one supplier's stockout does not sink the store. [L1090, 2021]

### Quality and Reputation Checks

- Before contacting a brand, research its reviews and check for repeated complaints about product quality, shipping or packaging. Brands with frequent negative feedback on these points tend to cause returns, so skip them. [L0970, 2023]
- Check product reviews on competitor sites before choosing a supplier. Repeated reports of damage or poor quality for a product are a reason to avoid that supplier. [L0962, 2021]
- Check supplier product quality before onboarding:
  1. Search the supplier company name together with the word "reviews."
  2. Look at product reviews on future competitor sites.
  3. If you see repeated complaints like breakage or quality issues, treat it as a warning. [L0804, 2023]
- Research a brand's reputation before contacting it:
  1. Search the brand name on Google.
  2. Search the brand name followed by the word "reviews."
  3. Look for quality complaints on competitor websites that sell the same brand.
  4. Use findings to decide whether to contact the brand or skip it. [L0468, 2020]
- Find suppliers on competitor websites that have a traffic rank within the top one million. That indicates real traffic and suggests the site is converting visitors into sales. [L0970, 2023]

### Supplier Tiers and Supplier Types

- Use supplier tiers to decide which brands to contact. Gold and silver brands are contacted; bronze brands are excluded. The tier is based on the brand's online reputation and how it deals with resellers. [L0468, 2020]
- To tell a gold supplier from a silver one, check the supplier's where-to-buy page. A gold supplier links to several online stores and will send them traffic and sales, while a silver supplier does not. Silver suppliers made up nearly all of the revenue in 2020, so a silver supplier is not a problem. [L1277, 2020]
- Profitability with silver-tier suppliers is possible during the first year. [L1281, 2022]
- Traditional dealers and brick-and-mortar suppliers are acceptable if their products appear at online-only stores. Suppliers already found selling through online-only stores should go on the outreach list. [L0835, 2023]
- B2B (business-to-business) opportunities can be a source of products that consumer competitors do not sell. [L0920, 2018]
- Products already in a niche on other dropship stores point to suppliers worth contacting. [L1094, 2023]

### Sourcing Channels

- Trade show exhibitor lists can be used to find brands to contact. The TSNN site (tsnn.com) lists trade shows by country and industry. The exhibitors on each show's website are potential suppliers. [L0357, 2019]
- Finding suppliers through trade shows:
  1. Open tsnn.com.
  2. Choose the country in the search field.
  3. Choose an industry or enter keywords for your niche.
  4. Open the website of each relevant trade show.
  5. Find the exhibitor list and collect the companies to contact. [L0357, 2019]
- If a trade show directory has no results for your niche in a country:
  1. Search Google for your niche plus "trade show" plus the country.
  2. If that finds nothing, search a broader term such as home interior or interior design plus "trade show" plus the country.
  3. Collect exhibitors from the trade shows you find. [L0357, 2019]
- Find brands through trade show exhibitor lists on TSNN.com:
  1. Enter your niche keyword and search.
  2. Select the country where you do business.
  3. Open the websites of the listed trade shows.
  4. Go to the exhibitors list and search for your product terms.
  5. Contact the exhibitors, who are often manufacturers or brands renting booths. Attending the shows in person is not required. [L1113, 2022]
- To find US-based brands, search crowdfunding sites such as Kickstarter for funded projects in the niche over the past five to seven years. Brands found this way are often US-based. [L0963, 2022]
- High-ticket suppliers:
  1. Research which high-ticket product types exist and which markets they belong to.
  2. Choose products with an average price of about $1,000 or more.
  3. Identify all local manufacturers, distributors or importers of those products in the country where the business will sell.
  4. Contact each supplier to ask for approval to resell their products through an online store.
  5. Once approved, place the supplier's products on the store website and build marketing around them. [L1481, 2021-22]
- Premium cookware from a well-known brand such as Le Creuset can work only if enough brands can be found to build a range. Whether a category works is subjective and requires research. [L0766, 2020-26]
- Prefer ranking suppliers using metrics such as search volume when you have dozens of new candidates. [L1357, 2018; L1441, 2018]

### Outreach Targets and Order

- Use this order to approach suppliers:
  1. Identify at least 20 brands selling on online-only stores in your niche.
  2. Contact those first for supplier approvals.
  3. After approval with online-only retailers, circle back to brands sold by online-plus-offline stores. Their approval rate is lower.
  4. Use the niche verification tool in the members area for external validation before launching. [L0268, 2020]
- Suppliers that do not enforce MAP: keep contacting new suppliers. Continue reaching out to more suppliers in the list until approvals come through. A list of over 200 suppliers still has room to expand. [L0856, 2024]
- Brands found selling in the niche on other dropship online-only stores are the best outreach targets, because they are more likely to approve new retailers. [L1094, 2023]
- Suppliers to contact: products that already appear on other dropship stores are a better outreach target. [L1094, 2023]
- Contact the brands that competitors sell, not the competitors. [L0276, 2024]
- Contact procedure:
  1. Find online stores selling in your target niche.
  2. Identify all brands those stores carry.
  3. Search for and contact those brands directly.
  4. Request authorisation to resell their products.
  5. Do not contact the competitor stores themselves. [L0276, 2024]
- Check supplier price-to-retailer relationship: if a supplier sells direct and at the same price as its retailers, do not approach it for approval. [L0835, 2023]
- Look beyond the standard supplier criteria only after standard research is exhausted. [L1125, 2020-26]

### Refusals, Short Counts and Next Steps

- When a supplier will not accept a new reseller, keep working through the supplier list you already built. Return to the future-competitor and supplier research stages to find more suppliers to contact. [L0835, 2023]
- If fewer than 20 brands with contact information are found, go back to the earlier research, find more future competitors and extract their brand names. Repeat until the minimum is reached. Do not move forward with a niche below this count. [L0931, 2020-26]
- If you identify fewer than 20 suppliers, return to the previous lesson and research additional competitor websites. Collect contact information for at least 20 suppliers; finding more is ideal. [L0056, undated]
- A niche passes validation when at least one future competitor has a traffic rank under 100,000 and at least 20 brands in that niche have contact information. Both conditions must be met before the niche is taken further. A niche that fails this test is not pursued. [L0931, 2020-26]
- The count is about contactable brands, not the total number of brands extracted. Twenty is the bar to move forward with confidence. [L0931, 2020-26]
- Traffic-rank thresholds disagree across sources:
  - At least one future competitor under 100,000 (required for validation). [L0931, 2020-26]
  - 2 to 3 or more future competitor stores under 1 million. [L0805, 2024]
  - Supplier-site traffic within the top one million. [L0970, 2023]
- A niche that falls short on suppliers is not built on until the count is met, but a niche with fewer than 20 brands can still be pursued if it is close and the store is already built. [L0801, 2021]
- Suppliers that refuse a reseller are a normal outcome; a niche is never guaranteed approvals in advance. [L1283, 2023]
- Demo-store supply problems: if the demo store is built but suppliers in your niche have supply problems, keep contacting suppliers and review results after about 10 to 20 contacts. Then decide whether to start a second niche in parallel instead of switching early. [L1112, 2021]
- A niche should be tested for supplier availability and competitor count before committing. Supplier willingness to approve a retailer cannot be checked before choosing a niche. [L1283, 2023]
- Do not sell a product without checking that it is sold by enough suppliers and passes the pricing test, even if a single supplier looks strong. [L1281, 2022]
- If two or more similar order values exist, choose the niche with more suppliers (for example about 60 rather than 20). [L1115, 2024]
- The minimum product count is a guideline to be weighed, not an automatic cut-off, when a store is already built. [L0801, 2021]
- Concentration risk in suppliers: a niche with only about 15 products from a few suppliers can still work but carries much higher risk. If one supplier with 10 of those 15 products goes out of business or raises prices, the business is badly hurt. [L0934, 2023]

### Judging Whether a Category Works

- A category is worth pursuing only when the 20-supplier and 100-product thresholds are met for the niche. [L0961, 2020; L0960, 2019]
- Products across 20 to 30 styles: if fewer than 20 to 30 styles exist across all suppliers, the niche is too narrow. [L0268, 2020]
- Whether a category works is subjective and requires research. [L0766, 2020-26]
- A sub-niche with three manufacturers is not worth pursuing, even with high prices and good volume. [L0468, 2020]
- Do not build a store around one category from a mixed range if the category alone fails the thresholds. [L0357, 2019]
- A niche with limited variety, such as chandeliers, may stock only a few dozen options at big-box retailers. Its supplier pool is narrow. A niche like wallpaper, where patterns, colours and textures are nearly unlimited, offers far more supplier and product room. [L1125, 2020-26]

### Going Beyond Standard Criteria

- Sticking to the standard criteria gives the highest probability of success. Use expansion methods as a later step when you need more suppliers, more products or less competitive products. [L1125, 2020-26]
- Once a store is running with suppliers and traffic, sourcing from outside the standard criteria is a next step for growth. It can add suppliers and products, reduce competition, and increase traffic and sales, and it is worth doing even when the store is already performing well. [L1125, 2020-26]
- Be careful with categories where a brand's products are not typically sold to retailers. Consider whether the item is sold more widely across multiple sources. [L1125, 2020-26]
- A dropshipper's growth comes from adding as many products as possible from as many suppliers as possible and keeping them updated. A product can take off unexpectedly, so the catalogue should always grow. [L1068, 2019]

### Keeping the Supplier List Current

- Pre-made supplier lists are never fully current because new suppliers appear every year. The most current list comes from checking which brands the top competitors use. [L1092, 2022]
- Keep the supplier list current:
  1. Identify the top competitor sites in the target niche when building the store.
  2. Record which suppliers those competitors sell through.
  3. Add any supplier not yet on your list.
  4. Repeat the check every six months to a year and add newly appeared suppliers. [L1092, 2022]
- Repeat the brand and supplier search from time to time. [L0857, 2025]
- Look for new brands and products through B2B opportunities. [L0920, 2018]
- Repeat the validation of the niche when the supplier list changes. [L0857, 2025; L1092, 2022]

### Within-Niche Breadth

- Within a chosen niche, list supplier candidates across every product category it covers. [L0932, 2021-22]
- Brands across many categories should be considered before the niche is judged too narrow. [L0932, 2021-22; L1456, 2021-22]
- Make the niche as specific as possible, but only as long as the 20-supplier and 100-product minimums are met. [L1068, 2019]
- A niche should have at least 20 brands and at least 100 products across them. Some niches have hundreds of brands and thousands of products, which is also acceptable. [L0667, 2025]

### Measuring Risk and Diversity

- Sell for many brands rather than two or three so one supplier's stockout does not sink the store. [L1090, 2021]
- Inflation was expected to push wholesale and retail prices higher in the coming months. Having many suppliers protects margins and availability when costs rise. [L1090, 2021]
- A large catalogue from many suppliers reduces the risk that one supplier's stock, prices or survival can sink the store. [L0934, 2023]
- Require at least 20 suppliers so that a few rejections do not leave too little to build on. [L1109, 2018; L1079, 2020-26]

#### Further details

- Time allocation: 5-10 minutes per competitor to extract brand names; 20-25 minutes total to research and enter supplier contact information; complete the entire task in under 30 minutes. [L0056, undated]

- Complete this task when you have at least 20 suppliers with contact information from real competitor websites, and your niche has passed the pricing, demographic, brand loyalty, seasonality, dropship-friendly, and competitor traffic tests. [L0056, undated]

- Find online-only retailers in the niche and note the brands they list, since those brands already supply online-only stores. If no such brands appear for a niche, move to a different niche. [L0247, 2021]

- Minimum criteria: at least 20 dropship-friendly suppliers and at least 100 products per niche. [L0249, 2022]

- Minimum 100 unique products (different SKUs and brands) required in a niche; if fewer, niche up to broader category. [L0268, 2020]
