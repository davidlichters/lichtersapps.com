## Brand, Product and Supplier Count Validation

### The 20 Brands and 100 Products Rule

- A niche needs at least 20 brands or suppliers and at least 100 products before you commit. Both thresholds apply to the niche as a whole, not to a single store. [L0551, 2020; L0961, 2020; L0972, 2025; L0667, 2025; L0852, 2020; L0964, 2023; L0275, 2024]
- Beyond these minimums, the number of suppliers and products should be as high as possible. Some niches have hundreds of brands and thousands of products, and that is acceptable. [L0667, 2025; L1068, 2019]
- Count products across all suppliers combined, not per supplier. Twenty suppliers with two or three products each (about 60 in total) fail the 100-product threshold. [L1097, 2025; L1277, 2020]
- Treat the 100-product minimum as a firm rule. A large catalogue from many suppliers reduces the risk that one supplier's stock, prices or survival can sink the store. [L0934, 2023]
- Twenty brands leave room for a low supplier approval rate, and 100 products give Google Shopping enough variety to show. [L1275, 2019]
- Aim for around 20 brands because not every supplier approves you right away. Even with several rejections, the remaining approved brands still give a sizable catalogue. [L1277, 2020; L1109, 2018]
- The 100-product minimum gives ads enough different searches to run against and generates the data needed to learn. A small range raises the risk that one product must succeed. [L1109, 2018]
- Experienced operators can work with fewer suppliers and products. [L1109, 2018; L1085, 2018]
- Successful stores have carried more than 3,000 SKUs. There is no upper limit on products if they come from quality suppliers and fit the niche. [L1109, 2018]
- A niche is not disqualified because no store sells that exact niche. A broader store that carries the category is acceptable evidence, as long as the subcategory meets the 100-product and 20-brand minimum. [L0664, 2020]
- Finding only a few stores that sell the niche is acceptable if the 20-brand and 100-product thresholds are met. Competitor stores that mix the niche with three or four other categories are fine if they carry the products you want to sell. [L0551, 2020]
- Brands owned by the same parent company each count separately toward the 20-brand minimum, as long as each is a distinct brand. [L1279, 2021]
- Different colours of the same product type count as separate products. An orange surfboard and a blue surfboard are two different items in your product count. [L0036, 2020-26]
- Store-level counts: a store should have at least 100 products, although stores with 5,000 or more products exist. [L0356, 2019]
- Accessories, carrying cases and free add-ons do not count toward the 100 core products. Google ads run for core products, and accessories are used as free bonuses or cross-sells. [L1110, 2019]
- Only brands that supply the core products you would sell count as real suppliers. Sellers of add-ons are useful only as cross-sell ideas. [L0931, 2020-26]

### Supplier-Count Values by Source

Sources differ on the supplier count. Use the value that matches your situation.

| Source | Suppliers / brands | Products | When it applies |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard rule (most units) | 20 | 100 | Default check before committing [L0050, undated; L0245, 2020; L0551, 2020; L0852, 2020; L0964, 2023] |
| Beginners, 2018 | 20 suppliers with more than 100 products each | 100 | New sellers who may face rejections [L1085, 2018; L1109, 2018] |
| Experienced, 2018 | 5 to 12 | Not stated | Sellers confident in sales skills; 2 or 3 approvals is too risky [L1085, 2018] |
| Fallback, 2025 | About 10 | Widen the product range with related items | Only when about 10 suppliers are available [L0966, 2025] |
| one source | 20 or more in the US; 10 or more elsewhere | Not stated | Brand-count check on reseller sites outside the standard thresholds [L1484, 2021-22] |
| Lower bound, 2023 | 5 to 10 suppliers: broaden the niche rather than build on so few | 100 | Warning level [L0804, 2023] |
| Few suppliers, high risk | Several suppliers | About 15 | Possible but much higher risk [L0934, 2023] |

- The minimum for suppliers has stayed at 20 even through the 2023 downturn. [L0804, 2023]
- When two niches have similar order values, choose the one with more potential suppliers, for example about 60 rather than 20. More suppliers give more room for error. [L1115, 2024]
- A niche with 20 suppliers and 100 products is the baseline; more is better. [L0050, undated]

### Product-Count Warning Levels

- If the niche has fewer than 20 to 30 styles across all suppliers, it is too narrow. Broaden it to the next category level. [L0268, 2020]
- If a narrow niche cannot reach 100 products, broaden it one level, for example from brown leather chairs to all leather chairs, until the count is reached. [L0356, 2019; L1275, 2019]
- The counting procedure for a niche:
  1. Count the suppliers and the distinct products available for your niche.
  2. If there are fewer than 100 products, move one level up to the broader category.
  3. Count again and repeat the step up until you reach at least 100 products and enough suppliers.
  4. Stop at the level where both thresholds are met. Do not go broader than needed. [L1109, 2018]
- Illustrative counts: 20 products for a very specific item, 80 at the category level, and 150 at the broader table level. The broader level is the one that meets the 100-product minimum. [L1109, 2018]
- Worked example: a narrow niche with 20 brands but only 50 products fails. A broader category with 25 brands and 150 products passes. [L1275, 2019]
- Products and brands must both be counted; a niche that passes one but not the other fails. [L0356, 2019; L0960, 2019]

### Counting the Niche: Procedures

- Validation procedure: [L0466, 2018]
  1. Check that at least 20 brands sell the product.
  2. Check that at least 100 products exist in the niche.
  3. Check that the product is evergreen and not a short-lived trend.
  4. Check that the price point works for a profitable store.
  5. Check the demand for the niche with Google Keyword Planner.
- Validation procedure: [L1275, 2019]
  1. Confirm the niche passes all validation criteria.
  2. Count the brands you can find; you need at least 20.
  3. Count the products across those brands; you need at least 100.
  4. If the product count is too low, broaden the niche one level (for example from brown leather chairs to leather chairs) and count again.
  5. Only launch once both thresholds are met.
- Before committing to a niche, find at least 20 brands and 100 products that could be sold in it. These are minimum research thresholds. [L0972, 2025]
- A niche should offer at least 20 suppliers or brands and at least 100 products before you commit to it. It must pass every niche criterion. If it falls short, move up one level to a broader related category. [L0671, 2021]
- Choose a niche only after finding at least 20 possible brands that can be approved. If fewer exist, either research a different niche or move one level broader. [L0962, 2021]
- Before committing, you need at least 20 potential brands to sell for. If you find fewer, widen the niche sideways (for example from chandeliers to all home lighting) or up one level. The catalogue should reach about 100 products across those brands. [L0834, 2021]
- Make the niche as specific as possible, but only as long as at least 20 suppliers and 100 products are available. If the minimum cannot be met, broaden to the next category level, for example from chandeliers to all lighting. [L1068, 2019]
- A niche should have enough supply to build a store: a rule of thumb is at least 20 suppliers and over 100 products. (Foosball example, 2022, older: one product type needed 20 or more makers and over 100 products.) [L0803, 2022]
- Before building a store, aim for at least 20 suppliers or brands and at least 100 core products. [L1110, 2019]
- A niche with fewer than 20 brands can still be pursued if it is close to the threshold and the store is already built. The threshold is a guideline, so weigh the remaining supplier count against the work already done. [L0801, 2021]
- Niche validation thresholds: find at least 20 brands on dropship-friendly websites and at least 100 products across them, and find 2 to 3 future competitor stores (or more) with a traffic rank under 1 million. [L0805, 2024]
- Niche validation (2023 and later, current wording): a niche needs at least 100 products and at least 20 dropship-friendly brands to pass. [L0964, 2023]
- Validation of the niche on a single dropship-friendly threshold: a niche needs at least 20 brands with contact information. [L0931, 2020-26]

### Minimum Brand Counts and What Counts

- A niche requires at least 20 suppliers to be acceptable for dropshipping. A strong niche has 100 or more. [L0050, undated]
- Require at least 20 separate suppliers or brands, not 20 products from one source. This gives a pool large enough to approach several suppliers for account approval, so you are not left with too few options if some decline. [L1079, 2020-26]
- Confirm that more than 20 brands in the niche can be found with their contact information accessible. [L1080, 2020-26]
- Count each supplier that carries even a few matching products toward the 20. [L0960, 2019]
- A supplier with only a few products in your niche can still be approved and listed. Two or eight relevant products are enough to start if the supplier is otherwise a good fit. [L0934, 2023]
- Find at least 20 potential suppliers within a niche before committing. If only about 10 are available, widen the product range with related items. [L0966, 2025]
- Target at least 20 potential suppliers per niche. If a niche has only 5 to 10 suppliers, broaden it rather than building on so few. [L0804, 2023]
- A niche with at least 20 brands is more useful than one with a few popular items. Gather brands from future competitors' stores until the list reaches 20 or more. [L0963, 2022]
- The number of brands and products matters more than having many suppliers in a single product line. A sub-niche with only three manufacturers is not worth pursuing even with high prices and good volume. [L0468, 2020]
- If a niche has only a few manufacturers, widen the category to find enough brands. Fewer than 20 different brands is a warning sign. [L0801, 2021]
- If one manufacturer supplies most of the brands, the concentration risk is high. Aim for at least about 20 brands from different manufacturers. [L0247, 2021]
- Brands from different manufacturers are needed; the count must reflect distinct brands, not one source. [L0247, 2021; L0801, 2021]
- Within a chosen niche, list supplier candidates across every product category the niche covers (for example air purifiers, dehumidifiers and humidifiers for an air quality niche), not just the first product type searched. [L0932, 2021-22]
- A dropshipper should aim to carry many brands and options within a product category rather than rely on one brand, since the brand customers prefer is unpredictable and unexpected brands, including low-margin ones, often sell best. A new store will naturally start with few brands and should keep adding suppliers over time. [L1456, 2021-22]

### Competitor-Based Supplier Counting

- Competition matters at the product and supplier level, not the niche level. Count how many brands you can get approved with and how many products those brands make available. [L0852, 2020]
- To judge competition, count how many brands the niche-specific stores sell and how many of those brands appear in Google Shopping ads. If niche stores exist but few brands are advertised, the niche is workable. Exact thresholds are defined in the research module. [L0941, 2022]
- Brands a competitor sells are the main source of supplier names. [L0963, 2022; L0930, 2020-26]
- Finding suppliers by researching the brands that competitors sell takes one to three hours, and paid supplier lists rarely do better. [L0963, 2022]
- The source for supplier names is the future competitor list for each winning niche. It records every competitor's URL, store name, traffic rank and niche. Only competitors already known to dropship are used as sources, because their brand filters show suppliers that work on dropship terms. [L0930, 2020-26]
- Competitor research in two passes:
  1. During niche research, list the likely future competitor stores for the niche.
  2. For each competitor, identify the brands and suppliers it sells, then add them to the supplier list. [L0967, 2020]
- Competitor research prioritisation:
  1. Find competitor websites for the niche, including online-only stores, retailers and warehouses.
  2. Note the brands each site sells.
  3. Prioritise brands sold by online-only stores with no physical presence, because those brands have likely approved other online sellers.
  4. Treat brands that appear on both an online-only site and a physical-store site as the most likely to approve you.
  5. Keep the remaining brands as later contacts, since approval and pricing are not guaranteed. [L0357, 2019]
- Brand-count procedure for reseller sites:
  1. Search Google for competitor stores in your niche.
  2. Open a store and look for a Brands page or a brand list.
  3. If there is no brand page, check the product titles, the sidebar filters and the category navigation for brand names.
  4. Scroll through the full brand list and note the brands you see.
  5. Repeat on a second, third and possibly fourth site, adding any new brands found.
  6. Stop once you can confidently count 20 or more distinct brands in the US, or 10 or more elsewhere. [L1484, 2021-22]
- Reseller sites often do not list brands in the same place. Search the store's menus, filters and sidebars. Brand names frequently appear in product titles or category links, and the lack of a brands page is normal. [L1484, 2021-22]
- At the validation stage you only need to count how many distinct brands exist in the market. Record no details or contact information for each one. Whether the reseller dropships or holds stock is irrelevant to this count. [L1484, 2021-22]
- Validate the brand count across multiple competitor websites rather than one site. Brands that appear on several sites are reliable signals. If the count is still uncertain after three or four sites, continue searching Google results. [L1484, 2021-22]
- Record only the supplier count (for example 20 or more) in the tracker's Suppliers column for each market idea. Complete the same check for every idea before moving to the next stage. [L1484, 2021-22]
- Run the brand-count check for five market ideas, not just one. Write the supplier count for each idea into the tracker before moving on. [L1484, 2021-22]
- A first pass only gives a starting list and can understate the real number of suppliers. If a niche looks promising but has few suppliers, go deeper: open more competitor sites, search their categories for more brands, and look for other online-only sellers. [L0459, 2020-26]
- Large multi-category retailers can be used to find suppliers in your category by collecting the brands they sell in that category. Do not copy their full range, and do not treat their high traffic ranking as a model for a niche store. [L0801, 2021]
- If most Google Shopping competitors in your niche are big-box retailers, check whether many brands in that niche are sold outside those retailers. If many are, the niche is a go-ahead for a small store. [L1274, 2018]
- Prefer mid-sized, specialised, up-and-coming brands that are not sold at big-box stores such as Home Depot or Lowe's. Being sold at big-box stores is acceptable only if enough brands in the niche still come from drop-ship-only suppliers. [L1085, 2018]
- Check the category section of a general competitor. For a general water sports store rather than a specialist, the category section should contain at least 20 brands and at least 100 products. If the category exists only as part of a mixed range, do not build a store around only that category. [L0357, 2019]
- A competitor's full brand list is a starting point and needs filtering before use. This keeps the validation count honest. [L0931, 2020-26]
- Brand concentration: a single retail site in the same niche can carry many brands. (Example: in the generator niche, brands found on Google Shopping included Honda, Champion, Firman, Generac, DuroMax, Westinghouse, Briggs & Stratton, Yamaha, Cummins and Onan. A single retail site in the same niche carried many of these brands, which was taken as enough suppliers.) [L0458, 2021-22]

### Google Shopping Counting and Validation

- The sellers in Google Shopping for a product are both your future competitors and a ready list of brands you could approach as suppliers. Record their brand names while you count competitors. [L0458, 2021-22]
- Count the products for a niche by going through the Google Shopping results for the niche keyword. [L0801, 2021]
- Google Shopping research: search the generic product term, click the Shopping tab, and filter by Price (Up to $500, $500-$1,000, $1,000-$3,000, Over $3,000) and by Brand. Page through the results to list brand names and sellers. Open the "compare prices from X stores" link for each product to count competitors. [L0771, 2021-22]
- Validation procedure through competitor brands in Google Shopping:
  1. List all brands you found in the niche.
  2. Search each brand in Google Shopping.
  3. Count how many stores pay to advertise each brand.
  4. Get approved with all the brands, but run Google ads only for brands sold by 10 or fewer stores.
  Example: 30 brands with 15 of them sold by 10 or fewer stores means you move forward, get approved with all 30, and advertise the 15. [L0467, 2019]
- Verify each brand extracted from a competitor list by searching it on Google Shopping and checking the competitor's site. Confirm the brand sells the products you plan to offer before contacting it. [L0247, 2021]
- Check how competitors price a brand's products on Google Shopping before contacting the supplier. If the brand has no price policy, a wide spread of prices across sellers is normal. [L0468, 2020]
- Brands with weak pricing discipline can still be sold if other stores in Google Shopping show healthy margins. Check which stores sell the brand and at what markup before dropping it. [L0963, 2022]
- If a category on Google Shopping shows only Alibaba-type sellers, search the organic Google results for US-based stores with US suppliers that do not use Shopping ads, since some suppliers are only visible there. [L0834, 2021]
- Sellers in Google Shopping can be both competitors and a source of brands, but only sellers that dropship are useful outreach targets. [L0458, 2021-22; L0930, 2020-26]

### Finding the Real Brand Behind a Product

- Find the original source of a product photo:
  1. Right-click the image in Google Chrome.
  2. Choose Search Google for image.
  3. Review the websites where the image appears.
  4. Look for a brand name, SKU or supplier that a retailer has left out of its listing. [L0962, 2021]
- Find competitor supplier names via image search:
  1. Right-click on competitor product image on their website.
  2. Select "Search Google for image."
  3. Review visually similar results to see which retailers carry the same product.
  4. Check which retailers display the brand name on their product page to identify the supplier. [L0268, 2020]
- To find a supplier's real brand when a search returns only resellers, right-click the product image in Google Chrome and choose "Search image with Google." This finds visual matches on other websites that may show the manufacturer or another brand name. [L0964, 2023]
- If a future competitor sells products without naming the brand, check whether it is private labelled. You can also right-click a product image and use Google Image search to find the same image on other sites that name the brand. [L0805, 2024]
- Partner suppliers that say they do not have a manufacturer role: check the About us and Contact us pages of the site. The word "partner" does not always mean the company makes the products. [L0805, 2024]
- Products already run by brands with logos should be avoided or deprioritised in favour of other candidates. Dropshippers running the same product are acceptable, but established brands indicate high competition. [L0708, undated]
- A manufacturer that sells only direct and has no retail program does not make the product a bad choice. It only means you cannot sell that brand, so look for other suppliers. [L1002, 2019]

### Brand Verification and Brand Extraction Hygiene

- Remove private-label brands and products exclusive to a single marketplace. Focus only on suppliers that operate independently and sell through their own channels. [L0056, undated]
- Before committing to a brand, confirm it sells the products you plan to offer. [L0247, 2021]
- A niche with many brands should still be checked for brands that are only sold on marketplaces. Marketplace listings on Walmart or Target are not reasons to avoid a niche. [L0970, 2023]
- Brands found selling in the niche on other dropship online-only stores are the best outreach targets. Brands whose products go only through distributors with warehouses usually do not dropship. [L1094, 2023]
- Products in a niche that are sold on other dropshipping stores are more likely to come from brands that will approve retailers, so brands found this way are a better outreach target. [L1094, 2023]

### Keeping Your Count Honest: Exclusions

- Avoid sourcing from overstock-style liquidators that sell leftover or previous-season stock at a discount. That is not a reliable supply model. [L0970, 2023]
- Retailers such as Wayfair and marketplace listings on Walmart or Target are not reasons to avoid a niche. [L0970, 2023]
- Wayfair and Houzz are primarily brand-authorised retailers and advertising platforms, not direct competitors. When you see brands on Wayfair, those suppliers likely dropship and accept new retailers. Search the brand name on Google to find the brand's official store and determine whether they sell direct to consumers. [L0253, 2024]
- A supplier that sells only to brick-and-mortar stores should not be pursued for an online-only store. The initial list should consist of brands sold by online-only future competitors. [L0666, 2024]
- Do not contact the competitor stores themselves. Contact the brands. [L0276, 2024]
- Repeated brand and supplier searches keep the list valid, because new brands and new competitor stores appear over time. [L0857, 2025]
- A supplier that refers you to another company to open an account is usually a distributor. Distributors should be avoided. [L1277, 2020]
- If a supplier sells at the same price as its retailers on a direct-to-consumer site, it gives retailers no price advantage and should not be approached for approval. [L0835, 2023]
