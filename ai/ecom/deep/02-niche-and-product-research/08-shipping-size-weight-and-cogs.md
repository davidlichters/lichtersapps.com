### Shipping: Size, Weight and COGS

- Profitability is determined by the relationship between COGS and shipping costs, not by product size or weight. [L0015, undated]
- Disqualifying products during niche research based only on size or weight, without analyzing COGS and shipping, is a mistake. [L0015, undated]
- Size and weight alone do not disqualify a niche. The decisive question is whether the sale price leaves enough net profit after COGS and shipping. Disqualify a product when the margin left over is too small. [L1180, 2020-26]
- Large and heavy products should not be ruled out automatically. Viability depends on the arithmetic of selling price, COGS and shipping, plus common sense about whether the seller can afford the shipping. [L1180, 2020-26]
- Most products in a category cost roughly the same to ship, so a fixed shipping burden hits low-priced items much harder than high-priced ones. The selling price decides whether a niche can absorb shipping. [L1179, 2020-26]
- For large and heavy products, shipping costs remain similar across price points. A $500 item and a $1,500 item may cost nearly the same to ship, making the higher-priced version far more profitable. [L0016, undated]
- A low-priced bulky or heavy product can lose its entire gross margin to shipping, leaving zero net profit. Avoid such niches even if the product sells well. [L1179, 2020-26]
- Avoid niches dominated by inexpensive, large or heavy products, because shipping eliminates profit margins and leaves minimal or zero net earnings. [L0016, undated]
- Heavy or bulky products are workable only when the selling price is above $1,000. Below that level, shipping costs on large, heavy items eat the profit (2019). [L0663, 2019]
- Do not sell large, heavy, inexpensive items. Shipping makes them unprofitable unless the retail price leaves a large spread over wholesale (2022). [L0969, 2022]
- Heavy or bulky products need a higher price point than standard items, because freight costs require a wider spread between wholesale and MAP to cover free shipping (2022). [L1092, 2022]
- Heavy or bulky items are better when their selling price is high enough to absorb shipping. Higher-priced items carry heavy freight better, and free shipping is possible only if the price covers the shipping cost. [L1285, 2024]
- The shipping problem comes mainly from products whose price leaves too little room between MAP and wholesale, not from size or weight alone. Large or heavy products can work if the price is high enough to cover shipping and ads. [L0359, 2020]
- Product options (variants) and product weight are generally not reasons to drop a niche. Heavy products are a problem only when prices are too low to cover shipping. [L0551, 2020]
- Products that are difficult to ship, such as large or heavy items, should not automatically be rejected. Shipping is harder than parcel shipping but can be learned quickly, while high customer service load is a stronger reason for caution (2020). [L0967, 2020]
- Heavy and bulky items have fewer sellers because many avoid freight shipping, so competition is often lower. Their shipping costs must be built into margin math before choosing them (2020). [L1111, 2020]
- Before listing a heavy B2B product, compare the item price with the wholesale cost and the shipping cost to check whether it leaves a healthy profit after free shipping (2021). [L0671, 2021]
- Before choosing a heavy or bulky product, calculate the shipping cost. A single item can cost hundreds of dollars to ship across the country. Avoid products whose shipping cost would be more than half of a low selling price (2020). [L0551, 2020]
- Shipping of around $2,500 on a $20,000 order is a sign that a supplier should not be listed if the customer saves that amount by ordering direct (2023). [L0964, 2023]
- Keep only the products that stay at least as profitable after shipping (2023). [L0964, 2023]
- Check the shipping feasibility of a product before testing. Liquids or large packages can be a problem, and shipping cost can kill the margin. [L1176, 2023]
- Bulky, heavy or installation-dependent items such as sinks, cabinets, lifts, saunas, pools, hot tubs, sheds and tiny homes raise shipping, handling and return costs. Weigh these costs before picking them. [L0546, undated]
- Heavy commercial and industrial items such as Commercial Ranges and Ovens, Walk-in Refrigerators and Freezers, Forklifts, Car Lifts and Industrial Sewing Machines are often too heavy for standard parcel shipping. Plan freight or pallet delivery and a quote-based sale for them rather than a general checkout. [L0546, undated]
- Bulky seasonal products such as barbecue grills can be considered for dropshipping if shipping costs are manageable. Timing the launch with the season, such as barbecue season, is a sensible way to leverage demand. [L0660, 2019+]
- Products should weigh no more than 2 kg. Products of about 3 kg or more are very hard to ship and should be avoided. [L0708, undated]
- Lightweight products with high perceived value, such as jewelry, keep shipping cost low relative to the selling price. Their low unit weight and room for custom boxes or private branding make them easier to scale. [L0780, 2024+]
- If a bulky or heavy product fails at a low price point, check whether a higher-quality supplier version of the same type sells at a price high enough to absorb the shipping. Moving up the quality tier can turn an unprofitable item into a profitable one without changing the category. [L1180, 2020-26]
- Large and heavy products are harder to manage but not impossible to sell. Household furniture such as a table is acceptable, while items at the scale of a car are too big and too heavy. [L0994, 2021-22]
- Shipping is a yes-or-no check based on common sense: are the products very large or very heavy? Detailed logistics are not needed at this stage. [L1329, 2021-22]
- Shipping limits to check: in Australia, products over 100 kg are difficult to ship and may fail the shipping check. In the US, products weighing hundreds of pounds may be a problem. Products too large to fit in a small room of a home are too big to ship. [L1329, 2021-22]
- Examples of shippable high-ticket products: canopies for pickup trucks at about $3,000 are not small but are not very large or heavy, so they pass. Airbag suspension kits at about $825 to over $1,000 come in boxes that can be sent by post, so they clearly pass. [L1329, 2021-22]
- Products must be small enough to ship comfortably. Very large or heavy items, such as big machines shipped from overseas, count against a niche in the shipping check. [L0490, 2021-22]
- Avoid products that are too big or too heavy, because shipping them becomes difficult and costly for you and your supplier. [L0994, 2021-22]
- Big or heavy products are only worth considering if their retail price is $3,000 or more. Below that, the shipping burden usually outweighs the margin. [L0994, 2021-22]
- Sources differ on the threshold for bulky or heavy products: one mention says 300, while the summary checklist sets 3,000. Use 3,000 as the working threshold and treat anything below as a likely shipping problem. [L0994, 2021-22]
- Check product size, weight and shipping cost against expected profit before going further with a niche. Shipping can make an attractive product unprofitable (2021). [L0247, 2021]
- Bulky and heavy items may be a niche to consider, but the seller should judge whether a sale is worth the freight. [L0551, 2020]
- Shipping problems come mainly from prices, and high-ticket items absorb freight. [L0359, 2020]

#### Heavy items and expensive versions within a niche

- Large and heavy products with a low sale price are generally not profitable with free shipping. If shipping eats the margin, sell only the more expensive products within the niche, for example fabric sofas instead of futons. [L0665, 2022]
- Within one category, subtypes can differ widely in price. A futon is a low-priced sofa, while a three-seater fabric sofa averages about $1,500. A generic rug can be a cheap entryway mat, while Persian rugs sit well above $500. Narrow the niche to the subtype whose price can absorb shipping. [L1179, 2020-26]
- Avoid niches where all products are large and heavy and the selling price is around $600 or less, because there is typically no room for profit. A niche confirmed above this level can be pursued. [L1180, 2020-26]
- A product from a high-end segment, such as a $3,500 desk where a profit of $1,750 covers shipping, can be profitable where a cheaper heavy item cannot (2022). [L0969, 2022]

#### Procedure: Niche refinement with price and margin analysis (no year)

1. Open your niche research worksheet. [L0016, undated]
2. Review each niche and refine it using the price and margin analysis. [L0016, undated]

4. Add new niches that meet high-ticket (above $1,000) and profitability criteria. [L0016, undated]
5. Spend 15 to 20 minutes on this refinement exercise. [L0016, undated]
6. Complete the worksheet and proceed to detailed shipping cost validation. [L0016, undated]

#### Procedure: Freight estimate before a supplier is found

1. Enter a likely supplier warehouse zip code on freightcenter.com (Calculate freight rates). [L0665, 2022]
2. Enter a test zip code in a region such as Florida, California or New York. [L0665, 2022]
3. Enter the package dimensions and weight. [L0665, 2022]
4. Click View my rates to see rough shipping costs to different locations. [L0665, 2022]

### Margin Targets and Gross Margin Rules

- Target a net profit margin of approximately 30% on each sale. Some niches allow 25%, while others sustain higher margins, but 30% is the benchmark. [L0016, undated]
- COGS from suppliers averages approximately 50% of the retail sale price. [L0016, undated]
- A niche with a few brands ranking for the main keyword is viable if margins meet a 20% threshold and suppliers allow you to sell it. [L0664, 2020]
- For a niche with 20 to 25% margins including shipping and an average product price around $1,500 to $2,000, first try to move to better pricing tiers with suppliers, and grow free organic traffic (product feed optimization, SEO) so ad costs do not eat the margin (2024). [L0805, 2024]
- Focus on gross margin percentage rather than margin dollars. High-ticket products still need enough percentage to pay for the more hands-on and expensive sales process. [L1068, 2019]
- Example: a $4,000 sale at a 20% margin gives $800 per sale, which is strong because more margin can absorb ad and discount costs. A $5,000 item with only $250 margin is not worth selling. [L1068, 2019]
- Example: brands showing a 20 to 25% margin before ads and shipping can fall to 10 to 15% once a $100 shipping cost is included. A margin that thin is too small for paid traffic. [L1068, 2019]
- In furniture, a $200 futon has so much shipping cost that little margin is left, while a $5,000 leather sofa leaves room for margin and shipping. Avoid low-priced bulky items where shipping absorbs the profit. [L1068, 2019]
- Procedure for low-margin brands in a niche:
  1. Run Google Shopping product listing ads only for brands with healthy margins. [L1068, 2019]
  2. Upload low-margin products to the site but exclude them from Google Ads campaigns. [L1068, 2019]
  3. Let Facebook remarketing follow visitors who viewed those product pages. [L1068, 2019]
  4. Keep these brands for brand recognition, a larger catalog and organic sales, and do not buy cold traffic for them. [L1068, 2019]
- Only pursue products that can reach a gross profit margin of at least 3x the purchase price, with a minimum profit of $20 per product. [L0895, 2021+]
- Technology gadgets can be sold at 3x to 6x their cost, and categories with high perceived value such as beauty, health, wellness and tech gadgets make it easier to set a high price. [L0638, 2023]
- A product with a sourcing cost around $40 and a selling price around $70 to $80 was judged a mid-range ticket item with a workable $30 gross margin. A price point near $100 was seen as a downside. [L0530, 2022]
- A low sourcing cost is not enough on its own. The selling price should sit where perceived value clearly exceeds the price, because an item that looks expensive for its value can kill the sale. [L0530, 2022]
- Price the product so that it still earns money after paying to acquire a customer. If the margin does not cover paid acquisition, every sale loses money. [L0790, 2017]
- Price tier decides how much profit each sale brings. A product priced around $300 with $60 to $70 net profit can work, and higher-ticket items add profit faster but may bring more service work (2021). [L0801, 2021]
- Higher-priced products in the $300 to $600 range can generate far more profit per sale than cheap products, so a store does not need $1,000 or more products to make Google Shopping work. Every ad dollar should go toward products that return the target ROAS when they sell (2020 and no-year). [L0748, 2020-26]
- Products selling well in this model typically retail for $20 to $40 and cost $10 to $20 through CJ dropshipping. [L0562, undated]
- Check a product's real profitability by comparing competitor prices and margins. A product with high margins should be matched to the minimum price, not undercut (2021). [L0962, 2021]
- When two products have the same shipping cost, choose the more expensive one to protect the margin. Shipping is a fixed cost per item size, so higher prices keep the margin higher (2021). [L0962, 2021]
- Higher-priced products are where the real money is in this model, even though cheap products feel less risky. The perceived risk of large orders is usually greater than the real risk, and fraud protection tools cover most of it (2020). [L0551, 2020]
- Higher-priced products are a reasonable starting point when all other research criteria are equal, because expensive items give more revenue per order. [L0941, 2022]
- Higher-priced products are generally easier to make profitable than cheap ones, unless the product is a luxury good with different buying behavior (2018). [L1085, 2018]
- A product with about a 20% wholesale-to-MAP margin is not workable for paid ads. True margins only become visible after suppliers are set up and approved (2023). [L0970, 2023]
- Each niche is not a fixed margin. Margins depend on each supplier and product. After approval, compare each brand's wholesale price with MAP to see the real profit per product (2020). [L1277, 2020]
- Within a niche with a wide price range, check whether low-priced items can produce a real margin. If they cannot, drop them, because a niche with very cheap items may not pay for the work (2020). [L1277, 2020]
- Price high-ticket items above about $79 for a high-ticket store. One store sold items between $350 and $700 with about 250 units sold in one month. [L1440, 2021+]
- Products priced around $25 fall into an impulse-buy range, where many shoppers buy spontaneously. Price points in this range are easier to sell cold to new audiences. [L1251, 2024+]
- Products in a store should be evaluated by sourcing cost and ability to price. An AliExpress bottle opener costing about $5 with shipping is a very cheap product, which makes it attractive to test. [L0656, undated]

### MAP and Non-MAP Suppliers

- The gap between wholesale and MAP differs per supplier and per product, and no tool reveals it in advance. The actual margin only becomes known during the approval phase, when price sheets arrive (2023). [L0804, 2023]
- In a niche where one of 20 suppliers had MAP, margins were 7 to 15%. Without MAP, margins can vary widely, so compare prices before committing (2022). [L0963, 2022]
- A margin seen from only a few supplier conversations is not evidence that every supplier in the niche offers the same terms. Contact more suppliers before judging a niche's margins (2025). [L0966, 2025]
- For a niche with many brands but unfamiliar products, check that the wholesale price is well below the selling price before committing. Products selling direct at a lower price on the supplier's own site are a red flag (2020). [L0551, 2020]
- Search the exact product name in Google Shopping as a shopper would. If you are the only seller shown, a higher price plus a discount code can work. If competitors list it cheaper, shrink the discount or remove it (2025). [L0966, 2025]
- A niche with many suppliers and a high-priced outlier is best handled by including outliers only when they are relevant products you would list. If one is a pricing error, exclude it (2023). [L1283, 2023]
- A supplier's shipping cost from offshore sources can distort average price. Refine the keyword rather than adding shipping to the average (2023). [L1283, 2023]

#### Procedure: Evaluating a non-MAP supplier

1. Get the supplier's price list. [L0671, 2021]
2. Search Google Shopping for several of its products. [L0671, 2021]
3. Check whether anyone already advertises those products and at what markup. [L0671, 2021]
4. If competitors mark them up only 10% or 15%, do not list them. This is a no-go. [L0671, 2021]
5. If there is little competition, become the main seller for those products. If competitors sell them at healthy margins, list them too. [L0671, 2021]

#### Step: Large non-MAP supplier catalogs (no year)

- For a non-MAP supplier with a large price list (example: 100 SKUs), search each product name individually on Google Shopping and record the average competitor price for each one. Plan the time for this, because the research is done product by product. [L1051, 2020-26]

### Phone-Sale and Customer-Service Thresholds

- To avoid phone sales, focus on niches where the average product price is under $2,000. [L0050, undated]
- If the business cannot handle phone calls, keep niches at product prices of $2,000 or less. Higher-priced niches (for example $2,000 and up) are only worth testing if phone sales are part of the model. [L0459, 2020-26]
- Target average order values of about $2,000 to $3,000 only if you are willing to handle phone calls and extra emails. If you want less customer service work, stay in the normal price range (2020 and no-year). [L0359, 2020]
- A niche with high technical complexity or a price point around $2,000 or more requires extra pre-sale and post-sale customer service. Such a niche is viable if the business can offer that service at first and outsource it later (2024). [L0836, 2024]
- Average order value in the $500 to $1,000 range is generally the sweet spot. It leaves enough margin per order and needs the least free selling and customer interaction. Higher averages (about $1,200 to $2,000) still work but bring more pre-sale calls and emails (2021). [L0834, 2021]
- Average prices above the $200 to $800 range can still work, but they bring more pre-sale and post-sale questions. Plan support capacity before choosing high-ticket products (2021). [L1279, 2021]
- A niche should show an average of at least $200 across organic first-page results. There is no maximum, but very high average order values bring more phone orders (2024). [L0806, 2024]
- To reach $2,000 profit per sale, the average order value must be between $6,000 and $7,000 (2025). [L0278, 2025]
- When targeting $2,000 profit per sale, eliminate all products under $6,000 during price research. [L0278, 2025]
- For upper-middle-class customers, target product prices between $1,500 and $3,000 (2025). [L0278, 2025]
- Choosing a product only because it meets a minimum price, without checking shipping, can lead to a loss. Free shipping must still leave profit after actual shipping cost is subtracted. [L1180, 2020-26]
- A $200 futon earns much less per sale than a $2,000 product, but its buyers often still need support and return handling. Weigh profit per order against the workload each order creates before choosing a price point. [L1517, 2020-26]
- When choosing a first niche from several that pass testing, pick the one with the highest average order value, ideally at least $1,000 (2024). [L1115, 2024]
- Premium appliances priced around $10,000 for some models can have very good margins, but the volume is low. Plan the operation for fewer, more valuable orders. [L1472, 2022]

### Impulse, Cold Traffic and Buyer Price Points

- Very expensive products are hard to sell to cold traffic, because buyers research longer before purchasing. Keep the product price within a range that impulse buyers accept. [L0361, 2024]
- Products around $25 fall into an impulse-buy range, which makes them easier to sell cold to new audiences. [L1251, 2024+]
- Dropship products for an Instagram viral approach are priced between $9.99 and $49.99. [L1367, undated]
- For shoes sold online, the best-selling price range is roughly $20 to $40. Above about $40, a shoe stops being an impulse purchase and buyers need more convincing. [L0661, undated]
- Expensive niche products that do a narrow job, with limited capacity, rarely convert on price alone. If buyers need several units to get value, offer a quantity discount or rethink the price; otherwise most will buy just one. [L1186, 2022]
- Customers do not care about the retailer's margin. They choose by the outcome or benefit they expect from the product, so margin belongs in the decision of what to stock, not in the customer's decision to buy. [L1456, 2021-22]
- Products with high perceived value, such as beauty, health, wellness and tech gadgets, make it easier to set a high price. [L0638, 2023]
- Lightweight products with high perceived value keep shipping cost low relative to price. [L0780, 2024+]
- Phone cases and other cheap, very common products look easy to sell but compete with huge demand, so they usually earn little per order. [L1147, 2020-26]
- If a niche has much cheaper variants, do not advertise the cheap versions when they would pull buyers away from higher-priced items. List low-price items only when the margin is there, and never spend ad money on them because they erode profit (2020). [L1088, 2020]
