### Competitor limits: how many to track

- Go deepest into the top three competitors rather than spreading thin across many [L0801, 2021].
- Identify the top three future competitors by traffic using a competition tool [L0247, 2021].
- Identify the top two or three online-only competitors, or the first page of Google results [L0969, 2022].
- Select at least three competing stores, or five to six if more are available [L0963, 2022].
- A niche with only a few dropshipping competitors can be a first store if it passes all criteria [L0359, 2020].
- The niche is only proven when several qualifying online-only, multi-brand sellers are found. Keep checking sites until there are enough to contact for supplier approval [L0460, 2020-26].
- One online store is not proof that a niche is dropship-friendly. It may be a special case, so look for several online-only, multi-brand sellers [L0460, 2020-26].
- Keep only those competitors that fit the online-only and niche criteria [L0468, 2020].
- Keep the online-only, multi-brand sites open for future reference [L0460, 2020-26].

### Traffic rank check

#### Which rank to read: country, not global

- Always record the country rank for the country where you plan to do business, never the global traffic rank. A competitor is only relevant through its traffic in your target market [L0687, 2020-26].
- Using a global rank instead of the country rank misstates how big the market is in your country and can make a niche look stronger or weaker than it is [L0687, 2020-26].
- A country traffic rank is a position among all websites in that country. A rank of 91 means the site is the 91st most visited website there. A low number is good [L0687, 2020-26].
- In SimilarWeb, use the country rank for the target market, for example the US rank for US sales [L0836, 2024; L0891, 2020-26].
- For a dropshipping business that targets one country, the country rank matters more than the global rank, because global traffic from other countries does not help [L0891, 2020-26].
- Read competitor rank from the result for the target country, not the global result [L0247, 2021].
- A competitor with a good global ranking but no ranking in the target country is probably getting little traffic and is not a top competitor [L0962, 2021].
- SimilarWeb shows a country rank only for countries where the site is popular. To check another market, such as the UK or Australia, search the same URL and read the rank listed for that country. No rank there means little traffic in that market [L0891, 2020-26].
- If Alexa shows only a global rank and no US rank, the site has very low traffic in the United States [L0960, 2019].

- If the first niche you research already has a future competitor with a country rank under 100,000, the niche passes and you can move on. If none qualifies, keep working through your list until you find one with enough traffic [L0687, 2020-26].
- The minimum test for a pass: one competitor in the 1-100,000 range [L0051, undated; L0687, 2020-26].

#### Benchmarks for rank

- A traffic rank of about 4,000 is excellent. Anything under 100,000 is acceptable. For a site that sells a wide range of related items, aim for competitors around 30,000 to 40,000 [L0963, 2022].
- Treat the lowest number as the strongest player when comparing future competitors in SimilarWeb. There is no true number one, since platforms like Facebook or Google usually hold the top spots [L0965, 2024]. (2024)

#### Collecting the rank in SimilarWeb

- SimilarWeb shows traffic and ranking data for any website. The free version, used without an account, is enough to collect the country rank for competitor research. A paid plan is not required [L0891, 2020-26].
- SimilarWeb ranks websites by traffic within the country where they are most popular, with 1 as the highest. A lower number means more traffic. The rank is only a comparison tool for the country you plan to sell in [L0891, 2020-26].
- The traffic ranks collected here are used later to choose the niche and to identify the most popular sites within it. At this stage they are only gathered, not yet interpreted [L0891, 2020-26].
- Do not spend time on other data SimilarWeb shows at this stage. Only the country rank is needed, and other data can easily lead to a time-consuming detour [L0891, 2020-26].
- For a free account as of July 18, 2024, click "skip this step" during registration to bypass the trial offer and unlock the limited plan [L0061, 2024+].
- The SimilarWeb Chrome extension retrieves competitor traffic and store data directly in your browser. Use it as an alternative if signup issues occur [L0061, 2024+].
- SimilarWeb's signup asks for a website address. A placeholder or the brand's own domain can be entered to access the free data [L0971, 2024]. (2024)

#### Procedure: SimilarWeb homepage lookup

1. Copy the first competitor URL from the sheet. [L0891, 2020-26]
2. Open the SimilarWeb homepage and paste the URL into the search field at the top. [L0891, 2020-26]
3. Click search and wait for the page to load. [L0891, 2020-26]
4. Read the number under Country rank for the country where you plan to do business. [L0891, 2020-26]
5. Enter that number in column C of the sheet. [L0891, 2020-26]
6. Repeat for every competitor URL in the list. [L0891, 2020-26]

#### Procedure: SimilarWeb extension lookup

1. Install the SimilarWeb Chrome extension (free). [L0051, undated]
2. For each competitor, open their website and click the SimilarWeb extension icon. [L0051, undated]
3. Scroll to Country Rank and record the rank for your target country (e.g., United States). [L0051, undated]
4. Complete the worksheet by identifying other niches each competitor sells in. [L0051, undated]
5. Verify that at least one competitor ranks between 1 and 100,000. [L0051, undated]
6. If yes, mark this lesson complete and proceed to the next lesson. [L0051, undated]
7. If no, return to your niche list and continue testing other niches. [L0051, undated]

#### Caveats on traffic data

- Use the traffic rank of a competitor as a rough guide only. Estimates from tools such as Alexa are no longer reliable in 2026 and must be checked against current data [L0468, 2020].
- A competitor's traffic rank is a signal of demand. Check that the rank tool used is one option among others [L0468, 2020].
- Supplier websites are showcase pages and are not expected to have high traffic. Judge the competition by the retail stores that sell in the niche [L0801, 2021].
- If the niche-level stores show low traffic, broaden the category one level, find the top stores there, and check their traffic. Use those larger stores to judge demand [L0801, 2021].
- A niche with zero Alexa rank but other data available usually indicates a tool glitch rather than a real lack of traffic. Check the ranking by testing other large websites before excluding the niche [L0961, 2020].
- Competitors with no Alexa rank usually mean little traffic [L0664, 2020].
- Competitors with no rank because the tool counts zero when no country is listed. A site with no country listed counts as zero [L0359, 2020].

#### Older Alexa-era rules (2019-2021)

- An Alexa rank under 1 million (as of 2020) is the threshold for competitor sites in a niche. A niche whose competitors have no Alexa rank usually means little traffic, so look for at least one ranked store in a wider category that includes the niche [L0664, 2020].
- A niche with weak competitor Alexa rankings can still be pursued if other criteria pass: enough brands and products, and enough search traffic in keyword tools. Abandoning it is not automatic [L0664, 2020].
- If a niche's competitor sites rank only globally, the niche is generally not viable. Similar sites that sell related products and rank within the top 100,000 on the Alexa US ranking can still justify going ahead [L0961, 2020].
- Alexa.com can be used for free to look up competitor sites. Scroll to the bottom of the page and use the "browse all sites" search field to enter a competitor's domain. No paid plan is needed [L1002, 2019].

### Future competitors sheet

#### Structure

- Keep one separate spreadsheet tab per niche instead of listing all niches on one sheet. Each niche's future competitor list then stays clean and easy to review [L0687, 2020-26].
- Layout of a future competitors tab, one row per competitor:

| Column | Content | Ids |
|---|---|---|
| Store name | Name of the competitor store, e.g. Wayfair | [L0687, 2020-26] |
| URL | The store's web address, copied from its website | [L0687, 2020-26] |
| Traffic rank | Country traffic rank for the country where you plan to sell (e.g. US), not the global rank | [L0687, 2020-26] |
| Niches sold | Every category the store sells, not only your target niche | [L0687, 2020-26] |

- Alternative column order in the Niche Future Competitors list, one row per competing store found for a niche:

| URL | Store Name | Traffic Rank | Niches Sold |
|---|---|---|---|
| | | | |

  [L0543, undated]
- The sheet is built from the master tab "Niche Future Competitors" in the Niche Research Worksheet. The Store Name and Store URL columns hold the competitors found in earlier research [L0051, undated].
- In the SimilarWeb lookup procedure, the traffic rank goes in column C of the sheet [L0891, 2020-26].

#### Procedure: build the list for one niche (worksheet tab, 11 steps)

1. Open your Niche Research Worksheet and select your top niche. [L0051, undated]
2. Right-click the "Niche Future Competitors" tab and click Duplicate. [L0051, undated]
3. Rename the duplicated tab with your niche name (e.g., "Outdoor Kitchens, Future Competitors"). [L0051, undated]
4. Add the future competitors you identified in your previous research to the Store Name and Store URL columns. [L0051, undated]
5. Install the SimilarWeb Chrome extension (free). [L0051, undated]
6. For each competitor, open their website and click the SimilarWeb extension icon. [L0051, undated]
7. Scroll to Country Rank and record the rank for your target country (e.g., United States). [L0051, undated]
8. Complete the worksheet by identifying other niches each competitor sells in. [L0051, undated]
9. Verify that at least one competitor ranks between 1 and 100,000. [L0051, undated]
10. If yes, mark this lesson complete and proceed to the next lesson. [L0051, undated]
11. If no, return to your niche list and continue testing other niches. [L0051, undated]

#### Procedure: build the list for one niche (sheet copy, 10 steps)

1. Copy the niche name from the research worksheet. [L0687, 2020-26]
2. Right-click the master tab named for future competitors and choose Duplicate. [L0687, 2020-26]
3. Right-click the copy, choose Rename, paste the niche name, and name the tab "[niche name], future competitors". [L0687, 2020-26]
4. Open the first future competitor store found in the previous step and copy its URL into the sheet. [L0687, 2020-26]
5. Enter the store name. [L0687, 2020-26]
6. Open the store's website with the SimilarWeb Chrome extension active and read the country rank for the country where you plan to sell, not the global rank. [L0687, 2020-26]
7. Enter that country rank in the traffic rank column. [L0687, 2020-26]
8. Enter every category the store sells, not only your target niche, in the niches sold column. [L0687, 2020-26]
9. Repeat steps 4 to 8 for every future competitor on the list, including any with no visible rank. [L0687, 2020-26]
10. Check whether at least one competitor has a country traffic rank between 1 and 100,000. If yes, the niche passes this test; if no, return to the research worksheet and keep working the list. [L0687, 2020-26]

#### Procedure: record the niches a competitor sells

1. Open the competitor's website. [L0687, 2020-26]
2. Click "Shop all" to open the full catalogue. [L0687, 2020-26]
3. Read the main categories the store shows there. [L0687, 2020-26]
4. Note any other product areas the store also carries, even if they are not its main focus. [L0687, 2020-26]
5. Enter each category in the "niches sold" column, separated by commas. [L0687, 2020-26]
6. Keep every category, not only the one you are researching, so you can spot related niches for later expansion. [L0687, 2020-26]

#### Using the list

- A competitor's category list shows how broad the store is. A store that sells many related categories, such as a large home retailer, may reveal secondary niches you could later build a separate store for [L0687, 2020-26].
- Once a first store in one niche is profitable, look at the related categories of a broad competitor and consider building a second store in one of those categories [L0687, 2020-26].
- Note every category a competitor sells beyond your target niche. A broad store that sells related categories can reveal secondary niches for a second store later [L0687, 2020-26].
- Before counting a competitor in the sheet, check the address and whether it is online-only [L0804, 2023; L0460, 2020-26].
- Supplier websites or competitors with no rank: record them, but they do not count toward a pass [L0687, 2020-26].

### Worked cases

- **CNC Machines.** Multiple direct-to-consumer manufacturers were found, one online-only future competitor was found, and only 8 total suppliers were estimated. Result: marked dropship-friendly but weak because the supplier count is too low [L0050, undated].
- **Outdoor Kitchens.** Wayfair and Zoro sold over 100 distinct brands, with strong demand, pricing and profit margins. Result: marked as a strong niche candidate [L0050, undated].
- **Three validated future competitors.** Wayfair (traffic rank 91 in the US, sells everything home), Zoro (2,350, sells tools, restaurant supplies and furniture), and Home Outlet Direct (50,116, sells appliances, grills and outdoor products). All three rank under 100,000, confirming strong market demand [L0051, undated].
- **Three US competitor ranks, recorded at the time of the lesson.** A furniture store with a lighting category had a US country rank of 38,442. A lighting store had 84,612. A home decor and lighting store had 21,829 [L0891, 2020-26].
- **Failing example.** Three competitors in a target country with ranks of 200,000 and 500,000, and a third site showing no rank because its traffic is very low. None falls below 100,000, so this is a bad sign and the niche fails this test. Return to the list and look for more competitors [L0687, 2020-26].
- **Marketplace example.** A large online marketplace that dropships most of its catalogue can confirm a niche before smaller sellers are checked [L0459, 2020-26].
- **Benchmarks for scale.** A furniture and decor online store with over 300 brands and over 350,000 products, a lighting seller with over 100,000 lights, and a chandelier category with 1,855 products from one store. These figures show what an online-only multi-brand seller looks like [L0460, 2020-26].
- **Build-your-own case.** If a niche has 20 or more brands and no dedicated niche store exists, you may build that niche store yourself. A missing competitor store is not a reason to reject the niche [L0551, 2020].

### Where the sources disagree

- **Amazon and Wayfair.** In 2019, sources said to exclude Amazon, Wayfair and other warehouse-based operations from validation [L0243, 2019]. Another 2019 source accepted Amazon and multi-category marketplaces as competition research [L0243, 2019]. From 2022 on, Wayfair counts as a competitor through its subcategories [L0803, 2022] and a large marketplace can confirm a niche [L0459, 2020-26]. Use the marketplace to confirm the niche, but count only pure online-only sellers for validation [L0460, 2020-26].
- **Number of competitors.** One 2019 source requires one to three online retailers [L0243, 2019]. A 2021 source requires at least three online-only stores [L0853, 2021]. A 2022 source says two or three are enough [L0969, 2022]. A 2023 source says two in range are enough [L0964, 2023]. The current test requires at least one online-only, multi-brand competitor [L0459, 2020-26]. Use the strictest source that applies to your decision, and check the newest guidance.
- **Traffic threshold.** The current test uses 1-100,000 [L0051, undated; L0687, 2020-26]. A 2023 source uses 1-1,000,000 [L0964, 2023]. A 2021 source accepts 169,000 [L0247, 2021]. Use 100,000 for the pass decision; use the 1,000,000 figure only as a broader screen when the niche is otherwise strong.
- **Supplier count.** The worksheet bar is 20 suppliers, with 100+ strong [L0050, undated]. The transcript also names 100 suppliers as the bar to move to the next research stage [L0459, 2020-26]. Check the stricter threshold before advancing.
- **Price filter.** One 2021 source recommends a price filter for competitor research [L0801, 2021]. Two other sources say not to use it [L0834, 2021; L0806, 2024]. Use keywords for the price tier instead of a sidebar filter.
- **Parent company.** A physical-retail seller is excluded even if it is multi-brand [L0459, 2020-26]. An online-only store owned by a company with physical stores still counts [L0460, 2020-26; L0804, 2023]. The distinction is the site itself: a store locator or pick-up option on the site excludes it; an online-only site owned by a physical company is counted, with a possible harder approval.
- **Physical presence for supplier and competitor.** A warehouse or store is not a reason to avoid a supplier [L0357, 2019]. It excludes a competitor from validation [L0853, 2021]. Both apply to different decisions.
- **Country versus global rank.** Competitor validation uses the country rank [L0687, 2020-26; L0891, 2020-26]. Supplier extraction in 2023 used a global rank between 1 and 100,000 [L0855, 2023]. Use country rank for validation; the 2023 rule applies only to finding suppliers.
- **Column order.** The sheet layout in one source lists Store name, URL, Traffic rank, Niches sold [L0687, 2020-26]. Another lists URL, Store Name, Traffic Rank, Niches Sold [L0543, undated]. The SimilarWeb procedure puts the rank in column C [L0891, 2020-26]. Use the layout your worksheet already has and keep the rank column consistent with that.
- **Stand-up desks example.** The source phrases the narrow and broad levels as "desks to stand-up desks is the narrow case", while also calling a broader category like desks the fallback [L0941, 2022]. The consistent reading is that stand-up desks is the narrower term and desks the broader fallback.
