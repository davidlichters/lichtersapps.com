### Delegating Product Research

- Do product research yourself across several products, niches and countries before handing it to a team. Without knowing which niche works for you, a team returns a huge generic product list that is hard to use. [L1358, undated]
- Delegating product research (short version): [L1358, undated]
  1. Give the team member access to your product research software. [L1358, undated]
  2. Show them exactly which product criteria and customer problems you are looking for. [L1358, undated]
  3. Have them send you a list of products they believe will work at the end of each week. [L1358, undated]
  4. Scan the list, choose the products you like, and start testing them. [L1358, undated]
- Delegating product research (full version): [L1360, undated]
  1. Develop your own product research skill first and test multiple products, niches and target countries. [L1360, undated]
  2. Define the target audience and the problems you are looking for, based on what has worked in your past stores. [L1360, undated]
  3. Teach the team member your exact research methods and give them access to your research tools, for example Peeksta. [L1360, undated]
  4. Show them exactly which problems and needs to look for. [L1360, undated]
  5. Ask for a list of products at the end of each week. [L1360, undated]
  6. Scan the list, choose the products you like and start testing them. [L1360, undated]
- Delegating without a defined target audience leads to a huge, general list of products that may or may not work. Team members do not know what to look for unless the owner has already identified the best niche and audience. [L1360, undated]
- Give product research criteria to the team, for example upsell potential and a maximum supplier cost of 30 USD on AliExpress. Clear criteria let the assistant screen products before the owner picks one. [L1431, 2020+]
- A team member with a personal interest in a niche suggested it for a second store, which then converted better than the first store. Letting people who know a category choose products can beat the owner's own guesses. [L1437, 2018] **
- Every research step can be split into smaller tasks, and each task with clear instructions can be taught to a person or an AI agent. The quality of the result depends on how well the instructions are written and which best practices are applied. [L0464, 2025-26] **

### End-to-End Research Process

#### Phase Timeline

- Phase 2, Product Research and Validation (Days 15 to 30): identify a proven product worth building a real brand around, not just testing for quick sales. [L0199, 2026]
- Research and tests begin early, run in parallel with training, and keep running after the first winner. [L0491, 2026; L1184, 2023+]

#### Niche Stage

- Niche selection and market research, in order: [L0393, undated; L0532, undated] **
  1. Review the niche selection material. [L0393, undated; L0532, undated]
  2. Brainstorm at least 50 niche ideas and list them in a niche worksheet. Add 50 or more niche ideas to the niche worksheet. [L0393, undated; L0532, undated]
  3. Begin the market research tests. Start the research tests from the market research material. [L0393, undated; L0532, undated]
  4. Complete all market research tests. [L0393, undated; L0532, undated]
  5. Choose the niche for your first store. [L0393, undated; L0532, undated]
- You do not need to research all 50 niches. If 2 to 3 feel strong and meet criteria, research those. Most lists contain multiple winners, and thorough research is only needed on the top candidates. [L0274, 2023]
- Research every niche only until a winner is found. It is not necessary to search suppliers for every niche on a list of 50. [L1069, 2019] **
- Niche checks procedure:
  1. Confirm the niche has at least 20 brands that sell the products through online stores. [L0468, 2020]
  2. Confirm there are at least 100 different products available across those brands. [L0468, 2020]
  3. Check that demand and interest in the niche exist. [L0468, 2020]
  4. Run the seasonality test and avoid niches that collapse for part of the year. [L0468, 2020]
  5. Search Google Shopping and review the first page of results to see the average selling price. [L0468, 2020]

#### Product Stage

- Product research procedure, in this order: [L0335, 2023] **
  1. Look only at products already advertised on the ad platform, not untested items. [L0335, 2023]
  2. Check proof of concept on competitor ads: likes, comments and especially shares. [L0335, 2023]
  3. Find early winners: ads launched in the last 1 to 2 weeks, with about one month as the widest window. [L0335, 2023]
  4. Check the Facebook page creation date of the competitor. [L0335, 2023]
  5. Confirm scaling: count active ads and new ads per day on the competitor's page. [L0335, 2023]
  6. Verify sales with at least two tools. [L0335, 2023]
  7. Check product criteria: at least 3x markup, longevity, quality rating, and a problem-solving or wow factor. [L0335, 2023]
- Product research process: [L0637, 2023+]
  1. Use spy tools to find products. [L0637, 2023+]
  2. Compare 10 to 15 candidate products. [L0637, 2023+]
  3. Check the pros and cons of each niche and product. [L0637, 2023+]
  4. Look at the value the product gives, including whether it solves a problem. [L0637, 2023+]
  5. Find marketing angles competitors are missing. [L0637, 2023+]
  6. Pick the products that meet the most criteria, with a clear competitor gap and room for better creatives and websites. [L0637, 2023+]
- Product selection approach with the Peak tool: see the seven-step procedure in the screening section above. [L0577, 2023]
- Product research routine for a batch of 8 to 10 products: [L1176, 2023]
  1. In AfterLib, find trending products and sort them by percentage change in ad count, viewing up to 80 ads per page. [L1176, 2023]
  2. Open each product's store and note its price, shipping offer and target countries. [L1176, 2023]
  3. Copy the store link into the research sheet. [L1176, 2023]
  4. Open the Facebook Ad Library, check the number of active ads, the ad copies and the age of the Facebook page. [L1176, 2023]
  5. Remove products whose ads are inactive, because nobody is selling them right now. [L1176, 2023]
  6. Check the supplier or CJ shipping cost and the sourcing cost before testing. [L1176, 2023]
  7. Keep the products that pass and test them one at a time. [L1176, 2023]
- Product research order:
  1. Go through a product criteria checklist of six to seven points to define the kind of product you want. [L1382, undated]
  2. Search TikTok ads in a spy tool (PeakStar or PP Ads) with the filters for that tool. [L1382, undated]
  3. Check supplier quality on AliExpress by reading ratings and reviews of multiple listings. [L1382, undated]
  4. Check the competition and whether a product is already heavily sold. [L1382, undated]
  5. Verify sales of competing stores with a store-tracking tool. [L1382, undated]
  6. Check product quality, the size of the problem it solves, the number of use cases, how the store looks, and whether the margin is sufficient. [L1382, undated]
- Product selection approach shown in the lesson: see the seven-step procedure in the screening section above. [L0577, 2023]
- Competitor analysis: check how many competitors run the same or a related product, and how strong they are. [L0708, undated]
- Product quality check: review ratings on AliExpress and Amazon and, if possible, order a sample. [L0708, undated]
- Product spy research: find products that are currently selling, using filtered spy tools. [L0708, undated]
- Five success factors for profitable product research, in order: [L1158, 2023]
  1. Use a solid product criteria list of validation points to judge potential winners. Adapt it to your own process, since no single process fits everyone. [L1158, 2023]
  2. Learn to be a differentiator: find how you can stand out from competitors. [L1158, 2023]
  3. Understand why a customer buys from you and not from a competitor, Amazon, or another store, by critically evaluating your own landing page and branding, ideally with outside feedback asking for negative points as well. [L1158, 2023]
  4. Understand your exact audience by stepping into their shoes. [L1158, 2023]
  5. Combine points 2, 3 and 4 to turn a regular product into a stronger potential winner. [L1158, 2023]
- Women's products and other audience-led categories, tested through the Jungle Scout route: see the women's product test procedure in the stop rules above. [L1184, 2023+]
- Fashion dropshipping research workflow:
  1. Create a free AdSpy account. If the free page limit is used up, create a new account with a different email address. [L0661, undated]
  2. Set the gender filter to female, the age filter to 40 to 65+, and the country filter to United States. [L0661, undated]
  3. Type a product keyword into the ad text filter, for example shoe, sneakers, boot or slippers. [L0661, undated]
  4. Scroll through the results and note ads with many likes, comments and shares, and check the ad creation date. [L0661, undated]
  5. Open the AliExpress app on your phone, use the camera search on a photo of the product, and open the matching listing. [L0661, undated]
  6. If the AliExpress order count is low, check whether the seller fulfills through CJ and look at the CJ listing. [L0661, undated]
  7. Watch how the ad uses copy, video type and images, and use that as inspiration for your own product page. [L0661, undated]
  8. Test the product in a market where it is not yet saturated, such as Australia, New Zealand, Germany, Switzerland or Austria. [L0661, undated]
- Use the 4-stage sequence in order, because each stage filters out products before the more time-consuming checks begin. [L0636, undated]

#### Daily and Weekly Cadence

- Keep product research running in parallel with testing, and check the list every week. [L1184, 2023+; L1172, 2023+]
- Test the sales hypothesis against the rule set each week rather than on a fixed guess. [L1158, 2023]

#### Ads, Pages and Variant Tests

- Test a few products in one segment rather than all products at once, so each product gets enough attention. [L0995, 2023; L0999, 2023]
- For a product test, use the full research order, then ad testing, then store and offer checks. [L1382, undated; L0502, 2020+; L0650, undated]
