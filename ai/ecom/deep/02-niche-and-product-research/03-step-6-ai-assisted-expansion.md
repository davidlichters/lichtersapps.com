**Rules for the AI step**

- Paste your complete existing niche list into the "Existing Niches, Do Not Repeat" section of the AI prompt to prevent the tool from suggesting ideas you have already brainstormed. [L0024, undated]
- Use the AI prompt to generate at least 50 additional niche ideas beyond your manual list. This quantity ensures a comprehensive pool of candidates to evaluate in market research. [L0159, 2020-26]
- Note the difference in counting: [L0159, 2020-26] asks for at least 50 additional AI ideas beyond the manual list, while [L0591, 2020-26] and [L0024, undated] count the final list as at least 50 with the AI supplying the remaining 40 goal. Use whichever matches your plan, and make sure the final list reaches at least 50. [L0159, 2020-26; L0591, 2020-26; L0024, undated]
- Expansion example: 17 manually brainstormed niche ideas plus the AI prompt generated 51 additional ideas that met all criteria, for a total research list of 68 niches qualified for supplier research and demand validation. [L0159, 2020-26]
- Use ChatGPT (free tier available) or Gemini. Both tools process the same prompt structure and produce equivalent results for niche expansion. [L0159, 2020-26]
- Any AI chat tool such as ChatGPT or Gemini can suggest narrower sub-niches and higher-price variants of a broad niche. Its output is a list of ideas to test, not a verified demand source. [L1517, 2020-26]
- AI niche ideas must be checked against every research criterion before use, because the tool can return niches that break the rules, such as consumables or custom-only products. [L1067, undated]
- ChatGPT draws on training data from a very large part of the internet, and for brainstorming it often produces ideas that a person would not reach through manual research alone. [L1056, 2020-26]

**Expansion prompts**

Expensive-products prompt (short form):

```
Please list expensive products in the [EDIT] niche.
```

Broad-category expansion prompt:

```
Please list examples of expensive products in the [niche] niche. Replace [niche] with the broad category or niche you want to expand. Only list physical products, not services. Skip products dominated by a few big brands, and name the product type instead of a brand.
```

High-ticket expansion prompt (full text):

```
I'm building a list of at least 50 product categories for e-commerce dropshipping where typical prices exceed $500, buyers are affluent with low vendor loyalty, items are physical goods shipped directly, regulatory burden is minimal, niches avoid luxury or collector markets, and excluded are consumables, personalization-dependent products, made-to-order-only items, and low-ticket accessories unless paired with high-ticket inventory-avoiding duplication with an existing category list.
```

Full research prompt with CSV output:

```
I'm looking into premium product categories for a web shop that sells online.

Formatting rule: deliver the output as a comma-separated, CSV-like table without any markdown styling, with commas only as column separators. The header row must read: Niche,Estimated average sale price (USD),Target buyer,Why buyers choose on features or price. Every idea gets its own row. Inside any field, swap commas for semicolons. Add no text before or after the table.

Research criteria:
- Produce at least 50 more niche concepts that:
  - reach an average sale price of $500 or higher
  - attract upper-middle-class or wealthy shoppers
  - show weak brand loyalty, so buyers decide based on features or price
  - involve physical goods that get shipped
  - sit outside heavy regulation (no firearms, medical devices, and the like)
  - are neither luxury fashion nor driven by collecting
- Steer clear of:
  - consumables such as coffee pods, vitamins, or pet food
  - products that are only personalized or made to order
  - low-priced accessories, unless they come bundled with high-priced products

Niches already covered (don't repeat these):
[PASTE YOUR LIST HERE]
```

**Filtering AI and expansion results**

Procedure for filtering ChatGPT results (keep in order):

1. Paste the broad category into the prompt and run it. [L1056, 2020-26]
2. Read through the returned products. [L1056, 2020-26]
3. Remove entries dominated by a few big brands, such as well-known skincare brands or luxury perfume houses. [L1056, 2020-26]
4. Remove services, such as professional cosmetic procedures. Keep the products used to deliver those services, such as the equipment or supplies. [L1056, 2020-26]
5. Turn single-brand items into generic product types. For example, change a specific brand of bike to fitness bikes or exercise bikes. [L1056, 2020-26]
6. Keep plausible ideas such as home gym systems, high-end hair tools and home spa treatments. [L1056, 2020-26]
7. Add the remaining items to the niche worksheet. [L1056, 2020-26]

- Some ChatGPT results are too advanced to start with, such as surgical robots. Advanced ideas such as advanced prosthetics can still be added at the brainstorming stage. They are checked later in research. [L1056, 2020-26]
- The expensive-products prompt on health and fitness returned high-end treadmills and ellipticals, branded exercise bikes, smart fitness tech, home gym systems and personal trainers. Personal trainers are a service and are dropped, while generic items such as exercise bikes and home gym systems go on the list. Branded items are turned into product types before they are added. [L1056, 2020-26]
- Broad categories such as health, fitness and personal care are too wide to be a niche. Look for the specific sub-niche before adding a listing to the list. If the listing gives no detail, the broad label should not be added as is. [L1056, 2020-26]
- Narrow broad categories into specific product types before testing them. [L0359, 2020]

### Sold Stores, Listings and Marketplaces

**Business brokerage and sold-store research**

- Business brokerage websites list online businesses for sale, showing asking price, business age and net profit. Browsing their online and technology section reveals which products real stores sell. Use these sites for idea research only, not for buying a store. [L1056, 2020-26]
- The online and technology section of one brokerage site listed 1,368 businesses for sale at the time of recording. [L1056, 2020-26]
- A two-year-old Shopify store selling premium wall paneling was listed for almost $1.5 million and made about $600,000 net profit per year. Premium wall paneling is therefore a niche to add to the list. [L1056, 2020-26]
- A current e-commerce listing for LED lighting was shown with $362,000 in sales per month. Listings for active stores with high monthly sales are useful for finding product categories. [L1056, 2020-26]
- Empire Flippers is a marketplace for selling online businesses, and its e-commerce section shows stores for sale with details. Use the listings for research: look up what the store sells and which products it carries. A store listed for almost $3 million selling health, fitness, personal care and medical products is one example. [L1056, 2020-26]
- Sold-store and listing research can include content sites rather than pure e-commerce stores. A content-driven site that sells gear still shows product categories, such as fishing, camping and hiking equipment, that are worth adding to the list. [L1056, 2020-26]

**Warnings for pre-made and public sources**

- Do not start niche brainstorming by searching online for best dropshipping products or products to sell this year. Those searches mostly surface middlemen suppliers and make it very hard to find something that works. Generate ideas from your own purchases, surroundings and daily life instead. [L1082, 2020-26]
- Ready-made lists of top-selling products or niches are shared with thousands of people, so the top items are usually too competitive. Do not start from such a list; generate your own market ideas. [L0458, 2021-22]
- Do not rely on pre-made lists of approved niches. When many people pick the same few markets, competition rises and those markets stop being profitable for everyone. Find market ideas through your own research. [L0996, 2021-22]
- In dropshipping communities, asking other sellers for their winning niche is considered taboo. Generate niche candidates from your own research instead. [L1513, 2019]

### Stalled Brainstorming and Outside Sources

- When brainstorming stalls, use outside sources to find ideas, such as internet searches on passions and hobbies, marketplace category browsing, special interest magazines, library books, and hobby lists. [L0996, 2021-22]
- When a category produces a promising product, use that category name as a new search term in other sources, such as Google, magazines or libraries. A single category can lead to a broader or more refined market idea. [L0996, 2021-22]
- Stuck-brainstorm procedure:
  1. Go through a list of hobbies and mark the ideas that stand out. [L1002, 2019]
  2. Run each marked idea through the niche verification tool. [L1002, 2019]
  3. If an idea passes all checks, start building the store right away instead of delaying. [L1002, 2019]
- Note on the 2019 procedure: it moves straight to building the store once an idea passes its checks. Current guidance requires at least 50 niche ideas before narrowing to one niche for the first store. [L1002, 2019; L0532, undated; L0591, 2020-26]

### Narrowing and Filtering the List

**Quick checks (keep in order)**

1. Brainstorm a broad initial list of niches. [L0766, 2020-26]
2. Filter the list using the quick checks: electronics, emotional attachment, strict deadlines, personalized items that must be seen or felt, and seasonality. [L0766, 2020-26]
3. Verify the remaining niches against size, price and target demographic before committing. [L0766, 2020-26]

**Connection, passion and market size**

- Do not limit yourself to one niche at the start. If you have a personal connection to a niche, start there; otherwise choose by problem and market size. Sub-niches can be built later within the same problem area. [L0871, 2021+]
- Pick a niche by listing the brands you would like to resemble and studying the customer base they already attract. If your brand differs clearly from those brands, build your own audience instead of copying theirs. [L1438, 2023+]
- Passion is set aside at the first brainstorm stage, where price is the only criterion. [L0012, undated]
- Conflict: the first brainstorm disregards passion and interests [L0012, undated], while the one source approach starts from a personal connection when one exists [L0871, 2021+]. Use the price-first list for the initial 50, and apply the personal-connection rule when choosing where to begin among the survivors.

**Other filters**

- Hobbies that require equipment, supplies or specialised gear are promising; hobbies that only involve advice or skill are weak candidates. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
- Do not start with a single niche: scoring, validation and elimination happen. [L0024, undated; L0034, undated]

### Common Mistakes

- Sources warn against starting from a short list of pre-approved niches, against broad labels, and against cheap hobbies. [L0458, 2021-22; L0996, 2021-22; L1056, 2020-26]
- A list of a hundred niche ideas can still be all bad if the research criteria are not set before brainstorming. [L0506, 2020-26]
- Avoid searching for best dropshipping products or products to sell this year at the brainstorm stage. [L1082, 2020-26]
- Do not request winning niches from other sellers; generate candidates from your own research. [L1513, 2019]
