## Brainstorming Niche Ideas

### Targets and Minimum Counts

- Write at least 10 ideas yourself before using any AI tool, then expand the list with AI to 50 or more. [L0010, undated; L0024, undated; L0159, 2020-26; L0591, 2020-26]
- If fewer than 10 manual ideas exist, pause and brainstorm more before going on. [L0024, undated; L1179, 2020-26]
- The 10-idea minimum gives the AI enough context and keeps its suggestions from being purely generic, without your market intuition. [L0159, 2020-26]
- Your manual brainstorm captures market intuition and unique angles, while AI finds systematic variations that meet your explicit criteria. [L0159, 2020-26]

### Principles of the Brainstorm

- The brainstorm only collects candidates; you do not need to be sure any idea is the best one. Profitability is checked later, where weak ideas are set aside. [L1082, 2020-26]
- Brainstorming produces candidates only, and every candidate must then be validated. [L0468, 2020; older]
- Brainstorm many ideas before choosing, because most will be weak and only one to three will fit you. Discarding weak ideas is a normal part of the process. [L1040, 2021-22; older]
- Generate ideas freely without filtering them against criteria during brainstorming, because filtering early kills ideas before they are developed. The goal of this stage is volume. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
- Add any idea that might work, even if it seems unlikely; strict filtering happens later in market research against defined criteria. [L1056, 2020-26]
- Sources differ on timing. One view says the research criteria must be set before brainstorming, because a list of a hundred niche ideas can still be all bad if you do not know what to look for. [L0506, 2020-26] The other says do not filter while generating; filtering comes after. [L0996, 2021-22; L1056, 2020-26] Apply both: know your criteria first, and apply them only after the list is written.
- Brainstorming is a habit. Opportunities for product ideas are everywhere once you look for them, and the idea list grows faster with practice. [L1082, 2020-26]
- Aim for a long idea list now, because the later action tasks ask for many ideas. Keep all ideas in one spreadsheet so they are ready for research. [L1082, 2020-26]
- Record product types rather than single brands or models, for example electric mountain bikes, car rims or chandeliers. Product types are broader and easier to research. [L1082, 2020-26]
- A market idea can be a product type or a market defined by its audience or interest. Both forms are valid, and you can use whichever is clearer to you. [L0996, 2021-22]
- A product-type idea names one specific item, such as a gear category like wetsuits or dive equipment. An audience-type idea names the interest group and leaves the product range open, such as scuba divers who buy a range of diving gear. [L0996, 2021-22]
- Narrow a broad category, such as home furniture, into specific product types, such as sofas, dining room tables, patio furniture or hammocks, before researching niches. [L0359, 2020]

### Step 2: Personal Environment and Purchase History

**Brainstorm order (keep in order)**

1. Look around your home and your workplace and write down physical products you see or own, keeping the $200 threshold in mind. [L1082, 2020-26]
2. Open your credit card statements, find past purchases over $200, and add every physical product to your list. [L1082, 2020-26]
3. Ask close friends and family which products they have bought, then ask a wider group, for example with a Facebook post to your connections. [L1082, 2020-26]
4. Go through your hobbies: note products advertised in magazines and blogs, products mentioned or advertised in podcasts, product-type questions in Facebook groups about your hobbies, sidebar ads and product reviews on blogs you follow. [L1082, 2020-26]
5. Observe products in everyday places such as work, the gym, the shopping mall and the doctor's office, and add anything that could become a product idea. [L1082, 2020-26]
6. Write every idea into column A of the niche research worksheet. [L1082, 2020-26]

**Examples from everyday surroundings**

- Everyday places are full of product ideas. At a doctor's office these include reception desks, medical chairs, examination lights, medical tools such as scalpels and lobby chairs. At a gym they include weight machines and cardio machines. [L1082, 2020-26]
- Ideas can come from whatever is in front of you. Examples are a chandelier in your home, a built-in wine refrigerator in your kitchen, or car rims on a car next to you in traffic. Each goes straight onto the list. [L1082, 2020-26]
- A single living room contains more than 17 product categories that can be niches: chandeliers, wall lighting, table lamps, crown molding, wallpaper, coffee tables, sofas, fireplaces, mirrors, vases, bookshelves, dining tables, dining chairs and wood flooring. Look around for product types. [L0988, 2020-26; L1082, 2020-26]
- Items to list from one living room photo include chandeliers, wall lighting, crown molding, lamps, sofas, coffee tables, wood flooring, dining tables and chairs, fireplaces, mirrors and bookshelves. [L1082, 2020-26; L0988, 2020-26]
- Product ideas can come from everyday observation, such as outdoor items, pools, fire pits, garden structures and large smokers seen around a home or on a walk. [L0967, 2020]
- Niche ideas can come from everyday objects around you, such as furniture in your living room. Test each idea against the validation criteria before you commit to it. [L1275, 2019]
- Niche ideas can come from everyday observation, such as background products in movies or shows. Test each idea against the normal validation criteria before committing. [L0853, 2021]
- Everyday personal needs, such as children's play equipment, can suggest niche ideas that are worth checking against price and shipping criteria. [L1179, 2020-26]

**Principles for personal sources**

- Hobbies are a source of product inspiration, not a requirement. You do not have to build a business around a hobby you are passionate about to use it for niche ideas. [L1082, 2020-26]
- Record product types rather than single brands when you note an item from your surroundings. [L1082, 2020-26]

### Step 4: Hobbies, Interests and Magazines

**Hobby brainstorm procedure (keep in order)**

1. List things people do, love or are passionate about, such as hobbies, sports and interests, and not things they only have to do. [L0771, 2021-22; older]
2. Use Google searches for lists of hobbies, activities and interests, and check books and specialist magazines for more ideas. [L0771, 2021-22; older]
3. Write the ideas into a spreadsheet or on paper, aiming for about 20 ideas. [L0771, 2021-22; older]
4. Do not evaluate them yet; the brainstorm is only the list that is researched next. [L0771, 2021-22; older]

**Market-idea procedure (keep in order)**

1. Open the research tracker and work only in the Market Idea column. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
2. Write down ideas without judging them against any criteria. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
3. Each idea can be a product you could sell, a customer group you could serve, or an interest you could serve with products. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
4. If you are stuck, take ideas from outside sources such as online searches, marketplace categories, magazines, library books, or hobby lists. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
5. Keep adding ideas until you have at least 20 market ideas. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
6. Only then move on to assessing the ideas against the other criteria. [L0996, 2021-22; older]

**Interest-group procedure (keep in order)**

1. Brainstorm at least 20 market ideas, using paper or a spreadsheet, based on hobbies and interests you, your friends and family, Google hobby lists, and books or magazines. [L0458, 2021-22]
2. For each interest group, list the high-ticket products its members would need or want to buy. [L0458, 2021-22]
3. Run each idea through the six market criteria using Google Shopping and a keyword tool. [L0458, 2021-22]
4. Mark each criterion yes, no or maybe in your tracker. [L0458, 2021-22]
5. Prioritise ideas with yes on all six criteria; keep the others on the list in case they are needed. [L0458, 2021-22]

**Google hobby-list procedure (keep in order)**

1. Search for 'list of hobbies' in Google. [L0996, 2021-22]
2. Open one of the hobby lists that appear in the results. [L0996, 2021-22]
3. Go through the listed hobbies one by one. [L0996, 2021-22]
4. For each hobby, ask whether participants buy equipment or products, and if so, write that product or the hobby as a market idea. [L0996, 2021-22]
5. Move on to the next hobby until you have enough ideas. [L0996, 2021-22]

**Hobby sources and examples**

- Products that people buy for their own hobbies, see on special interest TV shows, or that friends and family use are good starting points. People who already buy expensive products for a pastime are a strong signal for a high-ticket market. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
- Special interest magazines are useful because their advertisers are typically companies selling products for that interest. Flipping through a magazine's ads shows which product types are sold to that audience. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
- Ads in a hobby magazine or blog for a new type of product can be added to the list directly. A mountain biker who sees ads for electric mountain bikes in biking magazines or forums would add electric mountain bikes as an idea. [L1082, 2020-26]
- One hobby list was described as containing 549 hand-picked hobby ideas, which gives a large pool to screen. This is one example of how a single search can deliver hundreds of candidate interests. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
- A Google search for a list of hobbies returns Wikipedia's list, a compiled list of about 549 hand-picked hobby ideas, and hobby category sites such as Discover a Hobby. Together these give a large pool of candidate interests from a single search. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
- Hobby lists can be used from any country, because the hobbies are not tied to one region. A hobby that is popular locally but not in the target market should still be recorded and checked later. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
- Not every hobby contains products worth selling. Hobbies that require equipment, supplies or specialised gear are promising, while hobbies that involve only giving advice or performing a skill often have no products to sell. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
- Beekeeping implies equipment purchases, and commercial t-shirt pressing may involve expensive machinery, so both are worth recording. A handyman trade or giving advice typically has few products, so those are weaker candidates. Interior design usually involves many relatively high-priced home products. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
- An activity such as horse riding can be recorded as a market idea, or a specific expensive product linked to it, such as horse saddles, can be recorded instead. Product ideas that look expensive can be written down now and researched later. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
- On a Sports & Outdoors hobby page, bodybuilding, cardio workouts, cycling, golf, horse riding and camping are examples of hobbies that involve purchasable gear. Each can be recorded as a market idea or narrowed to a specific product such as horse saddles. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
- Water hobbies such as kayaking and canoeing are worth recording because quality kayaks and canoes tend to be expensive. Water sports is a broader alternative label that can be used instead. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
- Crafts such as engraving and car detailing should be screened even though the craft itself is not a product. The tools and consumables used in these crafts can be the products to research. [L0996, 2021-22; older]
- A list of over 1,000 hobbies can be scanned for niche ideas. Not every hobby is a niche or a product category, so look for the ones that stand out. [L1056, 2020-26]

### Step 5: Pitch-a-Niche and Reflection Exercises

**Pitch-a-niche (keep in order)**

1. Write down every niche you think is a good fit for dropshipping. [L0831, 2019]
2. For each niche, write down the reasons behind your choice. The purpose is to bring out the underlying 'what for' motives that drive the choice. [L0831, 2019]

**Timed first-reaction brainstorm (keep in order)**

1. Set a two-minute timer. [L1513, 2019]
2. Write down as many possible niches as you can in that time. [L1513, 2019]
3. Note the first thoughts that come up, such as worthiness, fear or excitement. These thoughts reveal the programming that will influence later decisions. [L1513, 2019]

**Timing rule**

- Complete the first brainstorm within 15-20 minutes. [L0012, undated]
