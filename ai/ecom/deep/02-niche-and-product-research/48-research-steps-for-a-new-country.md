### Research steps for a new country

1. Set a VPN to the target country, then search the supplier name or product on Google Shopping to see the local results [L0665, 2022].
2. Use the one source research tool with the target country selected to see whether people search for the product there [L0665, 2022].
3. Search results depend on location. When researching a market for a country you do not live in, use a VPN and set the location to the country you plan to sell in, or results may come from your home country or other countries [L1329, 2021-22].
4. Use a VPN set to the target country so Google returns local results. Without it, search results in another country distort competitor and niche findings [L1279, 2021].
5. Market size check for the Facebook Ad Library:
   1. Search for the outlook for growth or market valuation of the industry (for example the pet industry).
   2. Check the ad library for each target country (for example Germany) to see how many sellers run ads there.
   3. Countries with lower saturation are better test markets [L0895, 2021+].
6. Facebook Ad Library country check:
   1. Search a product keyword in the Facebook Ad Library and use the country filter to limit results to one market, such as the United States.
   2. Compare it with a test market such as Germany. Low scaling in the test market suggests room for a new seller [L0895, 2021+].
7. Check whether a product is popular in each target country before testing. A product that sells well in one country may not sell in another. For example, air fryers are less common in Switzerland than in the US [L1176, 2023].
8. Compare the new country's shipping infrastructure and supplier responsiveness with a market you already know. Check how quickly suppliers reply and how reliably parcels arrive before committing money to ads or inventory [L1441, 2018].

### Language, translation and language arbitrage

- Test products with English-speaking markets before translating the store into other languages, even if competitors are not marketing there. Translation requires both professional translation services and a customer service virtual assistant fluent in that language, which adds significant cost and complexity [L0280, undated].
- Start product research with English-language ads. The US English market has a larger total addressable market (TAM) than smaller language markets. Study which creatives and messages already run there before looking at underserved markets [L1195, 2026].
- Language arbitrage: take an ad that already converts in one language and run it in a language market that has not seen it yet. The Spanish-speaking market in the US has about 60 million people, a smaller total addressable market than the English market, but far less competition [L1195, 2026].
- The Spanish-language market in the US is largely untapped. Its total addressable market is smaller than the white and Black American markets, but auctions are less saturated, so demand is proven while competition is not [L1019, 2026].
- Products whose ads are mostly in Spanish can be run, but only if the landing pages are customized and everything is translated into Spanish. This is harder for a beginner, so beginners should skip Spanish-language ad products at the start [L1187, 2026].
- Taobao videos mostly feature Asian presenters and may underperform in the US market. Recast them with white or Black American talent when producing custom ads for the US [L1195, 2026].
- When the market is English-speaking, a product that worked for a long time there can be tested in other languages such as German or French. Native-language markets often have less competition [L1173, undated].
- Validate a product that sells well in one country by running the same product with ads in the language of another target market. If the new market also shows a strong hook rate and CTR, the product is worth more store work [L1108, 2023].
- When testing an ebook or digital product in a new country, translate the mockup and ad copy into that country's language before running ads. The lesson's example built a German mockup for a German market test, which makes it possible to judge whether the offer itself is attractive to local buyers [L1131, 2018+].
- Entering an untapped market needs only minimal effort beyond the product. Native translations and local payment methods are required, and Stripe lets you enable the most used payment methods for each country. Custom branding or UGC is not needed from the start [L1158, 2023].
- Study what works in other markets to find message patterns, but do not copy a competitor's exact wording. Adapt the underlying structure to your own market [L0378, 2019+].
- Pricing differs by market. Australian dollars look cheap to US buyers, so pricing and currency must be checked per country [L0995, 2023].
- When pricing in countries where the local currency is significantly smaller than USD (such as Malaysia at 4 MYR to 1 USD), do not automatically adjust price points by converting wholesale cost. Validate your intended prices against what other online stores in that country are selling for, and use market-based pricing [L0249, 2022].
- For an Italian-market test in Q4, classic, mid-priced jewelry was suggested because Italian buyers were said to prefer classic styles over expensive fashion. Treat this as a hypothesis and validate it with a small test budget before scaling [L1219, undated].

### Testing a proven product in a new market

- Do not reinvent the wheel. Take a product that sells well in one market and test it in a market where nobody is selling it in volume. Check the product in the new market before scaling [L0388, 2024+].
- Start by testing products that already show scaling ads in the USA or Australia, because a product that works there may also work in other countries. Adapt the store to the new market, including language and payment methods [L0361, 2024].
- Differentiating by market: take a product proven in the US and launch it in English-speaking countries such as Australia, New Zealand, the UK and Canada, or in European markets such as Germany, France, the Netherlands and Switzerland. Check in the Facebook Ad Library whether the product is scaling in the new market. Low competition there is the differentiator [L1158, 2023].
- Comfy case: a sleeved blanket, first sold in the US market, was adapted with minor product and design changes and launched in Australia and New Zealand, reaching over 300 million dollars in sales. The same concept was later launched in Switzerland under a different brand name by another seller, who knew the market well and sold well there [L1158, 2023].
- Question for research into a new product market: if a product is not selling in a new country, check whether competitors exist there, whether their store and ads are good, and whether the market accepts this product type. A lack of sales usually comes from missing trust, missing native-language content, or the market not yet knowing the product, which requires educating customers [L1158, 2023].
- Competitor products proven in one market can be tested in another by copying the product and store and changing the store currency (for example to euros or Australian dollars) and the language. Stores in weak condition with good ad counts still prove the product sells, so the offer and store can be improved [L1161, 2024+].
- Example Swiss test: an early dropshipping test in Switzerland with about 200 to 300 USD in ads produced roughly 3,000 CHF in sales. That low-spend result was the signal to test the product further [L0492, 2023+].
- Test the product's appeal on a shared identity rather than on local details. A product tied to a shared identity, such as an African heritage map shape, can reach a broad audience across many countries because buyers respond to the same feeling of connection [L0502, 2020+].
- Validate a product idea by testing it in the new market before scaling, and let the new market's hook rate and CTR decide whether it earns more store work [L1108, 2023].

### Supply chain, lead times and delivery

- A supplier with inventory in the country where you sell is necessary. Cross-continent shipping (for example Australia to Europe) makes costs too high and roughly doubles product prices when shipping is included [L0971, 2024].
- Products that are backordered for months should not get paid traffic until stock returns. Genuinely custom made-to-order products with a known build time (for example custom pool tables taking about two months) can still be advertised [L0834, 2021].
- Products with production or delivery times of about 10 weeks or more should get very limited ad spend, because customers rarely buy them without knowing the lead time, so the conversion rate tends to be very low [L0834, 2021].
- Fitness products suffered the most from port backlogs and supplier shortages after 2020. In the 2021 situation, the fitness industry was the niche to avoid for the time being, because stock was thin and a store could not reliably fill orders [L1090, 2021].
- A weak supply chain can make an otherwise promising niche unworkable, so check supplier responsiveness and parcel reliability before committing money [L1441, 2018].
- Seasonal products may be copied quickly, and shipping times from suppliers can delay launches. Plan the next seasonal product early [L0352, 2022].

### Older examples and previous-period results

- A product that worked in 2018 in the kids niche and was picked up for 2020 generated about 10k in first sales after its competitor data was used to build a better offer [L0895, 2021+].
- Products that take a practical approach, such as a tool-type home product, can be tested in these markets as shown in the earlier sections; treat them as examples, not guarantees [L1171, 2024+].

### Holiday and event procedures

**Valentine's Day campaign (procedure, as written):**

1. Build a separate store for the Valentine's Day campaign about one month before the holiday.
2. Prepare around five products with everything needed: creatives, offers and videos, so ads or organic posts can start immediately.
3. Target men and parents as well as women, since many buyers are not the female partner.
4. Consider products for couples, such as matching bracelets where each partner wears one part.
5. Check shipping times and start ads early enough that the product arrives before the date.
6. Find the winning product at least about 15 days before the holiday [L1469, 2023+].

**Q4 product research procedure (as written):**

1. Find products that are trending in Q4 (gifts, winter clothing, personalized jewelry).
2. Look for similar designs on AliExpress.
3. Choose designs that look decent and test them in ads.
4. Repeat in Q1 with fitness products [L1220, undated].
