### Common mistakes

- Testing your entire niche list at once [L0050, undated].
- Treating a direct-to-consumer site as proof of the dropshipping model [L0050, undated].
- Confusing a brand's footer logo with a multi-brand range [L0459, 2020-26].
- Counting a single product listing as a dropshipper without verifying the brand [L0459, 2020-26].
- Assuming a store is a dropshipper from its name or a single listing [L0459, 2020-26].
- Opening every search result or every seller in Google Shopping, which adds clutter and wastes time [L0460, 2020-26].
- Treating one online store as proof that a niche is dropship-friendly [L0460, 2020-26].
- Using a competitor's global traffic rank instead of its country rank [L0687, 2020-26].
- Spending time on the rest of the SimilarWeb page at this stage [L0891, 2020-26].
- Excluding a niche because of an Alexa tool glitch without testing other large sites first [L0961, 2020].
- Keeping a default myshopify.com store as a real competitor without checking its traffic rank [L0806, 2024].
- Relying on a store that ships directly from China: long delivery times, inconsistent quality, and a famine-and-feast revenue pattern [L0554, 2019].

### Decision: pass, fail, or loser list

- Mark the niche "Yes" in the DS Friendly column when online-only sellers are found. If only sellers with a physical location (warehouse, showroom or retail store) are found, mark it "No" and move the niche to the loser section [L0460, 2020-26].
- Niches marked "No" move to the bottom of the list [L0460, 2020-26].
- A niche passes when it has at least one online-only, multi-brand competitor with no physical stores or warehouses, and at least 20 suppliers [L0459, 2020-26].
- A niche passes the traffic check when at least one future competitor has a country traffic rank between 1 and 100,000. Otherwise, reject it or keep working the list [L0051, undated; L0687, 2020-26].
- A niche that passes the worksheet test: update the worksheet with "Yes" or "No" in the DS Friendly column and the estimated supplier count [L0050, undated].
- A niche with a pattern of a few online-only sellers that carry many brands, confirmed by the About Us or footer pages, stays in play [L0459, 2020-26].
- A niche with a few dropshipping competitors can be a first store if it passes all criteria [L0359, 2020].
- A niche that still stocks its own products can be a second store, after the first produces steady cash flow [L0356, 2019].
- A niche with weak competitor rankings is not automatically abandoned if other criteria pass [L0664, 2020].
- If a niche has no dedicated niche stores but has 20 suppliers and at least 100 products, it is not a reason to reject it [L0960, 2019].
- A niche in which all top competitors are broad physical retailers, with no strong sub-niche sites, is a warning sign [L0358, 2020].

### Identifying Online-Only Multi-Brand Sellers

- A future competitor is an online store that sells to end customers in the niche. Suppliers are not future competitors and are not tested on traffic rank. [L1097, 2025]
- Build the candidate list from Google results, not from ads:
  1. Search your niche on Google.
  2. Look only at the top organic results, not sponsored ads.
  3. Check each site's traffic rank. The highest-ranked and most niche-specific sites are your top future competitors. [L1279, 2021]
- Online-only stores that sell a broader category can still count, if their exact sub-niche holds at least 20 brands and more than 100 products within it. [L1094, 2023] (2023)
- Competitors with a traffic rank under about one million are the most useful to study for suppliers, conversion approaches and promotions. Stores on pages three and four of search are less useful sources. [L1283, 2023] (2023)
- When you research a brand, check that the websites that carry it are online-only retailers. This shows the brand already works with online-only stores. [L1277, 2020]
- One established store that dropships and carries about 25 brands is enough evidence that a niche is dropship-friendly. Its position in the search results does not matter. [L1275, 2019]
- Before you build on a niche, check the supplier list for online-only stores that already sell the products. If none exist, the supplier may refuse to work with a store that has no physical location, or may offer better prices to physical stores. Check this risk first. [L1114, 2023] (2023)
- Industry knowledge does not replace the dropship test. Products that fail the test should not be used because you know the industry. [L1114, 2023] (2023)

### Excluding Warehouses, Showrooms and Physical Stores

- Online-only competitors that hold warehouses or stock are not counted as future competitors. You cannot assume their suppliers are dropship-friendly. [L1279, 2021]
- Future competitors with a physical showroom, store or warehouse are not counted. Suppliers can still be extracted from the pure online dropship stores among them. [L1113, 2022]
- A future competitor should not have its own warehouse and should be based in your country of operation. A single showroom is acceptable. A nationwide chain of showrooms is a poor sign for a supplier. [L1281, 2022]
- In a niche where most brands sell through physical showrooms, the niche is usually not dropship-friendly, even if the showrooms are temporarily closed. Brands that lost retail sales may still approve new online sellers, so contacting them can be worth a try. [L1279, 2021]
- The units give no separate rule for DTC sites. Apply the end-customer test and the warehouse and showroom tests above to them. [L1097, 2025; L1279, 2021]

### Verifying Addresses

- A supplier or competitor site that shows no showroom, retail store or warehouse address is treated as dropship-friendly enough to source from. [L1113, 2022]
- To check an unclear address:
  1. Paste the address into Google Search.
  2. Open the Maps result.
  3. Zoom in and check Street View.
  4. Decide whether it is a real building, such as a warehouse or office, or a virtual address, such as a shipping-store mailbox or PO box.

  If no address is shown at all, the site can still be used for supplier sourcing. [L1113, 2022]
- Google Maps and Street View show whether a competitor or supplier has a warehouse or showroom. An office without an attached warehouse still counts as an online-only business. [L1279, 2021]

### Traffic-Rank Tools and Procedures

**Tool status**

- SimilarWeb is now used for traffic data and replaced the Alexa rank. The research tool site still shows the same traffic data through its first tool in the toolbar. [L1092, 2022]
- SimilarWeb's free market research allows 15 free searches per week. The free version does not require an email address. It shows traffic data for competitor and niche analysis. [L1083, 2024+]
- Do not start a SimilarWeb free trial to get market data. Some users reported that the signup flow asked them to link an Amazon seller account. Use the free search allowance instead, which gives up to 15 searches per week without an email address. [L1083, 2024+]

**Key figures**

- In market research, use the Global Rank and Country Rank from the extension to compare competitor websites. Use the extension to find these two ranks, not to read every data point. [L1332, 2020-26]

**Extension procedure**

1. Open the target website in Chrome.
2. Click the SimilarWeb icon in the toolbar.
3. Accept the terms the extension asks for. Its panel opens on the right side of the page.
4. Scroll down the right-hand panel to about halfway, to the ranking section.
5. Read the Global Rank and the Country Rank. The other traffic data is further down the panel. [L1332, 2020-26]

**Country-rank test for a future competitor**

1. Confirm the site is a store selling to end customers in the niche, not a supplier.
2. Open the competitor's website and click the SimilarWeb Chrome extension.
3. Open the ranking section and read the Country rank for your target country.
4. Count the competitor as a qualifying future competitor if its country rank is 1,000,000 or lower. [L1097, 2025]

**Worked example**

| Future competitor | Country traffic rank | Under 1,000,000 |
|---|---|---|
| Competitor 1 | 50,000 | Yes |
| Competitor 2 | 250,000 | Yes |
| Competitor 3 | 4,000,000 | No |
| Competitor 4 | 30,000 | Yes |
| Competitor 5 | 2,000,000 | No |

All five count as future competitors. Three qualify, which meets the requirement of at least one under 1,000,000. [L1097, 2025]

**Setting and caveats**

- When you check a competitor's traffic rank in the keyword tool, enter the full URL, including https:// and www. If every competitor shows zero, the URL is probably incomplete. Test the tool with a known large site, such as wayfair.com, to confirm it works. [L1279, 2021]
- A product with very few competitors and an unusually high-traffic sample does not necessarily have low traffic only because of low ad investment. Low traffic can also come from weak SEO or from too few people searching for the product. [L1115, 2024] (2024)

### Traffic Thresholds and Fallbacks

**Alexa-era rules**

- For a valid niche, the Alexa ranks of the leading competitor sites should fall between 1 and 100,000. Ranks far above that signal too little traffic to support a store. [L1111, 2020]
- Example Alexa ranks of about 4,000, 35,500, 42,000, 55,000 and 94,000 were judged acceptable under the 100,000 threshold. A rank close to 100,000 still passes. [L1111, 2020]
- A competitor without an Alexa US ranking has traffic below the threshold at which Alexa displays a rank, so its traffic is smaller than that of ranked sites. [L1069, 2019]

**Threshold table**

| Source | Year | Threshold | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| [L1111, 2020] | 2020, older | Alexa rank 1 to 100,000 | Leading competitor sites in a niche |
| [L1279, 2021] | 2021, older | Top 100,000 | Whether a competitor is a real future competitor |
| [L1080, 2020-26] | Undated | At least one future competitor below 100,000 | Niche check |
| [L1287, 2025] | 2025 | At least two to three future competitors in the top 100,000 on SimilarWeb | Niche submission |
| [L1097, 2025] | 2025 | At least one future competitor at 1,000,000 or better in the target country | Future competitor test |
| [L1115, 2024] | 2024 | Benchmark sites between the top 1 and 1 million | Benchmark selection |
| [L1283, 2023] | 2023 | Under about one million | Most useful competitors to study |

- The sources give two levels: 100,000 and 1,000,000. The units do not reconcile them. Our reading: 1,000,000 is the minimum for a single competitor in the country rank test (2025), and 100,000 is the stricter level for the niche's listed competitors. Where both apply, meet the stricter figure. [L1097, 2025; L1287, 2025; L1080, 2020-26; L1279, 2021]

**When the traffic is too low**

- If competitor sites in a niche have too little traffic to measure, widen the niche one level and check again. Example order: brown leather chairs, then leather chairs, then chairs. [L1111, 2020]
- If niche competitors show zero in a small market such as the UK, look at a related, broader category where the top sites have measurable rankings. [L1277, 2020]
- A competitor with a zero US Alexa rank but a high global rank can still be studied. Prefer competitors with a higher US rank as the benchmark, because they show that demand is active. [L1111, 2020]
- Competitors with a zero US rank and a high global rank are weaker benchmarks, because their traffic does not come from the target market. Use them for ideas, not for volume estimates. [L1111, 2020]
- Use the highest-ranking organic competitor sites in your niche as benchmarks. Check their traffic rank in a tool such as SimilarWeb, and aim for sites ranked roughly between the top 1 and 1 million. [L1115, 2024] (2024)

### Competitor Counts and Limits

- A niche should show at least two to three future competitors ranked within the top 100,000 on SimilarWeb. These should be online-only brands without showrooms. [L1287, 2025] (2025)
- Submit the niche with its listed future competitors for manual verification. The reviewer checks that they really compete with you. [L1287, 2025] (2025)
- At least one future competitor must have a country traffic rank of 1,000,000 or better in the country where you plan to sell. The other future competitors can rank lower and still count. [L1097, 2025] (2025)
- A store in a broader category counts as a competitor if its exact sub-niche holds at least 20 brands and more than 100 products. [L1094, 2023] (2023)
- One established dropshipping store with about 25 brands is enough to confirm a niche is dropship-friendly. [L1275, 2019]

### Filling the Future Competitors Sheet

**Working order**

1. Search the niche on Google and take the top organic results. [L1279, 2021]
2. Confirm each site sells to end customers and is online-only. Exclude suppliers. [L1097, 2025; L1279, 2021; L1113, 2022]
3. Check the address and Street View for warehouses and showrooms. Exclude competitors with a warehouse, and exclude competitors with more than a single showroom in the country. [L1113, 2022; L1279, 2021; L1281, 2022]
4. Get the Country Rank for your target country and record it. [L1097, 2025; L1332, 2020-26]
5. Check the count and thresholds for the niche. [L1287, 2025; L1097, 2025]
6. Record each site in the sheet. [L1159, 2023+]

**Sheet columns**

- Store each competitor's website in the WEBSITE column. [L1159, 2023+]
- Label each competitor as BRANDED or DROPSHIPPING. Use the label to judge whether it is a real brand or a dropshipping store. [L1159, 2023+]
- Rate each competitor GOOD, OK or BAD. [L1159, 2023+]

#### Further details

- Competitors that sell with physical locations are not reliable signs that brands work with online retailers; only online-only competitors show drop-ship-friendly brands. [L0969, 2022]

- To check whether a product can be drop shipped, do the following. 1. Find competitors who sell it online. 2. If none exist, broaden the search one level. 3. Check large multi-brand retailers such as Wayfair to see whether many brands make the product. [L1068, 2019]

- A niche can be drop-ship friendly even when the sites selling it also carry other niches or product types. A niche-specific store is not required if the supplier and product thresholds are met. [L1068, 2019]
