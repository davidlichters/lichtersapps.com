## Competitor Intelligence and Offer Analysis

### Competitor research comes first

- Competitor research is required before conversion work. Without knowing the competition, you cannot position your business as unique, and blue ocean opportunities are often found through this analysis. [L0414, 2021-22]
- Study competitors thoroughly, including their claims, prices and offers, and collect everything published about the product category. Competitive intelligence should come before you set your positioning. [L1261, undated]
- Competitive weakness analysis reveals specific gaps in the market where you can differentiate and capture sales by delivering superior solutions. [L0040, 2020-26]
- Beyond initial research, analyse the top three competitors both as a consumer and as an e-commerce operator. Look for what they do not do, because each weakness can become your opportunity. [L1109, 2018]
- If a product is new to you, the market for it usually already exists and a competitor is selling to it. Competitor sites and social accounts show whether that demand is real and where the gaps are. [L0497, undated]
- Traffic already exists, so you do not need to invent it. Find where competitors' audiences already gather and redirect that attention toward your offer. Creating traffic from scratch is slower and costlier than borrowing existing traffic. [L0523, 2015+]
- Early product research method: look at the stores making the most sales in the chosen niche, list them, and compare them with each other to find trending products. Competition is acceptable if it shows demand and is not overwhelming. [L0999, 2023]
- Study brands in other verticals for inspiration. For example, a commoditised fragrance sold at about $40 next to a designer bottle at about $400 shows how a large price gap can be used as a marketing angle. [L1016, 2026]
- Find a product by looking at competitors on several platforms, not only TikTok. Check competitor websites and ads to see what they promote, how their sales message is framed and which channels they use. Where large competitors sell mainly through retail partners and do not advertise on Facebook, that gap can be a channel opportunity. [L1077, undated]

### Finding and selecting the competitor set

- Before building a store, find the product in a research tool such as Kalodata, then collect five to ten other stores that sell the same product. Treat these stores as the competitor set you study for offers, layout and content. [L0600, 2026]
- Choose the competitors to analyse from the top-ranked sites by traffic rank found during product research. Use the two or three highest-ranked sites for each niche. [L0894, 2020-26]
- Monitor the top two or three organic Google results for your niche and main supplier brands, since Google already treats them as authorities. Watch their pricing and product pages most closely. (2023) [L0804, 2023]
- For organic research, focus on Google organic results (the free listings below the sponsored carousel), not the sponsored carousel at the top. The carousel shows premium or featured products; organic results show the mainstream competition you will face in your price range. (2023) [L0251, 2023]
- To research a niche, review the websites of established competitors, including product pages, about pages, content pages and blogs, and watch relevant YouTube videos. This shows how they group products, their typical price points and which items are their best sellers. Use these findings to decide whether the niche is worth pursuing. [L1088, 2020]
- Competitor check for a niche: if most sellers are general stores rather than specialist brands, and few run ads or build a following, the niche is open for a focused brand. Check the Meta Ad Library, filtered to ads running for at least a few months, to see which ads have lasted and what they promote. Ads without a click-through or with no following are weak signals. [L0450, 2022+]
- Choose the number of competitors by purpose. For a store or product check, five to ten stores is the set (Kalodata workflow) [L0600, 2026]. For a Facebook or TikTok product, list five to six competitors (see the procedure below) [L0895, 2021+]. For brand-level research, list at least 10 to 15 direct competitors and 7 to 10 indirect alternative categories (see the procedures below) [L0613, 2026].
- Indirect competitors sell different products to the same demographic, and their websites can reveal new placements and ad formats you would never find otherwise. Analysing one profitable indirect competitor can open a new traffic source worth thousands per month. Include them in your research list. [L0523, 2015+]
- Competitor research should not take hours. Use a few tools for a short, focused check instead of spending five or six hours searching for competitors. [L0708, undated]
- Use sites that look professional and trustworthy as the design benchmark, since a buyer would trust a store that looks like that. Filter competitor stores by how credible they appear before using them as a source. [L0600, 2026]

**Procedure: quality check for competitor stores**

1. Open each competitor store found through Ad Library.
2. Judge whether it looks professional and loads quickly. If it looks cheap, scammy or slow, discard it.
3. Check whether it uses a shared dropshipping theme or template, such as Atlas, and note it as a dropshipping store.
4. Keep only the professional stores as design benchmarks for your own store.

[L1249, 2026]

- Do not copy stores that look cheap, unfinished or scammy. They are often supplier sites run by Chinese manufacturers rather than dropshipping competitors, and copying their look or claims can hurt your store's credibility. [L0600, 2026]
- A competitor with a weak-looking website is an opportunity, because a product can sell even on a poor store and a better store can outperform it. Analyse the store's offer and copy before deciding to test the product. [L1173, undated]
- A strong landing page is a positive sign for a product, because sellers invest in pages only for products that sell. A very poor landing page may still run, but it can also indicate weak competition or a seller who is not yet scaling. [L0638, 2023]

### Checking that competitors really sell

- Competitor sales cannot be verified. Estimate them from traffic rank and the countries the traffic comes from, not from reviews, which can be fake. [L0963, 2022]
- Sales data from a tracker outweighs ad activity. A competitor with many active ads who makes no sales is not a valid signal to copy. [L1177, 2023]
- Do not conclude a product is a winner from the store's surface sales or a single claimed total. Hidden products and sales on other products may sit behind a store's listing, and the public product list can be misleading. [L1385, undated]
- A store may show only one product on its public pages while other winning products exist and can be reached only by editing the URL. When researching a store, check for product URLs that do not appear in the navigation. [L1385, undated; L0372, 2022]
- Some competitor stores hide their product URLs so visitors cannot browse the full catalogue. Competitors may also run several accounts linked to the same domain, so check related accounts before judging a store's traction. [L0372, 2022]
- A large order count for a competitor product may be carried mainly by paid ads, so check whether it is advertised heavily before treating its demand as organic. [L1186, 2022]
- An older, established store with a consistent brand and several months of history often indicates a store that has been reused for ongoing sales. A new account launching a long-running store is less likely to show that stability. [L1385, undated]
- Check how old a store is against its traffic. A store launched about one year earlier with around 2.6 million visitors signals strong marketing and copywriting skill. Use the monthly traffic graph to see whether visits grow month over month. [L1164, 2026]
- A store that has run for four years and generated about 2.7 million in revenue within 27 months shows that its products really sell. Copying the same product or a very similar one from such a store is a faster path than testing from scratch. [L0549, undated]
- A store that began as a dropshipping store and now invests in its own branding for a product can signal that the product is worth testing. Such a shift suggests the product has shown enough demand to justify building a brand around it. [L0708, undated]
- Watch how long a competitor has been posting and whether it is still uploading. Continuous posting over a long period is a sign that the product is still selling. [L0657, undated]
- When monitoring a competitor's TikTok account, a pause of about 24 hours in posting is not a warning sign. Concern becomes justified only when the account has gone silent for several days. [L1429, 2022]
- Check whether a competitor is still active before entering a product. [L1444, 2022]
  1. Open the store link from the competitor's TikTok or Instagram profile.
  2. If the store link is gone, the competitor has most likely stopped the product, which is a sign the product may be dead.
  3. If the competitor still uploads and keeps the store active, the product is more likely still viable.
- Pick brands with a clear following rather than brands with few posts. A brand with a following of about 78,900 Instagram followers in a hair-removal category is worth studying, because its comments and posts show real audience language. [L0450, 2022+]
- A competitor's product description reflects how the seller sees its customer base, but testimonials can be fabricated. Do not treat either as proof of real demand. Verify demand through social accounts and genuine customer comments. [L0450, 2022+]
- Competitor pages showing out-of-stock products are not automatically a bad sign. Stores often keep discontinued or backordered items listed without running ads, to catch organic search traffic. [L1277, 2020]
- Do not compare prices for a competitor whose price is shown as N/A, because the price was not found. Re-check the store before entering a price into the comparison. [L1159, 2023+]

### Store tracking and traffic tools

**Tool conflict: can store sales still be tracked?**

- Store-tracking tools were described in one lesson as no longer possible because Shopify restricts them. That is a 2023 statement; check current platform rules before relying on any tracking method. [L1219, undated]
- Other lessons describe tracking tools as working: Sales Tracker, Shop Hunter and Dropship.io are named as ways to verify competitor sales (see the tool list below) [L1382, undated]. The Peekster tracker is described as working, though a 2023-era approach is said to work less well now [L1177, 2023; L0892, 2026].
- Use the older or unverified method only as a rough signal, and confirm with a second source before deciding. [L1382, undated; L1219, undated]

**Tools and their use**

- Trackers (Peekster, Shop Hunter, PP Spy, Dropship.io, PeakStar store tracking) estimate sales for a store to confirm that an ad is producing revenue. [L1382, undated]
- Peekster sales tracker: paste the competitor's store link to see estimated daily and monthly revenue. Tracker accuracy is typically around 50 to 70 percent. Stores that are not on Shopify may return no data. Shop Hunter is named as another tracker, and Ad Library tools are considered accurate. [L1177, 2023]
- Example of a successful store found through sales tracking: about $5,000 daily income and roughly $200,000 monthly revenue. The lesson treats sales as the most important signal, because ad activity counts for nothing without sales. [L1177, 2023]
- SimilarWeb is used to look up the traffic rank of a competitor store. Its free access is limited to a short trial (7 days), then it is paid. Alexa rankings no longer exist. (2024) [L0806, 2024]
- A competitor's traffic score is a quick way to judge how popular a store is in its niche. Use it together with your own reading of what the store does well or poorly. [L0853, 2021]
- BuiltWith shows which platform and which apps a competitor store uses. Enter the competitor's website, click Look up, and read the results for platform and apps. (2024) [L0806, 2024]
- The Meta Ad Library shows a launch date for each ad, so you can see how long a competitor has been running it. Use this to judge which competitor stores are stable enough to learn from. [L0600, 2026]
- Explore Shops store explorer filters: Monthly revenue, Avg. product price, Monthly Visits, Based in, Product Count, Shopify Theme, Creation date, Language, Visitor Country, Currency, Traffic Growth and Trustpilot. Quick filters are All Shops, Highest Revenue Shops, Whale, Recent Scalers and Brands. [L0892, 2026]
- Use the one-month Traffic Growth filter in the store explorer to find Shopify shops whose traffic rose by 30 percent or more in the last month. Such shops are a starting point for finding rising competitors. [L0892, 2026]
- The Store Tracker takes the URL of any store and shows its estimated sales. The estimate is produced by an AI model fed with traffic and several data points, not by a revenue-per-visitor calculation. The lesson says the older Shop Hunter approach works less well now. [L0892, 2026]
- The Sales Tracker details page shows store creation date, number of other users tracking the store, connected ad libraries, pixels, socials, monthly traffic, traffic share by country, an estimated revenue range, similar shops and SimilarWeb global and country rank. [L0892, 2026]
- Example: a supplement store created in March 2024 had about 2.6 million monthly visits, up 48% month over month, 67 active ads and 49 products in 5 collections. Its estimated revenue was $11,745,000 to $17,617,000, and about 53% of its traffic came from the United States. [L0892, 2026]
- Milled.com is a free resource where you enter a competitor's name to find the marketing emails they send, which reveals the offers they run. [L1404, 2025-26]

**Procedure: order-number check for store sales**

1. Place a test order at a fixed time of day.
2. Place another order at the same time 24 hours later.
3. Compare the order numbers to estimate how many orders the store took in that day.

[L1219, undated]

**Procedure: count the store's domains and traffic **

- A brand running several subdomains or domains at once often sends ads to several stores to test which funnel converts best. Count the domains, their product pages and their product counts to see how wide the test is. [L1164, 2026]
- Check the traffic by country. [L1164, 2026]
- Check the Trustpilot site that matches the store's main market before comparing numbers. Trustpilot can redirect visitors to a regional site with different ratings and review counts. [L1164, 2026]

### Validating a product against competitors (step by step)

**Procedure: product validation workflow**

1. Find the product in the Meta Ads Library and open its brand in Brand Tracker to see personas, USPs and angles.
2. Open the store in Winning Hunter's Sales Tracker and check the launch date, monthly visits and estimated revenue bracket.
3. On Trustpilot, check the star rating, review count and AI review summary.
4. Filter the Trustpilot reviews to 1, 2 and 3 stars.
5. Copy all low-star reviews into a CSV file.
6. Give the CSV to an AI assistant such as Claude or ChatGPT and ask for a full gap analysis of the complaints.
7. Count the store's subdomains and domains.
8. Check the traffic by country.
9. Open the store's best sellers and every similar product page.
10. Check the pricing and offer tests (bundles, free items, price changes).
11. List the apps the store runs.
12. List the installed pixels and tracking tools.
13. Open the top ads sorted by ad rank.
14. Review the similar stores and competing products.
15. Open the brand's ad copies in Brand Tracker and study its offers, proof and urgency.

[L1164, 2026]

- Open every best seller and every similar product, because a brand's best sellers show which offers and angles already sell well. [L1164, 2026]
- Check the Similar stores and similar products sections to build a list of competitor brands to analyse next. [L1164, 2026]
- High traffic at an established competitor shows that demand exists, so it is not an entry barrier. You can still enter by offering a clearly better formulation, improved packaging or a new design. [L1164, 2026]

**Procedure: competitor check before launching a product**

1. Search the product keyword in Google.
2. Open the top competitor stores that appear.
3. Note their bonuses, guarantee length, bundles and free add-ons.
4. Choose a bonus or angle that none of them offers.

[L0577, 2023]

**Procedure: early and alternative competitor checks with spy tools**

1. Remove the spy filters in the tool you used for product research.
2. Add 5 to 10 keywords related to the product, such as the product name or broader terms.
3. Run the same keywords in spy tools such as Peak Star and Droppy Spy.
4. Search the same keywords in the Facebook Ad Library.
5. Count the competitors, their engagement, and look at their landing pages before deciding.

[L0708, undated]

**Procedure: competitor check without spy tools (2024)**

1. Browse their product pages and categories like a normal customer.
2. Watch their remarketing ads on Facebook and on sites showing Google display ads.
3. Join their email list with a personal email address to see email frequency and offers.
4. Search your product on Google to see their shopping results.

[L0471, 2024]

- Before listing a product, check the price competitors charge for it on their product page. If the price looks fair and the competitor's description is complete, use it as the price reference for your own listing. [L1171, 2024+]
- If a competitor appears to be selling in the same niche, that overlap is normal. Copying their content is not allowed. Enter the niche only with a unique value proposition and a community code of ethics in mind. (2024) [L0806, 2024]
- Competing with other members in the same niche is usually fine. The real problem is copying another store, so build a unique version of the store instead. [L0968, 2021]

### Competitor ads, creatives and landing pages

- Study competitors' ads, creatives and landing pages to see which pain points and angles they use. Check whether their landing page matches the message of their ad. [L1194, undated]
- Ripping ads without understanding the messaging behind them burns budget on someone else's creative. Understand your ICP and USP before testing. [L1195, 2026]
- Copying a winning competitor's store, product, offer and creative does not guarantee success. The competitor may have seasoned their pixel earlier, caught the trend wave earlier, or have a more mature pixel with better data. Copying is a good starting point for beginners but not a guaranteed success path. [L0067, 2023]
- If copying a competitor's approach gets zero sales:
  1. Review their targeting strategy.
  2. Consider that they may have a first-mover advantage or a more mature pixel.
  3. Try a different marketing angle or audience segment.
  4. Target a slightly different audience with the same product, but a different context or problem emphasis.

  [L0067, 2023]
- Competitor stores often reuse the same layouts and offers, so the layouts themselves are not a unique advantage. Your differentiation should come from your own product angle, brand and customer feedback that appears once you have sales. [L0600, 2026]
- A competitor's proven store structure can be used as a base for a test, but it should be differentiated with your own angle, offer or market. Copying exactly the same page gives no advantage over the original seller. [L1185, 2023]
- Copying a store word for word, including its typos, is a sign that a competitor is cloning the same seller's store rather than building an original one. [L0656, undated]
- Ads that work for others: when you find competitor ads that other brands have tested, save screenshots, the targeting reason, the ad creative and the ad copy in a document. Testing proven ads is cheaper than writing and testing untested ones from scratch. [L1243, 2017]
- Influencer marketing: before committing to it in a niche, check whether competitors already use it. If no one does, that is an opportunity. If many do, the product itself must stand out. [L0790, 2017]
- Creative and copy research for a Facebook or TikTok product:
  1. List 5 to 6 competitors.
  2. Pick the 2 to 3 with the best landing pages and strongest engagement, which suggests they are scaling.
  3. Take paragraphs from each, mix them, add your own style, and build your own page.
  4. Ask a freelancer on Fiverr to recreate the winning competitor video or hook when a creative is used in 10 or more ads.

  [L0895, 2021+]
- Procedure for analysing a competitor's product:
  1. Open the competitor's video ads and landing page.
  2. Note the price, colours, offer structure (for example buy-one-get-one at 50% off) and product description.
  3. Add the product, store link and notes to the shared product sheet.

  [L1185, 2023]
- Competitor category menus often split a main category into sub-categories by product type. For example, surfboards split into hybrid, longboard, mid-length, pro series and shortboard. Filtering a competitor's category by product can reveal the same breakdown, giving a ready list of sub-collection names for your own store. [L1312, 2020-26]
- Find marketing ideas for a product on Amazon:
  1. Take the exact product name you want to sell.
  2. Search for that name on Amazon.
  3. Open the first result, which is often the same product even if it is sold under a private label.
  4. Study the order of its images and what each image shows, such as use cases, dimensions and benefits.
  5. Note how the listing presents its badges and benefits, then write your own version and do not copy the images.

  [L1346, 2020-26]
- Brand and product analysis, in order:
  1. State the specific desire or problem the product solves.
  2. Decide whether it is a superior product or uses identity marketing.
  3. For a superior product, document at least 3-5 specific mechanisms that make it better. For an identity product, document detailed identity markers.
  4. Describe how it positions itself against direct and indirect competitors.
  5. List the technical and emotional mechanisms that deliver results.
  6. Record the exact price relative to alternatives.
  7. Document the brand voice with at least 10 example phrases, and collect at least 20 key benefit and problem phrases.
  8. Note whether it is a new mechanism or a better version of an existing one, and how it claims to fix failures of previous solutions.
  9. Record visual identity elements, the awareness-to-purchase journey, and guarantees or risk reversal.

  [L0613, 2026]
- Competitor research, in order:
  1. List at least 10-15 direct competitors with their websites, and analyse their hooks and headlines (with quotes), ad visuals, positioning, longest-running ads (likely the most successful), pricing and value propositions, site structure and sales approach, trust signals, how claims are substantiated, and which emotional narratives they use or miss. Identify at least 7-10 categories of indirect alternatives, and for each analyse how it meets the same desire differently, its advantages and disadvantages against the product, its language, price points, whether its share is growing or shrinking, and its emotional connection to the audience.
  2. For both groups, document recurring ad creative approaches, channels, audience segments, common objections addressed, guarantee and risk-reversal tactics, how success stories are used, and emotional gaps that represent opportunities.
