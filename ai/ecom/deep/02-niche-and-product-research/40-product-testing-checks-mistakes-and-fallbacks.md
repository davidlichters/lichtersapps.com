- Relying on just one tool leads to missed products and an incomplete research list. [L0708, undated]

### Tool Overview and Limits

- **Meta Ad Library:** Shows the ads competitors currently run and whether each is still active. Check it just before launch, because a discontinued creative may mean poor results or an account issue [L1219, undated].
- **Meta Ad Library limits:** It shows page transparency details such as active ads and page creation date, but not likes, comments or engagement. Check engagement on the Facebook page or through a tool [L1177, 2023].
- **Tools and the Ad Library don't connect:** Peekster and similar tools cannot show the full Facebook Ad Library data because they scrape Facebook pages rather than the Ad Library. The two views cannot be linked directly [L1177, 2023].
- **Ad spy tools show ads, not sales:** Tools such as AfterLib show Facebook Ads Library data (ads running), not actual sales or revenue. Ad count is used as a proxy for profitability, because advertisers only keep scaling products that make money [L1185, 2023].
- **Store trackers are unreliable:** Store trackers gave unreliable data when tested on the coach's own stores. Check winning products through Ad Library ad counts instead [L1220, undated].
- **Peekster name variants:** The same tool appears as Peekster, Peeksta and PeakStar in the sources, and PP Ads as PPAds [L1177, 2023; L1358, undated; L1382, undated; L1436, undated].

### Finding Competitors Through Keyword Searches

#### Common mistake

- Pasting the full product name or title straight into the Ad Library search usually does not find competitors. Search terms should come from words the ad actually says [L1249, 2026].

#### Keyword method (Meta Ad Library)

1. Open a video ad for the product and listen closely to the spoken words [L1249, 2026].
2. Choose a specific keyword said in the ad, such as a problem or benefit term. Skip generic phrases, greetings and your own name [L1249, 2026].
3. Type the keyword into the Ad Library search and set Active status to "Active ads" so only currently running ads show [L1249, 2026].
4. Narrow results with the available filters, then open each store that appears and check whether it sells the same product [L1249, 2026].
5. Scroll down the results and keep finding more stores from the same ad [L1249, 2026].
6. Repeat steps 2 to 5 with keywords from other ads until you have a list of competitor stores [L1249, 2026].
7. Check the quality of each store before using its design, as described in the quality check procedure [L1249, 2026].

- **Keyword rules:** The Ad Library matches words spoken or shown in ads, so a keyword must really appear in the video. Pick words from the ad itself, not from the product description or your own assumptions [L1249, 2026].
- **Distinctive terms work best:** Keywords should be distinctive terms tied to the product's problem or benefit, not everyday speech. Ordinary words match many unrelated videos and create a noisy list, while a distinctive term returns a short list of stores running similar offers [L1249, 2026].
- **Example:** For a rice-based serum ad, the spoken phrase "nose pores" was typed into the Ad Library, which surfaced several other stores selling a similar product. Each store found this way was reviewed as a possible competitor [L1249, 2026].
- **Product name as a second search:** If the product name is said in the ad, searching the name directly is quicker than side keywords and usually returns more competitor ads. Use it as an efficient second search after the distinctive keyword search [L1249, 2026].
- **No unique keyword:** If a product has no unique spoken keyword, search its general product type name, such as the drink or supplement category, because the ad will almost always mention it. This works for simple, generic products such as an instant tea powder [L1249, 2026].
- **Keep searching:** One product can have many competitor stores. Keep searching with new keywords from different ads rather than stopping at the first matches [L1249, 2026].
- **Competition check:** If only two to three competitors already run the same product, skip it and move to the next one [L1195, 2026].

### Scaling Signals from Active Ad Counts

#### Thresholds by source

Sources define the counting unit differently. L1177 counts active ads for a single creative, while L1183 and L1316 count ads for a product or store. Use the per-creative count to judge one ad and the product total to judge a store overall.

| Source | Count | Reading | Applies to |
|---|---|---|---|
| [L1177, 2023] | 18 active ads on one creative | Roughly 18 ad sets, still testing; weak positive signal only | Per creative |
| [L1177, 2023] | 30 or more active ads on one creative | Real scaling; few sellers use 30 ad sets to test | Per creative |
| [L1183, undated] | 20 to 30+ active ads for the same product | Scaling and likely profitable | Per product |
| [L1183, undated] | 190+ active ads | Very strong scaling | Per product |
| [L1316, 2024+] | Hundreds of active ads | Probably profitable for competitors; worth testing | Per product or store |
| [L1316, 2024+] | More than about 1,000 active ads | Saturated in that market; skip unless entering a new market | Per product or store |

- **Use these counts as market responsiveness signals** [L1183, undated].
- **Under 30 is not scaling:** Require at least 30 active ads for a single creative before treating a competitor as scaling [L1177, 2023].

#### Percentage progression

- **Shortlisting threshold:** A product's ad count should show progression above 400%. Values around 370% or 3.4% are too low, while progression around 1,100% to 1,600% makes a strong candidate [L1185, 2023].
- **14-day growth:** Ad activity that has risen roughly 100 to 300 percent over the last 14 days is a strong signal of a product starting to take off. Track it with an ad library or ad spy tool [L1215, 2023+].
- **AfterLib progression column:** The key column is progression, meaning how many new ads a product or store has launched recently. A rising number of new ads signals a product gaining traction [L1220, undated].

#### Ad count growth over time

- A competitor's ad count can grow quickly, so the number of ads a store runs over time shows how hard it tests. In one example, a store went from 3 to 115 ads over the period shown in AfterLib [L1216, undated].
- A competitor running the same product in several countries at once while adding more ads over time is scaling. A product with a rising ad count across multiple markets is a stronger test candidate than one with a single ad [L1220, undated].
- A competitor's ad that has run for a month is proof that a product sells. Running the same product in other countries is fine, and you do not need to be the first seller [L1220, undated].

#### Inactive ads

- **Red flag:** If ads have stopped (inactive), treat the product as a red flag and do not test it [L1316, 2024+].
- **Past winners:** A product with many inactive ads may have scaled well in the past year, but inactive ads mean it is not being tested now. Flag such products for later review instead of treating them as current winners [L1185, 2023].

#### Seasonal caution

- Early in the year many merchants cut back on ad spend, so competitor ad counts can look lower than normal in January. Re-check a product's ad activity a couple of weeks later before deciding whether to test it [L1316, 2024+].

#### Single-image ads

- A competitor running only a single image ad for a product is often sourcing it from AliExpress. The same product, or a similar design, can then be sourced and tested with the same simple ad style [L1220, undated].

### Page Age

- **Preferred age:** Prefer competitor pages created less than four months ago, ideally four to six months. Older pages have a warmed-up pixel and a data advantage that makes them hard to beat with the same data [L1177, 2023].
- **Acceptable range:** Four to six months is acceptable, per the procedure in [L1177, 2023] [L1177, 2023].
- **Fresh entry:** For Facebook-based dropshipping research, check the page creation date in the Ad Library (About section). A page created within the last 4 to 5 months signals a fresh market entry with a level playing field and high potential. An older page, around 1.5 years, is a weaker signal [L1183, undated].
- **Older example (March 2022 page):** A competitor with a page created in March 2022 and more than one and a half years in the market had a warmed-up pixel. An identical product launched later was not a safe copy, even though it scaled with 31 active ads [L1177, 2023]. *(Older example, from 2022-era research.)*
- **Fast-scaling principle:** A recently created competitor page that already runs many active ads is a sign of fast scaling. This points to a positive market response and profitability (product-market fit). Entering the same product under those conditions gives a higher chance of reaching similar momentum [L1183, undated].

### Engagement Checks

- **Recent ads with engagement:** An ad launched within the last one to two months that already gets a good number of likes and comments signals that the market is engaging with the product [L1177, 2023].
- **Engagement comes from the page:** Because the Ad Library shows no likes or comments, check engagement on the Facebook page or through a tool [L1177, 2023].
- **Recent launches with few competitors:** Search PP Ads and similar tools for products launched in the last two weeks with strong engagement, and check how many competitors run ads on them. A new product with few competitor ads is easier to test than a saturated one [L1377, 2022+].
- **Weak stores can still be tested:** A product can be added to the list when its store has weak reviews or poor design, as long as ad activity is strong. The store can then be improved with better pages and ads [L1185, 2023].

### Competitor Store Tracking

- **Find the store's best seller:** To see which product a competing store is scaling, compare the active ad counts of all its products in the Facebook Ad Library. The product with the most active ads is usually the store's best seller, and it is the one worth researching first [L1185, 2023].
- **Use the video library:** Use the Ad Library's video library and filter to the exact product rather than relying on store trackers [L1220, undated].
- **Check sales with the store link:** Track the competitor's sales in Peekster by pasting the store link to confirm real revenue [L1177, 2023]. AfterLib does not show sales, only ad activity [L1185, 2023].
- **Store review checklist (AfterLib):**
  1. Open the product research tool and filter for currently trending products [L1220, undated].
  2. Check the progression, meaning how many new ads are being launched [L1220, undated].
  3. Note which countries the store is targeting [L1220, undated].
  4. Open the store in a new tab and compare prices across variants and styles [L1220, undated].
  5. Check the offers, for example "buy 2 for 10% off" or "buy 3 for 15% off," and whether a gift box option is included [L1220, undated].
  6. Save the product links for later testing [L1220, undated].

### Filtering Cadence and Shortlist Routine

#### Daily shortlist routine

1. Rank candidate products by their current number of active ads in the ad library [L1185, 2023].
2. After about 10 to 20 minutes, re-rank the same candidates by percentage change in ad count to see which are scaling fastest [L1185, 2023].
3. Build a shortlist of 5 to 10 products from both lists that pass the criteria for growth, niche fit and data validation [L1185, 2023].

#### Bulletproof method (three criteria)

Use Peekster and the Facebook Ad Library together [L1177, 2023]:

1. In Peekster, open the Facebook ad section and set the filters: country United States, sort by first seen date, minimum 300 likes, minimum 150 to 200 comments, English, suitable post type [L1177, 2023].
2. Pick ads launched within the last one to two months that show good likes and comments [L1177, 2023].
3. Open the advertiser's page and check the page transparency section to find the page name [L1177, 2023].
4. Open the Facebook Ad Library, search for the page under all ads, and count the active ads for the single creative [L1177, 2023].
5. Require 30 or more active ads for a single creative. Fewer suggests a testing phase; 30 or more suggests scaling [L1177, 2023].
6. Check the page creation date and prefer pages created less than four months ago. Four to six months is acceptable [L1177, 2023].
7. Track the competitor's sales in Peekster by pasting the store link to confirm real revenue [L1177, 2023].
8. Only test a product that meets all three criteria: scaling, new page, and sales [L1177, 2023].

#### Ad-count check before testing

1. Search the product or the store running it [L1316, 2024+].
2. Count the active ads [L1316, 2024+].
3. If ads have stopped (inactive), treat it as a red flag and do not test it [L1316, 2024+].
4. If hundreds of active ads run, the product is probably profitable for competitors and worth testing [L1316, 2024+].
5. If more than about 1,000 active ads run, treat the product as saturated in that market and skip it unless you enter a new market [L1316, 2024+].

- **Relying on store trackers:** Store trackers gave unreliable data when tested on the coach's own stores [L1220, undated].
