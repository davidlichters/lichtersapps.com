### Demand signals and proof of concept

- Products that already show proof of demand, such as steady sales on another marketplace or a store scaling them, are safer to test than completely unproven items. Taking a proven product into a new market or segment is a common strategy. [L0950, 2023]
- Prefer products with proof of concept: check whether competitors or other stores are scaling them, and use an ad spy tool to see what is trending before committing. [L0950, 2023]
- Trend research should look for products that already have an audience and are scaling, and should be combined with checking how saturated the product is before joining the trend. [L0950, 2023]
- If a product is already scaling on another marketplace or in another store, you can enter that market with an improved version, such as one with better features, provided the market is not overly saturated. [L0950, 2023]
- Start product research with products that are already selling, because a proven product gives more reliable evidence than an untested idea. The goal is to take a product that already works and improve its offer, store and ads. [L1172, 2023+]
- Look for products that already sell on the platform and in the market where you plan to run ads, because that is the proof of concept. Avoid being the first to test an untapped product, especially as a beginner. You do not need to reinvent the wheel. [L1174, undated]
- Pick products that already sell for competitors (market-proven) instead of testing untapped products with a wow factor, because the competitor has already paid for the market test. Untested products are risky because you do not know whether the market responds to them at all. [L1177, 2023]
- Evaluate a product on three data points together: how fast a competitor is scaling, how new the competitor is in the market, and how many sales the competitor makes right now. A product that passes all three is a strong candidate to test and scale. [L1177, 2023]
- Recently appearing products usually address a large market, often estimated at 50 to 100 million potential buyers, so a competitor with a few thousand likes has probably reached only a small share of it. [L1177, 2023]
- A product where a competitor has a fresh page, a scaling ad count and confirmed sales is low risk, because the market has already proven the sales. The seller should still improve the landing page, offer and ads to win against the competitor. [L1177, 2023]
- Products with no ad proof can still be tested by sourcing them from AliExpress or CJ, asking friends and family whether they would buy, and testing with image ads. This works best for items that need little explanation. [L1316, 2024+]
- An original product idea with no visible sellers is worth examining, but the lack of competitors can also mean weak demand or hard sourcing. Before committing, confirm that buyers actually search for and pay for the product, and that suppliers will sell it to a dropshipper. [L0905, 2021-22]
- When a product is brand new with only one or two players in the market, it can sell with minimal marketing and a decent landing page, because novelty gives an advantage. This is luck-dependent and does not replace proper research. [L1158, 2023]
- New and unique products convert better on landing pages because fewer competitors are selling them and audiences have not yet been saturated with ads. Prioritise products that have been on the market for less than 1 to 2 months. [L0321, undated]
- A product that is new to the market is a good candidate for a short test. Use the new-product window when the market is still uncrowded, and treat it as an early signal rather than proof. [L0321, undated; L1158, 2023]
- Both types should be checked for a real demand signal before testing, so a new product still needs the same demand check as an established one. [L0335, 2023]
- Look for demand in a market already validated for a similar product: a competitor that sells the same product, or a product that succeeds in one country before it is tested in another. [L0388, 2024+; L0950, 2023]
- Bottom-of-funnel ads work when a product category is universally understood (for example phone case, water bottle, posture belt) regardless of how many competitors exist. A niche with only two sellers can still support profitable bottom-of-funnel ads if the product needs no explanation. [L0316, 2026]
- Amazon bestsellers and beginners: for beginners, products already proven on Facebook ads are safer than Amazon bestsellers, because a product that sells on search may not sell on a social platform. Once experienced, testing Amazon bestsellers on Facebook can be an advantage. [L0693, 2024]
- Concerning how many marketing angles a product needs, a product needs at least 5 distinct marketing angles drawn from detailed positive Amazon reviews to be kept, and more angles are better. [L0636, undated]
- The first product-level check should be the product's problem, not its popularity: a product that is popular without a clear problem still needs a demand test. [L0280, undated; L0335, 2023]

**Conflict: new products versus proven products**

- Some sources favour new or unique products because few competitors and fresh audiences help conversion. Others favour products that already sell for competitors because the market has paid for the test. Use proven products as the default for beginners and new products as an early test when the market is still uncrowded. [L0321, undated; L1158, 2023; L1172, 2023+; L1174, undated; L1177, 2023]
- Being first to sell a product can be a long-term advantage, but only after you have gained experience and sales. [L1219, undated]

### Market size and audience

- Estimate the total addressable market (TAM) for each product by asking how many people actually suffer from the problem it solves. In the lesson, a market of roughly 50 to 100 million potential people was cited as a large enough example. [L0363, 2022+]
- A product must appeal to a large audience. A broad audience, such as several demographic groups plus seasonal gift buyers, makes a product easier to scale than a tiny niche. Broad appeal also creates more data for testing. [L0347, 2026]
- Products with a niche angle are preferred over broad products such as general jewelry. Broad products depend more on luck, and whether they work can only be judged by their performance. [L1176, 2023]
- Products that are not sold in many stores but show strong ad counts and a clear niche can be tested, since many niche products are still under-tested. [L1176, 2023]
- Personal taste is a poor filter for product choice. Judge by market evidence, not by whether you would buy the product yourself. [L0577, 2023]
- A generic product, such as a plain sports jersey, has weak demand unless buyers have a clear reason to own it, such as supporting a specific team. Before testing, define who would buy the product and why. [L0577, 2023]
- Before testing a product, check who would use it repeatedly and whether cheap substitutes already solve the same job, such as jars or simple bag clips. A product that replaces a cheap everyday habit faces strong price resistance. [L1186, 2022]
- Phone accessories such as cases are a broad category because almost every buyer owns a phone, so a simple single-product store in that category can perform well. [L1186, 2022]
- Bottom-of-funnel and niche claims are not enough on their own; test the audience with a small budget before scaling. [L0316, 2026; L0678, 2023]
- Influencer marketing works best with small, affordable, high-margin products that can be given away free to creators. For products that are large or expensive, paid creators or other channels are more practical. [L0790, 2017]
- Find new products by looking for B2B-adjacent opportunities, such as office lighting for a home chandelier seller or a commercial-grade version of a home kitchen tool. A separate sister store is usually recommended for B2B sales, but combining B2B and B2C products in one store has also worked well. [L1357, 2018]

### Markup, margin and price rules

- A product must be sellable at a minimum of 3x markup over total cost (product plus shipping). [L1174, undated]
- Electronics can sell at 4x to 5x or more because of their higher perceived value, but verify reviews before testing. [L1174, undated]
- Real deal breakers are poor quality and insufficient profit margin. [L0361, 2024]
- The break-even ROAS for a product should be 1.4 to 1.5 or lower. [L0692, 2023]
- Prefer low COGS (product plus shipping), which improves cash flow and lowers break-even ROAS. [L1174, undated]
- Products that are heavy or bulky are rejected, because shipping costs rise sharply and reduce margin. [L0692, 2023; L1171, 2024+; L1172, 2023+; L1174, undated]

- The one source price threshold is a filter for brainstorming, not a rule for final product selection. Test each idea for demand before committing. [L0474, 2020-26]
- A neoprene wine bottle holder had a retail price of about $10 to $20 and a manufacturing cost of about $1 to $2, giving very high margins. [L1152, 2021-22]

### Quality, reviews and ratings

- Require at least 4.2 stars on Amazon for a product, since this suggests acceptable quality and few refunds. [L0692, 2023]
- Avoid products whose Amazon reviews average under 4 stars. Poor reviews usually point to products that break after a short time, which damages customer trust and can increase the risk of bans. [L0693, 2024]
- The band between 4.0 and 4.2 stars is not defined in these sources. The 4.2 figure is the pre-test requirement, and under 4.0 is the avoid threshold. [L0692, 2023; L0693, 2024]
- If the identical product on Amazon has many ratings but a low average rating (for example 3.4 stars across 474 ratings), this suggests weak product quality. Rank such products lower on the list. [L1183, undated]
- Avoid products with poor AliExpress ratings or recurring customer complaints. [L1174, undated]
- Quality matters more than virality, because a viral video for a weak product brings little interest and few sales. Use going viral as a skill to be learned first, then choose only good products once you can reliably generate views. [L0372, 2022]
- Quality checks matter most for beauty and health products, because a poor-quality product can harm the buyer and damage a store's reputation. Quality problems should be treated as a red flag before any ad budget is spent. [L1382, undated]
- When judging a product, check its size, weight and price first, then think about the customer experience. Choose products where customers are lenient about delays and mistakes, because satisfied buyers return and recommend the store. [L0766, 2020-26]
- Images and videos matter most in visual niches such as fashion and jewelry, but they matter in all niches. Beginners with small budgets can repurpose existing competitor or YouTube videos instead of paying for new shoots. AI tools such as Canva can also create product images. [L1174, undated]
- Make sure high-quality images and 1 to 2 existing videos (Facebook video ads or YouTube videos) are available for repurposing. [L1174, undated]
- Check Facebook ad comments for positive sentiment, realistic names from Western countries and no repeated or copy-paste comments, and click profiles to see their location. [L1174, undated]
- Check Google Trends for the main keyword or niche to confirm it is trending or stable. [L1174, undated]
- A simple product can sell thousands of orders without looking special. A sheet of paper with 100 ideas for a first date sold thousands of orders in a single season, which shows that a product does not need to look special and must be tested to know if it wins. [L0780, 2024+]

**Case: a niche store reaches its first profit**

- A dropshipper with about six months of experience had started with a done-for-you agency. He had about five years of marketing experience, mostly B2B work with Google, Facebook and LinkedIn ads, and had tested over 20 products without a clear selection method. [L0549, undated]
- He pulled a list of 50 product candidates from TikTok, Facebook ads and spy tools, and filtered it with product criteria, which narrowed it to 2 products. He launched one of them, which became his most successful store so far. [L0549, undated]
- He first tested Canada, then launched in Australia and New Zealand, where he got his lowest cost per lead and his first profitable sales. Before that he had been breaking even with one to two sales per day, and one Friday he got five sales. [L0549, undated]

### Product types to avoid

**Avoid by material, size or shipping**

- Avoid glass products because of the high risk of breakage and returns. [L0692, 2023]
- Avoid heavy products, because shipping costs rise sharply and reduce the margin. [L0692, 2023]
- Avoid bulky products, because shipping costs eat the margin. Prefer small, light products that solve a clear problem. [L1171, 2024+]
- Avoid very large or bulky products when selecting candidates, because the supplier can charge high shipping costs that reduce margin. Products of this size were excluded from the list. [L1172, 2023+]
- Avoid liquids. [L1173, undated]

**Avoid by category or sales channel**

- Avoid electronics, because they break quickly and generate complaints and returns. Home decor is a better choice when it is light enough to ship cheaply. [L1171, 2024+]
- Electronics are an exception to the markup rule only if reviews confirm the product works. [L1174, undated]
- Avoid products that can easily be bought at local stores. [L1173, undated; L1174, undated; L0280, undated]
- Avoid products that look like established brands, since they are hard to differentiate. [L1173, undated]
- Avoid products with visible Chinese writing or third-party brand logos, such as a Samsung logo, in the ad video or product. Do not test such products even if their ad counts look strong. [L1161, 2024+]
- Avoid products linked to the Kardashians, or products from GoPure, MaryRuth, URO, Lemme, or any book from Global Brothers. [L1164, 2026]
- Avoid product categories such as shilajit, ashwagandha, perfumes, anything featured on Shark Tank, clothing and baby items when choosing products to test. [L1164, 2026]
- Avoid products tied to health risks, such as supplements or chemicals that touch the skin, unless certificates and proof exist. [L1174, undated]
- Avoid products linked to a single event. Prefer products that can be sold for one to three years or more, can be branded and can reach new audiences. Products tied only to one event, such as Valentine's Day, Father's Day or Mother's Day, have a short sales window and cannot be scaled much after the event. [L0335, 2023]
- Pump-and-dump products are driven by a trend, season, aesthetic or impulse buying, are easy to copy and are not meant to become long-term brands. They usually have no brand moat, and many sellers can enter quickly. [L0347, 2026]
- Pump-and-dump products that win are usually simple, common and already sold, so the edge comes from marketing rather than invention. Beginners should not expect the product itself to be unique. [L0347, 2026]
- Pump-and-dump products fit the emotional vibe of a season through colour, sound and visual style. Warm, soft, cozy and neutral tones suit fall. Products that look like they belong to the season sell by feeling, not by specification. [L0347, 2026]

**Avoid by harm, use or sizing**

- Products sold for use on the skin, such as foundation, are not recommended for a dropshipping store. [L1161, 2024+]
- Be careful with items applied to the face or ingested, such as cosmetics, because the buyer's trust and the product's safety are at stake. Only sell products you would use or consume yourself. [L1171, 2024+]
- Reject products where wrong use can harm the buyer, such as non-prescription eyewear sold as corrective glasses. Harm leads to complaints, refunds and legal risk that outweigh the sales potential. [L1173, undated]
- Customizable products are not recommended for beginners. [L1161, 2024+]
- Swimwear is not recommended because of sizing issues and ad restrictions. [L1184, 2023+]
- Avoid apparel that often causes sizing problems. [L1173, undated]
- For fashion products, prefer items sold in one size where possible, because they avoid most sizing problems and the returns that come with them. [L1161, 2024+]
- Pants and shoes caused fewer sizing problems than jackets and shirts. Products without sizing avoid sizing returns entirely. [L1128, 2023]
- A generic product, such as a plain sports jersey, has weak demand unless buyers have a clear reason to own it. [L0577, 2023]
- Products that are only unique or novel, such as simple toys and gadgets, are hard to scale and keep going long-term. [L0706, 2020+]
- Avoid products whose Amazon reviews average under 4 stars. [L0693, 2024]
- Avoid products with recurring complaints about breakage or battery issues. [L1174, undated]

**Avoid by buyer decision**

- Choose products that buyers can decide on quickly at first sight. First-time buyers rarely accept a complex or high-priced product, and such products are harder to validate with cold traffic. [L0661, undated]

**Conflict: fashion as a niche**

- For a brand, fashion and jewelry are the easiest niches to build around, since almost any product can be branded. Start from knowing the target audience and build the brand around the product. [L0492, 2023+]
- Fashion products were common among the trending items, and fashion was described as a good niche at the beginning of the year. Customizable fashion products are still not recommended for beginners. [L1161, 2024+]
- One operator reported that roughly 80% of his sales came from women's fashion, so this category can be prioritised when shortlisting products. Treat this as a single-source signal and confirm it with your own test results before scaling. [L1173, undated]
- Apparel is avoided where sizing causes returns, but one-size fashion items avoid most of that risk. [L1173, undated; L1161, 2024+]

### Product types to favour

- Jewelry and bags, such as shoulder or crossbody bags, are suggested for beginners, since they carry less sizing risk and can have strong margins when the perceived value is high. Jewelry sells well on Facebook. [L0693, 2024]
- Choose products based on data, quality and demand instead of limiting the options by category. A focused niche can be developed later, while a second store can use other niches. [L0693, 2024]
- Small, light products with a visible problem-solving use, a strong visual design, a fair price, and simple variants such as colour or plug type fit the favoured profile. Use these traits as a filter when shortlisting candidates. [L1172, 2023+]
- Home decor is a better choice when it is light enough to ship cheaply. [L1171, 2024+]
- Vacation-related products can work because demand for travel has grown after the lockdowns. [L1184, 2023+]
- Products that replace a cheap everyday habit face strong price resistance, so favour products that solve a need the buyer does not already cover cheaply. [L1186, 2022]
- Choose products you can make content about two to three times a day, because a product that is hard to film or present will not be uploaded consistently. Convenience of content creation is a selection criterion. [L0706, 2020+]
- An upgraded version of a winning product, or a product with several use cases, is a strong candidate. [L0788, undated; L0871, 2021+]
- Products in categories where the problem is ongoing and recurring, such as the knee brace, are favoured. [L0660, 2019+]
- Look for products where a problem is recognised and the audience already looks for a solution. [L0708, undated]

### Private label and white label

- Private label and white label products work well because they carry your own unique brand name and product identifier. Google will not associate your white-labelled products with competing brand names. [L0276, 2024]
- Start by selling a generic product and watch which products get traffic and sell best and what buyers like or dislike. Then private label a product that is the best of the best in the niche. [L0805, 2024]
- You do not have to sell every product a brand offers. You can choose which items to list from the catalogue, keeping only the products that fit your niche and margins. [L0965, 2024]
- When choosing a product to develop as a private-label item, the lesson sorts candidates into four types: accessory, smaller item, lead magnet or large product. Each type can serve as a fit for a private-label launch, so match the product type to your own capacity and goals. [L0346, 2021-22]
- Accessories that you already sell, or that you should sell but skipped because the margin looked too thin, are good candidates for private labelling. Review both groups. [L1152, 2021-22]
- Neoprene wine bottle holders protect bottles from knocking together during transport and help keep their temperature. Buyers of wine coolers, wine cellar equipment and other wine-industry products are a natural audience. [L1152, 2021-22]
- Pellet grill accessories form a large range of white-label and private-label candidates: utensils, pellets, cedar planks, remote thermometers, smoking racks, grill covers, slicing knives, marinades and rubs, meat shredding claws, and cast iron cookware. [L1152, 2021-22]

**Lead magnets and cross-sell**

- A lead magnet is a low-cost product that brings buyers into the store. Check whether its buyers already own or plan to buy a related main product, such as a wine cooler. Then remarket them with the main product or related items. [L1152, 2021-22]
- Neoprene wine bottle holders fit a lead-magnet role for buyers of wine coolers, wine cellar equipment or other wine-industry products. [L1152, 2021-22]
- Lead-magnet products can be sold on marketplaces such as Amazon or on Etsy, with custom sayings or designs, to reach customers in the same niche before they reach your own store. [L1152, 2021-22]
- To find upsell and cross-sell products, check the "frequently bought together" section on Amazon for the main product. Alternatively, open the AliExpress store that sells the original listing, because each listing belongs to a store that usually carries related products. [L0363, 2022+]
- Once a hero product is chosen, upsell and cross-sell products should be small and closely related to it. Complex unique products would need their own landing page, which adds friction. [L0363, 2022+]

**Case: travelling pellet grill**

- The store owner received white-label offers from a major pellet grill brand's manufacturer and wanted a version with clear differentiation. [L1155, 2021-22]
- Repeated customer feedback showed that portability was a real problem for pellet grill buyers. Only one grill on the market addressed it, and it sold strongly. [L1155, 2021-22]
- The owner bought two units to test them and found weaknesses: poor heat retention, clunky handles that doubled as legs, and a large size. [L1155, 2021-22]
- The proposed improvement was a smaller grill sized for a single brisket, with better heat insulation (for example, a fitted cover) and better handles. It was to be sourced from an American manufacturer and built out through manufacturer outreach, which was expected to be difficult but likely successful. [L1155, 2021-22]
