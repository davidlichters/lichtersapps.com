## Choosing a Business Model and Setting Research Goals

### Definitions: Niche, Product and Market

- A niche is an industry, product category or product type, such as outdoor lights or solar streetlights, not one specific product. Each category can be researched separately, and a first dropshipping store should be built around a niche [L0585, 2020-26; L0963, 2022].
- Market selection means deciding three linked things: the market you sell in, the customers you serve and the products you sell. These decisions shape the rest of the business [L1059, 2021-22].
- Market selection is important but is not the single factor that decides success in high-ticket dropshipping. Store setup, supplier relationships and marketing matter just as much [L1059, 2021-22].
- When using a broad category as the search term, you only need enough products within it that meet the price, shipping, supplier and competition needs, not every product in the category. A specific product type can be used instead [L1329, 2021-22].
- Focusing on a whole niche rather than a single product tends to produce more repeat business, because customers notice a brand that knows its category and product quality. Research the niche first, then build the brand around it [L1251, 2024+].
- Dropshipping lets you experiment with products and niches without large inventory commitments. Gather customer feedback, then pivot product offerings or focus on the niches showing the most potential, and optimize based on what customers actually want [L0007, 2023].
- Without holding inventory, a dropshipper can test any product available from supply sources such as AliExpress or Alibaba, and can test any niche, since no stock is tied to one product [L0760, undated].

### Why Niche Selection Comes First

- Niche selection is the foundation of a profitable dropshipping business and the first step in building a semi-automated store. Selecting the wrong niche causes business failure even when every other aspect of the operation is executed correctly [L0010, undated; L0591, 2020-26].
- Niche selection deserves the most careful work before other store setup, because a poor niche choice undermines everything built on top of it [L0585, 2020-26].
- Validate thoroughly before launch rather than rushing the decision [L0009, undated].
- Validate niche ideas on objective, measurable criteria rather than gut feeling or assumptions. This removes guesswork and increases the probability of long-term success [L0043, undated].
- Market research is the foundation for everything that follows. Skipping it or treating it lightly leads to stores built around weak niches that cannot succeed [L0043, undated].
- A niche that makes sales is not automatically a good choice. Launching without proper research can bring revenue while the business becomes unsustainable (mistake) [L0585, 2020-26].
- Testing removes weak niches before time is spent on them, so many failing niches are a good sign [L0451, 2020-26].
- A brainstormed list of niche ideas does not show which niches will work. Each idea has to pass a series of market research tests before it is worth pursuing [L1057, 2020-26].

### High-Ticket Products

- High-ticket is defined as a product with a single-unit price of about USD 150 to 200 or more. The example store sold low-ticket products up to about USD 80 to 90, mostly leather jackets [L0660, 2019+].
- High-ticket dropshipping focuses on higher-priced physical products, such as lighting, that fit the store model. These products were sourced and sold to customers directly, with no need to hold inventory [L1014, 2021-22].
- The research process for high-ticket dropshipping is specific because it aims to find a market with a high probability of success. Generic product research is not enough for this model [L1058, 2021-22].
- Higher-priced products around USD 2,000 are a good fit for a first store with many suppliers, because ads reveal the winners and fewer customers need to be contacted. Choose products that meet the criteria, not cheap items that cannot carry ad spend [L1281, 2022].
- High-ticket products such as custom luxury items at several thousand USD need more phone calls, follow-up and support before and after the sale. Enter this segment only if you are ready to provide that level of service [L1277, 2020].
- High-end or luxury products can still sell but need more work. They suit a seller willing to handle more customer contact, not one who wants a low-touch business [L0766, 2020-26].
- Between a high-demand niche and a high-price niche, both can work. High search volume gives more opportunity, while high-price niches with fewer orders often earn the most profit per order [L1275, 2019].

### Low-Ticket and Trend Products

- The author does not recommend low-ticket dropshipping of cheap products. If a store is built that way anyway, competitor listings can still be a source of marketing ideas, although this does not always work [L1346, 2020-26].
- Good pump-and-dump products usually have viral potential that works well on TikTok and Instagram Reels. They should be eye-catching or emotionally triggering, with a clear wow factor [L1211, 2026].
- Good pump-and-dump products are usually gadgety or gimmicky. Their appeal is the novelty of the item, not an established need or a lasting use case [L1211, 2026].
- Target a low to moderate ticket price, roughly USD 30 to 50. Lower prices make impulse purchases easier [L1211, 2026].
- Beginners should avoid supplement, beauty and pet niches. The hit rate is low, and large brands spend six or even seven figures on ads, which a small seller without large cash flow cannot outspend [L1175, 2026].

### Service Resale and Local Services

- When starting out, a dropshipping operator can resell a service they do not perform themselves, such as Invisalign. The operator runs ads for it and receives payment per lead or as a monthly fee, which multiplies income without building a separate product business [L0440, 2023+].
- Local service niches with high ticket value, such as dentists offering Invisalign or veneers, are attractive because a single converted patient can be worth about USD 3,000 to 5,000 [L0440, 2023+].

### B2B Niches

- B2B niches can be as profitable as B2C niches, and the store setup changes little between them. Do not reject a good B2B niche only for its audience [L0664, 2020].
- When targeting B2B niches, include a phone number on the site, because businesses typically require phone contact before purchasing. Also prepare for bulk orders and more intensive customer service demands [L0031, undated].

### Physical Products, Lead Value and Private Label

- Physical-product niches are not a fit for this business model unless they serve lead value optimization, such as promoting affiliate products to existing physical-product customers [L0835, 2023].
- Private labeling (own brand, produced by a current supplier) is a later stage for established stores with profits and data. It is more work, takes more time and often more money than dropshipping [L0971, 2024].
- Some niches and product types, such as jewelry, are hard to turn into a real brand. Decide early whether a product is a quick private-label flip or a product with long-term brand potential [L1469, 2023+].

### Service Resale and Effort per Revenue

- Niche and product selection should aim for products that need little effort for big results: little time on phone calls and email handling, and no need to list thousands of products. Building a business still takes real work, but the goal is to minimize effort per unit of revenue [L1518, 2020-26].
- Some industries should be avoided by default because they are too time- and resource-intensive for the money they return, not because no profit is possible. This is a judgment about upside relative to workload [L0766, 2020-26].
- Whether a niche is good depends on the seller's goals and the effort they want to put in [L0766, 2020-26].
- Niche choice is a judgment about upside relative to workload, so compare the profit of each niche with the amount of time and support it requires [L0585, 2020-26].

### Criteria for Choosing a Niche

- Select a niche with high sales volume, minimal customer service requirements and high profit per transaction [L0009, undated].
- Optimal niches support semi-automated operations with minimal post-sale customer support, which reduces operational overhead and workload [L0009, undated].
- Review each potential niche against a fixed set of criteria before entering it, comparing profit with the amount of time and support it requires [L0585, 2020-26].
- For every researched niche, identify the price points, calculate the profit margins and track search demand to confirm that enough people are actively looking for the product [L1057, 2020-26].
- Run a brand loyalty test and a dropship-friendly test on each niche before choosing it. Both filter out niches unsuited to a dropshipping store [L1057, 2020-26].
- A niche must be dropship friendly, have enough demand, have acceptable competition and be evergreen, meaning it sells year round [L0671, 2021].
- A niche must pass these criteria: dropship friendly, enough demand, acceptable competition and evergreen [L0671, 2021].

#### Six Yes/No Market Criteria

Run every candidate niche through six yes/no criteria. A niche that answers yes to all six is the strongest choice. The lesson's example with five yes answers and one maybe was still viable but weaker [L0458, 2021-22].

Validate each market idea by answering six questions. A viable market gets a yes on every question [L0771, 2021-22]:

1. Is the average product price right? The average should be 800 USD or more, with examples of 1,000 to 6,000 USD [L0771, 2021-22].
2. Can you identify local suppliers and brands? Aim for at least 10 potential brands or suppliers in total across the product types of the market [L0771, 2021-22].
3. Is the competition right? Count competitors per individual product, not in total; 5 to 15 per product is the target [L0771, 2021-22].
4. Is there recurring sales potential? Find other products or accessories the same customer can buy over time [L0771, 2021-22].
5. Are enough people searching? The main keyword should have at least 10,000 monthly searches in the US, or at least 5,000 in a smaller country [L0771, 2021-22].
6. Is the market an enthusiast market? Customers buy because they want to, not because they must [L0771, 2021-22].

An enthusiast market is one where people buy products because they want to, not because they have to. Answer it yes or no per idea. A minority of buyers needing the product for work does not disqualify a market if most buyers are hobbyists [L1329, 2021-22].

#### Four Checks Before Committing

Before committing to a niche:

1. Check the price point [L0671, 2021].
2. Check the number of available suppliers [L0671, 2021].
3. Check the competition on the first page of Google Shopping results [L0671, 2021].
4. Confirm the niche is dropship friendly, has enough demand, acceptable competition, and sells year round (evergreen) [L0671, 2021].

### Passion, Interest and Personal Fit

- Judge a niche by the quality of its opportunity, not by how much you personally enjoy it. The same amount of work produces very different results depending on whether the opportunity is strong or weak [L0924, 2020-26].
- Do the market research before committing to a niche, because research reveals opportunity quality while enthusiasm does not. A niche that looks attractive on passion alone may score poorly once researched [L0924, 2020-26].
- Choosing a niche to fund personal interests such as travel is a valid reason, as long as the niche still passes the opportunity research [L0924, 2020-26].
- If you are passionate about a product that meets all the niche criteria, choose it, because you will face a smaller learning curve [L0769, 2020-26].
- Shortlist ideas you feel at least some personal interest in, because you will talk to customers and work with suppliers in that market for years. You do not need to be fully passionate, but an idea you actively dislike should not be pursued [L1329, 2021-22].
- Choose a category you are genuinely interested in, because daily interest makes the extra work feel less like work. In a saturated market like clothing, the reality is harder than it appears, and paid advertising is needed to get noticed among many competitors [L1438, 2023+].
- Choose a clothing niche or style that you can connect with emotionally and already know well, because this makes the brand easier to build and talk about [L0779, 2023+].
- Stronger niches are built around strong identity or passion, such as sports clubs, music fan bases, religion, or pride in a home country or heritage. Buyers spend money to show belonging [L1131, 2018+].
- Passionate niches with a specific target audience tend to outperform random product designs, because buyers respond to designs that clearly speak to their identity or interest. Broad themes like religion or astrology can reach large audiences, but only if the design is specific and emotionally strong [L1184, 2023+].
- Competitive niches with strong brand-building potential include fashion, baby, jewellery, makeup, beauty, cosmetics, outdoors and gym. Choose a niche with these kinds of demand before picking individual products [L1251, 2024+].
- In the beginning, test multiple niches to find ones that are both interesting to the seller and profitable. Products that sell to women, such as fashion and jewelry, tended to work for this seller, while products for men did not [L0678, 2023].
- An existing business or hobby can give you a competitive advantage. If the niche meets the criteria, follow the same validation process and use what you already have, such as existing supplier relationships [L1002, 2019].

### Who Does the Research

- Niche selection and market research should be done by the business owner rather than delegated to a virtual assistant, even for low-capital online businesses. The owner must know the numbers and prove the business works before investing. The same logic applies to larger physical retail investments, such as a store with a USD 10,000 monthly lease and a USD 300,000 build-out [L0429, 2022].
- Choosing a niche is the foundation of the whole business, so do the research yourself even if it takes longer. Paying someone else to pick it is not recommended [L1002, 2019].
- Doing product research yourself rather than hiring freelancers is recommended. In the example, a freelancer who made video ads for the speaker produced outdated, low-quality work [L0690, 2022+].

### Research Time Limits

- A niche search for a product should take about three to four hours of brainstorming and research and should never run longer than one week. Beyond that point, the extra time adds delay without adding much information [L1083, 2024+].
- A niche should be selectable within a matter of hours when a structured process is followed. A niche search that runs for years indicates the process is being done without a clear method or time limit [L0555, 2026+].
- Do not spend months searching for the perfect niche. Once a niche meets all criteria, commit to it and build, then research more niches later [L1111, 2020].
- The goal of early research is to find one viable market quickly and keep momentum, not to do hundreds of hours of research. Enough research to find a viable idea that appeals to you is the target [L1329, 2021-22].

### How Many Ideas and Niches to Research

- Start niche research with a brainstormed list of at least 50 niche ideas before running any validation tests [L1057, 2020-26].
- Do not research every brainstormed idea in depth. Brainstorming only gets ideas onto paper, and researching 40 ideas in detail can take weeks and delay launching the business [L1329, 2021-22].
- Shortlisting and first market research with the tracker:
  1. Pick five ideas from the list that appeal most to you at face value [L1329, 2021-22].
  2. Research only these five ideas against the market criteria: enthusiast market (Y/N), average price, shipping (Y/N), and later suppliers, competition, recurring revenue and traffic volume [L1329, 2021-22].
  3. If one of the five meets the criteria, choose it and stop researching the others [L1329, 2021-22].
  4. If none of the five meet the criteria, select the next batch of three to five ideas and research them the same way until a viable idea is found [L1329, 2021-22]. [L1329, 2021-22]
- Testing several products over the budget gives a fair result, while quitting after a few small tests usually does not [L0690, 2022+].

### Research Sequence and Definition of the Business

- Choose the market in sequence [L1058, 2021-22]:
  1. Research products, customers and competition through a set of exercises [L1058, 2021-22].
  2. Identify candidate markets [L1058, 2021-22].
  3. Select one market and build the business around it. Do not start building the store before the selection is made [L1058, 2021-22].
- Define the business in this order before contacting suppliers [L1438, 2023+]:
  1. Decide which product type you want to release [L1438, 2023+].
  2. Define what is needed to make that product (materials, production requirements) [L1438, 2023+].
  3. Define the audience you will sell to [L1438, 2023+].

  Narrowing these decisions first avoids repeated back-and-forth with suppliers, which is costly and slow [L1438, 2023+].

### Test Budgets

- Commit to a total product-testing budget in advance, roughly USD 5,000 to 6,000 in the speaker example, before deciding the model does not work [L0690, 2022+].
- Do not spend much money on product research tools in the beginning. Use free or cheap methods and put the budget into running ads, since the winners often come from low-cost sources [L1184, 2023+].
- A niche often earns far less than first expected, for example USD 15,000 to 25,000 per month instead of USD 100,000. A profitable store at that level should still be kept running and used to fund the next store [L1086, 2019].

### Where Sources Disagree

- **High-ticket threshold.** one source defines high-ticket as a single unit of about USD 150 to 200 or more [L0660, 2019+]. one source's market test requires an average market price of USD 800 or more [L0771, 2021-22]. one source suggests around USD 2,000 for a first store [L1281, 2022]. The 150 to 200 figure applies to the product price, the 800 figure to the market average, and the 2,000 figure to a first-store recommendation [L0660, 2019+; L0771, 2021-22; L1281, 2022].
- **Low-ticket ceiling.** one source places low-ticket at up to about USD 80 to 90 [L0660, 2019+]. one source describes low-ticket as items of USD 20 to 100 [L0994, 2021-22]. Viral pump-and-dump products target USD 30 to 50 [L1211, 2026]. Use the range that matches the model you are testing.
- **Customer contact load.** Custom luxury items at several thousand USD need more calls and follow-up [L1277, 2020], and luxury products need more customer contact [L0766, 2020-26]. Products around USD 2,000 with many suppliers need fewer contacts because ads reveal the winners [L1281, 2022]. The difference depends on whether the product is custom or off the shelf and sold through ads.
- **Physical products.** one source states physical-product niches are not a fit for this business model unless they serve lead value optimization [L0835, 2023]. one source's high-ticket model uses higher-priced physical products that fit the store model [L1014, 2021-22]. Follow [L0835, 2023] for lead value work and [L1014, 2021-22] for high-ticket physical dropshipping. The sources do not reconcile these further.
- **Research depth.** Brainstorm at least 50 ideas [L1057, 2020-26], but do not research every idea in depth [L1329, 2021-22]. Brainstorm broadly and research narrowly.
- **Competition check.** one source counts 5 to 15 competitors per individual product [L0771, 2021-22]. one source checks the first page of Google Shopping results before committing [L0671, 2021]. Use both checks.
