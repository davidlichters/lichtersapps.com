## Setting Up the Research Worksheets and Trackers

### Where the worksheets live and how to set them up

- The Niche Research Worksheet is the central spreadsheet for niche selection. It is available as a Google Sheets template or as a downloadable Excel file, and it works in Microsoft Excel or Apple Numbers as well [L0591, 2020-26; L0010, undated].
- The worksheet is a free spreadsheet that stores the niche idea list. The Google Sheets version opens as a copy in your own account, is editable, and can be used from a phone or computer. The downloadable Excel version works with Microsoft Excel, Apple Numbers and other spreadsheet software [L1082, 2020-26].
- Google Drive is the preferred free option. Alternatively, download the template as an Excel file and open it locally in Microsoft Excel or Apple Numbers [L0591, 2020-26].
- To get an editable copy of the Niche Research Worksheet in Google Drive [L0591, 2020-26]:
  1. Open the Google Drive link of the template (named 'one source X research').
  2. Click 'File'.
  3. Click 'Make a copy'.
  4. Rename the copy as desired and keep it in your own Google Drive.
- Make one editable copy and keep using that same copy for the whole program. Each later module adds more columns and sheets to the same file, so it should not be recreated for each module [L0591, 2020-26].
- The niche research worksheet is a spreadsheet-style list of candidate niches that is revised as your understanding of prices, margins and shipping improves [L1179, 2020-26].
- The worksheet collects product ideas for the niche, with the product ideas listed in column A. It can be kept as a Google Drive file or downloaded as a local file [L0474, 2020-26].
- To set up the one source Product Research Sheet [L0038, 2026]:
  1. Open the Google Sheets template provided.
  2. Click File (top left).
  3. Click Make a Copy.
  4. Save it to your Google Drive.
  5. Begin adding your product research data.
- Use File > Make a copy (top left) to get your own editable version of the product research sheet, then add one row per product candidate [L0354, 2026].

- Keep one row per candidate niche so that all candidates can be compared side by side on the same criteria before you choose one [L0540, undated].

- The earlier data-source table uses the same columns with a different format. Column entries are listed below, with the Data Source and How to Fill columns [L0045, undated]:

| Column | Data Source | How to Fill | Entry Value |
|--------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Grade | one source Research Tool results | Copy the grade directly from tool | Tool's assigned grade |

| Average Profit | Calculate from Average Price | Formula: =Average Price * 0.25 | Calculated value (25% of price) |
| Demographic | one source Research Tool results | Enter "Pass" if box is green | "Pass" or blank |

- Average profit formula: =Average Price * 0.25. It calculates net profit assuming a 25% net profit margin on the average product price. For example, if the average price is $100, the calculated net profit is $25 per sale [L0045, undated].
- Average price format: the earlier layout copies the price and formats it as currency, with cents shown (e.g., $49.99) [L0045, undated]. The later layout formats it as currency and removes the decimals [L1081, 2020-26]. The value is the same in both; use one format per sheet.
- Demand is the average monthly search volume shown by the tool (example: 15000) [L0045, undated; L1081, 2020-26].
- Demographic, Brand Loyalty and Seasonality take "Pass" (or "pass") when the tool's box is green or, for seasonality, when it shows 4 out of 4 seasons. Otherwise leave the cell blank [L0045, undated; L1081, 2020-26].
- The Demographic column records the demographics test result for each niche, so you can track which niches pass and which need a pivot [L0457, 2020-26].
- Insert an optional Grade column: click the column B header, right-click, choose "Insert one column left", and name it "Grade." The column is not required but helps with comparison [L0045, undated; L1081, 2020-26].
- The columns depend on each other: Seasonality, Demand and Average Price must be filled in before the Average Profit column is calculated [L1150, 2020-26].
- Columns I (DS friendly) and J (estimated suppliers) stay empty while grading niches. They are filled in later, after columns B to H are complete [L1081, 2020-26].
- Fill in the Estimated Suppliers column with the number of suppliers counted in the niche's master supplier list (see the master supplier section) [L1079, 2020-26].
- Fill the DS Friendly column with Yes or No and the Estimated Suppliers column with a number [L0459, 2020-26].
- Complete one full research worksheet per niche, containing all collected data. The worksheet is the basis for choosing which niche to launch the first store with [L1057, 2020-26].
- Fill in every empty column of the niche research worksheet, together with the future competitors and master suppliers sheets, before choosing the first niche. The goal is to pick one niche for the first store based on complete competitor and supplier data [L0584, 2020-26].
- The niche research can be split into three sheets: one for niche research, one for future competitors, and one for master suppliers. Each sheet tracks a different layer of the research, from the market candidates to the competitors and the suppliers behind them [L0584, 2020-26].

### Phase 1: brainstorming with column A only

- In the first phase, populate only Column A on the 'Niche Research Worksheet' sheet to list niche candidates. Defer analysis of other columns, such as average price, profit, demographic and brand loyalty, until later phases, once you have refined your list [L0010, undated].
- In the first module, only column A of the first sheet ('niche research worksheet') is filled in. Other columns such as average price, profit, demographics and brand loyalty are covered in later modules, and the other sheets are not needed yet [L0591, 2020-26].
- During brainstorming, column A receives the raw idea list. The other columns hold research steps from the next module and can be ignored at this stage [L1082, 2020-26].
- Expand the list until it contains at least 10 candidate niches [L1179, 2020-26].
- Research all 50 or more brainstormed niches in the test round, filling in the demand, seasonality and average price columns for each, before continuing [L0451, 2020-26].

### Early price pass before data

Use this pass before the research tool data is complete. It is based on rough intuition, not on data [L1179, 2020-26].

1. Open the niche research worksheet.
2. For each niche, estimate its likely selling price using rough intuition.
3. Remove or deprioritize niches whose price is too low for bulky or heavy items.
4. Split broad niches into higher-priced subtypes.
5. Add new niches in higher-ticket categories, especially large or heavy products.
6. Flag niches with unclear pricing so they can be checked with real data later.
7. Keep expanding the list until it contains at least 10 candidate niches.

- When a $200 average-price filter is applied, a niche that falls below $200 is moved to the bottom of the list and no longer counts as a candidate. In one worksheet, tripods were the only niche under $200, and the other niches stayed on the candidate list [L1150, 2020-26].

### Getting the data from the research tool

- The one source research tool is a custom tool for one source members. It evaluates and validates niches using real data instead of assumptions [L0043, undated].
- The tool gives members unlimited searches. Enter a niche keyword and click search. It returns grade, average price, demographic, brand loyalty, seasonality and monthly demand data from linked data sources, which saves many hours of manual research [L1081, 2020-26].
- The research tool at the source's own site requires login. It replaces the earlier research tools by combining their functions in one place. Select the target country before searching (the example uses the US) [L1075, 2025+].
- Searches for niches that were not researched before can take 10 to 15 seconds. Previously researched niches usually return within a few seconds. The tool allows unlimited searches [L1075, 2025+].
- Workflow in the research tool [L1075, 2025+]:
  1. Log in to the research tool at the source's own site
  2. Select the target country.
  3. Enter the niche to research and click search.
  4. Read the demand test result (monthly search volume).
  5. Read the evergreen test box: green passes, red fails.
  6. Read the price analysis result.
  7. Refresh the page before starting a new search.
- Opening a niche's full data in the research tool shows the exact reasons it failed each test. This is optional and useful for understanding a weak niche, but it is not needed to choose the top 5 niches from the worksheet [L1081, 2020-26].
- The one source Research Software is a research tool for finding and evaluating drop shipping products [L1096, 2025].
- A niche screen can check several criteria for each candidate niche: demand, whether sales hold up across all four seasons, average price point, the target buyer demographic, and brand loyalty. Scoring every niche on the same checks makes it easier to compare a long list of candidates side by side [L1097, 2025].

Procedure for grading each niche in the worksheet [L0045, undated]:

1. Open your Niche Research Worksheet.
2. Optionally add a Grade column by clicking on Column B, right-clicking, selecting "Insert one column left," and naming it "Grade."
3. For each niche on your list, copy the niche name from your worksheet.
4. Paste the niche name into the one source Research Tool.
5. Click Search in the tool.
6. Review the tool's results for this niche.
7. Fill the Grade column with the grade shown in the research tool.
8. Fill the Average Price column with the average price shown by the tool and format it as currency.
9. Calculate Average Profit using the formula =Average Price * 0.25 to represent 25% net profit margin.
10. For the Demographic column, enter "Pass" if the box is green in the tool.
11. For the Brand Loyalty column, enter "Pass" if the box is green in the tool.
12. For the Seasonality column, enter "Pass" if the tool shows 4 out of 4 seasons.
13. For the Demand column, enter the average monthly search volume shown in the tool.
14. Repeat steps 3-13 for all niches on your list (typically takes 30 minutes or less).
15. Sort the Demand column from A to Z (lowest to highest).
16. Highlight all niches with under 10,000 monthly searches.
17. Change the text color of highlighted niches to red.
18. Sort the Seasonality column and mark all failures (fewer than 4 seasons) in red.
19. Sort the Average Profit column from Z to A (highest to lowest).
20. Move all low-profit or red-marked niches to the bottom of your list.
21. Focus your analysis on the black (non-red) entries at the top of your list.
22. From the top candidates, narrow your selection down to exactly five niches that meet all top niche criteria.
23. Highlight your top five niches in green for clarity.
24. Keep your top five candidates at the top of your worksheet.
25. Keep all eliminated niches on your sheet for reference when planning future stores.

- Compact version of the same grading work [L1081, 2020-26]:
  1. Copy the niche keyword from your list (for example Persian rugs).
  2. Paste it into the research tool and click search.
  3. Enter the letter grade the tool gives the niche (for example A or A-).
  4. Enter the average price from the tool, format the cell as currency, and remove the decimals.
  5. Enter the average profit using the 25% formula (average price x 0.25).
  6. Enter 'pass' for the demographic test if its box is green.
  7. Enter 'pass' for the brand loyalty test if its box is green.
  8. Enter 'pass' for the seasonality test if it shows 4 out of 4.
  9. Enter the average monthly searches for the keyword as the demand.
  10. Drag the profit formula down the column to fill the other niches.
- Time per batch: repeating the grading steps for all niches typically takes 30 minutes or less [L0045, undated]. The worksheet is completed in roughly 28 to 30 minutes per batch of niches, so plan 30 to 35 minutes for a full worksheet [L1081, 2020-26].

### Grading with traffic lights and colours

- Traffic light grading [L0490, 2021-22; older]:
  - Green means approved.
  - Red means rejected because of a strikeout. Red niches are removed from the active shortlist.
  - Orange or yellow means maybe, for borderline cases.
- Market research tracker colour coding [L0490, 2021-22; older]:
  - Green for approved.
  - Red for not approved (a line through the row also works).
  - Orange or yellow for maybe.
- one source's colour scheme for the worksheet [L0045, undated; L0459, 2020-26; L0451, 2020-26]:
  - Demand under 10,000 monthly searches: red text.
  - Seasonality failures (fewer than 4 seasons): red.
  - Average profit sort: highest at top, low-profit or red-marked niches at the bottom.
  - Top five niches: green.
  - Mark a niche green when it passes on DS Friendly or estimated suppliers, and red when it fails [L0459, 2020-26].
  - Failing niches are marked red text and moved to the bottom, not deleted [L0451, 2020-26].
- In the evergreen test of the research tool, a green box passes and a red box fails [L1075, 2025+].
- Black (non-red) entries at the top of the list are the focus of the analysis [L0045, undated].

### Top five, the shortlist and the rule for failed niches

- Narrow the top candidates to exactly five niches that meet all top niche criteria. Highlight them in green and keep them at the top of the worksheet [L0045, undated].
- Keep the red losers and their data on the sheet below the top 5 instead of deleting them. The data can be reused when researching a second, third or later store, so the research does not have to be repeated [L1081, 2020-26].
- Keep all eliminated or rejected niches on your worksheet rather than deleting them. These niches may be relevant for different business models or future store planning when market conditions change [L0045, undated].
- Keep all eliminated niches on your sheet for reference when planning future stores [L0045, undated].
- Keep passing niches (winners) at the top of the list and move failing niches (losers) to the bottom in red text instead of deleting them, so the record can be reused for future stores [L0451, 2020-26].
- Fill in the demand, seasonality and average price columns for failing niches as well as passing ones, so the worksheet keeps a complete record for future store research. Mark failing niches in red and move them to the bottom of the list rather than deleting them [L0451, 2020-26].
- Expected outcome of the test round: research all 50 or more brainstormed niches, fill in the demand, seasonality and average price columns for each, move losers to the bottom in red and keep winners on top before continuing [L0451, 2020-26].

### Future competitor sheets

- Create a separate competitor sheet for each niche that still passes all earlier tests [L0891, 2020-26]:
  1. Right-click the template sheet and click Duplicate.
  2. Click and hold the copy, then drag it to the left so it sits next to the template.
  3. Right-click the copy, click Rename, and name it '[niche name] future competitors', for example 'chandelier future competitors'.
  4. Repeat for every other niche that is still on the top of the list.
- Research future competitors in the worksheet by navigating to the tab labeled "Niche Future Competitors." For each niche you research, right-click this tab, click Duplicate, and rename the copy with your niche name (e.g., "Outdoor Kitchens, Future Competitors") [L0051, undated].
- The future competitors sheet is part of your Niche Sheet [L0061, 2024+]. To populate it [L0061, 2024+]:
  1. Open your Niche Sheet.
  2. Navigate to the [Niche] Future Competitors sheet.
  3. Populate with competitor URLs, store names, traffic ranks from SimilarWeb, and niches each competitor sells.
- Competitor sheet columns [L0051, undated; L0540, undated; L0542, undated]:

| Column | Purpose |
|---|---|
| URL / Store URL | Website address of the competitor store |
| Store Name | Name of the competitor website |
| Traffic Rank | Country-specific rank from SimilarWeb in your target country |
| Niches Sold | Other product categories the store sells in (informs future niche expansion and multi-store planning) |

- The future competitor tracking sheet records each store that sells in the chosen niche, with its traffic rank and the other niches it sells, to monitor who may enter the market [L0542, undated].
- The competitor table is the same as in the earlier version, with columns URL, Store Name, Traffic Rank and Niches Sold [L0540, undated].
- Column layout of the niche future competitors sheet. Every competitor needs all four columns [L0891, 2020-26]:

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| A | URL |
| B | Store Name |
| C | Traffic Rank |
| D | Niches Sold |

- Column C (Traffic Rank) is filled in only after the URLs and niches are listed [L0891, 2020-26].
- Take the competitor URLs from the store research done in the previous step, which came from the first page of Google results and the Google Shopping sidebar. Copy each store's URL into column A and type a short store name in column B [L0891, 2020-26].
- In column D, list every product niche or category the store sells, not only the niche you are researching. Check the store's category menu to find them, for example office furniture, kitchen furniture, living room furniture, bedroom furniture and lighting [L0891, 2020-26].
- Example: one multi-category furniture store had over 1,850 chandeliers in its lighting category, so lighting was listed next to its furniture categories in column D. A store with bathroom lights, ceiling fans and other lighting types was logged with ceiling fans and lighting fixtures [L0891, 2020-26].
- A competitor list is complete only when columns A, B, C and D are filled in for every competitor. Only then should the lesson be marked as finished [L0891, 2020-26].
- Once a product is chosen, list three to five competitor stores selling it in the product research sheet before building any site. If a product has fewer competitors, record what exists and report the gap instead of skipping the step. Many products will still have three to five competitors [L0897, 2026].

### Master supplier sheets

- Keep a separate master supplier list for each niche you plan to work in, for example one list for chandelier and lighting brands [L0940, 2020-26].
- The master supplier list is one of the three sheets of the niche research worksheet [L0584, 2020-26].
- Enter the number of suppliers counted in the niche's Master Supplier list in the Estimated Suppliers column. Use a rough count from a few competitor stores rather than a full competitor analysis (example: chandeliers, 55 estimated suppliers) [L1079, 2020-26].
- Fill in the master supplier sheet before choosing the first niche, so that the choice rests on complete competitor and supplier data [L0584, 2020-26].
