### Reverse engineering a competitor's campaign

**The five variables of a successful online campaign**

| Variable | What to find out |
|---|---|
| Demographics | Age, sex, education, location, income level, race, language, political affiliation and any other traits that define the target group |
| Offer | What is sold, at what price, and every upsell and downsell in the funnel |
| Landing page | The page visitors see right after clicking, such as an opt-in page or a sales page |
| Traffic source | The websites, networks and media (banner, social, email, video) that deliver the traffic |
| Ad copy | The headline, body text, images and video used in ads that get clicks |

[L0523, 2015+]

- Enter a niche only when you know at least four of the five variables: demographics, offer, landing page, traffic source and ad copy. One unknown can be guessed, but two unknowns mean you should keep researching or choose another market. Never build offers, pages and ads with two blanks. [L0523, 2015+]

**Procedure: reverse engineer a competitor's campaign**

1. List direct competitors (selling the same thing) and indirect competitors (selling something else to the same demographic) with their landing page URLs. If you do not know them, search your keyword phrases on Google and click the paid ads, usually shown on the right side.
2. Enter each URL into a traffic research tool such as SimilarWeb to see paid traffic sources, visitor demographics, the sites where ads run, when each ad was first seen and how long it has been running.
3. Note the ads that have run longest, since long-running ads usually convert well. Collect their copy and images, and record which landing pages receive most traffic.
4. Buy the competitor's product, and record every upsell, downsell and follow-up email, including how many emails are sent and how often they sell.
5. Save everything in a swipe file of ideas to model before building your own funnel.

[L0523, 2015+]

- Spend freely on competitor research. Buy each of their products and take careful notes on sales videos, emails and the number of follow-up messages. The first visible offer is usually an entry point, while the real revenue sits further down the funnel. Mapping the full sequence matters more than the first sale. [L0523, 2015+]
- For a long-term business, buying a competitor's product is worth the cost, because it gives you their post-purchase email sequences and advertising funnels. A small purchase can reveal a full retention system. [L1243, 2017]

**The Facebook Pixel events to check on a competitor site**

- The six events are Page View (usually the homepage), View Content (usually the product page), Add to Cart, Initiate Checkout, Add Payment Info and Purchase. Each event marks a different stage of the buyer journey. [L1243, 2017]

**Procedure: competitor retargeting analysis with the Facebook Pixel Helper**

1. Open the competitor's front page and click around until the Page View pixel fires.
2. Wait seven days, then check Facebook and Instagram during that time and record which retargeting ads you receive.
3. Open a competitor product page until the View Content pixel fires.
4. Wait seven or more days and again record which retargeting ads you receive.
5. Save the screenshots and the reason each ad appeared, so you can compare the competitor's funnel timing and offers.

[L1243, 2017]

### Offers, bonuses and pricing

- The price of a competitor selling below your wholesale cost suggests they bought in bulk and secured a much lower per-unit cost. You can check Alibaba for 1,000-unit bulk pricing to understand their cost structure. [L0067, 2023]
- Some good products are poor entry products. Choose the front-end offer by what the market wants most urgently, and then sell higher-value products after the first purchase. [L0378, 2019+]
- Bundles and offers, such as buying one item and getting a second at 50% off, or buy two and get one free, help a store win customers over competing sellers. [L1184, 2023+]
- Compare offers element by element. List the competitors' offers for the same product, compare them on each element, and identify where yours is weaker. Work through the competition research questions from the foundation stage to find the gaps that need fixing, so your offer ends up at least as good, ideally better. [L0408, 2021-22]
- Record the competitor's offer, how visible its return policy is, its shipping approach, the proof it gives for trust, and what customers say about it. Review this tracker before rewriting your own offer so each change is compared against real competitor data. [L0411, 2021-22]
- Map your offer as a CURVE offer:
  1. Review your competition research, including what competitors offer and what customers say about them.
  2. Compare your total offer against each direct competitor.
  3. Map how you can turn your offer into a CURVE offer.
  4. Record the results in the CURVE offer builder spreadsheet, including a section for marketing messages. [L0411, 2021-22]
- Research competitor offers:
  1. Search a competitor name, the term BOGO or other offer keywords in ad and email tools.
  2. Open the saved landing page to study how they present the offer, if it is still live.
  3. If the brand has removed the site or landing page, use the Wayback Machine to view archived versions.

  [L1404, 2025-26]
- Check product pages for bonuses or premiums, such as freebies or content upgrades. [L0893, 2021-22]
- Check the footer and policy pages for the returns policy and any other risk reversal, such as price-match guarantees. [L0893, 2021-22]
- Before pausing ads on a product whose competitor breaks MAP, search that competitor by name and read its reviews. If customers complain about slow answers, late shipping or payment trouble, the violator will likely lose customers, so it can be sensible to keep your ads running. (2023) [L1094, 2023]

**Manufacturers that also sell direct (checking MAP and MSRP)**

1. Check whether the manufacturer sells directly to the public. If it does not, there is no direct price conflict.
2. If it sells direct, check its public price. If it sells at MSRP, you can list the product at a better price. If it sells at MAP, you will have to match its price.
3. If it sells at MAP, judge its website. Outdated design, one image, little product information, and no phone number or live chat mean you can outsell it with a well-built store.
4. If its site is as strong as yours, do not run paid traffic to the product. List it only for organic traffic or skip it. [L1110, 2019]

### Competitor emails, retargeting and retention

**Procedure: competitor email research**

1. Identify your closest competitors, at minimum the nearest ones in your market.
2. Visit each competitor's website and look for opt-in forms, newsletter subscribe boxes, pop-ups and opt-ins on their blog.
3. Subscribe to each list using a dedicated research email address.
4. If no opt-in is visible, add a product to the cart, enter the research email at checkout, and abandon the checkout so you enter their abandoned checkout flow.
5. Review the emails you receive to see what they promote, how they present offers and which topics they avoid. [L0609, 2021-22]

- Use a separate, throwaway Gmail account under a neutral name for competitor sign-ups. This keeps the main inbox clean and prevents competitors from recognising the research address. [L0609, 2021-22]
- If competitors are not running email marketing, the inbox is an uncontested channel in that market. This creates an opportunity to build a dominant presence in customers' inboxes, which is hard to achieve in search or social feeds. [L0609, 2021-22]
- Frequently promoted products or messages in a competitor's emails usually indicate what works for them. Topics they never mention may reveal openings, but competitor emails should be used for inspiration, never copied outright. [L0609, 2021-22]
- Competitor email research also helps identify market gaps, since older competitors may not email at all or may avoid certain subjects. Those gaps can be used for your own email topics. [L0609, 2021-22]

**Procedure: website analysis of a competitor**

1. Record the competitor's website URL(s).
2. Note whether they sell the same or similar products.
3. Identify traffic sources: search for your product types on Google and check whether the competitor appears in paid ads. If you have never seen them running Google ads, paid search is likely not a traffic source for them.
4. Enter the competitor's domain into an SEO research tool to estimate organic monthly traffic. Estimates are approximate, since some traffic sources take time to detect.
5. Check for social media presence, such as Facebook, and record it even if it looks light.
6. Check for email marketing: look for opt-in forms on the homepage and on content pages.
7. If no opt-in exists, add a product to the cart, enter your name and email at checkout, then leave the checkout and close the tab so it looks like an abandoned cart.
8. Use a dummy email inbox (separate from your main inbox) to subscribe to all competitors' lists, then review what they send, including special deals, content and any abandoned cart sequence.
9. Record the USP from the homepage.
10. Record proof points shown on the site.
11. Check product pages for bonuses or premiums such as freebies or content upgrades.
12. Check the footer and policy pages for the returns policy and any other risk reversal, such as price-match guarantees.
13. Record price and terms: is their price the same, lower or higher than yours, and are there extra sales terms?
14. Record the hook, or leave it as none if there is no one-liner.
15. Record the primary marketing promise.
16. Record features and deliverables they mention.
17. Check whether they have content, such as guides, how-tos and comparisons, and judge its quality by length, depth, images and product links.
18. Add comments on gaps and opportunities. [L0893, 2021-22]

**Case: competitor analysis of an Australian online caravan parts retailer**

- Range: about 7,000 products.
- Traffic: organic traffic of tens of thousands to around 100,000 visits per month, with SEO as the main traffic source, little Google Ads and light Facebook activity.
- Email: no opt-in on the homepage or content pages, and no abandoned cart email received after a test checkout.
- USP: fast delivery, a large range, specials, free shipping, guides and live stock levels.
- Proof points: a 4.97 out of 5 rating from 58,005 reviews, and over 200,000 items shipped in 12 months.
- Offer: no bonuses on product pages. A 30-day change-of-mind policy with a store credit and a 10% restocking fee.
- Messaging: hook "simple", primary promise "great parts delivered fast".
- Content: dozens of guides with mixed content quality.

[L0893, 2021-22]

**Range and trade checks**

- When analysing a competitor's range, count the listed products on each category page and add the category counts together to estimate total catalogue size. Record the largest categories too, since they show where the competitor concentrates its inventory. [L0893, 2021-22]
- Check a competitor's navigation and footer for a trade or wholesale section and for a downloadable price list. These pages indicate a separate customer segment or pricing channel that should be noted in the competitor sheet. [L0893, 2021-22]

**Competitor comparison checklist**

Answer these questions for each primary competitor and keep them in a spreadsheet (for example a Google Sheet) for regular updates:

| Question | Purpose |
|---|---|
| What is their unique selling proposition? | Find positioning gap |
| What is the marketing message for their products? | Compare messaging |
| How do they price their products? | Price benchmark |
| What other value or benefits do they provide with purchase? | Find added-value gap |
| Where and how do they generate their traffic? | Find traffic sources |
| What do they do to retain and elevate existing customers? | Find retention gap |
| What search terms do they rank for organically? | Find SEO keywords |
| Are they present at each stage of the customer awareness pyramid and journey, and how? | Find stage coverage gap |

[L0414, 2021-22]

- Competitor analysis for unique mechanisms, when the manufacturer documentation is unclear:
  1. Check the manufacturer documentation for the product's features and attributes.
  2. If the mechanism is not clear there, list the competing products that customers would consider buying.
  3. Write down the features and attributes of each competing product.
  4. Compare them with the features and attributes of your product.
  5. Identify what your product has that the others do not. This is your unique mechanism or mechanisms.

  [L0465, 2021-22]

### Reviews, complaints and Trustpilot

**Procedure: Trustpilot complaint check**

- To see only low-rated reviews on Trustpilot, open the company's review page and add the URL parameters stars=1, stars=2 and stars=3 (for example trustpilot.com/review/[domain]?stars=1&stars=2&stars=3). This filter shows 1 to 3 star reviews, so complaint themes are quick to collect. [L1164, 2026]
- Copy the filtered low-star reviews into a CSV file and ask an AI assistant for a full gap analysis of the complaints. [L1164, 2026]
- Trustpilot does not allow companies to offer incentives for reviews or to pay to hide them, so a company's review record is a relatively hard-to-fake research signal. Still check the review count and the recency of reviews before judging a product from its rating. [L1164, 2026]
- Check how competitors list a supplier's brand. Many stores order brands by sales, and a brand near the top of the brand menu or sorted-by-bestseller view is likely selling well. Review counts also signal volume: for example, a product with over 3,000 reviews is likely a strong seller. (2026) [L0808, 2026]
- Check the product's review count and rating as a signal of volume, alongside the sales data. [L0808, 2026; L1164, 2026]

### Checking whether two stores belong to the same seller

**Procedure: checking whether two TikTok accounts or stores belong to the same seller**

1. Compare the store domains. Different domains for the same store design can indicate one seller running several stores.
2. Check the Terms of Service, Privacy Policy and Contact Us pages for missing or identical content.
3. Compare the email addresses on the contact pages. Identical emails are the final confirmation that the same person runs both stores.
4. Compare the posting times and the uploaded clips. Identical times and identical content point to the same operator.

[L0656, undated]

- A seller with a new second account may be reacting to a viral success on the first account. Opening a second store under a different domain can be a marketing move to run ads through the other store. [L0656, undated]

### Brand-level and channel-level signals

- Successful sellers rarely reveal their winning products, and competitors quickly copy ads that work. Do not expect a direct product answer from people who are already profitable, and assume a visible winning ad will be copied within a short time. [L0690, 2022+]
- Competitor sales of winning products are not visible to you directly, so use signals (traffic, ad longevity, store age, activity) together. [L0523, 2015+; L1164, 2026]
- Analysing a competitor's product for landing-page message match and offer structure helps you spot gaps. [L1185, 2023; L1194, undated]
- Use a competitor's pain points and angles as input for your own message, not as a copy. [L1194, undated; L0609, 2021-22]
- Monitor direct and indirect alternatives to see whether they are growing or shrinking, and note their emotional connection to the audience. [L0613, 2026]
- Competitor analysis of an earlier market check, from the one source course: check whether a competitor's ads and pages are consistent over time before treating them as a model. [L0523, 2015+]

### Gaps, differentiation and what to avoid

- Competitor gaps can be turned into your angle: the gaps you find become the reason a buyer picks you. [L0040, 2020-26; L0414, 2021-22]
- Low competition in SEO means building links can help you outrank stores that rank without strong effort. This is an advantage when competition is low. [L0963, 2022]
- If a niche has few SEO-focused competitors, invest in links and content to take those positions. [L0963, 2022]
- Competitor shifts in brand focus (from dropshipping to own brand) indicate the product has shown enough demand. [L0708, undated]
- Do not copy another store's content or store word for word. Build an original version, with your own value proposition. [L0806, 2024; L0968, 2021; L0656, undated]
- Do not treat a competitor's winning ad as proof that the product is a winner, since the ad may run on paid traffic without sales. [L1186, 2022; L1177, 2023]
- Do not treat a single claimed total as proof. Verify with multiple signals. [L1385, undated; L0963, 2022]
- A product with many competitors selling it is not automatically a poor choice. It shows demand, but the product itself must stand out. [L0790, 2017; L0600, 2026]

### Sourcing and product selection from competitor best sellers

- Study a competitor's best sellers to decide what to test first. [L1164, 2026; L1285, 2024]

**Procedure: find best sellers from competitors (2024)**

1. Search Google for your top future competitor stores.
2. Open the category or collection that matches your product, such as standing desks.
3. Sort by best selling or most popular.
4. Note the top listed products from brands you also carry, because they already sell well.
5. Use that short list as the core of your best-seller campaign.

[L1285, 2024]

**Procedure: find likely best sellers on a future competitor's store (2023)**

1. Open the collection pages of the competitor store.
2. Check the default sort, which is often recommended or best-selling, and switch to sort by best selling if available.
3. Note which products sit at the top of each collection.
4. Use these products as the starting set for your first campaigns.

[L0470, 2023]

- On a competitor's brand filter, the brands shown first are usually the best sellers, not the alphabetically first brands. Check the full brand list and the top brands in each subcategory to find brands worth prioritising. (2023) [L0470, 2023]

**Procedure: select the best sellers from a large supplier catalogue (2024)**

1. Visit future competitor stores and open their Best Sellers or Most Popular sorting in each category.
2. Match those products to your supplier catalogue.
3. Submit only those products in the Merchant Center feed for the initial campaign.
4. Add a testing budget for the remaining products after the initial period.

[L0471, 2024]

**Procedure: find best sellers on a competitor's site (Wayfair example, 2024)**

1. Go to a top competitor store (for example, Wayfair).
2. Navigate to your product categories.
3. Sort by "Recommended" or "Best Selling" (these are top sellers).
4. Start uploading the best-selling products first.
5. Alternative: ask suppliers directly which products are their best sellers.

[L0253, 2024]

- Competitor menu structure: successful stores list the most popular categories first, reading left to right and top to bottom, so a sub-niche placed at the far right or bottom suggests weak demand. (2021) [L0469, 2021]
- Competitor check before a product with a hidden or cloned competitor: "reviews" and "testimonials" are not proof. Verify demand through the store's own record of sales and activity. [L0450, 2022+; L1177, 2023]

### Competitor installation, service and channel checks

- When researching how competitors handle installation, contact them from an email address that is not your business email. Ask which installer they use in your area, to see whether they rely on one national service or many local contractors. (2023) [L0835, 2023]
- Use this to decide whether a service-based product needs a national or local installer network. [L0835, 2023]

### Product viability and early research

- Check competitor sites and social accounts to see whether a product is still viable before investing, as a stopped store link or silent account can signal a dead product. [L1444, 2022; L0657, undated]
- Before listing a product, use the competitor's price, description and offer as a reference, then adjust for your own angle. [L1171, 2024+; L0577, 2023]
- Study what competitors sell and how, then decide whether the niche is worth pursuing. [L1088, 2020; L0999, 2023]
