### Vetting Viral Accounts

#### Account health and timeline

- Check the seller's account timeline. A product that went viral on day one, with no or very few new uploads afterwards, is a warning sign, especially when the account is fresh and the views are very high. [L0530, 2022]
- A healthy viral product account often posts a new viral video roughly every one to two weeks, or about one per month for a strong product, and keeps uploading steadily. [L0530, 2022]
- A genuinely organic creator with a viral video usually keeps uploading many videos. An account with tens of millions of views and only a few uploads is a warning sign that paid promotion or a second account is involved. [L1385, undated]
- A seller who stops posting after a viral hit is not always failing. They may be holding back to avoid disrupting a video that still converts, or the hit may have come from one region only. Treat these as explanations to verify, not proof that the product sells. [L0530, 2022]
- Judge a seller's momentum by the views of the single relevant video, not the lifetime view count of the account, because old posts can inflate the total. [L1186, 2022]
- When many unrelated creators post the same branded product at once, the seller is likely running creator-driven campaigns, which suggests active selling. Check which accounts are pushing it rather than assuming it is organic. [L1186, 2022]

#### Ad origin and fraud

- Use the TikTok ad profile link as a signal. If clicking it shows no account, the ad is a regular paid ad with only a logo and no profile attached. Spark Ads, by contrast, are linked to a creator profile. [L0372, 2022]
- A missing account link does not always mean the product failed, because the video may later have been removed or its sound muted. Check the product again on TikTok before dropping it. [L0372, 2022]
- Check whether a viral video is the seller's own content. Copied ads run by a different account with a different name, no real TikTok account and zero comments are signs of ad fraud or stolen creative. [L0530, 2022]
- A spike in views on a very new account can come from Spark Ads (boosted organic posts). This explains missing uploads but does not guarantee sales. [L0530, 2022]
- A video with zero comments on an ad is a red flag. Sellers often disable comments on ads, and negative feedback can be hidden, so do not judge viability from ad content alone. [L0530, 2022]

#### Repeat and re-upload checks

- Before treating a viral video as proof of sales, check the seller's account for a repeatable pattern. Several videos above 1 million views spread over weeks suggest a product that keeps working. Mostly low-view uploads with one hit suggest a single lucky moment. [L0530, 2022]
- If a viral video was re-uploaded and the second copy gets far fewer views than the original, the original hit is the only real signal. Compare the view counts of both uploads before deciding that the product sells. [L0530, 2022]

#### Competitor exposure

- When competitors expose a product as dropshipped, the effect is usually small. 100,000 views on a video that has 10 million views is only about 1 percent of the audience. Exposing accounts often reveal the supplier name and search terms for AliExpress, which can be useful. [L0372, 2022]

#### Case: a bunion product account

- A viral account selling a bunion (hallux valgus) product gained about 51M, 32M and 8M views on its first videos, all uploaded from 6 September, with only 8 uploads in roughly 3 to 4 weeks. It claimed about $800,000 in lifetime sales. [L1385, undated]
- Reviewers found a second account with the same website domain and very similar follower counts. Both posted a video on the same day, which pointed to split testing or paid promotion rather than purely organic growth. [L1385, undated]
- A dummy order on the website showed large sales, but a second dummy order placed 24 hours later added only about $3,000. [L1385, undated]
- Reviewers ran the product themselves. A student's product video passed 10M views and produced only about 3 sales in a few hours, then a slow trickle. A separate account with about 12M views produced 10 orders, and a newer account with about 10M views in 3 days produced only about 10 sales. [L1385, undated]
- The store listed one product, while the PPAds tool revealed sales on two other products reachable only by editing the product URL. [L1385, undated]
- The account then deleted or made private its videos, changed its name and domain while keeping the same website, and switched to a bunion product, which reviewers judged to be a weak converter that had misled many sellers. [L1385, undated]

### Your Own Accounts

- Use a new TikTok account for every product tested, unless the new product is in the same niche as an existing account. Only then is an older account acceptable, because a new account carries the new-account advantage. [L0501, 2022]
- Staying in the same niche makes the account's audience transition smoother. Changing niche is possible, but the new audience will be confused at first and views will start low. [L1383, 2022]

### Judging Virality Against Real Sales

#### Views do not prove sales

- Millions of views do not prove sales. A viral video can be a reaction or entertainment piece with no conversion, so judge the product by comments, store quality and pricing too. [L0530, 2022]
- A product with a viral video can still fail to convert if its audience is mostly curious rather than buying. Product quality and the match between product and audience determine conversion, so a high view count alone does not validate a product. [L1077, undated]
- A high view count on a viral video does not prove that a product sells. Before copying a viral product, check actual sales per view, because very large view numbers can coexist with only a handful of orders. [L1385, undated]
- A product with a few hundred thousand TikTok views, priced around $27 to $30, had a listing with only 17 orders. Because of the low order count it was not pursued, even though the views were high. Views alone did not prove sales. [L0706, 2020+]
- Treat high view counts and high sales on a video as proof that it is a viral, working ad. The sales figure is the main quality indicator. [L0704, 2026]

#### Signals to check

- Comments: many buyer questions such as "where can I buy this" from different people are a strong sign of demand. Comments can be more reliable than the video itself. [L0530, 2022]
- Reach graph: use VidIQ to see whether views drop off after paid ads or Spark Ads begin. [L0819, 2020+]
- Ad dates: recent first and last ad dates that are still running show the product works now. [L0895, 2021+]

### Screening a Candidate Product

#### Checklist

- Use a checklist for product screening: comments showing buying intent, store quality, price range, recency of uploads, and whether the video is original. Proceed only when the overall picture checks out. [L0530, 2022]
- Choose a product that has broad appeal and where competitors have gaps. Highly niche audiences such as woodworkers, teachers or nurses are harder to start on TikTok. [L0637, 2023+]
- Choose products that look appealing on video and solve a visible problem, because the video must show the product working. Products with a clear demonstration are easier to make convert. [L0657, undated]
- Before choosing, check that a product is already selling: prefer products already working in the market, and products with fresh results are easier to act on. [L1380, 2022; L1134, undated]

#### Seven-criteria procedure (TikTok product research)

1. The product must solve a problem big enough that people act and buy immediately (e.g., acne rather than dog bad breath).
2. The product must have broad appeal across many demographics, not a tiny niche.
3. The product must show instant results or a clear transformation within a 15 to 20 second ad.
4. Pre-existing TikTok videos of the product must exist that can be reused for custom ads.
5. The product needs a unique selling angle versus competitors (better price, higher quality, more options in colors or sizes, or a stronger offer).
6. The selling price must be low enough for impulse purchase, ideally under $50.
7. The product must have at least a 3x markup on landed cost (product plus shipping) before ad costs. [L1183, undated]

#### Niche and visible-result principles

- Broad-appeal niches such as makeup, beauty, health and fitness, wellness, life hacks and fun gaming products suit TikTok ads better than narrow niches like knee pain for men over 50. Narrow audiences raise CPMs, are hard to target with TikTok's algorithm, and are too small to scale. [L1183, undated]
- Products that show a visible result within about 10 to 15 seconds of ad time, such as life hacks or beauty transformations, perform well on TikTok. Products that need 30 to 50 days to show results require more social proof and tend to trigger outside research, which reduces impulse purchases. [L1183, undated]
- Products that solve a clear problem or have a strong wow factor tend to perform best on short video, so prioritise these. [L0820, undated]
- Short videos with strong sound, an aesthetic background and a satisfying look can earn high watch time and repeat views, so visual and audio quality matter when judging a viral candidate. [L0530, 2022]
- Products that are unhygienic, messy, or need an awkward use step (such as being pushed into a drink and leaving the room sticky) are unlikely to convert, even when the video gets views. [L0530, 2022]
- A product with a clear emotional or identity appeal, such as car owners showing passion for their vehicle, can sell well to a targeted audience even if it is niche. [L0530, 2022]
- A product tied to a clear identity or passion can convert within a small niche. The operator should understand and relate to that audience before judging its appeal, and should research further if they do not. [L0530, 2022]
- Choose the product by its problem, not its niche label: a surprising product paired with a strong audio or visual hook works only if the usage is practical, meaning the product solves a real problem clearly and without a messy routine. [L0530, 2022]
- Products in very specialised niches such as fishing, camping or horse riding perform poorly on TikTok ads. Big broad niches such as beauty, fashion and kitchen fit the platform better. High-ticket products also underperformed on TikTok even with good content. [L1376, undated]
- Products that are priced high do better on Facebook. Facebook still has more user data for targeting high-priced products ($200 to $300), while TikTok is cheaper and newer and does not yet find very specific customer groups well. [L1376, undated]

#### Price rules

- Products priced above $50 only make sense in beauty, health and wellness niches. For TikTok ads with a youth audience, the best-performing price point is under $50. [L1183, undated]

#### Niches that worked on TikTok

- Niches that worked well on TikTok include life hacks and problem-solving products (such as wallets and fidget spinners), beauty (described as evergreen), health and fitness, pet products (strong recently), and home improvement or home decor (including diffusers). A product outside these niches can still work, since the TikTok algorithm is improving. [L1382, undated]
- Products reported as consistently strong on TikTok included jewelry, home, kitchen, entertainment and beauty items. Relationship jewelry was a niche where a viral product was found first and a better paid-ads product was then found within the same niche. [L0819, 2020+]

#### Choosing a niche with conflicting advice

- Broad niches are the safer default for TikTok: highly niche audiences are harder to start with. Identity-driven niches can still convert when the operator understands the audience. [L0637, 2023+; L1183, undated; L0530, 2022]

#### Influencer marketing

- Influencer marketing tends to work best for products that solve a visible problem, such as pain relief, and for jewelry, beauty care and fitness products. [L1379, 2020+]
- Clothing is a saturated market for influencer marketing and usually needs a very unique product to work. Influencer results also depend heavily on the individual creator. [L1379, 2020+]

### Traffic and Product Choice

- When a test product gets views but no sales, look at the video quality and hook before deciding the product is wrong, because a weak first five seconds can cause the failure. [L0820, undated]
- A product that was used to drive traffic to a separate fitness clothing store was easier to make viral than the clothing itself. The creator did not care about buying the demo product, only that viewers visit the store. [L0820, undated]
- A viral product can be used as a traffic vehicle. Promote a product that is easy to make go viral and link to your main store or marketplace listing (for example an Amazon FBA page) to bring visitors who may buy other items. [L0820, undated]
- Products with high convenience, a chance to go viral, and a current trend on TikTok are the ones to commit to. Products that performed well on paid ads may still be worth testing on organic traffic. [L1384, 2022]
- Before running paid ads on a product found on TikTok, check whether it already has strong organic performance, such as several thousand to tens of thousands of views on organic TikTok posts. Organic traction is a signal to test with a small paid budget, then keep posting organic content if sales come in. [L1212, undated]
- Testing speed matters: reuse existing viral TikTok content (for example videos with 500,000 to 1 million+ views) rather than ordering and filming for each product before knowing it is a winner. This saves time and money and lets you test more products faster. [L1183, undated]
- Do not start TikTok ads before the store reaches about 50,000 dollars in monthly revenue and other channels are dialled in. This is advice from 2022 and reflects an earlier store-level rule. [L0969, 2022]
- Products whose revenue is growing and which can be tested with a small paid budget can be scaled once organic sales confirm them. [L1212, undated]

### Platform Fit: TikTok

- A product found on TikTok can be a test for Facebook: test on Facebook and TikTok before one is dismissed. [L0995, 2023]
- Products sold in a niche with large audiences can be tested on TikTok when Facebook targeting does not reach the intended younger age group. [L0995, 2023]

### Platform Fit: Facebook

#### Facebook feed procedure

1. Scroll the Facebook feed and find dropshipping products shown as ads.
2. Like the post and comment on it.
3. Open the product page and add the product to cart.
4. Go to checkout.

The platform then learns that you are interested in this type of product and shows more of them. [L0361, 2024]

#### My Ad Finder procedure

1. Install the My Ad Finder extension so only ads appear in the feed.
2. Scroll normally and note products with good engagement and positive comments.
3. Add them to a spreadsheet.
4. Run every candidate through the written product-selection criteria checklist (Word document).
5. Choose the best-fitting product to test. [L1107, 2023]

- Find candidate products by running keyword searches on Facebook for a category, such as baby products, and studying the posts that stand out. Look for products that already attract strong engagement. [L1251, 2024+]
- For Facebook Ads, focus on problem-solving products rather than broad niches. Niches that performed well include pets, kids, tech, work from home and beauty, with beauty especially strong on Instagram stories and reels. [L0788, undated]
- Facebook's ad algorithm reads the text, the video context and the visual content of an ad to decide who sees it. An ad must be relevant to the audience it targets. Narrow targeting combined with a message and visuals that match the audience helps the algorithm find the right people. [L0999, 2023]
- Consider the browsing context: people see feed ads while distracted, for example while standing in a queue, so the message must land quickly. Many viewers also watch without sound, so video should carry subtitles. [L0997, undated]
- Products that have worked on Facebook can be moved to a different brand and audience. The same back-stretcher product was scaled on Pinterest by a store with different branding and a different audience, while the Facebook version ran a separate pitch. Targeting a new platform can find a new customer base for an identical product. [L0871, 2021+]

### Platform Fit: Instagram

- Instagram, which Facebook ads also reach, has a younger audience than Facebook itself. [L0995, 2023]
- Search Instagram by hashtag and by product-term variants to find real accounts and images. [L0321, undated; L0450, 2022+]

### Platform Fit: Pinterest

#### Niches and audience

- Pinterest works in the US, where about 50 to 60 percent of its audience is located. Average order value is reported above $50, and about 55 percent of US users earn more than $100,000 per year. [L1134, undated]
- The 60% and "most users" figures for women come from different phrasing in the sources; both point the same way. [L0788, undated; L1135, undated; L1134, undated]
- A claim that about 70% of users expect a next purchase after getting inspiration on Pinterest was cited. This is an unverified claim, so test the platform with your own budget. [L0637, 2023+]
- Pinterest suits niches such as DIY, crafts and beauty, where users look for inspiration and plan purchases. [L0637, 2023+]

#### Reliability and restrictions

- The Pinterest algorithm is weaker than on TikTok or Facebook, and only a few niches reliably sell. Random products outside these niches often fail, so check niche fit before spending on Pinterest ads. [L0788, undated]
- Pinterest is cheap and underused by e-commerce sellers compared with Facebook or TikTok. It allows most traditional dropshipping products, with rare bans. Account restrictions are often lifted within one to two days, or a few hours via support. [L1134, undated]
- Pinterest ad restrictions: weight loss claims and before-and-after images are the main risk. Keep claims simple and not bold. Other beauty claims such as glowing skin or wrinkles were not seen as problematic, and rejected ads can usually be fixed quickly by editing. [L1134, undated]
- Pinterest works best for niches like home decor, fashion, beauty, health, home improvement and nails. Pet niches were also reported to work recently. [L1134, undated]
- Pinterest product research follows the same logic as Facebook or TikTok research: find products already working in the market, especially in strong niches, and use trend tools filtered by weekly winners. The speaker used the Mania tool; Pin Spy or Pinterest spy tools were also mentioned. [L1134, undated]

#### Launch settings

- Launch timing: set the campaign to run on specific days starting the next day and launch at 12:00 midnight UTC, because Pinterest uses only UTC time, not local time zones. Avoid abrupt launch times, since first-day data fluctuates strongly. [L1134, undated]
- Campaign creation settings: name the campaign after the product and launch date, select the country (US is a large market; Germany and the Netherlands also work), set status to Active, set the campaign budget to a daily budget of $5, and choose "run continuously" or "run on specific days". [L1134, undated]
- First-day Pinterest data is unreliable: CPMs are high and CTR is low on day one. The account stabilises by day two or three, so spending more at the start mostly wastes money. [L1134, undated]

#### Pinterest research procedure

1. Find products that already sell in the market.
2. Filter for the niches home decor, fashion, beauty and health.
3. Use a Pinterest spy or product research tool filtered by weekly winners to see which products run as Pinterest ads.
4. Prioritise the newest products that are working on Pinterest. [L1135, undated]

### Platform Fit: YouTube and Quora

- Products recommended by YouTube channels are often not saturated, because most viewers only watch and never implement. About one in ten viewers actually runs ads on the recommended product. [L1184, 2023+]
- Quora ads tend to fit business products and work-from-home products, where users actively search for answers to problems. Match the platform to where your audience asks questions rather than where most ads run. Test with a small budget before scaling. [L0637, 2023+]

### Collecting and Organising Product Assets

1. Open the product detail page in the research tool with the date range you want.
2. Go to the Video & Ad section and find the top videos by views.
3. Right-click each video and choose "Save video as", then save it to a dedicated product folder.
4. Save the product thumbnails and screenshots in the same folder.
5. Rename files in a consistent pattern so they can be found later.
6. Check that the saved images do not show identifiable people before using them, or blur or replace them. [L1188, 2026]

- Save and repurpose images from Instagram hashtag searches on your product page, with attention to copyright if using them directly. [L0321, undated]
- Reuse the TikTok videos as UGC where possible. [L1219, undated]

### Platform Selection and Ground Rules

- Niche is a guideline, not a rule: a product outside the recommended niches can still work because the TikTok algorithm keeps improving. [L1436, undated]
- Products that solve a daily problem or save time (wallets, fidget spinners and similar life-hack products) have worked well on TikTok. Niche categories are not ranked in priority order. [L1436, undated]
- Beauty is an evergreen niche on TikTok because women buy frequently from TikTok ads. [L1436, undated]
- Health and fitness products work consistently on TikTok. [L1436, undated]
- Pet products have been performing very well on TikTok recently. [L1436, undated]
- Home improvement and home decor products, including diffusers, work well on TikTok. [L1436, undated]
- Beauty and health products aimed at women are riskier on Facebook, which often bans these niches if the ad account profile is not solid. [L1440, 2021+]
- Products that worked well on paid Facebook ads can be tested organically on TikTok and sometimes perform strongly. Test products from several sources rather than relying only on TikTok discovery. [L1444, 2022]
- Creators should watch which content users choose to see more or less of, because platform feedback controls now shape the audience a brand reaches. Planning content around these controls can help attract the right viewers and creators. [L1504, 2025-26]
