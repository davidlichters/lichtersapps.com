## Final Niche Decision, Verification and Store Scope

### Grading each niche against the criteria

**one source validation tests (current)**

- A niche is ready to build on only if it passes every check on the Niche Research Worksheet, not just most of them. [L1080, 2020-26]
- A niche must pass all six validation criteria at once: pass every test on the Niche Research Worksheet; be dropship-friendly; have at least 20 viable suppliers; have at least one future competitor with a traffic rank under 100,000; have 20 or more suppliers with real contact information; and meet all thresholds for pricing, profit, target demographics, brand loyalty and seasonality. [L0058, undated]
- Passing all four checks together is the threshold for treating a niche as likely profitable: drop-ship friendly, at least 20 suppliers, at least one competitor with a traffic rank under 100,000, and more than 20 reachable brands. Passing only some of them is not enough. [L1080, 2020-26]
- Judge a niche on measured data (supplier count, competitor traffic rank, reachable brand contacts), not on gut feeling. An expert's approval confirms data you have already gathered; it does not replace it. [L1080, 2020-26]
- Pick niches from the data, not from what sounds interesting. A niche that seems appealing may earn far less per sale than a data-backed alternative, as the woven rugs versus Persian rugs comparison shows. [L1081, 2020-26]
- Check every candidate niche against all criteria before building a store. A niche that fails the criteria should not be used as a store. [L0359, 2020]
- A niche qualifies as a winner only if it has enough search demand, has other stores already selling in it (drop-ship friendly), and can supply at least 20 distinct suppliers or brands. Those suppliers together must offer at least 100 products. Competitor stores in the target country should have a traffic rank below 1 million. [L1079, 2020-26]
- Traffic rank limits differ between sources. The stricter 100,000 limit appears in the six-criteria test and the four-check threshold; the 1 million limit appears in the winner definition. Apply the 100,000 limit when the test is the verification-stage check, and treat 1 million as the looser screen for qualifying a winner. [L0058, undated; L1080, 2020-26; L1079, 2020-26]
- Approval checks the whole niche, not each individual product. Product-level questions are answered by the niche criteria and supplier approvals. [L1111, 2020]
- Before committing, a niche must be checked by a manual procedure:
  1. Find competitor stores selling the niche or a broader category that includes it. [L0664, 2020]
  2. Look in the target subcategory of those stores. [L0664, 2020]
  3. Confirm at least 100 products and at least 20 brands you could source from. [L0664, 2020]
  4. Test search demand with Google Keyword Planner or Ubersuggest and confirm enough search traffic. [L0664, 2020]
  5. Disqualify the niche if the subcategory has only a handful of brands and products. [L0664, 2020]
- Six-step check from the 2020 material:
  1. Check demand for the niche. [L0358, 2020]
  2. Check the level of competition. [L0358, 2020]
  3. Check saturation. [L0358, 2020]
  4. Check seasonality. [L0358, 2020]
  5. Check whether the niche is dropship-friendly. [L0358, 2020]
  6. Check whether the dominant sellers are online-only or sell both offline and online. Heavy offline dominance is a reason to avoid a niche even when competition looks manageable. [L0358, 2020]
- Five-step validation from the 2021 material:
  1. Search the niche keyword on Google Shopping and count the products on the results pages, aiming for at least 100 products available from suppliers before committing. [L0801, 2021]
  2. List the brands behind those products and confirm at least 20 brands exist. [L0801, 2021]
  3. Check which brands are sold by many retailers and avoid those. [L0801, 2021]
  4. Check demand with keyword research and competitor traffic. [L0801, 2021]
  5. Submit the top three candidate niches to the niche verification process and decide. [L0801, 2021]
- Compare the number of brands, the number of products and the search demand before choosing. A niche must pass all three checks. Failing one means widening or narrowing the category. [L0801, 2021]
- A niche should pass three checks: evergreen demand, a workable price point, and enough search demand. For a US-sized market the demand check used a target of about 10,000 in the demand data. The niche should offer at least 20 possible brands and about 100 products to stay specific. [L0962, 2021]
- A niche should be evergreen, have demand, have at least 20 suppliers and 100 products in the target country, and face no excessive competition on the first page of Google Shopping. All criteria must hold before a niche is accepted. [L0963, 2022]
- Niches are checked against a Google-based competition test and a drop-ship friendliness test. A niche is only pursued when all criteria pass. [L0969, 2022]
- A niche idea should pass checks for seasonality, good price points, drop-ship friendliness, a healthy number of Google searches, and competitor websites that are popular enough. Failing one check can be enough to drop the idea. [L0972, 2025] (2025)
- Minimum niche viability: at least 20 different suppliers, at least 100 total products across those suppliers (not per supplier), non-seasonal demand, sufficient search volume, low-to-moderate competition, and appeal to upper-middle-class buyers. [L0272, 2022]
- Approval requires at least 20 suppliers, at least 100 products from those suppliers, and enough buyers searching for the product every month. If a niche falls short, broaden it into a related set of products rather than abandoning the store concept. [L0469, 2021]
- Before building a store around a niche:
  1. Confirm at least 20 suppliers. [L1068, 2019]
  2. Confirm at least 100 products across those suppliers. [L1068, 2019]
  3. Have the niche reviewed by an experienced person for a go or no-go decision. [L1068, 2019]
  4. Build the store only after the niche passes the review. [L1068, 2019]

**one source approval procedure**

- Choose the target country first, because the competitor and supplier limits depend on it. Then run the following for each researched idea, in this order:
  1. Check average price: the average across the niche's products must be 800 USD or more. [L0490, 2021-22]
  2. Check shipping: the products must be suitable for shipping and not too large. [L0490, 2021-22]
  3. Check the enthusiast market: buyers should want the products for hobby or recreation rather than need them. [L0490, 2021-22]
  4. Count suppliers: more than 10 is needed for an Australian target, and the US target is 20. [L0490, 2021-22]
  5. Count direct competitors: at most 15 for the US and at most 10 for Australia. [L0490, 2021-22]
  6. Check recurring revenue: one customer should be able to buy several different products from the niche. [L0490, 2021-22]
  7. Check traffic volume: add up the monthly searches across all products in the niche. [L0490, 2021-22]
  8. Apply the strikeout rules: competition above the limit or average price below 800 USD means not approved, regardless of the other results. [L0490, 2021-22]
  9. Fill in the Approved? column: green if all boxes are ticked, red if there is a strikeout, orange or yellow if borderline. [L0490, 2021-22]

| Check | US target | Australian target | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suppliers | 20 | More than 10 | [L0490, 2021-22] |
| Direct competitors | Up to 15 passes | Maximum 10 (13 fails) | [L0490, 2021-22] |
| Average price | 800 USD or more | 800 USD or more | [L0490, 2021-22] |

- Competition is a strikeout criterion. If the number of direct competitors is above the limit for the target country, the niche is not approved, whatever the other criteria show. This is the most important rule in the approval step. [L0490, 2021-22]
- For a US target, a niche with up to 15 direct competitors passes the competition check. [L0490, 2021-22]
- For an Australian target, the aim is a maximum of 10 direct competitors. A niche with 13 direct competitors fails there but could pass for the US. [L0490, 2021-22]
- Only competition and price are direct strikeouts. Weak recurring revenue, a low supplier count or lower traffic lower the grade toward maybe but do not reject a niche on their own. [L0490, 2021-22]
- A niche without recurring revenue can still be profitable, but it cannot grow to the same size. For this reason it is graded maybe instead of approved. [L0490, 2021-22]
- Seven-criterion market check, in order (local currency applies; the size rule has an exception):
  1. Average product price of at least 800 in your local currency. [L0994, 2021-22]
  2. Products are not too big or heavy, unless they are valued at 3,000 or more. [L0994, 2021-22]
  3. The market is an enthusiast market, not everyday household items. [L0994, 2021-22]
  4. There is the right amount of competition: some, but not too much. [L0994, 2021-22]
  5. Established, local suppliers exist, but the brands are not household names. [L0994, 2021-22]
  6. There is enough online search traffic for the market. [L0994, 2021-22]
  7. The market has recurring revenue potential. [L0994, 2021-22]
  A market idea that meets all seven criteria is viable. [L0994, 2021-22]
- Compare all brainstormed ideas against the six questions and choose the one with the most yes answers. An enthusiast market with a yes on every other question beats a market with weaker answers, even if the other market was researched first. [L0771, 2021-22]

### Comparing graded niches side by side

- Compare candidate niches side by side in one worksheet, judging each on average price, average profit, customer demographic, brand loyalty, seasonality, demand, dropshipping friendliness and the estimated number of suppliers before choosing one. [L0543, undated]
- Complete the research worksheets in this order:
  1. Fill out all remaining columns in the Niche Research Worksheet. [L0043, undated]
  2. Complete the Future Competitors worksheet. [L0043, undated]
  3. Complete the Master Suppliers worksheet. [L0043, undated]
  4. Narrow your initial niche list down to your top candidates. [L0043, undated]
  5. Submit your top niche ideas for verification. [L0043, undated]
- Select exactly five niches for the top list. Each must meet all of these criteria: meets your pricing and profit requirements; appeals to upper-middle-class demographics; has low brand loyalty; sells year-round (passes the seasonality test showing all four seasons); and demonstrates strong average monthly demand. [L0045, undated]
- Filter and rank the worksheet to find the top 5 niches:
  1. Right-click the demand column and choose sort sheet A to Z (smallest to largest). [L1081, 2020-26]
  2. Highlight every niche under 10,000 monthly searches and change its text color to red, marking them as losers. [L1081, 2020-26]
  3. Sort by the seasonality column A to Z and mark every niche that failed the seasonality test red. [L1081, 2020-26]
  4. Right-click the average profit column (column D) and sort Z to A (largest to smallest). [L1081, 2020-26]
  5. Cut the red rows and paste them at the bottom of the list, then delete the blank rows left behind. [L1081, 2020-26]
  6. Remove further weak niches that failed enough tests, such as off-grid solar kits in the example. [L1081, 2020-26]
  7. Keep only the top 5 niches at the top of the sheet. [L1081, 2020-26]
  8. Color the top 5 green, using a shade that is easy to read. [L1081, 2020-26]
- Winner list procedure:
  1. Open the main research workbook and list every niche that has already been tested in the research phase. [L1079, 2020-26]
  2. Check each niche's average price, average profit, demographic, brand loyalty, seasonality, demand and whether it is drop-ship friendly. [L1079, 2020-26]
  3. Keep only the niches that meet all of these criteria; these are the winners. [L1079, 2020-26]
  4. For each winner, open its Master Supplier list and count the suppliers found so far. [L1079, 2020-26]
  5. Enter that count in the column labelled Estimated Suppliers for the niche. [L1079, 2020-26]
  6. Compare the winners and choose the one to build the first store in. [L1079, 2020-26]
- Demand and results: demand is not the only predictor. A niche with lower demand can still outperform, and the actual outcome only shows after the store is built. [L1079, 2020-26]
- Grading scale: a niche can be a base hit (a modest, profitable store), a double, or a grand slam (a store that can reach very high annual sales). Early research can confirm a niche is profitable but not how big it will become, which only shows after months of running it. [L0806, 2024] (2024)
- Traffic against competition: when one niche has more search traffic but more competition, and another has less traffic and less competition, both can still be approved. Choosing between them is a judgment call, not a rule. [L0490, 2021-22]
- Several niches passing every criterion means there is no single best niche. Run the numbers for each one in the tracking sheet before choosing, and gut feeling should not replace the data. [L0941, 2022]

### Choosing the first niche

**Ranking and tiebreakers**

- Prioritize niches with a higher average selling price and a large number of possible suppliers. These two measures help narrow a long niche list. [L0968, 2021]
- For a first store, choose the niche with higher average price points and more available suppliers when several niches pass the criteria. [L0855, 2023] (2023)
- If a niche has many suppliers and several niches pass the research test, pick any one that passes and keep the rest as a list for future stores. Higher-priced products are a reasonable tiebreaker. [L0665, 2022]
- If several niches meet all criteria, choose the one with the most suppliers and a price point that feels right to you. Other qualified niches can be built later. [L0469, 2021]
- A niche can contain a wide price range, so you can sell all tiers under one store. Example: a 3D printer niche could include a beginner kit under 300 dollars, a consumer model at 500 to 700 dollars, and a prosumer model at a few thousand dollars. [L1112, 2021]
- Top niche rankings and example lists show what makes a niche strong; they are not a pick-one list. Choose a niche that meets the same criteria, not simply one of the listed names. [L1090, 2021]
- Published lists of profitable niches are useful for widening the range of ideas, but a niche should be chosen only after it passes your own demand and supplier tests. A niche should not be picked simply because it appears on a list. [L1069, 2019]

**Decision rules**

- Research niches until one passes every criteria test. One niche that passes everything is enough to move forward. [L0855, 2023] (2023)
- If several niches meet all criteria, there is no single best niche. [L0941, 2022]
- Once several niches pass all criteria, they all have a real chance to become strong stores, so no winner is clearly better on paper. [L1079, 2020-26]
- Choose based on the collected data and on your own confidence in the niche. Take a day or two to decide, and do not spend a week re-reviewing the same data. [L1079, 2020-26]
- If three niches pass all criteria, they are usually good candidates to choose from. Submitting two of them for verification is acceptable. [L0964, 2023] (2023)
- Brainstorming dozens of niche ideas usually produces several viable winners, so the goal is to validate the most promising few rather than find the single perfect one. Submitting two or three top ideas for validation gives extra confidence. [L1094, 2023] (2023)
- If a niche passes every validation test, choose it and start building rather than waiting for a perfect niche, because thousands of niches can work with this model. More stores can be built later to grow revenue and profit. [L0967, 2020]
- Social media reach is not required when choosing the first niche. It becomes more relevant later when building more niche-specific stores or a brand around a niche with fewer suppliers. [L0967, 2020]
- Once a niche passes the viability tests and meets the criteria, it is good enough to start with, so further research should not stall the launch. Some niches can be confirmed in as little as five minutes, so overthinking the choice is a common waste of time. [L1083, 2024+]
- Limit the time spent choosing a niche: shortlist the three niches you feel most confident about, submit them for verification, and let the verification help you pick one. Do not rush the decision, but do not stay stuck on it indefinitely. [L0801, 2021]
- A niche that passes all the criteria should not be dropped because of saturation rumors or a recession. Once you have doubts after passing the test, a downward spiral of changing niches tends to cost more than it saves. [L0804, 2023] (2023)

**Tiebreaker order for approved (green) niches**

- Procedure for choosing between several approved niches:
  1. Remove the red niches from the active shortlist and set aside the ideas that were never researched. [L0490, 2021-22]
  2. Compare the remaining greens on average price, supplier count, direct competitors and traffic. With approved niches these differences are usually small. [L0490, 2021-22]
  3. Use personal interest as the tiebreaker: which niche would you feel most excited to sell day to day through your website. [L0490, 2021-22]
  4. Pick exactly one niche and start the next phase with it. [L0490, 2021-22]
  5. Keep the full list with its scores for later use. [L0490, 2021-22]
- Sources differ on the role of interest. The one source procedure uses it as the last tiebreaker after the measured comparison. The one source material says gut feeling should not replace the data (see above). Use the data first and personal interest last. [L0490, 2021-22; L0941, 2022; L1081, 2020-26; L1079, 2020-26]
