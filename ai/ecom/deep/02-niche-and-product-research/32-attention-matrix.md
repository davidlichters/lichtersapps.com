### Attention matrix **

- An attention matrix is a spreadsheet that maps every website, YouTube channel, blog, forum, Facebook group and page, and podcast with a large audience in your niche. You use it for ad targeting, content research, customer research and joint promotions [L0478, 2021-22].
- A new business has not yet earned its market's attention, so it must first find where prospects already spend their attention. Map those audiences before trying to use or win them [L0478, 2021-22].
- Thresholds for inclusion:
  - Websites or blogs with more than 5,000 monthly visits, checked with Ahrefs or Ubersuggest. Use these numbers as guidelines rather than strict cut-offs [L0478, 2021-22].
  - YouTube channels with more than 500 subscribers [L0478, 2021-22].
  - Facebook groups with more than 300 members [L0478, 2021-22].
  - Facebook pages with more than 500 likes or followers [L0478, 2021-22]. In a large market, many pages may pass this threshold, so raise the bar to 1,000 to 2,000 likes to narrow the list. Lower or raise thresholds based on how many qualifying entries you find [L0478, 2021-22].
  - Podcasts with more than 10 reviews, because review count is a reasonable proxy for real listenership. A podcast with few or no reviews usually has a small audience [L0478, 2021-22].
- For forums and Facebook groups, also record the owners or admins, since they control the audience. Note the group admins for each Facebook group you add [L0478, 2021-22].
- YouTube audiences are global. Subscriber counts overstate your market if you sell in one country. Estimate the share of subscribers in your sales country before judging audience size [L0478, 2021-22].
- Focus on audiences that are not direct competitors, such as adjacent publications and creators who reach your buyers. Competitors with large followings can be added too, but they are usually already known from competitor research [L0478, 2021-22].
- Channels covering many sports or products are less specific than niche channels. They can still be listed, but targeting them reaches a broader and less relevant audience. Prefer smaller, tightly focused channels for targeting [L0478, 2021-22].
- Some creators are pure YouTubers with no other online presence and may appear as a single entry. This is acceptable, and the entry is still useful [L0478, 2021-22].
- Worked examples:
  - A well-known surfing magazine had roughly 260,000 monthly organic visits in Ubersuggest, far above the 5,000 threshold, and about 175,000 YouTube subscribers. Its website and channel both qualify, so it is added as one entry with each property in its own column [L0478, 2021-22].
  - A surfing how-to channel with about 100,000 subscribers was added because its content is aimed only at surfers, especially beginners and intermediates who buy boards. Its subscribers are almost all inside the target market [L0478, 2021-22].
- Check a site in Ubersuggest: open Traffic Analyzer > Overview, enter the domain and read the Organic Monthly Traffic figure against the 5,000 visits threshold. Ahrefs can be used for the same check [L0478, 2021-22].
- Spreadsheet layout, one row per entry [L0478, 2021-22]:

| NAME | WEBSITE | FACEBOOK | INSTAGRAM | YOUTUBE | PODCAST | OTHER |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Name of person, company or organisation | Website URL | Facebook page or group URL and size | Instagram profile and size | YouTube channel URL and subscriber count (e.g. 175k) | Podcast name and review count | Any other relevant property, e.g. forum, newsletter or large social account |

  Example row: Surfer Magazine | https://www.surfer.com/ | (empty if none) | (empty if none) | https://www.youtube.com/surfer, 175k | (empty if none) | (empty if none). Leave cells empty when a property does not exist. Subscriber counts can be stored as rounded figures [L0478, 2021-22].
- Build the list, in order [L0478, 2021-22]:
  1. Create a free Gmail account used only for this research and give it a recognisable name.
  2. Search Google for a phrase such as "best [your niche] blogs" and check existing best-of lists, including the featured snippet.
  3. Open each candidate website in Ubersuggest or Ahrefs and check its monthly organic visits against the 5,000 threshold.
  4. Sign up for the website's email list using the research Gmail account.
  5. From the website, follow its links to its YouTube, Facebook, Instagram and podcast profiles.
  6. Check each YouTube channel's subscriber count against the 500 threshold.
  7. Join relevant Facebook groups with your personal profile, check member counts against 300, and note the admins.
  8. Check Facebook page likes or followers against 500.
  9. Search podcasts in your niche and check their review counts against 10.
  10. Enter each qualifying entry as one row in the spreadsheet.
  11. Repeat until you have at least 50 entries, then keep adding.
- If you have no list of sites yet, start on YouTube. Type a broad keyword for your niche into the YouTube search bar and open the channels that appear. Favour channels that focus narrowly on your niche over large general brands that cover many topics [L0478, 2021-22].
- Starting from a website is efficient, because most sites link to their YouTube, Facebook, Instagram and podcast profiles, so one site leads to several matrix entries. YouTube is a convenient platform to check first because it publicly shows subscriber counts and video view counts [L0478, 2021-22].
- Subscribe to the YouTube channels and podcasts you add, so that their new uploads and episodes reach you as notifications. This lets you see what they publish over time without checking each page by hand [L0478, 2021-22].
- The topics that big audiences discuss, and those they ignore, show what your customers are interested in. Patterns of topics nobody talks about are also useful signals [L0478, 2021-22].
- Customer research uses the places where buyers gather, such as forums and Facebook groups. Regularly reading and joining these spaces reveals customer language, interests and problems [L0478, 2021-22].

### Dream 100 **

- The Dream 100 is a list of roughly 100 targets (people or organisations with an engaged audience) you can contact and build relationships with over time. The number is a guide, not a requirement. Small or specialised markets may yield fewer, very large markets may yield more, and even 30 to 70 contacts are a strong start compared with knowing nobody in the market [L0527, 2021-22].
- Keep Dream 100 targets in the country where the business is based, because most of a creator's audience usually lives there and the majority of that audience must be able to buy from you. Ignoring this rule is acceptable for backlink research in SEO, where the link source matters less [L0527, 2021-22].
- Identify the primary interests your products serve, not the product itself. Someone selling surfboards should look for people who talk about surfing, rather than surfboard sellers or surfboard-only creators. A sauna seller should consider interests such as weight loss, longevity and biohacking as separate research tracks [L0527, 2021-22].
- Target audiences that are small to medium in size, typically under 500,000 followers, though anything from about 10,000 to 100,000 works well. Very large accounts usually cover many unrelated interests, are hard to reach behind management teams, and make paid promotion expensive and poorly targeted [L0527, 2021-22].
- Choose only targets whose audience is engaged. They post regularly (for YouTube, roughly weekly uploads), their posts get likes and comments, and people ask questions. Large audiences with almost no comments often indicate purchased followers [L0527, 2021-22].
- Dream 100 research, in the original order [L0527, 2021-22]:
  1. Identify the primary interests that your products serve (for example surfing rather than surfboards; for saunas, weight loss, longevity and biohacking).
  2. Search the target platforms using keywords related to those interests.
  3. Identify individuals or organisations with small to medium audiences (under about 500,000 followers) that are engaged (they post regularly and receive comments and likes).
  4. Record each one in the Dream 100 tracker spreadsheet.
  5. Repeat across all target channels until you reach 100 or as far as the market allows.
- Search method per platform [L0527, 2021-22]:
  - Google keyword search for blogs and forums (for example "surfing forums australia").
  - Facebook search bar with the keyword, then filter results to Groups.
  - Instagram and YouTube search bars with interest-based keywords.
  - LinkedIn people search for B2B markets.
- For Facebook groups, check the member count, posts per day, new members per week and posts per month to judge activity. A group with around 47,000 members, about 10 posts per day and about 300 new members a week shows an active audience. Groups for buying and selling the niche's products can also be valuable targets, because they offer direct advertising options [L0527, 2021-22].
- If your business has been running for some time, start the Dream 100 search with the search terms your own keyword research has already shown for the primary interests. This saves time compared with guessing new keywords from scratch [L0527, 2021-22].
- Work through the Dream 100 list each day and engage only with prospects who have published something new since your last interaction, such as a blog post, a YouTube video or an Instagram post from the last few days. If a prospect has nothing new, skip them that day [L0453, 2021-22].
- Google audience insights can be used for a Dream 100-style list, because Facebook audience insights changed and no longer show what people look at [L0835, 2023]. **

### Communities and publications

- Prospects gather in congregations such as forums, Facebook groups, newsletters, blogs and niche communities. Instead of trying to create traffic from nothing, find these groups and place your message in front of them. The internet makes such groups reachable worldwide [L0523, 2015+].
- Find the congregations your target market uses [L0523, 2015+]:
  1. Google your core keywords together with the word forum.
  2. Search for groups related to your keywords on Facebook.
  3. Dig through results and communities until you find places where the audience is active.
  4. Keep notes on each congregation so you can later place ads or content there.
- To locate your audience, ask where its attention goes: which email newsletters it reads, which blogs it follows, which Facebook or Instagram groups it joins, which keywords it searches on Google, and which books it buys on Amazon. Real behaviour reveals the audience better than assumptions do [L0523, 2015+].
- Treat each newsletter, blog or group your audience uses as a vein of traffic. When you find one, dig deeper along it, because similar communities usually lie nearby. Systematic searching turns scattered attention into a predictable source of visitors [L0523, 2015+].
- Find your audience on Facebook. Search the phrase "pages liked by people who like [your niche]" in the Facebook search bar. Then like the relevant pages and join the groups to see what your customers follow and what competitors post [L1243, 2017].
- Stay in the customer's mindset while researching a niche, through six habits [L1370, undated]:
  1. Read every industry publication each month.
  2. Visit online forums where members of the niche discuss their work.
  3. Subscribe to email groups where only people in the niche write to each other.
  4. Attend industry conventions, seminars and trade shows.
  5. Play the prospect by buying from competing providers to see their sales process.
  6. Regularly brainstorm with members of the niche and with vendors who serve them.

  A coach who did this long enough was frequently mistaken for a member of the profession, and some went further by investing in businesses in the niche to test ideas firsthand [L1370, undated].
- Start the research by searching the topic directly, instead of spending time planning it. When a result looks useful, follow its references and bibliography to find the original sources. Keep going deeper than competitors do, since they usually stop at the first result. Save the most useful sources in a collection you can return to [L1343, undated].
- Amazon customer reviews are a source for customer research in local markets. Check the local Amazon store (for example Amazon France for a French market) and read the five-star reviews for descriptive feedback. Customer language from these reviews can be used in copy and creatives [L1382, undated].

### Survey and interview research

- Research with real consumers before writing ads, through interviews, questionnaires and direct sales conversations. Guessing what people want is very expensive [L1261, undated].
- Before one food campaign, a large group of consumers was interviewed over several weeks to learn their buying triggers. Another advertiser interviewed around a thousand people about what they wanted from a product before writing ads [L1261, undated].
- Case: a canvass of about 1,000 households showed that only 4 percent used a canned version of a food, while 96 percent made it at home. The right appeal was therefore to convert home cooks, not to sell one brand against others [L1261, undated].
- Use survey answers together with reviews, forums and Reddit, not instead of them [L0906, 2026].

### Deep-research prompts and deliverables

- Market research prompt. It pulls data from competitor websites, Amazon reviews, YouTube discussions and Reddit threads to provide a comprehensive market analysis. It generates a detailed research document targeting 25+ pages that identifies market gaps, competitor positioning and customer language patterns [L0221, 2026].
- Quality and depth matter far more than speed. Plan 5 to 10 minutes minimum for the investigation, and aim for 25+ pages of formatted content when complete [L0221, 2026].
- Deliverables typically have three parts [L0221, 2026]:
  - **Part 1:** executive summary, strategic verdict and market overview.
  - **Part 2:** competitive deep-dive, positioning gaps and differentiation opportunities, with source links.
  - **Part 3:** ranked hooks and objections, prioritised by conversion strength and emotional intensity.

  Tell the AI about the upcoming parts when starting the research. Later analysis improves significantly when earlier parts are added to the project context for reference [L0221, 2026].
- Run the customer and avatar research with a prompt pack [L1476, 2026]:
  1. Get the Desire to Scale System 2.0 document from the community resources.
  2. Copy each prompt, paste it into the chat and fill in the yellow-highlighted fields.
  3. Run all the prompts in one master chat (Claude or ChatGPT) with all research documents uploaded, so the context carries across prompts. Claude Projects can keep the memory.
  4. After the prompts, start a new chat, upload the documents you made and build the whitelist character or native ads from there.
- AI prompts can research an audience through three sources: Reddit discussions, Amazon book reviews and eBay research. Each source helps map the pain point and the marketing angle before you produce custom ads [L1030, 2026].

### Delegation and tools

- AgentGPT is an AI agent that takes a goal, feeds it to ChatGPT, reviews the output, adds further tasks and uses live internet access to run research automatically. Plain ChatGPT, at the time of that lesson, had no internet access and could only analyse information already provided to it [L0450, 2022+].
- PEAKS is a paid audience research tool that the speaker recommends for finding products through audience research, calling it the best tool on the market [L0690, 2022+].
- Use ChatGPT as a virtual assistant for audience research, with human research in forums and reviews as the lead [L0995, 2023].
- Delegate the prompt-driven steps to AI only after the source research is done. Verify each output against the sources, as described above [L0354, 2026].

#### Further details

- Customer research for creative strategy: 1. Identify existing customer avatars who have already bought from the brand. 2. Predict other problems or desires the product can solve. 3. Determine which people have those problems and desires, based on steps 1 and 2. 4. Search online for where these avatars share opinions or discuss related topics, such as forums, subreddits, Facebook comments, YouTube review videos and other places where the problem or desire is discussed. 5. Scrape the raw language from these sources, meaning the exact words real customers and potential customers use. 6. Summarise the findings and rank them by how often each thought or opinion is mentioned. [L0464, 2025-26]
