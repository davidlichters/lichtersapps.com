## Drop-Ship-Friendliness and Future Competitors

### Scope: which niches enter the test

- Run the dropship-friendly test only on your top 3-5 niches: those that already passed the earlier tests and have the best price points. Three to five niches is enough at this stage, not the whole worksheet [L0459, 2020-26].
- Those niches must already have passed screening for pricing, profit margins, demographic match, brand loyalty and seasonality [L0050, undated].
- Do not spend time on niches that already failed an earlier step [L0460, 2020-26].
- Testing the entire niche list at once overwhelms the research. Concentrate on the top candidates to keep focus and quality [L0050, undated].
- Avoid niches with no competition, because no one has proven demand or supply for them [L0459, 2020-26].
- A niche with only a few dropshipping competitors can be a first store if it passes all criteria and shows signs of future dropshipping competitors [L0359, 2020].

### Definitions: what counts as a future competitor

- A future competitor is an online-only store that sells multiple brands in your niche, without physical retail locations, public showrooms or owned warehouses [L0051, undated].
- Future competitors show that people already buy these products online. They validate that real demand exists and that the niche is large enough for new entrants [L0051, undated].
- A niche is dropship-friendly when existing online-only retailers successfully sell multiple brands in it without physical retail locations or public showrooms. This proves the model works in that category [L0050, undated].
- A niche is dropship-friendly, and so confirmed, when competitors operate without warehouses, showrooms or retail stores, which indicates they sell entirely through online dropshipping [L0060, undated].
- "Online-only" means no warehouse, no showroom and no retail store. A plain office is acceptable [L0460, 2020-26].
- A niche counts as dropship-friendly when online-only retailers sell products in it, because suppliers can approve these retailers [L0460, 2020-26].
- Only sellers that carry products from several third-party brands count as future competitors. Their brand count also feeds the supplier estimate for the niche [L0459, 2020-26].
- A seller whose site and products carry only its own brand is a direct-to-consumer brand and is not a future competitor. Confirm brand ownership from product images, the store name and the footer logos [L0459, 2020-26].
- A store with only its own brand, such as a customisation service with no manufacturer names on products, neither confirms nor rejects the niche. Close the tab [L0460, 2020-26].
- A competitor may sell a wider catalogue than your niche. It is acceptable as long as the niche itself still meets the supplier and product minimums [L0356, 2019].
- A seller can sell beyond the niche and still count. A furniture and decor store that also sells chandeliers qualifies for the chandelier test [L0460, 2020-26].
- A store that sells several niches is still a competitor if it sells the niche you want to enter. Do not remove it because it carries other product types [L0891, 2020-26].
- A store that carries related niches can reveal secondary niches you can expand into later [L0687, 2020-26].
- Parent companies or affiliate programmes do not disqualify a competitor [L0804, 2023].
- An online-only store that is the e-commerce division of a company with physical stores still counts as a future competitor. Its supplier relationships may make some brand approvals harder to get [L0460, 2020-26].
- Competitors should be in the country where you will sell. Overseas competitors are a disqualifier [L0855, 2023].
- A store that only ships within a country you will not sell in cannot count as a future competitor for your store [L0460, 2020-26].
- Stores that sell niche products with no dedicated niche store are not a reason to reject the niche [L0551, 2020].

### Signs of real online-only dropshipping

- True dropshipping competitors show clear signs: notes about delayed shipping in product descriptions, shipments from supplier warehouses, or mentions of multiple suppliers or distribution partners [L0050, undated].
- A footer with only phone, live chat and email suggests an online-only store [L0460, 2020-26].
- A returns page that does not mention returns to a warehouse supports an online-only, dropship model [L0460, 2020-26].
- A seller with no address anywhere in its footer or contact details is a likely online-only store. This is only a signal; confirm it on the About Us page before counting the seller [L0459, 2020-26].
- A store name containing the word "Direct" is a hint of dropshipping. Verify it on the About Us page and in the footer before counting it [L0459, 2020-26].
- A store that lists many different brands in the niche category is a strong indicator of dropshipping. Count the brands and the products per brand, since a spread across brands shows the store is not making its own stock [L0460, 2020-26].
- A competitor on a default myshopify.com address often indicates a weak business. Check its traffic rank before treating it as a real competitor [L0806, 2024].
- Stores that ship directly from China often have long delivery times, inconsistent product quality and customer issues that are hard to resolve. They also depend on one style of Facebook ads, which creates a famine-and-feast revenue pattern that concerns buyers [L0554, 2019].

### Exclusions: what does not count

- Direct-to-consumer sites, where all products carry the company's own brand, do not validate the dropshipping model and are excluded [L0050, undated].
- Exclude any site with physical retail indicators in its footer, About page or Contact page: store locator pages, "Find a store" links or listed physical locations [L0050, undated].
- A multi-brand seller with physical stores, a store locator or a pick-up option is not a future competitor, even if it sells many brands. Physical retail disqualifies it for this test [L0459, 2020-26].
- A company with a warehouse, showroom or retail store does not count as a future competitor, even if it also sells online. Only pure online sellers count as evidence for the niche [L0460, 2020-26].
- Remove any research-list entry that has a physical presence such as a showroom, retail store or warehouse [L0891, 2020-26].
- Exclude large chain retailers with physical stores, such as Home Depot, Lowe's, Walmart and Tractor Supply. Exclude general sites that are not specific to the niche, such as broad restaurant supply sites [L0459, 2020-26].
- Count only online-only stores that sell the niche. Exclude big-box chains such as Home Depot or Lowe's, because they cover too many categories [L0358, 2020].
- Skip sites you already know have warehouses or physical stores, such as sellers owned by large retail chains, marketplaces with fulfilment centres, or big home-improvement chains. Do not open them for research [L0460, 2020-26].
- A competitor with a physical store but thousands of products should not be used as a future competitor. Instead, search its brand names on Google and add the online-only brands you find as potential suppliers [L0805, 2024].
- A single product listed at one store often signals a direct-to-consumer seller. Verify the brand before counting it, and do not assume a store is a dropshipper from its name or a single listing [L0459, 2020-26].
- A seller that shows another brand's logo in its footer or on its products, while selling only one brand, is a single-supplier store and should be eliminated. Do not confuse a brand's footer logo with a multi-brand range [L0459, 2020-26].
- A store that only shows a street address is not proof of a physical showroom. Check the address before counting the store as a real retailer [L0551, 2020].
- Do not use a competitor that shows no Alexa data as validation. Find competitors with available data that show real traffic [L0245, 2020].

### Physical presence: where the rule applies and where it does not

- For competitor validation, retailers with warehouses or showrooms are not true competitors. They may compete for some customers, but only online-only stores count toward the minimum [L0853, 2021].
- For supplier sourcing, the presence of a retail store or warehouse is not in itself a reason to avoid a supplier [L0357, 2019].
- Suppliers that have retail stores are often unwilling to drop ship. Check competitors' retail presence and product listings before you choose a niche [L0671, 2021].
- Avoid contacting brands that are sold primarily through companies with physical showrooms or retail locations, because they are less likely to approve dropshippers [L0270, 2021].
- Source suppliers from online-only retailers rather than from retailers with showrooms. Showroom retailers often carry brands that are not suitable for dropshipping, so some overlap can still happen [L0852, 2020].
- Niches where competitors hold their own stock can still work. Suppliers usually need more convincing before approving a dropshipper and may offer different pricing terms than to stocking retailers. Treat such a niche as a second store once the first one produces steady cash flow [L0356, 2019].
- Items priced at $2,000 to $5,000 or more can still be sold online. Buyers usually contact the seller before ordering, so expect a pre-sale conversation [L0357, 2019].
- If most suppliers in a niche have a showroom or warehouse, check how many products each competitor website stocks. Stores often list many brands but keep only a few in stock, which is normal. If no suitable online-only option exists, broaden to a category sold by online-only stores and check which brands they list [L1068, 2019].
- A product category does not have to be sold mostly by online stores to be viable [L1069, 2019].

### Finding sellers: Google search

- Search the niche with the string `buy [niche keyword] online` in google.com and submit with Enter. Open the text ads and organic results in new tabs [L0460, 2020-26].
- Search for competitors with `buy [your niche] online` on Google and visit the linked websites [L0060, undated].
- Take the top organic results, not the paid ads [L0963, 2022].
- Review only the first page of organic Google and Google Shopping results. Do not view multiple pages or use "View More" [L0270, 2021].
- To research competitors, search the niche on Google and open the top 3 organic results. Treat those as future competitors. If no store sells the exact niche, move one level up. For example, stand-up desks is the narrower term and desks the broader fallback. Then study their brands, SKUs and product pages [L0941, 2022].
- Ignore the sponsored ads and open the top five or so organic results in separate tabs [L0806, 2024]. (2024)

### Finding sellers: Google Shopping

- Google Shopping, the Shopping tab in Google search results, is used to find sellers of a niche's products. Its All products section shows more sellers than the subcategories do [L0459, 2020-26].
- Google Shopping seller entries often show no website URL. Find the website by searching the seller name on Google in a new tab [L0460, 2020-26].
- The Google Shopping seller list only shows some of the more popular online stores, not every seller [L0460, 2020-26].
- When a product shows only one or a few sellers, click the more stores option to list the other sellers offering the same item. This reveals sellers you have not yet checked [L0459, 2020-26].
- Price filters in the Google Shopping sidebar are not the right tool for narrowing a niche. Use keywords that describe the target product tier, so the results already sit in your price range [L0806, 2024].
- Price filters: one 2021 source suggests adding a price filter (for example 200 dollars and up) on the left side to remove cheap products for competitor research, but not for the popularity test, because it hides the most popular products and their average prices [L0801, 2021]. Another 2021 source says not to apply price filters when researching a category, because they skew the picture. Check what Google shows by default as the most popular products [L0834, 2021].

### Finding sellers: popular searches, brand lists and marketplaces

- On a competitor's site, check the popular searches list for the niche keyword. It can surface additional products and brands that help find more online-only sellers [L0459, 2020-26].
- The header menu of a seller's website may contain a "Brands" item. It opens an alphabetical page of all brands the store sells, which is a quick alternative to the category brand filter [L0460, 2020-26].
- On a niche category page of a large marketplace such as Wayfair, open the left navigation, click Brand, then click show more to expand the full brand list [L0459, 2020-26].
- A store with a brand that has a Wayfair subcategory for your niche counts Wayfair as a competitor, even if it stocks few products directly. Potential customers may shop there [L0803, 2022]. (2022)
- A large online marketplace that already dropships most of its catalogue can confirm a niche as dropship-friendly before smaller sellers are checked. Its brand filter gives a rough supplier count, which can be recorded as a rounded figure when the list is very long [L0459, 2020-26].

### Checking each seller on its website

#### Procedure: ordered checks for each seller

1. Count the products and brands on the category page, and hover over product images to see whose logo they carry. [L0459, 2020-26]
2. Open the footer menu and look for Find a store, store locations, showroom, pick-up options or a warehouse. [L0459, 2020-26]
3. Open the About Us page, then the Contact Us page, which are the two most useful pages for stores, warehouses and fulfilment. [L0459, 2020-26]
4. If an address is listed, copy it into Google and open it in Google Maps Street View to see what the building is. [L0459, 2020-26]
5. Check the brand count in the category sidebar filter. [L0459, 2020-26]

#### Checks in order of priority

- Check the header menu first for a "Stores" link or a store locator, since these signal physical locations. If nothing appears in the header, check the footer for an address [L0460, 2020-26].
- On the About us page, look for stores, warehouses or a physical location in the US beyond an office. Mentions of design, production or tech teams in other countries do not by themselves show a physical store in the US [L0460, 2020-26].
- Check the returns page for returns sent to a warehouse [L0460, 2020-26].
- Check the footer for a physical address. A footer with only phone, live chat and email suggests online-only [L0460, 2020-26].
- Check the About us page for mentions of a store, warehouse or physical location in the US beyond an office [L0460, 2020-26].

### Verifying an address

#### Procedure A: Google Maps check (2024)

1. Go to Google Maps. [L0253, 2024]
2. Search for the business address. [L0253, 2024]
3. Click Street View. [L0253, 2024]
4. Observe whether it is a warehouse, commercial space, or mailing service. [L0253, 2024]
5. Google the address to see if multiple other businesses use it. [L0253, 2024]
6. If many businesses share the address, it is likely a mail-forwarding service, not a warehouse. [L0253, 2024]

#### Procedure B: address check in Google Maps

1. Copy the address shown in the footer or on the Contact us page. [L0460, 2020-26]
2. Open a new tab and go to google.com. [L0460, 2020-26]
3. Paste the address and search. [L0460, 2020-26]
4. Open Google Maps from the results or the Maps link to see the location on the map. [L0460, 2020-26]
5. Use Street View to see the building. A private home suggests the business runs from home and is not a warehouse. An office suite address in an office building is most likely not a warehouse. [L0460, 2020-26]
6. If the address shows a store, a showroom or a warehouse, the seller is not a future competitor. [L0460, 2020-26]

#### Procedure C: verify before counting (2023)

1. Copy the address from the competitor's site. [L0804, 2023]
2. Paste it into Google Maps. [L0804, 2023]
3. Check street view if available, otherwise the overhead view, to see whether it is a warehouse or other business. [L0804, 2023]

#### Procedure D: address check with fallback call (2020)

1. Copy the address shown on the competitor's website. [L0551, 2020]
2. Search the address on Google Maps and open Street View to see the building. [L0551, 2020]
3. If the building is a shipping store (for example a UPS store) or a mailbox forwarding service, treat the competitor as a dropshipper. [L0551, 2020]
4. If the address does not appear on Maps or cannot be seen, call or email the competitor and ask whether it has a showroom and what its opening hours are. [L0551, 2020]

#### Reading the building

- Google Maps with Street View shows what kind of building an address is: a home, an office or a warehouse. A suite number in an office building is a sign that the address is an office, not stock storage [L0460, 2020-26].
- A street address alone does not prove a physical showroom. Verify the building first [L0551, 2020].

### Counting brands

- Brand filter on a category page: click "More filters" and choose "Brand". Some sites label it "Manufacturer". The dropdown lists all brands with the number of products per brand in that category [L0460, 2020-26].
- On sites without a "More filters" brand option, look in the sidebar under "Types" for "Brands", click to expand it, then click "More" to see the full brand list with product counts per brand in the category [L0460, 2020-26].
- Count how many brands a store sells and how many products each has in the category, since the split across brands shows it is not making its own stock [L0460, 2020-26].
- Only multi-brand sellers count, and their brand count builds the supplier estimate [L0459, 2020-26].
- A seller that lists a logo from another brand but sells one brand is a single-supplier store. Do not count it as a multi-brand range [L0459, 2020-26].

### Dropship-friendly test: complete procedures

#### Variant A: worksheet procedure (14 steps)

1. Select one of your top 3-5 pre-qualified niches from your worksheet. [L0050, undated]
2. Go to Google.com. [L0050, undated]
3. Paste the niche name into the search box. [L0050, undated]
4. Click the Shopping tab. [L0050, undated]
5. Scroll to "All products." [L0050, undated]
6. Open multiple unfamiliar store listings in new tabs. [L0050, undated]
7. For each store, check if it sells multiple brands; exclude stores selling only their own brand. [L0050, undated]
8. For stores with multiple brands, check the footer, About page, and Contact page for physical retail indicators. [L0050, undated]
9. Exclude any sites showing physical locations, store locators, or "Find a store" links; focus only on online-only operations. [L0050, undated]
10. For remaining online-only stores, look for dropshipping signs: shipping delays, warehouse shipments, or mentions of multiple suppliers. [L0050, undated]
11. Navigate to product category pages within qualifying stores. [L0050, undated]
12. Use brand filters or brand lists to count the distinct brands offered. [L0050, undated]
13. Verify the niche has at least 20 suppliers (100+ indicates strong potential). [L0050, undated]
14. Update your worksheet: enter "Yes" or "No" in the "DS Friendly" column and record the estimated supplier count in the "Estimated Suppliers" column. [L0050, undated]

#### Variant B: Google Shopping procedure (6 steps, plus the seller checks)

1. Copy the niche name from the worksheet. [L0459, 2020-26]
2. Search it on google.com and click the Shopping tab. [L0459, 2020-26]
3. Scroll past the subcategories to the All products section. [L0459, 2020-26]
4. Right-click each product from a seller you do not know and open it in a new tab. [L0459, 2020-26]
5. Skip sellers you already know, large marketplaces such as eBay, and sellers whose products are clearly their own brand. [L0459, 2020-26]
6. Work through the sellers one by one using the checks in the seller-check procedure above. [L0459, 2020-26]

#### Variant C: worksheet tab procedure (19 steps)

1. Open the niche research worksheet and find the column "DS Friendly". [L0460, 2020-26]
2. Go to google.com and search: `buy [niche keyword] online`. [L0460, 2020-26]
3. Right-click each top text ad and organic result and choose "Open link in new tab". [L0460, 2020-26]
4. Skip sites you already know have warehouses or physical stores (for example chains owned by large retailers, or marketplaces with fulfillment centers) and do not open them. [L0460, 2020-26]
5. Open the remaining relevant results in new tabs. [L0460, 2020-26]
6. Go to Google Shopping and search the niche keyword alone. [L0460, 2020-26]
7. In the sidebar, scroll to the bottom to "Seller" and click "More". [L0460, 2020-26]
8. Open the listed sellers in new tabs, skipping the ones you already know have stores or warehouses. [L0460, 2020-26]
9. For sellers without a URL in the list, copy the seller name, open a new tab (Command+T on Mac), search the name on Google and open its website. [L0460, 2020-26]
10. On each site, check the header menu for stores, locations or a store locator. [L0460, 2020-26]
11. Check the footer for a physical address. A footer with only phone, live chat and email suggests online-only. [L0460, 2020-26]
12. Check the About us page for mentions of a store, warehouse or physical location in the US beyond an office. [L0460, 2020-26]
13. Check the returns page for returns sent to a warehouse. [L0460, 2020-26]
14. If an address is found, copy it and check it in Google Maps (see the Maps procedure). [L0460, 2020-26]
15. Check whether the site sells for multiple brands in the niche category (see the brand filter setting). [L0460, 2020-26]
16. Close tabs of sites with a physical presence or single-brand sites. [L0460, 2020-26]
17. Keep open the online-only, multi-brand sites for future reference. [L0460, 2020-26]
18. If online-only, multi-brand sellers are found, mark the niche "yes" in the DS Friendly column. [L0460, 2020-26]
19. Repeat for all remaining winning niches. Niches marked "no" move to the bottom of the list. [L0460, 2020-26]

#### Variant D: fast test (2024, 6 steps)

1. Search the target niche on google.com. [L0806, 2024]
2. Ignore the sponsored ads and open the top five or so organic (non-sponsored) results in separate tabs. [L0806, 2024]
3. Check each store for a physical location: look first in the footer for an address, then on the contact page for a home base or local pickup option. [L0806, 2024]
4. If an address appears, search it on Google to see whether it is a retail store or warehouse. [L0806, 2024]
5. Stores with a physical location or stock are not treated as future competitors. [L0806, 2024]
6. Keep only online-only stores that sell for multiple brands. If the niche has such stores, it is drop ship friendly. [L0806, 2024]
