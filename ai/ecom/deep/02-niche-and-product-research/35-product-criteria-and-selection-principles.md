## Product Criteria and Selection Principles

### Execution and mindset

- Execution creates revenue and results, not knowledge of the framework alone. The goal is to find and launch a winning product, not to complete course modules. [L0038, 2026]
- Product research is the first and most important priority. Spend most of your early effort on finding the right product rather than building the store or designing pages, because a poorly chosen product guarantees failure regardless of store quality or ad spend. [L0280, undated]
- Product selection matters more than platform specialisation in the early stage. A weak product cannot be rescued by a good website, creatives or ad strategy. Master one platform first, since every platform takes a long time to learn well. [L0349, 2024]
- There is no perfect product or perfect market, so do not search for one. Find products that meet your minimum set of business criteria, pick one and move on. [L1059, 2021-22]
- A perfect niche rarely exists. Many good stores were not perfect niches, and the first store is still a learning experience. [L1112, 2021]
- A niche that fails the verification criteria but is an emerging product can still be tested with a small store. It is a calculated gamble, not a guarantee. [L1111, 2020]
- Do not give up on a product because your first niche idea was too narrow for big results. A niche that brings in a few thousand dollars of net profit per month with minimal daily work is still a success. [L1111, 2020]
- Experience comes mainly from testing many products and learning from failed tests. Beginners should test quickly rather than wait for the perfect product. [L1187, 2026]
- Do not limit yourself to one product type, because a successful product idea is quickly copied by many stores. Apply the selection criteria rather than the example products. [L1111, 2020]
- Products with no proof of concept are riskier, but being the first to sell an item can be a long-term advantage. Start with a niche you have chosen, then test other products once you have gained experience and sales. [L1219, undated]
- Niche selection should be based on the research criteria and your knowledge of the market, not on familiarity alone. Many successful stores were started in niches the owner knew nothing about before starting. [L1111, 2020]
- Passion for the product is not required. Basic knowledge of the niche is enough, and deeper expertise builds while you research competitors and run the store. [L1088, 2020]
- Pick the products for the store through market research, and identify which one is likely to be a best seller before building the site around it. Products chosen without research are harder to position and sell. [L1346, 2020-26]

### Written product criteria

- Write a product criteria list before you start researching, and use it to remove candidates. A product that meets only a few of your rules (for example 3 or 5 out of 10) should be dropped even if it looks promising. [L0363, 2022+]
- Write down a set of product criteria and filter every candidate through it before launching anything. A list of 50 candidates can narrow to a few strong products, which is better than testing random items one by one. [L0549, undated]
- The criteria checklist is a guide, not a strict rulebook. Individual criteria can be weighted differently. The 3x markup criterion is especially important, while for electronics verified product quality matters more than the other points. [L1174, undated]
- Real deal breakers are poor quality and insufficient profit margin. Other selection criteria can be less strict if a product passes these two checks. [L0361, 2024]
- Before choosing a product, confirm that it solves a problem, is marketable, fits the market and ideally has a feature that differs from what competitors offer. That feature can become the angle for a specific audience. [L0998, undated]

**Procedure: product selection criteria**

1. Do you understand or care about this product? [L0280, undated]
2. Is the price around $50? Avoid $40 or $60 starting points. [L0280, undated]
3. Does it solve a real customer problem? [L0280, undated]
4. Does it have visual or functional distinctiveness? [L0280, undated]
5. Is it hard for customers to find locally in stores or online? Products that are difficult to source locally have higher profit potential. [L0280, undated]

**Procedure: pre-test checklist**

1. Confirm it solves a clear problem or visually stops people while scrolling the Facebook feed. [L0692, 2023]
2. Find the same product on Amazon and confirm at least 4.2 stars as a quality signal with low refund rates. [L0692, 2023]
3. Reject glass products because of breakage and return risk. [L0692, 2023]
4. Reject heavy products because shipping costs become very high. [L0692, 2023]
5. Confirm the break-even ROAS is 1.4 to 1.5 or lower. [L0692, 2023]
6. Check competition and reject products that many advertisers already run with the same creatives. [L0692, 2023]

**Procedure: full product criteria checklist**

1. The product can be sold at a minimum of 3x markup over total cost (product plus shipping). [L1174, undated]
2. Avoid heavy products, because shipping costs and damage losses become large. [L1174, undated]
3. Avoid products tied to health risks, such as supplements or chemicals that touch the skin, unless certificates and proof exist. [L1174, undated]
4. Avoid products with poor AliExpress ratings or recurring customer complaints. [L1174, undated]
5. Competitors selling the product are a positive sign, since they have already tested demand. [L1174, undated]
6. Weak competitor product pages are an opportunity to win by building a better store. [L1174, undated]
7. Electronics have higher perceived value and can sell at 4x to 5x or more, but check reviews on AliExpress, Facebook ads and Amazon to confirm the product works, as battery and quality issues drive returns. [L1174, undated]
8. Check Facebook ad comments for positive sentiment, realistic names from Western countries and no repeated or copy-paste comments, and click profiles to see their location. [L1174, undated]
9. Check Google Trends for the main keyword or niche to confirm it is trending or stable. [L1174, undated]
10. Prefer low COGS (product plus shipping), which improves cash flow and lowers break-even ROAS. [L1174, undated]
11. Prefer products with only 2 to 3 variants, since many variants add friction and delay the purchase. [L1174, undated]
12. Make sure high-quality images and 1 to 2 existing videos (Facebook video ads or YouTube videos) are available for repurposing. [L1174, undated]
13. Prefer problem-solving products that give clear pain points and marketing angles for ads and the website. [L1174, undated]
14. Prefer a wow factor: something new or innovative, or an upgraded version of something customers already know. [L1174, undated]
15. Avoid products that are easily found in retail stores. [L1174, undated]
16. Consider deep desires the product can address, such as survival, enjoyment of life or saving money. [L1174, undated]

**Procedure: five-question screen**

1. Does the product solve a problem? [L0993, undated; L0997, undated]
2. Is it marketable? [L0993, undated; L0997, undated]
3. Is there a good fit for the product in the market? [L0993, undated; L0997, undated]
4. Can you segment the audience and angle the product to one specific group? [L0993, undated; L0997, undated]
5. Is there a specific feature that differs from what else is available and can be used in the marketing? [L0993, undated; L0997, undated]

**Procedure: self-test for products found on TikTok or AliExpress**

1. Ask yourself whether you would buy the product. [L0706, 2020+]
2. Ask whether you would use it in your daily life. [L0706, 2020+]
3. Ask whether family members would buy it. [L0706, 2020+]

A yes from yourself and from others is a strong sign that other buyers will also purchase it. [L0706, 2020+]

**Procedure: validate a niche with people around you**

1. List the niches you find interesting and that could make money. [L0678, 2023]
2. Show candidate products to people you know, such as family or friends, and ask whether they would buy them. [L0678, 2023]
3. Test several niches in the beginning, keeping the budget small. [L0678, 2023]
4. Keep focusing on the niche that performs best for you. [L0678, 2023]

Ask people in your circle whether they would buy the product, and judge by market evidence, not by whether you would buy it yourself. [L0577, 2023]

**Procedure: pain points and product ideas**

1. Start from a problem you or people around you struggle with. [L0388, 2024+]
2. Ask what you could offer that solves that problem. [L0388, 2024+]
3. Listen to calls with fulfillment partners (for example CJ Dropshipping) to learn which products customers order most. [L0388, 2024+]
4. Check Etsy for products that already sell. [L0388, 2024+]
5. Look at what sells well in one market and check whether it is offered in another market before testing it there. [L0388, 2024+]

**Procedure: brainstorm worksheet**

1. Open the research worksheet, either in Google Drive or as a downloaded local file. [L0474, 2020-26]
2. Brainstorm product ideas and list them in column A. [L0474, 2020-26]
3. Look at your surroundings and daily activities and write down any item you could sell. [L0474, 2020-26]
4. Only add products you believe sell for $200 or more, regardless of your personal interests. [L0474, 2020-26]
5. Aim for at least 10 ideas, and keep adding more when they come to mind. [L0474, 2020-26]
6. Limit the brainstorming session to about 15 to 20 minutes. [L0474, 2020-26]
7. Once the initial list is complete, mark the lesson as done and continue. [L0474, 2020-26]

Brainstorm ideas are everyday examples worth testing at a price of $200 or more: a standup desk, an ergonomic office chair, a leather club chair, a rug, a horse saddle, a 3D printer, an espresso machine, a Smith machine or a Pilates reformer. [L0474, 2020-26]

The first brainstorm list contains hypotheses based on expected selling price, not confirmed winners. Each idea must still be tested for real demand before the store commits to it. [L0474, 2020-26]

An AI tool can expand an initial product idea list into a longer list of candidates, but the starting list should already contain at least 10 ideas. [L0474, 2020-26]

The product idea list is an ongoing working document, not a one-time task. Keep adding ideas when they come up in daily life rather than stopping at the first 10 entries. [L0474, 2020-26]

**Step: compare competitor products before choosing how to differentiate**

1. Buy two or more units of the leading competitor products and test them yourself. [L1155, 2021-22]
2. Record each weakness you find, such as poor heat retention, awkward handles or excessive size. Each weakness is a possible point of differentiation. [L1155, 2021-22]

### Three-criteria filters

Several sources give a short filter of three traits. They overlap, but each one stresses a different point. Use them together.

| Source | Three criteria | Ids |
|---|---|---|
| one source | Solves a clear problem, looks new or unique, cannot be bought in a typical local store | [L0706, 2020+] |
| one source | Solves a clear problem, sits in an evergreen niche, has broad mass appeal | [L1251, 2024+] |
| one source | Stands out from common alternatives through a different angle or feature, solves a clear problem, appeals to a large audience | [L0950, 2023] |
| one source 2026 | High emotional demand, strong margins, wide audience | [L0491, 2026] |
| one source | Problem-solving, actionable, visually interesting (wow factor) | [L0209, 2023] |

- A product that fails any of the checks in the "stands out, solves, large audience" filter is harder to sell even with good ads. [L0950, 2023]
- A product that passes the "solves, unique, not local" filter is a good candidate for dropshipping and scaling. [L0706, 2020+]

### Problem-solving versus wow factor

- Shift product research from searching for winning products to searching for winning problems. Identify the problem, issue, state of mind or dream outcome of the customer, then find a product that solves it. [L0388, 2024+; L1184, 2023+]
- Problem-solving products are the preferred type. Wow-factor products are also viable if you add clear lifestyle or experience benefits. Both types should be checked for a real demand signal before testing. [L0335, 2023]
- Prefer problem-solving products over novelty products that only have a wow effect. Customers need problem-solving products rather than just wanting them, which supports longer-lasting sales. [L0363, 2022+]
- Problem-solving products usually outlast pure wow-factor products because a group of buyers already looks for a solution. The same product can often be pitched through several marketing angles, which can revive an older product. [L0708, undated]
- Problem-solving products usually keep selling longer than novelty products, which fade once the wow factor is gone. Novelty items such as a galaxy projector tend to die out faster. [L0693, 2024]
- Products that are only unique or novel, such as simple toys and gadgets, can produce a short burst of sales. They are hard to scale and keep going long-term because they solve little real need. [L0706, 2020+]
- Consider leaving a product that has a strong wow factor but solves no real problem. Such products often get views without converting. A product with a clear use typically converts better. [L0501, 2022]
- A product works when the problem is specific and the ad shows the problem and the solution quickly. Choose products that solve a clear pain point for a defined market, and use a strong marketing angle to make a simple product stand out. [L0638, 2023]
- A winning product should feel new to the viewer, meaning they have not seen it on Amazon, in ads or on YouTube. On Facebook and TikTok the creative must stop the scroll, so the product needs an immediate attention-grabbing hook. [L0708, undated]
- On Facebook a product can also work if the audience can easily relate to the problem or concept it represents. Pure wow-factor products are riskier than products that combine attention with a clear use. [L0708, undated]
- Products that solve a clear problem and visually stop people while scrolling must make the stop themselves, because the product has to create it. [L0692, 2023]
- Choose products by the specific problem they solve for a defined customer, not by what the seller personally likes or would buy. A clearly stated problem makes ad copy and the offer easier to write. [L0549, undated]
- Find products by identifying a problem people face in their country or season, rather than by chasing trending winning products. A clear problem is easier to communicate in ads, for example a product that keeps people warm in a cold winter. [L0562, undated]
- Products that solve a clear problem with very low time delay, effort and sacrifice for the customer are easier to sell, for both the offer and the ads. [L0549, undated]
- Products with fast, visible results are easier to sell than products that need long, effortful routines. A spray tan that takes about 10 minutes is an example, compared with hours of sun exposure to get the same look. [L0549, undated]
- A product can sell without a wow factor if a strong community or culture surrounds it, such as pet owners or hobby groups. Community strength is a signal worth weighing when choosing a niche. [L0549, undated]
- Products with a visible, demonstrable effect are easier to show in a short video. A water bottle that shows its temperature and keeps drinks hot or cold for about 12 hours was praised in TikTok reviews and later sold on Amazon. Confirm the product solves a real everyday need, because a wow factor alone may not sustain sales. [L0706, 2020+]
- Problem-solving products that people need on an ongoing basis are a strong category. A specialised knee brace is an example: it was sold only in the US with credit card and PayPal and reached about EUR 3,000 revenue per day at an early stage. [L0660, 2019+]
- Products with a clear problem solution and a fast, simple buying decision work best. Choose products that buyers can decide on quickly at first sight, since first-time buyers rarely accept a complex or high-priced product. Products that need more explanation or cost more are harder to validate with cold traffic. [L0661, undated]
- Products that solve a problem, such as cold-weather leggings in winter, tend to sell well during their season. [L1185, 2023]
- Problem-solving products sell without a strong wow factor in health, wealth, happiness and relationship niches. During spy-tool research, check which problem a product solves and what wow factor it offers, because the problem size shows how large the market can be and which complementary products fit. [L0871, 2021+]
- Entertainment is also a problem a product can solve, for example through a solid toy. The niche itself matters less than the product, so follow a structured product research process instead of chasing a specific niche. [L0694, 2024]
- For products that are attractive and have high perceived value, lifestyle pictures and UGC do most of the selling. Investing in these images is worthwhile. [L0693, 2024]
- Build products and ads around desires people already have, or around rising trends, instead of trying to create demand for an unwanted need. Riding a popular trend is far cheaper than creating a new habit. [L1261, undated]
- When researching products, first identify the problem being solved and the benefits or use cases. Upgraded versions of older winning products and products with multiple use cases are strong candidates. [L0788, undated]
- Choose products that solve a problem or have a visual element that makes a person stop scrolling, because the product itself has to create the stop. [L0692, 2023]

**Conflict: wow factor as a preference**

- one source's full checklist lists a wow factor (new, innovative or upgraded) as a preference, while the same checklist, and most other sources, put problem-solving first. Use the wow factor as a tie-breaker when the problem is already clear, not as the reason to choose a product. [L1174, undated; L0335, 2023; L0363, 2022+]

### Upgraded versions and differentiation

- An upgraded version of an existing solution, such as a new design or an improved model for the same problem, can sell at a much higher price and scale. In health niches, new variations of older solutions repeatedly succeed because buyers keep looking for better results. [L0871, 2021+]
- In a neck-pain niche, an upgraded version of a supplier product that cost about $50 to $60 on AliExpress or Alibaba was sold at about $244 by another seller. A better design for the same problem can support a much higher price than the basic version. [L0871, 2021+]
- If a product has weak reviews but the same function exists in a better variation, source the better variation from the same supplier and sell that instead. The same function with better quality is a stronger product. [L0693, 2024]
- When a product in a niche is widely available, aim for quality above the average brand. When a common product has clear weaknesses, such as poor heat retention, bad handles or excessive size, each identified weakness is a possible point of differentiation. [L1155, 2021-22]
- Creating a private-label version of a main product without differentiation makes the store just another seller of the same item, which competes on price alone. [L1155, 2021-22]
- Customizing a product with your own creatives and messaging is recommended from the start, even though launch takes longer. Copying a product one-to-one launches faster but offers less differentiation. [L1187, 2026]
- A product that is customizable with your own creatives and messaging gives control over the ads, even if it takes longer to launch. Speed to launch is traded against control and uniqueness. [L1187, 2026]
- Product research should favour products that can be customised with your own creatives and messaging, even if launch takes about one to two weeks longer before the first product can go live. [L1187, 2026]
