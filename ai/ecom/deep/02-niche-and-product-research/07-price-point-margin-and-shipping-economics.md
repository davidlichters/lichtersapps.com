## Price Point, Margin and Shipping Economics

### Minimum Price: The $200 Floor

- Products in a store should average at least $200, and this minimum has been used since the program began, so do not sell items priced below it (2025). [L0667, 2025]
- High-ticket products start at $200 or higher (2025). [L0278, 2025]
- Focus only on categories where most products are priced at $200 or more, so each sale leaves enough profit for a sustainable model. [L0012, undated]
- Only consider products priced above $200, ideally higher, because lower-priced items usually leave too little profit to cover ads and fees (2020). [L0961, 2020]
- The minimum product price to sell is about $200. Within a niche, carve out a higher tier rather than selling cheap versions, for example studio lighting sets at $400 or more instead of $100 sets (2021). [L0801, 2021]
- Do not list products under $200 unless they are add-ons or accessories, because low-priced products rarely produce enough margin to justify ad spend (2025). [L0837, 2025]
- Sort a supplier's catalog by price and list only products above the set threshold. Products under $200 are usually not worth listing because their margins are too low (2024). [L0965, 2024]
- Core products should cost at least $200, and ideally more. The lesson's stores average above $1,000. Products below $200 can still be sold, but they should not be the products that paid traffic is bought for. [L1147, 2020-26]
- Cheap, very common products such as phone cases look easy to sell but face huge competition and many variations, so they usually earn little per order. Easy-looking demand is not a reason to choose a product. [L1147, 2020-26]
- Inexpensive items such as decorative pillows, ornaments and regular pillows are almost never a good niche for dropshipping. [L0766, 2020-26]
- Avoid the cheapest commodity segment of a niche (example: basic ceiling fans at $20 to $60) and focus on the premium segment (example: $300 and up). The premium segment has large demand and enough margin, and cheap items are not worth the time (2018). [L1085, 2018]
- Minimum price for a niche product is about $200, with no maximum. Higher-priced products are acceptable if supplier quality and pricing are right (2018). [L1109, 2018]
- Minimum price used as a filter is $200 or more. This floor must be combined with the target demographic and not used on its own. [L1518, 2020-26]
- A niche passes the price point test only when its average selling price is above $200. Niches below $200 move to the bottom of the research list. [L1150, 2020-26]
- The average price point of a niche's products should be at least $200. A niche with a lower average fails the price check even when demand and seasonality pass. [L0451, 2020-26]
- Half or more of the first-page search results for a product should be priced above $200. [L1092, 2022]
- If all candidate products in a list are under $100, look for higher-priced products within the niche, or niche up to a broader category that contains a higher price range (2019). [L1110, 2019]
- Products with an average retail price under about $200 are not recommended, even with search volume above 200,000, because the margin cannot cover shipping, fees, fixed costs and ad spend (2020). The lesson framed this as a room-for-profit issue, not a niche problem. [L0358, 2020]
- For small, light products shipped with standard parcel carriers such as UPS or FedEx, a selling price of at least about $200 is the minimum threshold. [L1179, 2020-26]

**Conflicting thresholds for niche ideas (when each applies):**
- For generating niche ideas, the average sale price must be $500 or higher to qualify as viable for dropshipping. [L0024, undated]
- For a niche to count as high-ticket, target an average product price of $500 or more. This attracts affluent buyers with more purchasing power and creates viable margins. [L0159, 2020-26]
- The $200 floor remains the price check applied to a listed niche. [L0451, 2020-26; L1150, 2020-26]

### Average Price: What to Measure and How

- Calculate average prices from new products only, excluding used items. Leave out shipping costs when shipping is not included in the listed price. [L1279, 2021]
- Ignore auctions and auction-based prices. Include only products at fixed prices that are new and currently for sale; eBay fixed-price new sales count. (2024) [L0276, 2024]
- Use the products on the first page of Google Shopping results for the niche term, since these are the most popular products and their prices give a usable average. [L0941, 2022]
- Use the first page of Google Shopping results. A few cheap, lower-quality competitors are acceptable because you sell the better, higher-priced products (2021). [L0469, 2021]
- Look at paid results first and weigh them more heavily than organic results, though organic prices also help. [L0671, 2021] (older: 2021, identical text)
- Organic first-page results should show an average of at least $200. There is no maximum price, but very high average order values bring more phone orders (2024). [L0806, 2024]
- Do not filter Google Shopping results by a price floor such as $200 when estimating a niche's average price. Default results show the most popular products, and a price filter distorts demand research (2021). [L0853, 2021]
- Find average prices from the default search results, not from price filters, because filters change which products appear as the most popular ones (2021). [L1112, 2021]
- Do not apply a price filter such as "over 500" when researching on Google Shopping. It changes which products show as most popular. Keep the unfiltered first page for research (2021). [L1279, 2021]
- When one product type spans a huge price range (example: sofas from $200 to $20,000), do not mix price segments. Change the search term (example: leather sofa or three-seater sofa) instead of using the sidebar price filter (2024). [L0806, 2024]
- When a niche has many products with very wide price ranges, add a qualifying keyword to the search to narrow results to the desired range rather than relying on sidebar price filters (2023). [L0855, 2023]
- Price variation inside a niche is normal. Sofas may range from about $100 to $20,000, so keep the selected items in the range where the brand and product mix is strongest (2020). [L0967, 2020]
- When a category has two price tiers, such as standard $1,500 sofas and $30,000 custom Italian leather sofas, pick one tier for the store. Choose the lower tier for simpler customer service, or the higher tier if you accept more service work (2021). [L0469, 2021]
- Estimate prices from several retailer sites and categories, not from a single product page. A rough estimate that reflects the range you see is enough for the decision. [L1329, 2021-22]
- Judge a niche by the typical average price across several retailers and categories, not by a single product. Individual products can sit above or below the target. [L1329, 2021-22]
- To find retailers for price research, use the sponsored ads and scroll down the main Google results for more store sites. One website does not show the full price range of a niche. [L1329, 2021-22]
- Keep a few high-priced outlier competitors in the average if their products still fit the niche. Deluxe versions that cost much more do not disqualify a niche (2023). [L0970, 2023]
- Include outliers only when they are relevant products you would list. If an outlier is a pricing error, exclude it. [L1283, 2023]
- Exclude price outliers that are obvious data errors, such as a $23,746 dresser caused by a misplaced decimal or a multiplication bug. Real products with genuine prices stay in the average (2021). [L0834, 2021]
- A supplier's shipping cost from offshore sources can distort average price. Fix this by refining the keyword, not by adding shipping to the average. [L1283, 2023]
- Products priced well above the calculated range can still be sold in the store, and suppliers offering higher-priced items raise profit margin. The average price remains the main pass criterion for a niche. [L1150, 2020-26]
- Average prices are estimated from what you see. Exact calculation is not needed at this stage. [L1329, 2021-22]
- Price research for a broad niche: 1) search the main keyword and look at the most popular products on the first page of Google results; 2) if you plan to sell only a subtype, price research that subtype; 3) if you plan to sell all types, average the prices across them (2021). [L0968, 2021]

#### Procedure: Average price from Google Shopping

1. Search Google for the main keyword a potential customer would use for the product. [L0358, 2020]
2. Look at the first page of the Google Shopping results. [L0358, 2020]
3. Calculate the average price across those results. This average approximates what most buyers are willing to spend, because top results reflect the most popular products. [L0358, 2020]

#### Procedure: one source's Shopping-tab check

1. Open Google, search the product type, and click the Shopping tab. [L0458, 2021-22]
2. Read the listed prices and scroll through the results pages. [L0458, 2021-22]
3. Use the price filter with the ranges Up to $500, $500 to $1,000, $1,000 to $3,000 and Over $3,000 to see how many listings sit in the high-ticket range. [L0458, 2021-22]
4. Calculate the average price of the listings and check it is at least $800. The lesson's generator example averaged about $1,000 or more. [L0458, 2021-22]
5. Mark the price criterion as yes only if the high-ticket range is well covered. [L0458, 2021-22]

#### Procedure: Estimating average price with Google Shopping

1. Search the broad market idea or a specific product type in Google. [L1329, 2021-22]
2. Make sure results come from your target selling country. If you live elsewhere, use a VPN and set the location to that country. [L1329, 2021-22]
3. Review the Google Shopping ads (product image, title, price), which appear at the top or side of results, and open the Shopping tab for a dedicated page. [L1329, 2021-22]
4. Use the Shopping filters to narrow prices to your target range. [L1329, 2021-22]
5. Note the products that fall within your target price range, and note the product types you see (for example drawers or turbos) for later research. [L1329, 2021-22]
6. Open retailer websites from the ads and shopping results, browse their categories, and note products above and below your target range. [L1329, 2021-22]
7. Skip low-ticket categories that do not fit your price target. [L1329, 2021-22]
8. Estimate one average price per idea from the prices you saw. Exact calculation is not needed. [L1329, 2021-22]
9. Repeat for each of the five ideas. This takes about 10 to 20 minutes for all five. [L1329, 2021-22]

#### Procedure: Price test for a high-ticket product

1. Search the product name on Google Shopping. [L1281, 2022]
2. Check the price spread of the results. [L1281, 2022]
3. Pass the product only if at least half of the results are priced at $200 or more. Products with mostly $20 to $50 results fail, even if they sell. [L1281, 2022]

#### Price filter brackets (conflicting sources, when each applies)

- one source's Shopping-tab check uses the brackets Up to $500, $500 to $1,000, $1,000 to $3,000 and Over $3,000 to check the high-ticket range. [L0458, 2021-22]
- one source's general price check uses the Shopping preset brackets Up to $100, $100 to $250, $250 to $600 and Over $600 to see whether results reach the target range. [L1329, 2021-22]
- On the Shopping results page, the Product rating filter's "4 and up" option narrows listings to better-rated products. [L1329, 2021-22]

#### Tool-based price analysis
- Before automated research tools, average prices were gathered manually: search the niche keyword on Google, open the Shopping tab and read the listed prices. [L1150, 2020-26]
- The research tool's average uses the interquartile range, a statistical method that removes outliers such as very expensive items and accessories in keyword searches. The displayed range is therefore narrower than the full set of prices found. [L1150, 2020-26]
- Example: chandeliers had an average of $305.49 from 33 products, with a range of $44.48 to $999 after outliers were removed. Raw listings such as $1,800, $2,730, $4,400 and $4,999 were excluded. [L1150, 2020-26]
- Judging a niche by individual high or low listings instead of the filtered average gives a misleading picture. Accessories and extreme outliers distort the impression of what a niche sells for. [L1150, 2020-26]
- The price panel at the bottom of the research tool's results shows three figures: the average price, the number of products the average is based on, and the price range after outliers are removed. For example, chandeliers showed an average of $305.49 from 33 products, with a range of $44.48 to $999. [L1150, 2020-26]
- The price analysis in the research tool checks a niche's average price against the price-point criteria taught in the Blueprint testing module. [L1075, 2025+]
- AI tools such as ChatGPT often give pricing data that does not match live Google Shopping prices. Verify a few products manually before using AI for pricing, and drop a niche that fails at the rough filter stage (2024). [L0471, 2024]
- To estimate a product's selling price, check what similar items sell for on eBay and in Google Shopping results. Use those observed prices as the planning sale price before running margin calculations, for example a cheap phone case at about $10 and a paddleboard at about $1,000. [L1147, 2020-26]
- Judge a Shopping price against local retail as a ceiling check. Example: a vacuum canister sourced from AliExpress was priced around 37 pounds (about 45 dollars) in UK retail, compared with 20 to 40 dollars on Amazon. [L1186, 2022]
