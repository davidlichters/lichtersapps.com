### Likes, Shares and Comments as Filters

Engagement thresholds differ by tool and by product age. Use the row that matches your filter setup.

| Setting | Values | When it applies | Ids |
|---|---|---|---|
| Winning Hunter-style Facebook filter | Likes 500+, Comments 150+, Shares 100+ | Ad spy search with Post Creation Date last one month and a fixed country list | [L0636, undated] |
| Peak Star or Droppy Spy starting point | 300 to 500 likes, 50 to 100 shares | General Facebook spy research | [L0708, undated] |
| Flexible lower threshold | 200 to 250 likes | Some products with these numbers stayed profitable; lower the number based on search results | [L0708, undated] |
| DropiSpy starting thresholds | 250 to 300 likes, 300 shares, 300 comments | Starting point in DropiSpy | [L0335, 2023] |
| First-month product | 100 to 200 likes | Good signal when the store is new and the ads are scaling | [L0335, 2023] |
| Thin results | Lower from 1,000 likes to 500, or even 250 likes | Products with many likes are easy to miss; widen the date range to about 45 days | [L0335, 2023] |

- Products with a large number of likes are easy to miss. If filters return too few products, lower the thresholds and widen the date range to about 45 days. [L0335, 2023]
- Likes and comments can be bought, so do not rely on engagement numbers alone. Check the comment section to see if buyers are real and whether they complain about quality. [L0708, undated]
- Use the comment section to judge genuine demand. People tagging friends on an ad is a strong sign of real engagement. Repetitive comments or fake-looking profiles suggest bought engagement, and the ad should be discounted. [L1174, undated]
- Engagement without sales is a weaker signal. A competitor ad shows market fit when its comments contain buying questions and people tag friends to buy. [L0363, 2022+]
- Positive comments such as buyers asking about the product, sharing reviews and tagging friends signal real demand. Products with strong comments can be ranked higher. [L0708, undated]
- A product with many positive comments about the concept, buyers asking for details, people tagging friends and reviews from real buyers is a good sign. Comments praising the concept or saying buyers want the product show real demand. [L0636, undated]
- Many repeated short comments of 2 to 3 words posted by different accounts are a red flag, because bots may have added them. Ignore such comment sections as evidence of demand. [L0636, undated]
- Comments that criticize product quality are a red flag and should lead you to drop the product. [L0636, undated]
- Compare the engagement and comments of a competitor's winning ad across a second tool, such as PPSpy, Shop Hunter or DropiSpy. [L0335, 2023]

### Page Age and Advertiser Quality

- An older Facebook page is not automatically a warning sign. If a page created years earlier is adding many active ads quickly, for example rising from about 30 to about 57 active ads in one day, the product may still be worth testing. [L0335, 2023]
- Page age of a product's store is a soft guideline, often cited as four to six months. An older page is not an automatic deal breaker. A page older than a year that runs high-scale ads may have a well-trained pixel that is harder to compete with. [L0361, 2024]
- Competitor Facebook page age does not matter much for product selection, because pages and ad accounts get banned often and are replaced. Judge a product by its active ads and store, not by the advertiser's age. [L1173, undated]
- A product that was popular a year or two earlier can return. Check its current ad activity instead of dismissing it because of its age. [L1176, 2023]
- A declining percentage in AdLib may indicate the advertiser's account was banned and recently restarted. [L0209, 2023]
- Avoid products when the competitor is a general store testing many products and the active ad count for the specific product is unclear. Without a clear count, you cannot tell whether the competitor is scaling it or profitable. [L1174, undated]

### Store and Competitor Tracking

- Peakster has a store tracking feature showing a competitor store's revenue, last orders, and each last-sold product with its name and image, which reveals winning products. [L0561, 2023+]
- To confirm a store found through Peak Star store research: [L0335, 2023]
  1. Check the store's active ads and scaling pattern in the Meta Ad Library.
  2. Open the About section of the store's page and read the page creation date.
  3. Find the same winning ad in a second tool, such as PPSpy, Shop Hunter or DropiSpy, and compare its engagement and comments.
- Use a competitor's store to find more candidates: [L1161, 2024+]
  1. Open the advertiser's page in the Facebook Ad Library.
  2. Note the other products they currently run ads for.
  3. Screen each new product the same way: count active ads, check ad age, check the store, and infer the target country.
- When one competitor store sells several products, the product with the most active ads is usually the one they are scaling. Use that product's ads as the reference for your own test. [L1176, 2023]
- Each competitor's ad activity in the Ad Library: open the competitor's Facebook page, search its name in the Ad Library, and check whether it is scaling. [L0895, 2021+]
- Recheck ad counts after a few days to confirm the ads are still running. [L1173, undated]
- Check whether the store runs other products with many active ads, which shows what it is testing. [L1173, undated]
- Peak Star-style tracking is used to monitor a competitor's selling pace on a specific product. [L0950, 2023]
- Shop Hunter confirms a product is actually selling before it is tested with ads. [L1108, 2023]

### Competitor Screening Workflows

**Product screening workflow (Ad Library)** [L1161, 2024+]
1. Search the product in the Facebook Ad Library and count the active ads.
2. Skip the product if it has fewer than about 50 active ads or only inactive ads.
3. Check the advertiser's store: offer, price, and whether it is a product you would test.
4. Infer the targeted country from the store currency and language.
5. Find the supplier product via AliExpress using the camera (image) search.
6. Add the product to the test list with the Ad Library link and the store link.

**Product research procedure in the Facebook Ad Library** [L1172, 2023+]
1. Open a product list sorted by performance or by number of active ads.
2. Open each product's store link and note the product, price, offers and bundles.
3. Open the Facebook Ad Library, find the product or store, and count the ads marked as active.
4. Check when the latest ad was started or edited; recent activity shows that money is still being spent.
5. Note the countries targeted in the ads.
6. Review the ad copy and videos, and save videos for reference.
7. Compare the store's problem and solution messaging, description and offers with what the ads promise.
8. Add the product to the research list with its store link and its Ad Library link.

**Product research in the Meta Ad Library, in order** [L1173, undated]
1. Search for the product or brand.
2. Filter for ads that are currently active.
3. Sort results by number of active ads.
4. Keep only products with more than 100 active ads, since fewer usually means weak demand.
5. Open the competitor's store and check its offer, product description and landing page.
6. Check whether the store runs other products with many active ads, which shows what it is testing.
7. Recheck the ad counts after a few days to confirm the ads are still running.

**Product research with Afterlib** [L1171, 2024+]
1. Sort the Afterlib product list by active ads.
2. Keep only products with more than 100 active ads in the Facebook Ad Library.
3. Open the product page and check whether the product clearly solves a problem and whether the store has room for a better page.
4. Search the product in the Facebook Ad Library and confirm its active ad count and the date the ads started.
5. Check which countries the ads run in and choose the test market from there.

**Product research process for Facebook and Meta** [L1174, undated]
1. Choose an ad spy tool and set its filters.
2. List all potential products the filtered results show, aiming for at least 10 and ideally 10 to 15 candidates.
3. For each product, open the competitor page and find its Facebook Ad Library page.
4. Count the active ads for that specific product on the Ad Library page.
5. Record the data in the product research sheet: product name, competitor store link, Ad Library link, active ads, AliExpress link, COGS, competitor selling price and break-even ROAS.
6. Use the recorded COGS and selling price to calculate break-even ROAS and rank the candidates.

**Competitor count per product (scaling analysis)** [L1174, undated]
- Only count active ads that belong to the specific product when doing the scaling analysis, excluding ads for other products on the same competitor page. Record the adjusted active ad count in the sheet for each candidate. [L1174, undated]
- On a competitor's Facebook Ad Library page, apply the Active ads filter before counting. Count only the ads for the specific product and subtract ads for other products the advertiser tests on the same page. [L1174, undated]

**Facebook Ad Library competitor research (full workflow)** [L0638, 2023]
1. Open the Ad Library and log into your Facebook account.
2. Select the country, for example United States.
3. Select All ads.
4. Enter a discount or offer keyword in the search field, for example percent off.
5. Open the filters and set Active ads so only currently running ads appear.
6. Set the Impression date range to a window of two to three days for recent scaling products, or to a window of about one to three months (for example 1 to 31 March) for proven older products.
7. Scroll the results and note the products that show a high number of ads using the same creative.
8. Open each candidate's ads, copy the ad link, and check the advertiser's full ad count in the Ad Library.
9. Record each candidate and rank products against your product criteria before testing them one by one.

**Find long-running products** [L0638, 2023]
1. Open the Ad Library and select the United States (or another chosen country) as the country.
2. Select All ads.
3. Enter keywords such as percent off, free shipping, or get yours today.
4. Set the Active ads filter.
5. Set the Impression date filter to a past window of one to three months, for example 1 March to 31 March, to find products whose ads have been running a long time.
6. Review the results and look for products whose ads are still active from that window.
7. Check whether the seller is still scaling, and note the product if the ad is still running.

**Verify a candidate's ads and store** [L0638, 2023]
1. Click View ads, Go to page, or the three-dot menu and Copy ad link on the candidate ad.
2. Open a new browser tab and paste the link to see the page that runs the ad.
3. Go back to the Ad Library, select United States, then All ads.
4. Search for the advertiser's page name to see the total number of ads that page runs.
5. Count how many ads use the same creative and copy to judge whether the product is being scaled or only tested.
6. Open the store and check the landing page and product proposition before deciding to test.

**Find products to test through the Ad Library as a buyer** [L1108, 2023; L0693, 2024]
- Click an ad on Facebook or Instagram, visit the advertiser's website, add a product to cart, start checkout and leave. Repeat, so the platform shows more ads from that niche. The same approach works on TikTok. [L1108, 2023]
- Click an ad that appears in the feed, land on the product page, add the product to cart and go to checkout. Expect Meta to show more ads for similar products afterwards, which signals a market. [L0693, 2024]

**Shortlisting volume** [L1171, 2024+]
- Examples from the lesson: a magnetic absorption product with 686 active ads and a clothing item with 672 active ads were both kept for testing. A home decor item with 390 active ads and a product with 385 active ads were also shortlisted. [L1171, 2024+]
- A product with 1,000 or more active ads is a strong trust signal that it works, so it is worth testing. [L1171, 2024+]

**Trend research on Facebook and TikTok, as taught** [L0895, 2021+]
1. Check spy tools (Peak Star, Droppy Spy) and the ad platforms for products that worked in the last month.
2. Check which products worked in the same period of the previous year, since seasonal products that worked last year often work again because they tie to events.
3. Check products from two years earlier in the same season for ideas that could be improved.
4. Build a list of 10 to 15 candidates with basic criteria and margin checks.
5. Run competitor analysis on each candidate.

**Products that worked before** [L1108, 2023]
1. Filter products with your conditions for roughly the last six weeks.
2. Go through all matches until none are left.
3. Go back to previous years and look for products that worked then and could work again now.

**Trend example** [L1176, 2023]
- A trend product rose from 618 ads on 27 November to 1,423 ads on 10 December, a sharp jump within about two weeks. A rapid rise in ad count like this signals that demand is growing. [L1176, 2023]

### Market and Country Checks

- Check competitors in the Meta Ad Library filtered by your target country before launching there. If many advertisers already run the same ad in that market, expect higher competition. [L0692, 2023]
- Searching by country, for example United States, reveals products that can be tested in another market. [L0678, 2023]
- Checking competitors in a new country with the Facebook Ad Library: [L1158, 2023]
  1. Open the Facebook Ad Library.
  2. Set the country filter to the target country.
  3. Search a broad keyword for the category around the product, such as women's care or healthcare.
  4. Search the exact product name.
  5. Note which brands run ads for the product, what they sell, how their ads are built, and on which platform they run them.
  6. If the product has no scaling competitors in that country, the market may be open; if strong brands already sell it, study their ads and offers before entering.
- When a product is over-run in one market, look at the same product sold in another physical form, such as a drink mix versus drops. That form usually has its own video ads from other sellers, which gives a fresh pool of creatives to study. [L0901, 2026]
- Markets to avoid for the US research filters: Exclude the European and UK countries GB, DK, NL, DE, NO, SE and FR from the country filter when researching the US, so non-US ads do not crowd the results. [L0772, 2026]
- Set Countries to the target markets in the spy tool, for example Australia and UK, with the Language filter left open. [L0708, undated]
- Countries used in the spy-tool filter for broad research: USA, Canada, Australia, UK and Germany, with Language set to English. [L0636, undated]
- Smaller markets such as Australia and New Zealand return a comparatively small library and can be used for a quicker overview; Germany and the UAE were also named as options. The United States produces a very large result set, so extra filters are needed. [L0638, 2023]
- Infer the targeted country from the store's currency and language. [L1161, 2024+]
- Choose the test market from the countries the ads run in. [L1171, 2024+]
- Home decor items are a good niche for picture-only ads, because they are visually self-explanatory and cheaper to advertise than video. [L0642, undated]

### Winning Hunter Meta Ad Library Filters

**Country, language and technology** [L0772, 2026]
- In the Winning Hunter Meta ad library search, set Countries to the US, set Technology to Shopify, and set Languages to English while excluding the other listed languages. This keeps results on the target market's native-language ads. [L0772, 2026]
- Leave the EU adspend filter unused when targeting the US, because it mainly covers ads running in Europe. [L0772, 2026]

**Store traffic** [L0772, 2026]
- The Store Traffic (monthly visits) filter helps find stores that are booming. Beginners without e-commerce or dropshipping experience should skip it, because high-traffic products are often crowded and harder to enter.

**Saved presets** [L0772, 2026]
- Winning Hunter's Meta filter panel includes saved presets such as Week's biggest winners, European winners, US winners, Dropship Ads, Advertorials, Native ads and Quiz Funnels. Clicking a preset loads its filter set, and Save current filters stores your own filter combination for reuse.

**Ad card data** [L0772, 2026]
- Winning Hunter ad cards show estimated spend, reach, average daily spend, estimated monthly store traffic and estimated product revenue. They also show how many duplicate copies of a creative exist, which helps judge whether an ad and its product are worth copying.

**Full product research procedure in Winning Hunter's Meta ad library** [L0772, 2026]
1. Set Countries to the US and exclude the European and UK countries.
2. Set Technology to Shopify.
3. Set Languages to English and exclude the other languages.
4. Leave the EU adspend filter unused.
5. Set the Ad copy length filter to find native ads, and set Video length to a long range to find VSLs.
6. Set Media type to Videos.
7. Leave Page type blank to see every funnel type.
8. Choose a niche (Health in the example) and leave Apps and Theme blank.
9. Set Days running to a minimum of 7.
10. Optionally use Store traffic (monthly visits) to find booming stores, but skip this step as a beginner.
11. Set Ad creation date to the last three days.
12. Sort by longest running or days running.
13. Open the most promising ad and check whether it is worth copying as a reference.

### Peak Star, Droppy Spy and AdSpy Facebook Filters

- Facebook spy filters in Peak Star or Droppy Spy: Post creation date set to the last 1 month; Countries set to the target markets, for example Australia and UK; Language left open; CTA set to Shop Now; Platform set to Shopify. If results are too few, widen the post creation date slightly and add more countries later. [L0708, undated]
- Ad spy search filters in the Winning Hunter style: Post Creation Date set to LAST ONE MONTH; Countries set to USA, CANADA, AUSTRALIA, UK and GERMANY; Language set to English; CTA set to SHOP NOW and BUY NOW; Likes 500+, Comments 150+, Shares 100+. [L0636, undated]
- If a product search returns no results, broaden the filters rather than adding more criteria. Keep the filter setup simple so viable products are not excluded. [L0639, 2023]
- Filter setting for conversion-led ads: Set CTA filters to the store's shop-related buttons, such as SHOP NOW and BUY NOW. [L0636, undated]

### Ad-Level Filters and Product Qualification

- Use the Ad Library and the TikTok spy data together to qualify products. [L0895, 2021+]
- Check which countries the ads run in before choosing a test market. [L1171, 2024+]
- Use specific keywords, narrow date windows and product-related ads to remove brand and non-product noise. [L0638, 2023]
- The method can produce a list of 15 to 25 potential products in one session. Rank them by criteria fit and test the strongest candidates first. [L0638, 2023]
- A product with no ad scaling signal yet, such as one with only a few ads, should not be treated as a winner based on its appearance alone. Wait for evidence from the Ad Library count or the ad running time before investing. [L0638, 2023]
- Do not choose products on gut feeling or because they look new and untapped. Pick products already advertised on the same ad platform and to the same audience you plan to use, because sales on a marketplace like AliExpress do not prove the product works on Meta. [L0335, 2023]
- When a product with many active ads suddenly drops to zero, check whether the seller stopped testing or switched products. [L1171, 2024+]
- Use the ad count, the store and the Ad Library history together. Judge the product by its active ads, not by the advertiser's page age. [L1173, undated]

### Adapting Proven Creatives

- When researching a source niche for ads, match the emotion or fear the ad triggers rather than the product category. For example, a hair-loss ad that makes viewers worry about visible decline can be a model for a skin product that addresses a similar self-image concern. [L0374, 2026]
- The second method adapts proven videos from a different niche that trigger the same emotion as your product, for example taking health-related winners for beauty or acne-related winners for skincare. It is recommended most, because it lets you copy a proven concept without copying a competitor's exact product. [L0374, 2026]

**Procedure: find proven videos in a similar niche with the Meta Ad Library (Method 2)** [L0374, 2026]
1. Pick a niche that triggers the same emotion as your product (for example, acne and skincare, or health and beauty).
2. Open the Meta Ad Library with the filters of the brand tool.
3. Set Language to English.
4. Set Countries to US and exclude other markets as needed.
5. Set Niche to Beauty or Skin Care.
6. Set Media type to Videos.
7. Set Days running to a minimum of 7.
8. Set Page Active Ads to a minimum of 200.
9. Set Ad creation date to the last 90 days.
10. Exclude Dutch, German, Norwegian, Swedish, Danish and French.
11. Sort by Longest running.
12. Turn off the one-ad-per-brand limit, which otherwise shows fewer results.
13. Open the best ads, check running days, duplicates, landing page and price, then download the video.
14. Send the video to Gemini or Claude with the analysis prompt, then adapt its structure, emotion and hook to your product.

- Winning-creative replication when you cannot order the product: [L0895, 2021+]
  1. Download the competitor video.
  2. Make small changes such as playback speed (for example 1.05 or 1.1).
  3. Test the altered version.

  Creatives that win with high CPC/CPM stability can be reused with minor changes, but the platform may penalize duplicate content.
- When a competitor has many creative ads, replicate the winning creative by ordering the product and producing custom content. This is the best first step before editing a competitor's video. [L0895, 2021+]
- A product already advertised by several competitors can still be tested if your creatives differ from the top ads everyone runs. Check what the leading ads show before producing anything. [L0901, 2026]
- When the leading product is over-run, look for the same product sold in another physical form. That form usually has its own video ads from other sellers. [L0901, 2026]
- Old Facebook winners and products from the same season two years earlier give ideas that can be improved. [L0895, 2021+]
