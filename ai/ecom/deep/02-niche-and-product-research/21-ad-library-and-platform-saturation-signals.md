### Ad-library and platform saturation signals

#### Reading ad counts

- Do not judge product saturation on ad count alone. Test ads first; only after running campaigns can you accurately assess market oversaturation. [L0209, 2023]
- Finding the same product across multiple competing stores running ads indicates market saturation, but this is not conclusive. Run actual ad campaigns before excluding a product. [L0209, 2023]
- Do not stop a product for being saturated if competitors' ads are actively scaling. A product is worth testing if competitor ads show it running at scale. If competitors are not scaling, look for a new competitor or a new product instead. [L0361, 2024]
- Checking competitor ad activity in the target market helps judge a product, because a product easily scaled in one market is often being scaled by competitors there too. [L0691, 2024]
- A product is not saturated until its market and targeting are saturated. The same product can keep selling by changing demographic, platform, country or marketing angle. [L0871, 2021+]
- The Ad Library is a search tool for count and duration. A product that many accounts already promote with the same video style is considered saturated. [L0706, 2020+]

#### Costs and timing

- When many advertisers run the same product, CPMs and CPAs rise over time as copies appear. A product that was unsaturated at launch can become expensive after 1 to 1.5 years. [L0692, 2023]
- Check how long a product has been circulating, using the earliest video dates on TikTok or ads. Products that have been around for years may already be saturated; newer products may still have room to grow. [L0706, 2020+]
- A product can stop performing when many competitors begin selling the same item, so check how many stores advertise it before testing. A product's early success does not guarantee long-term demand. [L1172, 2023+]
- Gap between competitive and uncompetitive products: a well-tested product might bring 20 to 50 sales per day at 5x to 10x ROAS, while a crowded product brings 1 to 2 sales per day. [L1158, 2023]
- Your pixel versus competitors' pixels: a product a competitor is scaling may not work for you because their pixel already has data on their buyers, while a new pixel has none. Early entry into a product with few competitors can give that seller a data advantage. [L0349, 2024]

#### Sales concentration and sellers

- A high concentration rate in Kalodata, such as 91.6% on a 24.99 USD study guide, means most sales come from a few sellers. Treat it as a warning and check how many creators drive the revenue before entering the niche. [L0354, 2026]
- Products with thousands of sales on AliExpress often already have a dedicated supplier and brand competing for the same buyers. Strong sales volume can signal a crowded market, not an easy opening. [L0706, 2020+]
- Visible sales by other sellers show demand. Competition is healthy in a market only if it is not overcrowded. Sellers can check which stores make the most sales in a niche and compare them. [L0995, 2023]
- Before creating an audience, check whether a niche has enough demand, which can be seen from other sellers doing well. Competition is good as long as it is not too heavy, since many sellers suggest demand exists. [L0999, 2023]

#### Procedures

**Competitor check before testing a product** [L0361, 2024]

1. Find competitors running ads for the same product, for example in the Meta Ad Library.
2. Check whether those ads are running at scale and for how long.
3. Review their creatives and landing pages.
4. If the competitor ads are scaling, test the product. If not, look for a new competitor or a new product.

**Checking whether a product is unique or saturated for a country** [L0788, undated]

1. Search the product on Amazon; more than 20 listings suggests the product has been on the market for a long time.
2. Search the product name in the Facebook Ad Library or a spy tool, filtered by the target country.
3. If multiple stores run ads for the same product in that country, and some ads have run for about a year, the product is already established.
4. Use these checks together to measure how many competitors and ads exist and how long they have been running.

**Rip-check a product before testing it** [L0892, 2026]

1. Search the product by name or image in Magic AI, or open it from the TikTok Shop product list.
2. Review which stores and advertisers already sell or run the same or a similar product.
3. If few or no results appear, the product is not saturated and can be tested.
4. If many sellers already run it, skip the product.

- Skipping a product that is already heavily ripped saves roughly $20 to $100 in ad spend per test. The lesson presents this as the main financial reason to run the check before every test. [L0892, 2026]

**Competition check before launch** [L1158, 2023]

- Search the Facebook Ad Library and the TikTok ads library (TikTok Ads, abbreviated as "pp ads" in the lesson) to find big players selling the same product. See how long and how heavily they run it.
- Check how many competitors run the product in the target country before entering it.

**AdSpy-style competitor check** [L0690, 2022+]

- Search the Meta Ad Library with product keywords and count the number of advertisers, or use an ads spy tool, to gauge how much competition a product has.

**Keyword count for competitor search** [L0636, undated]

- Keep 10 to 15 keywords per product for the competitor search: product name, name variations, name subsets, niche keywords, product-related keywords and ad copy keywords from the ad spy research.

### Competitor and engagement thresholds on Meta and TikTok

#### Facebook and Meta

| Signal | Threshold | Meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Competing sellers | 5 to 6 | Acceptable | [L0895, 2021+] |
| Competing sellers | 7 or more | Warning sign; product drops lower in the ranking | [L0895, 2021+] |
| Established brands scaling | 2 or 3 | Avoid; they can scale 2x to 4x faster than a new seller | [L0895, 2021+] |
| Competitors with ads over ~5k likes | 7 or more | Heavy competition; drop or deprioritise (guideline, market size unknown) | [L0708, undated] |
| Competitors with ads over ~5k likes | 7 or more, together with 2 or more big brands | Saturated; mark as SATURATED / AVOID | [L1159, 2023+] |
| Competitors with ads over ~5k likes | More than 5 | Drop in the competitor check | [L0636, undated] |
| Competitors with ads over 5,000 likes | More than 7 | Avoid; choose a different product | [L1162, undated] |
| Competitors | 1 to 10 with fewer than 1k to 2k likes and room for improvement | Good test candidate; mark as GOOD PRODUCT / TEST | [L1159, 2023+] |
| Competitors | 1 to 10 with fewer than 2,000 likes and room to improve ads | Good product to test | [L1162, undated] |
| Small number of competitors with 1k to 2k likes | Few | Positive sign: product works without being saturated; reveals angles and copy | [L0708, undated] |

- Thresholds conflict on the number of heavy-engagement competitors: five in the competitor-check step [L0636, undated] and seven in the guideline and category rules [L0708, undated; L1159, 2023+; L1162, undated]. Use the stricter count of five when screening, and the seven rule as the saturation label. This is a reading of the sources, not a figure either source states.
- Two or more big brands selling the same product also marks a product for drop. [L0636, undated; L1159, 2023+]
- A product with a big brand promoting the same product should be dropped. [L0636, undated]

#### TikTok

- On TikTok, a product with 50,000 or more ad impressions shows active competition. Products with 1 million or more impressions in a single ad indicate strong winning creatives worth studying. [L0895, 2021+]
- On TikTok, a brand running a product for a long time with hundreds of millions of impressions usually means the niche is saturated. A brand with 343 million impressions and a launch in early 2021 was cited as an example of a saturated product. [L0895, 2021+]
- A TikTok product with one or two large brands already scaling and a logo on the product is a red alert. It is not a reason to ignore it if the market is large and you can offer a better product. [L0895, 2021+]
- Dropshipping competitors on Facebook: a product with 7 or more competitors whose ads have about 5k likes and 2 or more big brands selling it is saturated. [L1159, 2023+]

#### Creative lessons from saturated products

- A product that has already gone viral also shows which video styles work, which gives a roadmap for your own videos. Check saturation before copying a viral one, because a saturated product can still sell with a clear angle, but a product with a bad reputation or heavy overexposure becomes a weak bet. [L0657, undated]
- A face-care ice-cube product was filled with water and frozen, and was described as a six-figure product. A small test earlier had made a few thousand dollars, the margin was high at the time, and the product became saturated as many viewers recognised it. Viewers also said it was just an ice cube from the refrigerator, so the product must show a clear problem and solution to sell. [L0657, undated]

### Saturation is not a blocker

#### Principles

- Saturation is not a blocker. Competitors do not eliminate opportunity because new brands continuously appear, existing brands launch new product lines, and superior website optimisation and customer acquisition outperform lazy early entrants. [L0245, 2020]
- Competitors in a niche validate the market rather than eliminate opportunity. You can profit by bringing your product to a new market segment within an existing category. [L0037, 2026]
- Saturation is not a reason to reject a product, because many competing ads show that money is being made in that product. What decides success is your own creative, your angle and your execution. [L0347, 2026]
- A product with high potential has a large addressable audience. A cozy fall sock example was estimated at over 100 million potential buyers. The number of people who could buy is what matters, not whether a single competitor covers part of the market. [L0347, 2026]
- A product that has only recently appeared often sits in a large niche market, where competitors have reached only a small share so far. Niche markets often span 10 to 100 million potential people, so new entrants still have room to grow. [L0335, 2023]
- Some competition is a good sign that a niche has demand. Do not drop a niche just because many sellers are already in it. [L0963, 2022]
- Forty dropshipping competitors in a niche is a good sign of market viability. Many competing stores show that people are buying. [L0963, 2022]
- Generic-looking product names from suppliers are not a problem by themselves. What matters is that the niche passes the tests. Products that are not unique can still sell when many brands in the niche draw buyers. [L0964, 2023]
- Boring niches can sell well. Success often comes from finding an opportunity within brands and products in an apparently saturated industry. [L1277, 2020]
- Niches with dominant brands can still be entered when the margins work. Look for weaknesses that competitors do not address and do those things better. [L0664, 2020]
- A crowded niche with many products and a few hundred competing products can still work if brands are not oversold. [L0801, 2021]
- Low competitor counts for a single product are not the only sign of saturation. With tens of thousands of niches available, a new store does not need to avoid every niche that others also sell in. It must never copy a competitor's site. [L1274, 2018]
- Competition is healthy in a market only if it is not overcrowded. [L0995, 2023]
- A viable market needs some existing competition, because it proves people already buy that product online. A market with zero competitors usually has no online buyers and is not viable. [L0994, 2021-22]
- Competition in this model happens at product level, so a strong niche can still contain products with room. [L0854, 2022]
- A product's saturation can be worked around by a beginner or an experienced advertiser. A beginner should look for a proven product with active ads and then find a new angle or an audience competitors have not reached yet. [L1215, 2023+]

#### Proven demand with unproven competition

- Proven demand combined with unproven competition is the opportunity to look for. A product or language market with little competition can beat a larger, saturated market. [L1195, 2026]

#### Products with no competition

- A niche with no direct competition is acceptable only if the product type is actively sold elsewhere online. If the products are not sold by anyone on the internet, the market likely does not exist for good reason and should be avoided. [L0243, 2019]
- A brand's own pages: before investing in branded social pages for a planned store, check whether the niche is already crowded by similar businesses. A planned homebrewing store was dropped because it was judged too competitive, so the competitor check should come before the brand is built. [L0653, 2020-26]

### Saturation is a blocker

#### Warnings

- Competition is the biggest research criterion that can eliminate a product. [L0896, 2021-22]
- Avoid markets with too much competition, since that is one of the main reasons a niche fails. The goal is a middle ground with some competitors but not a saturated market. [L0994, 2021-22]
- Trending products are a signal to avoid rather than enter. Electric bikes passed the price and local-brand checks but were too competitive because they were a popular trending product. [L0458, 2021-22]
- Products that appear on public trend lists draw many store owners at once, so competition rises quickly. Choose a related product that is not on those lists instead. [L0853, 2021]
- A niche with too many paid advertisers for the same brands is harder to win through ads. [L0467, 2019]
- A niche with saturated competition is a poor choice for a first store. [L1057, 2020-26]
- Before launching, check whether the people who need the product are already served by a strongly established rival and what it would cost to win them. Products in fields dominated by entrenched brands require a clear, convincing advantage to succeed. [L1261, undated]
- Heavily tested categories such as books have very high competition. Copying the same creatives as many sellers makes it hard to stand out. Differentiate through the message, the avatar or the angle. [L1195, 2026]
- Two or three established brands already scaling a product on Facebook: avoid it. [L0895, 2021+]
- Visible high-ticket dropshipping stores signal crowded markets. Markets where high-ticket dropshipping stores are already visible usually have suppliers who approve dropshippers, but they also tend to be more competitive. Using visible dropshipping as the only filter means choosing the most crowded markets. [L0905, 2021-22]
- Products with many paid advertisers and the same brands are harder to win through ads. [L0467, 2019]

#### Blockers without differentiation

- Competition against an established brand can still work if you use a clearly different marketing angle. The risk is high when the angle is the same as theirs. [L0895, 2021+]
- A product that a competitor is scaling may not work for you because that competitor's pixel already has data on their buyers. [L0349, 2024]
- A product that is saturated in one market can work in a different market or segment. Rethink the target audience and angle rather than dropping the product entirely. [L0552, 2023]

### Differentiation for crowded products

#### Choose the lever

When a product is old or already strongly competed, differentiate through one of three factors: a different market or demographic, a different platform, or a different marketing angle or audience. Do not switch the product itself. [L1158, 2023]

| Lever | What changes | Example | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market or demographic | Target segment or country | A common product sold to a new niche | [L0552, 2023; L1158, 2023] |
| Platform | Channel where the product is run | A product peaked on one platform, still unique on another | [L1158, 2023] |
| Angle | Main benefit, same audience | Head massager: migraine relief vs productivity or sleep | [L1158, 2023] |
| Audience | Same product, different group | Harness for cats instead of dogs | [L1158, 2023] |
| Use case | Same product, different use | Dog shower spray reframed for pet washing | [L1158, 2023] |
| Buyer and problem | Narrow to one buyer's problem | Generic product repositioned to a niche with a specific problem | [L0639, 2023] |
| Feature | One visible difference | Blender with one feature top sellers lack | [L0657, undated] |
| Layered narrowing | Audience then features | Men only, then natural ingredients and strong masculine scents | [L0524, 2023] |

#### Market and audience

- A product that is saturated in one market can work in a different market or segment, such as a common product sold to a new niche. Rethink the target audience and angle rather than dropping the product entirely. [L0552, 2023]
- Product selection filters such as high order counts, low negative reviews and Amazon competition can be relaxed when the segment is different. A few negative reviews are acceptable if the rest is strong. [L0552, 2023]
- A saturated product can still work when you find a buyer group with a related concern that competitors ignore. Example: a relaxation-focused head massager could also speak to men worried about hair loss. A claimed link, such as scalp tension and hair loss, should be checked against studies before it is used in ads. [L0552, 2023]
- A commodity product can stand out in a crowded category by narrowing to one specific audience first, then narrowing again by ingredients or features that matter to that audience. Example: targeting men only, then using natural ingredients and strong masculine scents. [L0524, 2023]
- A competitive product can be made to work by narrowing it to one specific buyer and their problem, or by serving it to a different segment, such as dog owners instead of cat owners. A product that is generic in one niche can perform well when repositioned into a niche with a specific problem. [L0639, 2023]
- Changing the audience means selling the same product to a different group of people, such as cats instead of dogs for a harness. [L1158, 2023]
- Changing the marketing angle means keeping the audience but changing the main benefit. For a head massager, one seller focuses on migraine relief, while another can focus on productivity or sleep quality. [L1158, 2023]
- Differentiating by marketing angle or audience: keep the same product but target a different use case or customer group. Well-known example: a dog shower spray sold for car washing from 2017 to 2020; a store reframed it as a pet-washing product, opened a new audience and reportedly did 3,000 to 10,000 dollars per day. [L1158, 2023]
- For health and wellness products, most sellers target gym-goers. Differentiate by targeting people who have no time for exercise, such as desk workers, since time-saving is a usual selling point. [L1158, 2023]
- Build on the audience. Where the product is the same, pick the people who are not served: a common product sold to a new niche wins if its angle fits that niche. [L0552, 2023; L1158, 2023]

#### Platform

- Differentiating by platform: a product that has peaked and collapsed on one platform can still be unique on another. Check the Facebook Ad Library for scaling products, and test platforms such as Pinterest or Snapchat for products that are saturated on Facebook. [L1158, 2023]
- Bodysuit trend on TikTok: many dropshippers ran the same product in March and April 2023, then demand collapsed and few sellers kept selling it on TikTok. A brand still scaled it on Facebook, showing that a platform switch can open a new opportunity. [L1158, 2023]

#### Angle and creative

- Competition can be reduced in a crowded category such as blenders by showing one visible feature the top sellers lack. Show that difference in the first seconds of the video so viewers see why this product is different. [L0657, undated]
- A product is not saturated until its market and targeting are saturated. [L0871, 2021+]
- Your own creative, angle and execution decide success even when competitors run the same product. [L0347, 2026]
- Differentiate through the message, the avatar or the angle when many sellers run the same creatives. [L1195, 2026]

#### Product-level differentiators

- Narrow within the niche: competition occurs at product level, and crowded niches still contain products with low competition. Products that few competitors bid on are the easiest way in. [L0803, 2022]
- Brands and variants: the opportunity is in selling the brands and variants that big-box chains do not stock. [L0968, 2021]
- Hundreds of suppliers with different products suitable for online sale. [L1068, 2019]
- Market or niche with many products and brands: a broad niche with thousands of products from hundreds of companies leaves room. [L1002, 2019]
- Entering a dominant-brand niche: find weaknesses competitors do not address and do those things better. [L0664, 2020]
- Ideas at the product level for a new entrant: look at the product gap, not the niche gap. [L0249, 2022]

#### Unique mechanism

- A unique mechanism is a real attribute of the product, such as a proprietary technology, a special material or a distinctive design feature, that competing products do not have. A product with nothing unique is a commodity and is much harder to market. [L0465, 2021-22]
- If you make the product yourself, you usually already know its difference from competitors because of your research, planning and production process. Resellers need to dig into documentation and competitor products to find it. [L0465, 2021-22]
- A product can have one, two or three unique mechanisms, and one is enough. The mechanism you lead with should enable the product to deliver the primary promise, at least in part, so the copy hangs together. [L0465, 2021-22]

#### Generic products and brands

- Generic-looking product names from suppliers are not a problem by themselves; what matters is that the niche passes the tests. [L0964, 2023]
- Differentiation can come from brand presence: a new brand entering with a clear angle can take share from crowded products. [L0249, 2022]

#### Searching for new products

- A product with high saturation can still be tested when you change the product type. Do not switch the product itself when the lever is market or angle. [L1158, 2023]

### Pre-launch decision points

#### When saturation is not a blocker

- The product still sells when the market or targeting changes. [L0871, 2021+]
- The product has a large audience even if a few competitors cover part of it. [L0347, 2026]
- Competitors show money is being made. [L0347, 2026]
- A new product in a large niche has room. [L0335, 2023]
- Competitors are small, with 1k to 2k likes. [L0708, undated]

#### When saturation is a blocker

- Competitor ads are not scaling and no new angle, audience or platform is available. [L0361, 2024; L1158, 2023]
- Many established brands already scale the product. [L0895, 2021+]
- A product has many competitors with heavy engagement. [L0708, undated; L1159, 2023+]
- The first-page store test fails and the keyword or niche cannot be narrowed. [L1125, 2020-26]
