### Submitting for external verification

**Whether to submit**

- Before you submit a niche for an outside check, ensure that all worksheet data is complete, all future competitors are listed, and all suppliers are listed with their real contact information. [L0058, undated]
- Submitting before the worksheet is fully filled out wastes the reviewer's time and weakens the review. Complete all research data first. [L1080, 2020-26]
- If all four checks pass and you are confident in the data, you may proceed without outside review. [L0058, undated; L1080, 2020-26]
- If a niche passes all validation tests, proceed to store building without additional validation. Based on 20 years of dropshipping experience, completing these tests is sufficient to move forward with confidence. [L0058, undated]
- Before committing to a first niche, submit the shortlisted top niche ideas for an outside check. The second opinion helps confirm which niche has the highest chance of success. [L0584, 2020-26]
- Submit the niche for verification before building the store. [L0857, 2025] (2025)
- Verification is optional and can be used as many times as needed. Adding a new product type within an already verified niche does not require a new verification. [L0666, 2024] (2024)
- Verification is typically done after completing module two, where the niche tests are finished. [L0964, 2023] (2023)
- Run the demand and competition check before spending money on products or ads. [L0675, 2025] (2025)
- If you are unsure whether a niche is viable, submit the niche idea through the niche verification system before contacting suppliers. Use it when you have a candidate you want confirmed, not as a substitute for the research steps. [L1088, 2020]
- Before building a store, the niche must pass a review by an experienced person; the build starts only after the review. [L1068, 2019]
- A shortlisted market that you are unsure about can go to an external reviewer before you move on. The reviewer checks whether the idea is viable and flags potential problems, and you receive an explanation of the findings. [L1058, 2021-22]
- External market verification is optional. If a researched market clearly meets all selection criteria and you are confident in it, you can proceed without verification. [L1058, 2021-22]
- Some high-ticket dropshipping programs offer a niche verification service. You send the proposed niche by email and receive an opinion on whether it is viable, which may confirm the choice or suggest changes. Using it is optional and not required for success. [L1059, 2021-22]

**Procedure (full)**

- Niche selection procedure (2025 unit 0058.004 as written):
  1. Review your Niche Research Worksheet. [L0058, undated]
  2. Confirm your chosen niche passes all required tests. [L0058, undated]
  3. Decide whether to request optional niche verification from the instructor. [L0058, undated]
  4. If requesting verification: click **Niche Selection Verification**, fill out the form with data from your research worksheet, and submit your completed research. [L0058, undated]
  5. If not requesting verification: proceed knowing your niche is validated by the research process. [L0058, undated]
  6. If verification was requested, wait for confirmation via email. [L0058, undated]
  7. Mark this lesson as complete. [L0058, undated]
  8. Move on to the store-building step to begin building your store. [L0058, undated]
- Verification steps before the review step (2024):
  1. Submit niche ideas for verification. [L0276, 2024] (2024)
  2. Wait for response (check spam folder). [L0276, 2024] (2024)
  3. Requires minimum 20+ suppliers for approval. [L0276, 2024] (2024)
  4. Each application must include comprehensive supplier research showing names and contact information. [L0276, 2024] (2024)
- Verification procedure with a guided form (2024):
  1. Open the members area and find **Get your niche verified** in the sidebar. [L0360, 2024] (2024)
  2. Click it and submit your niche ideas, ideally the top two or three. [L0360, 2024] (2024)
  3. Expect feedback within about one day, and treat a green light as the signal to start the store build and supplier contact. [L0360, 2024] (2024)

**How many to submit**

- Choose two or three niche ideas you feel good about, and go through the niche research material before submitting them. [L0551, 2020]
- Submit your top three niche ideas. If the form does not open, email support with the subject line niche verification and list your niches there, ideally three at most. [L0960, 2019]
- Start the niche verification with one niche, the one you are most excited about, rather than submitting several. The team responds with feedback whether the niche passes or fails, including how to adjust it or pivot. [L0804, 2023] (2023)
- Submit the top two or three niches through the niche selection form in the members area sidebar. Submitting too many niches produces more noise, so keep the list short. [L0963, 2022]
- Narrow the niche down to the top two or three candidates before submitting them for verification. There is no limit on submissions, but too many make the review harder for everyone. [L1094, 2023] (2023)
- For new stores, submit two or three niche ideas for review; new stores are best limited to about two submissions, with preliminary research done beforehand. [L0969, 2022]
- When you ask for an outside niche validation, send no more than two or three candidate niches at once and narrow the list yourself first. [L1113, 2022]
- A niche can be validated by submitting two or three ideas to the niche verification process. The verification team then helps select one that passes the criteria. [L0962, 2021]
- Limit submissions to the top two or three. If you are stuck between several, submit the two you are most torn between. [L0941, 2022]
- Conflict on count: the sources agree on two or three. Some say two is the working maximum for a new store [L0969, 2022], one may be sent when you have a single candidate [L0804, 2023], and no limit is stated but short lists are preferred [L1094, 2023]. Use two or three for a shortlist and one when only one candidate is in doubt.

**What the submission must contain**

- A niche idea can be submitted for external verification before you commit. The reviewer either confirms the niche or suggests a related niche that fits the selection criteria better. [L0970, 2023] (2023)
- Enter the top three competitors ranked by traffic that are closest to your niche. If there are no direct competitors, use multi-category stores that sell in your niche. [L0854, 2022]
- A niche can be chosen when the demand data is strong, even if the competitors are general stores. Being the first focused store in the niche can be an advantage. [L0854, 2022]
- Dropship friendliness is checked through the future competitors you list on the submission form. [L0837, 2025] (2025)
- Submit the niche information, including research on future competitor stores. The team reviews it within typically two business days and gives honest feedback on whether it meets the blueprint criteria. [L0836, 2024] (2024)
- Each application needs complete supplier research with names and contact information (see the procedure above). [L0276, 2024] (2024)
- Niche approval is decided by reviewers with long industry experience who have researched thousands of niches, combined with a fixed set of validation tests. It is not decided by a single checklist alone. [L0801, 2021]
- Niche approval checks cover the whole niche, not each individual product. [L1111, 2020]
- Niche ideas can be submitted for a verification review by a small internal team of two or three people who check them against the criteria. Submitted ideas are not exposed to others, so there is no real risk of a niche idea being stolen. [L0962, 2021]

### Niche-specific store rules

**One niche per store**

- Keep one niche per store. Add a second niche to the same store only when fewer than 20 suppliers or fewer than 100 products can be found for the first one. [L0469, 2021]
- Each store should target one niche. A niche can hold many products and SKUs as long as they stay within that niche and meet the niche criteria. [L0967, 2020]
- Build separate stores for each niche that passes all criteria, because separate stores convert better, allow more targeted ads and give a better return on ad spend. Combine niches only if neither had enough suppliers on its own or one of them failed the criteria. [L0359, 2020]
- A niche can stand as its own store when it passes all criteria and at least 100 products from at least 20 brands or suppliers can be found for it. If one niche does not reach that level, combine it with another niche. [L0359, 2020]
- Build one niche-specific store per niche only if you can find at least 20 brands and 100 products that meet all blueprint criteria. If not, move up one level, for example from motorized wheelchairs to all wheelchairs. [L1086, 2019]
- Before moving a niche up a level, check whether a sub-category such as chandeliers already has at least 20 brands and 100 products. If it does, a dedicated sub-niche store can be built. [L1086, 2019]
- Build a store around one niche that passes all validation criteria. Put several categories in one store only when they are closely related and naturally bought together. An example is a sofa sold with matching loveseats, ottomans and chairs. [L0853, 2021]
- Only put products in the same store if they are logically related, such as a sofa with a matching loveseat and chair that buyers might purchase as a set. Otherwise, build separate stores, each with at least 20 suppliers and 100 products per niche. [L1112, 2021]
- Build a store around a whole niche rather than one product, and include all legitimate brands in that niche that are based in the US and sell only through online-only stores. Multiple products in the same niche give more room for winners than a single item. [L0807, 2025] (2025)
- Only broaden the niche when a niche cannot pass the research criteria. More niche-specific stores generally convert visitors better than general stores that sell many unrelated items. [L0965, 2024] (2024)
- Keep a store as niche-specific as possible, as long as it still meets the minimum of 20 suppliers and 100 products. General multi-category stores are not recommended. [L0852, 2020]
- Niche-specific stores convert better than broad multi-category stores, which is why the niche structure is kept whenever the supply criteria are met. Adding several categories is possible, but it is best kept close to the blueprint. [L1086, 2019]
- Niche-specific stores often outperform a mixed-niche store with the same products. Build a second store once the first runs smoothly and the easy wins are captured. [L1112, 2021]
- Single-niche stores consistently converted visitors into customers at a higher rate than multi-niche stores, according to the research author's reported decade of store building. The reasoning given is that a single-niche store can offer a more tailored experience for the product being sought. [L0891, 2020-26]
- Always build niche-specific first due to higher conversion rates. Multi-niche stores can be used only if a single niche fails to meet module two criteria and needs to be broadened to reach minimum demand or competition thresholds. [L0270, 2021]
- A more specialized niche (for example office chairs instead of office furniture) lets you become the category authority and prevents customer distraction. Customers searching for a specific product type are more likely to buy from a specialist store than from a generalist. [L0251, 2023] (2023)
- A kayak-specific store has more SEO authority and customer trust in that category than a broader water sports store that sells kayaks, paddle boards and surfboards together. The more specific niche ranks better for category keywords and builds deeper expertise perception. Broaden only when supplier count requires it. [L0249, 2022]
- A small, narrow product type can compete well when it is built into a standalone store with its own site and experience. Such niches should still pass the demand tests before launch. [L1069, 2019]

**Store scope and combining**

- For a second store, avoid seasonal niches and apply all the full research criteria to it, as you would for the first store. [L0803, 2022]
- Conflict on the second niche: the first-store rule requires year-round demand [L0045, undated], and the second-store rule repeats the avoidance of seasonal niches [L0803, 2022]. Both apply.
- If the store domain is tied to one niche, related niches can be added to the same store when the current store is established and the new products sell well under the domain. A separate domain (around 12 USD for a new domain purchased through Shopify, per 2022 pricing) with a new logo is an option for brand-new projects. [L0429, 2022]
- If several products in different types all meet the criteria, start the first store with one product type and add more stores later. [L0469, 2021]
- A store can be pivoted to a different product type within the same niche without renaming it or building a new store. [L0671, 2021]
- A store with one niche may use the category that already generates the best sales. Narrow the store to one product category rather than mixing unrelated products such as children's gadgets, men's accessories and dresses. Mixed categories reduce credibility when the brand is built around a person's expertise. [L0838, 2020-26]
- Specializing in one category works because visitors expect a personal brand to be an expert in one area. A mismatch between the person's stated expertise and the products sold creates a disconnect that undermines trust. [L0838, 2020-26]
- A jewelry brand can sell its bracelets through a fulfillment center instead of dropshipping and still keep a homepage focused on its top seller. Even when the store has a few minor categories, such as a small blanket section, the homepage should show mainly the best-selling product, here bracelets. [L0838, 2020-26]
- The first filter to add to a store should be by product type, matching the main niche category in the top navigation dropdown. This is the recommended single starting filter for a new store. [L0890, 2020-26]
- Adding decor or lighting to an established dining furniture store is not recommended. Cheaper, unrelated categories lower the average price point and average order value and can reduce the conversion rate. [L0623, 2020-26]
- A shopper buying a dining table usually does not buy lighting at the same time, so a lighting category attracts only a small share of customers. This is one reason not to merge closely related but different products into one store. [L0623, 2020-26]
- A single niche such as dining furniture can have well over 100 supplier brands, so there is usually enough depth within the main niche. Expanding into related categories is generally less valuable than working through the existing niche's brands. [L0623, 2020-26]
- A product in the same niche across several products (for example face, hair, shaving and personal care) keeps the store focused. Staying in one niche makes it easier to build a consistent brand and audience. [L0657, undated]
- Subniche counts decide store structure:
  1. A subniche with many suppliers and products (for example around 40 suppliers and about 1,000 products) can get its own store. [L1110, 2019]
  2. Subniches with fewer than 20 suppliers and few products should be combined. [L1110, 2019]
  3. Where some subniches are strong and others weak, build the strong ones as their own stores, build the best one first, and skip or merge the weak ones. [L1110, 2019]
- Choose a store name broad enough for future expansion, such as a name built on a larger theme like surfing or watches, instead of a narrow product name like leather watches. [L0466, 2018]

**Brands**

- No sector is excluded by default, including beauty. Any niche that passes all the niche criteria can be used, and no extra restriction applies to a specific category. [L0359, 2020]
- Working with one to three well-known brands that people already search for brings traffic through brand searches. These brands also help sell the other products in the store and can be used for monthly promotions. [L0429, 2022]
- Conflict on brands: the one source material favours one to three well-known brands for store traffic [L0429, 2022], while the one source market screen requires established local suppliers whose brands are not household names [L0994, 2021-22]. The first applies to the store build; the second applies to the market screen.
- Single-product stores or funnels are not recommended. Each product page should be treated as a sales funnel inside a full store, because one-product businesses depend entirely on that product. [L0801, 2021]
