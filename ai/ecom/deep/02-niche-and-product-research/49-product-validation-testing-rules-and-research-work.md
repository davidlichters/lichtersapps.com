## Product Validation, Testing Rules and Research Workflow

### Research Volume and Shortlist Size

- Research at least 10 products using the course framework, enter all of them into the Product Research Sheet, and validate each one before advancing. Progression is blocked until all three conditions are met: 10 products researched, all 10 entered in the sheet, and each validated with the framework. [L0038, 2026]
- Build a research list of at least 10 product ideas, not just one. [L0280, undated]
- Research at least 15 product ideas before submitting anything for approval. A shortlist of 10 to 15 ideas is the minimum before narrowing down. [L0491, 2026]
- Aim for 10 to 12 candidate products per research session, each backed by its active ad count, targeted countries and store data. [L1172, 2023+]
- One research session can surface about 15 to 20 products, and the research itself should take about three to five days. [L1436, undated]
- Use one or two research tools that together give at least 10 to 15 candidate products. If a tool lacks the filters you need, switch to a free method, such as the burner account method or a Chrome extension method for Facebook. [L0363, 2022+]
- Research 10 to 15 potential products across different niches rather than committing to one niche at the start. For each product, record the margin, whether it is new, whether it solves a problem, the value it gives the customer, and how many marketing angles it offers. [L0363, 2022+]
- Build a sheet of 10 to 20 candidate products before committing any budget. [L0639, 2023]
- Build a Facebook product research list of at least 10, ideally 10 to 15, products so they can be ranked later. Add a column for the Facebook page creation date next to the ad library link in the research sheet. [L1183, undated]
- Keep a list of 5 to 10 solid, validated products ready at all times, because individual products fail and the next one must already be prepared. Product research is time-consuming, so start it early. [L0693, 2024]
- Before competitor analysis, build a shortlist of roughly 10 to 15 product candidates that already meet basic criteria, such as being unique and not seen before in the market. [L0895, 2021+]

### Shortlists and Scoring

- Refresh the product research list regularly, for example every two weeks, because active ad counts and trends change quickly. A product that was strong last month may lose active ads and need to be dropped. [L1172, 2023+]
- If a competitor's store page or ad link is not loading, do not add the product to the test list yet. Keep it on a watch list and recheck it once the page works. [L1172, 2023+]
- Score each viral product candidate from 1 to 10 on separate criteria, such as comment demand, product appeal and store quality, instead of one overall impression. A candidate with buyer comments scored 10 for demand can still be worth a test even if the product itself scores around 7. A candidate scoring 4 or 5 overall should be dropped. [L0530, 2022]
- Rate each competition criterion on a numeric scale in the Facebook competitor scoring sheet, with 7 or higher marked as a red flag, then add the ratings into a total score to rank products. This method is a custom sheet, not a standard tool. [L0895, 2021+]
- Rank each product against the number of validation criteria it meets, since products that meet more criteria deserve a higher rank. Products that fail critical checks should be dropped or deprioritized. [L0708, undated]
- Rank final product candidates by how many marketing angles they have, how well they fit your criteria, their quality, and how little competition they face. The top-ranked product is the best candidate to launch. [L0636, undated]
- Use the Weighting column to rank candidate products against each other after the four phases are complete. Products with better engagement, lower competition and a workable break-even ROAS should receive higher weights. [L1159, 2023+]
- Score the product list in a spreadsheet with a checkbox for each criterion. Products that miss criteria drop to the bottom with a rank of 1 out of 5, while products that meet them rise with a higher rank. [L1194, undated]
- Use a 1 to 10 opportunity scale when comparing niches. Low-scoring opportunities (around 1 to 5) rarely become profitable regardless of effort, so pursue niches scoring around 8 to 10. [L0924, 2020-26]
- Rank new brands and products using the same analytics you use for your current products. Search volume is generally a good metric for this ranking. [L0920, 2018]
- Use one consistent product name or search term for all research steps, because switching terms makes the data incomparable. Related terms can be used later in ads. [L0970, 2023]
- Check the research tools for product ranking and sales data using the same method every time, so results stay comparable. Reliable niche decisions depend on consistent measurement, not on a single data point. [L1279, 2021] **

### Screening Criteria and Product Filters

- Define validation criteria before choosing a product. The main failure for beginners is spending money on untested products until they give up. Use the results of each tested product to decide the next one. [L1440, 2021+]
- Do not select products based on personal intuition or random AliExpress browsing, because beginners cannot afford such guesses. Back each research step with data showing that the product is currently selling for others. [L0708, undated]
- Only promote products you have tested yourself and that actually do what they claim. Genuine belief in the product makes it easier to create convincing videos and sell it to customers. [L0657, undated]
- Before choosing products, check the price point, the number of available suppliers, and the competition on the first page of Google Shopping results. [L0671, 2021] **
- Check the product, its type, and its seasonality with this sequence:
  1. Find candidate products in a product research tool (Peak was used). [L0577, 2023]
  2. Check the product is legitimate and does what it claims, e.g. by reading the reviews on Amazon. [L0577, 2023]
  3. Check the product type: entertainment products that attract parents, such as activity products for children's summer holidays, suit seasonal selling. [L0577, 2023]
  4. Consider seasonality: pick products that fit the upcoming season, e.g. summer products such as water dispensers for offices and barbecues, or Apple Watch straps, which sell well in summer. [L0577, 2023]
  5. Check the platform fit: decide whether the product works on Facebook and TikTok. [L0577, 2023]
  6. Ask family, friends or partners whether they would buy the product, instead of relying on personal taste. [L0577, 2023]
  7. Test with a budget of about 200 dollars and see how it performs. [L0577, 2023]
- Product selection order: first check whether the product has a lot of competition and whether it can be sold profitably, e.g. at a 3x markup on a cheap item. Then check that real demand exists in the market, since a very small niche makes scaling difficult compared with a large market like pets. [L0690, 2022+]
- Before testing a candidate, define a customer avatar, list several marketing angles for it, and estimate the total addressable market. Products with a clear audience, several angles and a large market are the ones worth testing. [L0363, 2022+]
- Judge the market before entering. If the product is new and ads run by others are working, check the problem size, the number of use cases, the store and the margin. These checks decide whether to proceed. [L1436, undated]
- Complete customer research, product research and primary text research before testing, so that the product page and ad copy are dialed in. Testing without them makes it much harder to know why an ad works. [L1476, 2026]
- Check TikTok Shop traction before building a product page: a strong creator conversion ratio, steady revenue across weeks, and a concentration rate that shows sales are spread over several creators rather than one. Compare these numbers with the price and commission rate to see whether the product is worth testing. [L1188, 2026]
- Keep a fixed set of criteria for every product, for example a product criteria checklist of six to seven points that defines the kind of product you want. [L1382, undated]
- Set clear criteria for the team, for example upsell potential and a maximum supplier cost of 30 USD on AliExpress. Clear criteria let the assistant screen products before the owner picks one. [L1431, 2020+]
- Products that sold well organically on TikTok do not automatically work with paid ads. Once a proof of concept exists, test horizontally within the same niche to find a product that converts with ads. [L0819, 2020+]
- Keep the full research order in mind. It runs: product criteria checklist, TikTok ad spy search, AliExpress supplier quality, competition and sales check, competitor store verification, and product and store quality check. [L1382, undated]

### Four-Phase Research Process and Sheet

- Thorough product research happens in four phases, and each phase weeds out weak candidates before the next one begins. Use this four-stage sequence in order, because each stage filters out products before the more time-consuming checks begin. [L0636, undated]
- Work through product research in four phases, in this order: [L1159, 2023+]
  1. Phase 1, Initial research (criteria based): enter the product name, the ad link, the competitor store link and the AliExpress supplier link (Ali).
  2. Phase 2, Competitor analysis: record the ad engagement (likes) and the competition level for the product.
  3. Phase 3, Market validation: record the market and mark whether the product should be tested (TEST?).
  4. Phase 4, Decide: enter COGS, selling price, competitor price, break-even ROAS (BE ROAS), mentor notes, your own notes and the weighting score.
- Work through the product testing sheet in phase order. First research the product and review competitor ads, then fill in the competitor analysis (phase 2) with engagement (high or low) and competition (low, medium or high) plus your findings. Next set the market size as small, medium or large (phase 3), and finally record the yes/no decision on testing the product (phase 4). [L1162, undated]
- Mentor review of a shared product list lets two people compare candidates and pick the test product together, with a comment left per product. [L1162, undated]

### Demand Validation Before Testing

#### Signals and Their Limits

- Beginners often overthink validation and ask whether a product is guaranteed to win. There is no guarantee, so testing is required. [L0347, 2026]
- For validating a product before testing, look for active ads, recent creatives, and multiple advertisers. These signals are enough to justify a test. [L0347, 2026]
- Look only at products already advertised on the ad platform, not untested items. [L0335, 2023]
- Confirm product demand from several sources, such as supplier sales counts, the follower count and views of organic accounts, and ad activity. Do not base a decision on a single signal. [L0706, 2020+]
- Validate a product with data, such as sales and engagement signals, instead of choosing it by guessing. [L0914, 2026]
- Only scale a product after validation shows it sells consistently. One lucky sale is not validation. [L0914, 2026]
- Validate niche ideas with actual numbers and real market data gathered free online. When time is limited, choose the niche with more opportunity over the weaker one. [L0506, 2020-26]
- Do not judge a product by one viral video or one spike in sales. Check that revenue holds across the Last 30 Days window and that the product has a rising trend before you commit. [L1479, 2026]
- A product page with only about 10 reviews after a large viral spike is weak evidence of quality, and such reviews may be fake. Do not judge demand from review count alone; combine it with real order checks. [L0655, undated]
- A common product research guideline is a minimum of 300 orders before a product is considered proven. Treat this as a guideline and not an absolute rule, since some products with strong potential sell below this level. [L0644, undated]
- Low AliExpress order counts are not a reason to drop a product if the ads show strong engagement, such as several thousand likes on one ad. Check the CJ listing for the same product and test the product anyway. [L0661, undated]
- Test product variants separately instead of assuming a whole category works. In one example, one version of a door-lock style product sold while another version of the same product did not. Track each variant's sales on its own. [L0706, 2020+]
- Research the product category before launching videos for it. A lack of category research slows the first days of filming, because the creator has to learn what to show and how. [L0764, 2022]

#### Saturation and Trend Checks

- Before testing, check when the first ad for the product started running. Look for viral ads already run for the product; if there are many, the market may be saturated. [L0502, 2020+]
- Search TikTok for old trending videos. If trends from several years ago are still circulating, the trend has likely passed. [L0502, 2020+]
- Check the product in ad-spy apps for old ads. [L0502, 2020+]
- Mark which candidates look saturated or heavily competed. Get an experienced outside opinion on saturation for the marked candidates, then test the ones that remain. [L0639, 2023]
- If you are unsure whether a product is saturated, test it with a new TikTok account and no store. Building the store takes significant time, so skipping it removes much of the cost of a failed test. [L1444, 2022]
- Before investing in a product from scratch, validate demand with a market test on Facebook, TikTok and Instagram. A small group of friends or testers cannot show whether a product can reach millions of buyers. [L0760, undated] Test with an audience of roughly one thousand to tens of thousands of people on these platforms before committing money to inventory or custom product development. A small group of friends or testers cannot show whether a product will work at scale. [L0760, undated]
- Pushing a product that the market does not respond to leads to poor ad performance on social platforms. Founders should not rely on intuition alone and must let the market react. [L0760, undated]
- Test demand on Facebook and TikTok first, not Google. Product testing is about spending time and money efficiently. [L0577, 2023]
- Check the trend with Google Trends and news, and collect marketing angles from Amazon reviews. [L0708, undated]
- For a product that is too new to check with Amazon reviews, the goal is proof of concept and demand, not quality certainty. Start with a small test spend. If the first two or three sales come in, stop or scale based on results, because the cost of failure stays small. [L0552, 2023]
- Validate a product with a small amount of sales on your store before spending a large budget. The goal is proof of concept, not a large test. [L1219, undated]
- Before buying a large quantity of a custom-brand product, get approval of the concept by testing it. Scaling before the concept is validated increases chargeback and cash-flow risk. [L0351, 2026]
- Dummy test order refunds: competitors rarely accept refund requests for test orders, and a cancellation request is often refused with a claim that the order already shipped. Plan for losing the order cost. [L0501, 2022]
- Do not rush into buying a product or spending on it all at once after a single positive check. Test in small steps and compare multiple reference points, because a store's age and account history can change the reliability of dummy orders. [L1385, undated]
- Check the supplier or CJ shipping cost and the sourcing cost before testing. [L1176, 2023]

#### Conversion Depends on the Product

- Conversion from views to sales depends heavily on the product. A good, unique product that solves a clear problem sold 5 units from under 50,000 views, while some other products got one sale or none from a million views. [L0764, 2022]
- When a video or account does not convert, the cause could be the audience, the product, the videos or a mix of all three. Diagnose which one is responsible before changing everything. [L0764, 2022]
- A 500 to 600 views per post result over three weeks at two to four posts per day, across several accounts and devices, suggested the product or the video angle was the cause rather than the account itself. [L0820, undated]
- Bottom-of-funnel ads work for market-aware products: universally understood items (phone cases, water bottles), or products already validated and run by competitors. For niche products that need explanation (specialized supplements), warm audiences with video ads first, then revisit bottom-of-funnel once you have repeat customers or brand awareness. [L0316, 2026]
- Attention rate and click-through rate can be lower for a relevant audience that buys, so a low soft-metric ad that makes sales can still be profitable. Retargeting the people who clicked can also build a useful audience later. [L0995, 2023]

#### Research Cadence and Sources

- Start product research while still going through the training rather than after it. Also check TikTok Shop, Kalodata and competitor stores for demand signals. [L0491, 2026]
- The research approach is to launch small tests, look for a little demand and some sales, and only then go deeper. Deeper research uses sources such as Reddit, Amazon and emotion valence exports, which are fed into an AI tool like Claude for analysis. [L1175, 2026]
- The Market Research habit is a daily 30-minute block whose purpose is to understand the target audience better through a structured research process. Doubling or more of that time is encouraged when possible. [L0456, 2026]
