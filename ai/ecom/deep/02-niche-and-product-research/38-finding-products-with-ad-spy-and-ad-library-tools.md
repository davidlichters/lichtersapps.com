## Finding Products with Ad Spy and Ad Library Tools

### Ad Spy Tools: Roles and Limits

- The Meta Ad Library is the most complete public ad archive for dropshipping research, because advertisers cannot hide their ads from it, while third-party spy tools can miss ads that advertisers pay to conceal. Use it as the primary source for finding products. [L0638, 2023]
- No spy tool shows every ad, and some products appear in only one tool. Each tool has its own library, so use several tools for a more complete list. [L0708, undated]
- Some spy tools do not update their databases and keep showing the same products every day. Supplement them with manual searches in the Ad Library and on supplier marketplaces. [L1108, 2023]
- No tool gives exact competitor store data or sales for a product. Use the Ad Library and keyword searches to estimate competition. Spy tools with a product section show some stores that sell a product, but they miss many of them. [L0638, 2023]
- Product research with spy tools works best by studying the best sellers of competitor stores and the ads they run, rather than hunting for new products every day. Products with proven sales are a better starting point than constantly chasing new ones. [L0561, 2023+]

### Meta Ad Library: Setup and Basic Search

- The Meta Ad Library is a free public tool for checking which ads run for a brand or keyword, filtered by country and active status. It is used to validate products and to find winning ads for inspiration. [L0347, 2026]
- Log into a Facebook account before using the Ad Library, because without a login the results may not show everything. Start from the Ad Library page, choose the country, then open the All ads view. [L0638, 2023]
- Steps for checking a product in the Meta Ad Library: [L0347, 2026]
  1. Open the Meta Ad Library.
  2. Set the country filter to United States.
  3. Set the active status filter to Active ads and the ad type and media type filters to all.
  4. Search the product or brand keyword.
  5. Count the results and check the launch dates and Library IDs of the ads.
  6. Open the ads and check the impression label: ads marked with a low impression count (under 100 impressions) are weak performers.
  7. Look for the ads with more impressions and with multiple advertisers running similar creatives, since these are the likely winners.
- The Ad Library can show which products competitors advertise. Search the product idea and sort by newest to see which products have recently started running ads. Use this as a market check before testing. [L0577, 2023]
- The Ad Library shows which stores run ads for a product. If other stores run the same product creatives with their own landing pages and product titles, that helps validate demand. [L0361, 2024]
- Searching for an exact product name can return nothing, because some ads only run on general pages. When the exact product is not found, look at the ads of large niche stores in the same category to see what the market runs. [L0450, 2022+]
- Searching by hand gives scattered and incomplete data. A paid ad spy tool with a large library and product filters produces more candidates and more reliable scaling data in less time. The aim is a list of at least 10 candidates, ideally 10 to 15. [L1174, undated]
- Facebook Ads Library searches with niche keywords, such as kids toy or kids gift, show which ads are running in a niche and are used for manual niche and competitor checks. [L1108, 2023]
- Ad spy search by product keyword for a specific audience: AdSpy filters for female 40+ fashion research are Gender = female, Age = 40 to 65+, Country = United States, and Ad text = a product keyword such as shoe, sneakers, boot or slippers. To see a specific competitor store's ads, search by its URL. [L0661, undated]

### Keyword and Offer Searches

- Keyword searches in the Ad Library are the first filter for dropshipping ads. Use typical offer phrases such as 30 percent off, 35 percent off, 50 percent off and free shipping. Broad phrases like percent off capture most discount ads, while 70 or 80 percent off are rarely used by dropshippers but can still be tested. [L0638, 2023]
- A single broad keyword is not enough on its own. The keyword percent off returned more than 50,000 results for the United States without other filters. Adding the Active ads filter and a narrow date window reduces the list to a workable number. [L0638, 2023]
- Combine several keywords in one search with OR logic, using the pipe character, to see ads containing any of the phrases, such as percent off together with get yours today. Use quotation marks for exact-phrase search when you need ads containing the exact phrase, such as free shipping. [L0638, 2023]
- Brand and dropship ads often appear side by side in the Ad Library. Large non-product advertisers such as brands, non-profits and political groups add a lot of noise. Use specific keywords, narrow date windows and product-related ads to filter them out. [L0638, 2023]
- Free research workflow with keywords: [L0895, 2021+]
  1. Prepare a list of 5 to 10 keywords per product.
  2. Search each keyword in the Facebook Ad Library, the main free source for Facebook competitors.
  3. Review the last one to two months of data, including the previous month, to see who is running ads.
  4. Note each competitor, its ad count, creative reuse and launch dates in a sheet.
  5. Open the competitor's Facebook page and search its name in the Ad Library to check whether it is scaling.
  6. Check the competitor's landing page and copy.
- Search Google, Amazon, AliExpress, Alibaba and TikTok with the same keywords as the Facebook Ad Library, and compile the competitors found outside the ad tools, since ad spy tools do not cover every store. [L0693, 2024]
- Winning Hunter is not needed for a beginner's basic keyword check; the Ad Library workflow above is enough to start. [L0638, 2023]

### Date Windows and the Active-Ad Filter

- Set the Active ads filter in every research session, because it hides ads that have stopped running. Running ads show what other sellers are currently scaling or testing, which is the signal being looked for. [L0638, 2023]
- The Ad Library sometimes shows results that do not match the active filters, for example older ads when a recent date window is set. Re-check the filters and dates before trusting a result. [L0638, 2023]
- A short recent window with the Active ads filter shows which products other sellers are scaling right now, rather than just testing. Ads launched in the last few days give a current signal of demand. [L0638, 2023]
- Long-running active ads are a signal of profitability, because advertisers rarely keep ads running for months without return. An ad launched in March that is still active in May suggests the product is profitable for that seller, although a forgotten ad can also stay active. [L0638, 2023]
- Advertisers do not scale ads for products that fail to make money, so active advertising by others is a signal of profitability. Check which products have sustained ad activity before choosing what to test. [L1172, 2023+]

**Date windows by purpose**

| Purpose | Window | Ids |
|---|---|---|
| Products others are scaling now (Impression date filter) | 2 to 3 days, for example 30 April to 2 May; avoid windows longer than about 3 days | [L0638, 2023] |
| Proven older products (Impression date filter) | About 1 to 3 months, for example 1 March to 31 March, or 1 January to 31 March | [L0638, 2023] |
| Ads launched recently (Ad Library date preset) | Last 90 days, to avoid older ads that may have stopped working | [L0374, 2026] |
| Ad creation date in Winning Hunter | Last three days, then sort by longest running or days running | [L0772, 2026] |
| Recently launched competitor ads (spy tools) | Last 1 month; extend to 1.5 to 2 months only if needed | [L0708, undated] |
| Fresh product launches | Ads launched in the last 1 to 2 weeks, with about one month as the widest window | [L0335, 2023] |
| Widening a search that returns too few products | Date range about 45 days | [L0335, 2023] |

- Ad Library date presets are Today, Yesterday, Last 7 days, Last 14 days, Last 30 days, Last 90 days, Last 180 days, Last 365 days and All Time. [L0374, 2026]
- Filter for ads launched in the last 1 to 2 weeks, using about one month as the widest window. The aim is to find products that competitors have only just started to scale. [L0335, 2023]
- Choosing a window that is too recent, such as April, mostly shows products still in testing or early scaling without product market fit. For a proven product, use an older window such as 1 to 31 March or 1 January to 31 March. [L0638, 2023]
- Filter for product ads created in the last 1 month, and extend to 1.5 to 2 months only if needed. Recently launched ads show which products are currently working for other dropshippers. [L0708, undated]
- Winning Hunter has a Last seen date filter, separate from the Ad creation date filter. Use it to find ads still being shown recently, regardless of when they first launched. [L0772, 2026]
- Check when an advertiser's ads started before trusting their ad count. Ads that began only a day or two ago are not yet proof of profit, while ads that have run for about a week or more are a stronger signal. [L1161, 2024+]
- Change the date range in the Facebook Ad Library to look at past ad counts for a product. A product that once had over 1,000 active ads in an earlier window has scaled before, even if the current count is lower. [L1161, 2024+]

### Days Running and Video Filters

- Set Days running to a minimum of seven days. An ad that has run that long suggests the product is either working or at least breaking even. [L0772, 2026]
- Dashboard filter ranges include Days running from 0 to 365, Video length from 0s to 3600s, Product Price Range from 0 to 5000, and Products on Store from 0 to 100000. Combine them with Store Traffic (Monthly Visits) and Scaling to narrow results to stores that are already proven. [L0374, 2026]
- Set Media type to Videos, because the goal is to recreate video ads that already perform. [L0772, 2026]
- Set the Video length filter to a long range, with the default range 0 to 3600 seconds, to find VSLs. Long-video filtering works well for finding sales-video funnels. [L0772, 2026]
- Use the Ad copy length filter to surface native-style ads, which tend to carry long body copy. [L0772, 2026]
- Leave Page type blank, so advertorials, listicles, quizzes and blog-style funnels appear next to product pages. This lets you study the full funnel a brand uses. [L0772, 2026]
- Choose a niche relevant to your search (the example used Health) and leave the Apps and Theme filters blank. Winning Hunter warns that the niche filter may not show all ads, so use it only when it saves time. [L0772, 2026]

### Active-Ad Count Thresholds

The sources give different numbers for different purposes. Each row states the value and when it applies. Count only active ads that belong to the specific product. [L1172, 2023+; L1174, undated]

| Active-ad count | Meaning and use | Ids |
|---|---|---|
| Under about 50 | Skip the product in Facebook Ad Library screening; products with only inactive ads are dropped | [L1161, 2024+] |
| Under 100 | Below the shortlist minimum in AfterLib and Ad Library research; skip it, since fewer usually means weak demand | [L1171, 2024+; L1173, undated] |
| Over 100 | Main filter for a winning product; a much stronger candidate than under 50 | [L1161, 2024+; L1173, undated] |
| 200 or more | Stronger signal of a product that converts | [L1173, undated] |
| 300 to 600 | Strong performance, the rule of thumb for Meta ads | [L1172, 2023+] |
| Over 300 | Example: a pet product with over 300 active ads counted as strong validation worth testing | [L1161, 2024+] |
| Over 500 | Worth adding to a research list | [L1172, 2023+] |
| 600 or more (Facebook) | Strong scaling signal for a product on Facebook | [L1174, undated] |
| 1,000 or more | Strong trust signal that the product works and is worth testing | [L1171, 2024+; L1176, 2023] |
| Over 1,000 | Extremely strong signal of profitability; little further proof needed, though the store and margin are still checked | [L1172, 2023+] |

**Scaling thresholds for one product:** 20 or more active ads for scaling, 30 or more for solid proof of concept, and 50 or more for likely high revenue. [L1174, undated]

- Products with 2 to 10 active ads: a seller with 10 to 15 active ads is usually still testing. [L0335, 2023]
- Products with a very high count that appeared only days earlier may be a short burst of testing; a product whose ads run weeks or months later is a stronger signal of sustained performance. [L1171, 2024+]
- Ads that have run for a week or more strengthen the signal from a high active-ad count. [L1161, 2024+]
- Skip products with very few active ads, such as 18, when comparable products in the same search have many more active ads. Come back to them later instead of testing them now. [L1171, 2024+]
- A product with many active ads that drops to zero may simply have been stopped or replaced by the seller. It can still be tested in other markets, but its success probability is lower. [L1171, 2024+]
- A falling number of active ads signals declining demand or quality. Treat such products as lower priority, even if they were strong in earlier research. [L1172, 2023+]
- Track how many active ads a competing brand runs for a product over time, and check again after about two months. A clearly falling number signals the trend is fading and demand is being lost. [L0347, 2026]
- A product that had over 1,000 ads a few days ago and now shows fewer active ads may still be a proven winner. Check the Ads Library history and the active ad count together before rejecting a product. [L1173, undated]
- A product that started running ads about a month earlier and already shows around 645 active ads is scaling very fast and is worth shortlisting. Compare its start date with its current active-ad count to judge growth speed. [L1173, undated]

### Reading Ad Counts Correctly

- Read the active ad count as a signal of intent. Around 10 to 15 active ads usually means a seller is still testing, while a steady flow of several new ads per day suggests the seller has found audiences that convert and is putting budget behind them. [L0335, 2023]
- Multiple active ads running for a long time, combined with recent edits or new ads started within days, are stronger evidence than a single high count. A competitor who recently started or edited ads is actively testing and spending. [L1172, 2023+]
- Check recent activity: when the latest ad was started or edited shows whether money is still being spent. [L1172, 2023+]
- The Ad Library counts an ad that runs in several ad sets once per ad set, so an ad in five ad sets shows as five ads. A high count means the advertiser tests the product heavily, not that many separate creatives exist. [L1161, 2024+]
- The unfiltered total on a competitor's page can be several times higher than the active count. For one product, 2,200 total against 640 active was shown. Apply the Active ads filter before counting. [L1174, undated]
- Some tools show how one creative's ad count grows over time, for example from single digits to several hundred ads for the same creative. Rising counts mean the advertiser is scaling that creative and the product is probably profitable for them. Check the date range to confirm the growth happened recently. [L1174, undated]
- A product with a high and growing ad count is a usable proxy for profitable demand, because advertisers do not keep paying for ads that do not perform. This makes ad volume a practical filter. [L1176, 2023]
- Sorting by active ads is the first filter for shortlisting candidate dropshipping products. [L1171, 2024+]
- Sorting by percentage change in ad count shows which products are gaining ad volume fastest, not only which have the most ads. [L1176, 2023]

**Same-creative counts in the Ad Library**

Count ads using the same creative and copy. The table shows what the count indicates. [L0638, 2023]

| Same creative count | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 3 | Early test; not yet a scaling signal |
| 4 | Testing phase, or about to scale |
| 6 | Testing or initial scaling |
| 10 | Likely scaling; next scaling phase |
| 13 to 16 | Winning creative and clear winning product |

- A competitor running 5 or more ads with the same creative is probably scaling that creative, which usually means the product was profitable for them. [L0895, 2021+]
- A competitor running 10 to 15 ads with the same creative is in an active scaling phase and is a strong competitor signal worth tracking. [L0895, 2021+]
- For a product with ads at a scaling threshold, count the same creative across the advertiser's page to judge whether the product is being scaled or only tested. [L0638, 2023]
- Mark every competitor's scaling status (Y/N). Competitors who scale ads show the product is currently profitable for them. Count this with the competition and engagement numbers when deciding to test. [L1159, 2023+]
