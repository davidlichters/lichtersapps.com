### Qualitative and Quantitative Data

- Quantitative research gives countable value data, such as the number of people in a country or the number of buyers of a product category last year. [L0353, 2023]
- Qualitative research gives opinion and problem-solving data from forums, reviews and Reddit. It is harder to read but richer in insight. Forums are a good source of qualitative research when a product is not listed on Amazon. [L0353, 2023]
- Quantitative data answers how many, how much and what: numbers, percentages, ratings on a scale, and statistics that go into a spreadsheet and are analysed statistically (for example Shopify analytics or Google Analytics). Qualitative data answers why: opinions, long open answers, observations, interpretation and grouping of common themes. Both are needed. [L0497, undated]
- Quantitative data is measured and tested to show what happens (counts, percentages, scale ratings, analytics such as Shopify or Google Analytics). Qualitative data (open answers, comments, observed language) is interpreted and grouped into themes to show why it happens. Use both, because numbers alone do not reveal motives. [L0497, undated]
- Two research types: data analysis (statistics and hard figures) and voice of customer (what customers say in their own words about their problems, through reviews, surveys and social media). [L0497, undated]
- Start audience research with qualitative data such as customer reviews and forum posts, then back it up with quantitative figures such as counts and ratings. Quantitative data shows what and how much; qualitative data shows why. [L0995, 2023]
- Combine qualitative data (customer comments on Amazon, Reddit and forums that express problems and reasons) with quantitative data (hard numbers such as how many people own a pet type). Customers reveal their problems when they explain why something matters to them. [L0450, 2022+]
- Desire and problem mapping, in order:
  1. List the primary desires driving the market and rate each urgency from 1 to 10, with an explanation. [L0613, 2026]
  2. List the problems that block those desires and how many solutions people usually try first. [L0613, 2026]
  3. Assess the emotional state of the market (desperate, curious, skeptical, hopeful). [L0613, 2026]
  4. For each desire and problem, collect at least 5-7 exact customer quotes with source links. Rate intensity from 1 to 10. Judge whether it is growing or fading, with evidence. Name the emotional triggers, and note seasonal or cyclical patterns. [L0613, 2026]
- Final strategic analysis, in order:
  1. Market opportunity: market size and growth, competitive landscape, key trends, barriers to entry and growth, and emotional gaps. [L0613, 2026]
  2. Positioning recommendation: optimal strategy with rationale, key differentiators, repositioning options and identity angles. [L0613, 2026]
  3. Message strategy: core value proposition, messaging hierarchy, message changes for each segment, objection-handling framework and identity-level messaging. [L0613, 2026]
  4. Channel strategy: channels based on audience behaviour, content formats by channel, and a testing approach for scaling. [L0613, 2026]

### Buyer Personas and Ideal Customer Profiles

#### Defining the Dream Customer

- A dream customer is someone who buys your products and actively engages with your content to expand your brand's reach, becoming an advocate who refers new customers. [L0237, 2018]
- Serving whoever buys, without a defined dream client, produces a business full of customers who cannot afford higher offers and need basic help. The owner ends up spending most time on fundamentals instead of valuable work, which drains energy over years. Choose the dream client deliberately at the start. [L0523, 2015+]
- Build a dream client avatar as a named character with written traits such as success level, passions, goals and attitude toward money:
  1. Choose a name and write down what you know about the person. [L0523, 2015+]
  2. List their traits in concrete terms, such as business revenue, hobbies and personal values. [L0523, 2015+]
  3. Search Google Images for a photo that matches the traits. [L0523, 2015+]
  4. Print the photo and keep it visible while planning your offers and ads. [L0523, 2015+]
- Bait has to match what the dream client already wants and understands. Bait that only advanced buyers appreciate filters out beginners and attracts people who can later afford higher offers. Beginner-friendly bait does the opposite and brings in price-sensitive shoppers. [L0523, 2015+]
- Know the ideal customer in detail: the problems the products solve, where they already search for answers, who they listen to, where they spend time online, demographics (location, gender, age, income), interests and hobbies, and what they must believe before buying. Record the answers in a living document that can be shared with a team. ** [L0414, 2021-22]

#### Persona Construction

- Build each persona from the problem, its importance, the behaviour causing it, psychographics (what the buyer values) and the goal behind the purchase. [L0450, 2022+]
- Build an audience persona before writing ads or a store. Name the ideal persona in one short phrase that combines a type of customer and a shared frustration. [L0395, undated]
- Persona example from the lesson: a German Shepherd owner loves the dog, but the dog sleeps on the bed and leaves hair everywhere. They have tried sticky lint rollers but dislike the paper waste. Their allergies flare up, their sleep suffers, and they feel frustrated, tired and ashamed of a dirty home. Their goal is a clean bed for peaceful sleep and peace of mind. [L0450, 2022+]
- Vague audience descriptions, such as a broad pet-owner group, do not give usable marketing direction. Narrow the persona to a specific type of customer with a specific shared frustration before writing ads or copy. [L0395, undated]
- Audience persona procedure, in order:
  1. Name the ideal persona in one short phrase that combines a type of customer and a shared frustration. [L0395, undated]
  2. Describe demographics (geographic location, age, sex), psychographics (personality traits, attitudes, values) and behaviour (what they do and why). [L0395, undated]
  3. Write the goal the customer wants to reach. [L0395, undated]
  4. List their problems and pains in concrete terms. [L0395, undated]
  5. Split motivation into what they move toward and what they move away from. [L0395, undated]
  6. Identify where and when the problem appears and what triggers it. [L0395, undated]
  7. Note why the problem matters to them emotionally or practically. [L0395, undated]
  8. List their deepest fears. [L0395, undated]
  9. Explain why your product is the preferred solution over alternatives. [L0395, undated]
  10. Describe how the product helps them avoid or solve the problem and feel more capable, important or independent. [L0395, undated]
  11. Write the brand or marketing message. [L0395, undated]
  12. List where they spend time online (forums such as Reddit and Quora, Facebook groups, Instagram, websites they visit). [L0395, undated]
  13. Research their questions, language and what they care about. [L0395, undated]

- Build a brand target audience sheet with questions for demographics, a goals-needs-motivations sheet (what the person desperately wants, what they fear, the biggest problem) and a research checklist. Record the goal, wants, pain points and trigger for each segment. A spreadsheet works well for comparing several segments. [L0450, 2022+]

#### Avatars in Practice

- Surfboard retailer example: split buyers into three avatars.
  - Beginners buying a first board.
  - Casual surfers with some experience who do not surf daily.
  - Advanced surfers who surf every day or compete.
  - A soft foam board fits the beginner avatar, so its copy should be written for beginners rather than advanced surfers, who would not respond to it. ** [L0907, 2021-22]

#### Targeting Principles

- Target gift and relative buyers as well as the end user where this is relevant to the ad. [L0819, 2020+]
- A product's persuasive power comes from framing it as a safe way to satisfy a natural instinct the buyer already has. Example: a toy that mimics prey lets an animal hunt without real risk. Explaining the solution in terms of the instinct it fulfils is stronger than listing features. [L0395, undated]
- Target audience research should start from the fears and pains of the buyer, not from product features. [L0395, undated]

### Segmentation

#### Why Segment

- Market segmentation groups people who share characteristics, so a product can be aimed at a smaller audience. Segmenting a crowded product category into a new sub-market can lower competition, especially for an existing product that competitors already sell generically. [L0995, 2023]
- Market segmentation means splitting a broad market into a smaller group of people who share characteristics, contexts or needs, so that one focused message can reach them. Segments respond much better to a focused message than to a general one. [L0997, undated; L0998, undated]
- Market segmentation means splitting a market into a specific group of people who share characteristics or needs, so that you can address them with a focused message they respond to more strongly. [L0998, undated; L0997, undated]
- Competitors who sell a product to many generic groups ("we cater for these guys and these guys") produce a diluted message that does not play well on social media. A narrow niche with a specific problem produces a more potent message. [L0450, 2022+]
- Targeting everyone with a general message weakens the response because the audience is too diluted. Example: a product that simply keeps items organised. A narrower context, such as travellers who must match items to outfits, makes the same product far more compelling. [L0997, undated]
- Targeting every buyer of a product category, such as all snowboarders or all motorcycle owners, dilutes the audience and weakens the message. Splitting the audience by context, such as beginners versus park riders, produces a more focused and more responsive message. [L0998, undated]
- Targeting all snowboarders is too broad. Narrowing to beginners or to riders in the park gives a segment with a specific problem in a specific context. The same logic applies to motorcycle products. [L0997, undated]
- A generic product message in a flooded market gets lost. Name a specific audience or problem (for example, a large-dog or specific-breed owner instead of "removes pet hair"). Use broad messages only when the segment is already well understood. [L0995, 2023]

#### Segmentation Dimensions

- Segment the audience in several ways: by pet type (dog, cat), breed, use context (bed, sofa, car, RV) or trigger. Each segment becomes its own persona with its own problems and messages. A product can be re-targeted to another segment if the first one does not perform. [L0450, 2022+]
- Context-based segment from the lesson: owners who travel with pets in RVs. At least 60 percent of RV owners bring their pets, and a small space makes allergies worse. A product positioned as an RV or car pet hair brush, designed for tight spaces, speaks to that context directly. [L0450, 2022+]
- Segment by context or problem, such as beginners versus riders in a park. [L0993, undated]
- A narrow segment can shape the whole store, or it can be carried by the ad alone. A segment focused on the bed problem, for example, can drive the advert even if the store stays broader. [L0997, undated]
- Rebuild a proven product's page for a distinct audience, such as people working from home or gym-goers with neck pain. Use ads, GIFs and copy aimed only at that group. The same product can reach a new market this way. [L0871, 2021+]
- A segment is strong when it has a specific culture, identity or situation, such as a cultural couple segment for jewellery. Validate each segment with research in the niche. [L0995, 2023]

#### Segmentation Examples

- A wallet product that did poorly with broad targeting worked better when aimed at a camping and outdoor audience. The creatives showed the wallet in mountains or at the sea, and the landing page was written for that audience. [L0995, 2023]
- Two snowboard audiences were used: beginners and experienced riders trying new things. Snowboard interests performed better than broad targeting. [L0995, 2023]
- A pet hair removal brush could be aimed at owners of heavy-shedding breeds, such as German Shepherds, whose dogs sleep on the bed and trigger allergies. Owners of other breeds, such as Golden Retrievers or Labradors, would be a weaker segment for that bed-related message. [L0998, undated]
- Pet hair brush research: German Shepherd owners complained most about shedding on the bed. Owners of other large shedders, such as Golden Retrievers and Labradors, also mentioned the dog being on the bed. The bed context came up repeatedly, which validated it as the segment angle. [L0993, undated]

### Awareness, Sophistication and Positioning

#### Awareness Levels

- Target all three awareness levels:
  - Customers who have the problem and already know a solution.
  - Customers who feel the problem but do not know a solution.
  - Customers who do not yet see the problem.
  - Educating unaware customers about a hidden health risk is a route few competitors take. [L0450, 2022+]
- Positioning depends on how informed the market already is. Before drafting copy, gauge the buyer's knowledge of the pain point, their familiarity with the offering, and how many times they have already encountered the same solution elsewhere. [L0450, 2022+]

#### Market Sophistication

- A market shows inverted sophistication when claim complexity is high (every competitor uses identical messaging formulas) but customer understanding is low (most prospects fail basic knowledge tests). This produces jaded customers who are ignorant of the actual dangers. Strategy must shift from aspirational gain-framing ("maximise benefits") to naming specific, irreversible consequences and one-way doors that prospects face ("the decision you make now sets the floor she lives on for life"). [L0221, 2026]
- Prospects give sanitised, socially acceptable answers when speaking publicly, but hold raw, honest, unfiltered thoughts privately. Market research must uncover the private truth. Not "I want to maximise my benefits" but "I don't want to be the fool who left money on the table," because private thoughts, not public statements, drive purchasing decisions. Ads must speak to the genuine driver, not the polished justification. [L0221, 2026]

#### Positioning

- Position around the underlying need, not surface features. Example: a fitness brand positions around staying consistent and removing distractions, not around getting fit. [L0307, 2023+; L0450, 2022+]
- Position a product by the specific problem it solves for a defined group, not by a broad category claim. [L0450, 2022+; L0995, 2023]
- Where a product is sold to a crowded market, the message should name the specific buyer and problem, so the message stands out on social platforms. [L0450, 2022+]

### Defining the target market

- Older source (2021-2022): An e-commerce market has three parts: the products you could sell, the customers who would buy them, and the competitors already selling to those customers. Research has to cover all three before a market is chosen. [L1058, 2021-22]
- A brand should serve a specific market segment with clear characteristics rather than everyone. Niching down lets the store speak to customers in a way they feel understood, and a broad brand can be built later as it grows. [L1044, undated]
- Target audience research covers three core parts:
  - Demographics: who they are.
  - Psychographics: personality and behavior, and how the product fits into their life.
  - Goals, needs and motivations related to the product or the desired outcome.

  A short customer research document is enough to start. [L1044, undated]
- Entering a niche that is already served can still work when a product is specifically adapted to a segment, such as separating a large-dog owner from a general pet owner. Specificity lowers competition in a flooded market where many products already solve the same basic problem. [L0999, 2023]
- Market segmentation by culture can work well for products such as jewelry, because culture defines a specific buyer group. A niche chosen this way can be dominated early by a single brand before broader competition arrives. [L0999, 2023]
- Avoid making up audiences out of thin air. Only target segments that research supports, and validate each segment with reviews, forums or data before building ads around it. [L0999, 2023]
- Check the size of the appeal for a niche before building a product around it. A small niche may require hundreds of wasted impressions for each real prospect. Commit only when the cost per prospect fits the margin. [L1261, undated]
- When many competitors cover the same topic, look for the adjacent subject nobody is covering and build your position there. An overlooked angle near a crowded market can give you visibility that copying the leaders cannot. [L1343, undated]
