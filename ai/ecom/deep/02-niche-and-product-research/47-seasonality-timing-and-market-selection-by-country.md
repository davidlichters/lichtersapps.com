## Seasonality, Timing and Market Selection by Country

### Season and timing principles

- A product must match the season that is active in the target market. A cold-weather product should not run in hot summer months, and seasonal timing matters more than most beginners expect [L0347, 2026].
- The same offer can fail in one month and succeed in another. A winter product that did not sell in April started selling in November when the weather turned cold [L0502, 2020+].
- A product that did poorly in August or September can be retried in November (Q4) and may perform better [L0501, 2022].
- Seasonal products sell most when launched well before the event, because early sellers capture most of the demand. For Christmas products, the recommended timing is a launch in late October, on Halloween or on the day after [L0352, 2022].
- Seasonal products can be copied quickly, and supplier shipping times can delay launches. Prepare the next seasonal product as early as possible to avoid competing with copycats [L0352, 2022].
- Seasonal ideas need early timing. A trend from last year, such as a holiday costume trend, is usually too late once the season has started, so list seasonal products well before the peak [L0372, 2022].
- Seasonal products can be planned months ahead. Check whether competitors already post content for the season, and search older seasonal terms to see whether demand existed in past years [L1186, 2022].
- Use Google Trends to confirm a spike before testing, and check the trend for the product itself, not only its category [L0638, 2023].
- Test seasonal ideas before the season begins. For a summer product, prepare the offer several weeks before the season starts [L0577, 2023].
- Time the test of cooling or ice-based products ahead of the warm season, so the test runs while buying interest is rising. A product tested out of season may show weak sales that do not reflect its real potential [L0657, undated].
- Time the launch to the start of the season in the target market. The speaker launched a summer-type product in Australia around September, when the weather there turned warm [L0661, undated].
- Seasonal products must launch in their peak window. An ice cream product tested late in summer converted poorly, and early summer (about May to June) is the better launch window [L0764, 2022].
- Timing can matter more than the video. A pen product generated about 30,000 USD in one month (August) with about 6 million views because it matched back-to-school demand [L0764, 2022].
- Start research by identifying problems or needs people have in a specific period, then look for trending products that solve them. Do not hunt for winning products at random [L1108, 2023].
- Before a Q4 push, choose a product unique enough to stand out in a crowded marketplace, such as one that solves a problem differently from common alternatives [L0950, 2023].
- Products that are not yet saturated in January and February often have low CPC, because there is less competition for ad space. If you find a working product before that period, buy stock early, since factories may close during the Chinese New Year period [L1347, undated].
- Seasonal products such as kids' toys can be tested before Q4 and Christmas. Start searching early enough to have stock and ads ready before demand peaks [L1454, 2026].

### Q4 and gift products

- Products that peak in Q4 (October through December) include gadgets, jewelry, kids' items, dog and pet products, fashion items, customizable gifts, toys and baby products. Gift products sell especially well because customers buy for others, not only for themselves [L0309, 2024+].
- Gift niches that performed strongest in Q4 were kids toys, personalized jewelry and winter clothing. Christmas gifting drives this demand [L1108, 2023].
- Winners in Q4 included children's gifts, winter clothing, winter shoes that keep feet warm, and personalized jewelry such as name or message pieces. These categories are not to be copied exactly but used as a basis for product research [L1220, undated].
- Personalized gifts and gifts for parents, kids or grandchildren sell well around Christmas and in Q4. Kids toys were also a Q4 product for the lesson's seller [L0774, 2023].
- Gift products such as personalized necklaces, and gifts for parents or kids, suit Christmas [L0773, 2023].
- Non-electronic gifts are preferred over electronics because they carry less customs and quality risk. Children's toys work well in Q4 [L1220, undated].
- Giftable products tend to perform strongly in winter, but shipping times must be reliable, because late delivery is a serious risk around gift periods [L0387, 2022].
- Seasonal winners in Q4 2024 included:
  - hoodies that began in five simple colors and later grew into many designs;
  - family matching clothing for kids, babies and adults in the winter, when energy prices were high;
  - games, toys and puzzles, which also performed well in Q4 when people stayed at home during lockdowns [L0780, 2024+].
- Health supplements show strong Q4 demand around Black Friday and Christmas [L1187, 2026].

### Climate and weather-linked products

- Fashion and seasonal apparel align with climate shifts. Push sweaters and cold-weather items in fall and winter, when customers actually need them [L0209, 2023].
- Look for products that solve a seasonal or environmental problem in the target market. In Switzerland, summer runs roughly from May to the end of September, so cold-weather products have demand during the rest of the year [L0492, 2023+].
- Energy shortage concerns in Europe from 2022 onward made warmth a stronger need, which supported a cold-weather product. Watch current events that affect how people heat or warm their homes. (Dated: this reflects the 2022 situation.) [L0492, 2023+]
- Climate-related products sell better where the cold is extreme. Sweden at minus 20 degrees compares with Switzerland at minus 4 degrees. Check where the use case is strongest before choosing the test market [L1316, 2024+].
- Warm-weather products such as summer outfits or swimwear should be tested in warm markets such as the US or Australia rather than Europe at the start of the year. Cold-weather products, such as winter items, can be tested in every market [L1161, 2024+].
- A season-tied product, such as golf scratch repair in winter, may have low demand at certain times. Cold and rainy weather in Europe can make a warming or comfort product relevant [L1176, 2023].
- In cold months in Europe and the US, products that fit the weather and winter gifting can be tested, such as heated tea cups or tea warmers. Choose products by what fits the current season in the target country, not only by what a spy tool shows [L1108, 2023].
- Tool-type products that solve a practical home problem may work best in Australia, the US and Canada. Weather-related products, such as summer dresses, should be tested in countries where the season is only starting, using a seasonal angle such as a pre-summer sale [L1171, 2024+].
- Relaunch a seasonal niche ahead of its demand peak. For home decor in Switzerland, the spring cleaning season starts about one month before the relaunch, and cold, snowy months showed little interest [L0388, 2024+].
- Weather-linked and seasonal products can be good niches, such as cooling products for summer and warm or pet protection products for winter [L0774, 2023].
- Niches such as camping and adventure perform well when targeting Australia, New Zealand, Canada and the US. Sub-niches with passionate audiences also work well because they make audience targeting clearer [L0788, undated].
- Seasonal product example: Apple Watch straps are a summer product because people walk and do outdoor activities more and the watch is used more. Heat-friendly fabric and bright colors help. In the lesson's test, TikTok ads were cheaper per acquisition than Facebook ads for these straps. A free wallpaper or theme download was used as an add-on offer [L0577, 2023].

### Hemisphere shifts and extending a season

- A seasonal product can be extended by switching to a market in the opposite hemisphere. For example, a winter product can be sold to US buyers in winter while the Australian audience is in summer. This keeps sales going after the home-market season ends [L0347, 2026].
- Seasonal products sell well only in their season, so a seasonal brand needs extra tactics and new markets in the off-season. Testing winter products in Australia while it is summer in Europe can keep sales going [L0502, 2020+].
- Seasons are reversed in the Southern Hemisphere. A product that sold well in the Northern winter can be tested in Southern winter markets such as Australia. Summer products that worked in the Northern hemisphere can be tested there during the Southern summer [L0562, undated].
- Winter products depend on hemisphere. It is summer in the southern hemisphere while it is winter in the north, so check the target market before choosing seasonal items [L0387, 2022].
- Seasonal niches can be tested by checking what sells in one hemisphere's season for the other market. For example, winter products in the US can be tested in Australia during its winter. Searching for a product in the US and selling it in another market is a valid test method [L0678, 2023].
- Products that did well in summer can be tested in Australia, where summer starts when the Northern Hemisphere is in winter. Products that sold well in Australian summer can be tested in Europe or the US during winter [L0773, 2023; L1108, 2023].
- Products that worked in the northern summer can be tested in Australia, which enters summer in Q4 [L0774, 2023].

### Holiday and event launch deadlines

- A holiday is only worth targeting if there is time to prepare and run ads before it. If the holiday is only two to three weeks away, it is generally too late, so choose the next upcoming event instead [L0577, 2023].
- Avoid testing a product with a very close deadline, such as Halloween when it is only a day away. Products for a holiday need to be tested well ahead of time [L1220, undated].
- Launch windows and deadlines differ by step, not by contradiction: the Christmas launch window is late October through Halloween or the day after [L0352, 2022], while a product with a deadline one day away should not be tested [L1220, undated].
- Seasonal products such as Valentine's Day and Mother's Day can be sold, but orders must be delivered before the date to avoid chargebacks. The presenter avoids fixed-date products because of delivery risk and Chinese New Year timing [L1184, 2023+].
- Q4 gift products should be tested early enough to arrive before Christmas. Establish the last date an order can be shipped and still arrive on time [L1220, undated].
- For a Valentine's Day campaign, pre-test content 2 months before the event. For gift products that overlap with Christmas, such as love bracelets and charm jewelry, use the same products and content with adjusted messaging 2 weeks before each occasion [L0309, 2024+].
- For a Valentine's Day campaign, the store is built about one month before the holiday, and the winning product should be found at least about 15 days before the holiday [L1469, 2023+].

### Choosing the target market

- The same niche can be approved in one country and rejected in another, because competitor and supplier targets differ per market. Set the target country before grading any niche [L0490, 2021-22].
- When researching high-ticket dropshipping products, choose the market to sell into first, then select the specific products within that market. This keeps product ideas tied to a market that can support them [L0913, 2021-22].
- Infer the target country from the competitor store. A store in US dollars and English is assumed to target the US. Traffic-estimation tools were unreliable in the mentor's own checks, so trust their country data only when it points overwhelmingly to one country [L1161, 2024+].
- Competitors running ads for the same product usually target the same top four or five countries. Use that cluster as the starting market list for a new test, and expand to other markets only after the first data comes in [L1176, 2023].
- Most dropshippers in the lesson examples target the top markets of the US, UK, Australia, Canada and New Zealand. Products that target the US, Australia and Canada were treated as the main markets to evaluate [L1172, 2023+].
- Starting countries for first testing: the US, Canada, Australia and the UK are the strongest. They are English-speaking, accept credit cards and PayPal, and have good online buying habits [L0645, 2023]. (Conflict note: this is the default for English-speaking tests; when CPM in these markets is too high, the European choice applies instead, see the competition section.)
- The Big Five markets (USA, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand) are the usual default. Smaller markets such as Greece are weaker because of lower buying power and higher shipping cost, which makes dropshipping harder, even though simple pages may convert more easily there [L0691, 2024].
- Targeting options for initial tests: the US, the UK or Australia, or a native-language market such as Germany or France if the operator speaks that language [L1173, undated].
- Test first in a market whose language you speak and whose buying habits you understand, such as your home country. There is usually less competition from dropshippers there. Once you have results you can expand, and you do not have to stay in that country [L1219, undated].
- Germany, Switzerland and Austria can offer lower competition for many products and strong buying power. Test them when capacity allows [L1185, 2023].
- When a shortlist is built for one home market, only test products that suit that country's buyers. A list of 5 to 10 products may yield only two or three candidates for a single market such as Switzerland [L1185, 2023].
- For a trend-based approach, find products trending in the US or Canada over the last two months, then test them in smaller markets such as Australia, New Zealand, Germany and Switzerland [L1184, 2023+].
- Pick products that could sell in German-speaking and English-speaking markets, such as Germany, Switzerland, Austria and Australia. Build a separate store branding and language for each market [L1431, 2020+].
- The ideal product idea is one that is already selling well in another market and can be brought to a new one. Find the problem first, then the solution [L1432, undated].
- Sending a product to multiple countries may reveal different results. A product that failed in one market can work in another. Consider countries with e-commerce potential beyond the usual German-speaking markets, and test them [L1316, 2024+].
- Start in the country where you speak the language well. For an English speaker, that is the US. Starting in a country with a language barrier adds work at the beginning [L0962, 2021].
- France was one of the first markets where shoppers encountered dropshipping, so French buyers easily recognize generic dropshipping stores. A French launch needs custom content and full native translation, and it still requires research into local brands and trust [L1158, 2023].
- In the EU, fashion is presented as a strong niche for general stores, especially in France, where fashion and orthopedic sneakers sell well. Beauty products also perform well in France [L0660, 2019+].
- Test products through pricing and messaging in a single market first, then expand to other countries once the audience is clear [L0995, 2023].

### Competition, CPM and market-size checks

- Check market competition in each country before choosing it. Alternative markets such as Germany, Switzerland, Austria, the Scandinavian countries, France and Belgium were recommended over the US and Canada, where ad costs are high [L0492, 2023+].
- Canada, Australia and the US were described as expensive for testing in recent periods, which pushed the choice toward European markets. Check current CPM in each country before choosing a test market [L1219, undated].
- A store focused only on Canada usually faces less competition than a store primarily targeting the US market. The same logic applies to other English-speaking markets such as Australia or the UK [L0905, 2021-22].
- Products trending in the US can be sold in other markets, where competition is lower. A US trend tested on TikTok produced weaker results than expected [L0492, 2023+].
- Choose products that are trending in the United States and sell them in other markets such as Germany, Switzerland, Austria, Australia, New Zealand or Canada. Competition there is lower, and the combined German-speaking market (Germany, Switzerland, Austria) is about 100 million people [L0519, 2020+].
- Saturation in the US does not rule out a product. Being an early adopter in a new market and offering a better offer can still produce strong results, even if many advertisers run the same product in the US [L0519, 2020+].
- Avoid launching a product in the United States when it is already heavily sold there, since saturation makes it hard to win. Test instead in markets where the same product is not yet common, such as Australia, New Zealand, Germany, Switzerland or Austria [L0661, undated].
- Products already advertised heavily in one market are proof of demand, so they are a good starting point for research. Take such a product and test it in a different market where competition is lower [L0661, undated].
- An ad running in only one country tells you that the product may already sell there, not that it will fail elsewhere. A product with strong engagement in one market can be tested in another [L0661, undated].
- A product that is saturated in the US, which has high CPMs, can still be a valid candidate when moved to a cheaper market, where competition is often lower and ads cost less. Test the transferred product in the new market before judging it [L1161, 2024+].
- Target countries where the product is in season and CPMs are lower. For seasonal holidays such as Halloween, consider other countries with lower CPM if the US CPM is too high [L0773, 2023].
- Halloween is celebrated in many countries, not only the US. For seasonal demand such as Halloween, sell in countries where CPM is lower if the US CPM is too high [L0774, 2023].
- Niche criteria must be adjusted to the market size. The US demand numbers are not comparable with Canada because of population differences [L0805, 2024] (2024).
- Countries with small populations, such as Canada, do not work with the same niche criteria as the US. Use revised criteria for smaller markets instead of applying US thresholds [L1285, 2024] (2024).
- For a demand test in a smaller country, a population share can justify a lower target. If the UK has about 20 percent of the US population, either find a bigger niche or move one level broader rather than lowering the target blindly [L0962, 2021].
- The US niche validation uses a demand threshold of 10,000. Smaller markets such as Australia have fewer sub-niches, so sellers there may need to niche up a level to reach that threshold [L1088, 2020].
- Niches selling to a small population should be widened to a broader category so enough visitors arrive. Large-population markets can use very specific niche stores, which convert better [L1111, 2020].
- Pandemic conditions in 2020 did not change the product selection criteria. Recession or fear-driven concerns should not be used to drop a niche that passes the tests, because online buying remained strong [L0967, 2020].
