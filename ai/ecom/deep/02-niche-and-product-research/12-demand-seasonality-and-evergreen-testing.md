## Demand, Seasonality and Evergreen Testing

### Two tests before time is spent on a niche

- Validate every niche with at least two tests before investing time: demand (minimum 10,000 average monthly Google searches) and seasonality (evergreen check). Drop niches that fail either test early. [L0451, 2020-26]
- The Demand Test checks that people actually want the products in a potential niche. A pass validates real market demand. [L0047, undated]
- The Evergreen Test decides whether a niche is seasonal or evergreen, meaning it has year-round demand. [L0047, undated]
- Run the evergreen test on each niche to confirm steady demand across the year, not peaks at certain times. [L1057, 2020-26]
- Before committing time to a niche, verify that enough buyers exist, using market research. [L0769, 2020-26]
- Avoid seasonal niches for the first store. Profit is concentrated in a small time window, which lowers viability. [L0047, undated; L0855, 2023; L1002, 2019; older]
- Market research matters most for long-term product survival, so judge a product on sustained demand trends, not on a short spike in ads or searches. [L0636, undated]

### Demand threshold: the 10,000-search minimum

- The minimum is 10,000 average monthly Google searches per niche, measured as a consistent monthly average over months. [L0451, 2020-26]
- At or above 10,000 in the country where you sell is a green light to continue. [L1072, 2020-26]
- Mark any niche below 10,000 in red and deprioritize it. [L0045, undated]
- Read the number against the threshold only. Do not compare it with other niches' volumes. A similar monthly range for different niches is expected. [L0451, 2020-26]
- The threshold is one criterion among many, not a standalone winner marker. Viability comes from combining all research criteria. [L0971, 2024]
- In 2024 a core keyword at about 10,000 monthly searches was still the working threshold for a viable niche. It is always judged together with competition, Google Shopping results and other research data. No fixed 500,000-search requirement applies. [L0971, 2024]
- The demand figure is the average monthly searches for the niche keyword reported by the research tool. [L1081, 2020-26]
- The minimum is also reported as "about 10,000 per month" in a 2022 lesson. [L0969, 2022; older]
- A 2023 lesson applies the same 10,000 figure to the niche keyword and the average across the niche, and says to use it together with the other research tests, not on its own. [L0934, 2023; L0835, 2023]
- A 2018 lesson set the same minimum for the UK and the US, measured as search volume, not clicks. [L1085, 2018; older]
- Above 10,000, no specific higher or lower number has proven to predict better store success. [L0666, 2024]
- Search volume above 10,000 does not reliably indicate higher or lower success or competition. Volume alone is not a ranking signal. Judge success over time, after the store is live, optimized, and has more suppliers and customer data. [L0666, 2024]
- A niche above 1 million searches has no upper limit. Narrowing it to a sub-niche is optional and only makes sense if it sharpens the store's focus. [L0663, 2019; older]
- Average monthly volume reflects general interest in a niche, not the traffic you will get. Actual traffic comes from brand, supplier and SKU keywords, so conversion math based on niche volume is not useful for forecasting. [L0803, 2022]
- A 2018 lesson also checks that the average product price is $200 or more alongside 10K Google searches and evergreen demand. [L1109, 2018; older]

#### Thresholds by market (sources differ; each applies to a different test)

| Market / measure | Minimum | What it applies to | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Any target country, niche keyword (demand test) | 10,000 average monthly searches in the country where you sell | Demand test for a niche | [L1072, 2020-26; L1085, 2018] |
| US and Canada targeted together | 90,500 US searches is sufficient even if Canada shows 8,100 | Combined two-country targeting | [L0268, 2020] |
| Small-population countries, e.g. New Zealand | Niche up until the niche reaches about 10,000 | Demand test in a small market | [L0806, 2024] (2024) |
| US, main keyword for a product type | 10,000 | Main keyword check | [L0458, 2021-22] |
| Australia or smaller country, main keyword | 5,000 | Main keyword check | [L0458, 2021-22] |
| US, combined niche total | 50,000 | Traffic volume | [L1485, 2021-22] |
| Australia and other countries, combined niche total | 20,000 | Traffic volume | [L1485, 2021-22] |

- The sources disagree. The 10,000 figure of the demand test and the 10,000 / 5,000 figures for a main keyword apply to one keyword check. The 50,000 / 20,000 figures apply to the combined total of all relevant product keywords in a niche, used as the traffic test. [L0458, 2021-22; L1485, 2021-22]
- The 10,000 minimum stays the same for every country. A smaller market gets no lower threshold. A product that falls short there needs a broader or combined niche to reach 10,000 in that market. [L1072, 2020-26]
- Population size does not change how you test demand. Judge a product by its search volume in the target country, not by how many people live there. [L1072, 2020-26]
- Do not decide that a small country cannot support a product because its population is small. Use the search-volume test for that country. [L1072, 2020-26]
- Do not compare a smaller country's volume with US numbers and reject it for being lower. The target country's own volume is the only one that matters. [L1072, 2020-26]
- A niche that fails the 10,000 minimum in a smaller country does not mean you change your research method. You need a niche in that country that reaches 10,000. [L1072, 2020-26]
- Example: stand-up desks had 6,600 searches in the UK (fail) and 40,000 in the US (pass). [L1072, 2020-26]
- Example: stand-up paddleboards showed about 40,500 average monthly US searches in 2020, used as a volume check when comparing product keywords. [L1111, 2020; older]

### Demand test procedure in research tools

Procedure, the source's own site (demand test for a target country). [L1072, 2020-26]

1. Open the keyword research tool at the source's own site
2. Select the target country.
3. Search the product keyword, such as "stand-up desk".
4. Read the search volume shown for that country.
5. If the volume is 10,000 or more, the niche passes and you can move forward.
6. If the volume is below 10,000, niche up to a broader category and test again.
7. If it is still below 10,000, test an alternative niche.
8. Only if no single niche reaches 10,000, combine two similar niches so their combined search volume reaches 10,000.

Procedure, one source research tool (niche test). [L0451, 2020-26]

1. Copy the niche name from the niche research worksheet.
2. Open the research tool, set the country dropdown to the selling country (default US), paste the niche and click Search.
3. Read the monthly search volume. If it is at least 10,000, the demand test passes (green box). If not, the box is red and the text says the niche fails.
4. Copy the monthly search number into the worksheet's demand column.
5. Read the evergreen result. A green box with the message that the niche shows little to no seasonality means it passes. A seasonal result means it fails.
6. Copy the average price from the price analysis at the bottom of the tool into the worksheet's average price column, rounded as shown.
7. Mark the seasonality column yes or no accordingly.
8. Refresh the page before testing the next niche.

- Set the country dropdown to the country where you plan to sell, not where you are located. The default is US. [L0451, 2020-26]
- In the research tool, a passing niche shows a green result box with a message that it passes. A failing niche shows a red box, and the text names the failed test, either demand or evergreen (seasonality). [L0451, 2020-26; L1075, 2025+]
- The tool analyzes the search-volume graph itself and reports seasonality automatically, so you do not need to read the graph manually to judge seasonality. [L1075, 2025+]
- Example: a wine refrigerator niche showed over 14,800 monthly searches, passed the demand test, got a green evergreen box for little to no seasonality, and passed the price analysis. [L1075, 2025+]
- Example: a Christmas tree stand niche passed demand but failed the evergreen test for strong seasonality. Its average price of $43.15 also failed the price analysis. The tool shows the reason in the text box next to the result. [L1075, 2025+]

### Keyword tools and their limits

#### Country and setup rules

- Select the target country before running a search. Changing the country after a search still shows the earlier country's data. [L0247, 2021; older]
- Select the country first when checking whether a tool returns real results. Zero results in an otherwise working account can signal a glitch worth reporting to support. [L0853, 2021; older]
- The same keyword has different volumes in different countries, so select the target country before searching. [L1072, 2020-26]
- A tool's keyword data for a brand-new Google Ads account gives very broad search volume ranges. The data becomes more accurate once the account starts spending money. [L1277, 2020; older]
- Check whether a keyword has an accurate figure before you rely on it. The rank of a demand-tool result should fall between 1 and 1,000,000. A reading above 2,000,000 is not expected and should be double-checked before use. [L0360, 2024]
- Demand graphs built on low search volume become spiky and volatile, because they rely on few average searches. Spikes on low-volume keywords carry little signal. Check the search volume figure at the top of the tool before reading the trend line. [L0360, 2024]
- A flat line at the start of a trend graph usually means search volume was too low to show, not that the niche is seasonal. [L0806, 2024]

#### Cost-per-click and competition figures

- Ignore CPC during niche research. Focus on long-tail product-level search volume instead. CPM varies by broader niche category and does not reflect your specific product opportunity. [L0272, 2022; older]
- For niche demand research, look at average monthly search volume for broad niche keywords to confirm people search for what you want to sell. Ignore the CPC for those broad terms, because ads run on product-level keywords that cost much less. [L0551, 2020; older]
- Ubersuggest can show average monthly searches for broad niche keywords. Use the volume and ignore the CPC shown for those broad terms. [L0551, 2020; older]
- CPC and competition figures from Google Keyword Planner or Ubersuggest refer to Google search text ads, not Google Shopping. Do not use them to set Shopping bids. [L0852, 2020; older]
- Measure demand by search volume, not impressions. If a product gets many impressions but few clicks, look up its search volume in Google Keyword Planner to see whether people search for it at all. [L1085, 2018; older]

### Keyword selection and search intent

#### Finding product keywords (procedure)

Procedure, finding specific product keywords from a broad category (Google). [L0251, 2023]

1. Go to Google.
2. Type your broad product category (e.g., "aquarium").
3. Review the autocomplete suggestions.
4. Note which suggestions are actual products and which are non-product searches (e.g., "aquariums near me").
5. Scroll to the bottom of the search results and review "Related searches".
6. Use the related suggestions Google provides (e.g., "aquarium tanks" instead of "aquarium") to identify specific product keywords that match your target customer's search intent.
7. Use specific product keywords instead of broad terms for your niche research.

- To find more rising keywords, use Google Keyword Planner. [L0636, undated]
  1. Click "discover new keywords".
  2. Search a broad keyword related to your product, such as "dog leash" or "lights".
  3. In the results, click the "three months change" column to see which searches grew over the last three months.
  4. Use the results for product name ideas, SEO keywords and marketing angles.

Procedure, finding more search demand for a product with low volume (2019). [L1002, 2019; older]

1. Search the product term on google.com.
2. Scroll to the footer and note the "searches related to" terms.
3. Check the search volume of each related term, such as "kids ride-on cars", "push ride-on toys" or "ride-on toys".
4. If the combined demand is still under $10K, move one level up to a broader category (for example "kids rockers") and combine two niches to reach $10K.

#### Intent: buying terms, qualifiers and brands

- When checking keyword demand, pick keywords whose search intent is clearly to buy the product, such as "barbecue grills", not terms used for other purposes, such as "Korean barbecue restaurants". This step needs judgment, so the demand numbers alone are not enough. [L0965, 2024]
- Example: the US term for a generic sauna showed about 165K monthly searches, but many are for gym or public saunas. Adding "home" dropped it to about 33,000, the number that matches home buyers. Keep or remove an adjective based on whether it changes buyer intent for the product you sell. [L1090, 2021; older]
- If a brand name gets more search volume than the niche keyword, the niche keyword probably has too many qualifiers. Remove qualifying words from the niche search and check the results again. Never use a brand name as the name of your store. [L0965, 2024]
- Very broad keywords rarely convert, because shoppers searching them are often not ready to buy. Target specific buying keywords rather than broad category terms. [L1088, 2020; older]
- Buyers in brand-less niches search for product attributes such as a specific size, color and style. Those attribute searches create keywords you can appear for. Selling through a brand site or a comparison shopper still leads buyers to your store. [L0804, 2023]
- Use 5 to 10 keywords for trend analysis: two or three direct product keywords plus related problem or niche keywords, since people search for the problem before the product. Compare alternative product names and keywords to choose the wording for copy and ad titles. [L0895, 2021+]
- Compare product name variants in Google Trends and in a dropshipping research tool to see which term has more search volume. Then run the research with one chosen term. [L0970, 2023]
- Broad keywords can inflate traffic estimates, because they mix uses that do not match your product. Searches for "winches" include boat and trailer uses, and keyword lists can include unrelated meanings such as a firearm brand or inch-to-metre conversions. Only count terms that match what you plan to sell. [L1485, 2021-22; older]
- Mark the traffic-volume question yes only if the main keyword meets the minimum for its market. [L0458, 2021-22; older]

#### one source's keyword procedure (Ubersuggest, 2021-22)

Procedure, Ubersuggest keyword check for a market idea. [L1485, 2021-22; older]

1. Open the Keyword Ideas section.
2. Set the country dropdown to the market you will sell in, for example United States, United Kingdom, Australia or Canada.
3. Enter your market idea as a keyword, for example "4x4 accessories", and click Search.
4. Read the VOL column, which shows average monthly searches for that exact keyword in the selected country.
5. Review the similar keyword suggestions and add the relevant variations to your count.
6. If a broad term mixes several product types, search narrower terms such as "winches for trucks" and count those separately.
7. Ignore keywords with unrelated meanings and add up the relevant ones.
8. Enter the combined estimate in the market research tracker.

Procedure, Ubersuggest keyword check. [L0458, 2021-22; older]

1. Open a keyword tool such as Ubersuggest in your browser. The lesson says anyone can use it without signing up, though a CAPTCHA check may appear.
2. Set the country to your market, for example the United States.
3. Enter the main keyword for your product type and read its monthly search volume.
4. Check the variations shown, such as "for sale" or "for home" versions, and add them to your total.
5. Mark traffic volume as yes if the main keyword meets the minimum.

- Ubersuggest (ubersuggest.com) is a free keyword tool that shows monthly search volume for a term. Set the country before searching. [L0771, 2021-22; older]

### Combining related products and niching up

- If a niche is too narrow to pass the search-volume criteria, combine closely related product types, such as charcoal and gas barbecues, rather than unrelated ones. The charcoal barbecue example had about 6,500 monthly searches. [L0965, 2024]
- Procedure, reaching 10,000 in the target country (research tool). If the volume is below 10,000, niche up; if still below, test an alternative; only if no single niche reaches 10,000, combine two similar niches. [L1072, 2020-26]
- Small-population countries: niche up (broaden the niche to a higher level) until it reaches the minimum. [L0806, 2024]
- Very small sub-niches can still reach roughly 2 to 3 million people on Facebook, and about half may show buying intent. A small niche is worth testing if the pain point is clear and the ad shows it in the first few seconds. [L0638, 2023]
- A niche that feels small can still contain millions of people in the target audience, so even a small conversion rate from a narrow ad can indicate a viable market. [L1016, 2026]
- Niches can take off when one seller tests a product and others follow on TikTok and Facebook. Watch fast-growing sub-niches, such as golf in recent years, and track which products appear in new sub-niches to spot the trend early. [L0871, 2021+]
- Niche accessories that are very expensive, such as premium phone cases, are unlikely to have enough monthly searches to be worth it. [L0855, 2023; older]
- If a product term stays near zero, search the broader niche or the problem, feeling or occasion the buyer has (e.g., "kids drawing", "gifts for Valentine's"). [L1183, undated]
- Demand that follows the product, the problem and the wider market points to different levels of opportunity. Compare them in Google Trends. [L0708, undated]
- A gift product can be promoted for two close occasions, such as Valentine's Day and Mother's Day, using the same product. Plan the creator content and ad angles for each occasion separately. [L1379, 2020+]
