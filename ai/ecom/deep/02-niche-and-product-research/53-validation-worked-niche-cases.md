### Validation: worked niche cases

- Generator niche validation (US, 2021 setting): the owner searched Google Shopping for generators and saw many listings above 1,000 USD, so the price test passed with an average of 1,000 USD or more. More than 10 brands were found on Google Shopping and a supplier site, so the supplier test passed. Competitor counts per product were mostly 5 or fewer or borderline at 10 plus, so competition passed. Recurring potential existed through log splitters, snowblowers and pressure washers. Search volume was high across the related keywords, so traffic passed. The enthusiast question was rated maybe because some buyers need a generator and others want one, giving 5 yes answers out of 6. [L0771, 2021-22]
- Bunkers, prepping and off-grid: average price 800 USD, products suitable for shipping, classed as a want and therefore an enthusiast market. There were 18 suppliers and 8 direct competitors, and a range of product types with healthy search volume. 18 suppliers passed the Australian target of 10 but would count against a US target of 20, so it was approved (green) for Australia. [L0490, 2021-22]
- First idea: average price 1,000 USD, products good for shipping, enthusiast market, 20+ suppliers, 13 direct competitors, recurring revenue and at least 20,000 combined monthly searches. With 13 competitors it passes the US limit of 15 and was approved for the US, but it fails the Australian limit of 10 and was rejected for Australia. [L0490, 2021-22]
- CNC machines and tables: average price about 3,000 USD, so high ticket, and shipping was mostly acceptable despite some larger products. The niche is an enthusiast market with 8 suppliers, which is low against the Australian target of 10, and 5 direct competitors, described as the minimum acceptable for Australia. Traffic volume was okay but lower, and with no meaningful recurring revenue, because buyers keep one machine for years, it was graded maybe (orange) and not a strong option. [L0490, 2021-22]
- Paintball: the paintball guns cannot be advertised on Google, which is a strikeout. Cheap accessories pulled the average price down to 200 USD, below the 800 USD minimum, and the few high-ticket items, such as machines that produce paintballs, were overseas and very large, so the niche was rejected (red). [L0490, 2021-22]
- Worked example of a 4x4 accessories niche for the United States, in order:
  1. Recurring revenue: the niche sells several products to the same owner, such as rear cargo drawers, nudge bars, turbo systems, suspension and wheels, so the answer is yes. The competitor sites were also checked, and they sell a range of products. [L1486, 2021-22]
  2. Keyword research in Ubersuggest with the country set to United States, starting with 4x4 accessories, which shows 1,000 monthly searches as an exact match. The variant 4x4 accessories off road adds 720 searches. Other relevant variants, such as accessories for 4x4 trucks, bring the relevant total to about 2,000. The term 4x4 accessories near me was removed because it is a local search. [L1486, 2021-22]
  3. The broader US term truck accessories was checked because US buyers often use it, and it shows 60,000 monthly searches. It is a much more common term than 4x4 accessories and confirms that the market is large. [L1486, 2021-22]
  4. Product keywords were checked: winches for jeeps 6,600, winches for trucks 6,600, winches with synthetic rope 5,400, winches 12,000 lb 2,900 and winches for sale 2,400. The broad winches term was not used because it includes trailer winches. Winches for UTVs was judged a product that could also be sold. [L1486, 2021-22]
  5. Bumper keywords were checked: bumpers for trucks 5,500, a second matching bumper keyword 5,500, and winch bumpers for trucks 320, plus smaller close variations. [L1486, 2021-22]
  6. The 50,000 target was passed quickly. The estimated total for the whole niche was about 200,000 monthly searches, which was entered into the tracker's Traffic Volume column. [L1486, 2021-22]
  7. The tracker entry for the niche also recorded an average price of $1000, 13 direct competitors, recurring revenue YES, and suppliers for rear drawers, turbo systems, nudge bars, bull bars and winches. [L1486, 2021-22]
- The 4x4 accessories market idea was checked for traffic in the US using Ubersuggest. The exact term 4x4 accessories showed 1,000 monthly searches, and the related term 4x4 accessories off road added another 720, so the relevant total grew to about 2,000 after adding a few extra relevant variations. The broader term truck accessories showed 60,500 monthly searches, which the researcher judged as a large market signal and used as the more common US wording. Then winches showed about 60,000 searches, but most of those related to uses unrelated to trucks, so the researcher narrowed it to winches for trucks with 6,600 monthly searches. Bumpers for trucks showed 5,500 searches and truck bumpers variants added more, for example winch bumpers for trucks with 320. The relevant total passed the 50,000 US threshold easily and was entered as an estimate of about 200,000 searches per month, which completed the research for that idea. [L1485, 2021-22]

### Price and profit illustrations

- Persian rugs: grade A, average price $2,319.13, average profit about $580 per sale, passes demographic, brand loyalty and seasonality tests, 49,500 monthly searches. [L1081, 2020-26]
- Woven rugs: grade A-, average price $186.16, average profit only $47 per sale, passes the three tests, demand of 5,400 monthly searches. This is about 10% of the demand for Persian rugs and roughly one tenth of the profit per sale. [L1081, 2020-26]
- Oriental rugs: grade A, average price $484.08, average profit $121 per sale, passes the other three tests, demand of 22,200 monthly searches. [L1081, 2020-26]
- The top 5 niches in the example all have a minimum profit of $854 per sale, pass the price point, demographic, brand loyalty and seasonality tests, and have demand above 10,000 monthly searches. [L1081, 2020-26]
- CNC machines: average price $11,000, average profit about $2,700 per sale, most rated A+ by the research tool, with pass results for the tests in the lesson. The high price means pre-selling by phone is usually needed before the order, so the niche only suits someone willing to take phone calls. [L1081, 2020-26]
- The CNC starting product in the validation example was priced at about $1,900, while the average price benchmark here is $11,000. Both figures come from different data sets, so apply each to the price range it describes. [L0459, 2020-26; L1081, 2020-26]
- Massage chairs: average price $3,400, average profit $854 per sale, 110,000 monthly searches. Some buyers may still call before ordering because the price is above $2,000, but the niche looks strong. [L1081, 2020-26]
- Rooftop tents: price about $2,000, profit about $500 per sale, 33,000 monthly searches. Buyers at this price usually purchase online without calls, making it a fit for a hands-off business. [L1081, 2020-26]
- Budget dining table: a dining table sells for $500 with free shipping. A big, heavy table of this kind costs about $250 to ship on average, and the seller's COGS is also $250. Before customer acquisition, card fees, the seller's time and traffic costs, net profit is $0, and once those costs are added every sale loses money. The conclusion is not that dining tables are a bad niche but that this price point is too low for the item's size and weight. [L1180, 2020-26]
- Premium dining table: a solid wood dining table sells for $1,200 with free shipping. Because it is heavier, the average shipping cost is $300, and the COGS is $600, which is 50% of the selling price. Total costs are $900, so net profit is $300 per order before other expenses. The $300 shipping figure is a high-end assumption that applies to roughly the top 2 to 3 percent of orders, since most orders ship for less. If supplier and customer are both in California, shipping drops to $150 while COGS and price stay the same, raising net profit to $450 per order. Even a cross-country shipment at $300 still nets $300 per order. [L1180, 2020-26]
- Gun safes: a large, heavy safe priced at $200 cannot be shipped profitably with free shipping. Such safes are viable only in the roughly $800 to $1,000-plus price range, where the margin covers COGS and shipping. [L1180, 2020-26]
- A liver detox supplement example was judged promising with these figures: 46,000 dollars revenue in the last 30 days, 1,008 to 1,100 units sold, a product price of 34.18 dollars, a shipping fee of 5.00 dollars and a 30 percent commission rate. Its revenue graph went up and down, and its ads were mostly bottom-funnel with strong editing and a varied set of avatars. [L1187, 2026]
- The liver detox example was checked further over the last 90 days: the revenue chart peaked at about 3k and then started to slow down. Before committing, the trend was reviewed again, and the decision was to customize the ads rather than copy them. [L1187, 2026]

### Market signals: views, sales and ad counts

- Benchmark case: a Korean calcium multi-balm stick sold about 50k per day and about 1.5 million in the last 30 days in the United States, with a sold item count of about 68k in the same window. Its average unit price was about 22.27 dollars, while the displayed product price was 23.00 dollars. [L0707, 2026]
- The teach-your-child-to-read book showed these figures for the last 30 days (05/25 to 06/23 on screen): a price of 32.90 USD, a commission rate of 15%, a shipping fee of 5.99 USD, ship from local, and an earliest recorded date of 10/13/2025. Revenue was 991.29 USD (141.81 USD per day), of which video revenue was 566.58 USD (80.94 USD per day) and product card revenue was 424.71 USD (60.67 USD per day), with live revenue at 0.00 USD and an average unit price of about 33.04 USD. The presenter planned to list the book on a Shopify product page, run ads and check whether demand held for the next two to three days. The result was not shown in the lesson. [L0354, 2026]
- The leather jacket single-niche store in the Netherlands and Belgium was cited as doing about 20k per day with Facebook ads, even with some refunds. Currency was not stated in the source. [L0660, 2019+]
- A TikTok account sold a showerhead with a vortex effect, and the product was also listed on Amazon. The account grew mainly through organic posts, reaching about 20 million views on one video and about 43,000 followers. Once organic sales were coming in, the brand had enough revenue to fund branding, paid ads and sponsorships from upcoming TikTok creators. The brand became well known, but the account later stopped posting for a long time, so a product needs repeated organic reach in the millions, and consistent posting matters more than a single hit. [L0706, 2020+]
- A seller used a proven product found through a community call and added it to an existing general Shopify store in about three hours by reusing the store design and adding the product link to TikTok. The product generated about 100 orders in its first three days, after the seller had tested four earlier products without a clear winner. [L0372, 2022]
- A product that won on the fifth or sixth test was run with three creatives: one or two videos and one or two pictures, all edited from supplier content. The product kept working for nearly two years afterwards. A different product later produced about $2,000 in sales but could not be scaled, so it was stopped and a new product was tested. [L0349, 2024]

### Viral and challenge tests

- A TikTok challenge account for an instant ice cream product that the team was given, not chosen, was tested late in the summer season. About six videos were drafted. In the first two days the checkered red-and-white background looked messy and results were weak. A blue juice video using a corn trend reached only about 7,000 views, while comments kept mentioning Breaking Bad. The team posted a video reply to a top comment built on that Breaking Bad reference, which reached about 1.1 million views and went viral. [L0764, 2022]
- On day three the team changed the background and switched the whole account to Breaking Bad themed videos using dialogue and theme songs with on-screen text. Four videos passed 1 million views, and the account reached about 8 million views in two weeks (nearly 10 million total as later stated). It had around 400,000 likes, but only about 1% converted to followers, against an expected ~40,000 followers. The Instagram version reached about 500 followers after late posting and did not convert sales. [L0764, 2022]
- Uploads were 3 per day, 4 on the viral day, posted around 12:00, around 16:00 and between 18:00 and 19:00. Sales did not reach the team's sales-per-million-views target, so the challenge was treated as a views exercise and uploads stopped when it ended. Ad spend was zero; the only costs were the product, which was returned for a refund, and food. [L0764, 2022]

### Ad and audience signals

- Lumera-style evidence: a filtered search for ads running at least seven days is a starting point for finding validated ads. The approach is covered under the skincare and hair cases above. [L0772, 2026]
- The product of a leading seller with a validated product and a strong audience was a gift relationship case; see the customized message gift above. [L1185, 2023]
- A health or supplement brand can gain audiences it did not expect; see the pain-relief supplement case above. [L0523, 2015+]
- A necklace store began as a one-product store, targeting African Americans and Africans in the US. The operator sourced the same design from an AliExpress supplier, which offered a golden necklace for every African country. [L0519, 2020+]
- The operator started dropshipping in June 2019 with a first store built from YouTube tutorials and ran Facebook ads himself for six months without success, because he did not understand CTR and CPM. He joined a mentorship in January 2020 and tested five to seven products for about 2000 USD, with no profitable result. His eighth product, an Africa map necklace, was found in late March or early April 2020 and recovered all earlier losses. [L0519, 2020+]

### Failed and stopped products

- A cheap hair-removal shaver sourced from AliExpress at about 10 USD or less was sold at 29.99 USD to match competitors, with a 60% profit margin. The product was tested by its own team for two weeks with consistent uploads and got about 8 million views, with one video at about 5 million views. Sales reached about 5,000 USD, of which about 3,000 USD was kept after costs. Views and sales did not match: 5,000 USD on 8 million views is about 625 USD per million views, below the 1,000 USD per million target. Quality problems (the product worked only for 3 to 5 uses, caused skin redness and burns, and was awkward to use) led to exposure, and the team stopped the product. Competitors had used one consistent female model and reused the same intros until their views dropped. [L1444, 2022]
- The paintball niche was rejected because its guns cannot be advertised on Google and its average price fell below the minimum. [L0490, 2021-22]
- A health product that sold over 745 units was skipped because its ads were heavily bottom-funnel. [L1187, 2026]
- The liver detox example was not committed to until its 90-day revenue slowdown was reviewed. [L1187, 2026]

### Seasonal and short-term products

- Trampolines and motorcycle helmets both showed strong seasonality and failed the evergreen test, so both were moved to red. [L0451, 2020-26]
- Halloween and gifting products with a holiday angle are seasonal and will fade after the event. [L0352, 2022; L1440, 2021+]
- A seasonal product should be checked against its sales window before committing to a store around it. [L1440, 2021+]
