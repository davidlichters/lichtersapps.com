## Scaling the store: more suppliers, niches, content and delegation

### Adding suppliers and products in stages

**Supplier approval and catalogue build**
1. Get approved with the first one or two brands. [L0468, 2020]
2. Upload every product from those brands, using CSV files for speed. [L0468, 2020]
3. Upload the next brands only after the first ones are complete. [L0468, 2020]
4. Aim for at least 100 different products before running Google ads. [L0468, 2020]

- A brand can start as a parked Shopify store with no products while the audience is being built. Products launch later, so audience reactions can guide what to sell. [L0487, 2018]
- The store can go live and advertising can start before every product is uploaded. In markets with many products, upload a first set, launch, and add more after launch. [L1166, 2021-22]
- Keep adding quality supplier products while adjusting ads, even if the store is not yet profitable. [L0969, 2022]
- Source the product and confirm the supplier can ship it before running any ad. Never start an ad for a product you cannot fulfil. [L1171, 2024+]
- Look for missing variants such as colours and sizes on competitor sites, for example light and dark wood finishes or new colours. Upload all available variants so shoppers who want a specific option choose your store. [L0894, 2020-26]
- Oberlo and AliExpress are named as sources for product supply and price markup. [L0838, 2020-26]
- Replace AliExpress or other Chinese products on an existing store with US suppliers only if the store's niche fits the niche criteria of modules one and two (2023). [L0964, 2023]
- A native Shopify supplier integration is a win. External enterprise-level apps are not recommended for a new store (2024). [L0971, 2024]
- Adding related products to an existing store is fine when analytics, Facebook pages, Google Merchant Center and Google Ads are already connected. Do not recreate them for a new store. [L0834, 2021]

**Product count: sources differ by purpose**

| Situation | Product count | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Testing a product on a general store | One product and one product page first; expand after sales show buyers | [L0388, 2024+; L1217, 2023+] |
| Multi-product store running Google ads | At least 100 different products | [L0468, 2020] |
| Multi-product store target | At least 20 brands and 100 products; more variety drives more traffic and steadier sales than a one-product store | [L0852, 2020] |
| Done-for-you base catalogue | Designer loads about 20 base products; owner adds more; stores usually need at least 100 products | [L1281, 2022] |
| Overbuilt test store | Fashion store with more than 250 products and about USD 2,000 spent before demand was proven produced one sale and then a chargeback in the first month of ads | [L0660, 2019+] |

- A store with no revenue has almost no resale value. Test a few products with a small budget before building a full catalogue. [L0660, 2019+]

### Testing niches and products in one general store

**Start small and expand on proof**
1. Start a new product test with one product and a product page. [L0388, 2024+]
2. Once sales show the audience buys, move to a collection page with several items to increase cross-sales and average order value. [L0388, 2024+]
- A general store can be used for testing with one product page. Building a community and a brand requires a niche store, so move to a niche store once a product is proven. [L0335, 2023]
- Start with a general store under a brandable random domain rather than a single-product store, then test products one by one. Focus effort on landing pages with a strong offer and trust elements. [L0637, 2023+]
- During product testing, a simple store is enough to collect data. Invest in a polished design, stronger branding and a product page builder after one product shows clear winning numbers. [L1176, 2023]
- A beginner is better off building one reusable general store personally than paying a freelancer to build a product page for every test product. Outsourcing each test becomes expensive, while skills carry over to every later test. [L1212, undated]

**One domain, one general store for many tests**
- Run one brand-style, generic domain name (under 10 characters, easy to say and remember, for example made-up names checked with Namelix). Test products by adding a new product page rather than a new store. [L1410, 2023]
- The home page needs only 2-3 sections: a product banner, the product section, and trust badges such as priority shipping and a money-back guarantee. [L1410, 2023]
- Keep one general store domain for all products tested in the same broad category, such as shoes. Buy a new domain and brand only when a proven product moves to its own store or a different market. [L1131, 2018+]
- Using one domain for all tests avoids the cost and friction of buying new stores and domains. A single-product focus with a strong product page still builds a brand identity. [L1217, 2023+]
- In a new store, test one product at a time instead of loading dozens of products. Each product gets its own focused page. [L1217, 2023+]
- Every new Shopify store requires its own home page, legal pages, notification settings and checkout setup. This is the main reason to keep testing products on one domain and only add a new product page. [L1410, 2023]
- Keeping a single store and swapping products is the cheaper option for early testing. Rebuilding under a new domain costs money and means losing the free trial. [L0372, 2022]
- To test many niches without paying for a new Shopify store each time, use one general store for all tests. When a winner appears, change the logo and name and keep the store setup, since opening a new Shopify store means paying again. [L0388, 2024+]

**Product testing flow under one domain**
1. Build one generic homepage. [L1194, undated]
2. Keep the About us, Contact, Track my order, FAQ and all policy pages the same across the store. [L1194, undated]
3. Create one product page template and duplicate it for each product, so every tested product has its own URL (five products, five URLs). [L1194, undated]
4. On the homepage, add a Shop now menu with a dropdown that lists every product. [L1194, undated]
5. On the product pages, remove or hide the top navigation so the page reads like a single landing page. [L1194, undated]
6. Launch one product in a single day. [L1410, 2023]
7. Check performance after 3 to 4 days. [L1410, 2023]
8. If it fails, create a new product page in about two hours and launch it within two hours. [L1410, 2023]
9. When a product wins, either turn the store into a niche store or devote a store to the winner, and keep testing new products under a separate domain. [L1410, 2023]

**Single-domain launch with unpublished backups**
1. Buy one brandable domain with no meaning, under 8-10 characters. Generate ideas with Namelix or ChatGPT, and combine two words into a new name if needed. [L1217, 2023+]
2. Build one high-converting product page for the first product using a page builder such as PageFly or GemPages. [L1217, 2023+]
3. Build the homepage with one banner that has the store name, a benefit-driven claim, and an image. [L1217, 2023+]
4. Under the banner, add a short section about the store and why the product helps, then show the product. [L1217, 2023+]
5. Prepare pages for the next products but keep them unpublished. [L1217, 2023+]
6. After about three days, if the product does not work, publish the next product page under the same domain. [L1217, 2023+]
- Keep unpublished product pages ready while the first product is tested. Judge a product after about three days of ad data before deciding whether to switch. [L1217, 2023+]
- Judge timing differs between sources: about three days (above) or three to four days (the flow above). Use three to four days for a full read when running the step-by-step flow. [L1217, 2023+; L1410, 2023]

**Converting a winner: options compared**

| Method | What happens | When it fits | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rename the store, keep setup and domain | Change the name to the niche, keep the domain, remove other products | The general store has one clear winner | [L0661, undated] |
| Move the winner to its own store | Build a dedicated store and keep the general store running for further tests | The product needs its own brand or market | [L0372, 2022] |
| Convert to a single-product store, or add a new theme | Keep the existing Shopify store and pixel, so its sales data stays | Avoids losing sales data from the pixel | [L1212, undated] |
| Add a second brand as a new theme | Add a theme inside the same store, upload the logo, switch the primary domain | You want a second brand without a new Shopify account | [L1131, 2018+] |

**Winner-first store structure**
1. Start with a general store that carries several test products at once. [L0661, undated]
2. Once one product clearly wins, rename the store to the niche, for example from caricia to caricia shoes. [L0661, undated]
3. Keep the same domain name when renaming the store. [L0661, undated]
4. Remove the other products and leave only the winning product line. [L0661, undated]

**Store structure approach**
1. Start with a general store or a niche general store containing several products. [L1212, undated]
2. Send ads to a collection page and let customers choose. [L1212, undated]
3. Identify the best-selling product. [L1212, undated]
4. Add a new theme focused on that product, optionally with a new domain and logo. [L1212, undated]
5. Keep the existing Shopify store and pixel. [L1212, undated]

- A general store can hold several products tested together. If a product wins, either convert the store into a single-product store or add a new theme focused on the winner, rather than building a new Shopify store, so the existing pixel keeps its sales data. [L1212, undated]
- The usual setup is one product per store, or one general store used to test products and then move winners into their own store. Setting up a new store for a product can be quick, around three hours, if the design is reused. [L0372, 2022]
- For a fast setup for product testing, use these steps: [L1171, 2024+]
  1. Use the Shrine theme, which has an inbuilt quantity discount. [L1171, 2024+]
  2. Name the store with a general female first name rather than a product name, so the same store can test many products. [L1171, 2024+]
  3. After sourcing a product, add it to the store and make the product page live. [L1171, 2024+]
- When testing many products on TikTok, a store can be set up as a general deals-style store with a domain such as onlinedeals.com, instead of one store per product. Testing many products this way needs a lot of content. [L0561, 2023+]
- Use a store that can sell a product line well. Build it around the product's season or niche, and expand with 5 to 10 new products per month to raise the store's product range. [L1440, 2021+]

**Niche rules**
- Run each store around one niche, with multiple categories inside it. For example, a standing desk store can have categories for bamboo, whiteboard and L-shaped desks. [L0801, 2021]
- Keep unrelated niches in separate stores. Single-niche stores tend to have higher conversion rates, since the buyer experience is focused on one product type. [L0801, 2021]
- Use niche-specific stores rather than one broad store covering many categories. A niche store can be built even when competitors sell a wide range of categories. [L0664, 2020]
- A single store should cover one niche only. [L1281, 2022]
- Build a separate demo store for each new niche, even if you already run other stores. Mention your existing experience to suppliers and say the new store launches on a specific date (2023). [L0934, 2023]
- When products do not fit the theme of the first store, open a second store for them instead of forcing them in. The second store reportedly performed well within about three months (older, 2020 is not stated; source undated). [L0832, 2019]
- Do not create several identical clone stores with the same name, address and phone number to split a niche. This caused Google Merchant Center suspensions for one operator. Instead, after one sub-niche sells best in the main store, create a new store containing only those products. [L1090, 2021]
- To turn an existing low-ticket store into a high-ticket store, keep the same Shopify account, payment setup and categories when the niche is related. Replace the main products with more expensive items, update the logo if needed, and link a new domain in the Shopify admin. [L1002, 2019]

**Reusing a store when the niche is wrong**
1. Change the store name in the Shopify backend. [L1069, 2019]
2. Replace the logo. [L1069, 2019]
3. Replace all custom images that carry the old branding. [L1069, 2019]
4. Check that the colour scheme still fits the new niche. [L1069, 2019]
- The Shopify account can stay as it is, so this is easier than starting from scratch. [L1069, 2019]
- If the niche of a Shopify store turns out to be wrong, the store can be reused by swapping products and connecting it to a different domain name in the Shopify settings. This avoids building a new store from scratch. [L1111, 2020]
- An existing Shopify store can be completely rebuilt from scratch, including the domain name, theme, code and all products. [L1092, 2022]

### Additional stores and domains

**Costs of extra stores**
- Each separate Shopify store costs about $30. Run several stores inside one account with separate themes instead of opening new accounts. [L1131, 2018+]
- Keep the old theme as a saved version. Seasonal stores, such as a Christmas store, can be republished later with the same logo and domain setup. [L1131, 2018+]
- Old store versions remain stored as themes, so you can switch back and forth. [L1131, 2018+]

**Several stores on one account (procedure)**
1. Create each new store with the same email address already used for the account. [L1428, undated]
2. Go to shopify.com > Log in with email and click the green button "Create another store". [L1428, undated]
3. All stores owned by that email then appear in a list in the account, for example on the left side. [L1428, undated]

**Duplicating a store**
1. Create the new Shopify store with another email. [L1436, undated]
2. In the old store, go to Online Store > Themes, download the theme and export it. [L1436, undated]
3. Import the theme into the new store. [L1436, undated]
4. Export the product and customer data and import it into the new store. [L1436, undated]
5. Export the email and SMS lists (for example from Klaviyo or SMS Bump) and import them into the new store. [L1436, undated]
6. Rebuild any settings that do not transfer, such as logo and notification settings. [L1436, undated]

**Multiple countries and continents**

| Scenario | Recommended setup | Source |
|---|---|---|
| US and Canada | One store serving both US and Canada | [L0268, 2020] |
| Other continents such as the UK | Separate domains and stores for each continent | [L0268, 2020] |
| Canada and US, with separate marketing | Two separate Shopify stores: domain.ca for Canada and domain.com for the US, with identical themes, logos and branding. This keeps marketing cleaner for ads, email and remarketing. Mention on About Us that you serve both countries. | [L0270, 2021] |

- The 2020 and 2021 sources differ. Use one store for North America if you do not need separate marketing per country. Use two stores if you want clean ad, email and remarketing campaigns per country. [L0268, 2020; L0270, 2021]

**Transfers**
- Transferring a Shopify store is complicated because the domain, the revenue source and account control all have to move cleanly. Many sellers use a broker or transfer service to handle it. [L1468, 2014]

### Copying competitor structures

**Finding and comparing competitors**
- Koala Inspector, a browser extension, shows which apps, themes, and upselling or cross-selling techniques competitor Shopify stores use. It also displays their best-selling products, revealing the sales tactics that work in your niche. [L0280, undated]
- CopyFile is a French-language store analysis tool. It shows a store's theme, lets you export its code and images, tracks daily revenue and a 30-day forecast, shows best sellers and recent orders, and looks up advertising networks. The lesson reports its revenue numbers matched the presenter's own store. It costs 77 euros per month. [L0387, 2022]
- A collection URL with the best-selling sort order can reveal the best-selling products of any Shopify store. Append the path collections/all?sort_by=best-selling to the store domain. This works only on Shopify stores. [L0388, 2024+]
- To compare a product across stores, search for the supplier's SKU first. If it does not show up, search by the product name and check whether the product exists on the other site. [L0894, 2020-26]
- To check whether two stores have the same owner: [L0656, undated]
  1. Open the Contact Us page or the Terms of Service page of each store. [L0656, undated]
  2. Note the email address shown on each page. [L0656, undated]
  3. If the emails are identical, treat it as the final confirmation that the same people run both stores. [L0656, undated]

**Documenting differences (2023)**
- When analysing competitor stores, document specific differences in writing: product descriptions (length, detail level, technical specs), features or benefits highlighted, warranty or guarantee mentions, shipping or return policies, pricing structure, page layout, customer testimonials, and social proof elements. This documentation guides what improvements to make on your own store. [L0251, 2023]

**one source method: find, copy, adapt**
1. Find the product and list five to ten other stores that sell it. [L0600, 2026]
2. Open each store and pick the parts that look proven: offer, photos, FAQ, reviews, testimonials and page sections. [L0600, 2026]
3. Rebuild the offer on your store with the Coaching Bundles app from the App Store. [L0600, 2026]
4. Copy the photos, FAQ block, reviews and testimonials that fit your product. [L0600, 2026]
5. Copy the section layouts: video carousel, before-and-after block, timeline block and testimonial slider. [L0600, 2026]
6. Adapt everything to your own theme and add your own brand name to copied images. [L0600, 2026]
7. Launch the test store within about two days of finding the product. [L0600, 2026]
8. Once the test produces sales, customise the landing page so it looks unique. [L0600, 2026]

**Rules for copying**
- Copy only small parts from many competitor sites instead of large parts from one site. A store that mirrors a single competitor looks like a copy and loses credibility. [L0600, 2026]
- Many competitor stores start with a basic design and add more polished elements once daily sales are high. One observed store doing over $10,000 per day had added these elements after its early phase. [L0600, 2026]
- Copying the layout of a proven competitor store is a faster starting point than designing from scratch, and it lets you start testing sooner. A store selling the same product has already been shaped by what works in that niche. [L1249, 2026]
- Building a store from scratch takes a few hours, or almost half a day, for an experienced builder. Budget that time per store when planning pre-launch work. [L1249, 2026]
- Model your site on at least two to three competitors, borrowing their photos, text and layout structure. Then take the best elements from each and combine them into your own design, which is expected to outperform any single competitor. [L0897, 2026]
- Beginners in dropshipping often do not know what a winning store looks like, so studying proven stores makes building far easier. Modelling your store on existing successful stores avoids starting from zero. [L0897, 2026]

**Competitor research before the build**
1. Choose the product and confirm it through product research. [L0897, 2026]
2. Find three to five competitor stores that sell the same product and list them in the product research sheet. [L0897, 2026]
3. Take a screenshot of each competitor's homepage. [L0897, 2026]
4. Submit the competitor list and screenshots before starting to build your own site. [L0897, 2026]
5. If fewer than three competitors exist, tell the program team instead of stopping the process. [L0897, 2026]
6. Build the site only after this research is complete and submitted. [L0897, 2026]
- Competitor homepage screenshots must be made and shown before the store is built, not after launch. Some programs refuse to approve a site built without this prior competitor research. [L0897, 2026]
- Building a store from scratch without studying competitors often produces weak quality. Fixing a poor site after launch can take about a week of revision and is harder than getting it right the first time. [L0897, 2026]
- Building the site first and sending competitor research afterwards wastes time, because a poor build then needs a full week of revision. Competitor research should always come before the build starts. [L0897, 2026]
- The source range for competitors differs: five to ten stores to browse for proven parts (above), three to five to submit in the research sheet (above). Use five to ten as your browsing pool and three to five as the list you submit. [L0600, 2026; L0897, 2026]

**Store for a proven product**
1. Find a winning brand in your niche with long-running ads in the Meta Ad Library. [L1479, 2026]
2. Study its product offer and page structure as a reference. [L1479, 2026]
3. Use the ChatGPT prompt to rewrite its copy for your product and target avatar. [L1479, 2026]
4. Paste the rewritten text into your store. [L1479, 2026]
5. Add a few product images and short, clear texts, and keep the design clean. [L1479, 2026]
6. Add testimonials, an FAQ section and custom reviews. [L1479, 2026]

- A proven layout copied from another successful store is a far cheaper starting point than hiring a web developer for around $30,000 USD to build the whole store, which is not necessary for a store like this. [L1325, 2020-26]
- A store-build service can include design, uploading demo products and content pages, and setting up Google Analytics and Google webmaster tools. It does not include getting approved with suppliers, which the owner must still do. [L0852, 2020]
