## Legal pages, policies and trust pages

### Core page set and where to create each page

- A supplier-ready and customer-ready store needs six pages created and published: About Us, Contact Us, Shipping Policy, Return & Refund Policy, Privacy Policy and Terms of Service. [L0090, undated]
- Newer guidance (2025) lists six core pages: About Us, Contact Us, Shipping Policy, Refund Policy, Privacy Policy and Terms of Service. [L1048, 2020-26]
- Older guidance (2021-22) lists six Shopify pages every store needs: About Us, Contact Us, Shipping Policy, Privacy Policy, Terms of Sale (or Terms and Conditions) and Returns Policy. Each must be linked from the homepage and site navigation. Product pages use one template, set up once and applied to all products. ** [L1463, 2021-22]
- Older guidance (2021-22) lists eight pages to set up before launch: privacy policy, terms of service, contact us, a page explaining why to buy from the store, a price match guarantee, an easy returns page, a shipping page and an about us page. [L1264, 2021-22]
- Another list of core content pages: About Us, price guarantee, product guarantee, shipping policy, returns and exchanges, order tracking, support contact and FAQ. Buyers check these before paying for an expensive item, so a missing page directly costs sales. [L1130, 2020-26]
- Competitor headers and footers link to Shop, About us, Track order, FAQs, Contact us and Our Policies. Create these pages before launch, because buyers check them to judge whether a store is legitimate. [L0600, 2026]
- Early supplier-approval stage: only four pages are needed: About Us, Contact Us, Privacy Policy and Terms of Service. Shipping, returns, refund or financing pages can be created later. [L0509, 2020-26]
- Before contacting suppliers, the store should have About Us, privacy policy, shipping policy and return policy pages, even as adapted templates, because suppliers may check the footer links before approving the account. ** [L1281, 2022]
- A store with no Terms of Service, Privacy Policy or full Contact Us page looks unprofessional and risks trust issues. Legal pages and a complete contact page should be present before launch. [L0656, undated]
- Store pages hold customer-facing information (About Us, Contact, policies) that changes infrequently and that customers reference when making purchase decisions or managing orders. [L0090, undated]
- Shopify pages hold customer information that rarely changes or that customers look up often, such as About Us, a price match guarantee, a shipping and returns policy, a buyer's guide or a financing page. [L0509, 2020-26]
- Legal policies are a separate case: they are created in Settings, not as ordinary pages. [L0509, 2020-26]
- Check large brands in the same niche to confirm your legal and policy pages cover everything customers look for. Typical topics are order status, payment information, sales tax, shipping rates and the return policy. [L0421, 2020-26]

### Where each core page is created

| Page | Where to create it | Theme template or method |
|---|---|---|
| About Us | Shopify Admin > Online Store > Pages > Add page | Default page |
| Contact Us | Shopify Admin > Online Store > Pages (rename the automatic Contact page) | Contact |
| Privacy Policy | Settings > Policies > Create from template | Shopify-generated template, then edited |
| Terms of Service | Settings > Policies > Create from template | Shopify-generated template, then edited |

[L0509, 2020-26]

- A complete basic set of six pages uses two Pages entries, About Us and Contact Us, under Online Store > Pages. The other four (Shipping, Refund, Privacy and Terms of Service) are created under Settings > Policies. The Contact information policy is completed later, not during initial setup. [L1048, 2020-26]
- Pages are managed under Shopify Admin > Online Store > Pages. [L0509, 2020-26; L1048, 2020-26]
- Policy pages are not created under Pages. They are found under Settings > Policies, the last link at the bottom of the Settings menu. The list there shows Refund policy, Privacy policy, Terms of service, Shipping policy and Contact information. [L1048, 2020-26]
- Privacy Policy and Terms of Service are created under Settings > Policies. Saved policies are linked automatically in the checkout footer and can be added to the online store menu. [L0509, 2020-26]
- Settings > Policies > Written policies lists the policies Shopify links in the checkout footer: Privacy policy, Terms of service, Contact information and Refund policy. Each can also be added to the online store menu. [L1250, 2026]
- Settings > Policies (newer walkthrough) lists these written policies: Return and refund policy, Terms of service, Shipping policy, Contact information, Legal notice and Purchase options cancellation policy. Each can be generated with Use automated policy or filled with templates. Policies appear in the checkout footer and can be linked from the store footer. [L1327, 2026]
- The store policy templates from Shopify can be modified under Settings > Policies. They were already set up when pages were created. [L1305, 2020-26]

### Creating a page in Shopify

- Steps to create a page:
  1. Go to Shopify Admin > Online Store > Pages.
  2. Click Add page in the top right corner.
  3. Enter the page title, for example About Us.
  4. Paste the finished text into the WYSIWYG editor.
  5. Click Save.
  6. Click View to check how the page looks on the storefront. [L1048, 2020-26]
- Page content is typed or pasted into the WYSIWYG editor, which determines what the customer sees. [L1048, 2020-26]
- Steps to create a standard page:
  1. Go to Online Store > Pages in the Shopify admin.
  2. Click Add page.
  3. Enter the page name in the Title field.
  4. Paste the page text into the Content editor.
  5. Under Visibility, keep Visible, or choose Hidden or Set visibility date.
  6. Under Template, leave Template suffix on 'page' unless the page needs a special template.
  7. Expand Edit website SEO and set Page title, Description, and URL and handle.
  8. Click Save.
  9. Click View page to confirm the live page shows the text. [L1264, 2021-22]
- The Pages screen has an Add page button and lists all existing pages with their last-updated time. [L1264, 2021-22]
- A page becomes publicly available as soon as it is saved with Visibility set to Visible. Visibility can also be set to Hidden or scheduled with Set visibility date. ** [L1264, 2021-22]
- The editor of an existing page shows Duplicate, View page and Delete page next to the Visibility section. Use Duplicate to copy a page that already has the right layout, then change its title and content instead of building from scratch. ** [L1264, 2021-22]
- Shopify builds the page URL from the handle. A page saved under the handle shipping is reached at /pages/shipping, and a Contact Us page at /pages/contact-us. Set the handle when the page is first saved, because later changes break existing links and search results. ** [L1264, 2021-22]
- After creating or editing a page, click View page to open it on the live store and check that text and layout appear correctly before moving on. ** [L1264, 2021-22]
- Paragraph spacing can be adjusted in the WYSIWYG editor by adding space between paragraphs. Save and view the page afterwards, since pasted AI text can look dense without it. [L1048, 2020-26]
- Reusable content page templates speed up building informational pages such as About, FAQ and shipping policy pages, so the same structure does not have to be created from scratch for each page. [L0430, 2023]
- Create the Contact us page in this order:
  1. In the Shopify admin, go to Online Store > Pages.
  2. Click Add page.
  3. Under template, choose the template named Contact.
  4. Add a contact form to the page and save it. [L0970, 2023]
- Pages can be built and checked in one pass per page. Standard one source order:
  1. Go to Online Store > Pages and click Add page.
  2. Create a Contact us page.
  3. Create an About us page with the brand story, and remove meta placeholders from the copy.
  4. Create a shipping and delivery page with accurate policy details.
  5. Create an FAQ page if needed.
  6. Read every placeholder and replace it with accurate information before saving. [L1331, 2025-26]

### Generating and customizing legal policies

- Shopify has a built-in generator for the refund policy, privacy policy and terms of service. It produces the core legal pages in a few clicks and is the fastest starting point. [L0421, 2020-26]
- Shopify's Help Center holds current instructions for the legal page generator. Search for 'privacy policy' there, because menus and wording can change. [L0421, 2020-26]
- Generator path given in one walkthrough: Settings > Checkout, scroll to the bottom, section 'Refund, privacy, and terms of service', then use the generate function for each policy. [L0421, 2020-26]
- Generator path given in another walkthrough: Settings > Policies, where each written policy can be generated with Use automated policy. Check your own Shopify admin, because the two walkthroughs name different locations. [L0421, 2020-26; L1327, 2026]
- The generator is available without a trial: choose the option to skip the Shopify trial and create the policy directly, then enter company information to receive the document by email. [L0377, 2020-26]
- Steps to create the core legal pages:
  1. Open the Shopify Help Center and search for 'privacy policy' to confirm the current instructions.
  2. In the Shopify admin, go to Settings.
  3. Click Checkout.
  4. Scroll to the bottom of the Checkout page.
  5. Find the section 'Refund, privacy, and terms of service'.
  6. Click generate for each policy: refund policy, privacy policy and terms of service.
  7. Read every generated policy in full.
  8. Customize each policy for the store's specific terms, refund rules and any special conditions. [L0421, 2020-26]
- Steps to create a return and refund policy with an AI text generator:
  1. Ask ChatGPT for a return and refund policy and include your store name.
  2. Check that your supplier can actually deliver what the policy promises.
  3. Copy the generated text and replace every bracketed placeholder with your real values.
  4. In the Shopify admin, open Settings > Policies.
  5. Open the Refund policy, paste the text into the editor and enter the correct store address.
  6. Click Publish and confirm the status shows the policy as saved.
  7. Repeat steps 1 to 6 for the shipping policy and add your international shipping rates.
  8. Set up return rules (see the Manage return rules setting).
  9. Review the Customer privacy settings.
  10. Buy or connect your domain.
  11. Check which apps you actually need before installing any. [L1250, 2026]
- Settings > Policies > Shipping policy shows 'No policy set' until you publish one. The editor has a required text field with formatting options (Paragraph, menu) and a Publish button. After saving, Shopify confirms with a 'Store policy saved' message. [L1250, 2026]
- Forgetting to enter the correct store address in the refund policy before publishing is a mistake. The address required is the return address customers should use. [L1250, 2026]
- Publishing generated Shopify policies without reading them is a mistake. A generic policy may not match the store's own refund rules, shipping terms or special conditions, so each policy must be checked and adjusted. [L0421, 2020-26]
- Shopify auto-generated policies still need manual additions. Review module 6.02 for recommended policy modifications and add them to the auto-generated templates. *(Newer, 2025.)* [L0255, 2025]
- Shopify policy templates are not legal advice. By using them you agree to the disclaimer shown on the page. Replace the sample store and business details with your own before publishing, and have the wording checked where your legal requirements apply. [L1327, 2026]
- Until supplier terms are known, a shipping and return policy can be made from a generic template. Once suppliers are approved, update the policy to include supplier-specific exclusions. [L1094, 2023]
- A store must not look like an unchanged template. Privacy policy, return policy and other pages must be rewritten for the actual business. *(Newer, 2023.)* [L0855, 2023]
- Include the company name and at least an email address on the refund and shipping policies, because a missing contact is seen as unprofessional. The privacy policy and terms of service are secondary. [L1259, undated]
- Check that the email address and phone number in each policy are correct. Open the refund policy, privacy policy, terms of service and shipping policy under Settings (bottom left) > Policies to review and edit them. [L0724, 2020-26]

### Policy text rules

- Template text for pages is a starting point. It can be used as is at launch, but once the business is running, rewrite the pages in the owner's own voice with the real details of the business and products. ** [L1264, 2021-22]
- Templates for content pages are only a starting point. Customize them to your own store before publishing. Guarantee and policy pages must reflect the terms you actually offer and the policies of each supplier. [L1130, 2020-26]
- Any template for the About Us text (prebuilt general templates or from scratch) is only a starting point and must be edited to match the real business before publishing. [L0509, 2020-26]
- When filling an AI prompt for an About Us or policy page, change only the input fields at the top of the prompt. The instruction section at the bottom is written for the AI and does not need editing. [L1048, 2020-26]
- Creating an About Us page with the AI prompt:
  1. In Shopify Admin, go to Online Store > Pages and create a new page titled "About Us".
  2. Locate the About Us AI Prompt and copy the section beginning with "You are a professional e-commerce brand copywriter".
  3. Complete the Student Inputs fields: brand name, website domain, primary niche, target customers, brand personality, business location, store differentiators, customer service philosophy, shipping and fulfillment approach, and optionally your founder story.
  4. Generate the page copy.
  5. Review the output and make edits as needed.
  6. Insert the final text into Shopify, save the page, and preview it. [L0090, undated]
- Setting up all six basic Shopify pages with AI prompts and templates took about 15 to 16 minutes of video time. Treat this as a first-pass benchmark, since reviewing each generated text against your real business terms takes additional time. [L1048, 2020-26]

### Supplier-specific policies

- Handling policies from multiple suppliers (2025):
  1. Add warranty information as collapsible sections on product pages for suppliers with unique warranties.
  2. Modify shipping text on product pages for suppliers with different shipping timeframes (for example, 'This product ships in 2-3 days via freight').
  3. Create a general return policy for most suppliers.
  4. On the return policy page, add exceptions at the bottom listing specific brands with different policies (for example, brand A has 90-day returns while most have 30 days).
  5. On individual product pages, highlight any unique benefits (for example, a longer return window). [L0255, 2025]
- Set up the shipping and return policy with one main policy that fits most products, then list exclusions in that policy. For a product that cannot be returned, add a shipping policy tab on that product page stating it is not eligible for returns. ** [L0801, 2021]
- Assigning a supplier-specific return policy to individual products (2024):
  1. Open the theme editor and go to the Products templates.
  2. Create a new template based on the default product template, name it after the policy, add a section stating the policy, and save.
  3. Open each affected product, change its theme template from default to the new template, and save. [L0471, 2024]
- If mixing MAP and non-MAP suppliers, remove the price match guarantee from the footer. It can stay on individual product pages for MAP-eligible items only. ** [L0270, 2021]
- Price match promise on the homepage was shown with free shipping across the country and easy returns ('don't need it? send it back'). ** [L0771, 2021-22]

### Contact Us page

- A new Shopify store starts with one page already present: a Contact page that uses the default template named Contact. It includes Shopify's built-in contact form, and each submission arrives by email with the sender's message. [L1048, 2020-26]
- Leave the default Contact template so customers can submit messages directly to your email. [L0090, undated]
- Conflict on the Contact page source: one view says the automatic Contact page should be renamed when creating the Contact Us page (table above), while another says to create it under Add page with the Contact template. Use the automatic page if it exists, and create one with the Contact template only if it does not. [L0509, 2020-26; L0970, 2023; L1048, 2020-26]
- Contact page: in Shopify admin, go to Pages > Contact us. Type the phone number, email address and business hours into the page text, then save. The text appears above the contact form. [L0966, 2025]
- Keep the contact form and add at least two more contact methods, such as hours of operation (for example, 9:00 AM through 5:00 PM Eastern), a real phone number, and an email address or live chat. Save the page afterwards. [L0724, 2020-26]
- The contact page must offer at least three ways to reach you: an email address, a contact form and a phone number. ** [L0671, 2021]
- The Contact information policy is completed later, not during initial setup. [L1048, 2020-26]

### About Us page

- A new store can begin its About Us text from one of several prebuilt general templates or write it from scratch. Any template is only a starting point and must be edited before publishing. [L0509, 2020-26]
- Include the brand story and remove meta placeholders from the copy. [L1331, 2025-26]

### FAQ page

- Build an FAQ page without an app:
  1. Create a new page in the Shopify admin titled Frequently Asked Questions.
  2. Type each question as a headline.
  3. Type the answer below it in normal body text.
  4. Add images only if needed. Fewer apps is better. [L0853, 2021; L0963, 2022]
- FAQ content should answer questions your own customers ask about the niche, not generic e-commerce questions. Add an entry only when a niche-specific question keeps coming up. ** [L0853, 2021]
- Include an FAQ section if the product solves a problem, and display a 'Try risk-free' or '30-day money-back guarantee' prominently on the store. [L1259, undated]

### Shipping and delivery pages

- Create a shipping and delivery page with accurate policy details, then create an FAQ page if needed. [L1331, 2025-26]
- Show the shipping message 'free shipping from a warehouse close to the customer' (in the example, a Spanish warehouse) as a trust badge near the buy area. It adds trust and reduces doubt about delivery time and cost. [L0916, 2022+]
- Handling a damaged delivery, to be stated on the shipping page:
  1. Ask the customer to inspect the packaging when the parcel arrives.
  2. If there is visible damage, note it on the delivery signature or refuse delivery.
  3. If the customer accepts the delivery and finds damage, photograph the damaged packaging or the products inside immediately with a phone or camera.
  4. Email the photos to the store's support address.
  5. Lodge a return claim online through the Returns page. [L1264, 2021-22]
- Show shipping carrier logos only for carriers you ship with yourself or that your suppliers use. Well-known carriers such as FedEx, UPS or USPS build recognition and trust. [L1050, 2020-26]
- To show carrier logos on product pages, place them in the Shipping section (tab) of the product page with the text 'We proudly ship with'. Click the image icon in that section and choose the file from your computer. [L1050, 2020-26]

### Privacy and customer privacy settings

- Include a privacy policy on the site and make sure it is complete before setting up Google Merchant Center. ** [L0671, 2021]
- Settings > Customer privacy:
  - Publish the privacy policy on the online store.
  - Set Data sharing opt-out to the recommended setting.
  - Keep Shopify Network Intelligence enabled as the default.
  - Set Data storage hosting location to United States for a US-focused store. [L1327, 2026]
- Privacy and terms pages: the policy HTML and layout are covered in the code unit below. [L1327, 2026]

### Policy page layout and HTML

- Text content pages (contact, shipping and privacy) should stay in a narrow centered column rather than spanning the full width of a large screen. Readers scan a narrow column with short eye movements, as in a book. Do not spend effort making these pages full width. ** [L0370, 2021-22]
- The lesson's policy HTML uses one wrapper and one block per section, with the same inline styles repeated for each section. Keep the same structure when pasting the policy HTML into the Shopify policy editor. [L1327, 2026]

```html
<div style='font-family: -apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,Segoe UI,Roboto,Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif; font-size: 15px; line-height: 1.8; color: #333; max-width: 720px; margin: 0 auto; padding: 0 16px 60px;'>... </div>
```

```html
<div style='margin-bottom: 32px;'>
<p style='margin: 0 0 10px; font-size: 11px; font-weight: bold; text-transform: uppercase; letter-spacing: 1.2px; color: #888; border-bottom: 1px solid #e0e0e0; padding-bottom: 6px;'>Heading</p>
<p style='margin: 0 0 14px;'>Body paragraph</p>
<p style='margin: 0;'>Last body paragraph</p>
</div>
```
