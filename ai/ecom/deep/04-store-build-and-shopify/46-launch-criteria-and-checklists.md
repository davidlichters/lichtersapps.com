### Launch criteria and checklists

Launch criteria, by source. The supplier and product numbers differ, so each applies to a different situation:

| Source | Criterion | When it applies |
|---|---|---|
| [L0356, 2019] | Launch when the store works and has no obvious red flags; check that the cart and checkout work | General go/no-go |
| [L0671, 2021] (older, 2021; merged, the two units differ only in "a few hours" vs "hours") | Launch when real products are uploaded, checkout works and the site is live. Google traffic starts once the product feed is approved, which can take hours or a few days. | Before Google traffic |
| [L0808, 2026] (2026) | Launch once the first two suppliers are onboarded with live products uploaded and demo products removed. Launching before the full catalog is loaded is acceptable. | Minimum for launch |
| [L0967, 2020] | Before launching ad campaigns, products from at least two different suppliers must be uploaded. Ads can be turned on then, and new suppliers added over time. | Before ads |
| [L0853, 2021] | Upload at least two suppliers' products before the store is opened to traffic | Before traffic |
| [L1446, 2020-26] | Confirm at least two suppliers are onboarded and uploaded; see the readiness check below | Before ads |
| [L1446, 2020-26] | Eight supplier approvals and more than 500 uploaded products is more than enough to launch a store. Cross-sells are an advanced add-on not needed before the first launch. | Scale reference, not a minimum |
| [L0963, 2022] | Before expecting the first sale: the site is not behind a password, real products are uploaded, and Shopify Payments is set up | Before the first sale |
| [L0834, 2021] | Keep Shopify Payments turned off until real products are live, so nobody can place an order | Before real products are live |

- Payments timing: [L0834, 2021] keeps Shopify Payments off until real products are live, and [L0963, 2022] requires Shopify Payments to be set up before the first sale. Both apply in sequence: off while products are not yet live, then set up before the first sale. [L0834, 2021; L0963, 2022]
- Supplier count: the minimum is two suppliers with live products ([L0808, 2026; L0967, 2020; L0853, 2021; L1446, 2020-26]). The eight approvals and more than 500 products in [L1446, 2020-26] describe a store that is more than enough, not a requirement. [L0808, 2026; L0967, 2020; L0853, 2021; L1446, 2020-26]
- Do not chase a perfect performance score before launch: a B grade on a performance tool is not a reason to delay launch, because large e-commerce brands often score lower than a typical new store. [L1446, 2020-26]
- A store that is set up is ready to launch with a high-converting design, because the store is where paid traffic lands and must turn visitors into buyers. [L0914, 2026]
- Publish before launch: see the readiness section, [L0934, 2023] and [L0934, 2023].

Launch readiness check, in order [L1446, 2020-26]:

1. Confirm that at least two suppliers are onboarded and their products are uploaded to the store.
2. Link all policy pages in the store footer before running any ads, for Google compliance.
3. Complete the conversion optimization module so the store is optimized for conversions.
4. Check for broken links, product pages with missing information, mispriced items, and empty collections.
5. Have a family member or friend who has not seen the store shop it while you watch. If no glaring problems remain, launch.

Family-member test, in order [L1446, 2020-26]:

1. Ask a family member or friend who has not seen the store to shop it as if buying one of your products.
2. Sit beside them while they browse and watch where they click.
3. Ask what stands out, what is not working, and what is confusing.
4. Thank them and make the changes they point out.

- Launch when the store works and has no obvious red flags. Have a friend or family member test whether they would buy from it, and check that the cart and checkout work. [L0356, 2019]

Checklist before sending traffic [L1345, 2021+]:

1. Payment processor: at least one payment method that accepts credit cards, plus PayPal for top markets.
2. Markets: all target countries are active in Settings > Markets, and a test checkout works in each.
3. Shipping prices: default rates are deleted, custom rates are set, and shipping is free or clearly priced.
4. Legal pages: refund policy and shipping policy exist, with company name and email address.
5. Checkout page: trust badges and payment icons are added, and taxes are disabled where no tax is charged.

Pre-launch Shopify store checklist, in order [L1324, 2026]:

1. Create the Shopify store.
2. Install the Elixir theme.
3. Upload the logo.
4. Customize the homepage.
5. Check the mobile version of the store.
6. Import the product.
7. Add images and/or videos to the product page.
8. Add customer reviews to the product page.
9. Add FAQs to the product page.
10. Install the Kaching Bundles app.
11. Create the bundle offer.
12. Add quantity breaks to the bundle.
13. Highlight the best-selling offer.
14. Add the shipping, refund and privacy policies.
15. Complete a test checkout.
16. Review the store on both desktop and mobile.
17. Confirm the store is ready to receive paid traffic.

Launch checklist before the store is opened to traffic [L0853, 2021]:

1. Remove the storefront password so the store is publicly live.
2. Upload at least two suppliers' products.
3. Configure all payment options.
4. Click every link and confirm nothing is broken.
5. Run a full test purchase through checkout to confirm it works.
6. Set up the ads.
7. Install analytics.

Full pre-launch checklist for a Google Shopping store. Use it as a reference; items about Google accounts apply only if you run those channels [L1145, 2021-22]:

1. Connect Shopify Payments and PayPal so checkout works.
2. Activate billing on the Google Workspace email.

5. Adjust the main navigation to the live products.
6. Show the phone number in the header and footer of the homepage.
7. Link Privacy Policy, Terms of Service and Contact Us in the header and footer.
8. Make the Contact Us page list phone number, email and postal address.
9. Update the Legal section in Shopify Settings.
10. Set at least one free shipping rate in Shopify shipping settings.
11. Set correct tax rates in Shopify settings.
12. Connect the domain to Shopify and set it as primary.
13. Turn off password protection.
14. Update the Preferences and General areas of Shopify.
15. Add online chat, review and email marketing apps.
16. Add USP information to product pages.
17. Open a Google Analytics account and connect it to Shopify.
18. Activate ecommerce tracking in Google Analytics.
19. Homepage: check that the USP highlight bars still match your offer.
20. Homepage: update the featured product and collection sections to show live products.
21. Products: delete all demo products.
22. Products: check that every product has the correct price.
23. Products: set correct stock levels, or mark products in stock with a placeholder quantity.
24. Products: add correct SKU, MPN and barcode where relevant.
25. Products: optimize product titles.
26. Products: check that each product image matches its product.
27. Products: set up and check the product page tabs.
28. Products: check that every product is in at least one collection and reachable from the main navigation.
29. Merchant Center: install the Feeds for Google Shopping app and submit the feed.
30. Merchant Center: make tax and shipping details match Shopify.
31. Merchant Center: update business information.
32. Merchant Center: verify the domain.
33. Merchant Center: verify the phone number.
34. Merchant Center: add branding.
35. Merchant Center: enable Free listings under Growth > Manage Programs.

38. Merchant Center: link the account to Google Ads.

### Traffic spikes and viral moments

- Before a large influencer post goes live, check that the store can handle a traffic spike. One brand saw more than 3,000 people shopping at once, compared with a normal load of 20 to 100 visitors. [L0790, 2017]
- A store that went viral was shut down by Shopify two to three days after the first viral sales, when order volumes were large. Customers reported they could not purchase, the seller noticed through comments, and the store was restored within about one hour, though more than $5,000 in sales was estimated to be lost during that hour. Shopify also requested paperwork such as proof of inventory once orders reached tens of thousands. [L0352, 2022]
- Check the comments on your videos regularly, because customers writing that they cannot buy is an early warning of a store shutdown. Shopify may also restrict accounts when order volume is very high. [L0352, 2022]
- A broken product link in a viral video or post sends traffic nowhere and directly reduces conversion. Check the link in every post before and during a viral moment. [L0655, undated]
- Shipping or checkout issues on the store page can cost sales even when a video goes viral. Check the product page and checkout flow every time before promoting. [L0655, undated]
- After adding the link to the bio, check it regularly to confirm the landing page and product page load without errors, since an error can appear after the link is applied. Fix any errors in Shopify when they show up. [L1380, 2022]

### Zero-sales and add-to-cart diagnostics

- Checking a product that gets add-to-carts but few sales [L0660, 2019+]:

1. Check which payment methods are offered at checkout, including credit card and PayPal.
2. Check that the checkout works without errors.
3. Check the pricing point against the market.
4. Check the website speed.
5. If these are fine and sales are still low, the product is probably not good enough, so test a different product.

- Checkout test for a store with zero sales after add-to-cart [L0361, 2024]:

1. Check the shipping setup in Shopify and confirm that shipping rates are correct.
2. Check the market setup if you sell to several countries and confirm that each target country is active.
3. Try to check out with two random addresses in each target country, for example USA and Canada.
4. If checkout fails for those addresses, fix the setting that is blocking customers.

- Use this order when ad metrics are good but there are no sales, although add-to-cart and checkout ratios are healthy [L0774, 2023]:

1. Check the shipping options and whether shipping is being charged to the customer.
2. Check the markets: make sure every country you run ads to is enabled in Shopify Markets (Settings > Markets), because customers from a country that is not activated cannot choose it at checkout and abandon the cart.
3. Check that the payment providers work: create a 95% discount code for yourself and test a payment through PayPal, Shopify Payments or Stripe, whichever you use.
4. If a payment provider fails the test, uninstall it and install it again, which fixes setup problems in many cases.

- A common cause of zero sales with healthy funnel numbers is a targeted country that is not activated in Shopify Markets, so checkout cannot offer shipping to that customer. Check the country list against the countries in your ad targeting. [L0774, 2023]
- Review the drop-off rate at each step in analytics to find the bottleneck. Check in this order [L1225, 2025]:

1. Check the homepage.
2. Check the collection page.
3. Check the product page.
4. Check the cart.
5. Check checkout.

- A high bounce rate, for example 87 percent, is normal for ad traffic landing on product pages. Use Lucky Orange screen recordings to see why visitors leave and fix what causes it. [L0665, 2022]
- Diagnosis order when funnel numbers are weak: the offer, theme and checkout are the first suspects, as stated in the conversion section. [L1316, 2024+]

### Post-launch maintenance

- Constantly update the store as you learn: change the header, footer and apps, and optimize the product page as more visitors arrive. A store needs ongoing maintenance rather than a single build. [L1444, 2022]
- Check the store on mobile at every stage of editing, since mobile remains the main traffic source after launch. [L1331, 2025-26]
- Keep checking the store links and product pages after launch, especially after changes to the bio link or the header and footer. [L1380, 2022; L0855, 2023]
- Re-check the store's speed score once the store is public. The score becomes available 1 to 3 days after protection is disabled, and a B grade is not a reason to pause sales. [L1292, 2021-22; L1446, 2020-26]
- A new Shopify store is password protected by default and shows a coming-soon page to visitors who are not logged in, asking for a password instead of showing the storefront. [L1294, 2020-26]
