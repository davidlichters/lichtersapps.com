## Demo store for supplier approval

### What a supplier-ready demo store is

- A demo store is a fully functional Shopify storefront with placeholder products. It shows that the business is supplier-ready without being connected to approved suppliers or accepting real customer orders. It becomes a live store later, when the demo products are replaced with approved supplier products. [L0069, undated]
- It has a complete layout and design, populated with a small number of representative demo products. Suppliers review it to picture how their own products would look before they approve real uploads. [L0245, 2020]
- It is built to be supplier-ready and looks and behaves like a live store. The finished design is used to show brands when applying to become an authorized retailer. [L1062, 2020-26]
- It should be built exactly like the permanent store, with the same design and pages. The only difference is that the real products stay unpublished until the supplier approves. [L0356, 2019]
- The store built during setup is the same store that will later take real sales, not a separate test site. Only the content differs, because no suppliers or real products exist yet. [L1061, 2020-26]
- Before suppliers are contacted, fill the store with demo products instead of real supplier products. The store should look as it will when it sells, so suppliers can picture their own products on it. [L1061, 2020-26]
- Examples: ShakaSurfboards.com (surf niche) and Happy Paws (pet supply niche) are demo stores built to a supplier-ready standard. Both are design showcases that resemble a real store. [L0069, undated; L1062, 2020-26]
- A supplier-facing store should look exactly like a live store, the only difference being that the products are demo items. It must not look half-built. [L0767, 2020-26]
- Suppliers review the store before approval, so it should look complete even with only a few products. It should show what the store will look like once the supplier's products are uploaded. [L0467, 2019]
- Suppliers apply the same skepticism a future customer would. A store that looks untrustworthy fails both audiences. Trust signals are therefore the core criteria. [L0767, 2020-26]
- Suppliers judge a retailer partly on how the store looks, so store quality matters before the first sale. A professional look raises the chance of approval. [L1086, 2019]
- The store should be lean but credible: sample collections and sample products that match the target niche, clean, not a large store with many pages or products. [L0960, 2019]
- Build it lean but good-looking, without many extra content pages or a large product upload. Add the extras after vendor approval. The goal before approval is a credible, functional store, not a finished one. [L0960, 2019]
- Minimum set for credibility: a working logo, favicon, colors, fonts, a single slide, a few collections, a pre-footer and an email signup. Defer announcement bars, mega menu details, blog content, buyer guides and advanced product card settings. [L0775, 2020-26]
- The store must look relevant to its niche before outreach. Add products from the chosen niche, even placeholders, so the brand sees a store that matches its category. [L0468, 2020]
- Use the niche-specific demo products and stock sources described below. [L0468, 2020]
- Build a new demo store for each niche, even if you already own other stores. Mention your existing experience when contacting suppliers, and say the new store goes live on a specific date. [L0934, 2023]
- A demo store can stay small while suppliers are contacted, for example three collections with three products each. It does not need rebuilding when the niche changes. [L0247, 2021]
- Build the store to its final look before contacting suppliers, so they can picture how their products will appear. Finish the build first, then start outreach. [L0941, 2022]
- Timing: the store is built before suppliers are contacted. Demo products are uploaded so suppliers can review it during approval. [L0969, 2022; L0506, 2020-26]
- Before applying to silver suppliers, build the demo store so the supplier can see a working, professional storefront when it reviews the application. [L0486, 2020-26]
- Have a live, high-quality demo store before contacting suppliers, so they can see how their products would look on your site. [L1422, 2020-26]
- Supplier outreach comes only after the supplier-ready demo store is complete. [L1062, 2020-26]
- At the demo stage, no real products are uploaded and no supplier approval is needed yet. Demo products fill the catalog so the store looks realistic, and they are replaced by live products when the store goes live. [L1062, 2020-26]
- Loading real products before a supplier has approved you is outside the demo stage. Use demo products in the catalog instead. [L1062, 2020-26]
- Store build sequence from the one source course, with product validation first:
  1. Build the store with Elixir by following the step-by-step store build guide.
  2. Optimize the product page: images, copy, urgency elements and trust badges.
  3. Keep the design clean and trustworthy.
  4. Submit the store for review via ticket before it goes live.
  [L0491, 2026]
- Conflict on sequencing. one source says not to build a store until a product has been approved, because building early wastes time on products that may not pass review. [L0491, 2026] The one source and one source units assume the product choice is made and say to build the demo store before supplier outreach. [L0506, 2020-26; L0969, 2022; L0771, 2021-22]
  - Applies: validate the product first. Once the product line is chosen, build the demo store before contacting suppliers.
- Store domain: the store must have its own domain name. Suppliers see this as a sign of professionalism and an established business. [L0123, undated]
- The shop name and the website domain: a Shopify subdomain such as store-name.myshopify.com in outreach or on the store makes the business look unprofessional and untrustworthy. [L0767, 2020-26]

### Stock images: number, sources and legal sourcing

#### How many images per product
- One image per demo product is sufficient. Multiple images are not required. [L0036, 2020-26]
- One product photograph is sufficient for each demo product. [L0219, 2020-26]
- One image per product at acceptable quality is enough. It does not need to be perfect; the best available image is sufficient. [L0964, 2023]
- Demo products that have only one product photo are fine. Stock images with only one angle are acceptable. [L0855, 2023]
- Recommended: at least one product photo without people. Lifestyle photos with people are optional. If none is available on Canva, search Google royalty-free images for one product shot per product. [L0834, 2021]
- Five to 10 images in total are enough for five demo products plus a homepage banner. [L0268, 2020]
- For a mock store, use three to five royalty-free stock images found through a Google image search. Do not use the supplier images you plan to show in outreach. [L0671, 2021]
- Five distinct product photos are enough to start. Search the niche plus the words white background. [L1111, 2020]
- A handful of product photos can be enough at launch. [L0247, 2021]
- Only a handful of images per product type is needed, because dummy products are removed as soon as real suppliers are live. [L0359, 2020]
- Upload no more than two source images per product when copying from a source page. If the source page has only one image, upload only that one. [L0452, 2021-22]
- Never launch with only demo or stock images. Use stock photos during the build and demo phase, and replace them with supplier product photos as soon as a supplier is approved. [L0270, 2021]

#### Sourcing procedures

Procedure: Commercial rights on Google Images [L0036, 2020-26]
1. Search your niche on google.com/images.
2. Click Tools.
3. Click Usage Rights.
4. Select 'Commercial and other licenses'.
5. Review results and verify each image by visiting its source site before using.

Procedure: Labeled for reuse on Google Images [L0359, 2020]
1. Open Google and search for the product type, for example leather sofa.
2. Click Images.
3. Click Tools.
4. Open the usage rights menu and select Labeled for reuse.
5. Save only a handful of images for the demo products.

Procedure: Labeled for reuse, from the one source-style store guide [L0767, 2020-26]
1. Search the product type on Google.
2. Switch to the Images tab.
3. Open Tools.
4. Set Usage rights to 'Labeled for reuse'. Downloaded images are then royalty-free and safe to use on a website.

Setting: Google Images path for royalty-free photos [L0767, 2020-26]
- Google search > Images tab > Tools > Usage rights > 'Labeled for reuse'.
- 'Labeled for reuse with modification' is the alternative when edits are planned.

Procedure: Royalty-free images for demo products, older (2020) [L0852, 2020]
1. Search Google for the product name, for example leather sofa.
2. Click Images.
3. Click Tools.
4. Click Usage rights.
5. Choose labeled for reuse or labeled for reuse with modification.
6. Download a set of images and name the products as you want.
7. Write a one or two sentence basic description for each product.

Free stock photo sites can also be used. [L0852, 2020]

Procedure: Free images for demo products, older (2021) [L0853, 2021]
1. Search Google Images for the product name.
2. Click Tools, then choose Usage rights and select a free-to-use option.
3. Pick suitable stock images to use as demo products.
4. Alternatively, browse Pixabay for free stock images.
5. Upload 5 to 10 demo products and replace them with real products once suppliers are approved.

Procedure: Free images with usage rights, older (2021) [L0962, 2021]
1. Run a Google image search for your product.
2. Open Tools.
3. Under Usage rights, select Creative Commons licenses.
4. Use the results that show the product clearly.

Pixabay is a free stock photo alternative. [L0962, 2021]

Procedure: Demo product pages, older (2018) [L0466, 2018]
1. Open Google Images and search for the product, for example stand-up paddle boards.
2. Click the Images tab, then Tools, then Usage rights, and choose Labeled for reuse.
3. Select a royalty-free image of the product.
4. Create a product page named after the product, for example orange paddle board.
5. Add a few lines describing the product, such as size, color, brand, and included parts.

Setting, older (2018): Google Images > Settings > Advanced search > Usage rights dropdown > Free to use or share > Advanced search. Choose images labeled for reuse and use them only as placeholders until supplier images arrive. Do not copy images from other sites. [L1274, 2018]

Setting, older (2019): Google Images > Tools > Usage rights > Labeled for reuse. Use this filter to find product photos you can legally use as stock images for a demo store. [L1275, 2019]

Setting, older (2019): To find demo product images, search the product term in Google, open the Images tab, click Tools, then set Usage rights to 'Labeled for reuse'. Use the generic photos you find for your demo store. [L1002, 2019]

Procedure, older (2020): Image sourcing for a new store before brands send photos [L1277, 2020]
1. Start with stock photo sites, since only a handful of images per product is needed.
2. If no suitable stock photos exist, use photos from brands you have not contacted yet, for example from their public sites.
3. Replace these placeholders with supplier images once the supplier is approved.

- Images labeled for reuse can be published on your own website without the risk of a copyright claim. [L0767, 2020-26]
- Google Images labeled 'licensable' means the copyright holder expects payment. Always verify the source website to confirm the actual licensing terms before using any image found through the commercial license filter. [L0036, 2020-26]

#### Legal rules for images
- Do not copy product photos from competitor websites. Use free stock sites to avoid legal risks and to demonstrate professionalism. [L0036, 2020-26]
- Do not copy product photos from Google Shopping or competitor sites. Use your own photos or licensed stock images to avoid rights problems. [L1462, undated]
- Do not copy images from other sites. [L1274, 2018]
- Do not use actual supplier photos or PLR (Private Label Rights) content. [L0268, 2020]
- Never upload real supplier products, photos or descriptions before you have approval. [L0551, 2020]
- If you use royalty-free stock photos from image search, you can keep them on a demo store. If the rights are unclear, remove them and use images from suppliers that have approved you. [L1112, 2021]
- Demo products that use another supplier's photos should be deleted once that supplier approves. [L1112, 2021]
- Demo product pages should show only a general idea of a future product page. They must not be used to claim that a brand has already approved the store. [L0623, 2020-26]
- Using a supplier's photos for demo products before approval usually does no harm. Stock photos avoid the question entirely and are easy to swap out. [L1275, 2019] *(Conflict noted below.)*
- Conflict on supplier photos: [L1275, 2019] (2019) says supplier photos usually do no harm, while [L0268, 2020] (2020), [L1462, undated], and [L0551, 2020] say not to use them before approval. Applies: use royalty-free or licensed images before approval. [L1275, 2019] is older advice.
- Conflict on competitor copying: the competitor-copy procedure in [L0452, 2021-22] includes copying images from the source page, while [L0036, 2020-26] and [L1462, undated] forbid copying competitor photos. Applies: copy only the title and description where allowed, and replace photos with licensed or own images.

#### Image quality and sizing
- Demo store images do not need top quality. A demo store shows the setup, not conversion. Focus on consistent sizing. [L0806, 2024]
- Canva procedure, older (2024) [L0806, 2024]
  1. Click Create a design in the top right.
  2. Choose Custom size.
  3. Enter 600 x 600 pixels and click Create new design.
  4. Add images with Magic Media (AI generated) or drag in your own.
  5. Save; all images have the same size.
- Demo-site images can stay in place during setup. After launch and once suppliers are onboard, swap them for supplier and brand lifestyle images that show the actual products. [L1310, 2021-22]
