### Recommended hire order

#### Sequence from the sources

Procedure, each function scaled before adding complexity:
1. Start with product listing specialists. Why: they enable unlimited brands, because you manage suppliers. [L0263, 2022]
2. Add a supplier relations specialist, who manages supplier outreach and partnerships. [L0263, 2022]
3. Add a customer service team, with pre-purchase and post-purchase roles. [L0263, 2022]
4. Add an operations manager, who oversees fulfillment and is the biggest team, in the middle of the organization. [L0263, 2022]
5. Add a bookkeeper, who tracks costs, reconciles, and finds unprofitable orders. [L0263, 2022]
6. Add advertising, content and email specialists. [L0263, 2022]
7. Build each store with the same structure. [L0263, 2022]

Procedure:
1. Hire one person to handle support (live chat, phone and email) and order processing. [L0834, 2021]
2. Add a second person when inbound volume grows. [L0834, 2021]
3. Hire part-time (around 20 hours a month) for social media and website content once repetitive tasks are offloaded. [L0834, 2021]
4. Test content yourself first, and hand it off once you know what works. [L0834, 2021]

Procedure, typical hiring order for a drop shipping operation (costs per month are estimates):
1. Hire a customer service VA first, at about $1,000, since customer communication usually takes most of the owner's time. [L1334, 2021-22]
2. When one VA is overloaded, split the role into customer service and order processing, the latter at about $600 for order management and supplier coordination. [L1334, 2021-22]
3. Hire a catalog manager at about $1,200 to keep product information, listings and stock current. [L1334, 2021-22]
4. Hire a customer service manager at about $1,500 so the owner no longer answers the team's questions and only hears about resolved problems. [L1334, 2021-22]
5. Once the operation is stable, hire growth roles one at a time: an SEO person at about $800, a PPC person for paid ads, and a content writer for blog posts and emails, all under a marketing manager. [L1334, 2021-22]
6. When the business gets busy, hire a sales rep at about $1,000 and split sales off from customer service. [L1334, 2021-22]
7. Add a product uploader at about $500 for data entry when product volume requires it. [L1334, 2021-22]
8. Add a finance and admin role for bookkeeping, Profit First planning and financial reporting, and outsource tax work to an accountant. [L1334, 2021-22]

Procedure:
1. Hire first for order fulfillment and customer support. [L1431, 2020+]
2. Next hire someone to build stores. [L1431, 2020+]
3. Then hire someone to run ads, because each role needs different skills. [L1431, 2020+]

- Outsource customer service first, then order fulfillment (if no agent or supplier already handles it), and then agencies that run ads. VAs for fulfillment are not needed when an agent or supplier already does the work. [L0780, 2024+]
- For a product business, hire a dedicated operations or supply-chain lead before the founders are overloaded. A hire with prior operations experience at a large company can take over supply-chain work the founders were handling themselves. [L0334, 2017]
- Hire the first helper quickly, even part-time and for a single task, instead of waiting until the full team is planned. Each task moved off the owner's plate frees time for growth work. [L0337, 2022]
- Hire one full-time employee responsible for both customer service (email and chat) and order management (status updates and supplier coordination). [L0230, 2019]
- Build an in-house team through a VA agency, and keep order fulfillment in-house as long as it is manageable. [L0492, 2023+]

#### Comparison of first, second and third hires

| Source | First hire | Second hire | Third hire |
|---|---|---|---|
| one source, profit stages | Customer service at $2,000 net profit per month | Product upload and optimization at $3,500 net profit per month | Outsourced marketing at $5,000 net profit per month |
| one source | Support (live chat, phone, email) and order processing | Second person when inbound volume grows | Part-time social media and website content (about 20 hours per month) |
| one source | Customer service VA (about $1,000 per month) | Order processing (about $600 per month) | Catalog manager (about $1,200 per month) |
| one source | Order fulfillment and customer support | Store builder | Ads |
| one source, revenue phases | Phase 1 ($0-20k): customer service agents, fulfillment agents if dropshipping, influencer outreach | Phase 2 ($20-100k): project manager, then first media buyer | Phase 3 ($100k-500k): supply chain manager, payment processing agent |

Sources: [L1446, 2020-26]-[L1446, 2020-26; L0834, 2021; L1334, 2021-22; L1431, 2020+; L1129, undated]

- Customer service is the first hire in most sources, and the first hire in one source's profit stages, one source's order, and the one source fulfillment and support role. The second and third hires differ between sources, so use the one that matches your store's bottleneck. [L1446, 2020-26; L1334, 2021-22; L1431, 2020+]
- The first VA for phone and email support frees the owner to focus on traffic and supplier relationships, and it improves customer service. [L0852, 2020]
- Growth roles can be hired one by one under a marketing manager. Hiring someone to work through the training modules and implement them is a valid first step before specialists. [L1334, 2021-22]

#### Case: team build-up timeline

Store team timeline:
- 2014: A dropshipping store started on a different model. The owner learned on their own and saw it could not reach financial independence. [L0337, 2022]
- 2016: The owner switched to the one source method, still working a corporate job and handling orders and customer calls in early mornings, nights and breaks. [L0337, 2022]
- 2017: A part-time phone helper was hired and paid per answered call. The owner still worked the full-time job and got burnt out. [L0337, 2022]
- 2018: A full-time virtual assistant was hired for operations. The owner left the corporate job. [L0337, 2022]
- 2020: A team of three, then four (including a US hire with experience and a second US hire). Revenue reached $1.4 million. The team handled operations, customer service, and an outsourced social media, ads and accounting function. [L0337, 2022]
- 2021: A fourth virtual assistant was hired. The team handled all day-to-day operations and escalations, so the owner only did sales, some marketing and accounting, from anywhere. [L0337, 2022]

#### Affordability check before each hire

- Do not hire virtual assistants until the store is profitable and you have run it yourself for about a month. [L0941, 2022]
- Tie each hire to the profit it can be paid from, and do not hire until the business's own profit pays for it. [L1287, 2025; L1437, 2018]

### What to hire first

- Start with the job that takes the most of your time and needs no judgment. In most sources that is customer communication. [L1334, 2021-22; L1431, 2020+; L0780, 2024+]
- When the business needs a skill you do not have, hire for that gap, for example supplier negotiation, customer support quality or store graphics. [L0765, undated]
- Before the first hire, document each process as a playbook, so a new hire can take over tasks with clear instructions. [L0941, 2022]
- Hire a VA for order fulfillment and support first, then a person to build stores, then one to run ads, because each role needs different skills. [L1431, 2020+]
- Test content yourself first and hand it off once you know what works. [L0834, 2021]
- Hire the first helper quickly, even part-time, and for a single task. [L0337, 2022]
- Hiring for advertising and content roles takes longer. Build your own email marketer internally and train them. [L0263, 2022]
- When a media buyer is needed, the first one should come in phase 2 of the revenue stages, once customer service, influencer outreach and project management are covered. [L1129, undated]
