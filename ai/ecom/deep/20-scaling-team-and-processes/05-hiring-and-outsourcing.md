### Hiring and outsourcing

Hiring a Business Process Outsourcing freelancer to build documentation when the owner is overloaded ** [L0467, 2019]:

1. Post a job on Upwork for a BPO specialist, not a day-to-day virtual assistant [L0467, 2019].
2. Choose a candidate whose Upwork profile shows experience writing SOPs (standard operating procedures) [L0467, 2019].
3. Have them do the tasks and document each process as they go, so the owner spends only a few hours a week with them [L0467, 2019].
4. Once the documentation exists, bring on a customer service VA and hand over the documents [L0467, 2019].

A BPO freelancer costs more than a customer service VA, but the Philippines offers strong workers at good prices ** [L0467, 2019].

- Write standard operating procedures before hiring a virtual assistant so they know how to run the business. Hire someone who has held jobs at other companies for long periods, since short tenures often signal instability ** [L0853, 2021].
- Write the SOP before posting a job advertisement, not after hiring someone. Documenting the task first clarifies what to ask candidates in the application and interview stages and avoids pressure during onboarding ** [L1339, 2021].
- Create a standard operating procedure for every task you delegate, regardless of how small or simple it seems. For large tasks, several SOPs covering individual components can be useful, but every outsourced task needs at least one ** [L1339, 2021].
- Every SOP should be adapted to the specific business before it is handed to a VA. Add the business's own preferred methods, storage locations for files and any other specifics, and update the SOP whenever the business changes ** [L0945, 2021-22].
- Link building outsourcing SOPs should be written first for the Skyscraper method, since it is the most time-consuming and most complicated link building approach. Its tasks are the priority for documentation ** [L0945, 2021-22].
- Learn as you go and document each step, so that whoever runs the operation later can understand how every supplier works without asking the owner ** [L0468, 2020].
- Build the business so that it can be handed over. Create a video of every process from the start, and keep the team's SOPs current [L0337, 2022; L1122, 2016].

### Systemizing and continuous improvement

- Build repeatable systems for every recurring task, because systems maximize efficiency and produce consistent results. A good system can be handed to an employee or virtual assistant with predictable output [L1039, 2019+].
- Use the same proven selection criteria every time you choose a product, and the same repeatable process every time you build traffic, instead of starting from scratch each time. This makes results predictable across stores [L1039, 2019+].
- Study competitors who already succeed in your market and reverse-engineer their systems to see what works. Then replicate it and improve on it rather than guessing [L1039, 2019+].
- A system should work like a recipe: anyone who follows it exactly should reach the same result. This allows you to outsource or automate tasks without wondering whether they are being done correctly [L1039, 2019+].
- If you cannot describe a task as a clear step-by-step process, you do not understand it well enough to delegate or repeat it. Document every routine as you go, including tasks like uploading a product or researching suppliers [L1039, 2019+].
- Look for inefficiencies in repetitive tasks and improve them, since a system should be the most direct route to the result. Keep what works and build it into the process for your future employees [L1039, 2019+].
- Build systems as an ongoing habit rather than a one-time to-do list, and keep refining them as the business changes. Treating process improvement as part of your normal way of working makes it continuous [L1039, 2019+].
- Build a standard operating procedure for repeatable tasks that are not business specific, and share and store them so the team can reuse them ** [L0801, 2021].

### Mistakes to avoid

- Running a business with all processes stored in the owner's head makes it impossible to hand over work. Write down processes and build a system before hiring anyone [L1000, 2017].
- Do not wait for a major life event to force you to build systems. Start documenting and building the operation while you still run it yourself [L1473, 2019].
- Writing an SOP for a task you have never performed yourself leads to wrong time estimates and missing steps [L1359, 2014].
- Sending SOP files directly to new team members creates copies that drift apart. Use one shared Google Drive link [L1340, 2020-26].
- Hiring for an unproven process means paying someone to learn something you have not yet proven works [L1359, 2014].
