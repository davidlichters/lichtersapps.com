## Scaling TikTok, Pinterest and other channels

### Deciding When to Add a Second Channel

- Keep Google Ads as the main traffic source until the store reaches roughly $30,000 to $50,000 in monthly revenue before adding other channels. This is older advice from 2019. [L0663, 2019]
- At monthly sales of about $5,000 to $20,000, it is not worth taking time away from Google Ads to build other channels, even if Google seems not to be scaling. This is older advice from 2019. [L0663, 2019]
- Channels like Pinterest work but take time to master, because they require learning a new skill. Adding them too early takes focus away from the core Google setup. This is older advice from 2019. [L0663, 2019]
- Move to other channels when Google growth stalls, meaning budget increases no longer add sales. Then test Pinterest properly and try Facebook and Instagram ads to cold audiences. This is older advice from 2019. [L0663, 2019]
- When the profitable Google Shopping limit is reached, more traffic has to come from new sources such as organic search and social media. Pushing harder inside Google Shopping does not solve it. This is advice from one source to 2022. [L0744, 2021-22]
- Once Google Shopping cannot be scaled further at a 10X return, it is time to consider adding other advertising platforms. [L0750, 2020-26]
- Scale a winning product on Facebook until its audience is exhausted, then test TikTok ads for it. Most products in this approach performed best on Facebook. [L1017, undated]
- When a store is profitable but not scaling, open another channel such as Google to turn the business into an ecosystem with more touch points. [L0950, 2023]
- Build an ecosystem of channels (Facebook, TikTok, Google) so that each channel covers gaps the others leave, especially as Q4 approaches. [L0950, 2023]
- Scaling is limited by payment setup and cash flow rather than by the number of TikTok accounts. Fix the setup, systems and rules before scaling aggressively. [L0363, 2022+]
- Direct-response TV and radio campaigns are a scaling option to consider once social ads are working. They require significant spending and carry financial risk if they do not work, so test before committing large budgets. [L0334, 2017]

### Fallback When a Main Ad Account Is Suspended

- While a main Facebook ad account is suspended, the seller can keep testing products on other channels such as Snapchat, Pinterest, TikTok and Twitter, then move to Google Ads and return to Facebook once it is restored. [L0562, undated]
- Running several channels in parallel reduces dependence on one blocked account. [L0562, undated]

### Small Budgets and the Five-Month Checklist

- On a small budget, growth can come from a unique angle or a viral post rather than outbidding competitors on ads. [L0357, 2019]
- A post that gets comments, shares and reactions can be boosted with about $5 using targeting, which is cheaper than trying to win a bidding war. [L0357, 2019]
- Five-month marketing checklist when Google Ads alone is not enough:
  1. Run Bing Shopping. [L0357, 2019]
  2. Use influencer marketing. [L0357, 2019]
  3. Use blog banner advertising. [L0357, 2019]
  4. Raise search engine optimization to the highest priority. [L0357, 2019]
  5. Run paid social traffic campaigns. [L0357, 2019]
  6. Use referral marketing. [L0357, 2019]
  7. Run email marketing. [L0357, 2019]

### TikTok Organic: Posting and Momentum

- Standard upload frequency is 3 videos per day, 4 on a day when content goes viral. A target of 4 to 6 videos per day is suggested for accounts that have the time to produce them. [L0764, 2022]
- After a video goes viral, post more and do not hesitate to make similar videos. Repeating a proven format with small changes in sound, wording, color or movement can keep the momentum going. [L0764, 2022]
- Creators often stop posting after one viral hit because they fear damaging the account. This wastes the momentum, since a viral moment should be used by increasing output. [L0764, 2022]
- Inconsistent uploading, such as one upload a day with long gaps, lets viral momentum fade. Keep uploading regularly to make the most of any breakout video. [L0764, 2022]
- Once organic videos prove they sell, fund paid ads from the profit those videos already generated rather than from your own money. [L0820, undated]
- Keep the sequence: organic content first, then paid ads to maximize sales, then brand building once the product is proven. [L0820, undated]

### TikTok Accounts: Number and Setup

- You can operate 2 to 3 or more TikTok accounts simultaneously on the same phone. No additional devices need to be purchased; log in and out as needed. [L0309, 2024+]
- Use separate email addresses for each account. [L0309, 2024+]
- Assign each account a purpose: testing and research, product promotion, or personal use. [L0309, 2024+]
- Sources differ on the limit per phone. One source says no more than three accounts per phone, ideally two, with about seven mentioned as a maximum. A ban on one account can hurt the others on the same device. [L1444, 2022]
- Use separate phones for separate products and keep successful accounts apart from experimental ones. This protects the working account from risk when new tests are added. [L1444, 2022]
- Do not log into one TikTok account from two phones at the same time. Share finished video files through a cloud folder and let one person upload to the account. [L1444, 2022]
- Split team roles so one person builds the store while the other creates content. This allows the content creator to test products faster. [L1444, 2022]

### TikTok Account Structure and Prospecting Setup

- TikTok ad account structure should be simple and broad. There is no need for dozens of campaigns or many ad sets per campaign, because the platform performs best with broad targeting. [L1374, undated]
- Start with no more than five ad groups per campaign, and three is a good starting number. A typical start includes broad and recommended interest ad sets, plus one hashtag ad set that often underperforms over time. [L1374, undated]
- Set up a prospecting campaign with three ad sets:
  1. Ad set 1 is broad targeting with both genders, all ages except 13-17 excluded, and no interests added. [L1374, undated]
  2. Ad set 2 uses TikTok's Recommended Interests by clicking the use these button. [L1374, undated]
  3. Ad set 3 uses hashtag targeting with one ad set per hashtag. [L1374, undated]
- Start a new TikTok prospecting campaign with Lower Cost (maximized conversions), not Value Optimization. TikTok needs customer data for value optimization to work, and performance is poor without data. [L1374, undated]
- TikTok Recommended Interests is the best interest-targeting option. In the speaker's tests it outperformed manually entered interests in 9.5 out of 10 tests. Use the recommended interests as-is, even though some may look odd. [L1374, undated]
- For hashtag targeting, use about three hashtags, each in its own ad set so you can see which one produces results. Hashtag targeting is not available in every ad account yet, and hashtags that are brand-associated or product-specific may not show up as options. [L1374, undated]

### TikTok Budget Structure: ABO and CBO

- For testing, split the TikTok ad budget by ad set (ABO) rather than by campaign (CBO), because CBO can send most of the budget to one ad set and make the test unfair. [L1374, undated]
- The speaker prefers ABO at all stages, but notes CBO may improve as TikTok's algorithm matures, as Facebook's did. [L1374, undated]
- Ad set budget optimization (ABO): set the budget at ad set level, for example 10 dollars each for three ad sets. [L1374, undated]
- Campaign budget optimization (CBO): set a total campaign budget, for example 30 dollars, which the algorithm distributes across ad sets. [L1374, undated]
- Under CBO, ad sets can train each other through shared campaign-level optimization. Data from a well-performing ad set can help a broad ad set find buyers. With ABO, ad sets optimize separately and do not share data. [L1374, undated]
- Example: with a 30 dollar campaign budget under CBO, the algorithm can give about 25 dollars per day to one ad set and only about 2.50 dollars per day to the other two. This is why ABO gives each ad set an equal budget and makes a split test fair. [L1374, undated]
- Source disagreement: in the operator's experience, CBO performed poorly on TikTok, in the same way it did on Facebook when it was introduced, so budgets were set manually per campaign. [L0819, 2020+]
- Source disagreement: later units use CBO campaigns for scaling and for the staged budget ladder, once a creative and audience are proven. See the scaling sections below. [L1436, undated; L1382, undated; L1375, undated]

### TikTok Scaling: Horizontal and Vertical

- Scaling procedure for TikTok ads:
  1. Run one campaign dedicated to scaling and one dedicated to testing. [L0819, 2020+]
  2. Judge performance by comparing Google Analytics data with TikTok ad results using UTMs. [L0819, 2020+]
  3. If a campaign performs well, duplicate it three times. [L0819, 2020+]
  4. Double the budget on the duplicates. [L0819, 2020+]
  5. Run a separate testing campaign for new creatives continuously, and move creatives that beat the benchmark into a scaling campaign. [L0819, 2020+]
- Vertical scaling versus horizontal scaling on TikTok:
  1. Horizontal scaling: duplicate a winning ad set without changing anything, so 25 dollars per day becomes 50 dollars across two copies. [L1374, undated]
  2. Vertical scaling: increase the budget of the winning ad set step by step, for example 25, 50, 75, 100 dollars. [L1374, undated]
  3. Use horizontal scaling at lower budgets (around 25 dollars per day), because the percentage increase from vertical scaling is large and can trigger re-optimization. [L1374, undated]
  4. Use vertical scaling at higher budgets (around 500 dollars per day), because a 500 to 700 increase is a small percentage change. [L1374, undated]
  5. Split test both methods and keep the one that works best. [L1374, undated]
- A simple account with broad targeting and few campaigns is reported to have scaled smoothly to about 5,000 dollars per day, and it is claimed to scale to about 10,000 dollars per day. Scaling happens by raising the budget of ad sets with a positive return on ad spend. [L1374, undated]
- Duplicating an ad set that already has enough data can move the copy automatically toward value optimization. Duplicating into value optimization is only useful after data exists, because early value optimization without data can perform very badly. [L1374, undated]
- Value optimization transition on TikTok:
  1. After the ad set has enough data, duplicate the best-performing ad set. [L1374, undated]
  2. Switch the copy from Lower Cost to Value Optimization. [L1374, undated]
  3. Scale that copy by raising its budget. [L1374, undated]
  4. Do not switch to Value Optimization early when there is no data, because results can go badly wrong. [L1374, undated]

### TikTok Staged Budget Ladder (CBO)

- Staged scaling procedure for TikTok CBO campaigns:
  1. Confirm that CPC is below $1, CPM is below $10, CTR is above 1.5%, and the store conversion rate is good. [L1375, undated]
  2. Start from the initial CBO with a daily budget of $50. [L1375, undated]
  3. Double the budget to $100 and monitor results for three hours. [L1375, undated]
  4. If results stay consistent or improve, raise the budget to $150 after three hours. [L1375, undated]
  5. If results still hold, raise it to $200 after another three hours. [L1375, undated]
  6. If results still hold, raise it to $250 after another three hours. [L1375, undated]
  7. At the end of the day, reset the budget to $50 so the next day starts from scratch, then repeat steps 3 to 6. [L1375, undated]
- Each increase waits three hours. The budget reaches $250 nine hours after the first move to $100, and the method then ends with the end-of-day reset to $50. [L1375, undated]

| Step | Budget | Time after first move to $100 |
|---|---|---|
| Start | $50 | before scaling |
| 1 | $100 | 0 hours |
| 2 | $150 | 3 hours |
| 3 | $200 | 6 hours |
| 4 | $250 | 9 hours |
| End of day | reset to $50 | end of day [L1375, undated] |

- If results get worse after a budget increase, reset the budget to $50 and retry the scaling sequence the next day. [L1375, undated]
- Higher budgets bring more reach, more traffic to the store, and more sales, so budget increases should be staged and checked for consistency rather than made all at once. [L1375, undated]
- When several CBOs perform well, increase the budget on all of them and apply the same staged scaling method. [L1375, undated]

### TikTok Creative Testing and Break-Even

- Break-even test per creative: let each creative spend up to the product profit. For example, $20 profit allows $20 spend per creative. Keep the creative running only if it produces a purchase; kill it otherwise. [L1376, undated]
- TikTok ads work like a casino in their first two days, when they deliver the best traffic. A creative with no sales in those two days is unlikely to start selling later and should be stopped. [L1376, undated]
- Starting a testing phase: begin with one ad group per creative. With 10 creatives this means 10 ad groups at $20 per day each, with no interest targeting. [L1376, undated]
- Vertical scaling on TikTok with ad groups:
  1. Find more winning creatives first; with only three winners, scaling to large numbers is unrealistic. [L1376, undated]
  2. Create variants of the best performing creatives by changing small elements such as music, the first two seconds or the hook. [L1376, undated]
  3. When several creatives perform, raise each ad group budget from $20 to $100 per day. [L1376, undated]
  4. Stack the top three best performing creatives into one ad group instead of one creative per ad group. [L1376, undated]
  5. Each day at about 11:30 to 11:45 pm (before midnight), increase the budget of these ad groups by $20, for example $100 to $120, then $140, and so on. [L1376, undated]
- Judge creatives by CTR and CPC, and cut ad groups that reached the break-even cost without sales. [L1382, undated]
- In the testing stage, the goal is to learn which ad group, audience and creative work, not to get immediate sales from every test. If the landing page is good and add-to-cart and checkout rates are healthy, an ad group that spent the break-even cost without a sale can be turned off. [L1382, undated]
- The purpose of the testing stage is not an immediate sale but finding out which audience, which creative and which ad group works, judged by CPC and CTR. [L1436, undated]
- When sales are unclear, judge the creative by CTR. An ad showing a CTR of 1 percent or more across ad groups was the strongest candidate in the test. [L1436, undated]
- A high CPM on some TikTok ad accounts can distort the results, so a poor result is not always caused by the product or creative. Check the account before blaming the test. [L1436, undated]

### TikTok Manual Bidding Scaling

- Manual bidding scaling on TikTok:
  1. Create a new manual bidding campaign once you have several winning creatives and winning ad groups. [L1376, undated]
  2. Set the daily budget per ad group between $100 and $300, depending on total budget. [L1376, undated]
  3. Create five ad groups with different target cost-per-purchase values, starting from the average cost per purchase. Example: average $12, then $11.50, $11, $10.50 and $10. [L1376, undated]
  4. Lower-target ad groups may perform well and buy purchases at a lower cost. [L1376, undated]
  5. Do not raise budgets linearly. Duplicate a well-performing ad group and double its budget, for example $100 to $200, then $400, then a $300 to $400 range as used. [L1376, undated]
  6. Keep watching the ad groups closely, because manual bidding can overspend suddenly. [L1376, undated]
- Manual bidding on TikTok can suddenly overspend, for example spending $100 to $200 per day without matching purchases even when the budget is set to $300 and earlier spend was low. Monitor these ad groups daily. [L1376, undated]
- Scaling a manual bidding ad group on TikTok: do not raise its budget linearly. Duplicate the ad group that performed well and set the duplicate's budget to double the original, for example $100 to $200, then $300 to $400 with the next duplicate. Keep checking each duplicate because overspend can happen suddenly. [L1376, undated]

### TikTok Ad Group Scaling and Replacement

- Scale by duplicating winning ad groups, and consider switching to cost cap bidding once a campaign has about 50 or more purchases. [L1377, 2022+]
- Replace underperforming ad groups with new creatives rather than letting them run unchanged. [L1377, 2022+]
- If a test campaign is in the last four to five hours of the day, launch new ad groups the next day with fresh creatives instead of changing it mid-day. [L1377, 2022+]
- Compare the next day's results across creatives and identify which part of the funnel fails. [L1377, 2022+]

### TikTok Test Campaign Structures

- Test structure: one campaign with five ad groups (the original plus four duplicates), each holding two to four video ads. [L1378, undated]
- Let the test campaign run for two days before evaluating results. [L1378, undated]
- Testing and scaling in order, with one campaign of three to four ad groups:
  1. Launch one campaign with three to four ad groups, for example open audience, stacked interest, hashtags, and another open or stacked interest audience. [L1382, undated]
  2. Set each ad group budget to about $25 to $30 and let it run the first day. [L1382, undated]
  3. Put in the three best creatives per ad group, with one ad copy using an offer line. [L1382, undated]
  4. Judge creatives by CTR and CPC, and cut ad groups that reached the break-even cost without sales. [L1382, undated]
  5. Once one creative and audience are winning, build CBO campaigns (see the aggressive scaling procedure below). [L1382, undated]
- Testing structure for a new product on TikTok:
  1. Create one campaign with three to four ad groups, each with a different audience, for example open targeting, a stacked interest audience, a hashtag audience, and one more open or stacked audience. [L1436, undated]
  2. Set each ad group budget to $25 to $30. A $20 ad group budget caused spending problems on new accounts. [L1436, undated]
  3. Add the three best creatives to each ad group (add 1, add 2, add 3), with the same ad copy in each group. [L1436, undated]
  4. Use one ad copy with an offer line such as 70% off, get yours today or ending soon. [L1436, undated]
  5. Schedule the ads to launch at 6 am Eastern time (or 8 am), so spend begins around 9 am after the 30 to 40 minute ramp. [L1436, undated]
  6. Let the test run for the first day, then judge by CPC, CTR and sales. [L1436, undated]
  7. Turn off ad groups that have spent the break-even amount without a sale when the landing page is performing well. [L1436, undated]
- A winning product run through the TikTok scaling structure typically shows about 2 to 5 sales on day one and about 10 to 15 sales on day two. Results far below this suggest the test has not yet found a winner. [L1436, undated]

### TikTok Aggressive CBO Scaling

- Aggressive CBO scaling on TikTok in order:
  1. Once a winning creative and audience are known, launch five CBO campaigns, each at $150 with four ad groups. [L1382, undated]
  2. Use the same single winning creative and ad copy in each ad group, with open targeting: US, age 18+, all genders, no interests. [L1382, undated]
  3. Use manual payment and add no more than $50 at a time. [L1382, undated]
  4. After each campaign has spent about $50 total, check the results, and cut campaigns without a sale if any other campaign has one. [L1382, undated]
  5. Cut any campaign whose CPC is above $1 and keep the one with the best CPC. [L1382, undated]
  6. Scale the winning campaign after three profitable sales, first to $300 and then to $500 or more per day, with budget increases in steps. [L1382, undated]
- Scaling structure for a winning product on TikTok:
  1. Take the winning creative (for example add 2), the winning ad copy and the winning audience (for example open targeting, US, 18+, all genders, no interest filters). [L1436, undated]
  2. Launch five CBO campaigns at $150 each, each with four ad groups using the same audience and only the winning creative and ad copy. [L1436, undated]
  3. Use manual payment and add no more than $50 at a time. [L1436, undated]
  4. Let each campaign run until about $10 is spent, which is about $50 in total for the test. [L1436, undated]
  5. Cut every campaign without a sale. If no campaign has a sale, cut any campaign with a CPC above $1 and keep the one with a CPC around $0.5 to $0.6. [L1436, undated]
  6. When one campaign gives profitable sales (about three sales at 2x ROAS, with break-even ROAS around 1.4x to 1.5x), scale that campaign by increasing its budget step by step. [L1436, undated]
  7. Check campaigns several times a day because cutting and scaling are needed continuously. [L1436, undated]
- Source disagreement on the spend before the first check: one procedure says about $50 per campaign, the other says about $10 per campaign (about $50 in total for the test). Use the first check point that fits your campaign size. [L1382, undated; L1436, undated]
- Live example in the same lesson ('0 to $1,000/day in 2 days'): the speaker's own account showed several CBO campaigns at $300 each [35:49], and right before the call he had launched seven CBO campaigns at a $300 budget each [42:26]. From his earlier batch, CBO 6 won with good CPC, CPM and CTR and about $12 CPA on about $48 AOV [42:37]. So: start at $150 per CBO, then $300, then $500 or more per day once one CBO is profitable [43:43]. [L1382, undated; L1436, undated]
- Scale aggressively only when a campaign has shown several profitable sales, for example three sales at about 2x ROAS. Increase the budget in steps, such as $150 to $300, because TikTok tends to reward campaigns that bid more with better audiences. [L1436, undated]
- Once a campaign has produced profitable sales, raise its budget in steps of $50, waiting between steps, rather than jumping straight to a large amount. Move from the starting $150 toward about $300 per day and later about $500 per day only as results hold. [L1436, undated]
- TikTok scaling needs frequent hands-on checks during the day, because campaigns must be cut or raised soon after results show. Unlike Facebook, where an end-of-day review is often enough, plan to stay at the computer during scaling periods. [L1382, undated]
- After a budget increase on a TikTok campaign with a strong creative, the platform tends to direct the campaign to better-bidding audiences, so performance can improve after a raise. Judge the result after some time rather than in the first minutes after the change. [L1382, undated]

### Cutting Rules

- Stop any ad group or campaign that has spent its break-even CPA without a sale. Scaling requires this strict cutting rule. [L1436, undated]
- A good campaign keeps producing sales at each break-even CPA spend interval. If it fails to sell over two break-even intervals, the chances it will sell later are very low. [L1436, undated]
