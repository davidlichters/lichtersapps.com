## Scaling Google and Bing ad budgets

### Core principles of scaling Google ad budgets

- Scaling runs on three sequential levers: adding negative keywords, adjusting cost-per-click bids, and increasing daily budgets. Optimize each in sequence to keep profitability and control performance. [L0193, undated]
- The three levers are negative keywords, max CPC per product, and daily budgets. Repeat negative keyword and CPC work before each budget increase. [L0750, 2020-26]
- Start with a small budget so Google can learn and non-converting search terms are filtered out sooner. Scale spend only once campaigns are profitable enough to reinvest. [L0750, 2020-26]
- Scale a campaign only while it stays profitable with positive return on ad spend. Growth comes from adding profitable spend, not from spending as much as possible at once. [L1203, 2016; L1341, 2020-26]
- Grow budgets in steps. The priority is moving from a profitable $500 per month to $1,000, then $1,500, and so on, with the goal of being profitable at twice the current size. [L1341, 2020-26; L0463, 2020-26]
- Absolute profit rises even as ROAS falls toward 10X. Maintain a sustainable ROAS (around 15X) and grow revenue through larger budgets, not by preserving the highest percentage return. [L0276, 2024]
- Plan for ROAS to drop when a product scales. Keep enough margin that the product stays profitable at the lower return. [L1508, 2022+]
- Do not launch all Google Ads channels at once. Build and make one channel profitable before starting the next. [L1121, 2022]
- Do not raise budgets because Google recommends it. Google will always suggest a higher budget, so decide changes only from your own ROAS data. [L0972, 2025]
- Before raising ad budget, fully optimize negative keywords and campaign segmentation, check impression share, and improve store conversion so more visitors buy. ** [L0968, 2021]

### ROAS thresholds

| Threshold | Value | When it applies | IDs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Floor for raising budgets or bids | At least 10X ROAS, meaning at least $10 of revenue for every $1 of ad spend. Never raise budgets or bids below 10X. | Any increase, Google Shopping and general paid search | [L0750, 2020-26; L0972, 2025; L0359, 2020; L0193, undated] |
| Stop line per campaign | The campaign must stay above its own minimum ROAS. | Each campaign; the exact minimum is confirmed at the start of the plan | [L1203, 2016] |
| Working target while scaling | About 15X | Sustainable level while growing revenue; 2024 source | [L0276, 2024] |
| Later-stage example | 12X at $8,000 spend ** | Month 6 and beyond, as an example only | [L0270, 2021] |
| Early-stage example | 20X at $900 spend **; 20X at $1K spend (2024) | First months, when the account is being built | [L0270, 2021; L0276, 2024] |

- The 10X floor is the stop line for any increase. The 15X figure is the level to aim for while scaling. Both apply at the same time. [L0750, 2020-26; L0276, 2024]
- ROAS is judged as consistently above 10X over two weeks to a month before any raise. [L0972, 2025]
- Scale a channel only as far as its ROAS stays at 10X or above. When that limit is reached, put remaining budget into the next channel. [L0972, 2025]
- A ROAS drop at a larger budget is normal. Example: 15X at $3K, initial drop to about 10X at $5K, then optimize back to 15X at $5K. [L0276, 2024]

### The first month: learning phase

- Expect about 30 days of consistent optimization before most successful operators report meaningful momentum in Google Shopping campaigns. [L0193, undated]
- The first month is spent filtering bad traffic with negative keywords, calibrating CPC bids, and training Google to find the campaign's most profitable keywords. [L0193, undated]
- Reaching a profitable $1,000 per month from a profitable $500 per month often takes real time, even on large campaigns. This should be the top priority. [L0463, 2020-26]
- Time needed to manage paid search rises with spend. A store at $1,000 per month needs far fewer management hours than one at $10,000 or $50,000 per month. [L0463, 2020-26]

### Negative keywords and CPC levers

- Add negative keywords from the search terms report in every review, so budget is not spent on traffic that does not convert. [L0193, undated; L0750, 2020-26]
- Raise max CPC bids in small steps, such as five cents at a time, to find the ROAS sweet spot. [L1283, 2023]
- Large CPC jumps can cause spending at an excessive rate. If an ad is unprofitable and overspending, cuts can be made quickly. [L1283, 2023]
- Pausing underperforming products and reducing bids on weak performers moves spend toward the best-converting products. Successful stores know which products drive profit and bid aggressively on them. [L0299, 2020-26]
- Pausing non-performers frees budget and inventory slots to test new products, so the catalogue improves over time. [L0299, 2020-26]

### Review routine

- Set a recurring weekly or bi-weekly reminder for scaling optimization. Make it a fixed owner task in the calendar, not a one-time setup. [L0193, undated; L0750, 2020-26]
- Each review, in this order: [L0193, undated]
  1. Review search terms.
  2. Add negative keywords.
  3. Adjust CPC bids.
  4. Increase the daily budget if ROAS is at least 10X.
- Once familiar with the routine, a full review of product-level max CPC changes in a Shopping campaign takes about 15 minutes. The first one or two reviews take longer. [L0750, 2020-26]
- Track these numbers regularly: quality score, CTR, CPC, conversion rate, and ROAS. [L0510, 2018]

### Review frequency by conversion rate

Do not optimize more often than these click intervals. Too little data produces noise rather than signal. [L0299, 2020-26]

| Conversion rate | Review bids every | IDs |
|---|---|---|
| 2% | 50 clicks | [L0299, 2020-26] |
| 1.2% | 83 clicks | [L0299, 2020-26] |
| 1% | 100 clicks (think in 100-click blocks) | [L0299, 2020-26] |

### Budget progressions

Sources give different ladders. Use the ladder that matches the account's stage and ROAS, and never skip a step while ROAS is weak.

- **Google Shopping ladder, 2024 and 2025 sources:**
  - Example from one source: $10, $15, $20, $30, $50 per day, potentially reaching $500 to $2,000 per day if profitability is maintained. [L0193, undated]
  - Alternative: a new account starts with a shared daily budget of $10. Steps follow $10, $15, $20, $30, $40, $50, each only after the previous level has held the target return. [L0750, 2020-26]
  - Reaching $500 to $2,000 per day can take about a year of scaling, if the return holds. [L0750, 2020-26]
  - At a $2,000 daily budget with a 10X return, daily revenue must be at least $20,000. [L0750, 2020-26]
  - Stop increasing once the next level cannot be reached while staying at 10X. That level is the breaking point. [L0750, 2020-26]
- **Later Google Shopping steps (2025):** from $30 to $40 or $50. Never jump from $30 to $120, because large jumps make Google relearn the campaign and waste spend. [L0972, 2025]
- **Shopping ladder with pauses (2024):** $50 to $70 per day, wait about two weeks and check ROAS, then $90 or $100. [L1285, 2024]
- **Large-budget ladder (2023):** $100 to $120 per day, let it settle for a few days, then $140. [L1283, 2023]
- **Monthly search budget ladder (general):** start with a budget that delivers at least 10X, for example $1,000 per month. Raise in steps, such as $1,000 to $2,000, then $3,000 to $4,000. Keep ROAS at or above 10X at each step before going further. [L0359, 2020]
- Only make larger jumps once the business has a stronger bankroll and accepts more risk. [L0359, 2020]
- **Google Ads progression with ROAS, 2021 **:**

  | Month | Spend | ROAS | IDs |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | $900 | 20X | [L0270, 2021] |
  | 2 | $1,500 | 15X | [L0270, 2021] |
  | 3 | $1,500 (maintain) | 18X | [L0270, 2021] |
  | 4 | $2,500 | 15X | [L0270, 2021] |
  | 5 | $3,000 | 15X+ | [L0270, 2021] |
  | 6+ | $8,000 | 12X | [L0270, 2021] |

  Always increase incrementally. Never jump aggressively if ROAS is dropping. [L0270, 2021]

- **Google Ads progression with revenue, 2024:**

  | Spend | Target ROAS | Revenue | IDs |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | $1K | 20X | $20K | [L0276, 2024] |
  | $2K | about 15X | $30K | [L0276, 2024] |

  | $5K | initial drop to about 10X, then optimize back to 15X | $50K | [L0276, 2024] |

- **Monthly revenue benchmarks:** a viable store should generate about $20,000 to $30,000 per month from the three core Google and Bing campaign types alone. [L1203, 2016]
- A $6,000 monthly budget at 5X to 10X ROAS produces $30,000 to $60,000 in monthly revenue. At 7X, it produces over $40,000. [L1203, 2016]

### Raising a daily budget step by step

**Procedure A, 2024 source:**

1. Click Shopping campaigns at the top, then Campaigns to see the daily budgets. Example: both campaigns at $15 per day as a shared budget. [L0750, 2020-26]
2. Confirm that ROAS has held at 10X or higher for about two weeks, and that negative keywords and product CPCs were adjusted in that period. [L0750, 2020-26]
3. Click the edit icon next to the budget and change the value. Example: $15 to $20. [L0750, 2020-26]
4. Let the new budget run for one to two weeks. [L0750, 2020-26]
5. Then add negative keywords from the search terms, adjust CPCs based on results, and check ROAS again. [L0750, 2020-26]
6. If ROAS is still at least 10X, increase the budget again and repeat the loop. [L0750, 2020-26]

- Example: after two weeks at a 15X return, with negative keywords and CPCs already adjusted, a $15 daily budget can be raised to $20 per day. [L0750, 2020-26]

**Procedure B, 2025 source:**

1. Check ROAS over two weeks to a month. [L0972, 2025]
2. Confirm ROAS is consistently above 10X. [L0972, 2025]
3. Raise the daily budget modestly, such as from $30 to $40 or $50. [L0972, 2025]
4. Watch ROAS for the next period before raising again. [L0972, 2025]
5. If you raised the budget too far, lower it back right away. [L0972, 2025]

### Scaling a large catalogue on a limited budget

**Procedure (2023):** the two sources below describe the same method and are merged here. [L0934, 2023]

1. Use historical sales data, such as about three years, to identify the top 1 to 500 products out of 5,000. [L0934, 2023]
2. Place those products into a Google Shopping campaign. [L0934, 2023]
3. Optimize their product pages first. [L0934, 2023]
4. Keep the remaining products on free organic traffic only. [L0934, 2023]
5. When the winners take off, optimize the ads further, raise spend, and add more products gradually. [L0934, 2023]

### Product-level focus and Shopping actions

- Shopping action items: [L0510, 2018]
  1. Place customer search terms at the front of Shopping product titles.
  2. Run Facebook dynamic product ads for visitors who viewed products.
  3. Add an incentive opt-in on the site to capture emails and earn the email revenue lift.
  4. Review the numbers regularly: quality score, CTR, CPC, conversion rate, and ROAS.
- A strong brand can stay in one campaign while the other brands get dedicated budgets. Separating the strong brand's ads from the rest protects its performance. ** [L0853, 2021]

### Ceiling of Google Shopping

- Profitable Google Shopping traffic is limited. The ceiling varies with niche, product, brand, and market. In the lesson's examples, it ranged from tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands of dollars in sales per month. ** [L0744, 2021-22]
- Narrow niches have smaller search pools and a lower Google Shopping ceiling. A seller of beginner-only products hits the limit much sooner than one covering all skill levels. ** [L0744, 2021-22]

### Adding other channels

**When to start a channel**

- Start Google ads only after the first two suppliers are approved and have their products uploaded. Keep recruiting suppliers and uploading products while the ads run. [L0972, 2025]
- Build and profit from one channel before starting the next. [L1121, 2022]
- Add other channels such as SEO, social, content, and email once paid search runs profitably, since they tend to perform better on top of that base. [L0463, 2020-26]
- A profitable Google Shopping campaign can be a retargeting channel. Run it for a while before judging, since it can pick up the slack in profit. [L0950, 2023]

**Bing**

- Early-phase split (2024): focus most of the budget on Google, for example $500 of $1,000. Move $100 to $150 into Bing and reserve about $100 for Meta, growing Bing later. [L0856, 2024]
- Earlier rule (2019 **): Bing Shopping ads should start only after Google Shopping product listing ads are profitable and tuned. Starting Bing early will rarely double income. [L1275, 2019]
- Apply both: the 2024 split works for a small early budget, while the 2019 rule governs when Bing shopping should start. Bing is never a substitute for a tuned Google Shopping campaign. [L0856, 2024; L1275, 2019]

**Six-month sequence (2024)**

1. Before October is out, confirm the store's numbers and the exact minimum ROAS. [L1203, 2016]
2. In months two and three, roll out product listing ads, search text ads on PLA terms, and search retargeting (the three-pack), plus retargeting for product listing ads. [L1203, 2016]
3. In month four, launch dynamic product ads on Facebook. [L1203, 2016]
4. In month five, start cold lookalike audiences that feed Facebook pre-sales engagement content, which takes time to build. [L1203, 2016]
5. Roll out each piece in sequence, not everything at once. [L1203, 2016]

### Management time and handover

- Plan the owner's or manager's time to grow with the budget. The workload is lightest during the initial setup and learning phase. [L0463, 2020-26]
- Grow a paid search channel in this order: [L0463, 2020-26]
  1. While spend is small, invest your own hours to set up campaigns, learn the mechanics, and understand the account.
  2. Prove that the campaigns are viable and profitable.
  3. Once viability is proven, hand day-to-day management to a professional, or bring in a consultant for targeted review.
  4. With paid search running profitably, add other channels such as SEO, social, content, and email.
- A campaign profitable at $500 per month can realistically be scaled to $3,000 per month profitably with ongoing work. [L0463, 2020-26]
