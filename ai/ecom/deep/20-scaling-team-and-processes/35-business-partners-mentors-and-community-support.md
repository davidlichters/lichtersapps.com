## Business partners, mentors and community support

### Deciding whether to take a business partner

- Avoid business partnerships with friends or family members. The exception is a family member who performs specialized contracted services, such as graphic design or web design, on a project basis. That is acceptable. [L0276, 2024]
- Partnerships are hard to keep working. Write a contract before any work starts that defines each partner's responsibilities, and include clauses for buyout or removal if a partner does not deliver. *Older (2021).* [L1279, 2021]
- Avoid managing partners and equity partnerships early. Roles and effort often become unbalanced once a store takes off, and partners can fall out. Build the store from zero to one with your own effort first, then outsource the day-to-day work. *Older (2021).* [L1090, 2021]
- The partnership model can be repeated several times with different people, creating multiple extra income streams. Overseeing 5 to 10 or more stores this way avoids working 8 to 12 hours a day running each one yourself. [L1125, 2020-26]
- Sources differ in emphasis. The caution against early equity partnerships applies to your first store. The repeatable partnership model applies when you expand beyond one store. [L1090, 2021; L1125, 2020-26]
- Partnering to run several stores at once is a higher-risk expansion than building one store alone. It offers a larger payoff if it succeeds, but the outcome is uncertain. [L0826, 2016]
- Two people working as a team who find synergy tend to perform very well and move faster than solo operators. [L0780, 2024+]
- Choose business partners, suppliers and collaborators as deliberately as you would choose close friends. They shape the quality of your daily work and the time you have left for it. [L1343, undated]

### Choosing partners

- When choosing partners to run stores, prioritize people who have completed proper training, have already made a first sale, and are fully committed. If the program requires it, also check that they are willing to relocate to the operating location. [L0826, 2016]
- Surround yourself with people who are more talented than you in their areas, since your output tends to match the quality of the people and inputs around you. If you are the most talented person in your group, look for a group where you can learn more. [L1343, undated]

### Defining roles and decision rights

- Define each partner's role in writing so that responsibilities do not overlap or fall through the gaps. [L0829, 2019]
- Define roles with this procedure:
  1. List what each partner is good at and what each is not good at.
  2. Assign ownership accordingly.
  3. Use personality type, such as introvert or extrovert, to decide who handles outreach and who handles ads and back-end work. [L0832, 2019]
- Give each partner their own domain and the freedom to decide within it, so they do not need to approve every small choice. Escalate only decisions that affect the shared plan or money. [L0832, 2019]
- Partners do not need to report every routine action, such as a refund, to each other. Escalate only significant decisions and changes. [L0832, 2019]
- Partners in a small business should be willing to take on tasks outside their formal role when needed. [L0829, 2019]
- Share the workload and the stress of the business equally between partners instead of letting one person carry it. [L0829, 2019]
- Schedule important business conversations at a set time on a workday, not in social or family settings. Before the conversation, prepare the proposed solution, the reasons for it, and its pros and cons so a decision can be made quickly. [L0832, 2019]

### Working with family members

- Give family members clear rules and a written job description before they start working, the same as any other hire. Family work benefits from explicit expectations. [L0832, 2019]
- Working with family can work well, but set the rules first and treat the arrangement as a business relationship. Avoid vague arrangements that make it hard to ask for results. [L0832, 2019]
- The exception for contracted specialized services is covered under "Deciding whether to take a business partner." [L0276, 2024]

### Coaching and mentor programs

- Growth in dropshipping depends on three resources: a coach, virtual assistants, and continuous education. Plan for all three as the business scales. [L0345, 2018]
- An outside coach who audits the whole store and delivers a prioritized task list can produce a quick sales lift. In one case, nearly $6,000 more in monthly sales came after the first coaching call. [L1437, 2018]
- A coach's advice is that learning is best when you focus on one approach and avoid switching between too many tools. Once a process works, keep it. [L0964, 2023]

**Mentor programs**

- Join the Mentor Program by logging into the members area, clicking "Get Help," then selecting "Private Mentorship." No application is required. Book weekly 30-minute one-on-one video calls with mentors who earn a minimum of $100,000 per month. *Older (2022).* [L0249, 2022]
- Choose mentors by specialty: paid traffic (Google Shopping, Facebook), conversion optimization, supplier relationships, exclusivity negotiation, pricing terms, or web design. Select based on your specific needs that week. *Older (2022).* [L0249, 2022]
- A weekly 30-minute accountability call with the mentor is worth it even when there is little to discuss, because it keeps progress on track. Pooling unused call credits across months is not practical with current booking systems. *Older (2022).* [L0429, 2022]

**Monthly coaching**

- Monthly coaching accountability includes defining specific action items for the next 30 days and reviewing completion status, obstacles, and reasons for incomplete items at the next call. *Older (2021-2022).* [L0035, 2021-22]
- In the Master Plan 12-month structure, each client selects when to schedule their monthly coaching session within the upcoming month, rather than being assigned fixed dates. *Older (2021-2022).* [L0035, 2021-22]
- Members can work with different coaches from the the source team for individual sessions, including founder John, coach Ben, and other available coaches. *Older (2021-2022).* [L0035, 2021-22]

**one source mentoring**

- Mentors usually answer student questions within one hour, or within 48 hours when they are traveling. Send questions in advance through a form before group calls so the mentor can prepare answers. [L1017, undated]
- One call per week is the standard during the build phase, and calls can be spaced further apart depending on progress. Several calls per week are only useful in the scaling phase, when ads are live, sales are coming in, and Facebook campaign decisions are needed. [L1017, undated]

**one source support structure**

- Effective program support combines three layers: a personal pod with a coach and a Client Success Manager, a shared community channel for general questions, and scheduled live Q&A calls. Together they make sure a participant always has a clear route to get help. [L1482, 2026]
- In a personal pod, each participant gets a dedicated coach with real operating experience at six-figure revenue, plus a Client Success Manager (CSM) whose job is to keep the participant on track with their plan. The CSM role is about follow-through and accountability, not only answering questions. [L1482, 2026]
- A weekly live group call is a working session where students bring their numbers, ads, product pages and stuck points, and the coach reviews them in real time. The purpose is to unblock students each week, not to sell. [L1452, 2026]
- Combining one-on-one guidance, group knowledge and live call support gives you both personalized answers for your business and the benefit of collective experience. This combination shows you the next concrete step to take. [L1482, 2026]
- Revenue-based tiers in the one source community. Members move up when they hit the next milestone, and each move assigns them a coach who specializes in that revenue stage. [L1323, 2026]

- Do not hold back questions because you fear they are too basic or that you will speak up in a group. Asking early is what gets problems solved, and the team can only help with struggles that are reported. [L1482, 2026]
- Consuming all the material without asking questions hides real problems, because the support team cannot see that you are stuck. Raise struggles in the pod, the community or the calls as soon as they appear. [L1482, 2026]

### Booking calls

**Booking a one-to-one coaching call**

*Older (2021-2022).* Follow these steps:

1. Open the booking link for coaching sessions. It is the same link for every call, and it is also sent in your confirmation email.
2. Look at the calendar. Days with available slots are highlighted in blue. Click one.
3. Check the time zone shown above the times. It is detected automatically; if you will be in another time zone, open the time zone dropdown and choose a different one.
4. In the panel to the right of the calendar, select a time slot.
5. Click the blue Confirm button.
6. Enter your Name and Email. Both are required.
7. Optional: write something in the field "Please share anything that will help prepare for our meeting."
8. Optional: enter your mobile number to receive SMS reminders. The country code is preselected based on your location.
9. Click Schedule Event.

[L0362, 2021-22]

- The one-to-one booking link is listed in the resources section below the course video and is also sent in the booking confirmation email. The same link is used for every one-to-one call, so it does not need to be requested again. [L0362, 2021-22]
- A booked coaching call lasts one hour and is listed as "1 hr" on the booking page. [L0362, 2021-22]
- Coaching calls are held on Zoom. The Zoom link is provided only after booking is confirmed and is sent in the meeting confirmation email. [L0362, 2021-22]
- Available slots are offered in 30-minute increments. Every slot shown can be booked directly, with no need to ask the coach beforehand. [L0362, 2021-22]
- The booking page is connected to all of the coach's calendars, so only times the coach has marked as available are shown. If an expected time does not appear, it is already booked and cannot be selected. [L0362, 2021-22]
- Time zone: the booking page detects your time zone automatically and shows times in it. To change it, use the time zone dropdown, which has a search field. This is useful when traveling or when booking for a future time zone. The dropdown also offers an am/pm or 24h time format toggle. [L0362, 2021-22]
- Name and email are mandatory on the booking form. The email address is needed to send the meeting confirmation with the call link, and the booking cannot be completed without it. [L0362, 2021-22]
- The preparation field is optional. A pre-coaching survey is sent afterward, asking which topics to cover and what to share about the business, so the free-text field does not have to be filled in. [L0362, 2021-22]
- SMS reminders are optional. Enter the mobile number in the "Send text messages to" field. The country code loads automatically based on your location and can be changed. [L0362, 2021-22]
- The form has an optional "Add Guests" field below the preparation box. It is not required to complete the booking. [L0362, 2021-22]
- After Schedule Event, the call is added to the coach's calendar and to your own calendar. The confirmation email then contains the booking link and all details needed to join at the booked time. [L0362, 2021-22]
- After booking is confirmed, the confirmation page tells you to join the call with the Zoom link from your booking confirmation or calendar entry. The page also shows the booked time with the time zone name, so you can check it before the call. [L0362, 2021-22]

**Booking mentor and coaching calls**

- Book your own coaching calls through the members area: go to Blueprint, hover over "Activate," click "Activate Coaching," then select available times. You choose when to schedule; calls are not pre-assigned by the company. *Older (2022).* [L0272, 2022]
- Call lengths differ by program. one source's one-to-one coaching calls last one hour [L0362, 2021-22]. one source's mentor calls are 30 minutes each [L0249, 2022]. Check the length shown when you book.

### Peer networks and masterminds

- Work with your community instead of alone. Talk to others in the same position, learn from their mistakes, and elevate together. Solo work is significantly harder and less effective. [L0037, 2026]
- From the start, talk with peers about campaigns and goals through Google Ads support, Quora, Facebook groups, private forums and masterminds. [L0463, 2020-26]
- Get advice from other ecommerce entrepreneurs without needing to share your store. Peer networks help you learn the same tools and logic and can lead to partnerships. [L0780, 2024+]
- A mastermind group should be a small private group of 2 to 5 people, ideally owners who each spend less than $1,000 per month on Google Ads. Members share information and work together on scaling profitable campaigns. [L0463, 2020-26]
- Online marketing is competitive. A supportive group of peers makes the workload feel easier and helps keep momentum during scaling. [L0463, 2020-26]
- Joining a community of established store owners is a practical way to get a second set of eyes on a store, such as a peer review of the homepage and policy pages. [L0855, 2023]
- Brand ambassador community: build it through a private Facebook group, with reasons to join such as brand appeal, a social cause, and rewards. Aim to grow it to about 20,000 members, with about 3,000 reached by the time of the lesson. [L0790, 2017]

### Community resources and membership rules

**Discord and live calls**

- Use Discord community channels and monthly coaching calls to stay engaged, ask questions, and maintain accountability while executing your scaling plan. [L0195, undated]
- one source live group coaching calls are held on the first Wednesday of each month at 2 PM EST, hosted on Discord. Attendance is included in the membership as a bonus. [L1070, undated]
- All live coaching calls are recorded. Replays of the previous three months are available, so missed calls can be watched within that window. [L1070, undated]
- one source live Q&A calls take place every Thursday at 8 p.m. Singapore Time (SGT) in the Voice channel of the Discord community. [L1482, 2026]
- Weekly one source Q&A calls cover common roadblocks, advanced strategies to scale faster, and live reviews of stores, ads and funnels. You can ask anything in real time and receive direct feedback from the mentors. [L1482, 2026]
- Sources differ on schedule because they cover different programs. one source's live calls run monthly on the first Wednesday [L1070, undated]. one source Q&A runs weekly on Thursdays [L1482, 2026]. Follow the schedule of the program you belong to.

**Where to ask what**

- one source uses Discord as the community hub. The general community channel holds shared insights, updates and wins, and the Voice channel hosts the live Q&A calls. If the general channel is not visible after joining, report it to support right away. [L1482, 2026]
- Take personal and detailed questions about your own business to your small pod group, because that is where you receive feedback specific to your situation. [L1482, 2026]
- Post broad questions, such as what is currently working in ads or tips for choosing suppliers, in the main community channel. There you can learn from the combined experience of many operators, not only from the coach. [L1482, 2026]
- The shared community draws on the experiences of more than 100 other operators, so you can learn from many peers and not only from your assigned coach. This is most useful for broad, current questions. [L1482, 2026]
- Use the chat to share community wins rather than reposting questions. Conversations that include wins are encouraged in the share-your-wins channel. [L0964, 2023]
- Check Discord channel retention times in the Welcome channel. The social and for-sale channels clear their posts, while the module channels are kept. [L0964, 2023]
- Use the members area Bonuses section to find past coaching call replays and the links mentioned in them. [L0964, 2023]

**100K club eligibility**

- Shopify's Discord-based 100K club is open only to stores that have already done more than $100,000 in sales. Store owners below that threshold should not expect access yet. [L0430, 2023]
- The 100K club is a peer group on the course's Discord. Stores can apply to join once they have done more than $100,000 in sales. Eligibility is based on sales volume, not on time in business. [L1093, 2023]
- Member community rule: the 100K community is for store owners who have done at least $100,000 in sales. Use it for peer questions alongside coach answers. [L0856, 2024]
- Sources differ on the threshold wording. Two units say "more than $100,000" [L0430, 2023; L1093, 2023], and one says "at least $100,000" [L0856, 2024]. Confirm the current rule with the community before applying if your sales are exactly at $100,000.

**Onboarding**

New member onboarding in an online ecommerce community:

1. Create your Shopify store first, using the trial link provided in the community.
2. Join the Discord server and open a private support ticket. This is where support, coaching and progress tracking happen.
3. In the ticket, introduce yourself with your name, your goals, and whether you are completely new or already experienced, so the right helper is assigned.
4. Send a short greeting in the welcome channel to unlock the other channels.
5. Open the course and start with the first module, then work through the modules in order.
