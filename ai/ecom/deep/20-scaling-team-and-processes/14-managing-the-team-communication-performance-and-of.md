## Managing the team: communication, performance and offboarding

### Management framework and the manager's role

- Managing staff is a separate skill set from building a store, so plan to learn it before scaling the team. *Older advice (2022).* [L0803, 2022]
- Outsourcing is not a magic fix. After hiring, your job changes to managing that person, so budget time for management rather than expecting them to run the business alone. [L1515, 2019]
- Outsourcing does not mean being absent. The owner must stay reachable for questions, and a short daily check-in is enough. Communication runs through messaging tools such as Skype and Slack, with a video or verbal catch-up roughly every two weeks. [L1122, 2016]
- Give criticism in a clear, factual and professional way with few emotions, the same way you would to an employee. Personal criticism can make spouses and partners feel attacked, which reduces cooperation. [L0832, 2019]
- Hire A players and remove C and D players from the team. Many owners who believe they have B players in fact have C and D performers, and this is the key to making the team work. *Older advice (2021-22).* [L1334, 2021-22]

**Four management stages**

- Manage each new hire in four stages: onboarding, communication, performance review and rewards. Set clear expectations for each stage. *Older advice (2021-22).* [L1054, 2021]
- The four stages only need to meet basic principles, so you do not have to follow one exact method. Meeting those basic needs at each stage is enough for successful outsourcing. *Older advice (2021-22).* [L1054, 2021]

### Working hours and availability

- Set fixed schedules for all team members so they know when colleagues are available to collaborate. Example: 8 AM Eastern to 12 PM, 2 PM to 6 PM Pacific. Communicate clearly which hours each person works so the team can rely on each other and collaborate in Asana and Slack. [L0263, 2022]
- Flexible working hours are acceptable as long as the assigned tasks get done, for example starting early and leaving later around an appointment. Watch whether the task list is completed rather than how many hours are logged. [L0337, 2022]
- Set business hours for phone and messages. The VA answers calls Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:00. Messages arriving outside those hours are handled the next business day, which prevents the owner from working around the clock. [L1473, 2019]
- Time zones with Pakistan-based assistants: Switzerland is about 3 hours ahead of Pakistan, and the UK is about 4 to 8 hours ahead, so plan overlapping working hours accordingly. [L1432, undated]
- How the hour-related sources fit together: fixed schedules ([L0263, 2022]) define when colleagues can collaborate, and flexibility ([L0337, 2022]) applies to the rest of the time as long as the tasks are done. The sources do not describe a conflict between them. [L0263, 2022; L0337, 2022]

### Reply windows and contact rules

**Why reply windows matter** **

- Letting staff call you whenever they want interrupts your focus in the same way that unmanaged customer calls do. Replacing one constant interruption with another gains nothing. [L1054, 2021]
- Answering questions the moment they arrive breaks your workflow and lowers productivity. Set the reply habit from day one, because a habit of constant availability is hard to reverse later. [L1054, 2021]
- Most questions from virtual assistants do not need an instant answer, and about 80% can wait for your scheduled reply times. [L1054, 2021]

**Setting the windows**

| Setting | Rule | When it applies | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| One to two fixed times per day | Set the times when you answer questions, and make clear that replies will not be immediate. *Older (2021-22).* [L1054, 2021] | General rule for any team | one source |
| Start and end of the day, or middle and end | Agree on the windows during the end-of-onboarding call and keep them consistent from the first day. *Older (2021-22).* [L1054, 2021] | Setting the windows during onboarding | one source |
| Phone Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:00 | Calls are answered in these hours. Messages outside them are handled the next business day. [L1473, 2019] | Business hours for phone and messages | one source |
| Short daily check-in | A short daily check-in is enough for the owner to stay reachable. [L1122, 2016] | Daily contact | one source |

**Emergency exception**

- Allow immediate contact only in real emergencies, such as a family emergency that stops a key staff member, like the main customer service contact, from working. For everything else, most questions from assistants can wait for your scheduled reply times. *Older (2021-22).* [L1054, 2021]
- Only a real emergency, such as a key staff member being unable to work, justifies immediate contact. *Older (2021-22).* [L1054, 2021]

**Channels and group chat**

- Communicate mainly through written messages using tools such as Skype or Slack, with WhatsApp as a backup. Make sure the whole team can reach each other. *Older (2021-22).* [L1054, 2021]
- Day-to-day communication with a VA or freelancer should be about 80% written messages, not phone or video calls. Reserve calls for rare cases because they interrupt work and take more time. *Older (2021-22).* [L1055, 2021]
- Create one group chat for the whole team in Skype or Slack. Staff, especially in the Philippines, value feeling part of a team, so the group chat builds connection between people who work for you. *Older (2021-22).* [L1054, 2021]

**Reply template** **

The following message is a template for telling the team when you will reply. Use it unchanged or adapt the time to your own reply window.

```
Good morning everyone, I hope you are all doing well and have a great start to the day. I will check messages again around 3 pm today, so please send anything urgent before then. Have a good day.
```

[L1054, 2021]

### Team culture and communication between members

- Encourage team members to communicate with each other, not only with the owner. This makes the group feel like a team instead of a set of people who each receive a few tasks per week. [L0413, 2016]
- Build a team culture where members propose unconventional ideas daily. Instead of saying no automatically, evaluate each idea based on its risk and potential downside. If the risk is low, test the idea and measure its impact on the brand. This leadership behavior keeps the team ahead of trends and enables genuine innovation. [L0260, 2025-26]
- When staff or contractors suggest ways to make a repeated task more efficient, take the suggestion seriously instead of shutting it down. Ignoring such input keeps inefficiencies in place and discourages people from improving the work. [L1039, 2019+]
- Tell the team that mistakes are acceptable if they are reported honestly and owned. Respond to errors by simplifying the process so the same mistake is less likely, rather than by getting angry. [L1437, 2018]
- Including freelancers in team meetings gives them a sense of belonging and makes them feel part of a business worth being proud of. This loyalty helps the business in return. [L1000, 2017]

### Daily routine

**Daily VA routine**

1. The VA signs in each morning and posts a good-morning message stating availability and the day's plan. [L0921, 2022]
2. The owner reviews the plan and gives direction. [L0921, 2022]
3. Both sides keep a back-and-forth conversation during the day, and the owner asks promptly what the VA is working on if no reply comes. [L0921, 2022]
4. The owner asks about the VA's day to build the relationship and keep them engaged. [L0921, 2022]

**Daily communication and trust**

- Communicate with virtual assistants every day, for example through Slack. Once trust is established, give them authority to decide and fix problems without asking first, and review their decisions at the end of the month. [L1441, 2018]

### Meetings and team rhythm

| Meeting | Frequency and format | When it applies | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Team meeting | Twice a week, Monday and Thursday mornings. *Older (2019).* [L0356, 2019] | Small team of assistants | one source |
| Team calls | At least two per week to answer questions, give new tasks and discuss ideas. Experienced assistants can suggest ideas from their past clients. [L1431, 2020+] | Team calls for questions, tasks and ideas | one source |
| Weekly target meeting | Once a week, covering company and individual targets. *Older (2020).* [L1111, 2020] | Reviewing progress on goals | one source |
| Monthly team meeting | Once a month, for example on a Friday afternoon, once you have five or more people. Greet each other, congratulate the group, share upcoming opportunities and ask for feedback, not to criticize. *Older (2021-22).* [L1054, 2021] | Team of five or more | one source |
| Monthly Kaizen meeting | Once a month, with the owner, all full-time assistants and all freelancers, including accounting and graphics contractors. [L1000, 2017] | Continuous improvement for the whole team | one source |

The sources do not tie the meeting frequencies to one team size. Use the twice-weekly and two-calls-per-week rhythms for a working team of assistants, and add the monthly team meeting and monthly Kaizen once the team grows and includes freelancers. [L0356, 2019; L1431, 2020+; L1054, 2021; L1000, 2017]

**Team meeting procedure** **

1. Hold a team meeting twice a week, on Monday and Thursday mornings. [L0356, 2019]
2. Appoint one assistant as team lead who attends every meeting. [L0356, 2019]
3. Check in with each assistant by call several times a week and review the current state of the business with them. [L0356, 2019]
4. Treat each assistant as a regular member of the company so they feel part of the team. [L0356, 2019]

**Monthly Kaizen procedure**

1. Hold the meeting once a month with the owner, all full-time assistants and all freelancers, including accounting and graphics contractors. [L1000, 2017]
2. Ask each person what is working, what is not working and what should be done to fix it. [L1000, 2017]
3. Turn the answers into actions for the business. [L1000, 2017]

### Reporting structures

- Require daily reporting from direct reports (your direct employees). Use Asana, a doc file, or written bullet points. Any system is fine, but check it consistently. If you don't check reports, people stop trying. Review, verify, and give feedback on progress so people stay accountable and improve. [L0263, 2022]
- Create different reporting structures by role. Each role needs metrics that drive behavior and visibility. [L0263, 2022]

| Role | Reporting | Metrics |
|---|---|---|
| Sales team | Daily performance reports, incentive-based compensation | Daily performance [L0263, 2022] |
| Product listing | Monthly or quarterly metrics | Products listed, product options improved [L0263, 2022] |
| Operations | Order processing metrics, task completion in Asana | Order processing, task completion [L0263, 2022] |

**Asana order reporting procedure** (Asana Reports, paid version, $10 per user)

1. Create a task for every order. Task title = order number + customer name/email. [L0263, 2022]
2. A single team member owns each order from placement through delivery. [L0263, 2022]
3. Others collaborate via task comments (shipping updates, supplier issues, etc.). [L0263, 2022]
4. The Reports tab automatically shows completed orders (last 4 weeks, 7 days, yesterday), open tasks per person, and can be filtered by individual. [L0263, 2022]
5. Show team members their completed tasks and open task count to keep them motivated and aware. [L0263, 2022]
6. Show the operations manager only the Upcoming tasks next 8 weeks view, hidden from the team, to see who is overloaded and needs help. [L0263, 2022]

### KPIs, OKRs and targets

- Each team member needs a clear mission, company goals and a personal KPI. Review progress in a weekly meeting covering both company and individual targets. *Older advice (2020).* [L1111, 2020]
- Set OKRs (Objectives and Key Results) for 6 to 12 month periods. Conduct check-ins and reshape them every 6 months based on progress. [L0260, 2025-26]
- OKRs align your team to the business vision and leadership goals while creating transparency across the organization about priorities. [L0260, 2025-26]
- Plan staff tasks three to six months ahead by setting aside a full working day for the planning session. Load each person's weekly targets through the end of the year so they know their work well in advance. Planning this far out reduces daily decisions about what staff should do next. *Older advice (2021-22).* [L1396, 2021-22]

**When to set output targets**

| Work | Target rule | When it applies | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product listing | Do not set output targets at first, because complexity varies by brand. With only one lister you cannot benchmark. With 2-3 listers on similar brands, compare their output to set realistic standards. Use your own time as a baseline: a specialist should do the job faster because listing is their only focus. [L0263, 2022] | New listing team, no benchmark yet | one source |
| Content | Set clear weekly targets, such as twelve content items per week. Only follow up when weekly targets are missed. *Older (2021-22).* [L1396, 2021-22] | Content work with a clear weekly count | one source |
| Social media manager | Measure by engagement with posts and by collaboration with suppliers and niche partners, not by daily post count or follower target alone. *Older (2021).* [L0801, 2021] | Social media roles | one source |

### Measuring performance

**Measure results, not hours**

- Monitor virtual assistant performance by output (all customer emails answered, all tasks completed) rather than hours worked. [L0230, 2019]
- Measure the VA by results rather than hours: as long as all emails, messages and calls are handled, the exact working hours and breaks are not tracked. Downtime can be filled with assigned tasks in Asana. [L1473, 2019]
- Manage remote team members by results: check whether the task is done, not by screenshots or time tracking. If output is repeatedly late, first check whether the training or instructions were unclear before concluding the person is not right for the role. [L0337, 2022]
- Heavy time-tracking software such as Time Doctor did not work well with part-time staff, who often stopped using it or left. Daily reports and detailed tracking created friction without improving results. Measure weekly output instead of tracking hours. *Older (2021-22).* [L1396, 2021-22]
- Measure staff by weekly results instead of the process they use. If the agreed number of content items or uploads is finished by the end of the week, the method does not matter. Only follow up when weekly targets are missed. *Older (2021-22).* [L1396, 2021-22]

**Judging speed and effort**

- Before judging a remote helper's speed, estimate how long the task takes you to do well, including photos and details. If a task that takes you about one hour is still unfinished after half a day, the cause is usually unclear training or a misunderstanding, not necessarily laziness. Check the instructions and the training video first, then decide. [L0337, 2022]
- A staff member who reported ten or twelve hours for a task that produced only a few items in a week showed poor time estimates. Ask for an explanation of time spent and consider replacing staff whose output does not match their reported effort. Clear expectations at the start prevent this. *Older (2021-22).* [L1396, 2021-22]

**Detail orientation**

- Repeatedly missing details, such as missing tags or meta descriptions in product listings, is a sign of low detail orientation. After several repeated misses despite clear instructions, replace the team member. [L0337, 2022]

### Reviews and performance improvement plans

**Review cadence**

- Hold regular review catch-ups with each team member to review their work, raise improvement points, recognize good work, cover changes to systems or processes and let them suggest improvements. *Older (2021-22).* [L1054, 2021]
- Review frequency: start with a review about every two weeks, then move to monthly, and once a person is long-term you can reduce it to quarterly. A half-hour meeting is usually enough. *Older (2021-22).* [L1054, 2021]
- Set regular review dates with each team member about once per month, as the default cadence. *Older (2021-22).* [L1054, 2021]

**Reviewing work procedure** **

1. Set regular review dates with each team member individually, about once per month, and put them in your calendar and theirs. [L1054, 2021]
2. If you have a larger team, also set a time for team meetings. These are more general and cover reviewing work and seeking feedback on processes and opportunities. [L1054, 2021]
3. Stay positive and focus on developing people, not punishing them. [L1054, 2021]
4. Where performance is below standard, ask why and what the individual needs to improve, then give them time to improve. [L1054, 2021]
5. If improvement is not visible, end the arrangement. [L1054, 2021]

**Individual review meeting procedure** **

- First catch up on the person and their life, such as their family, studies or other commitments. Then go through a prepared agenda of points to discuss, and if they are doing well, say so clearly and ask for any further improvement suggestions. [L1054, 2021]

**Performance improvement procedure** **

1. At the end-of-month performance meeting, identify one aspect of the work to improve. [L1054, 2021]
2. Agree on a plan together by asking what they need to make the improvement happen. [L1054, 2021]
3. Set a target, such as improving that area by the end of the next month, and confirm they understand and accept it. [L1054, 2021]
4. Give them the time to improve. For a high-impact task where poor performance cannot last, use a shorter period. [L1054, 2021]
5. If improvement is not visible, end the arrangement. [L1054, 2021]

**Improvement window**

| Situation | Window | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Default | One month | The person does not have to meet every target, but they must show substantial improvement against what was set. Whether they did enough is a judgment call. [L1054, 2021] | one source |
| High-impact task | Shorter than one month | Poor performance cannot last on this task. [L1054, 2021] | one source |

### Monitoring and tracking

**How the monitoring units fit together**

| Stage | Monitoring approach | When it applies | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| New hire, first days | Track calls, chats and response times from day one. Record calls where legal and monitor them closely at the start. *Older (2019) for the tracking point.* [L0467, 2019; L0910, 2015] | Start of employment | one source and one source |
| Uncertain about a VA or new to working together | Use time monitoring. It is not recommended as a long-term measure because it can damage trust. [L0921, 2022] | Unsure about a VA or someone new | one source |
| After the hiring process or once training is complete | Give full trust and avoid monitoring screens. Trust remains unless they give you a clear reason to withdraw it. [L1000, 2017; L0910, 2015] | Passed hiring or completed training | one source |
| Warning signs appear | Use monitoring software and fix problems early. [L1441, 2018] | Excuses, slipping attendance, leaving early | one source |

Judge monitoring by the stage. Output and results remain the main measure throughout. [L0230, 2019; L0337, 2022; L1473, 2019]

**Recording calls**

- Staff who answer calls should be monitored closely at the start by recording calls where legal, then trusted once training is complete. Customer complaints that reach the store owner are a signal to act if performance drops. [L0910, 2015]

**Hubstaff**

- Hubstaff tracks virtual assistant work with periodic screenshots, an activity percentage and hours worked per day. Use the data to see how long tasks take, check activity levels, and reprioritize workload. [L1441, 2018]

**Asana visibility**

- Asana reporting shows team members their completed tasks and open task count, and shows the operations manager the upcoming task view that stays hidden from the team. [L0263, 2022]

**Flexible hours**

- For flexible hours, watch whether the task list is completed rather than how many hours are logged. [L0337, 2022]

### Absences, sickness, vacations and outages

| Situation | What to do | Source |
|---|---|---|
| VA is sick | The owner covers the work and the VA simply reports the absence. [L1473, 2019] The owner covers responsibilities to maintain service continuity. [L0230, 2019] | one source |
| Planned vacation | The VA gives notice about two weeks in advance. The owner covers those days too, so no replacement hire is needed. [L1473, 2019] The owner covers responsibilities to maintain service continuity. [L0230, 2019] | one source |
| Not present as expected | End the engagement and hire a replacement, applying a firm zero-tolerance policy. *Older (2021).* [L0469, 2021] | one source |
| Pattern of absences | Freelance virtual assistants often work for several companies at once, which can cause absences or fatigue. Consider this possibility when assessing a pattern. *Older (2021).* [L0469, 2021] | one source |
| Power or internet outage | Require the team member to alert the team from their phone. Short outages of about 15 minutes to an hour can be tolerated when the alert is sent. *Older (2021).* [L0469, 2021] | one source |

Note on the sources: a reported absence (sick or planned) is covered by the owner under the current rules [L1473, 2019; L0230, 2019]. The zero-tolerance rule for an absence that is not present as expected comes from an older source (2021) [L0469, 2021]. Consider the pattern rule before acting [L0469, 2021].

**Written working hours**

- Give each virtual assistant written working hours and duties. If they are not present as expected, end the engagement and hire a replacement, applying a firm zero-tolerance policy. *Older (2021).* [L0469, 2021]
