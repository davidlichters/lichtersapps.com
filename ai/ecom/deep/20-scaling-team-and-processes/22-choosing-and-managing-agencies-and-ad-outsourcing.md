## Choosing and managing agencies and ad outsourcing

### Default position: keep paid ads in-house first

- Keep hands-on control of ad setup and budgets in-house. Outsourcing ad work makes sense only once you are truly proficient in the ad platforms, so you know what to hire for and what results are possible. [L0359, 2020]
- Do not hire agencies for SEO, paid media or content. Agencies optimize for client retention and their own metrics, not for your niche and growth. Instead, build internal teams, or test carefully with your own money after you have proven you can execute. [L0263, 2022]
- Example: $3,000/month × 6 months = $18,000 spent on an SEO agency with zero results. The agency did not dig into the niche or build a real strategy; it optimized for client retention and billing, not for growth. Lesson: do not outsource core business functions to agencies before you can do them yourself and measure the results. [L0263, 2022]
- Do not outsource ad management or store building before you have a working system. Outside help can maintain and grow a working store, but it cannot build one from zero. [L0468, 2020]
- Do not outsource ad management until you have set up and proven profitable campaigns yourself. An agency that cannot deliver conversions at the start is a bad sign. [L0664, 2020]
- Run paid ads yourself and reach profitability for one to three months before outsourcing marketing. This gives you a baseline to judge an agency. Going to a marketing firm with a new store and no results usually fails. [L0467, 2019]
- Do not increase ad spend because Pinterest or Google account representatives push for bigger budgets. Their pressure does not lead to more sales. [L0671, 2021]
- Keep the management of Google and Facebook campaigns in-house rather than outsourcing it to a third-party app or agency. This was the presenter's approach at a spend of hundreds of thousands of dollars per month. ** [L0663, 2019]
- Keep pay-per-click management in-house until you understand it well enough to judge an agency. The owner spent about 20,000 USD on outside pay-per-click agencies without getting better results than doing it himself. [L1122, 2016]
- Keeping ads in-house can work when the know-how is internal to the business. [L1508, 2022+]
- Ignore the advice that a new store should never outsource: the sources below set the bar by results, not by store age. [L0801, 2021] *(Note: this line is a caution, not a rule of the sources; see the conditions in the sections that follow.)*

### Prerequisites: the baseline before you outsource

- Outsource paid ads only after the store has a baseline: it has impressions, clicks, abandoned carts and sales, and it is profitable. Without that baseline, a third party has nothing to beat and performance cannot be judged. [L1283, 2023]
- Outsourcing Google Ads only makes sense when the account is already profitable and the owner does not want to run it. The provider must commit to beating current results within a stated timeframe. Without that baseline, a new agency's underperformance cannot be judged. [L1116, 2025]
- Keep Google Ads in-house until the ad account is established and returns at least 10x ROAS. Only then consider an outside agency, because a new agency does not yet know the business or the traffic system. ** [L1116, 2025]
- Do not outsource Google Ads management until your store is already profitable and you have proven what works. Hire an ads manager for maintenance or scaling only after you have optimized the account yourself and validated the strategy. ** [L0270, 2021]
- Do not outsource paid advertising until your store is profitable and you know what works. First establish baseline performance, prove that the store converts, and build initial momentum yourself. ** [L0272, 2022]
- Outsourcing marketing should wait until the store has been run profitably in-house and results are clear. After that, an agency can be tested on whether it beats those results. [L0360, 2024]
- Prove that paid campaigns are viable before paying someone else to run them. Then outsource, so the owner can focus on other channels. [L0463, 2020-26]
- Outsource Google Ads or social media management only after those campaigns are already profitable, because an agency cannot fix unprofitable campaigns. ** [L1110, 2019]
- Consider a Google Ads agency only after your store is already profitable and you want to accelerate what works. A store that is not yet profitable should be fixed on its own first. ** [L1275, 2019]
- In the early stage, avoid outsourcing your advertising. Consider outsourcing only after you understand what your campaigns should achieve. [L0965, 2024]
- Hire marketing agencies with caution in the early phase. Without enough historic data, an agency can spend heavily without results, so monitor their spend and results closely. ** [L0801, 2021]
- Working with a mentor or a team on Google Ads, Shopping and budget decisions is recommended as a scaling step, not an early test, once the product is proven and branded. [L0577, 2023]
- When you add agencies and staff, fixed monthly costs rise, and profit can shrink before results improve. Judge the return on the agency only after its work has had time to improve results. [L0694, 2024]
- Delegate ad management only once you know what good results look like and growth leaves you without time for it. [L1508, 2022+]

### When outsourcing makes sense

- The main real advantage of an agency is saved time. It makes sense when you cannot find enough hours to manage your ads to the level you want. ** [L1053, 2021-22]
- Outsourcing ads management makes the most sense for tasks you do not enjoy, that you find tedious, and that take time away from other activities that grow the business. ** [L1053, 2021-22]
- Once a brand is established and paid ads take a lot of time, a specialist media-buying agency that manages Google and Facebook campaigns can take over that work. Owners of growing businesses often use this when running several projects at once. [L1251, 2024+]
- When the founder is too busy with paid campaigns to work on other channels such as SEO, outsourcing full PPC management frees time for the rest of the marketing. The stated aim is to scale campaigns so the business can move from five-figure to six- and seven-figure monthly revenue. [L1262, 2015+]
- A one-person business spreads its time across tasks and is more likely to make errors. An agency with specialists can run campaigns such as smart campaigns, Performance Max and retargeting separately. Experienced agencies also know account details that take years to learn. [L1214, 2023+]
- At $500k-1M+ per month, an in-house media buying team should work on at most one or two platforms, such as Facebook and TikTok. Less common platforms such as Pinterest, Snapchat and sometimes Google should go to agencies. [L1129, undated]
- A consultant for a focused review can be worthwhile at small budgets, where outsourcing the whole campaign management may not pay off. The consultant finds wasted spend and redirects it to better-performing areas. [L0463, 2020-26]
- A consultant who provides specific to-do items, setup feedback and campaign Q&A can save thousands of dollars in months of trial and error. The consultant can bring a campaign from a six-month timeline to a two- or three-month timeline. [L0463, 2020-26]
- Outsourcing makes sense only after you understand what your campaigns should achieve. [L0965, 2024]

### When to keep it in-house

- A freelancer or virtual assistant trained by you usually costs less than an agency, and gives you a person who works full time under your direct control. ** [L1053, 2021-22]
- An agency does not automatically outperform the owner at running ads. If you apply a proven high-ticket Google Ads approach yourself, you can get results as good as an agency would deliver. ** [L1053, 2021-22]
- Keep hands-on control of ad setup and budgets in-house. [L0359, 2020]
- Keep pay-per-click management in-house until you understand it well enough to judge an agency. [L1122, 2016]

### Scale-based split between in-house and agency

Two sources give different splits for large spend. Both are shown; apply each as stated.

| Source | Spend level | Split |
|---|---|---|
| one source (undated) | $500k-1M+ per month | In-house media buying on at most one or two platforms, such as Facebook and TikTok; Pinterest, Snapchat and sometimes Google go to agencies. [L1129, undated] |
| one source 2019 | Hundreds of thousands of dollars per month | Google and Facebook managed in-house, not by a third-party app or agency. [L0663, 2019] |

Note: the sources do not define a threshold below $500k per month. The rule in [L1129, undated] applies to that band; the 2019 approach in [L0663, 2019] is the older statement for a comparable spend level.

### Service types and levels of involvement

| Service | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Coaching or consulting | Strategic advice and guidance while the owner keeps running the account |
| Campaign tune-up | Fixing and optimizing an existing campaign setup |
| Campaign audit | Reviewing the account to find problems and gaps |
| Store-specific feedback | Individual review of one e-commerce store's campaigns |
| Full PPC management | Outsourcing all AdWords and paid ad management to the agency |

[L1262, 2015+]

- Before outsourcing paid ads, choose the service level that matches the stage. Start with consulting or an audit for a new or small account, and move to full management when scaling. Lighter options also let the owner learn how the account works before handing over control. [L1262, 2015+]
- PPC agencies may offer several levels of involvement: answering questions about a course or community, private consulting on specific campaigns, or the agency running and scaling the campaigns in collaboration with the owner. Choose the level based on how much control the owner wants to keep and how much time they can spend on the account. [L1262, 2015+]

### Agency vetting

#### Core competencies and specialisation

- Define core competencies upfront. Ask what the agency specializes in, which systems it uses for all clients versus custom setups, and which parts of the growth funnel it covers. Beware agencies claiming expertise in everything. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Choose a PPC specialist who focuses only on paid search, rather than a generalist agency that also offers design, CRO, SEO, content, logos or social media. Google Ads changes frequently, and a team that covers all of digital marketing is less likely to keep up. [L1448, 2020-26]
- When screening a PPC specialist, ask these questions in order: [L1448, 2020-26]
  1. How many hours per week do you actually work in client accounts? Compare the answer with the survey buckets: 40 hours, 10 to 39 hours, 1 to 10 hours, or less than one hour per week.
  2. Do you answer emails promptly? Check this during the screening itself.
  3. Will you schedule a call with me at least once a month?
  4. Can you share your current task list, showing which experiments, ad copy tests and bid optimizations are running?
  5. Do you focus only on paid search, or also on design, CRO, SEO, content, logos or social media?

#### Team and account manager

- The most visible person in agency sales presentations may not manage your account. Clarify who your actual point of contact will be, their experience level, and how long they have worked for the agency. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Verify whether the team assigned to your account is in-house, part-time, or outsourced to contractors. Ask how long each person has worked for the agency and whether they focus only on your account or manage multiple accounts. Big-name salespeople often do not manage accounts directly. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Read Glassdoor employee reviews for the agency to understand team dynamics and leadership behavior. Filter for patterns: whether juniors run client accounts, whether senior staff train or hand off work, and whether staff turnover is high. Employee frustration hints at problems you may inherit. [L0217, 2025-26]
- A large agency typically gives only about 5 hours of actual management time per month to one account. Any agency promising a dedicated staff member working on your account constantly is not telling the truth. ** [L1053, 2021-22]
- Agencies that use heavy software to manage many accounts at scale usually deliver lower quality than one person manually managing your account. Take this into account when comparing them. ** [L1053, 2021-22]

#### Position in the agency's client base

- Verify your position among the agency's client base. If you are their largest client, they pay you high attention but may lose focus if they land a bigger client. If you are small, they may deprioritize your account. [L0217, 2025-26]

#### Evaluating the agency

- Evaluate agencies on three dimensions: output (number of deliverables), integrity (brand alignment and messaging quality), and effectiveness (performance metrics in your ad account). Weight each appropriately when structuring compensation. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Money spent by an agency is not an indicator of quality work; it only shows the volume of clients served. Audit other metrics, speak to current clients, read employee reviews, and test results before hiring. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Request conversations with 2 to 3 current agency clients who have a similar business model and spend level. Case studies are handpicked and controlled. Unfiltered feedback from active clients reveals actual performance, speed of implementation, and communication quality. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Work through this procedure before hiring an agency: [L1053, 2021-22]
  1. Build a short list of agencies.
  2. Talk to each agency and ask about its experience with e-commerce businesses and who its clients are.
  3. Ask whether it has experience with dropshipping businesses and with Google Shopping ads.
  4. Ask the agency to explain how it manages campaigns.
  5. Compare its suggested approach with a proven high-ticket Google Ads approach. If it matches, the agency is reasonably likely to manage your account at an acceptable level.
  6. Ask the agency to review your ad account and suggest what it would change or improve.
- The most reliable way to find a good agency is a recommendation from a trusted e-commerce operator in your network. Someone who had a bad experience will not recommend that agency. ** [L1053, 2021-22]
- To find a good agency or consultant, ask the platform rep for their biggest agency spender, then talk to that client about how they work. Consultancy and teaching services are often offered at a high fee. [L1214, 2023+]
- Show the live ad account when talking to an agency, and ask them to beat the current ROAS. [L1116, 2025]
- Talk to several agencies before choosing one, because results and service quality vary between them. Compare what each one manages and what is included in the fee before you sign. [L1127, 2024]
- Check whether the agency is reachable through a support route. An agency account gives direct support contacts who reply through WhatsApp or Telegram, so response speed is a practical factor when choosing an agency. A personal ad account has no such support route, which makes its loss harder to recover from. [L0351, 2026]

#### Named references

- Search Scientist (searchscientist.com) is a paid-search agency that works best with stores that are already profitable, because it builds on existing sales momentum. Contact it only after the store makes money, since an unprofitable store may be turned away or redirected. ** [L0467, 2019]
- Search Scientists (searchscientists.com) is recommended for Google Ads management. The same source gives the condition that campaigns must already be profitable first. *(Older advice, 2019; note that [L0467, 2019] spells the domain without the "s".)* [L1110, 2019]

#### Screening a PPC specialist: activity and reporting

- Activity is the strongest predictor of success when hiring a PPC manager. Assess how often they actually work in the account, how they communicate, and how strong their technical expertise is. [L1448, 2020-26]
- In a WordStream study, about 90% of PPC account managers said they spend at least one hour per week on an account. In practice, most do not optimize regularly as they claim. [L1448, 2020-26]
- In the same WordStream study, about 20% of account managers (1 in 5) made no changes to campaigns for an entire month. Only about 10% (1 in 10) worked on accounts every week across a full quarter. [L1448, 2020-26]
- Agencies performed slightly better than individual freelancers in the same study. More time spent in an account generally correlates with better results, because ongoing optimization compounds over time. [L1448, 2020-26]
- Check the specialist's activity with this procedure: [L1448, 2020-26]
  1. Check that the specialist answers emails promptly.
  2. Check that they invite you to a call at least once a month.
  3. Check that they share task lists showing current work, such as campaign experiments, ad copy tests and bid optimization.
  4. Check that they report openly on what they changed and why.
  5. Verify their reports instead of trusting them blindly.

### Red flags

- Do not work with an agency that contacts you unsolicited, for example one that targets everyone who opens a new Shopify store. Such agencies usually rely on churning through clients quickly, which signals weak results. Strong agencies often have a waiting list. ** [L1053, 2021-22]
- Ignore unsolicited emails that ask you to book a call with an agency. ** [L1053, 2021-22]
- Ignore cold emails from ad-management agencies. Many find Shopify stores with lookup tools such as builtwith, mass-email them, and churn clients instead of retaining them. [L1116, 2025]
- Some ad agencies find Shopify stores created in the last seven days and cold-email the owners offering ad management. They often sign up many clients without improving results. ** Before engaging such a company, search its name in an owner community group to read the experiences of other store owners. [L1281, 2022]
- Avoid agencies that sell Google Ads management via cold calls and run templated work across hundreds of accounts. A boutique agency that understands your business and manages your account is the better choice. [L1285, 2024]
- Avoid agencies that cold-contact every new Shopify store. Their model is usually high-volume and short-term. [L0965, 2024]
- An agency charging around 200 USD per month will probably do little work on your account, or it will outsource the labour to a low-cost country where people lack significant experience in good ads management. ** [L1053, 2021-22]
- A company that cannot deliver conversions at the start is a bad sign. [L0664, 2020]
- Larger marketing agencies often push a 3-month commitment and assign senior media buyers who may not understand the brand. Check the contract terms and the team that will actually work on the account before signing. [L1129, undated]
- Avoid agencies that require a fixed contract, such as six months of payments regardless of results. Reputable agencies work month to month on a pay-as-you-go basis because they are confident they will deliver. ** [L1053, 2021-22]

### Fees and compensation

Fee figures differ by model and by year. Both values are kept.

| Fee model | Figure | Source | Applies to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Percentage of ad spend | 15% to 25% of total ad spend in a healthy arrangement | one source (undated) [L0463, 2020-26] | Ongoing paid traffic management |
| Percentage example | $500/month spend at 20% fee ≈ $100/month to the manager | one source (undated) [L0463, 2020-26] | Calculating whether the fee buys enough optimization time |
| Fixed monthly fee | $3,000/month for a Facebook and Google ads agency, including about 20 minutes of daily account checks, product and store research and creative scripts | one source (undated) [L1127, 2024] | Full Facebook and Google management |
| Minimum for a good agency | At least 700 USD/month, often considerably more | one source 2021-22, older [L1053, 2021-22] | Agency quality threshold |
| Red-flag fee | Around 200 USD/month | one source 2021-22, older [L1053, 2021-22] | Warning sign of little work or outsourced labour |

- The sources do not say which fee model fits which budget. The only budget-linked figure is the $500/month example, which shows how little a 20% fee buys at small spend. [L0463, 2020-26]
- Use a return test before paying any fee. Extra spend of about $1,000 per month and a ROAS improvement of about 3 points is a reasonable benchmark for a good paid traffic specialist. If that combination cannot roughly double your profit after the fee, the hire is likely not worth it. [L1448, 2020-26]
- Weight the compensation according to the three dimensions: output, integrity and effectiveness. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Before firing a specialist, compare the fee with the results over the same period. Outsourcing raises your break-even point, so the agency or new hire must beat your own prior results from the same period. Compare the agency period with the same months of the previous year. [L1508, 2022+]
- Consultancy and teaching services are often offered at a high fee. [L1214, 2023+]

### Contracts and terms

- Use short-term contracts and require agencies to earn a contract extension by demonstrating results. This maintains accountability and gives both parties clean exit points. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Avoid fixed contracts that lock you in regardless of results. Reputable agencies work month to month. ** [L1053, 2021-22]
- Watch for 3-month commitments and check who will work on the account before signing. [L1129, undated]
- Put the communication protocol in writing before engagement begins. Include channel (Slack, email, phone), weekly meeting frequency, response time expectation (for example, 24 business hours) and decision-making cadence. Example: Monday standup, weekly Slack updates, monthly performance review. Avoid assumptions about availability. [L0217, 2025-26]
- For a PPC agency, use a benchmark of email replies within 2 to 3 hours during business hours. Put this expectation in writing before signing. [L1262, 2015+]
- Communication channels must be established before engagement begins. Include response time expectations, meeting frequency, escalation procedures and backup contacts if the primary person is unavailable. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Define the escalation path and backup contacts in the contract: [L0217, 2025-26]
  1. Primary contact.
  2. Secondary contact (for example, team lead).
  3. Manager.

  Specify what happens when your primary contact is on vacation, sick or leaves the agency. This prevents service gaps and ensures someone owns your account at all times.
- Agree a realistic timeframe and a target uplift, such as 20 percent or 50 percent more ROAS, so results can be measured. [L1116, 2025]

### Onboarding roadmap

- Create a detailed 30-, 60- and 90-day roadmap with the agency before signing. Map specific deliverables to specific dates. Example: Day 1 (campaigns live), Week 2 (categorical search campaigns launched), Week 3 (YouTube testing begins). This prevents vague promises and gives clear weekly checkpoints for evaluation. [L0217, 2025-26]
