### Creative team roles and the human-AI balance

- A traditional creative team with fixed roles (strategist, copywriter, editor, designer) can be replaced by a breakdown into processes and tasks. Each task can be done by a person or an AI agent, as long as the process is predictable and can be written as step-by-step instructions. [L0464, 2025-26]
- Break every team role into processes, then into individual tasks from start to finish. Check which tasks can be automated or handed to AI. Any task that can be described as step-by-step instructions can in principle be completed by AI. [L0464, 2025-26]
- Any task a person can be trained to do can in principle be trained into an AI agent. The AI can run the same task up to 100 times faster than a human. [L0464, 2025-26]
- Projected capability (a forward-looking estimate, not a measured result): once the software and workflow are set up, AI could return creative test projects within about 10 seconds, and a full creative test cycle could be completed in about 20 minutes. [L0464, 2025-26]
- Measured experience: the first AI-produced videos took about one hour each while the creator was learning the tools. Once the workflow is familiar, the same production time drops to about 20 to 30 minutes per video. [L0888, 2023+]
- Some brands can run with one creative team member who oversees a group of AI agents. This works only with a well-defined, reliable workflow and a human who reviews the output before it goes live. [L0464, 2025-26]
- The right balance of humans and AI is a decision for each brand, based on its budget, niche, existing team and required approvals. Work out this balance before choosing tools or team size. [L0464, 2025-26]
- A creative strategist's typical tasks:
  - Analyzing what customers say. [L0464, 2025-26]
  - Identifying likely buyers and their motivations. [L0464, 2025-26]
  - Extracting core ideas for ads. [L0464, 2025-26]
  - Finding ideas and styles not yet tested. [L0464, 2025-26]
  - Running product, competitor and market analysis. [L0464, 2025-26]
  - Briefing concepts for ad assets. [L0464, 2025-26]
  - Reviewing the team's work. [L0464, 2025-26]
- Creative systems can be organized into stages covering conception, planning, production and management of ads. Each stage can use its own AI tools and written SOPs or instructions. [L0464, 2025-26]
- One AI prompt can set creative KPIs and check each team member's launch output per product. This is a simple way to confirm the creative team is delivering against its targets. [L0885, 2025-26]

### Production: batches, images and copy

- Run a small test batch of 2 or 3 images before a full run of 50, because each image and each retry costs credits. Scale up only after the test outputs look right. [L0794, 2026]
- Batch rollout order:
  1. Write one collection page with the prompt. Check the format, style and length against your template. [L0883, 2025-26]
  2. Refine the prompt until the output matches what you want. [L0883, 2025-26]
  3. Run all collection keywords together as an asynchronous batch, for example 20 collections in one run. [L0883, 2025-26]
  4. Write one blog post the same way, and refine the prompt until its format, style and length are right. [L0883, 2025-26]
  5. Run the remaining blog posts with more automation, relying more on the AI. [L0883, 2025-26]
  6. Have a team member or VA edit and fact-check every output. [L0883, 2025-26]
  7. Publish the edited copy to the site. [L0883, 2025-26]
- One batch covered 20 collection pages for one brand in a single asynchronous run. The operator started it, left the computer and came back to finished drafts ready for editing. [L0883, 2025-26]
- AI can take over work that used to require expensive freelancers or employees: drafting supplier approval messages, checking store pages for compliance before ads go live, and converting supplier spreadsheets. This saves weeks or months on large product catalogs. [L1097, 2025]
- Photoshop batch automation for product images : a freelancer found on OnlineJobs.ph can build a folder-based automation that resizes images, places them on a white background if needed, and compresses them. It then runs unattended at about $10 per 100 images. [L1456, 2021-22]
- Outreach automation tools:
  - Lemlist and Instantly are email automation tools for outreach. Import a list with first name, last name and email address, set up a sequence of follow-up emails, and the automation stops as soon as the recipient replies. [L0789, 2022]
  - Instantly allows 25 sending email addresses, each able to send 50 emails per day. Use several sending inboxes to scale outreach volume in a country without overloading one address. [L0789, 2022]

### Limits of AI workflows and creative automation

- Keep a human review step in any AI creative workflow. AI still makes mistakes, such as errors in content or visuals, so the review checks output before ads are published. [L0464, 2025-26]
- Have a human edit and fact-check every AI output before it goes live, even when the prompt is well tuned. The first draft can contain wrong product claims or inaccuracies. [L0883, 2025-26]
- Identify the tasks AI cannot yet do well, such as tools that do not exist for a combination of tasks, or tasks that are hard to explain in writing. For those tasks, find a workaround or keep a human, and use this list to cut costs. [L0464, 2025-26]
- The practical limits of an AI creative workflow are:
  - The number of separate software tools involved; [L0464, 2025-26]
  - The connections needed between platforms; [L0464, 2025-26]
  - whether the workflow is set up reliably enough to give predictable output. [L0464, 2025-26]

  Design the workflow so these three points are solved before adding more volume. [L0464, 2025-26]
- Automation handles rule-based tasks, not exceptions, negotiation or creative decisions. Use the time it frees for those. [L0286, 2018]
- AI agents are unreliable for now, so use AI Workflows with predetermined steps. [L0287, 2025-26]
