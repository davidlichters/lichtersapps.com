### The first hire and the first assistant

#### Timing

- Hire help once a store is live, running ads and making sales, and you need time for product research, ads and offers. Early on, when the owner has limited hours, delegate the most time-consuming tasks first. [L1358, undated]
- There are two entry points for building a team: once a store is live, sales are coming in and the owner needs help to free time; or early on, when the founder lacks the time to run everything and needs help with the most time-consuming tasks. Both lead to the same goal of delegating work so the owner can focus on product research, running ads and creating offers. [L1360, undated]
- Delegation frees time to focus on the highest-value tasks, such as product research, ads and offers. Outsourcing tasks like order fulfillment or customer support early is a common way to achieve this. [L1360, undated]
- Outsource as early as possible once the business is up, running and profitable, meaning it makes money. Hire help, adjust your schedule and free time from day-to-day tasks at that point. [L1447, 2020-26]
- Waiting until you can no longer cope with the workload before outsourcing is a mistake. Once the business is profitable, delegating routine tasks should begin rather than being postponed until you are overwhelmed. [L1447, 2020-26]
- Hiring the first VA earlier rather than later is recommended, since the sales gap after hiring was large. [L1437, 2018]
- At a low volume of high-ticket sales, around 20 sales a month, the owner can handle calls and emails personally, so a virtual assistant is not yet necessary. Add support once revenue and workload clearly justify it. [L1472, 2022]
- Many owners delay hiring a virtual assistant because they feel the business depends on them personally. Delegating only works once the owner accepts that the assistant will be the face of the business and must be trained to represent it. [L1000, 2017]
- Many owners delay hiring because they believe nobody else can run the business and because the project feels overwhelming with no clear starting point. Breaking the work into a system, a hire and training steps makes it manageable. [L1473, 2019]
- Once the early hustle phase is over (about six to twelve months at most), hand off tasks that fall outside your strengths and values. Keep only the work that sits in your sweet spot. [L0701, 2015]
- Founders who personally run websites, supplier contact, ads, email and phone for years end up with slow response times and neglected ad optimization. Build a team and systems within the first few months once sales start to take off. [L0852, 2020] **
- Outsourcing is useful for bringing in experience you lack. For example, a hired customer service specialist with 14 years of experience improved service processes and customer engagement. [L1122, 2016]

#### Hiring a virtual assistant

- To hire a virtual assistant: [L0671, 2021] *(Older advice, 2021; the two units are identical and merged here.)*
  1. Run the store yourself when it goes live and let orders and emails come in.
  2. Learn how to handle each supplier's orders and the most frequently asked customer questions.
  3. Write these steps in a Google Doc as an instruction manual for your business.
  4. Hire the assistant and give them that document.
- Outsourcing means hiring a person to take over tasks that still require human interaction, while tasks that repeat identically are automated. Early on, the typical outsourced worker is a virtual assistant hired remotely from anywhere in the world, not an employee on payroll in an office or warehouse. [L1447, 2020-26]
- A virtual assistant makes sense when you want to spend your time on scaling and growth rather than repetitive operational tasks. Delegating routine work frees the owner for decisions that move the business forward. [L0765, undated]
- A virtual assistant buys back time, which is the scarcest resource for a business owner, by taking over day-to-day tasks such as answering phones and managing email. The owner can then focus on growth, travel or rest. [L1000, 2017]
- Delegating daily tasks to a virtual assistant freed the owner to focus on the big picture. Hiring the first VA earlier rather than later is recommended, since the sales gap after hiring was large. [L1437, 2018]
- Handing over daily operations, including customer service, to the team let the owner stop working day to day and focus on the bigger picture. A team that treats the business as its own can run it while the owner is away. [L1437, 2018]
- When an employee is given responsibility and trust, their performance improves noticeably and they ask fewer questions. Letting go of tasks early is the main leverage for the owner. [L1473, 2019]
- Training and letting go: Train thoroughly and let go of day-to-day control. [L0230, 2019]
- Hire and train with the screencast and SOP: after hiring, follow up with the hire every week. [L0827, 2019]

#### Accountability

- Without someone to hold you accountable, tasks get postponed indefinitely. Hiring help is an investment that also trains leadership and team management skills. [L0519, 2020+]

### Owner role as the business scales

#### Core role

- The business owner should own strategy: find where the valuable traffic is, design the funnel and ladder, and then hire specialists to run ads and optimize tactics. Trying to do every task personally slows the business down. Division of labor makes growth possible. [L0523, 2015+]
- At scale the owner should act as the strategic leader who sets the vision, makes sure work gets done and puts the right people in the right roles. Daily tasks such as supplier calls, uploading product pages and optimizing the site should be handed to others. [L0413, 2016]
- Partnerships, branding and the X factor are the three most effective ways to raise revenue, so the owner's time should go there. Optimization, product uploads and research can be outsourced to a team. [L0413, 2016]
- Once the team handles day-to-day operations and escalations, the owner should keep only sales, some marketing and accounting, which can be done from anywhere. The owner's role shifts from running the business to building it. [L0337, 2022]
- Hand over any task that someone else can do, including code-level changes you are not able to make yourself. A strong business leader delegates tasks rather than doing everything personally. [L1300, 2021-22] **
- Outsourcing frees time for growth work, such as finding new businesses, new ideas and new opportunities. Using time on repetitive order handling leaves no room for this. [L1122, 2016]
- The purpose of outsourcing routine tasks is to free time for growth, because a business cannot grow while the owner only handles day-to-day operations. Delegating these tasks is the step that allows the business to expand. [L1447, 2020-26]
- Outsourcing is a major lever for e-commerce owners across marketing, growth and day-to-day operations, and it is worth starting early rather than waiting until the business is large. [L0499, 2021] **

#### Future role

- The owner's future role can be to step back from the day-to-day, for example by managing a team or a network of businesses. Decide in the vision whether the owner stays in the weeds or moves to an advisory or management position. [L0439, 2021-22] **
- An owner moves up the ownership ladder as early revenue produces free cash flow to pay the first hire. Each new hire takes one more task off the owner, from doing the work, to managing those who do it, to managing those who work on the business. [L1334, 2021-22] **
- Scaling works best when the owner only reviews what changed: a supplier that stopped responding, a backordered product, or a stock report with low inventory. Routine work is delegated. [L0468, 2020] **
- Before leaving the country or taking a long break, set up the store and hire someone to handle phone calls and customer messages. [L0468, 2020] **
- Early in a business one person may need to wear all roles, including supplier calls and uploads. Once a process is working, hand specific tasks to others. [L0832, 2019]

#### Owner-level example

- A dropshipping owner with a niche store at about 40,000 USD monthly sales had plateaued. Customer feedback showed the range was too narrow, so he planned to add about 700 products. He hired freelancers for image editing, product descriptions and uploads to Shopify, and finished in about three weeks. Within two months monthly sales rose from 40,000 USD to 80,000 USD. Afterwards his own time grew to 20 to 30 hours per week until he moved the tasks to a team, and he cut his weekly time from over 30 hours to under 5 hours per week while keeping pay-per-click management himself. [L1122, 2016]
- Starting point: a single-owner store that had run since 2016, with the owner still in a corporate job and working around 40 hours per week on the business in January. The owner set a goal of $2 million revenue for 2021 and $1 million by June 30; 2020 revenue was $1.4 million with a team of three. The owner then joined a state COVID vaccination response team in early January 2021 and worked about seven days a week, 12-hour days, for six months, coordinating 121 nurses and 70 National Guard members who administered 250,000 vaccinations. [L0337, 2022]
  - What was done: the business was run with help from a team and was reduced to about 10 owner hours per week by June while revenue kept growing.
  - Result: $1 million was reached in May, one month ahead of the June goal, and about $1.29 to $1.3 million was reached over the six months. [L0337, 2022]
- A store owner in one case ran a store from 2014 and handled all operations for four years while struggling with trust issues and overwhelm. In 2018, after having a child, she hired a virtual assistant who saved the business and her well-being. Key insight: building systems before a crisis forces you to delegate is better than waiting until life events demand it. [L0230, 2019]

- Running the business at 30 minutes a day combines two approaches: automation through apps for repeatable work, and delegation of day-to-day tasks to people. Automation covers what software can handle, and delegation covers what still needs a human. [L0592, 2020-26]
- The usual order is to set up automation apps for repetitive work first, then delegate the day-to-day tasks that remain to people. The two approaches are combined to keep the owner's daily time low, not used in isolation. [L0592, 2020-26]
- Routine execution work that moves the business forward, in order: [L1042, 2026] *(Dated 2026, source the source's own site
  1. Launch new creatives.
  2. Improve operations.
  3. Fulfill orders.
  4. Talk to customers.
- Priorities for a busy store owner, in order: [L1281, 2022] **
  1. Make sure every order ships on time.
  2. Make sure every customer receives tracking information.
  3. Ask customers for reviews once they are happy.
  4. Check ads at least once a week for tweaks.
  5. Track profit per order to see what really makes money.
- When the owner is overloaded, give the team a clear list of daily priorities. Keep shipping, customer tracking and reviews first, then ad monitoring and profit tracking. [L1281, 2022] **

### What must stay in-house

- Keep supplier contact in-house, because supplier relationships drive bulk rates and discounts that compound as the business grows. Do not hand this task to someone else, even when it is unpleasant. [L1122, 2016]
- Keep supplier relationships in-house, since those are central to the business and hard to hand over. [L1279, 2021]
- Keep the tasks you do best, and any task that depends on supplier relationships or on understanding paid traffic, in-house. [L1122, 2016]
- The owner should keep only sales, some marketing and accounting, which can be done from anywhere. [L0337, 2022]
- Owner keeps strategy and key relationships: Partnerships, branding and the X factor are the three most effective ways to raise revenue, so the owner's time should go there. [L0413, 2016]
- Focus yourself on ads, supplier relationships, offers and big-picture decisions. Outsource your weaknesses and keep your strengths. Give tasks like web design and customer service to others. [L0852, 2020] **
- Outsource your weaknesses and keep your strengths: give tasks like web design and customer service to others, and focus yourself on ads, supplier relationships, offers and big-picture decisions. [L0852, 2020]
- Keep the work that fits your strengths for yourself, while setting the framework or strategy. Outsourcing works best when you define the end result you want from a task and let someone else run the execution. You can keep the part you enjoy, such as setting the framework or strategy, while a team member manages the day-to-day work. [L0701, 2015]
- Ads: Owners who are weak at ads or dislike them should hand off ad management. Other sources advise the owner to keep paid traffic understanding in-house and to keep paid ads management for themselves in one case. These differ and both apply by case: Dislike or weakness: outsource. [L1247, 2021] Paid traffic understanding: keep in-house. [L1122, 2016] Owner kept pay-per-click after moving tasks to a team in the case. [L1122, 2016] Too early: do not outsource paid ads before learning and documenting the process. [L0856, 2024]
- one source's test for what must stay: a task the owner does personally and must keep is one where the owner's own knowledge is required for relationships or results. [L1122, 2016]

#### Mistakes to avoid

- Delegating a process you have never run yourself is a mistake. [L0592, 2020-26]
- Handing over the whole business to a virtual assistant without understanding it is a mistake. [L0519, 2020+]
- Outsourcing a task costs money, but the freed time usually lets the business do more growth work. [L1247, 2021]
- Never outsource a task at a loss: if outsourcing would push the business into the red, wait until the business can afford it, though owners may push closer to break-even because outsourcing enables faster growth. [L1247, 2021] **
- Busy work that does not contribute to results should be removed from the business, not just performed more efficiently. [L1039, 2019+]
- Many owners delay hiring a virtual assistant because they feel the business depends on them personally. [L1000, 2017]
- Many owners delay hiring because they believe nobody else can run the business. [L1473, 2019]
- Waiting until you can no longer cope with the workload before outsourcing is a mistake. [L1447, 2020-26]
- Refusing to outsource to protect every dollar of profit can limit growth. [L1247, 2021]
- Do not outsource paid ads, web design, content, SEO or social posts to outside providers too early. [L0856, 2024]
- Outsourcing a task only because you dislike it is a mistake. Some tasks the owner must personally keep. [L1122, 2016]

### Sources on timing and affordability

Sources differ on when to start outsourcing. Each rule applies to the condition stated.

| Condition | Advice | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Business is up, running and profitable | Outsource as early as possible | [L1447, 2020-26] |
| Business can afford the cost | Start early, in small steps | [L1122, 2016] |
| Outsourcing would push the business into the red | Wait until the business can afford it | [L1247, 2021] |
| Low volume (around 20 high-ticket sales a month) | Owner handles calls and emails; no VA yet | [L1472, 2022] |
| Order volume exceeds owner capacity | Outsource customer service | [L1279, 2021] |
| Paid ads, web design, content, SEO or social posts, early in the business | Do not outsource too early; learn and document first | [L0856, 2024] |
| Owner already outsources | Review how the process works | [L0499, 2021] |

- Outsourcing is useful for bringing in experience you lack, for example a hired customer service specialist with 14 years of experience who improved service processes and customer engagement. [L1122, 2016]
- Outsourcing works best when you define the end result you want from a task and let someone else run the execution. [L0701, 2015]

Note: the timing sources are not all the same age; the older (2019-2022) entries are marked above.

#### Further details

- Delegate in small, concrete tasks, such as 'can someone answer the phone for me', rather than in broad functions like 'I need customer support'. Only a specific task can be handed off cleanly and checked afterwards. [L0337, 2022]

- The operator first outsourced his own job (writing blog posts and managing a social media page) to a virtual assistant, which freed time to build his own dropshipping business. Tasks he had no time for, such as replying to customer emails and processing orders daily, were then also delegated. [L0519, 2020+]
