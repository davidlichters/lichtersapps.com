### Product uploads and freelancers

**Deciding to outsource uploads.**
- Product uploads are the most tedious part of store setup but must be done correctly. Checking the work of anyone you hire is essential. [L0855, 2023]
- A supplier with bulk upload needs a CSV, which is a large spreadsheet. One mistake in one row or column can make many product descriptions or prices wrong. [L0855, 2023]
- Product upload CSV work can be done by the owner in one evening (about four or five hours), following Shopify's step-by-step CSV import guide. If it fails, post the job with the CSV on Upwork with Shopify product upload experience. [L0856, 2024]
- Use CSV uploads to add or update products in bulk. Shopify provides a template, and most product data can be managed in a spreadsheet before uploading. [L0665, 2022]
- A supplier catalogue that already exists as a spreadsheet but is not formatted for Shopify can be uploaded by a freelancer on Upwork, Fiverr or Storetasker. Outsourcing saves time when a supplier has thousands of SKUs. [L0857, 2025]
- Product upload effort depends on the supplier's data format. Ready-made Excel or CSV files upload quickly, while data spread over a supplier's website takes much longer per product. [L0667, 2025]

**Freelance platforms and costs.**
- Outsourcing product uploads can be done on freelance platforms such as onlinejobs.ph or upwork.com, where workers can be hired at roughly $3 to $4 per hour. [L0663, 2019]
- Experienced Shopify product upload freelancers on Upwork, often based in the Philippines, charge around $4 to $5 per hour. There is no fixed price per product, because it depends on how the supplier sends its data. [L0667, 2025]
- Examples on Upwork were around $5 per hour for Shopify product upload freelancers. [L0807, 2025]
- On Fiverr, typical pricing for Shopify product upload services is around $10 per 50 products. Many freelancers work in different time zones, which enables overnight uploads while you continue supplier outreach. [L0128, undated]
- Hire freelancers for bulk Shopify uploads through Upwork or onlinejobs.ph to build a consistent working relationship that can be reused as the supplier count grows. There is no fixed price; the cost depends on how much work the supplier data needs. [L1069, 2019]
- For a full-time dedicated upload person, onlinejobs.ph was listed at $99 per month. [L0243, 2019]
- Image resizing has been quoted at about 10 USD for 100 images. The task needs no speaking or writing, so freelancers or VAs in any country can do it. [L1163, 2021-22]
- Fiverr and OnlineJobs.ph can be used to outsource upload tasks such as image resizing or writing product data at affordable rates. [L1163, 2021-22]

**Hiring procedure, step by step.**

*Upwork with a recorded SOP:* [L0243, 2019]
1. Time how long you spend uploading a batch of 25 to 100 products.
2. Record your screen and voice using Loom, showing the complete upload process (20 to 30 seconds minimum).
3. Post the job on Upwork (free) with the Loom video as the SOP, or hire from OnlineJobs.ph ($99 per month) if you want dedicated full-time help.
4. Send the Loom SOP to the freelancer.
5. The freelancer handles the remaining bulk uploads while you focus on other tasks.

*Upwork with a test batch:* [L0667, 2025]
1. Record a screen and webcam video with Loom, a free tool, uploading 5 to 10 products from one supplier.
2. Narrate each step: get the product name, get the description, get the images, enter the price, and enter the SEO meta description.
3. Post a Shopify product upload job on Upwork, and send a test job of about 100 products to three or four applicants.
4. Hire the freelancer who does the test best, and give them the rest of the uploads.

*Upwork, filtered by track record:* [L0807, 2025]
1. Upload five to ten products yourself first, and confirm the process works.
2. Record the steps with Loom as a reference video.
3. Search Upwork for a freelancer with Shopify product upload experience.
4. Filter for job success rates of 90 percent or higher.
5. Send the recording and examples of finished products, so the freelancer can follow the same steps.

*Posting for free:* [L0855, 2023]
1. Post the job on Upwork for free.
2. Review each applicant's reviews and portfolio, asking for screenshots or video of previous work.
3. Hire, then double-check every upload row before publishing.

*Handing off across suppliers:* [L0671, 2021]
1. Do a few uploads yourself to learn the process.
2. Record your screen with Loom while uploading 10 to 20 products from one supplier.
3. Send the recording to whoever will do the uploads.
4. Treat each supplier as a separate job, because the work differs even when product counts are the same.

**Suppliers that give only basic data.**
- When uploading product lists from suppliers that give only UPC, SKU, name and price, upload three to five products yourself, record it, and have a freelancer do the rest. Spot-check the pricing afterwards. [L0852, 2020]
- A store owner can outsource site design, product uploads and developer tasks to freelancers on Upwork. Record a screen video showing how you want a product uploaded, then have the freelancer upload 100 to 150 products. [L0852, 2020]

**Scale and launch.**
- Launch traffic once around 30 to 50 products are live, and keep uploading the rest of the catalogue in parallel. Stores with over 3,000 products exist, and growth to that size happens gradually. [L0663, 2019]
- There is no fixed maximum number of products. Keep uploading until every quality supplier in your niche is uploaded, then check regularly for new product lines and new suppliers. [L0835, 2023]
- Each new supplier adds variety, and more products are better when they all come from suppliers in your niche. Adding suppliers over time is the way to grow the catalogue. [L0835, 2023]

**VA platforms for uploads.**
- The same platform can hire VAs for product uploads too. Screen candidates, because a good VA can upload hundreds of SKUs per day. [L1274, 2018]
- A store can scale with VAs for customer support and uploads. Full-time support staff can handle the work for an established brand, but a new store can start with a reliable VA platform. [L1274, 2018]

### Catalog, content and pricing

**Product pages.**
- Maintain a consistent layout standard across all products. Ensure photo ratios are identical (for example, square) so they display properly in collections. Use a standard template for descriptions. Update the SOP template as you improve the design, and let team members contribute suggestions. [L0263, 2022]
- Each new product added to a multi-product page requires the full cycle: duplicate, set Active, create its template, assign the template and save. Skipping any one of these steps causes content changes to affect other products. [L1013, 2026]
- To audit products that are already live, work back through existing product pages and check each one against the on-page SEO checklist. This is simple enough to hand to a team member or VA, and most people find they are missing some items on older pages. [L1191, 2021-22]

**Content and images.**
- Product content such as photos and videos usually comes from the supplier, so you rarely need to create it yourself. Free online tools are available for ad overlays and text on images or video. [L0358, 2020]
- A VA can handle the repetitive work of uploading supplier product content and building product pages. Delegating these tasks frees your time for supplier relationships and strategy. [L0358, 2020]
- Resizing large batches of images is repetitive, manual work that should be outsourced. [L1163, 2021-22]

**Account location.**
- Upload from the team's own accounts and region rather than assigning posting to someone in another country, because account location affects reach. The owner had not yet considered this when outsourcing uploads. [L1077, undated]

**VA tasks in the store.**
- VA tasks in the store included handling pricing errors, updating products when suppliers change prices, and replacing product photos. One VA learned basic Shopify skills on the side to do these updates. [L0921, 2022]
- Store speed fixes can be outsourced to Shopify experts found on storetasker.com or on Upwork. This is an option when the owner does not want to fix performance problems personally. [L1446, 2020-26]

### Supplier outreach and product integration

**The integration cycle.**
- The product integration cycle is: gain supplier approval, upload their products immediately, return to supplier outreach, and repeat until all approved suppliers are live. [L0128, undated]
- Once a supplier approves you, upload all of its products immediately and remove the demo products. Continue contacting more suppliers on your list after the first approval. [L0835, 2023]
- Upload new products from a supplier as soon as the supplier approves you, then check for new suppliers every six months. [L0665, 2022]

**Recruiting suppliers.**
- For a new store, bring on 20 to 25 new suppliers per month. This creates a deep supplier pool to choose from when fulfilling orders. [L0263, 2022]
- Track every supplier in a supplier tracking sheet with the date of your last contact, the outcome, and the next follow-up date. Use it to schedule follow-ups, such as contacting a rejected supplier again in three months. [L0835, 2023]
- Supplier outreach, including contacting new suppliers, following up, and uploading their products, is a continuous task that never fully ends. Once it becomes too much for the owner, it is a candidate for a dedicated outreach person. [L1446, 2020-26]

### Quality checks on delegated work

- Check every product upload closely, whether you do it yourself or someone else does. Wrong descriptions, measurements or prices from CSV or Excel uploads can go live unnoticed and hurt conversions. [L0470, 2023]
- When outsourcing product uploads, check the work regularly. Common errors are the wrong image on a product or the wrong price on a product. [L0663, 2019]
- Check that each product row matches its title, description and price before going live. [L1111, 2020]
- If you outsource product uploads, work in small batches and double-check every price, image and product detail before publishing. Mixed-up prices or images are a common and costly error. [L0247, 2021]
- Before a new VA uploads a full batch, ask for one sample upload per manufacturer and check it. This catches formatting or quality problems on a small scale before they affect the whole catalogue. [L0832, 2019]
- Verify outsourced work before relying on it. Outsourced product review or approval work may give unsatisfactory results. [L1094, 2023]
- Check the daily fulfilment routine closely in the first weeks, then reduce it to periodic review once the routine runs as planned. [L1358, undated]

### Downtime tasks and customer insight

**Downtime ideas for a VA when the inbox is empty.** [L1000, 2017]
1. Research suppliers that sell the same products, checking whether they offer better deals or have lowered prices.
2. Learn to upload products, so the assistant can handle new supplier listings when they are added.
3. If the business is mostly B2B, have the assistant look for potential customers and make cold calls to generate more sales.
4. Keep a shared task board, such as Trello, where the owner adds downtime tasks as they come to mind.
