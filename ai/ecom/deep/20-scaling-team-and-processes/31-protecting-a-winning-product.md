### Protecting a Winning Product

- After three to five days of consistent sales, move to custom content to protect the product from copycats. This typically starts around day three. [L1436, undated]
- Start with small, low-key influencer tests and grow the number of creators as sales come in. Small creators can help, and when many of them post the same product, larger creators may reach out to you. [L1379, 2020+]

### Spark Ads and Coins

- Use Spark Ads for bigger budgets, roughly $100 to $500, and multiple ad groups, which can be created by copying an ad group up to 20 times. [L1429, 2022]
- Use coins for smaller budgets, such as $20 to $30, since coins count as one ad group. [L1429, 2022]
- Test organically first. Use coins only for a new product you are highly confident about or for a second account of a proven product, and move to Spark Ads once you have money to scale. [L1429, 2022]

### Duplicating Accounts for Proven Products

- After an account reaches about 10,000 followers and brings in sales, a second account promoting the same product can be created by someone else or on another phone. Reuse the videos that already performed on the first account. [L1429, 2022]
- Two accounts, each with about 10,000 followers and each getting about 1 million views per day, produce about 2 million views per day in total. Adding a link to each account multiplies the sales potential. [L1429, 2022]
- Duplicate accounts only for products that already perform. A good sign is strong sales per million views. If a product does not perform, copying its content is not worth the effort. [L1429, 2022]
- Assume competitors will copy winning videos and launch the same product. Competing against yourself with a second account captures more of the market before others saturate it. [L1429, 2022]
- Competitors can download posted videos and repost them without the watermark. Disabling downloads only partly limits copying, so plan for copies and keep ahead. [L1429, 2022]
- If a second account brings in thousands of dollars in sales, a third account can make sense if you can run and maintain it yourself. There is no fixed limit on accounts beyond your capacity to manage them. [L1429, 2022]
- A product account that grew slowly can still produce a big result after a later viral video. The cited example estimated that six million additional views could bring about 30,000 in sales. [L1429, 2022]

### Pinterest: Test Setup

- Test each new product at $5 per day, because Pinterest needs time to learn the audience and higher early budgets mostly waste money. [L1135, undated]
- In the lesson's tests, products that were profitable at $5 per day were also the ones that stayed profitable, while a $100 per day budget did not improve results. [L1135, undated]
- Start with a maximum of $5 to $10 per day for the initial test, and run three to five products in parallel if the budget allows, since one of them is likely to win. [L1135, undated]
- Pinterest has no ad set budgets, only a campaign budget (CBO), so set the daily budget at campaign level. [L1135, undated]
- Build the first test campaign:
  1. Choose the Conversions objective. [L1135, undated]
  2. Name the campaign with the product name and the launch date. [L1135, undated]
  3. Select the target country. US is the largest market; Germany and the Netherlands also work. [L1135, undated]
  4. Set the campaign status to Active. [L1135, undated]
  5. Set the daily budget to $5. [L1135, undated]
  6. Select run on specific days and choose the next day as the start date. [L1135, undated]
  7. Set the launch time to 12:00 AM UTC. [L1135, undated]
- Pinterest uses only UTC for scheduling, with no local time option, so launch campaigns at midnight UTC. [L1135, undated]
- To set up the next Pinterest test, duplicate a campaign that already works and change only the product name, launch date and targeting. Keeping the budget, country and schedule settings unchanged makes the new test easier to compare with the previous one. [L1134, undated]

#### Pinterest Targeting and Settings

- Ad group level: name the ad group stacked interest, then under Targeting details choose the interest keywords option and select interests from the dropdown. [L1135, undated]
- Select 5 to 20 interests, ideally 7 to 10 closely related to the product. Choose interests that describe the target audience rather than only the product itself. [L1135, undated]
- Keep the total audience size between 5 and 10 million, which the lesson calls the sweet spot, and never above 30 million. [L1135, undated]
- Select sub-interests individually instead of whole parent categories. The audience shrinks when sub-interests are added, but individual selections let you see which interest generates sales later. [L1135, undated]
- Demographics and audience: select only female if the product is for women, and set the age range from 21 and up, or 25 and up if that fits the product. Location: US or specific countries. Leave language and devices unrestricted and keep the default budget and schedule. [L1135, undated]
- Optimization and delivery: set Optimization to Checkout and Delivery to Automatic, then keep the ad group active. [L1135, undated]

### Pinterest: Scaling

- Scaling a winning Pinterest campaign:
  1. If results are highly profitable after day 3, double the budget. [L1133, undated]
  2. Find more interests and keywords and put them in a separate campaign. [L1133, undated]
  3. Launch the new campaign at higher budgets. [L1133, undated]
  4. Move to CPA-based scaling. [L1133, undated]
- Source disagreement on the trigger: one procedure doubles the budget when results are highly profitable after day 3. The other requires the campaign to stay above breakeven for two consecutive days. Use the day 3 check for highly profitable results and the two-day check for a stable campaign. [L1133, undated; L1135, undated]
- Scale a campaign only when its ROAS stays above breakeven for two consecutive days. The lesson example is a breakeven ROAS of 1.5 and an actual ROAS of 2.5, after which the daily budget doubles from $5 to $10. [L1135, undated]
- Raise budgets at the start of the day, around 12 AM UTC, or up to 4 to 5 AM UTC. Do not raise them at 6 PM or 10 PM, because a late increase spends within one to two hours. [L1135, undated]
- Scale a winning product:
  1. Confirm the campaign has stayed above breakeven ROAS for two consecutive days. [L1135, undated]
  2. Double the daily budget at the start of the UTC day, for example from $5 to $10. [L1135, undated]
  3. Open the targeting breakdown and select interest to see which interests produce sales. [L1135, undated]
  4. Identify the interests that generate profitable sales, such as skincare or makeup. [L1135, undated]
  5. Create new campaigns that target the winning interests and keywords, rather than only increasing the existing budget. [L1135, undated]
- Horizontal scaling means building more campaigns with profitable interests or keywords, instead of only increasing the budget of one campaign. [L1135, undated]
- For keyword campaigns, add 50 to 80 relevant keywords, use the add-all-results option to check audience size, and keep the audience between 10 and 30 million. [L1135, undated]
- Advanced CPA scaling is considered only after 10 to 15 days of consistent results. It sets a maximum cost per acquisition, for example under $20, and requires detailed setup beyond the basic test. [L1135, undated]
- Example: if a Pinterest campaign shows profitable sales broken down by interest, for example skincare and makeup at a ROAS of about 4 to 5, launch a second campaign that targets only the strongest interest, such as skincare alone. [L1135, undated]

### Platform Updates: TikTok Inbox and YouTube Shorts

- TikTok's inbox added games that can be played inside direct messages and labels for organizing conversations. The update commentary notes that Instagram messages are still seen faster than TikTok DMs, so Instagram is the stronger channel for reaching creators you work with. [L1504, 2025-26]
- On YouTube Shorts, the dislike button was removed and replaced with Not interested and Don't recommend channel options. The heart icon also replaced the thumbs-up as the like signal, so creators should not expect dislike feedback on Shorts. [L1504, 2025-26]
