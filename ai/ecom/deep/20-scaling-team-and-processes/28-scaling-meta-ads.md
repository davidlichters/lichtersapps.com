## Scaling Meta ads

### Scaling directions and principles

- Horizontal scaling means finding more audiences: while a product stays profitable, launch 2 to 3 new interests every day, keep testing for as many audiences as possible that work, and reuse them with new creatives. Turn off underperforming ad sets. [L0375, undated]
- Vertical scaling means raising the budget of existing campaigns or ad sets, or duplicating them. Use either method or both to scale aggressively. [L0375, undated]
- Test whether raising budgets or duplicating works better in your own account, because the best method depends on the account. [L0375, undated]
- Budget increase methods differ per ad account. Test doubling the budget, a 25 percent increase, or duplicating the ad set on one campaign first, and roll a method out to other campaigns only if it works there. [L0645, 2023]
- Every ad account behaves differently, so test everything instead of copying what worked for another account. Test a structure with a small budget before committing to it. [L0376, undated]
- There is no single strategy that works for every product or ad account. Keep testing broad and interest approaches and scale whichever one proves profitable. [L1161, 2024+]
- No single formula fits every account. Choose the structure based on what is profitable, and keep the cost per acquisition below your break-even. [L1195, 2026]
- Keep scaling campaigns running for the best audiences and creatives, using CBOs where they work. If CBOs do not work in the account, use ABO campaigns that only scale the best audiences and creatives. [L0375, undated; L0376, undated]
- Always keep scaling campaigns running. Use CBO for scaling the best audiences with the best creatives, and switch to an ABO campaign dedicated to scaling if CBO does not work for your account. [L0376, undated]
- The testing campaign is a starting structure. Once purchases show which interests and creatives win, move to CBO or other campaign types to scale. [L0647, 2024+]
- Scale a winning product only once the pixel shows real purchase data. Until then, keep testing. [L1195, 2026]

### Readiness to scale: proof thresholds

Sources disagree on the sales threshold. Use the values below according to the situation:

| Source value | Threshold | Context |
|---|---|---|
| Interest or ad set in ABO testing | At least 3 purchases with ROAS above break-even | Starting a CBO scaling campaign with a $100 budget [L0645, 2023] |
| Ad set with consistent sales | At least 4 purchases | Raising the budget by 20 percent every 24 hours [L0375, undated; L0376, undated] |
| Interest proven profitable | At least 6 profitable sales | Choosing a scaling method [L0639, 2023] |
| Audience or ad set | About 6 to 7 sales; 5 is too few | Most common rule, applied before scaling [L0646, 2023; L0692, 2023; L0773, 2023] |
| Winning interest | About 7 sales | Raising its budget or starting a new $50 CBO without interests [L0692, 2023] |

- Before scaling a Meta ad set, wait for roughly six to seven profitable sales in a consistent pattern. Four or five sales is too little data. A single unusually strong day can distort the picture, so look for steady sales across several days. [L0646, 2023]
- Consider scaling an audience only after it has about 6 to 7 sales, since five sales are too few to be confident. Early on, use a small budget rather than a high one. [L0692, 2023]
- Do not scale a Facebook ad set until it delivers more than six or seven sales. Before that point, keep collecting data at the current budget. [L0773, 2023]
- Once a winning interest reaches about seven sales, you can raise its budget, or start a new $50 CBO without interests to test further. Both options should follow the same ROAS and sales thresholds. [L0692, 2023]
- A campaign needs a minimum daily spend of about 10 or 50 pounds per day to be easily scalable. Below that level, scaling is considered difficult. [L0646, 2023]
- Once the pixel has 1,000 or more purchases, you can also target a broad audience with no interests. [L0376, undated]
- Do not scale an audience with a ROAS below 1.7 when the break-even ROAS is 1.5. A higher ROAS gives more confidence, and 1.7 is the minimum for scaling. [L0692, 2023]
- Advertisers disagree on how far ROAS should sit above break-even before scaling. Some use break-even plus 0.3 to 0.5. A more conservative rule with a break-even ROAS of 1.5 requires a minimum of 1.7, about 0.2 above. Use the margin your own account supports. [L0692, 2023; L0375, undated]
- Set your own benchmark ROAS and adjust the formula to it, because a 20 percent margin is not every seller's target. [L0375, undated]
- Test any new scaling method on only one winning campaign first. Do not test on your sole profitable campaign. [L0298, 2023]
- At low budgets, around $10 per asset, daily sales are very unstable. Even ad sets at about $300 can swing from a 2 ROAS to under 1, though higher budgets tend to stabilize results somewhat. [L1107, 2023]
- Scaling is not a day-one activity: segment and scale by profit only after the account has data and standard optimizations are done. [L0224, 2024+]

### Testing structure and creative flow

- Testing campaign structure:
  1. Interest-testing campaign: take winning creatives and test new interests.
  2. Creative-testing campaign: take the winning interest and test new creative variations.
  3. Once winners are identified, combine all winning creatives into one audience targeting for scaling. [L0067, 2023]
- Start each new product with $5 per ad set across five to six ad sets, about $25 to $30 per day. Once a test ad set shows a good CTR and consistent sales, choose how to scale: raise the budget on the winning ad sets, or add spend to test ad sets so the winning audience is found faster. [L1184, 2023+]
- Testing procedure for a new audience:
  1. Test the audience for two to three days.
  2. Remove creatives that perform badly in a consistent way.
  3. Wait until the audience has 6 to 7 sales.
  4. Confirm a ROAS of at least 1.7 when break-even ROAS is 1.5.
  5. Start with a CBO budget of about $50.
  6. Once it is proven, scale using the three-campaign structure below. [L0692, 2023]
- A CBO campaign with a daily budget of about $50 is an acceptable starting budget for testing a scaling candidate. [L0692, 2023]
- Do not double a budget abruptly. A product that converted above 3 percent in testing dropped to zero add-to-carts the day after the budget was doubled. [L0692, 2023]
- Move the winning creative into its own campaign, then duplicate the same creative into multiple ad sets to scale it:
  1. Find the winning creative among the test ad sets.
  2. Move that creative into its own campaign.
  3. Duplicate the same creative into multiple ad sets to scale it. [L1194, undated]
- Relying on a single ad to scale is risky, even if that ad has good metrics. Build more ad variants so the campaign does not depend on one creative. [L1107, 2023]

### Account layout

| Campaign | Purpose | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audience testing campaign | Test all audiences, including LLAs | Testing | Every audience gets tried |
| Creative testing campaign | Test new creatives with your best audiences | Testing | New ads run against the best audiences |
| Scaling campaigns | Scale the best audiences with the best creatives | CBO, or ABO if CBO does not work for the account | Use CBO first; switch to ABO if it performs better in your account |
| Retargeting campaign | Retarget warm visitors | ABO or CBO | Run both on a small budget to confirm which works better |

[L0375, undated; L0376, undated]

- The more audiences an account has, the easier scaling becomes. Launch 3 to 5 or more new audiences (single interest or LLAs) every day or every week, and calculate the budget needed for these new audiences before scaling. [L0375, undated; L0376, undated]
- Once the account has several winning audiences, scale with 3 to 5 or more new audiences per day, using single interests or lookalike audiences (LLAs). [L0376, undated]

- Graduate from the testing structure to the scaling structure only after the pixel holds real purchase data, roughly 500-1,000 website purchases. Until then, keep testing. [L1195, 2026]
- Revived creatives from the graveyard go back to ABO testing with cost cap, forming a repeating loop: testing, then scaling, then graveyard, then testing again. [L1195, 2026]
- A big-cap CBO can be limited to the best-performing countries, for example the top four countries plus New Zealand, while Singapore and all other markets are left out. Set the country list from results rather than keeping every market. [L1195, 2026]

### CBO and ABO

- ABO is used for testing interests and CBO for scaling in many accounts. CBO does not work for every account, and ABO can perform better, so the right choice depends on testing the setup. [L0349, 2024]
- CBO scaling setup: put a proven interest into a CBO campaign with a budget of 100 to 200 USD. Include 4 to 5 ad sets with the same audience, with about four creatives in each. [L0648, undated]
- Conflict on creative count per CBO ad set. Use about four creatives per ad set in a proven CBO scaling campaign [L0648, undated]. Use about three creatives per ad set when a campaign starts with 4 to 5 ad sets [L1107, 2023]. Use three creatives per ad set in the three-campaign structure [L0692, 2023]. Use two to three creatives rather than one when building the first scaling CBO [L0645, 2023].
- In a CBO, Meta assigns the budget to the ad sets that are reaching the best pockets of the audience, then searches for similar people to grow those pockets. This is why CBO works for scaling once there is a budget of about 100 to 200 USD. [L0648, undated]
- A campaign-level budget (CBO) moves money toward the best-performing ad sets, but it can sometimes allocate spend to a weaker one based on early engagement. Review the allocation regularly and turn off the weak ad sets after enough data has accumulated. [L0639, 2023]
- A high-budget CBO does not spend evenly across creatives. On one day one creative gets most of the budget, and on another day a different one does. A scaling campaign therefore needs several working creatives, not one. [L1107, 2023]
- Before building a scaling CBO, keep ads that work in other test ad sets even if they fail in one ad set. Remove an ad from the CBO only if it performs badly everywhere, and keep looking for more good creatives. [L1107, 2023]
- A CBO scaling campaign starts with 4 to 5 ad sets. Each ad set contains about 3 creatives. [L1107, 2023]
- If CBO does not work in an account, scale with ABO instead. Duplicate the winning ad set three times at 100 USD each in one ABO campaign, or create three separate ABO scaling campaigns with 100 USD each. [L0648, undated]
- CBO campaigns with several ads do not work for every account. In the case described, they gave zero sales and rising CPA compared with testing (testing CPA about $10 to $50, CBO CPA about $20). Switching off underperforming ads inside a CBO can disrupt the optimization, so ABO was preferred for scaling in this case. [L0995, 2023]
- Move a winning product from an ABO test into its own CBO:
  1. After the ABO test, shortlist the products whose ad sets show strong results.
  2. For each winning product, create a new CBO campaign with three creatives.
  3. Wait until the new CBO is live and running.
  4. Only then turn off the individual ABO ad set for that product. [L0348, 2026]
- Once the best cost cap is found, move it into its own individual CBO campaign. Use that cap across other winning audiences and broad campaigns as you scale. [L0977, undated]
- Create three CBO campaigns using the winning interest, then increase budgets there when the data supports it. [L1161, 2024+]

### Horizontal scaling procedures

- Horizontal scaling procedure for a winning ad set:
  1. Move the winning ad set into its own scaling campaign.
  2. Duplicate it, for example 10 times, as ABO campaigns with about $20 in each.
  3. After a short spend, switch off the copies that have no sales; for example, kill an ad after a day without sales.
  4. Keep the copies that perform well and scale them further. [L0995, 2023]
- Horizontal scaling means duplicating a winning ad set into a new campaign, often about 10 copies, then keeping the best performer and turning off the rest. It is used when the ROAS stays profitable but no longer supports a large budget jump. [L0646, 2023]
- Budget testing with a small ad set: run three copies at 10, 15 and 20 units of budget to find the best budget for the ad, then scale the winner horizontally by duplicating it again. [L0646, 2023]
- You can duplicate your top 3 to 5 winning audiences or interests into a CBO campaign with a $150 to $200 budget. Use this when a single ad set's budget increases stop working. [L0376, undated; L0375, undated]
- Once a campaign is unprofitable, check it at ad set and ad level first. A single audience or creative can drag down the whole campaign, so kill that bad ad set or creative. [L0375, undated]
- Keeping underperforming ad sets running alongside the winners drains budget, so turn them off consistently. [L0375, undated]
- If an interest or ad set stops working across several days, switch it off and try something else. An interest that stopped can sometimes work again after days or weeks, so a stop does not mean it is dead for good. [L1107, 2023]

### Vertical scaling: budget steps and rules

Merged from the table and procedure with the same content:

| Condition | Action |
|---|---|
| Yesterday ROAS is at or above the benchmark ROAS (BE ROAS + 20% margin) | Increase budget by 20% (vertical scaling). Or duplicate the ad set 2 to 3 times at higher budgets. Or duplicate the campaign at a higher budget. |
| Yesterday ROAS is about equal to BE ROAS | No action needed. |
| Last 3 days + today ROAS is about equal to BE ROAS | Reduce budget by 20% to stabilize. |
| Last 3 days + today ROAS is below BE ROAS | Turn off the ad sets (kill losers). |

[L0375, undated; L0376, undated]

| Yesterday ROAS is about equal to BE ROAS | No action needed. |
| Last 3 days + today ROAS is about equal to BE ROAS | Pause the ad sets, then duplicate and refresh them. |
| Last 3 days + today ROAS is below BE ROAS | Kill the ad sets. |

- Scaling an ad set with a 20 percent step: when an ad set consistently produces sales and has at least 4 purchases, raise its budget by 20 percent of the initial budget every 24 hours, as long as it performs 20 percent above BE ROAS. Check whether budget increases or duplication work better for your account. [L0375, undated; L0376, undated]
- Scaling by a 20 percent step on a moderate ad: when an ad has only a moderate result but is still profitable, duplicate it at a 20 percent budget step. Run it a few days, and if it holds, raise the budget again by 20 percent. This avoids risking large budgets on an ad whose ROAS may drop. [L0646, 2023]
- Scaling a profitable Advantage+ campaign:
  1. Start with a budget such as $100.
  2. Raise the budget by 20% every two to three days while the campaign stays profitable.
  3. If results drop after a few days, return to the previous budget level.
  4. If the campaign is not profitable after about a week, close it and scale a winning interest instead. [L1161, 2024+]
- Scaling a winning Facebook ad with a slow, step-by-step increase:
  1. Keep the winning ad in an ABO campaign with its own budget.
  2. Raise the budget by about $20 on the same ad, since it already has engagement and Facebook has learned from it.
  3. Run it for a few days.
  4. If the ad holds, add another $20 after about three more days.
  5. Repeat and raise the budget slowly while the return stays acceptable.
  6. Once the ad is strong at a higher budget (e.g. $100), test it against a more aggressive budget, then move to $200 and $400 if it holds. [L0995, 2023]
- Scaling an ad that has proven itself:
  1. Keep the original ad set running; do not touch it.
  2. Duplicate the ad set three times.
  3. Put more budget into each copy, for example three ad sets at about $40 per day.
  4. Test under the bigger budgets and see which one holds. [L1315, 2023]
- Scale a CBO campaign by raising the budget by 25 percent. If profit margin falls after a 25 percent increase in budget, reduce the budget again (descale). [L0348, 2026]
- Scaling by a fixed step on a CBO, with a 100-dollar starting point:
  1. Find an interest in ABO testing that gives at least 3 purchases with a ROAS above break-even.
  2. Launch a CBO campaign with a $100 budget.
  3. Create three duplicate ad sets, each with the same winning interest.
  4. Add the winning ads (creatives) to the ad sets, using two to three creatives rather than one.
  5. If the campaign runs for about 12 hours with a ROAS of 3 or more and at least 3 purchases (2 purchases can be enough), raise the budget by $100, for example from $100 to $200.
  6. Do not raise the budget again on the same day. Check the next day and repeat the +$100 step (to $300, then $400) only while the ROAS holds.
  7. If the CBO is not profitable on day one, cut it. If it is profitable on day one but not on day two, let it run to day three; cut it if day three is also unprofitable.
  8. Keep testing new interests with 15 to 20 percent of the budget so the next winners can be moved into CBO. [L0645, 2023]
- Aggressive scaling method: double the CBO budget repeatedly until reaching 500 dollars; thereafter increase budget by only 20% per adjustment. [L0298, 2023]
- Budget scaling rule: increase daily ad spend by a maximum of 2x (e.g., $50 per day to $100-150 per day), and only after observing 2 to 3 weeks of consistent profitability. Aggressive scaling without proven consistency leads to losses. [L0321, undated]
- When results are strong, scale aggressively by nearly doubling the budget, for example from 50 to 80 pounds per day, and keep it if performance holds for a few days before the next step. Sustained performance at the higher level then justifies moving to the next budget step, such as 100 pounds per day. [L0646, 2023]
- Return on ad spend typically drops sharply after the first aggressive budget increase, then falls by smaller amounts with each further increase. The largest drop comes from the initial jump, so a strategy must allow for that first decline. [L0646, 2023]
- Aggressive scaling is safe only if the ROAS after the jump stays about 0.8 above break-even. If it does, the budget can be doubled again and the ad set moved into its own scaling campaign. [L0646, 2023]
- A Meta campaign that performs well can be scaled in steps, for example from about $100 per day to $500 per day, and later to the thousands or tens of thousands per day. Each step should follow only a stable result with a strong audience and targeting. Jumping directly to high budgets is a common way to trigger restrictions. [L0637, 2023+]
- Scaling a test from 5 dollars per day to 100 dollars per day in one jump led to an ad account block. Scale in gradual steps after the product shows profit. [L1131, 2018+]
- Do not double a budget on one day, and do not copy a 300 dollar CBO to a new 300 dollar campaign. Neither carries a guarantee, and either can break a working setup. Increase in steps across days and watch the ad account's response before duplicating. [L0645, 2023]
- Do not change every campaign at once when scaling, because a bad budget change can hurt all campaigns in the account. Apply a change to one CBO campaign, watch the results, and then extend it to others. [L0645, 2023]
- Scale a winning campaign by duplicating it at a higher budget only when ROAS is well above break-even. If results are only acceptable, raise the budget of the existing campaign instead. [L0692, 2023]
- Example: a $50 CBO campaign with a strong ROAS can be duplicated at $100 while the original stays at $50, bringing total daily spend to $150. If the $100 copy fails, retry the duplicate at $50 or increase the original budget by 20 percent. [L0692, 2023]
- On Facebook, smaller daily ad set budgets of roughly £10 to £30 often deliver better and cheaper impressions than large budgets. Scale across several small ad sets first, and raise budgets in steps rather than doubling a single ad set, because big jumps make delivery search harder. [L1426, undated]
- Short event campaigns such as Black Friday or Christmas may be scaled up and down quickly, but evergreen campaigns need a steady 7-day window before any budget change. [L1016, 2026]
- Facebook budget steps for a campaign started at $50 per day:
  1. Start the Facebook campaign at $50 per day and make sure the creatives and page are solid.
  2. Let it run for 7 days.
  3. If all target metrics are hitting, raise the budget by 15%.
  4. Let it breathe for about 3 days.
  5. If it still performs well, raise the budget another 15% (a 15-20% step stays safe for the learning phase).
  6. If traffic arrives but there are no sales after 7 days, turn the ads off and refine the website before spending again. [L1016, 2026]
