## Owner time, task audit and what to delegate

### Why owner time is the scarce resource

- A business that depends on the owner's daily involvement stops when the owner is unavailable. The goal is a business that keeps running without the owner in day-to-day operations, which is the main reason to document and delegate early. [L0337, 2022]
- Build systems so the business keeps running when the owner is not working in it. A business that depends on constant personal effort does not scale. [L0631, 2026]
- Scale by building backend systems for customer retention, so the business keeps running without the owner doing daily work. [L0914, 2026]
- ** Scaling is easier and more fulfilling than remaining solo, because the owner can focus on work they enjoy rather than handling every task, including those they dislike. [L0107, 2021-22]
- ** To perform higher-value work, you must build a team. Scaling emerges naturally when you focus on meaningful work rather than pursuing it as a direct goal. [L0107, 2021-22]
- Running the business alone is possible for years. Outsourcing repetitive daily tasks is a lifestyle choice rather than a requirement. [L0968, 2021]
- Build the business as a lifestyle business. A single store can run part-time with about 15 to 20 minutes of work per day, and full-time work on one store suggests tasks should be outsourced. [L1111, 2020]
- Recurring daily tasks are what consume the owner's time, so they are the first candidates for automation or delegation. The owner's time is better spent on growth, finding new opportunities and other priorities. [L0592, 2020-26]
- Busy work that does not contribute to results should be removed from the business, not just performed more efficiently. Eliminating it is one of the fastest ways to stand out from people who have struggled for years. [L1039, 2019+]
- Growth stalls when the owner keeps senior-level tasks that are not worth their time or energy. Sustainable growth needs a team of people who outperform the owner in specific areas of the business. [L1129, undated]
- Income grows through leverage, which comes from working less in the business, not working longer hours. Leverage is built by hiring staff and learning to manage them. A business earning around 250,000 dollars a year cannot grow if the owner does every task, so delegation frees time for new ideas and new businesses. [L1513, 2019]
- Parkinson's law says work expands to fill the time available for it. Owners who work 40 to 60 hours a week can test this by deliberately cutting their working time, for example to 10 hours in one week, and often find the same output gets done. Use the test to find unnecessary work and tighten deadlines. [L1513, 2019]
- ** Outsourcing and personal effectiveness are closely linked for an owner-operator, because delegating tasks frees time and focus for the work only the owner can do. [L0802, 2021]
- ** Owner-level preparation, such as time management, prioritization and focus, comes before scaling tactics, because a business cannot grow faster than its leader's capacity to execute. [L0802, 2021]
- The owner's role shifts from running the business to building it. [L0337, 2022]

### Owner time and hourly value

- Calculate your hourly time value and multiply it by the weekly hours you spend in your current role. If an agency costs less than this weekly opportunity cost and frees you to work on higher-impact activities, the financial math improves. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Example: if you spend 10 hours weekly managing ads at five hundred dollars per hour, your time costs five thousand dollars weekly. Outsourcing frees that value to redirect toward higher-impact work such as supplier negotiations or product development. [L0217, 2025-26]
- Estimate how long a task should take by doing it yourself and then allowing for the staff member's experience. A product upload may take five to ten minutes, and experienced staff can be faster than the owner. Use a reasonable average and reject unreasonable estimates, such as a 500-word article in half an hour. [L1396, 2021-22]
- Outsourcing a task costs money, but the freed time usually lets the business do more growth work, so the extra activity can repay the cost over time. Refusing to outsource to protect every dollar of profit can limit growth and reduce total profit in the long run. [L1247, 2021]
- Outsourcing early can speed up progress considerably, even when the outsourcing cost is hard to carry and leaves little profit in the short term, because the business reaches a better position sooner than it would by doing everything itself. [L0499, 2021]

### Task audit: list, measure and test

#### Step 1: map the work

- Decide what you actually need before anything else. The four-step method to free up working time is applied in this order: [L0827, 2019]
  1. Definition: decide what you actually need.
  2. Elimination: remove everything you can get rid of.
  3. Automation: automate what can run without you.
  4. Liberation: outsource what remains and cannot be automated.
- Work time is drained by three groups of tasks: minimize expenses, material items, responsibilities, accounting and daily routine tasks. Automate lead generation, ad management, FAQs, email responders, call tracking numbers, order emails and passive income. Liberate by handing work to virtual assistants, phone support, a bookkeeper, partners, or by selling the store and living on investment income. [L0827, 2019]
- Build a task chart before hiring: [L0337, 2022]
  1. List the main business areas (in the example: operations, customer service, sales, product support, sales and marketing, accounting).
  2. Break each area into small, specific tasks, e.g. "answer the phone" instead of "customer support".
  3. Color-code who does each task: tasks the owner does are blue; tasks the owner does 100% alone get a heavy dark red outline.
  4. When a task is handed to someone else, recolor it green so the owner can see what has left their plate.
- To choose which tasks to outsource, work through these steps in order: [L1122, 2016]
  1. List every task you perform to run the business day to day.
  2. Record how many hours per week each task takes.
  3. Focus on the pain points, which in dropshipping are usually repetitive tasks such as product uploads during setup, or order placement and payment processing when sales are high.
  4. Outsource the simplest repetitive tasks first.
- Review the task lists of each priority in the 180-day plan (up to five lists). Ask how much of the work the owner should really be doing, and which tasks could be handed to someone else. [L1247, 2021] **
- Plan a delegation: [L1514, 2021-22] **
  1. Identify the task and ask whether only you can do it, not whether you are the best person for it.
  2. If not, plan when it can be handed off.
  3. Identify where and how to find someone to do it and the options available.
  4. Create SOPs and training materials.
  5. Hand the task over when the timing is right, or set a date if it is not possible immediately.

#### Step 2: test each task against the criteria

- Test each task on the task list against seven criteria: [L1247, 2021] **
  1. The task is simple and repeated.
  2. The task is urgent but not important.
  3. The task is neither urgent nor important.
  4. The task is time consuming for small returns.
  5. The owner is not good at or does not enjoy the task.
  6. The task is unpredictable and disruptive.
  7. The task is affordable to outsource.

  A task only needs to meet ONE of these criteria to be a candidate for outsourcing.
- Delegate any recurring task that takes more than one hour per day of the owner's time and does not need the owner specifically. The owner's top priority as a business leader is delegation, not doing everything. [L1514, 2021-22] **
- Never delegate a task you would not do yourself. Only hand off work whose results you can judge. [L1515, 2019]
- Outsource tasks as early as possible when you lack natural strengths for them or do not enjoy them, and create space for others to contribute. Keep the work that fits your strengths for yourself. [L0701, 2015]
- Work that does not come naturally to you or that drains you should be handed to someone else. Every time work must be converted into a form that another person has to handle, value is lost, so fewer conversions give a better return. [L0701, 2015]
- A task the owner keeps postponing or dreads is a sign it should be outsourced, even when it is important to the business. [L1247, 2021] **
- Tasks the owner dislikes or is weak at tend to be avoided and never completed, so they should be outsourced even if they are important, such as content writing for SEO or managing ads. [L1247, 2021] **
- Do not outsource a task only because you dislike it. Some tasks the owner must personally keep, especially those that build key relationships. [L1122, 2016]
- Unpredictable tasks that pop up and need quick handling, such as phone calls and online chats, should be outsourced to protect the owner's focus. [L1247, 2021] **
- Small optimizations such as product data, product descriptions and SEO metadata help but are not big growth levers, so they are good outsourcing candidates for time-consuming, low-return work. [L1247, 2021] **
- Customer service is urgent but usually not important, and it does not grow a store significantly on its own. The owner should keep focus on tasks that drive large gains, such as SEO, email marketing and ads management. [L1247, 2021] **
- Growth increases customer service volume. An owner who handles it personally loses time for growth work, creating a vicious cycle that threatens the business in the long run. [L1247, 2021] **
- Tasks that are neither urgent nor important should not be done by the owner at all. If they are still wanted, someone else should do them, and the owner's focus should stay only on important tasks. [L1247, 2021] **
- When a task needs research, the owner should define exactly what must be found and then hand the research to someone else. Doing the research personally keeps the owner stuck in manual work. [L1247, 2021] **
- Consider a virtual assistant when work-life balance suffers because the business takes more screen time than personal time. Delegation can restore free time for family and friends. [L0765, undated]
- Routine work that keeps getting too little time can be protected: move it into the first two work hours after the daily business and community tasks. Ad creation then gets protected focus time instead of leftover slots. [L1181, 2023]

#### Priority and sequencing

- Outsource the most outsourceable tasks first, build up gradually as the business grows, and keep the tasks that only the owner can do or that carry the highest return and complexity. [L1247, 2021] **
- Outsource the repetitive, low-skill tasks that eat owner time first, such as loading products, order placement and payment processing. Keep in-house the tasks you do best, and any task that depends on supplier relationships or on understanding paid traffic. [L1122, 2016]
- Outsource customer service once order volume exceeds what the owner can answer alone. Keep supplier relationships in-house, since those are central to the business and hard to hand over. [L1279, 2021]
- Prioritize outsourcing customer service within the next six months as a primary candidate for delegation, if you have not already done so. [L0223, 2021] **
- Owners who already outsource should review how they do it, not only look for new tasks to hand off, because the working process around outsourcing determines much of its value. [L0499, 2021] **

### Before you delegate: do the task yourself first

#### The rule

- Do a task yourself before you outsource it, so you know what good results look like. [L0413, 2016]
- Learn each task yourself before you delegate it, so you can judge the finished work. Only outsource fully once you have the skills and can check the quality. [L0519, 2020+]
- Before delegating any task, the owner should first build the skill personally and find out what works. A team member can only follow a method the owner already knows, otherwise they will produce generic output without a clear target. [L1360, undated]
- Only delegate a task after you have learned to do it yourself. Knowing the method lets you judge the output, teach the next person, and cover the work if a team member leaves or raises prices. [L1358, undated]
- Learning a skill yourself first protects the business if a team member leaves or suddenly raises rates. Once you know the method, you can run the task yourself for a short time or teach the next hire. [L1360, undated]
- Perform each day-to-day task yourself until you fully understand it, and only then outsource it. This applies to all routine business tasks, such as customer contact and order handling. [L1447, 2020-26]
- Outsourcing only works when the owner already knows the correct way to do a task. Without first doing the process yourself, you cannot write clear instructions or judge whether the outside person is doing it right. [L0592, 2020-26]
- Delegating a process you have never run yourself is a mistake, because you cannot explain it properly or verify the results. Start with hands-on work and only outsource after the process is proven. [L0592, 2020-26]
- Never outsource paid ads, web design, content, SEO or social posts to outside providers too early. Learn each process first, document it for each supplier, then share the documents with a future team. [L0856, 2024] *(Dated 2024, not older advice.)*

#### The steps

- Three steps to working less and delegating: [L0230, 2019]
  1. Build a documented business system with processes and tools.
  2. Find and hire the right person.
  3. Train thoroughly and let go of day-to-day control.
- Order for scaling a business with a team: [L1359, 2014]
  1. Do the task yourself until you know the steps work and how long it really takes.
  2. Confirm that the task makes money before you scale it.
  3. Document it as an SOP with short, simple steps.
  4. Decide whether the role needs a contractor or an employee, and whether a virtual assistant or an apprentice fits the work.
  5. Train the new person with the skill transfer process so that efficiency is not lost.
  6. Repeat the cycle for each new process as the business grows.
- Outsourcing sequence: [L0827, 2019]
  1. Do the task yourself first.
  2. Eliminate tasks that are not necessary.
  3. Automate what you can, then hire for the rest.
  4. Record your workflow as a screencast so someone else can follow it.
  5. After hiring, follow up with the hire every week.
- Before outsourcing a task: [L1515, 2019]
  1. Do the task yourself until you know exactly how it works.
  2. Remove every unnecessary step, spreadsheet column or report.
  3. Automate or buy software for what remains.
  4. Hire a person only for tasks that cannot be removed or automated, and train them with a screencast of you doing the task.
- Perform each task yourself inside the business until you know how it works and what the correct result looks like. Then: [L0592, 2020-26]
  1. Document every step you take, creating a user guide that works as the instruction manual for the business.
  2. Fill the ready-made templates with your own business information to complete the user guide.
  3. Hand the completed user guide to the person you bring in, so they can run the task based on what already works.
  4. Once the task is delegated, focus your own time on growth or other priorities.

#### What the owner must understand

- You cannot train someone for a task you have not done yourself. Before handing off customer support or order processing, you must be able to explain the likely customer questions, how to respond to them in different situations, and the supplier-specific steps, including who to contact and what to do when an order goes to a different supplier. [L1447, 2020-26]
- Without a basic understanding of what an outsourced person does, you cannot tell whether they are succeeding or doing good work. This applies equally to outsourcing store building or traffic setup, so a minimum base-level understanding of every area is needed before hiring. [L1447, 2020-26]
- A business does not need to be profitable before you start, but the owner must understand what is happening in it. For example, the owner should be able to explain CPM and conversion results when the assistant reports them. [L0519, 2020+]
- Handing over the whole dropshipping business to a virtual assistant without understanding it means you cannot tell whether store design, emails or ad results are good. Always keep enough knowledge to evaluate the work. [L0519, 2020+]
- A beginner should not outsource store creation entirely, because learning to build the store yourself lets you check and teach the assistant later. [L1432, undated]
- Early in a business one person may need to wear all roles, including supplier calls and uploads. Once a process is working, hand specific tasks to others. [L0832, 2019]

### What to delegate first: the order

#### General order

- Recommended delegation order for a running dropshipping business: [L1358, undated]
  1. Product research
  2. Store build
  3. Traffic and ads
  4. Order fulfillment
  5. Customer support
- Delegate in this order as the business grows: [L1360, undated]
  1. Product research: build your own skill first, then hand over the search with your criteria and tools.
  2. Store development: learn store building yourself, then have someone build the store to your structure once you have a product to test.
  3. Traffic and ads: learn the Facebook or TikTok ad strategy yourself, then let a team member prepare creatives and set up campaigns under your monitoring.
  4. Order fulfillment: outsource early, since it is time-consuming and not highly skilled work.
  5. Customer support: outsource early as well, covering email, live chat and social media messages.
- Virtual assistants can take over store setup, Facebook ad management, customer emails and order fulfilment, while the owner checks the finished results. Outsourcing is recommended when there is not enough time to finish tasks on schedule. [L0519, 2020+]
- As a store grows, outsource tasks such as customer service, email marketing and Google Ads so the founder can focus on the highest-impact work, such as Facebook ads and creatives. Founders need to leverage their time once the business reaches a size where doing everything personally no longer scales. [L0639, 2023]
- Outsourcing ads, email and creator scripts to an agency and hiring a virtual assistant can free up the founder's time for other work. This makes sense when customer service and operations are taking many hours per day. [L0694, 2024]
- Outsource repetitive roles such as customer service once they consume daily time. When one person covers customer service, ad creation, finances and fulfillment at the same time, a structured calendar becomes essential to get anything finished. [L1181, 2023]
- Early on, when the owner has limited hours, delegate the most time-consuming tasks first. [L1358, undated]

#### Beginner sequence (as described in the course)

- Typical beginner delegation sequence as described by the speaker: [L1432, undated]
  1. Do the product research yourself and pick the product to test.
  2. Let the assistant build the store and write product descriptions while you learn the store setup.
  3. Have the assistant install apps such as Klaviyo and SMS Bump and implement them, while you understand what each does.
  4. Let the assistant search for product images and videos on Instagram and Facebook.
  5. Once orders arrive, have the assistant learn CJ Dropshipping and handle order fulfillment.
  6. Create a separate support email address and give the assistant direct access to it. For tricky emails, the assistant sends you a screenshot and you dictate the reply, while the assistant sends it.

- Note on sequence: this beginner order keeps product research with the owner, while the general order above delegates research once the owner has built the skill. Both apply at different stages.

#### Early-stage and repetitive-first sequence

- Early-stage sequence for when to outsource: [L1447, 2020-26]
  1. Follow the blueprint.
  2. Start processing orders yourself.
  3. Once you are comfortable with the business and feel qualified to operate it, begin outsourcing.
- Start outsourcing early and in small steps, but only when the business can afford the cost. Build up gradually instead of handing over everything at once. [L1122, 2016]
- Assistants can also take over the owner's personal admin, such as drafting emails or researching documents for a task. Handing these tasks off frees time for the business. [L1431, 2020+]
- At the business-owner stage, delegate personal and university or study tasks as well as business tasks, and hire missing skills such as Google Ads or email marketing into the company rather than relying on outside help. [L1432, undated]
