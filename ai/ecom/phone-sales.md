# Selling by phone

> A short, honest phone call gets more yes answers than an email, from suppliers and from happy customers, so keep a business number and a simple script ready.

All phone lessons are from 2021-22 and were made for local brand suppliers. Our 2026 model (China dropshipping, Shopify, Meta ads) needs supplier calls less, so they are marked as older advice.

## Rules

1. Never put your personal mobile number on your store or give it to suppliers; get a business number instead.
2. You do not need a catchy number: a toll-free style number (800 in the USA, 1300 in Australia) is enough, because you are not running TV ads.
3. Call first and email second, because calls usually get a much higher success rate (older advice, for suppliers).
4. Call suppliers only during their business hours, in their time zone.
5. If a company only has a contact form, send it, wait 24 hours, and call if nobody replied.
6. Keep your introduction short, then stop talking and let the other person answer.
7. Never lie on a call.
8. If someone says no, ask why in a polite way; if they sound unsure, do not push.
9. Make the deal zero risk for the other side, because that is the easiest way to get a yes.
10. After every call, send an email that confirms what you agreed and includes your contact details.
11. Write the result of every call in a spreadsheet, then move to the next call.
12. Do not make the after-sale review call to customers you know are unhappy.

## Steps

1. **Get a business number.** Pick a provider, choose a number and plan, install the app and forward calls to your own phone.
2. **Set up voicemail.** Record a short greeting with your store name that promises a callback. The service tells you when a message arrives, for example by email.
3. **Show the number on your store** so customers can read it or tap it.
4. **Write a script.** It is a guide, not something to read word for word. Hit the same points in your own style, and keep it short.
5. **Plan the next step for each call:** a yes, a callback date, or a polite "maybe later".
6. **Make the calls.** Expect the first to feel rough, the second to be better and the third to feel natural.
7. **Log everything.** Write the outcome and any callback date in your spreadsheet and your calendar, and call back on that day.
8. **After a sale, call customers for a review.** Decide who calls (you, or a helper later). Offer three ways to leave it: tell you on the phone, email you the text, or use a link from your review app. Ask very happy customers for a short video review, and mention your loyalty or referral program if you have one.
9. **Follow up.** Track who you called, who agreed to leave a review, and who has not done it yet, and remind them.

## Key numbers

- **Review rate without calls:** very few new store owners get more than 10% of customers to leave a review.
- **Review rate with after-sale calls:** up to 50% or more has been seen with this process.
- **Unhappy customers:** usually 95% or more are not unhappy, so you can safely call almost any buyer.
- **Time per review call:** about 10 minutes.
- **Business number cost:** about $10 a month for a local toll-free style number in Australia; about $20 a month for a provider with numbers in over 70 countries, useful if you live in one country and sell in another (2021-22 prices).
- **Callback after "review in three months":** note it and really call back after three months (older advice).
- **Delayed start offer:** if a supplier takes no new online stores, you can offer to sign up now but start selling in about a month (older advice).

## Common mistakes

- Using your private phone number, which looks unprofessional and mixes business and private calls.
- Talking too much on a call instead of letting the other person speak.
- Taking a no without asking why, or pushing someone who is still unsure.
- Promising a callback and then forgetting it because it was not in the calendar.

## FAQ

**Do I need a phone number at all?** It helps. Customers who call your sales or service team often buy at a high rate, so a visible number can earn sales.

**Can I use my own mobile?** Use a business number that forwards to your mobile. Then you know which calls are business calls.

**What if I miss a call?** Voicemail plays your greeting, you get a notice, and you call back as soon as you can.

**I live in one country and sell in another. What now?** Get a local number in the selling country through an app, and answer it from anywhere.

**How do I call suppliers abroad cheaply?** Buy a local number for that country in a calling app and call from it (older advice).

**I am nervous. Is that normal?** Yes. One coach said almost all of 400+ students found supplier calls went better than they expected.

**What do I say first?** Your name, your store, that you like their products, and that you want to talk about selling them. Then stop.

**Should I say "dropshipping" to a supplier?** One older lesson avoided the word and explained the model instead: you run the store and marketing, pay up front for each order, and they ship to the customer. Explain it honestly either way.

**What if their first order is too big for my budget?** Say it does not fit your launch budget, that you would like to work together, and that you will come back after launch.

**Is a review over the phone allowed?** Only write down the customer's real words with their OK. Never make up reviews.

## Checklist

- [ ] Business number set up and forwarding to my phone
- [ ] Voicemail greeting recorded with my store name
- [ ] Number visible on my store
- [ ] Short call script written in my own words
- [ ] Spreadsheet for call results and callback dates
- [ ] Confirmation email sent after each call
- [ ] Review call plan: who calls, which customers, three ways to leave a review
- [ ] Follow-up list for customers who promised a review

## Templates

**Email after a call**
> Hi [name], thanks for your time on the phone today. To confirm: [what we agreed]. Next step: [who does what by when]. You can reach me at [email] or [business number]. Best, [your name], [store]

**Review reminder**
> Hi [name], thanks again for chatting with us about your order. Here is the link to leave your review: [link]. It takes a minute and helps us a lot.

## Where courses disagree

- **Call or email first (2021-22):** one lesson says always call a supplier first, but the same lesson says to send an email or the form first when the site shows no phone number.
- **Who makes the calls (2021-22):** one lesson says a solo founder makes review calls alone, another treats outbound sales calls to other businesses as a task to hand off.
- No 2025-26 lesson covers phone calls, so treat all of this as older advice for our China, Shopify and Meta model.

If you are under 18, Shopify, payments and ad accounts usually need an adult owner, so ask your parents. Calling and recording rules differ, so check the rules in your country.
