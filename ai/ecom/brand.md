# Brand and positioning

> In the test phase, a brand is a clean name, a .com domain, a simple text logo and a few colors; real positioning comes later, once a product sells.

## Rules

1. Pick a name of 5 to 10 letters that is easy to say and spell, and that has a free .com domain.
2. Do not lock the name to one product type, so you can add new products later without renaming.
3. Never use the brand colors, name style or logo of a competitor.
4. Make a text-only logo first; you do not need an icon in the test phase.
5. Use 3 main colors: one primary, one contrast color for buttons, one for small highlights, plus white.
6. Pair fonts: if the logo is sans-serif, use serif headlines, and the other way around.
7. Use the free fonts built into Shopify, not custom fonts you may not have the license for.
8. Spend at most 100 USD on a logo; in the test phase, spend nothing on a creative director and put that money into ads.
9. Write down 3 to 5 reasons why your store is different or better.
10. Never lie: do not claim to be number one or the best quality if you cannot prove it.
11. Keep the word "dropshipping" out of all customer-facing text.
12. Keep logo, colors and tone the same on the store, packaging and every social profile.
13. Keep the brand look consistent, but never let it block what your ads need in the early test phase.

## Steps

1. Ask an AI chat for 10 brand names for your niche. Keep only names with a free .com.
2. Check each name: is the .com free and cheap, is there a trademark with the same name, and does a web search show a bad meaning or slang use?
3. Add the domain to the cart to confirm it is free, then buy it and keep it forever. Under 18, a parent usually has to own the accounts and payments.
4. Write one sentence that names your buyer and the problem your product fixes.
5. Make a quick customer avatar: age, income, where they hang out online, what they want to feel or become.
6. Choose 3 colors that fit your market and note the hex codes.
7. Make a text logo with AI or a cheap freelancer. Get a transparent PNG and, if you can, a vector file.
8. Create a favicon from one part of your logo.
9. Put the logo in the store header, the footer and the checkout.
10. Launch and test products before you polish the brand.
11. When a product wins, do deeper research: read reviews, Reddit threads and competitor ads for at least 3 days.
12. Build 3 to 5 full avatar profiles, list the top 10 objections, and pick one positioning strategy.
13. Write a short brand guide (colors, fonts, logo files, voice) and send it to every designer or editor you hire.

## Key numbers

- 5 to 10 letters: name length so it passes the "say it on the radio" test (2026).
- .com: the ending to choose, not .us or .store (2026).
- 3 colors: primary, button contrast, highlight (2021-22).
- 32 x 32 px: favicon size as PNG (2021-22).
- 400 to 500 px wide, at most 200 px high: logo size for the store header (2021-22).
- Up to 100 USD: logo budget; 20 to 30 USD on a freelance site is common (2021-22).
- 1,000+ reviews at 4.8 stars: a freelancer you can trust; 70 reviews at 4.9 is also fine (2021-22).
- 3 to 5: points that show why you are better (2021-22).
- 2 to 3: things you wish every buyer knew about your product (2026).
- At least 3 days: customer research before you write the real positioning (2026).
- 3 to 5: full avatar profiles for a scaling brand (2026).
- Top 10: customer objections to write down (2026).

## Common mistakes

- Spending weeks on the brand before testing a single product.
- A name like "calmingblankets" that traps you in one product.
- Paying a high price for a premium domain that someone holds only to resell, instead of combining two words into a free name.
- A logo where the icon is bigger than the text, or a busy gaming-style logo that looks childish.
- Using an unlicensed font. Font owners scan websites, and one company was sued.
- Selling the same product as 90 others with no reason to pick you. Then only price is left.

## FAQ

**Does the brand name matter much?**
Less than you think. A free .com matters more than a perfect name (2026).

**Do I need a brand to start?**
No. Start with a simple niche store and test many products. Build the brand around the product that wins.

**Which products can become a brand?**
Products you understand well and can explain. If a product can never grow into a brand, a 2026 lesson says to cut it.

**Do I need an icon in my logo?**
No. A text-only logo is flexible and enough for the test phase.

**Which button color should I use?**
Any color that clearly contrasts with your site. Opposites on the color wheel work, like blue and orange.

**What is a USP?**
Your unique selling point: why a shopper should pick your store over every other one. Say it clearly; a confused visitor does not buy.

**Where do I learn what my customers want?**
Reddit, forums, Facebook groups, Quora, product reviews (yours and competitors') and surveys.

**What is a positioning strategy?**
Choose one per ad idea: a new mechanism, a better product with proof, or identity (speak to who the buyer is).

**When should I make my own product?**
Later. Once the store sells well, you can order a branded or improved version in bulk for a bigger margin.

**Should I customize a product I sell?**
2026 lessons say yes, always. A custom version stands out, and competitors find it harder to report you for copied ads.

## Where courses disagree

- Name: 2021-22 lessons give strict rules (fits the market, unique, real dictionary words). 2026 lessons say the name barely matters if the domains are free.
- Colors: 2021-22 says exactly 3 colors. 2026 AI brand kits produce a 5-color palette.
- Own label: 2021-22 says move to private label once a high-ticket store works (older advice). 2026 says customize every product from the start.
- Consistency: a 2026 lesson says one look across colors, tone and images lifts the brand, but hurts if it gets in the way of ad results early on.
