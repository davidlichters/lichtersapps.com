# Finding a niche and winning products

> Pick products by data, not taste: find something that already sells, check the margin, skip what too many people already run, and test fast and often.

## Rules

1. Treat testing as a volume game: plan one or two new product tests per week and focus on inputs, not quick results.
2. Research at least 10 to 15 product ideas before you narrow down.
3. Only test a product with 70% or more gross margin, so product cost is at most 30% of the selling price.
4. Aim for at least $50 gross margin per order, because less leaves little room for Meta ads.
5. Treat a competitor ad running 7 days or more as a sign the seller makes money or at least breaks even.
6. Skip a product when too many sellers are copying or running it right now.
7. As a beginner, sell one single product, not a bundle.
8. As a beginner, avoid supplements, beauty and pet niches, where big brands spend six or seven figures on ads.
9. Never copy just to copy: know your ideal customer and your selling point before you test.
10. Sell in season: autumn products in summer waste money.
11. Skip products that ad platforms or Shopify forbid, such as weapons, adult items, tobacco, drug equipment or gambling.

## Steps

1. **Collect ideas** from the Meta Ad Library, TikTok Shop data tools and Amazon or eBay bestsellers. Put every candidate in one research sheet.
2. **Filter the ad library** for your target country (for example the US), video ads, local language. Search words spoken in the ad, or the product name, not the long product title.
3. **Skip the first page.** Everyone runs those. Look at page 4 or 5 or further, where winners are still growing and few people run them.
4. **Check the signals:** active ads, recent creatives, several different advertisers. That is enough to test.
5. **Check the competition.** Count sellers. Good-looking stores are real competitors; ugly, scam-looking stores can be ignored.
6. **Read bad reviews.** Filter competitor reviews to 3, 2 and 1 stars to find weak spots you can fix.
7. **Run the margin numbers.** If cost is over 30% of price, raise the price, find a cheaper supplier, or drop it.
8. **Understand the buyer.** Read Amazon reviews and Reddit comments. Note the pain in their own words.
9. **Copy or customize.** A 1:1 copy launches now. A customized version takes about one or two weeks longer but is harder to copy.
10. **Launch and repeat.** Start ads, then move to the next candidate.

## Key numbers

- **70%+ gross margin**: the target for every product you test. 67 to 69% is the lowest you should accept.
- **Cost at most 30%** of the price. 60% is far too high.
- **$50+ margin per order**: a rule of thumb so each sale leaves enough money to pay for Meta ads.
- **7 days**: minimum run time of a competitor ad you treat as proof.
- **1 to 2 products per week**: suggested test pace.
- **About 65 products in 8 months**: what one student tested before a winner reached $200,000 revenue.
- **$20 to $100**: ad money saved by skipping a product that too many people already copy.
- **5 to 7 days**: time to plan for customer research if you need it.
- **Days 15 to 30** of a 90-day plan: the phase for finding a proven product.

## Common mistakes

- Taking two to three weeks to launch one product.
- Picking from page one of a spy tool.
- Starting in a huge niche like supplements. Broad products like gadgets are easier first tests.
- Testing at a price where the margin cannot pay for ads.
- Copying ads without understanding the customer, which means paying for someone else's creative.
- Running a product tied to a big brand or author, which can bring copyright claims and ad or store bans.
- Waiting for the perfect product. There is none. Pick one that meets the rules and move on.

## FAQ

**Is a saturated product bad?** Not by itself: many sellers means money is being made. But if many people copied it recently, skip it.

**Do I need a unique product?** No. Most winners are simple, common and already sold. A better angle or version helps you stand out.

**What makes a winner?** High emotional demand, strong margins and a wide audience. Viral potential, a wow factor, one size for all and a low to moderate price help.

**Which tools do I need?** The free Meta Ad Library plus one product data tool. Keep filters simple.

**Copy or customize?** Copy 1:1 to test fast and customize what works, or customize first to be harder to copy.

**What price?** One that gives you 70%+ margin and $50+ per order. If it does not, raise the price or find a cheaper supplier.

**How long until a winner?** It can take many tests, like 65 products in eight months.

**Can I sell pet products?** Lessons call pet, beauty and supplements hard for beginners. If you do, find a clear angle and a specific buyer group.

**Should I follow seasons?** Yes. Q4 favors gifts and kids' toys.

**How do I learn what buyers want?** Read 1 to 3 star competitor reviews and Reddit comments, and use buyers' exact words in ads.

**I am under 18. Can I do this?** Shopify, payments and ad accounts usually need an adult owner, so a parent must own them. Check the rules in your country for taxes and registration.

- [ ] 10 to 15 ideas in my research sheet
- [ ] Ads running 7+ days from several advertisers
- [ ] Looked past the first result pages
- [ ] Not too many sellers copying it now
- [ ] Gross margin 70%+, cost at most 30%
- [ ] At least $50 margin per order
- [ ] Single product, not a bundle
- [ ] Not forbidden or brand-protected
- [ ] Right season
- [ ] Competitor bad reviews read
- [ ] Ideal customer and selling point written down
- [ ] Next test planned for this week

## Where courses disagree

- **Competitor count:** one 2026 lesson skips a product once two or three competitors run it. Another 2026 lesson wants three to five competitors found before you build the site.
- **Sales trend:** one 2026 lesson filters for rising revenue; another prefers a chart that goes up and down over steady growth.
- **Customize when:** some 2026 lessons say always customize; others say copy first and customize only what works.
- **Older advice (2021 to 2022, high-ticket dropshipping from local suppliers with Google Ads):** average price $800+, 5 to 15 direct competitors per product, 20+ market ideas shortlisted to 5, 10,000+ monthly US searches for the main keyword. Still useful: define your market by the customer, not the product, and decide within days.
