# Offers, pricing and bundles

> Price for a high margin first, then raise order value with simple bundles that you test, and never fake a deal.

## Rules

1. Before you launch any offer, use a profit calculator and set new targets for ROAS and cost per purchase, so you can tell if the offer works.
2. Aim for about 70% margin on each product, and at least 67 to 69% if 70 is not possible (2026).
3. A simple floor from another 2026 lesson: if a product costs you $10 to fulfill, sell it for at least $30, and aim for about $20 margin per sale.
4. Keep the offer simple: buy one get one free, or buy two get one free, is the safe start.
5. For most products, quantity breaks (buy 1, buy 2, buy 3) are enough; "buy X get Y" deals fit supplements best.
6. Every bigger bundle must give a lower price per item than the smaller one, so the saving is easy to see.
7. Do not put free shipping on the first (single item) offer; put it on the biggest bundle, where order value is highest.
8. Set a free shipping or discount threshold above the single item price, so buying two unlocks it.
9. Always A/B test offers and check two numbers: average order value (AOV) and conversion rate.
10. If your margin is tight, do not give an automatic 5% discount, and do not run a BOGO without adjusting your ad cost targets first.
11. Only use real deadlines and real scarcity; a sale banner that never ends looks fake and trains people to wait.

## Steps

1. Ask your supplier for the cost of 1, 2 and 3 units shipped to the US (in one example $8 for one, $12 for two).
2. Price each quantity so every bundle stays near 70% margin.
3. Build the tiers in a bundle app: buy 1 at full price, buy 2 at a fixed price, buy 3 at a lower price per item.
4. Badge the middle tier "most popular" and the top tier "best value"; you may preselect the middle tier.
5. Add a free bonus (for example a short ebook of 15 to 25 pages you wrote for your niche) to the buy 2 and buy 3 tiers, and give each bundle a name.
6. Add free shipping to the top tier if your margin allows it.
7. Set up a post-purchase upsell: offer the same product again at a discount right after checkout.
8. Run the offer, then compare AOV and conversion rate against your old offer and stay with whichever earns more.

## Common mistakes

- Launching a BOGO without changing your ad cost targets, then losing money all month.
- Free shipping on the single item, so nothing pushes people to buy two.
- Bigger bundles that are not cheaper per item.
- Showing so many options that buyers freeze.
- A permanent discount on the product page. If you discount every sale, you cut your own margin.
- Offering a discount in the first abandoned cart email. Wait until people have not bought for two to three days.
- Running the same big offer again and again. One brand's BOGO did great the first time and flopped the second.

## FAQ

**Can I extend my Black Friday sale?** Yes, keep it unchanged. Do not give a deeper discount than early buyers got.

**How big should my Christmas offer be?** Smaller than Black Friday. Drop to about 20% and add a new gift to keep momentum.

**Is buy one get one bad for gift buyers?** People who want just one present may not want two. For Christmas, use gifting language, or try a percent discount on the second item.

**Free or paid gift wrapping?** One 2026 coach said charge $5 to $10 at checkout. Another 2026 lesson offers it free in Q4.

**When should Q4 offers start?** Start with a smaller offer and build up, so your best offer lands on Black Friday. Or switch to gift messaging in early November without a discount.

**My customers face too many options. What now?** Sell the most popular picks as a ready set, or make a small sampler kit for people who do not know what they need.

**My margin is too low. What can I do?** Add a premium bundle with accessories and give it a special name. An older Q&A (2021-22) said this can add $100 to $200 profit per bundle sold, even if only half of buyers take it.

**Should I take a deposit for custom orders?** Older advice (2021-22): around 20% feels safe to buyers, 50 to 60% scares them. Test a few options.

**Traffic is good but sales are low. What first?** Check your offer against competitors. It does not need to be perfect, it just has to beat what they offer.

**How often can I repeat an offer?** About once a year, not much more often.

## Checklist

- [ ] Supplier cost for 1, 2 and 3 units known
- [ ] Every tier priced near 70% margin
- [ ] Buy 2 and buy 3 cheaper per item than buy 1
- [ ] Bonus added to buy 2 and buy 3
- [ ] Free shipping only on the top tier or above a threshold
- [ ] Post-purchase upsell set up
- [ ] ROAS and cost per purchase targets updated for this offer
- [ ] No fake timers, no endless sale banners
- [ ] A/B test running, AOV and conversion rate tracked

## Where courses disagree

- **Margin:** 2026 lessons aim for about 70% margin. A 2021-22 high-ticket lesson says dropshipping usually lands at 20 to 30% (older advice, different model).
- **Free shipping:** one 2026 lesson keeps it only for the biggest bundle. Another 2026 lesson always offers free express shipping and builds the cost into the bundle price.
- **Dollar vs percent off:** some say use dollar off when the discount is over $100. A 2026 lesson says "save $X" works best on higher priced products and less well on cheap ones.
- **Timers:** a 2026 coach notes many brands reset countdown timers daily. A 2021-22 lesson calls such scarcity fake. Fake timers break shop and ad rules, so do not use them.
