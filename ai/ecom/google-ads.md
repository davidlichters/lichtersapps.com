# Google Ads and Shopping

> Google catches people who already search for your product, so add it after Meta works: clean feed, search-friendly titles, careful negatives, bids from your break-even numbers.

## Rules

1. In our 2026 model Meta ads come first; Google Ads works best for the middle and bottom of the funnel (people ready to buy), not for cold audiences.
2. In Shopping campaigns you do not bid on keywords: your product title is the keyword, and the first 25 to 35 characters are what shoppers see on mobile.
3. Every detail in Merchant Center (shipping, prices, tax, business name, phone, address) must match your store exactly, or the account gets suspended.
4. Add negative keywords at most twice a week, and read each search term before you block it, because a wrong negative silently blocks buyers.
5. Do not change product IDs once ads run, because Google then treats the product as new and you lose its history.
6. Turn location targeting to "Presence" for every new campaign, so a country campaign does not pay for clicks from other countries.
7. Base every bid on your target cost per sale and target ROAS; without them you cannot know where to bid.
8. Never let an AI or agent change bids and budgets on its own; one wrong change at scale burns a lot of money (2026).
9. Use a daily budget you can afford to lose for a day or two.

## Steps

1. Get Meta profitable first, then set up Google as the second channel.
2. Create a Google Ads account in Expert Mode; billing country and currency cannot be changed later, so match your business and Shopify.
3. In Merchant Center, claim your domain, verify your phone (voice call beats SMS) and add the same address as your website.
4. Set shipping in Merchant Center: real handling and transit times, a link to your shipping policy page, and costs that match checkout exactly.
5. Send products with a feed app, never by hand, and never run two feeds for the same products.
6. Fix the feed: square white images, a Google product category per product, barcode (GTIN) only if one exists.
7. Write titles for search: product type plus strongest keyword plus one key difference first, then spec or material, then use case. Keep the best words inside 70 to 80 characters.
8. Link Merchant Center to Google Ads and add conversion tracking. Wait for the feed to sync (up to 10 hours).
9. Build a starting negative keyword list before launch (free, cheap, jobs, wholesale, unrelated uses).
10. Launch, then review search terms every few days and move money to products above your target ROAS.
11. Later, add search ads for your own brand name and for very specific product searches you saw in the Shopping reports.

## Key numbers

- 150 characters: title length limit; first 25 to 35 count most, second zone runs to about 70 to 80 (2026).
- 15 headlines of 30 characters and 4 descriptions of 90 characters: responsive search ad limits (2026). No "!" in headlines, one per description.
- About 2/3 keyword headlines, 1/3 offer headlines in a responsive search ad (2026).
- Ad Rank = Quality Score x bid; good ad groups reach 9 or 10 out of 10 (2026).
- $1,500 to $2,500 per month: typical Google agency fee; scaling tests should start within 90 days (2026).
- 800 x 800 px square images on white; never under 600 x 600 (older advice).
- At least $20 per day to start; $1,000 per month is about $30 per day; $2 per day gives almost no data (older advice).
- Starting bids $0.30 / $0.60 / $1.20 for low, medium and high value searches; search text ads rarely below $0.50 per click (older advice).
- About 75% of searches are low value and only about 10% high value (older advice).
- 2% click rate is good for Shopping on expensive products; 0.14% is very low (older advice).
- Search impression share in the mid to high 90s means there is little left to win; 40% means room to grow.

## Common mistakes

- Putting size in the title, so shoppers skip it before clicking.
- Letting a campaign sit at "Limited by budget"; raise the budget if profitable, otherwise lower bids.
- Spending hours in the ad account every day. Fix the 1 or 2 worst ad groups (80/20) and leave the rest.
- Listing banned products (adult, weapons, vehicles); that suspension is very hard to undo.

## FAQ

**Should I start with Google or Meta?** In our 2026 model, Meta. One 2026 coach said you can launch ten campaigns at once on Facebook, but not easily on Google.

**My Merchant Center got suspended. Now what?** Do not panic. Check every detail against your store, then send one calm, detailed appeal.

**Do yellow warnings stop my ads?** No. Only red disapprovals do; yellow ones are worth fixing but are not urgent.

**Should I hire a Google agency?** Only with a clear goal. Fees run $1,500 to $2,500 a month, and proving real extra profit can be hard.

**Can AI run my account?** Use AI for search term sorting, negative lists and ad text drafts, but you make every change yourself.

**Is a competitor cheaper?** Shopping is a price comparison. Bidding higher will not fix a worse price; fix the offer.

**How often should I check?** Search terms twice a week at most; working bids can stay for weeks.

**Should I bid on my own brand name?** Yes, once people know you. Test it with about $10 a day.

**Will Shopping sell a brand-new brand?** Only through product searches; nobody searches a new brand name yet.

**My cost per click jumped. Why?** Check for "Limited by budget" and for negatives missing in some campaigns.

## Where courses disagree

- Responsive search ads: 2021-22 said avoid them; 2026 lessons build on them.
- Brand in the title: 2021-22 said use the maker's name first; 2026 says start with the words buyers type and put the brand last, except in brand campaigns.
- Offers in titles: 2021-22 said never; one 2026 lesson says a short offer after the keywords is worth a test, but Google's rules say titles must not be promotional, so we follow Google.
- Transit time: 2021-22 advised 1 to 7 days; China shipping takes longer, so state the real time.
- Main channel: 2021-22 put Google Shopping first for expensive products; our 2026 model puts Meta first.
