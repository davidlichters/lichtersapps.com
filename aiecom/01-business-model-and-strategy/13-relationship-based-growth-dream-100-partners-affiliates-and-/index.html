1.13 Relationship-based growth: Dream 100, partners, affiliates and influencers

Why relationship-based growth works

  • In any established market, established players already control customer attention. Build relationships with these authorities and leverage their credibility rather than building all your trust independently from scratch. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0297.001]
  • Customer attention directly drives revenue. Whoever has the most attention in a market can command the highest prices and attract the most sales. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0297.002]
  • Relationship-based growth is a distinct channel from direct marketing methods such as Google Ads, social media ads and email marketing, but it is equally powerful for customer acquisition. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0297.003]
  • Most e-commerce businesses, especially high-ticket dropshipping businesses, operate in market isolation and miss the partnership and collaboration opportunities available within their industry. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0297.005]
  • Established figures in your market are willing to collaborate in mutually beneficial partnerships and share audience access, because both parties gain value from the relationship. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0297.006]
  • Relationship-based business growth is a traditional, centuries-proven method that has fueled successful enterprises. It remains underutilized in modern e-commerce despite its effectiveness. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0297.008]
  • High-ticket dropshippers usually enter established markets rather than creating them. The brands or products may have existed online or offline for 10 to 40 years or more, so influential voices and audiences that solve the same problems already exist to partner with. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0412.005]

Transferred influence and intent-based traffic

  • Transferred influence means borrowing the credibility and existing audience of people or entities that already have influence in your niche, so they send traffic to your store. It adds a traffic source on top of search traffic, where shoppers look for your products, brand or SKU on Google or Bing. [L0528.001]
  • Intent-based traffic comes from shoppers who already search for a product or brand. Partner-based traffic reaches people through trusted third parties where they already spend time. Both sources complement each other. [L0528.002]
  • The top-ranked partner in a niche is usually a long-term target rather than a first contact. Outreach starts with the many smaller partners, because the goal is many relationships, not one big deal. [L0528.003]

Dream 100 outreach and engagement

Principles

  • Before you ask a high-value prospect for a partnership or sale, build familiarity first by engaging with their content over time. Direct cold outreach with a sales pitch tends to trigger a spam reaction and put people off. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0453.001]
  • Engagement works because an audience-facing person notices and remembers those who interact with them repeatedly. When you later reach out directly, the contact feels natural rather than like a stranger's cold message. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0453.002]
  • Engagement in this phase should give value and ask questions, not request anything. Do not ask for a sale or partnership yet. Help the prospect's audience where you can and show real interest in their work. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0453.014]
  • Treat relationship building like a normal conversation with a new acquaintance. Learn what they do and what they are interested in, and engage with that. Stay genuine and do not pretend to be someone you are not. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0453.015]
  • Dream 100 partnerships must give both sides a benefit, because a relationship that only serves one party tends to end. Frame every outreach as a win-win offer rather than a request for help. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0525.001]
  • Use the Dream 100 exercise for high-value targets. Use the affiliate recruitment email template for general affiliate recruitment searches outside your Dream 100 list. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0212.008]
  • Use the same outreach message whether you contact a potential affiliate by email or on social media (Facebook, Instagram and similar). The message content stays consistent across all contact channels. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0212.010]

Step-by-step engagement

Procedure (older advice, 2021-22):

  1. Find your Dream 100 prospects and follow, subscribe to, or join the email list of each one.
  2. Engage with their content on the channels where they are active: Facebook groups and forums, YouTube, Instagram, blogs and email.
  3. Keep this up over time. Only after familiarity has built should you contact them directly about working together.

[L0453.003]

Channel-by-channel tactics

Facebook groups and forums (older advice, 2021-22)

  • Engage by answering members' questions with useful, experience-based information from your market. Do not promote your product in replies, because promotional answers usually get you removed from the group. [L0453.005]
  • Group and forum owners want questions answered without having to do it themselves. Consistent helpful replies are visible to admins, who can track the most engaged members, so this builds goodwill with the people who run the community. [L0453.006]
  • At this stage, do not contact the owner or admin. Your target is regular, helpful participation in the community itself. [L0453.007]
  • Facebook setup: keep the personal profile and the business page separate. The personal profile follows Dream 100 contacts, joins their groups and forums, and handles relationship-building. The business Facebook page is used only to interact with other pages you run. Groups that refuse page accounts will block a page that tries to join, so the page should never be the one that joins. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0526.012]

YouTube (older advice, 2021-22)

  • When a prospect you follow publishes a new video, watch it and leave a comment. Either say what you liked about it, or ask a genuine question if the video taught you something and left you with one. [L0453.008]

Instagram (older advice, 2021-22)

  • Check your feed for posts from your Dream 100 prospects from the last couple of days and comment on them. Say what you like, ask a question, or offer a relevant suggestion. You do not need to react to every single post. [L0453.009]

YouTube and Instagram algorithms (older advice, 2021-22)

  • These algorithms give more reach to content that gets more engagement. Creators therefore value regular, genuine comments from people who engage with them, which is why consistent commenting builds goodwill. [L0453.010]

Blogs and websites (older advice, 2021-22)

  • When a prospect publishes a new post, read it and leave a comment. Say what you found interesting, add your own view, or ask a question that invites the author to reply and continue the conversation. [L0453.011]

Email (older advice, 2021-22)

  • Reply to the newsletters and emails your prospects send you. Thank them, say what you liked, or ask a question. Always sign with your name so they begin to recognise it. [L0453.012]
  • Email is usually the best long-term channel for ongoing contact with a prospect, since prospects send newsletters to subscribers and replies keep the relationship active. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0453.013]

Respectful disagreement (older advice, 2021-22)

  • When a prospect publishes something you disagree with, you may give respectful, constructive feedback in a comment. Thank them for the content and say what was good. Then explain clearly why you think a specific point is wrong, since most audience-building creators appreciate this. [L0453.018]

Working a prospect toward partnership

  • Most Dream 100 prospects already earn from their audience through coaching, advertising on their site or forum, or their own products that do not compete with yours. If a prospect does not monetize, helping them grow their audience is still a valid offer. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0525.002]
  • The list of monetization options is not exhaustive, and there are no fixed boundaries on what a partnership can be. The real task is a conversation about how both businesses can work together for mutual benefit. Any outcome both sides accept is the right one. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0525.003]
  • A single prospect can be worked through several partnership types over time, not only one. Start with one option and add others as the relationship grows. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0525.004]

Joint ventures and joint promotions

  • Joint content production and promotion: create a piece of content together and promote it to both audiences, or have both sides run ads to the same content. Agree in advance on how much ad spend each side will contribute. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0525.007]
  • A joint venture (JV) works with a prospect who sells a complementary product in the same market but is not a direct competitor. Build a deal where customers get a bundle of both products. The bundle does not have to be physically packed together. Promote it to both audiences. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0525.008]
  • Joint ventures cross-pollinate audiences: each side's customers join the other side's list. This is why joint product promotions are attractive to both partners. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0525.009]
  • A small ad purchase can be a starting point. For example, rent ad space for one month. If it works, the relationship can grow into ongoing deals such as sponsored emails or other joint promotions. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0525.031]

Affiliate programs: why they work

  • Affiliate programs let you leverage other people's audiences to drive sales. You pay commissions only when a sale completes. You never pay for traffic itself, which makes affiliate marketing less risky than paid advertising, where you pay for clicks regardless of outcome. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0213.001]
  • Affiliates generate qualified traffic by recommending your products to an existing audience that already trusts them on a particular topic. Their audience's trust transfers to your brand when they make a recommendation. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0213.003]
  • Affiliates naturally recommend only products that align with their audience, because content creation requires substantial effort and investment. Poor-fit recommendations waste their work, generate no sales or commission, and risk burning the audience trust that is their primary business asset. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0213.019]
  • Affiliate marketing works best as an extra revenue stream added to a dropshipping store, not as a standalone business. [L0359.004]
  • Affiliate marketing is an optional add-on for a store that is already live and profitable. It should not be a requirement before launch or before the first sales. (older advice, 2019) [L1068.010]
  • An older affiliate website with no traffic and no audience is usually better left aside, and a new store should be started with this model. An active site with real traffic, for example 1,000 visitors a day, can be extended with the model. (older advice, 2019) [L1068.011]
  • Affiliate programs suit stores that already do roughly $1 million or more in annual revenue, as a way to grow from about $1 million to $2 million. They are not a launch tactic for a new store. Refersion is the platform recommended for them. (older advice, 2022) [L1281.027]

Economics and incentive design

  • The 25% commission rate is positioned as an investment in the business, not a cost. Paying high commissions for member referrals aligns the company's goal, helping people build real businesses, with member incentives to grow the community. [L0214.009]
  • Case: A member referred a friend to E-Converts and received a $1,000 referral commission. He invested the entire amount as startup capital for a dropshipping store and is now generating approximately $100,000 in monthly revenue. [L0214.006]

Scope rules

  • With affiliate sales, the customer buys from the brand, so you do not own the customer and cannot market to them later. [L0855.074]
  • Affiliate promotions can run on a drop-ship store only for complementary products that do not compete with your own supplier products. Affiliate items earn a much smaller margin and cannot be advertised with the same Google Shopping campaigns as your drop-ship products. (older advice, 2021) [L0834.111]
  • Google Shopping ads cannot promote products you do not sell directly, so affiliate products cannot be used in Shopping campaigns. [L0855.075]
  • Affiliate offers are only worth adding for complementary products you do not sell, such as online lessons for products sold in the store. They should never be a competing product from the same niche. [L0855.077]
  • Shopify stores can carry affiliate deals in two ways: promoting affiliate offers on the store, and partnering with affiliates who send traffic. Both add income beyond product margin. (older advice, 2020) [L0852.114]
  • Affiliate cross promotion is useful when a brand will not approve you as a dropship supplier. An example is a monitor brand that sells to a standing desk store's customers. Affiliate payouts are lower than dropship margins, so use this route for the accessories and upgrades customers still want. [L0923.018]

Procedure: Amazon affiliate store on a WordPress theme

  1. Sign up for the Amazon Associates affiliate program and copy your unique tracking ID.
  2. Buy and install the Shopper Press WordPress theme.
  3. In the WordPress admin dashboard, enter your Amazon Associates information and tracking ID in Shopper Press.
  4. Choose the Amazon categories you want to sell, for example guitar and its subcategories.
  5. Let Shopper Press import the products, including images, prices and descriptions.
  6. Match the theme branding to your dropship store, so customers feel they are still on the same brand.
  7. Check that the add to cart button sends customers to the Amazon cart with your tracking ID attached.

[L0923.020]

Creators testing products

  • Some creators test many products on their own accounts and earn affiliate commissions from the ones that take off. This works only when the test content is cheap to produce and the affiliate program pays for the sales. [L1186.013]

Recruiting affiliates

  • Affiliates do not independently discover or approach ecommerce sites. Successful affiliate programs require active recruitment, where you identify potential partners and pitch the program to them. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0215.001]
  • The Dream 100 exercise is the primary method for finding affiliates, because it naturally surfaces partnership opportunities during outreach conversations. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0215.002]
  • Warrior Forum and JVZoo are job-board platforms where affiliate offers can be posted. These platforms are most effective for limited-time product launches rather than for establishing ongoing affiliate partnerships in traditional high-ticket dropshipping. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0215.009]
  • When searching YouTube for affiliates in your product category, examine the video descriptions and check the links for affiliate URL parameters. YouTube creators embed affiliate links in their video descriptions. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0212.011]

Influencer partnerships

Scaling

  • Once one influencer partnership works, scale to several parallel influencer partnerships in different niches or markets. The seller reports working with three influencers at once. [L0789.040]
  • Influencer partnerships can include paid services beyond promotion, such as TikTok organic growth, content creation and product sourcing. Offering these skills to influencers as added value gives the brand a reason for influencers to accept a deal over competing brands. [L0789.060]
  • For a direct-to-consumer brand grown mainly through influencers, about 90% of sales success was attributed to influencer marketing. Influencers were used mostly at the top of the funnel to bring in large volumes of qualified traffic, not only as social proof at the bottom. [L0790.001]

Readiness and budget

  • Before paying for influencer traffic, set up and test the full sales funnel: store, email capture, retargeting, branded search ads and SEO. Without it, influencer spending is largely wasted. [L0790.004]
  • Influencer marketing does not produce quick results. Expect the first year to be a costly testing phase in which some spending does not pay back, and plan the budget for that. [L0790.007]
  • Spending very large sums, such as around $100,000 on one big-name creator, is risky without an exceptional and hard-to-find product. Social proof alone rarely justifies it. [L1379.022]

Product fit by platform

  • Some products do not scale profitably through paid ads. For those, influencer campaigns may be the main way to keep sales going. [L1379.019]
  • Dropshipped products often convert poorly through Instagram influencers once buyers realize they are dropshipped. Products from a real small brand that makes its own goods tend to convert better on Instagram. [L1379.020]
  • TikTok influencer audiences appeared to buy more often than Instagram audiences for dropshipping products. The speaker presented this as an observation, not a proven rule. [L1379.021]

Creator whitelisting and owned characters

  • Paid creator whitelisting means running ads from a real creator's account. It requires a revenue share and negotiation. A self-owned fictional character avoids that cost and dependency, so it suits testing or a new brand with a small budget. Creator whitelisting is worth considering when more money is available. [L1476.002]

Business partners and partnership stores

Whether to take a partner

  • A business partnership works only when both partners are committed to a bigger vision and have the time to work on it, so that the combined result exceeds what each could do alone. Two partners who each want to work about 10 hours per week in a lifestyle business, often in different time zones, usually do not benefit from a partnership. [L0413.004]
  • A business partner should bring more value than hours and have skills that complement yours. For example, one partner may be strong at supplier approvals, phone calls and closing deals, while the other builds websites that convert. [L0413.005]
  • Choose a partner who has both succeeded and failed in other ventures. A partner who has only seen success may give up when sales fall short of expectations. [L0413.006]
  • Business partnerships rarely suit a new dropshipping store unless a partner brings a large network or a rare, specific skill set. A 50/50 partner takes half the profit forever and half the business value on a sale, while outsourcing a task is paid once. (Source A, 2025) [L1097.001]
  • A business partnership is worth considering mainly when the partner brings a large existing network or a rare, specific skill set, or when the partner is a spouse in a close family arrangement. Otherwise, paying an outside provider for a specific task is usually cheaper than giving a partner a permanent share of profit and of the business's sale value. (Source A, 2025) [L1097.044]

Procedure: agree before starting a business partnership (in writing)

  1. Each partner's exact role and responsibilities, so no two people do the same work.
  2. A shared long-term vision and goals. Agree on these before starting, because a partnership is very hard to exit once the business is successful.

[L0413.007]

Partnership stores (separate projects)

  • An experienced store owner can grow income by partnering with trusted people from their own circle who are interested in the model. The owner oversees operations while each partner does their assigned part. The partnership store is a separate business from the owner's main store. [L1125.001]
  • A done-for-you web design service delivers a fully finished, ready-to-sell store. It can be used to launch partnership stores quickly without building them from scratch. [L1125.003]
  • In a partnership store, do not give the partner equity or any share of your main business. You are only a co-owner of the new, separate store. [L1125.004]
  • Do not bring a partner into your main income source. A split main business with a partner who is not pulling their weight is a much bigger problem than a separate project. Partnership stores work best as future projects. [L1125.006]
  • Spreading income across several partnership stores diversifies risk. After three or four such stores, they may even earn more than the main store, so you are not locked into a single business partnership for your lifestyle income. [L1125.007]

Procedure: partnership model for additional stores

  1. Make sure your first store is already built, has suppliers and traffic set up, and is generating the income you need.
  2. Confirm you understand the model and the process well enough to run it yourself.
  3. Identify people you know and trust who are interested in starting a business with you.
  4. Choose a new, separate store project for the partnership. Do not use your main business for this.
  5. Have the store delivered through a done-for-you web design service, or have your team build it.
  6. Give the partner their part of the work while you oversee the operations.
  7. Share ownership of the separate store, so both of you earn from it and both receive a payout if it is sold.

[L1125.002]

Case: partnership stores after a first exit

  • A store owner first built a fully owned store and sold it for a large lump sum. He then invited applicants from his own audience to join him, gathered a group in one location, and helped them build stores together as partnerships. He earned from those stores and later sold them as well. [L1125.008]

Case: consultant turned partner

  • A dropshipping store was built with consulting help from the start and reached about 250,000 USD in sales in its first three months. The consultant stayed on for the first year, then became a partner to scale the business. In the second year of that partnership, annual revenue reached about 11 million USD before the partnership ended. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1502.006]

Brand, community and cause deals

  • Brand deals with complementary companies, such as breweries or tourism operators, become easy once the audience, email list and followers trust the brand. Such companies often approach the brand. The brand should only accept partnerships that suit its community. [L0487.017]
  • Brand partnerships on Karo can complement a high-ticket dropshipping store by filling gaps in the catalogue with products you cannot source from a supplier. This adds sales opportunities without inventory risk or fulfillment work. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0869.009]
  • Do not use Karo's brand partnership side as a first step if the store is new, because its benefits require sales history and an existing customer base. Start with the free influencer side to test the account. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0869.014]
  • A cause-linked model donates one product (for example one pair of reading glasses) for each item sold, through a partner organization. A small per-sale donation is affordable and gives customers and influencers a real reason to support the brand. [L0790.002]
  • Hosting community events, such as digital nomad conferences held in several countries, can grow a business and its network at the same time. The largest event in the lesson had about 400 entrepreneurs attending. The series ran seven events across Thailand, Las Vegas and Mexico before COVID ended it. [L0863.010]
