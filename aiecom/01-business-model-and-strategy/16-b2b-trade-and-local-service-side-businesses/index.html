1.16 B2B, trade and local-service side businesses

Sequencing income from expertise

  • Expertise can be turned into income in stages: first sell hands-on work to other store owners, then one-on-one coaching, and only later a structured online course. A course becomes worth building when the same questions and problems keep coming up across many clients. [L1014.021]
  • A dropshipping business and a service business can work together as cash flow and as a learning source while you work toward a long-term goal. Income covers living costs, and education builds the skills needed for the bigger vision. [L0519.012]
  • Running an e-commerce store trains marketing, logistics, customer service, project management and web design at the same time. These skills can be sold as a service, such as a copywriting, video editing or web design agency, based on the part of the business you enjoy most. [L0819.034]
  • Skills gained from dropshipping, including building websites and online stores, running ads, copywriting, creating offers and handling customer service, can be sold as services to other businesses. This is a route to building an agency alongside or after a store. [L1185.035]
  • Running a service agency alongside a dropshipping business lets you learn from a second income stream while testing other people's products. For example, one operator dropshipped a Swiss Invisalign offer by running ads for a dentist while growing his own brands. [L0440.012]

Setting boundaries and contract rules

  • Before a sale starts, write down what you will and will not do during the sale and after it. Also note what you expect from the buyer, what they expect from you, and how you will meet each expectation. [L0378.001]
  • Clear boundaries let a seller earn well from limited hours and keep time for a personal life. Income depends heavily on how much work you accept, so breaking your own limits to close a deal usually costs more later. [L0378.002]
  • Accept outside client work only with a minimum upfront fee of $25,000. Higher fees tend to filter out difficult clients and limit time taken away from your own products. [L0378.006]
  • Take on consulting or service work only when the client can realistically earn back significantly more than the fee. You should also like the product or niche you are working on. [L0378.007]
  • Ask prospective clients whether they care about how much effort you put in or only about the result. Clients who only care about the result fit performance-based pay, and you should only accept deals where you are paid when you win. [L0378.008]
  • Pricing and contract structure are among the main levers for earning more while working fewer hours. Plan them before you start selling, not after. [L0378.012]

Choosing a local service agency

  • A dropshipping operator with little cash can fund the business by selling services to local businesses, such as websites, online stores and social media setup, and reinvest the fees into ad budgets. The operator's existing dropshipping and paid-ads knowledge is the core asset of that service business. [L0440.001]
  • When cash is short, local businesses in your city can be helped to sell products with a web store. This monetizes dropshipping knowledge as a paid service instead of stopping the business. [L0440.011]
  • Offer local clients a small set of clear services: a social media presence, a website, and an online store. Once the client is satisfied with the setup, upsell paid ad management for them. [L0440.002]
  • A website is useful to a client who only wants to communicate their brand, while an online store fits a client who wants to sell online but lacks the infrastructure or time. Position the offer around these two needs to make the sales conversation easier. [L0440.003]
  • Start a local agency by choosing one city and focusing on clients there before expanding abroad. Expansion to countries such as Canada and Australia was planned only after the home city was established, and a remote cold-caller was considered for time-zone differences. [L0440.013]
  • Doing a first project for free for a friend, family member or early contact builds a portfolio and proves quality. Early free work can then lead to referrals that generate the first paid clients. [L0440.004]

Building websites and stores for clients

Tool choice

  • For a simple client who needs only text and pictures, use Squarespace, which offers attractive designs at a fair price and lets the client edit the site themselves. Use Wix when the client needs more custom layout, such as text positioned in specific places or embedded video. [L0440.007]
  • Choose a drag-and-drop site builder such as Squarespace or Wix for service websites, and use Shopify for any client who needs to sell products online. [L0440.008]

  • Shopify store builds for clients in one approach were priced between about $2,000 and $4,000, with the cheapest seen at $200 and the most expensive at $5,000. Let the price follow the client's budget, so check how much they are willing to pay before quoting. [L1017.037]

  • Photographers and videographers can be hired for a client shoot. Their cost is passed on to the client on the final bill rather than paid out of the operator's own pocket. [L0440.006]

Pricing ad management for local clients

  • After a two-week ad test shows results for a local client, the agency can charge a monthly fee for managing the client's Facebook ads, in a range of $2,000 to $5,000 per month depending on the client's size. The fee is discussed when the operator visits the client's office to review the results. [L0440.064]

Note: The $25,000 minimum upfront fee [L0378.006] applies to outside client work in general. The local ad-management range above [L0440.064] is much lower and applies to local retainers. Use whichever figure matches the engagement.

Building a sales team and reselling skills

  • Freelance sales partners can be recruited locally, trained on how to acquire customers, and paid a commission for every client they bring in. [L0440.014]
  • Pay commissions to a client-referral partner, and let referred clients pay the agency's virtual assistants through the agency website, with the agency keeping a commission on each engagement. [L0440.015]
  • The agency model can grow by recruiting and training freelancers in the operator's country and paying them per client. This lets the operator scale the sales function without doing every call personally. [L0440.061]
  • Skills such as writing product descriptions, blog posts and ad copy with ChatGPT can be sold as a copywriting or freelance service to local businesses as an additional income source. [L0549.015]
  • Any skill you lack can be sourced from Fiverr or Upwork and resold to clients. For example, a store owner resold a service to a local dentist by having a Fiverr freelancer produce tooth animations, adding a markup and delivering the result to the client. [L1431.066]

Combining services with physical products

  • A service business can be combined with physical products on one store. For example, sell an installation service locally, subcontract jobs in other areas, and sell the product to buyers who want to install it themselves. [L1086.052] (source 2019, older)

B2B fundamentals

  • The full process of building a store applies to business-to-business (B2B) niches just as it does to business-to-consumer (B2C) niches. Examples of B2B products include safety cones, bags of cement and commercial refrigerators. [L0769.005]
  • B2B orders are usually much larger than B2C orders, so the extra customer service effort they require is generally worth it. [L0769.007]
  • B2B and B2C both produce successful stores, and no clear rule shows that one always earns more. Income depends on the whole plan, including niche, supplier, ad optimization and execution. The niche is only one part of it. [L0961.011] (source 2020, older)
  • B2B buyers rarely convert through a normal add-to-cart flow. A few large orders a year can matter more to revenue than many small retail sales. [L1109.037] (source 2018, older)
  • Wholesale accounts usually do not fit a dropship store, because a buyer can simply order from the supplier directly. Offering wholesale only makes sense if you private label the products under your own brand or run your own dropship program. [L0968.105] (source 2021, older)
  • B2B can come first. A founder started with a B2B-only website for hotels and boutiques and received two wholesale orders through Instagram, from a tea room and an organic store. The wholesale demand validated the product, and the logos of those accounts can be shown on the website for credibility. [L1016.066]
  • B2B can come first when early orders come from hotels or boutiques, and a clean B2C site can serve as a credibility page for wholesale accounts. B2B can eventually reach 80 to 90 percent of revenue. [L1016.067]

Heavy and high-ticket B2B

  • Sell B2B with heavy products only if the margin covers the shipping cost. Heavy items can be unprofitable at low price points even when margins look fine on paper. [L0671.151] (source 2021, older)
  • Before listing a heavy B2B product, compare the item price with the wholesale cost and the shipping cost. Check whether it still leaves a healthy profit after free shipping. [L0671.152] (source 2021, older)
  • B2B high-ticket sales (for example, about $120,000 to $150,000 per container) are not sold through social ads directly. Generate leads first through an email list or a sales call, qualify them, and then run a longer consultative sales cycle with the economic buyer and decision makers. [L1315.049]

Finding business buyers

  • For B2B sales, LinkedIn advertising is expensive and hard to make profitable. Instead, create a business profile, join relevant associations and groups, and post content on the company page. [L0356.012] (source 2019, older)

  • To find business buyers, one-to-many online methods such as dedicated trade pages, LinkedIn content and group participation reach more buyers with less effort than one-to-one emails or cold calls. The same marketing that works for consumers also applies to B2B. [L0356.065] (source 2019, older)

  • Business buyers find an e-commerce store through the same channels as consumers, such as Google search and remarketing ads, so they need no separate acquisition setup. Add a dedicated trade page only after business inquiries start arriving. [L0972.094] (source 2025)

  • To reach business buyers for a niche product:

    1. Identify online and offline trade publications that already reach the people who buy that product.
    2. Propose a partnership in which the publication promotes your offer and its audience earns a commission on sales.
    3. Expect this to be harder for products that apply to every business, because no single trade audience fits them.

    [L0969.112] (source 2022, older)

  • Paid traffic training applies to service businesses too, with some modifications. A new store should start with a small ad budget to find what works, and scale up only after results show. [L1110.037] (source 2019, older)

  • Government and school vendor registration is handled through the form each agency or body sends. The lesson found nothing beyond completing that form was needed. [L0356.017] (source 2019, older)

Building a To the Trade page

Template for the page (source 2019, older):

Create a separate page on the store titled something like To the Trade. Describe the products for business buyers, state that volume discounts and custom orders are offered, and add a contact form, which Shopify can provide. Link the page in the footer menu, because business buyers often want to contact the seller before ordering in bulk.

[L0356.013]

Procedure for a B2B or trade page (source 2022, older):

  1. Add a short paragraph stating that you work business to business with a specific trade or niche.
  2. Add a contact form with name, email, phone, products of interest, quantity needed and required delivery date.
  3. Show logos of past business customers to add credibility.

[L0803.078]

Procedure for capturing business buyers on a B2C store (source 2021, older):

  1. Add a "To the Trade" page that lists logos of past business customers.
  2. Add a contact form that asks for the quantity needed, the products wanted, and the date they are needed by, so the seller can send a quote.
  3. Expect bulk orders and price them with that in mind.

[L1279.096]

Procedure for a trade or wholesale tab (source 2018, older):

  1. Add a clearly labeled trade or wholesale tab or button to the website.
  2. Show photos of gyms or businesses that already use your product type.
  3. Add a call to action and a simple form that asks about the buyer's needs.
  4. Call the people who submit the form to start the conversation and tailor the order to their needs.

[L1109.036]

Note on placement: The footer link in the 2019 template [L0356.013] and the header link in the 2020 advice [L0961.012] both make the page easy to find. Use the header for business owners who browse the main navigation, and the footer for buyers who look for contact details before ordering in bulk. [L0356.013, L0961.012]

Note on timing: The older advice (2018 to 2022) recommends building the trade page up front to capture bulk buyers [L1109.036, L1279.096, L0803.078, L0356.013]. The 2025 advice waits until business inquiries start arriving [L0972.094]. The older approach fits a store that is actively courting bulk buyers. The 2025 approach fits a store whose main traffic is consumer-driven and that has no inbound trade demand yet. [L0972.094, L0803.078]

Note on B2C stores with business customers: If you sell B2C but also get business customers, keep the store positioned as B2C and add a separate "To the Trade" page for business buyers. Link that page in the header navigation so business owners see it. [L0961.012] (source 2020, older)
