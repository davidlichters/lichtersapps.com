Quick wins on an established store

  • Apply these steps in order to a store that already trades. [L0575.028]
    1. Add abandoned cart emails. [L0575.028]
    2. Add "buy" and "online" to product title tags. [L0575.028]
    3. Make sure Google Shopping feed monitoring is set up daily, and split product types into separate campaigns. [L0575.028]
    4. Negotiate provider discounts (Shopify, payment processor, PayPal) by phone, and review annual billing. [L0575.028]
    5. Rewrite meta descriptions using the rules: capitals, unique per page, sales-letter style, rich snippets, and a phone number where relevant. [L0575.028]
    6. Disable pagination. [L0575.028]
    7. Set up uptime monitoring. [L0575.028]
    8. Validate Google Analytics e-commerce tracking, and remove PayPal Express buttons. [L0575.028]
  • A store doing about $1 million in revenue applied three of these quick wins (titles, Google Shopping structure and meta descriptions among them) and added about $200,000 in revenue within three months. [L0575.029]
  • A separate store cut Shopify costs by $100 per month with one phone call, saving over $1,000 per year. [L0575.029]

Return fees

  • Return fees spread first in the US, then in Australia, and more slowly into the UK and EU. Large fashion retailers such as ASOS and Zara now charge them. [L0955.059]
  • Expect fee-based return policies to become the norm in each market, at a different speed in each. [L0955.059]

Return on investment

  • Example: a $2,000 retreat investment (flights, hotel and ticket combined) should generate $20,000 in annual profit through applied strategies, a 10x return. [L0286.003]

Diagnosing a store that does not make money

  • Older advice (2019): if a store is not making money, find the cause. It is either traffic quality and conversion, or product and supplier quality. [L1068.013]
  • Spending more money on the same setup does not fix it. [L1068.013]
  • Do not close a store before the root cause has been checked. [L1068.013]

Competitive position

  • Older advice (2019): the business is relationship-based, which is a real barrier against copycats. [L1086.051]
  • Supplier relationships, optimized product pages, traffic setup and ad accounts all take work. That work is what separates the stores that win. [L1086.051]

Applying dropshipping methods to other online businesses

  • Human psychology and platform mechanics carry over from dropshipping to other online businesses. [L1251.048]
  • Only the objective changes. For example, a lead-generation campaign books sales calls instead of making a direct product sale. [L1251.048]

Direct mail

  • Direct mail sells very different products with the same method, because buyer reactions stay the same across products. A seller who understands those reactions can apply one approach to any new offer. [L1369.001]
  • Direct mail reaches buyers quickly and at low cost in good and bad markets. It shows early whether a product sells, so a campaign can still run within the selling season. [L1369.002]
  • This only works for products with real merit and enough people who want them or can be made to want them. [L1369.002]
  • Management can check every claim before a direct mail piece goes out, which makes written promises easier to control than a salesman's spoken pitch. [L1369.003]
  • Small mailings let a seller test an idea cheaply before committing to a large budget. [L1369.003]
  • Direct mail can prepare a sales team's prospects before a visit, so salespeople meet buyers who already know the offer. [L1369.004]
  • The same letter technique can help a business sell to its own staff and partners. [L1369.004]

AI-first dropshipping

  • An AI-first dropshipping system is designed for speed and leaner operations across product research, store build and ad creative. This is why its milestones are set aggressively. [L1452.013]
  • The old approach of guessing, testing blindly, or spending weeks building a store is replaced by AI-supported work that speeds up each stage. The goal is faster decisions and less wasted effort. [L1459.002]
  • AI dropshipping can be organized into five areas where AI tools add leverage: [L1459.020]
    • product research [L1459.020]
    • store creation [L1459.020]
    • creatives and content [L1459.020]
    • customer experience [L1459.020]
    • data-based decisions [L1459.020]
  • Each area is a place where manual work or guesswork can be replaced by tools. [L1459.020]

High-ticket dropshipping course: scope and audience

  • Older advice (2021-22): the growth phase applies to an existing business. The build phase applies only to someone starting a new business, so a store already running should focus on growth content. [L1456.034]
  • The course is aimed at people who already run a high-ticket dropshipping business, not at beginners. Learners still in the launch stage are told to start with the build-phase material. [L1495.001]
  • Older advice (2021-22): the program targets stores trading for anywhere from a few months to several years. A store at either end of that range can use the grow-phase material. A store still being set up should begin with the build phase. [L1495.012]
  • Sources differ on the entry point, and both apply in their own case. The course is framed for existing owners [L1495.001]. A beginner only needs to focus on the Build phase at the start [L1496.005]. Existing owners use the course directly, and beginners start with Build. [L1495.001, L1496.005]
  • Training is meant to stay useful well after launch, including one or two years into a running business. A beginner can leave advanced material until the business has grown. [L1496.011]

High-ticket dropshipping course: structure

  • The course is split into a build phase, which covers setting up and launching the business, and a grow phase, which covers scaling traffic and conversion. An existing business owner can skip the build-phase modules. [L1495.002]
  • The Build phase covers building and launching the business. The Grow phase covers marketing and outsourcing after launch. [L1496.005]
  • Older (2021-22): the high-ticket dropshipping process is described as a sequence of five steps: build the business, then launch it, then grow it. The build phase is the first stage. The unit names only build, launch and grow, so the other two steps are not named in this source. [L1490.003]
  • Older (2021-22): in the members area, the Build phase modules appear first, at the top of the page below the welcome video, under a heading called Build Phase. The course has about six Build phase modules, which must be completed in numbered order. [L1496.006]
  • The growth stage consists of about ten modules, each tackling a single dimension of marketing for high-ticket dropshipping. That count keeps rising over time. Named modules: conversion rate optimization, backlink acquisition, content marketing, search engine optimization (SEO), pay-per-click advertising, email marketing and retargeting. [L1495.004]
  • There is no fixed order for working through the grow-phase modules. Start with the weakest area of the business, or with a traffic channel not yet tested. [L1495.005]
