1.10 Traffic and channel strategy

Google First: The Core Channel

  • Rule: Google is the primary traffic source because it is the fastest and most reliable channel. For a dropshipping store it is generally the fastest to produce results, so traffic work starts there. Do not use other platforms until Google traffic is highly profitable. [L0163.001, L0586.001]
  • Principle: Maximize Google traffic before expanding to other channels. All traffic sources funnel visitors to the store as the central hub. Splitting focus across channels too early dilutes results, so squeeze as much volume as possible out of Google first. [L0163.002, L0586.003]
  • Expansion condition: Other traffic platforms can still produce sales, but expanding to them only makes sense once the store is highly profitable and Google ads can no longer be scaled further. [L0586.002]
  • Proven methods first: Use the proven traffic methods first in every country you sell in, not regional shopping comparison engines or local marketplaces. Even where a local platform is popular for e-commerce, the tested system is the default starting point. [L0710.001]
  • Blueprint: The proven traffic blueprint contains roughly 30 lessons. Work through all of them and implement each one exactly as taught, rather than picking a subset, before testing any other channel. [L0710.008]
  • Reason: Finishing the working system first gives it the highest likelihood of success, so it earns the money that funds later experiments. Switching to unproven channels while the existing system works adds risk without a clear gain. [L0710.003]
  • Common error: Jumping to a new channel while the core traffic system is not yet profitable or running splits attention and cash before the business has a proven base. [L0710.004]
  • Google Business Profile: Not necessary for running Google Shopping and Merchant Center campaigns. [L1115.028]
  • Local Inventory: If the business operates physical retail stores alongside online sales, enable Local Inventory in shopping campaign settings. It shows products to customers searching near your stores, combining online and offline sales. For pure dropshipping with no physical stores, this setting does not apply. [L0225.035]
  • Analytics (older advice, 2021-2022): Google Analytics is typically preferred over paid third-party attribution tools, which are expensive but can improve attribution at higher spend levels. [L0721.026]

Channel Overview and Core Traffic Methods

Traffic methods for a Google-first store:

Traffic method Cost Where the store appears Who it targets
Organic Google search Free Unpaid text links in Google results, ideally on page one People searching for a product, brand or SKU
Google product listings Free Product results in the Google Shopping section People browsing products on Google Shopping
Google Shopping ads Paid Top of the Google Shopping section and the front of Google results, showing product image, store name and price People searching for a product who are ready to compare prices
Google remarketing ads Paid ads (ad cost not stated in lesson) Ads shown after a visit, showing the exact product viewed Visitors who left the store without buying

[L0586.004]

  • Traffic for a Shopify store can come from Google Shopping product listing ads, Google search text ads, organic search, Bing ads, paid social on Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest and Twitter, and a Dream 100 source. The list is expected to grow as more channels are added. [L0676.003]
  • A store should use four traffic channels together: free organic search, free Shopping listings, paid Shopping ads, and remarketing. Each covers a different stage of the buyer journey. [L1393.005]
  • Google search is the primary marketing channel for a high-ticket dropshipping store, and paid ads plus organic search make up most of its traffic. Bing and other search engines are much smaller and should not form a significant part of the effort. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L1486.010]
  • A search-driven funnel of Google Shopping ads, organic search, email and retargeting, run without social media ads, is described as capable of growing a single high-ticket store to around $1 million a year in revenue. Other channels can be added later. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L0458.063]
  • A mature business can run four or five profitable traffic streams, such as affiliate marketing, a presence on Amazon, organic search traffic and the own website. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L0439.019]
  • Establishing profitable paid search first gives a strong base for later channels. SEO, social and content tend to perform better when added after paid campaigns already run profitably. [L0463.011]

Platform Choice and Channel Fit

  • Three selection factors: Platform choice depends on beginner level (Meta and TikTok), proof-of-concept status (Google Ads when the product already sells), and expertise in platform mechanics (Pinterest, Twitter/X, influencer marketing). [L0296.029]
  • Message placement: Even a strong product or skilled seller earns little when the offer appears in the wrong place. Put the message where buyers already spend time, both physically and online. [L0378.010]
  • Platform audiences differ: Different platforms reach different audiences, so the same product can perform differently on Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok or Pinterest. Match the product to the platform where its likely buyers spend time. [L0562.009]
  • New sources: New and unique traffic sources are found through market research in your specific industry, not copied from others. Some sources only work for certain niches, so test whether a source fits your customers. [L0413.041]
  • Omnipresence: Attention drives sales, and because customer attention is fragmented and short, a business should aim to be omnipresent across the customer's online experience, so it is repeatedly encountered wherever the customer is. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L0414.005]
  • Omnipresence defined: Being seen wherever your customers spend time online within your niche, so that you are the brand they encounter most often. The goal is to hold a larger share of your market's attention than competitors, because the most frequently seen brand tends to be chosen at the point of purchase. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L1117.001]
  • Why omnipresence: Four reasons support it: customers take a journey before buying, that journey is hard to hold attention through, attention translates into revenue, and a brand that appears more often than competitors gains the edge. Journey length varies by price and type. A low-cost t-shirt needs less consideration than a $3,000 coffee machine. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L1117.002]
  • Six components: The full marketing system is built from six components used together: Know, Convert, Educate, Promote, Develop and Consolidate. Each should make at least one other better, so they must run simultaneously rather than one at a time. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L0414.006]
  • Traditional funnel inputs: Traditional funnels are fed by paid traffic (Google Ads, Bing Ads, paid social), organic search (SEO on Google and Bing) and outreach strategies such as Dream 100. After the sale, a customer can also be pushed to bring in new buyers, which raises the value of each customer. [L0417.002]

Platform Account Setup and Location Rules

  • VPN rules: A VPN is required when creating a TikTok account, because the platform detects location and assigns accounts to that location. For Pinterest, create the account with a US VPN, after which ads can run without the VPN. [L0788.034]
  • Facebook and Google: Facebook Ads do not require a VPN; target the country directly in campaign settings. Google Ads also does not require a VPN, but business details must match the location being targeted. [L0788.035]

Shopping and Search Intent

  • Commercial share of search: Only about 20% of Google searches have commercial, bottom-of-funnel intent. The other roughly 80% are informational or directional. Paid traffic is easiest to scale by targeting buying-intent searches first, because people who already want to buy need less persuasion. [L0510.001]
  • Why Shopping works: Shopping and Product Listing Ads are shown mainly for commercial searches because the platform filters queries by intent, so the advertiser reaches buyers without extra targeting work. [L0510.002]
  • Two sources of buying-stage traffic: Informational searches are reached through content without ads. Commercial searches are reached through product listing ads (Shopping/PLA). [L1031.007]
  • Ad logic: Google's ad logic moves people through four stages: awareness, interest, consideration and conversion. An ad only earns revenue when people click it at these stages, so the ad must make people want to click. [L1031.008]
  • Foundational hinges: A small number of foundational changes to ads and product data can lower CPC, increase clicks and increase conversions at the same time. The lesson presents these as hinges that move the whole system. [L1031.009]
  • Quality over volume: Visitors actively searching for the product convert far better than large untargeted audiences. One store earned a $4,935 order from only 26 website visitors. [L1223.003]
  • Case, first sale from negative keywords: A new store with no ad history reached its first sale of $2,135 from only 10 website visitors. The owner had onboarded a new supplier, then spent about two weeks on Google PLAs with negative keywords, restricting traffic to SKU-number and brand-term searches. The conversion came from people already searching for the exact product or brand. [L1223.005]
  • Case, baseline before ads: A coached dropshipping store first built the baseline of 100 products and Google Shopping traffic, then spent about $700 per month on ads while losing money. Because the foundation was in place, sales grew from a few thousand dollars to about $15,000 in one month after optimization. A working baseline makes growth much easier. [L1126.031]
  • Price fit for Google Ads: Google Ads fits products priced around $200 to $300 and above, because shoppers usually search for the item before buying, so the purchase is intent-driven rather than impulsive. Search demand fits higher-ticket goods better than impulse-driven social feeds. [L1508.001]
  • Shopping competition: Competition in Shopping is measured per product. If one product is sold by 25 stores, appearing near the top is hard even with an aggressive bid. [L0856.116]
  • Undercut listings: Do not spend on Google Shopping for products where competitors undercut on every listing, because traffic will not convert at the price difference. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0969.096]
  • Keep paying on clicks without sales: Do not stop paid ads when a niche product only gets clicks without sales. Move the effort into optimizing keywords, bids and product pages, since data is already coming in. [L0856.113]
  • Free-only stores: A store running on free traffic alone is a valid option for products where ads do not convert. These products can stay on the site without ad spend. (Source 2023.) [L0855.081]

Build order for paid traffic:

  1. Bottom-of-funnel Google Shopping for buying searches.
  2. Facebook dynamic product ad retargeting for visitors who left.
  3. CTR and quality score optimization to lower CPCs.
  4. Email and lifetime value to raise revenue per customer.

Each step adds to the previous one. [L0510.003]

Funnel Stages (TOFU, MOFU, BOFU)

  • Definitions: TOFU is top of funnel, MOFU is middle of funnel and BOFU is bottom of funnel. Strategy and ads must match the audience's funnel stage, because a message aimed at a later stage than the visitor is currently in will not convert. [L1386.003]
  • Match the ad to the stage: A bottom-of-funnel offer served to a top-of-funnel audience is not profitable, because the searcher still needs answers before considering a purchase. Send these visitors first to informational content that answers their questions. [L1386.004]
  • Example, TOFU (surfing): A searcher typing "Do you need to be strong to go surfing?" is top of funnel, so an ad pushing a surfboard to that query is unprofitable. A top-of-funnel ad answering common beginner questions, with a landing page that captures the email address, is profitable within a few weeks, with the sale coming some weeks later. [L1386.005]
  • Example, BOFU (surfing): A searcher typing "surfboard for beginner" is bottom of funnel. An ad saying "buy the best surfboard for beginners," sent to a landing page explaining why it suits beginners, can produce a sale within a few days. [L1386.006]
  • Start at the bottom: Start paid traffic at the bottom of the funnel. Make campaigns profitable with people already ready to buy, and only then move up to awareness audiences. If a business cannot convert the small group of buy-now searchers, it should not spend large sums on awareness audiences. [L1386.007, L1031.004]
  • Do not convince non-searchers: Do not try to convince people who are not looking for the product to buy it. Spending on this audience wastes budget and lowers results. [L1031.005]
  • Expand later: Expand the funnel only after bottom-of-funnel campaigns run well. The lesson treats the rest of the funnel, labeled as 80%, as a later step rather than something to build first. [L1031.006]
  • Audience size by stage: Audience size grows as you move up the funnel. Searches for a product to buy are fewer than searches for the product category, which are fewer than searches for a symptom or problem. Plan budget and messaging with this size gap in mind. [L1386.008]
  • Match channel to stage: Paid traffic lets you reach a user at any stage with a message that fits that stage. Match each channel to a funnel stage instead of asking every ad for an immediate sale. [L1142.007]
  • Build bottom-up: Start with Shopping campaigns to capture immediate sales, then add upper-funnel channels that build brand trust over time. Plan the sequence backwards from the sale, so each earlier channel feeds the next. [L1142.008]
  • Upper-funnel for unsearched products: Starting with Facebook TOFU ads is possible but harder for products people do not search for, such as new unique products. These buyers need more touchpoints, and influencer content often works best as the first exposure before retargeting and an after-visit funnel. [L0510.005]
  • B2B, low-volume high-ticket: For low-volume, high-ticket B2B products, search capture still comes first, because buyers who search are commercially minded. The audience is then extended up the funnel with display, Facebook or LinkedIn ads aimed at professional groups and industry news readers, since most potential buyers are not actively searching. [L0510.006]
  • B2B audience size: B2B ads with small, specific audiences are harder than B2C ads because the number of people at each funnel stage is small. Social ads can still work when almost every business can use the product, because retargeting audiences then become large enough. [L0510.007]

Meta (Facebook and Instagram)

  • Still valid, but pricier: Facebook remains a valid platform for direct response ads, especially for cold traffic and retargeting, but it has become more expensive. Use interest-based targeting and strong creatives when pixel data is limited. [L0561.048]
  • Cross-platform effect: Google search and Facebook feed each other. Facebook ads can drive branded search on Google. [L0561.049]
  • Retargeting priority: Facebook dynamic retargeting was moved up to the second most important ad type for e-commerce stores, after Google Shopping, once the focus shifted to bottom-of-funnel audiences. It had earlier been ranked third, next to Google search retargeting. [L0510.004]
  • Warm audiences: Building audiences from people who already acted on your site or social accounts lowers ad costs and raises conversion rates. Advertising to warm visitors is cheaper than cold prospecting. [L0757.002]
  • Shopper chain: Shopping ads bring shoppers to the product page, then remarketing and trust-building emails bring them back. Set up each step to feed the next. [L0757.003]
  • Audiences per product: Aim for at least 3 to 4 winning audiences per product. Single audiences can die after a few weeks or months. Several winners let you scale each one separately and later build lookalike audiences from them. [L0648.039]
  • Social for high-ticket (2025): For high-ticket products costing $1,000 to $3,000, a large social following matters less than showing up in front of people already searching to buy. Social media marketing is a weak main sales channel for this kind of store. [L1097.004]
  • Facebook and Google on the Shopify list: Facebook paid social is one of the Shopify traffic sources listed under the core traffic methods. [L0676.003]

Older order advice (2020): Across Google results, the TikTok channel was not tested and the team put its effort elsewhere. Google Shopping and search came first, followed by Facebook and Instagram remarketing, with YouTube, Discovery and display as later expansions. [L0468.018]

TikTok

  • Organic before paid: Start with organic content to find a winning product before spending on Spark Ads. Paid promotion is most useful after organic results prove that a product and hook convert. [L1383.033]
  • Account age: A newer account often performs better than an older one, but an existing account with a large follower base still has value. An account with about 10,000 followers can apply for the Creator Fund, use a playlist linked from the profile, and reach more viewers. [L1383.034]
  • Switching products: When switching an existing account to a different product, expect slow results for the first one to two weeks. Keep posting without skipping days, and the algorithm usually picks the account back up. [L1383.035]
  • Age range: Set the age range to start at 18 and never target anyone under 18, because they cannot complete a purchase. Examples: women 18 to 35 for a female-oriented product, or men 18 to 45 for a male-oriented product that tested well. [L1383.011]
  • Account differences: TikTok ad accounts differ in performance, and some accounts show high CPMs for the same video. If a new account shows poor results despite good creatives and landing pages, the account itself can be the reason. [L1382.067]
  • Older advice, 2020: TikTok was not tested in that period; the effort went to Google, Facebook and Instagram first. [L0468.018]
  • Testing budget: TikTok ads can be one of the untested channels in the roughly 10% testing share of a larger budget (see Experimentation Budgets). [L0970.003]

Pinterest

Fit and audience:

  • Scale and intent: Pinterest has more than 400 million active users, most of them using it for inspiration, which makes it suited to products tied to home decor, fashion, beauty and health. Roughly 25% of the time users spend on Pinterest is spent on shopping, so buying intent is high compared with platforms where people only browse. [L1135.001, L1135.004]
  • Market: The US accounts for about 50 to 60% of the Pinterest audience. The lesson states that around 55% of US users earn more than $100,000 per year. [L1135.005]
  • Order value: The average order value on Pinterest is above $50, which the lesson presents as higher than most other ad platforms. [L1135.006]
  • Less saturated: Pinterest is less saturated than Facebook, TikTok or Google, so many advertisers have not yet tested it, which can reduce competition for ad space. [L1135.007]
  • Products banned elsewhere: Products that often get banned on Facebook or TikTok can usually run on Pinterest, including traditional dropshipping products. [L1135.008]
  • Regional costs: Pinterest ads are cheaper in the UK and Europe than in the US, but the UK and Europe still have a solid market. The US is more competitive because it has the largest user base. [L0788.032]
  • Account restrictions: Pinterest account restrictions can usually be lifted within one to two days, or even a few hours, and personal support from Pinterest is available for account problems. [L1135.009]
  • Account creation: For Pinterest, create the account with a US VPN, after which you can run ads without the VPN. [L0788.034]
  • Backup during bans: Pinterest and Snapchat brought in some sales while Google and Meta accounts were blocked. Keep alternative channels ready as a backup when a main ad account is suspended. [L1214.023]

Testing and budget:

  • Test budget and timing: Pinterest Ads is a low-cost platform for specific niches and can be tested with a daily budget of about $5. Results usually take around one week, and no sales may appear in the first seven days. [L0788.029]
  • Timing, second source: Results take longer than on TikTok or Facebook, typically six to seven days. Not every product works on Pinterest, so only specific products should be tested. [L1135.010]
  • Cost of the test: A $5 daily budget for seven days costs about $35. In one case, CPM for a US audience dropped from $15 at the start to $2 after seven days, with a CPC of about 2 cents. [L0788.030]
  • Low budgets win early: On Pinterest, higher budgets do not help early on, because the platform needs time to learn the audience. In the speaker's tests the most profitable products ran at $5 per day, even though some campaigns at $100 per day were tested. [L1134.010]
  • CPM and quality trade-off: Pinterest ads can deliver very low CPMs, so the platform is cheap to test on. Early traffic quality is usually weaker because the algorithm is still learning the audience. [L1135.016]

Organic and free traffic:

  • Free traffic: Pinterest works best as a free traffic source for stores that already have an audience, since those visitors are the easiest to convert into quick wins. Stores without an audience can still get traffic through a viral pin, but that outcome is less predictable. [L1136.001]
  • User intent: Pinterest users come for inspiration, discovery, new products and how-to content, so store content should be built for that purpose. Many save products as a wish list to buy later, so pinned products can convert after a delay. [L1136.002]
  • Peak days: Pinterest referral traffic on Black Friday and Cyber Monday produced 2x to 3x the e-commerce revenue of normal days with the same traffic volume. This points to high buying intent in Pinterest traffic during peak shopping days. [L1136.003]
  • Viral spikes: Pinterest traffic spikes of 100 to 500 visits per hour can happen regularly when content is shared well. Virality is easier to reach on Pinterest than on most other channels, according to the speaker's experience. [L1136.004]

Pinterest setup procedure:

  1. Search pinterest.com/source/ with your store domain to see who has already pinned your products, then reach out to those users.
  2. Make sure the Pin It button and Rich Pins are turned on in the store, which Shopify and BigCommerce do automatically.
  3. Share your pins and your Pinterest profile everywhere your audience already is, including on the website, in email and on other social networks.
  4. Build a great profile with product, inspiration, resource, behind-the-scenes and testimonial boards that support the store.
  5. Create good-looking vertical images for your content and products.
  6. Use group boards by finding them, getting access, posting your content, driving traffic and then retargeting those visitors.

[L1136.037]

Bing, Yahoo, X, Marketplaces and Other Channels

Bing and Yahoo:

  • Lenient policies (older advice, 2019): Bing and Yahoo Gemini ad networks are often more lenient than Google about which products can be advertised. Check their policies when Google rejects a niche. They are worth testing as additional paid channels. [L0467.052]
  • Small share: Bing and other search engines are much smaller than Google and should not form a significant part of the marketing effort. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L1486.010]
  • Taboola (case): Taboola ads were tested at about $10K monthly revenue and did not work, because the seller lacked good information on how to run that channel. Focusing back on Facebook ads brought a large increase instead. [L1127.012]

X (Twitter):

  • Not recommended (older advice, 2019): Twitter ads are not recommended, since they produced poor results in the presenter's own testing. [L0663.018]
  • Narrow fit: X advertising is considered worthwhile mainly for fashion and jewelry in the US, and to a lesser extent the UK. It is not a good channel for large spend, where Facebook, Google and TikTok are the main platforms. [L1215.024]
  • Bot traffic (2024): Advertising on X did not work for the course author, and the traffic appeared to be mostly bots. Do not expect it to convert. [L1285.061]

YouTube:

  • Alone rarely works: YouTube ads alone rarely work for drop shipping with a small budget. The platform is better suited to established brands with a strong budget and studio-quality creatives. [L0788.040]
  • Journey role: YouTube video ads can build brand awareness early in a multi-touch journey (see Multi-Channel Journeys). [L1142.003]
  • Expansion order (2018, older): YouTube ads are the first expansion step once a store is above $50,000 in monthly sales (see Expansion Triggers). [L1085.002]

Marketplaces and classifieds:

  • Amazon and eBay (older advice, 2019): For a dropship store, Amazon and eBay are not recommended as sales channels. Pinterest and Instagram are considered worth selling on alongside the store. [L0960.069]
  • Free marketplace posts (2023): Free posts in marketplaces such as Facebook and Craigslist can bring the first sales and cash before any paid advertising starts. Paid campaigns can follow once the store has a few sales and optimized product pages. [L0964.164]
  • Classifieds, zero-budget start (older advice, 2019): A zero-budget start is possible with classified ads, but reinvest the first sales into paid Google Ads at about $1,000 per month once they come in. [L0663.009]
  • Classifieds, city choice (older advice, 2019): Do not post the same classified ad in every city, because such ads get flagged. Post in the classified section of a few large, high-traffic cities such as New York (Manhattan) and Los Angeles. [L0663.010]

Blocked niches and outreach:

  • Direct placements (older advice, 2019): When Google and Meta ads are blocked for a niche, buy placements directly on busy niche forums, Facebook groups that sell cover image space, or niche blogs, either through affiliate deals or paid ads. Link these placements to strong content pages, not only product pages, to build authority. [L0467.051]
  • Snapchat restriction (2025-2026): Snapchat's Spotlight and Stories sharing was restricted for users under 16, who can no longer share outside mutual-friend groups. Brands targeting younger audiences on Snapchat need to account for this reduced sharing reach. [L1504.017]

Local platforms:

  • Later experiment: Test local shopping platforms or comparison engines only after the store is profitable and the owner has built up cash reserves. Before that point, they are a later experiment, not a launch channel. [L0710.002]
  • Not ruled out: Local shopping platforms are not ruled out for good. They can perform well in some markets, so they are a valid option once the core business generates profit. [L0710.005]

New sources:

  • Market research: New and unique traffic sources are found through market research in your specific industry, not copied from others (see Platform Choice and Channel Fit). [L0413.041]

Channel Expansion Triggers

  • Google first: Expansion beyond Google only makes sense once the store is highly profitable and Google ads can no longer be scaled further. [L0586.002]

  • Squeeze Google: Before adding new traffic channels, squeeze as much volume as possible out of Google, because splitting focus across channels too early dilutes results. [L0586.003]

  • Local platform trigger: Test local shopping platforms or comparison engines only after the store is profitable and cash reserves exist. [L0710.002]

  • Profit trigger: Local shopping platforms become a valid option once the core business generates profit. [L0710.005]

  • Mistake to avoid: Jumping to a new local channel while the core system is not yet profitable or running splits attention and cash. [L0710.004]

  • Do not switch while it works: Switching to unproven channels while the existing system works adds risk without a clear gain. [L0710.003]

  • Do not launch many at once: Launching several traffic channels at once, such as Facebook ads and PLAs together, spreads a beginner too thin and usually causes trouble. Build a foundation with one channel at a time and expand step by step. [L1203.028]

  • Paid search first: Establish profitable paid search first, then add SEO, social and content. [L0463.011]

  • Do not rely only on paid: Do not rely entirely on paid ads for traffic, because paid advertising keeps getting more expensive. Add other channels so the business is less dependent on ad spend. [L1251.015]

  • Defensive advantage: Running several profitable traffic channels together, such as paid search, SEO, social, content and email, makes the business harder for new competitors to displace, because each channel supports the others. [L0463.010]

  • Mature business: A mature business can run four or five profitable traffic streams (older advice, 2021-2022). [L0439.019]

  • Expansion order, store above $50,000 per month (older, 2018): Once a store is above $50,000 in monthly sales:

    1. Add YouTube ads to drive traffic (still under Google).
    2. Add Dream 100 outreach and influencer partnerships.
    3. Add ad platforms that fit your specific industry.

    [L1085.002]

  • Testing share: At larger ad budgets, about 10% of the monthly ad budget can go to untested channels, while about 90% stays on proven campaigns (see Experimentation Budgets). [L0970.003]

  • Keep learning the basics first: Learn each traffic channel in two passes, starting with a short platform overview before detailed material (see Experimentation Budgets). [L0806.109]

Traffic Ownership Types

Controlled (paid) traffic:

  • Definition: Controlled traffic is traffic you can direct anywhere, including solo ads, banner ads, native ads, pay-per-click on Facebook, Google and Yahoo, and affiliate or joint venture traffic. You pay again every time you want more of it. This makes paid traffic powerful but expensive to scale. [L0523.023]
  • Paid is controlled and measurable: If a business's website converts visitors into customers, paying to acquire those customers is a sound strategy, since paid traffic is controlled and measurable. [L1455.014]

Free and organic traffic:

  • Organic and free listings: The Google organic search and free product listing rows of the traffic table are free (see Channel Overview). [L0586.004]
  • Free-only stores: A store running on free traffic alone is valid for products where ads do not convert. [L0855.081]
  • Free marketplace posts: Free posts in marketplaces can bring first sales before paid advertising begins. [L0964.164]
  • Free Pinterest traffic: Pinterest works best as free traffic for stores with an existing audience. [L1136.001]

Owned audience and email:

  • Email and audiences: Building an audience from people who already acted on your site or social accounts lowers ad costs and raises conversion rates. [L0757.002]
  • Email capture: Lead generation pages need an email capture form, and retargeting plus email nurture the visitor. [L0922.005]
  • Email in the journey: Users sign up for an email list to get a coupon, which builds trust before the purchase. [L1142.003]

Outreach and partnerships:

  • Dream 100 and affiliates: Traditional funnels include Dream 100 outreach (see Platform Choice and Channel Fit). [L0417.002]
  • Affiliate and own website: Affiliate marketing, Amazon presence, organic search and the own website are named as examples of profitable streams (older advice, 2021-2022). [L0439.019]
  • Influencers: Influencer content often works best as the first exposure before retargeting and an after-visit funnel for unsearched products (see Funnel Stages). [L0510.005]

Omnipresence: Being present wherever the customer spends time online within the niche (see Platform Choice and Channel Fit). [L0414.005, L1117.001]

Experimentation Budgets

  • Test each channel with real spend: Not every traffic channel pays off. Some work well while others mostly waste ad budget. Test each channel with real spend and measure the results instead of assuming one works. [L0759.007]
  • Ten percent rule: At larger ad budgets, about 10% of the monthly ad budget can go to untested channels such as TikTok ads, Performance Max, a new banner placement, or content-driven ads, while about 90% stays on proven campaigns. For example, a $1,000 monthly budget splits into $900 proven and $100 testing. [L0970.003]
  • Pinterest test budget: A daily budget of about $5 for about seven days, roughly $35 in total (see Pinterest). [L0788.029, L0788.030]
  • Pinterest budget comparison: The most profitable products ran at $5 per day, while some tests at $100 per day did not perform better early on. [L1134.010]
  • Audience testing: Aim for at least 3 to 4 winning audiences per product, so each can be scaled separately. [L0648.039]
  • Reinvest the first sales: Reinvest first classified-ad sales into paid Google Ads at about $1,000 per month (older advice, 2019). [L0663.009]
  • Cash reserves: Test local shopping platforms only after the store is profitable and cash reserves are built up. [L0710.002]
  • Keep the test running: Do not stop paid ads when a niche product is only getting clicks without sales. Keep spending while optimizing keywords, bids and product pages. [L0856.113]
  • Time to learn on TikTok: When switching an existing account to a new product, expect one to two weeks of slow results. [L1383.035]
  • Organic first: Use organic content to find a winning product before spending on Spark Ads. [L1383.033]
  • Learning passes (2024): Learn each channel in two passes: first a short overview covering Google Ads, Microsoft Ads, Meta Ads and SEO, then the detailed how-to material. Understanding the basics first makes the detailed lessons easier to apply. [L0806.109]
  • Scaling readiness: Once the message resonates with the customer, reaching US$1k per day or even US$10k per day becomes a scaling problem rather than a mystery. [L1019.049]

Multi-Channel Journeys

  • Channels influence each other: Paid channels influence each other, so a campaign on one platform can lift total site traffic from other sources. Judge channels as a connected system, not as isolated silos. [L1142.001]

  • Typical journey with paid traffic:

    1. Google automatically places a user who watches videos on a topic (e.g. health benefits of a product category) into an interest targeting category.
    2. Show that user a relevant YouTube video ad to build brand awareness.
    3. The user later searches for a buyer's guide, sees the brand in a Google search ad and clicks, which lifts CTR.
    4. The user signs up for the email list to get a coupon, which builds trust.
    5. The user searches for the product and clicks a Shopping ad, where the prior touchpoints make conversion more likely.

    [L1142.003]

  • Cross-platform lift: Facebook ads can drive branded search on Google, so both platforms feed each other. [L0561.049]

  • Flywheel: Cold video ads prospect large audiences and build a warm pool as a byproduct (every 50% video viewer). Bottom-of-funnel ads harvest that warm pool with high ROAS. The longer cold video runs, the larger the warm pool grows, and the better bottom-of-funnel performs, all without changing bottom-of-funnel creative. This is why ROAS climbs passively over time. [L0316.033]

  • Shopper chain: Shopping ads, then remarketing and trust-building emails, each setup feeding the next (see Meta). [L0757.003]

  • Multi-channel thinking: The largest and most successful advertisers transfer learnings from Google Ads into content marketing, use Google Ads to amplify content, and use paid traffic to grow social media and SEO. Plan each paid channel with its effect on the other channels in mind. [L1142.009]

  • Four channels with four stages: Free organic search, free Shopping listings, paid Shopping ads and remarketing each cover a different stage of the buyer journey. [L1393.005]

  • Measurement: Use Google Analytics as the default measurement tool (older advice, 2021-2022). [L0721.026]

Advertising Principles

Foundation before tactics:

  • Strategy first: Paid traffic results depend on a solid strategic foundation, and tactics only add value on top of it. Collecting tips without that foundation makes campaigns weaker, not stronger. [L1031.001]
  • Tip versus strategy: A tip is a single action, while a strategy states the conditions under which that action works. For example, retargeting is a tip that performs well in e-commerce, but its strategic condition is that the first audience was targeted correctly. [L1031.002]
  • Tips on a broken base: Relying on blog tips and tricks without a strong foundation leaves paid traffic performing worse, because the tips get applied to a broken base. [L1031.003]

Cost and margin:

  • Margins are thin: High-ticket dropshipping has relatively modest margins, so the cost to acquire each customer must be controlled carefully. Each paid click has a direct cost, which is why selective targeting matters more in this model. Expect profitability to suffer if you buy every available click. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L1507.010]
  • Business model matters: Paid clicks cost money and more spend usually brings more traffic, so business model matters as much as ad skill. A business that can spend more to acquire a customer, due to a higher cart value or a funnel with lead magnets, can outbid competitors and appear in better positions more often. [L1386.009]
  • Case, two surfing models: Store A sells a single surfboard for $800 and pays $100 to acquire a customer, advertising commercial terms to people who are still asking whether they are strong enough to surf. Store B starts from the bottom of the funnel, sells surfboard packages with wax and wetsuit for $1,500, can afford $200 per customer acquisition, and offers a top-of-funnel ebook for $5 to collect leads who are later converted. Store B's higher cart value lets it outbid Store A and show more often in better positions, and the leads it collects feed more money into bottom-of-funnel ads. [L1386.010]
  • Reading high click costs: If clicks look expensive in your niche, competitors may have found a better business model or funnel. Treat high click costs as a signal to examine how rivals fund acquisition, for example through higher cart values or cheap lead magnets. [L1386.011]
  • Scaling: Once the message resonates, scaling is a question of execution rather than a mystery (see Experimentation Budgets). [L1019.049]

Relevance and intent:

  • Warm cheaper than cold: Advertising to warm visitors is cheaper than cold prospecting. [L0757.002]
  • Traffic quality: Traffic quality matters more than traffic volume (see Shopping and Search Intent). [L1223.003]
  • Do not chase non-searchers: Spending on people who are not looking for the product wastes budget (see Funnel Stages). [L1031.005]

Investment logic:

  • Acquiring customers is an investment: If a business's website converts visitors into customers, paying to acquire those customers is a sound strategy, since paid traffic is controlled and measurable. [L1455.014]

Channel-level caution:

  • Not every channel pays off: Test each channel with real spend and measure results (see Experimentation Budgets). [L0759.007]
  • Do not overspend on Shopping for undercut products: (see Shopping and Search Intent). [L0969.096]

Lead Generation

  • Purpose: Lead generation targets top-of-funnel searchers who are not ready to buy yet. The goal is to build brand familiarity and nurture them over time, so the brand is already trusted when they reach the buying stage, ideally before competitors do. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L0922.003]
  • Payback timing: Lead generation is an investment that pays back over months, not on the first sale. Hot audiences who search product names, brand names or SKU numbers buy fastest, but lead generation targets people who are not ready to buy yet. [L1076.004]
  • Three prerequisites: Lead generation with search ads depends on three elements: a content page with valuable content, retargeting set up for visitors of that page, and an email capture form on the page. Without follow-up retargeting and email, the visitor is not nurtured and the traffic produces no sales. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L0922.005]
  • Lead magnets in the model: A top-of-funnel ebook for $5 collects leads who are later converted, and those leads feed more money into bottom-of-funnel ads (see Advertising Principles). [L1386.010]
  • Email in the journey: A user who signs up for the email list to get a coupon builds trust before buying (see Multi-Channel Journeys). [L1142.003]
