1.12 Growth levers: AOV, repeat purchases and customer lifetime value

Three growth levers

  • A business grows in three ways: winning more first-time buyers, raising the value of each order, and getting existing customers to buy again and more often. Check each lever separately. [L0378.003]
  • (older, 2021-22) Store growth has three levers: more first-time customers through traffic or conversion rate, higher average order value or profit per order through higher-priced products, bundles or better margins, and a higher repeat purchase rate. Pulling one lever alone yields linear growth, while pulling all three at once yields exponential growth. [L0414.003]
  • (older, 2021-22) Any e-commerce business can grow in only three ways: attract more first-time customers or traffic, raise the average order value, or raise the rate of repeat purchases. Each customer who buys again adds to growth without a new acquisition. [L0482.001]
  • (older, 2021-22) The third lever is selling more than once to the same customer. Repeat sales from existing customers are a separate growth path from acquiring new ones. [L0448.002]
  • Focus on lifetime value (LTV) before average order value (AOV). First identify the channel or action that makes the most money and push it until its return stops growing before moving resources to another channel. [L0334.023]

Lifetime value and repeat customers

  • Customer lifetime value (CLV) and lifetime value (LTV) determine long-term success. Reacquiring existing customers costs far less than acquiring new ones, so focus on repeat customers, not just first-order volume. [L0324.003]
  • Do not treat dropshipping as a short-term cash grab where customer experience does not matter. Running out of new customers without retention systems leads to business collapse. [L0324.004]
  • Operating a successful business centers on creating exceptional customer experiences that build satisfaction. Satisfied customers become repeat buyers and advocates. [L0028.008]
  • (older, 2021-22) Selling again to an existing customer is usually easier and more profitable than acquiring a new one. If repeat sales are made without the same marketing cost as a new customer, the profit margin on the second or third sale is often higher. [L0482.002]
  • (older, 2021-22) Repeat customers cost nothing extra to acquire again, you can email them directly, and they already trust your business. This makes repeat sales cheaper and more profitable than finding new buyers each time. [L0994.019]
  • (older, 2021-22) Recurring revenue means the same customer buys from you more than once. Repeat business is a core way to grow because repeat sales are easier to close, cost less to market, and usually carry higher profit margins on the second and third order onward. [L1485.001, L1486.001]
  • (older, 2021-22) A store that sells only a single one-time product must keep finding new buyers and cannot market profitably to existing customers. Its growth is capped far below that of a store that can sell to the same customers again and again. [L1486.002]
  • (older, 2021-22) Large e-commerce companies such as Amazon grow mainly because customers return to buy other products. Without repeat purchases they would exhaust their market and stagnate, and the same risk applies to a small store. [L1486.003]
  • Returning customers usually contribute about 20% to 30% of revenue in dropshipping, and up to 40% in good cases. Subscription boxes and consumable products can reach much higher repeat rates. [L1213.078]
  • In most niches more than 80 percent of store visitors are first-time visitors, so repeat purchase rates stay low. Plan customer acquisition for new buyers, and use upsells, cross-sells, referral discounts for family and friends, and affiliate content for indirectly related products to add value per customer. [L1283.108]
  • The 20% to 30% figure measures share of revenue, while the 80% figure measures share of visitors. Both can hold at the same time in one store. [L1213.078, L1283.108]

Customer multiplier funnel

  • A customer multiplier funnel is a series of actions aimed at existing customers with two goals: selling more products after the first purchase, and turning customers into people who bring in other customers. It is not a paid advertising method such as pay-per-click. [L0482.003]
  • (older, 2021-22) Upselling is the first stage of a customer multiplier funnel. Onsells and resells follow the same logic: they aim at a second purchase from customers who have already bought once, so revenue per existing customer rises. [L0448.023]
  • (older, 2021-22) Upsells and resells happen only when they are deliberately planned and systematically executed. Repeat purchases cannot be left to chance or to customer loyalty alone. [L0448.011]
  • (older, 2021-22) Some funnel actions take a long time to pay off, while others can be set up once and then keep producing results. Immediately actionable steps can be put in place within days or weeks. [L0482.005]
  • (older, 2021-22) For most businesses that already make sales, have no other major problems, and can implement these actions, a customer multiplier funnel is expected to add 10 to 20 percent in additional revenue per month. [L0482.006]
  • (older, 2021-22) Formula: Expected additional monthly revenue from a customer multiplier funnel = current monthly revenue × 10% to 20% Example: at 100,000 USD monthly revenue, the funnel is expected to add 10,000 to 20,000 USD per month. [L0482.007]
  • Once a customer has made a purchase, run three follow-up sequences at the same time: a brand ambassador program, lead value optimization offers for related products or services, and email campaigns that bring buyers back to the store. Set these up after the first sale rather than treating any one of them as a separate tactic. [L0676.017]

Upsells, cross-sells and expanding the offer

  • Expanding the offer means adding more products to a store and building better offers such as bundles, upsells and cross-sells. The goal is a higher average order value and more bottom-line profit. [L0434.001, L0435.001]
  • Complementary products sold alongside a main product, either at cart level or as a bundle, increase the chance that the customer buys the second item. The stronger the relation between the products, such as pillowcases and hair accessories for the same audience, the higher this chance. [L0434.006]
  • After finding a winning product, sell different variants and cross-sells of it to raise average order value. Each additional item in a single order improves profit per customer. [L0562.026]
  • Upsells and cross-sells raise average order value and margin from a customer who is already buying, so they suit any store, including one with a single product. Add them at some level even when the store only sells one item. [L1412.001]
  • (older, 2021-22) Accessories with a lower price can be added to an expensive main product to raise the average order value and reach a broader customer base at a higher margin. [L1481.007]
  • (older, 2021-22) Pellet grill accessories can serve as upsells and downsells, and utensils or cedar planks can serve as lead magnets. A lead magnet offer sent to a content page can explain the benefits of owning a pellet grill to someone who has a normal grill. [L1152.017]
  • Selling one product only is fine, but selling several products can create trust and enable bundles. Monitor which products competitor stores sell most and test bundles based on those winners. [L0773.046]
  • A niche store can run ads on one winning product while other products are promoted to existing customers through email. Adding products to the same niche can be a second step after the current winner has been scaled. [L0694.032]

Money machines

  • A money machine is a repeatable process that converts prospects into customers or deepens customer relationships. It is designed to run on autopilot and multiply revenue through lead capture, upselling, cross-selling, retention and remarketing. [L0240.001]
  • A money machine is an automated process that takes people in and pays money out, bolted onto the core store, which stays the main sales engine. Five types cover the customer journey:
Type Stage of the journey
Lead generation Before the store
Cross-sell Product pages
Upsell Checkout
Post-purchase After buying
Remarketing After leaving the site, whether or not the visitor bought

[L1076.001]

  • A money machine should lift average order value by about 5 to 10 percent. A store with a 1,000 dollar AOV should be able to reach roughly 1,050 to 1,100 dollars. [L1076.002]

  • Money machines are built to raise average order value, customer lifetime value and net profit per customer. Most run on autopilot once set up. Each one needs a stated setup time and budget before you decide when to build it. [L1076.003]

  • Setting up a lead generation machine:

    1. Write the lead magnet content in a Google Doc.
    2. Outsource the design and add footers and links.
    3. Build the landing page with the free form or a builder.
    4. Connect the sign-up to Klaviyo and build the flow.
    5. Send traffic from blog banners, sponsorships, Facebook ads or influencers.
    6. Email at least twice a week and check the Klaviyo revenue after one to two months.

    [L1076.023]

Lead value optimization (LVO)

  • Lead value optimization (LVO) means increasing what each customer is worth over time through cross-selling related products, affiliate offers and promotions throughout the year. It is a separate lever from referral traffic and can be combined with it. [L0417.003]
  • Lead value optimization means earning more from every visitor, whether or not they buy the core product. Conversion rate optimization focuses on getting visitors to buy the core product. Start lead value optimization only after the store has real sales, because that is when the extra offers start to pay off. [L0923.001]
  • High-spending competitors on top paid search placements can stay profitable because they earn more from each lead than the core product sale alone. If a lead costs about 3 dollars per click, the store needs back-end or side-funnel revenue from that lead to cover the cost. [L0923.002]
  • Cross-promoting your own niche stores is the easiest way to raise the value of each lead. Buyers who just purchased are the most receptive to another offer. Most stores with strong sales still skip this step, even though it reuses an existing audience at almost no cost. [L0923.012]

Consumables and subscriptions

  • High-margin consumables are the most valuable product type for repeat revenue. If a consumable such as a filter or accessory can be added to the business, add it quickly by talking to the supplier. If no consumable fits, sell a warranty instead. [L0334.025]
  • (older, 2021-22) Consumable products such as protein powder give buyers a reason to reorder. A customer won through a low-cost entry product can become worth much more over time than a one-time buyer, which is why a break-even front-end sale can still be profitable. [L0511.025]
  • At a low unit price, a single-item order rarely pays for paid acquisition, so a consumable product needs bundles or a subscription to raise average order value. Repeat purchases of a consumable make the customer worth far more than the first order. [L0524.001]
  • A typical adult uses roughly 17 to 20 bars of soap per year, and some sources cite up to 26. For conservative planning, use 17 units per year. [L0524.002]
  • (older, 2021-22) Consumable products that customers reorder, such as filament, can be sold on a subscription basis to create recurring revenue. [L1152.013]
  • Monthly recurring revenue (MRR) comes from subscription systems and recurring models. It is the step that takes a store beyond one-time sales. [L0914.013]
  • A subscription app such as Recharge or the Shopify subscription payments app on a competitor store signals a subscription-based model. Check how the competitor sells its subscription when you model recurring revenue. [L1164.019]
  • Subscription upgrades in the cart raise customer lifetime value by turning one-time buyers into subscribers. Buy More Save More raises average order value by encouraging customers to put more quantity into each order. [L1231.004]
  • Peak sales periods such as Black Friday and Cyber Monday bring much more traffic, so cart features that convert visitors quickly become more important during those weeks. Letting shoppers upgrade a one-time item to a subscription even after it is in the cart is a key example. [L1231.005]

Loyalty

  • (older, 2021-22) High-ticket stores can still get repeat purchases if the catalog includes several products a customer might buy at different times. A loyalty program adds a reason to come back by giving customers a reward they can spend on their next order. [L0956.001]
  • When a customer does a favor for the brand, such as posting a photo or writing a review, they become more connected to the brand, remember the experience more and are more likely to buy again and recommend the store. The request itself therefore builds loyalty, not only content. [L0417.007]

Referral programs

  • (older, 2021-22) Referrals from existing customers compound over time. If each customer brings in another customer, sales and revenue roughly double in theory, though in practice only some customers will refer others. The effect grows as more new customers send more customers. [L0406.001]
  • (older, 2021-22) Recommendations from a customer's friends or family are trusted more than a business's own advertising. Customers who refer others bring in new business at a much lower direct cost than paid marketing. [L0406.002]
  • A post-purchase referral system can be started from the very first sale, because it only needs a customer who has already bought. It turns a normal one-way sales funnel into an hourglass by sending buyers back out as a new traffic source. [L0417.001]
  • The referral approach is not an affiliate or paid-referrer program and not a multi-level structure. Buyers are not paid to recruit. The goal is to give every customer a strong experience and then ask for specific actions that naturally lead to word of mouth. [L0417.004]
  • Referral traffic is not expected to be the largest traffic source and usually will not make up most of the revenue. Word of mouth is still very effective, and the big advantage is that the system only has to be set up once and then runs on its own. [L0417.005]
  • Choose the post-purchase ask based on product type and buyer profile:
Product type Suitable ask
Products people like to show off (trendy, cool, hobby or design items) Photo and social post requests
Products people do not want to show publicly (medical supplies, wheelchairs, products for an older demographic) Review requests
Mixed range Many stores use both

[L0417.006]

  • (older, 2021-22) Customers who feel well looked after are more likely to recommend a store to others. A referral program adds a deliberate nudge on top of good service. Rewarding each successful referral costs little extra and makes the recommendation more likely. [L1234.001]

  • (older, 2021-22) Set up a referral program for the store and reward every successful referral. Customers must know about the reward before they refer anyone. [L1234.002]

  • (older, 2021-22) A referral program can run as a separate system or be integrated with the loyalty program. How the two work together depends on the tool chosen. [L1234.003]

  • (older, 2021-22) Referral rewards can be loyalty points redeemable for future purchases, a discount on the store, or cash. Each option suits a different business model and should be chosen deliberately. [L1234.010]

  • (older, 2021-22) There is no single right way to reward referrals. One example is a cash affiliate payout made once every 30 days, covering referrals from the previous 30 days. [L1234.012]

  • (older, 2021-22) Choose the reward structure while configuring the app, based on what fits the business best. Expect to test and adjust it over time, as with any marketing activity. [L1234.013]

  • (older, 2021-22) Create a dedicated referral page on the website that explains how to sign up and how to refer others. Link this page in the site footer so customers can always find it. [L1234.016]

  • (older, 2021-22) Referral program procedure:

    1. Implement a referral program for the store.
    2. Pick a referral tool, exploring the available options and choosing the one that fits best.
    3. Set up the chosen tool in the business.
    4. Build the referral emails into the email marketing system.

    [L1234.018]

Promotions calendar logic

  • The growth plan has three parts that run together over 12 months: build audiences, create offers that are hard to refuse, and promote those offers to the audiences. The aim is more sales and profit that you can predict. [L0757.001]

  • Plan monthly promotions so they add roughly 20 to 30 percent of monthly revenue on top of regular sales. This gives a sales floor that still works if paid ads are turned off or organic traffic drops. [L1204.001]

  • The system is meant to add 20 to 30 percent to monthly revenue, according to the lesson. Treat that figure as a target to measure against, not a guaranteed result. [L0757.004]

  • Dropping monthly promotions after one underperforming offer is a mistake. Consistent testing shows which offers a given audience buys, and the contact pool grows with each month. [L1204.002]

  • Shoppers who did not buy during dynamic remarketing (about 7 to 30 days) or after an abandoned-cart email flow (up to about 9 days in Klaviyo) still form a warm pool. Monthly offers are a way to reach that pool again. [L1204.003]

  • Promotions aimed at existing audiences should not be started on day one. They become worthwhile once traffic, remarketing audiences and the email list have grown. Core traffic sources such as Google Ads, Facebook Ads, Microsoft Ads and SEO should come first. [L1205.001]

  • Peak periods such as Black Friday and Cyber Monday are the weeks when cart-level conversion features matter most. [L1231.005]

  • Next steps for the growth plan:

    1. Write down at least 4 offers you can promote to your audience.
    2. Assign them on a rotating basis across the next 12 months.
    3. Create offer assets: emails and ads for each offer.
    4. Schedule the offers in Klaviyo, Google and Facebook.
    5. Modify future offers based on the results of each monthly promotion.
    6. Track whether the system adds 20 to 30 percent to monthly revenue.

    [L0757.042]

Margin improvement priorities

  • Profit margin is the most common financial problem for dropshipping stores once they reach a certain size. Growing revenue without fixing margin multiplies the problems along with the sales. [L1046.040]

  • (older, 2021-22) Growing revenue at a 9 percent net margin brings little extra profit for a lot of extra effort, so raising gross margin is usually the better lever. In the example, a flat-revenue year with 20 percent gross margin would be worth more than doubling sales at the current margin. [L1046.041]

  • (older, 2021-22) More orders means more work and more operational risk, while margin gains bring cash without extra workload. Choose improvements that raise profit per order before spending on sales growth. [L1046.042]

  • (older, 2021-22) Margin improvement priorities, in order:

    1. Find new suppliers and product categories with higher margins, and add only products that clear the minimum margin including shipping.
    2. Renegotiate pricing with the current top suppliers, using the volume they receive from the store as leverage.
    3. Renegotiate shipping costs with suppliers or freight providers.
    4. Test price increases on products where a small volume loss is acceptable.
    5. Limit or stop discounts, including custom quotes.
    6. Move marketing and SEO to maintenance mode and keep the current sales level while putting energy into margin.

    [L1046.043]

  • (older, 2021-22) Stores that scale often run out of capacity to take new orders and stop adding suppliers and products. The fix is to keep adding products, but to choose suppliers selectively by margin. [L1046.044]

Discounts and offers

  • (older, 2021-22) A discount shrinks margin, but losing a customer you could have won usually costs more. A deal can be the right call when it closes a sale that would otherwise go to a competitor. Every sale you make also limits your competitors' budgets, because they cannot outspend you on marketing without revenue. [L0799.001]

Sequencing and guardrails

  • Do not start customizations such as post-purchase surveys, feedback loops or one-click upsells until the concept is approved and the business is profitable. Share your P&L before moving to these steps. Concept approval and profitability come first, then customization. [L0906.020]
