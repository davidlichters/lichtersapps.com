1.14 Content, organic traffic and audience ownership

Content's role in the marketing mix

  • Content marketing does not replace paid advertising. It adds another channel alongside paid ads. [L0500.001]
  • The marketing system of an e-commerce store is a set of interrelated components that improve each other when combined. Content sits at the centre of this system and makes several other components work better. [L0500.004]
  • Content creates options, such as more ways to attract customers, grow the store and keep customers, similar to how money creates options in life. The value comes from the options it opens, not from the content as an end in itself. [L0500.003]
  • Content marketing builds trust before the first purchase. Shoppers comparing your store with large marketplaces need a reason to buy from you, and detailed product pages, product videos and educational articles give you a selling point those marketplaces rarely match. [L0380.001]
  • An article that ranks for a relevant keyword keeps bringing visitors for as long as the topic has demand, while each paid click stops when the budget stops. Content is therefore a one-time production cost with long-term traffic, as opposed to paying per visit. [L0380.002]
  • Organic traffic costs time instead of ad money and can scale slowly, while paid traffic can start immediately and scales with the size of the market. Both should work together, and a store should not rely on only one of them. [L1114.004]

Outsourcing content production

  • Business owners should not create content themselves beyond a minimal amount, because their time is better spent on promoting content. [L0379.001]
  • Content creation should be outsourced to freelancers, especially for small to medium e-commerce businesses, where doing it in-house leads to frustration and no progress. [L0379.001]
  • A standard content plan is two pieces per month. [L1421.013] (Older advice, 2021-22.)
  • When an ultimate guide is planned as a truly epic piece, it can replace both monthly pieces as the only content for that month, because its promotion lasts longer. [L1421.013] (Older advice, 2021-22.)
  • A dropship store can grow from zero to over $20,000 monthly revenue using content marketing alone, without paid advertising, by building high-quality content through outsourced creation and systematised templates. [L0326.001]
  • Starting budget reference for content marketing:
Item Cost
Ahrefs 14-day free trial, enough for the keyword process
Writers (Upwork) $50 to $100 per 1,000-word article
Native English editors $20 to $25 per hour
Sales copywriter for headlines and meta descriptions $40 to $100 per hour
Starting test: 10 articles $1,000 to $1,500 total
Optimising headlines and meta descriptions about $2,000

[L0382.047]

When to start: sequence with paid traffic

  • You can either continue building your audience before monetising, or start selling immediately while continuing to build your audience. Both approaches are valid depending on your specific situation. [L0237.010]
  • If paid traffic such as pay-per-click is still profitable, scale it fully before investing in content marketing. Start content as an additional traffic channel if you are looking for alternatives. [L0382.045]
  • When paid channels such as PPC, Amazon and Facebook ads are unavailable in a niche, content marketing can become the main source of traffic. This reduces dependence on platforms that restrict the category. [L0382.052]
  • Build an audience before the product launch by posting educational content, such as recipes or health advice, on social platforms. This also reveals what the target audience wants. [L1016.014]
  • Community building comes after the first sales. Focus first on the first 10 and then 20 sales, and build community with the customers who arrive afterwards. [L1016.015]
  • Do not write blog posts to get a supplier approved, because they do not help approvals and only delay revenue. Blog content belongs in the SEO phase, once organic traffic is being built. [L0853.014] (Older advice, 2021.)

Which framing applies:

Situation Approach Source
Paid traffic still profitable Scale paid fully first; add content as an extra channel L0382.045
Paid channels unavailable in the niche Content can become the main traffic source L0382.052
Content as the only channel A store can reach over $20,000 monthly revenue with content alone L0326.001
Default guidance Treat content as an addition to paid channels, plan it alongside other sources from the start, and combine the sources L0380.036, L0500.001, L1114.004

The sources disagree on whether content can carry a store alone. L0326.001 describes a store that reached $20,000 monthly with content only. L0380.036 advises treating content as an addition rather than a guaranteed replacement. The main-channel framing applies where paid channels are unavailable, and the addition framing applies everywhere else, including where paid traffic is still profitable. [L0326.001, L0380.036, L0382.045, L0382.052, L0500.001, L1114.004]

Case: building the audience before the first offer

  • A homebrewing test brand had a parked Shopify site with no products yet while the owner built the audience first. A Facebook page was created, and a meme-style image was posted with a caption asking people to like and share, followed by a Facebook ad with only an engagement objective at about 5 dollars per day. [L0487.014]
  • The post reached about 46,000 people, received about 3,100 likes, 71 comments and 349 shares, and the cost per engagement was below one cent. [L0487.014]
  • After a few weeks, about 3,600 people had engaged on Facebook and about 5,300 on Instagram, which formed the seed audiences. [L0487.014]
  • No offers were planned for roughly six weeks while content was produced. The later plan was to use dropshipping for expensive products, private label for mid-priced products, and a seasonal subscription box. [L0487.014]

SEO and blog use

  • Do not sell ad space on your blog to other companies. Use the traffic and articles to sell your own products instead. [L0382.044]
  • Check domain authority (Moz) by comparing the domain authority of sites you could get links from against each other, using the top sites in your niche, instead of a fixed minimum score. [L0856.119]
  • Paying for Google Ads to promote blog posts only makes sense at several million dollars in annual revenue. Before that level, it is wasted money. [L1281.028] (Older advice, 2022.)
  • Building organic traffic through SEO is an important part of high-ticket dropshipping and should not be postponed indefinitely in favour of paid traffic alone. [L0443.006] (Older advice, 2021-22.)
  • A high-ticket dropshipping business that depends heavily on paid traffic is exposed as soon as ad costs rise or performance drops. Building organic traffic through SEO is a common weakness that is easy to neglect when paid advertising feels more comfortable. [L0443.005] (Older advice, 2021-22.)

Owning the audience and the email list

  • There are only three types of traffic: traffic you own, traffic you control, and traffic you don't control. The goal for all three is to convert it into owned traffic, such as an email list or a follower base. Growing owned traffic is the core task of the business. [L0523.020]
  • Owned traffic such as an email list, followers or past customers can be contacted at no extra acquisition cost. Repeat sales to this audience are essentially pure profit. [L0523.021]
  • Owning an audience you can contact at any time, traditionally through an email list, is the long-term goal of marketing. Paid traffic, ads and SEO reach people you do not own, so they do not give the same security. [L0412.001] (Older advice, 2021-22.)
  • Traffic you don't control includes social media, search engine optimisation, guest blogging, YouTube and guest interviews. Send all of it to your own blog, where the top section captures email addresses. Once visitors give an email address, they become owned traffic. [L0523.025]
  • A dropshipping store usually runs direct response marketing that pushes for an immediate sale, unlike brands that build awareness first. Cold visitors rarely buy on the first visit, so the store needs a way to keep interested visitors who have not bought yet. Email and SMS capture that group and move them toward a purchase. [L0605.001]

Risks and dependence on a single channel

  • Content marketing carries its own risks and takes sustained effort, so treat it as an addition to paid channels rather than a guaranteed replacement for them. Results arrive slowly, and a single article rarely changes sales. Plan it alongside other traffic sources from the start. [L0380.036]
  • Do not depend on one traffic source. Google Ads accounts can be suspended with little warning, especially in industries platforms consider risky, and a store that gets all its visitors from one paid channel can lose nearly all of them within a day. Combine paid channels such as PPC and Shopping with content marketing and email. [L0380.003]
  • A business that relies only on paid ads depends on a single traffic source. If the paid channel fails, for example through account problems or rising costs, traffic can stop entirely. Content gives the business other ways to attract visitors. [L0500.002] (Older advice, 2021-22.)
  • Relying only on pay-per-click ads and influencer marketing is a long-term risk. A high-ticket business without content tends to have higher cost per acquisition and limited traffic options. [L0414.022] (Older advice, 2021-22.)
  • Be skeptical of anyone who says content marketing is unnecessary, especially when they sell paid-ad training, because such claims often hide a commercial motive. [L0500.005] (Older advice, 2021-22.)
  • If someone says they tried content marketing and it failed, the content was most likely not done properly. This is not evidence that content marketing itself does not work. [L0500.006] (Older advice, 2021-22.)

Facebook groups and community channels

  • Facebook groups cannot be made read-only, so set new posts to require approval to stop questions. Move ongoing support to community channels organised by topic, which stay current. [L0934.114]

YouTube as an asset

  • A YouTube channel with many subscribers and steady engagement adds value to an online business, and that value matters if the owner later sells it. Each extra traffic source, including YouTube, makes the business less dependent on a single channel. [L1511.026] (Older advice, 2021-22.)
  • Case: a drop-shipping store that owns, produces and holds none of its products sold grow lights from many brands, priced from about 150 dollars up to about 1,000 dollars. [L1424.039] (Older advice, 2021-22.)
  • Over roughly two years it built a YouTube channel with reviews, buyer's guides and top-7 lists of its range, and it also embedded these videos on its website. [L1424.039] (Older advice, 2021-22.)
  • Results: the channel reached about 22,000 subscribers, single videos drew about 21,000 to 35,000 views, the videos probably reached millions of views in total, and the website receives tens of thousands of visits per month. [L1424.039] (Older advice, 2021-22.)
  • The video descriptions link to the products, and the closing section promotes the affiliate program. [L1424.039] (Older advice, 2021-22.)

Faceless AI channels

  • A faceless AI-presented channel works well in a niche where a specific language audience has little content of the required type, because the unmet demand is the opening. The creator does not need to appear on camera, which lowers the barrier for people who are not comfortable with their own face. [L0888.002]
  • Case: a new faceless YouTube channel was built entirely with AI tools (portrait, script, voice, avatar animation, subtitles), with no on-camera presence by the operator. [L0888.001]
  • After two days, the two uploaded videos had about 1,200 views, about 200 likes and 18 new followers across TikTok, and the operator used it to practise organic TikTok growth with a target of 1 million views. [L0888.001]
  • AI-presented videos can be turned into digital products, such as full video courses on a topic, product tutorials with a speaking presenter, or audiobooks made from text. An audiobook version can be offered as a free bonus with an ebook to add value for customers. [L0888.003]

Short-form social presence

  • Case: a new account started posting on 5 November with a single new product. The first uploads got about 500 views, then 3,000, 1,000, 1,000, 3,000, 9,000 and 26,000 views. [L1383.038]
  • After five days of uploads the account had about 800 followers. One video using the hook Why did you give me this free product? reached about 375,000 views within three days. [L1383.038]
  • That one video raised followers to about 6,000, viewers began commenting that they wanted the product, and at least one sale was reported. A later upload using the hook Do you see yourself needing this? reached about 23,000 views within three hours. [L1383.038]
  • Product accounts are easier to grow than personal accounts because their content stays on topic and focused. Build separate product accounts rather than relying on a personal profile. [L1384.034]
  • One creator reached 100,000 followers in about one month on a personal account by uploading every day. Personal accounts were considered harder to grow than product accounts, which grew more easily. The product account is the default recommendation, and a personal account can still grow fast with daily uploads. [L1384.033, L1384.034]
  • Consistent daily posting is a key growth driver, because most competitors post inconsistently. Consistent upload frequency with slightly better content can outperform many established accounts. [L1384.035]
