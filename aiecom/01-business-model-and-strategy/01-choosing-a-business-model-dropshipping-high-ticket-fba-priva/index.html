1.1 Choosing a business model: dropshipping, high-ticket, FBA, private label, digital and affiliate

What dropshipping is

  • Dropshipping is a fulfillment model that lets you sell products without holding inventory. You outsource fulfillment to a supplier, which reduces upfront costs and minimizes financial risk compared with traditional retail or wholesale. [L0007.018]
  • Dropshipping is a fulfillment method, not a separate business model. Research, selling and store building follow the same process as other Shopify e-commerce. [L0972.002]
  • The model in this course is online-only, with no physical store locations. Each order is shipped by the supplier directly to the customer after the sale. [L0759.001]
  • In a dropshipping model the store owner never buys or stores inventory. Each order placed on the store is forwarded to a supplier or fulfillment company, which picks, packs and ships the product straight to the customer. The store owner only builds the website, attracts buyers and passes on order details. [L0760.001]
  • A dropshipping business rests on three parts: an online store where sales happen, supplier fulfillment that ships the orders, and traffic that brings buyers to the store. Each part must work before the business can make a profit. [L0759.002]
  • Dropshipping is a modern version of retail: a store sells products made by other manufacturers, as department stores do. Being the middleman between manufacturer and customer is normal in retail and is accepted by customers. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0458.012]
  • Dropshipping is only a fulfillment method, so the customer experience is largely the same as with any other brand. The supplier ships the order directly, and the store owner remains responsible for the sale. [L1251.006]
  • With approved suppliers, the retailer holds no inventory for most products. Orders are placed with suppliers after each customer purchase, and the supplier ships directly to the customer. (2023.) [L1283.104]
  • In a non-stocking dealer setup, the supplier keeps the products in stock and ships the orders to your customers, while your store handles marketing, order handling and customer service. You do not hold inventory in this model. [L0933.004]
  • Dropshipping gives the seller no product control. You cannot change or design the product, and the supplier decides what is stocked and can remove a product at any time. Your job is marketing and customer service, and you can only suggest product changes to the supplier. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1480.007]

High-ticket and low-ticket

  • High-ticket dropshipping means selling higher-priced products online while a supplier ships them directly to the customer, so the seller does not hold stock. Beginners should confirm they understand this model before they search for products. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0913.002]
  • High-ticket dropshipping combines a low startup risk (under $500 to start), an evergreen model, profit of $300 or more per sale, a sellable branded asset, a time to first profit of about 30 days, and skills that transfer to other online businesses. Low-ticket dropshipping of $10 to $100 items from China, or via eBay or Amazon, keeps low risk and transferable skills but lacks the other benefits. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0458.002]
  • Dropshipping usually targets items above $200, and sometimes above $750 to $800, where the supplier ships and the store owner provides all customer service. Use this range for high-ticket products. [L1153.007]
  • Note on price thresholds: the two sources use different cut-offs. The $10 to $100 range [L0458.002] describes low-ticket products, while the $200 and above range [L1153.007] describes the usual dropshipping target. Both are older or undated advice, so check current supplier prices before you set a product range. [L0458.002, L1153.007]

How orders and payments flow

Basic cycle (older advice, 2021-22)

  1. Open a store and attract customers to it. [L0458.003]
  2. When a customer orders, place the same order with your approved supplier. [L0458.003]
  3. The supplier ships directly to the customer, so you never hold inventory. [L0458.003]
  4. Pay the supplier only after the customer has paid you. [L0458.003]
  5. You manage the website and marketing while the supplier handles fulfillment. [L0458.003]

Full order flow (older advice, 2021-22)

  1. The customer pays on your website by card or PayPal, and the charge is immediate. [L1480.001]
  2. The payment provider sends the money automatically to your bank account. [L1480.001]
  3. Only after you receive the customer's money do you place the order with your supplier. [L1480.001]
  4. The supplier invoices you. [L1480.001]
  5. You pay the supplier. [L1480.001]
  6. The supplier ships the product directly to the customer, who receives it at home or at their business. The cycle repeats for each order, and repeat customers can be sold other items. [L1480.001]

Order flow with an app-based supplier (Source C)

  1. The customer places an order on the online store, for example one built with Shopify. [L0760.002]
  2. The store owner forwards the order information to a supplier or fulfillment company, typically through that company's app with a few clicks. [L0760.002]
  3. The supplier picks the product from the manufacturer or stock, checks its quality, packs the order and ships it directly to the customer's door. [L0760.002]

Summary: the store is built, the customer orders, the order is passed to the supplier, and the supplier handles sourcing, packing and shipping. [L0760.002]

Payment timing and cash

  • Payment flows first from the customer to the seller at the time of purchase. The seller then orders from the supplier and pays for the product afterwards, so no large stock is bought upfront. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1481.009]
  • The payment gateways in use commonly pay out the sale amount on the same day, minus small fees. This means the seller has the money before paying the supplier. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1481.010]
  • At the start, pay each supplier only after the customer's payment has been received, so no cash goes out before money comes in. Paying suppliers before the customer's funds reach your bank account is an option to consider later, once the business is more experienced and earns more. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1480.034]
  • Dropshipping is self-funding: customer payments cover each supplier order, so profit can be taken at the end of each month. A physical product business requires fronting cash for inventory, so profit only comes after a difficult cash cycle is passed. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1154.014]

Who is involved

  • The model has three players: the customer, you as the seller, and the supplier, with freight companies handling the physical delivery. It is simple in structure, but that does not make it easy to run. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1480.002]
  • Marketing is the engine of the business. Without it, customers never find the website, so no sale or service can happen. High-ticket dropshipping is therefore effectively a marketing business that sells through a store. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1480.003]

Benefits

Low capital and low risk

  • Dropshipping has a low barrier to entry: no inventory is required, upfront costs and risk are reduced, no physical storefront is needed, and you can test new products and markets without large commitments. [L0007.019]
  • Because no inventory is bought in advance, the seller avoids the risk of unsold stock and can spend the budget on marketing and selling instead of warehousing. [L0760.006]
  • Dropshipping eliminates overstocking and understocking risk. You test products and niches without financial exposure, and you keep complete control over inventory levels. [L0007.021]
  • Low-priced products are a lower-risk way to start dropshipping because they need little capital and no storage. Choosing items that ship directly from the supplier keeps inventory risk near zero. [L0345.001]
  • Dropshipping is a low-risk way to gain business experience, since the main costs at the start are ad spend and the Shopify subscription. The real risk is wasted ad spend, so keep spend tied to measured results. [L0388.020]
  • Dropshipping is commonly recommended as a starting point for beginners because it needs little money, a laptop and an internet connection, with no physical store, warehouse or upfront stock purchase. [L0760.009]

Speed, scale and flexibility

  • Dropshipping enables quick scalability. You can expand product offerings rapidly, keep more control over operations, reduce warehouse and shipping overhead, and achieve better profit margins due to lower fixed costs. [L0007.020]
  • Dropshipping enables rapid pivoting to market trends, the ability to expand or scale back product offerings quickly, and reduced financial risk from diversified offerings. This flexibility is a core competitive advantage. [L0007.022]
  • Dropshipping allows a seller to switch products and suppliers easily, so many product ideas and niches can be tested without the cost of managing inventory. [L0760.007]
  • A dropshipping store can list as many products as needed, which allows several offers in the same niche to be sold to existing and new customers. [L0760.011]
  • Dropshipping eliminates the operational burden of inventory management and fulfillment. The capacity freed by outsourcing these functions can be directed to revenue-generating activities, such as marketing, customer acquisition and sales, which directly drive growth. [L0007.028]
  • Dropshipping applies the lean startup logic: a seller tests an existing market product first, then customizes branding and holds inventory only after the product proves demand. [L0760.020]

Control and margin

  • Selling on your own store avoids marketplace commissions, which allows higher prices or better margins than on platforms that take a cut of each sale. [L0760.008]
  • Owning the storefront gives the seller control over marketing, pricing and all other business aspects, instead of being bound by a marketplace's rules. [L0760.012]
  • Dropshipping removes warehousing and shipping overhead, so margin can be put into marketing rather than storage. The lesson presents this as a reason for higher profit margins than inventory-based models. [L0760.013]
  • Dropshipping gives higher margins and more control, because you own the customer, can cut costs by negotiating with suppliers, and can run every ad type. (2023.) [L0855.076]
  • On an own store the seller receives the customer data, which marketplaces typically keep. This data supports strategic decisions, community building and repeat sales. [L0760.026]

Skills and transfer

  • Dropshipping is a starting point for acquiring skills and generating cash, and the same skills can open other businesses, such as a local marketing agency or a coaching business. [L1432.034]
  • Once you can sell and market well, those skills transfer to agencies, coaching, consulting, online education, buying other e-commerce businesses, or building your own brand. The underlying sales and marketing concepts stay the same across products even when tactics change. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1480.008]

Risks and limits

Limits of the dropshipping model

  • You sell products that competitors also sell, so the product cannot be your differentiator. Better marketing and better customer service than competitors lead to more sales of the same products. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1480.004]
  • Dropshipping is a real business that has existed for at least 15 years and is still viable, and it is cheaper to start than many other models. It still requires real marketing and real customer service. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1480.005]
  • Dropshipping is a different proposition from selling your own brand. As a reseller you compete as one of many brands in a category, so relying on a single brand is risky, because if customers do not want it, nothing sells. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1456.033]
  • Dropshipping from China is not a long-term, sustainable business model. Do not continue operating an unprofitable China-based store to recover investment. Instead, focus on brand dropshipping with US-based suppliers. [L0253.033]
  • Dropshipping stores often fade after two or three months. Brands that last test consistently after a winning product and invest in customer experience. [L1213.071]
  • Platforms such as Facebook do not ban dropshipping as a model. They judge account quality, customer ratings and copied content, so the way the store operates is what matters. [L1213.072]

Cash flow and ad spend

  • Combine short-term cash flow with long-term recurring revenue. Cash from the first sale funds acquisition, while repeat revenue produces the profit. A churn-and-burn model depends on constant cash for traffic and is stressful. A long-game-only model suits high-ticket sales that rely on referrals and high margins. [L0378.004]
  • The real risk in dropshipping is wasted ad spend, so keep spend tied to measured results. [L0388.020]

Building your own brand versus dropshipping

Risks of starting a brand from scratch

  • Starting a brand from scratch carries five major risks:
    1. High upfront investment in product development and inventory. [L0007.026]
    2. Difficulty choosing a product or niche with sufficient market demand. [L0007.026]
    3. Time-consuming process of building a website, sourcing materials and manufacturing. [L0007.026]
    4. Difficulty attracting customers and gaining traction in crowded markets. [L0007.026]
    5. Risk of overestimating demand and holding excess unsellable inventory. [L0007.026]
  • Building a product and brand from scratch takes around four to six months from idea to product. It requires upfront investment in development and inventory, and it risks choosing a product with insufficient demand or overestimating sales and ending with excess stock. [L0760.016]

Lean startup approach

  • The lean startup method (described in Eric Ries' book The Lean Startup) states that a business should validate the product and product-market fit before investing heavily in production or marketing, using a minimum viable product and a proof of concept. [L0760.019]

Brand over the long term

  • A dropshipping brand takes longer to build, but it can produce steadier, more predictable income and allow the owner more time and stability. The trade-off is lower speed to launch and more upfront patience. [L1251.010]
  • Running a store to scale a single product and then shutting it down is the disposable model, which limits long-term income. Plan the store as a multi-product brand from the start, even if it begins as dropshipping. [L1251.008]
  • Dropshipping remained viable in 2023, but it needed more brand work to stay sustainable. Owners who treat the store as a long-term brand rather than a short-term sales vehicle are better positioned. [L1251.007]
  • In the business model that sells other brands' products through your store, the store itself is the brand. It does not involve introducing your own private-label products. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1275.055]

Compared with Amazon FBA and private label

Private label on Amazon

  • With Amazon FBA you own the private label product, so you set the price and decide how much inventory to hold. Unlike dropshipping, you cannot blame a supplier when an item is out of stock, because the inventory risk is yours. [L1153.001]
  • Amazon FBA handles storage, shipping, returns, refunds and most customer service for the seller. This lets one person or a small team scale a product without a large staff. [L1153.002]
  • In FBA the seller's core jobs are product selection, finding a manufacturer, managing inventory levels, building a high-converting product page and driving traffic, often with Amazon ads. Inventory management is the seller's biggest responsibility, especially before Q4 (the holiday season). [L1153.003]
  • One FBA product can reach about $10,000 profit per month, but this takes work and is not an overnight process. Most sellers become profitable within one to three months of starting, once the first inventory order is sold. [L1153.004]
  • Compared with dropshipping, FBA sellers source and own their product, face a longer and trickier supplier phase, and depend on Amazon as a third-party platform. Overseas suppliers add time zone differences and a language barrier, so the seller must plan for both. [L1153.006]
  • FBA private label products usually sit in a lower price range where impulse buying is strong, while dropshipping usually targets items above $200. [L1153.007]
  • Amazon pays sellers directly into their bank account every two weeks in eligible countries, whatever bank the seller uses. The payout schedule supports cash flow planning for the business. [L1153.008]
  • Case: a seller in the course group sold 430 units in two weeks, generating almost $9,500 in sales at an average price of $22 per unit. Over that span the sales data moved from 430 to 513 units, and the two weeks together brought in almost $10,000 in sales. This shows that a sub-$25 product can still produce solid revenue when the volume is consistent. [L1153.009]
  • Case: a different seller sold 129 units in one week for $5,000 in sales at an average price of $43 per unit. Results like this show that a price around $40 to $45 can still generate strong revenue without needing a very high unit count. [L1153.010]
  • Many new sellers aim to sell under $10, but this is a poor strategy for Amazon FBA. The course recommends choosing a product that can carry enough profit per unit to fund paid advertising. [L1153.011]
  • Amazon FBA carries risks: high competition and saturation in popular categories, very thin price margins, Amazon controlling customer data and branding, a 20 to 30 percent commission on each sale, and risk of account suspension or restriction for policy violations. [L0760.014]
  • Selling on Amazon requires bulk inventory and a higher risk. Margins can be as low as 15% or $5 to $10 per unit. Amazon keeps customer data and takes a cut of revenue, which makes building a brand harder than in dropshipping. [L0788.046]
  • The 15% commission figure comes from a 2020 comparison, where marketplace commissions were the benchmark for Shopping Actions. Amazon was cited as charging 15% of the sale price, while the original Shopping Actions commission averaged about 12% and ranged from roughly 8% to above 12%. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0738.035]

Note on Amazon commission: the sources give 15% (2020 comparison) [L0738.035] and 20 to 30% (Source C) [L0760.014]. The 15% value is older. Check the current fee schedule before you set prices. [L0738.035, L0760.014]

Digital products

Why digital products

  • Digital products have no fulfillment costs and no shipping delays, and they can educate buyers. They are a separate business model that requires its own research on creation, advertising and finding products to sell. [L0502.042]
  • Digital products such as ebooks give a gross margin of roughly 90 percent, because the product is created once and then sold repeatedly without extra cost per order. The only ongoing cost is traffic, paid or organic. [L0513.001]
  • Digital products such as ebooks and digital planners carry no fulfillment cost. This makes them profitable at low margins while still delivering the product instantly. [L1184.062]
  • Note on digital margins: one source gives a gross margin of roughly 90% for ebooks [L0513.001], while another describes them as profitable at low margins because there is no fulfillment cost [L1184.062]. Both apply: the first describes the margin on each sale, the second describes the profitability of low-priced items. [L0513.001, L1184.062]
  • Digital products have instant fulfillment. The buyer receives a download link or the PDF by email right after purchase, so there are no shipping delays or lost-parcel problems. Because fulfillment is easy and customers get what they expect, customer support load and refund impact are smaller than with physical shipped products. [L0513.002]
  • Digital downloads let a seller monetize traffic from countries the physical product cannot ship to, and designs with no written instructions avoid a language barrier. Use digital offers to capture buyers that a physical-only product would lose. [L1153.081]
  • Market saturation is not a real concern for knowledge products, because each new author can add value through a different point of view on the same topic. [L0513.003]
  • Digital products can run alongside physical dropshipping products in the same business, and copywriting skills transfer to both. [L0513.004]
  • When selling the same digital product in another country, adjust the pricing, the payment providers and the wording to that market. Expertise in how one country differs from another, such as its payment setup and local terms, is itself a value that can be turned into an ebook. [L0513.047]
  • Digital products such as Lightroom presets sold well because many people lack the skill or time to edit photos themselves and buy presets to adjust. Choose digital products that solve a clear problem for the buyer. [L1214.020]

Product size rules

  • A recipe ebook should not be sold below 20 pages. Other digital products can be shorter, for example a two-page Facebook ads strategy, as long as the buyer gets the full value they need. [L1131.038]

Starting with an information product

  • A first online venture can be a low-cost info product, such as a self-published e-book, used mainly to learn how online sales work. Asking an established operator what they sell and how they market it can lead to a more proven model, such as dropshipping. [L0863.014]

Affiliate marketing

Model and types

  • In affiliate marketing you promote a product you neither create nor fulfill, and you earn a commission when a visitor you referred buys it. For digital products sold through affiliate directories such as ClickBank, the merchant handles refunds and customer questions. [L0514.001]
  • A personal brand built on consistent content, such as a blog, podcast or regular guest posts, creates credibility and a loyal audience that follows you across products. It is a long-term model with a slow start and more time commitment. Once established, you can keep promoting new products in the niche. [L0514.002]
  • A funnel-based affiliate business has no personal face and builds a weaker audience connection, but it launches faster. You can set up several niche sites, most of the work is front-loaded, and afterward the sites run largely on autopilot with reduced maintenance. [L0514.003]
  • Digital affiliate offers include downloads, software subscriptions such as website builders and email autoresponders, and non-online offers such as cruises. Choosing among these depends on what your audience already searches for and wants to buy. [L0514.005]

Print on demand

  • Print on demand is very hard to make meaningful money with, because profit per sale is tiny. It is better treated as a brand-building add-on than as a profit source, especially since personal items such as clothing and bedding have high return rates when fit or feel does not match expectations. [L1097.005]
  • A brand merchandise store that sells inexpensive print-on-demand items such as t-shirts, mugs and hats has very thin margins. Keep it for brand merchandise rather than treating it as a profit source. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0852.113]
  • Print on demand is a community-building add-on, not a primary revenue source. Add it only after a store has a loyal audience that wants branded products. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0961.078]
  • Print on demand is not recommended, because product prices are too low, margins are too thin, ads are hard to run profitably, and there is too little profit per sale for the work involved. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0967.089]

Note on print on demand: the 2020 sources differ in strength. One treats it as merchandise only [L0852.113], one as a community add-on [L0961.078], and one as not recommended [L0967.089]. The 2025 source gives the most current position: a brand-building add-on, not a profit source [L1097.005]. [L0852.113, L0961.078, L0967.089, L1097.005]

Marketplaces

Marketplace selling as a separate model

  • Marketplace selling is a separate model from dropshipping. The seller lists products on large retail marketplaces without stocking them, and fulfills an order only once it sells. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0663.029]
  • Etsy suits small handmade or home businesses and is less suited for building a branded store than Shopify, which gives more control over brand presentation. [L0561.050]
  • Depending on a single marketplace is a single point of failure: an account suspension or restriction for a policy violation can shut the whole business down. A seller who runs their own website keeps the business alive regardless of what any one marketplace decides. [L0760.025]
  • Marketplaces such as Amazon can remove a seller's account and destroy the business overnight. An own website gives control over the brand, the customer data and the option to sell the business later. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0771.006]

Is dropshipping still viable?

  • Dropshipping is not dead. Claims that it has died come up every year, while e-commerce keeps growing. It would only die if people stopped buying online. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1480.006]
  • Dropshipping is a real business that has existed for at least 15 years and is still viable. It is cheaper to start than many other models. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1480.005]
  • Dropshipping is a short-term model only when done poorly. Done correctly, it builds a brand and customer knowledge that can be used later to sell your own products. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1481.025]
  • Dropshipping stores often fade after two or three months. Brands that last test consistently after a winning product and invest in customer experience. [L1213.071]
  • Platforms such as Facebook do not ban dropshipping as a model. They judge account quality, customer ratings and copied content. [L1213.072]
  • After the pandemic, e-commerce adoption normalized to the historical 20-year growth trend. The spike during lockdown created excess capacity. Shopify laid off 1,000 people (10% of its workforce) on July 26, 2022, because growth returned to normal rather than to a sustained high trajectory. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0272.055]
  • Shopify's public data reportedly showed about a 10% revenue increase for the average store in Q1 2025 compared with Q1 2024. Use this only as a general market signal. [L0972.003]
  • AI dropshipping keeps the same business model as regular dropshipping: selling products online without holding inventory. The difference is that AI tools are applied at every step of the process, so the seller can move faster, smarter and leaner. [L1459.001]
  • Between 2016 and 2018, vendors flooded AliExpress listings with lengthy delivery times. The approach paid off because Facebook advertising was inexpensive, yet the buyer experience suffered. [L1459.003]
  • Branded dropshipping took off from 2019 through 2021. Quicker delivery, stronger ad creatives and bespoke storefronts distinguished the successful operators from the rest, but most workflows stayed hands-on and experimentation moved slowly. [L1459.004]
  • From 2023 onward, the AI era allows a business to run with fewer people, lower costs and data-driven decisions instead of intuition alone. [L1459.005]
  • Note on viability: the sources do not conflict on whether the model works. "Not dead" [L1480.006] applies to dropshipping as a whole, while "fades after two or three months" [L1213.071] applies to stores run without a brand plan. The short-term outcome depends on how the store is built. [L1480.006, L1213.071, L1481.025]

Choosing a business model

  • When choosing an online business model, compare the options on three criteria: whether it can be run remotely, how much startup capital it needs, and whether it can be built part-time next to a job. Common options to compare are affiliate marketing, selling a service, selling online education and selling physical products through e-commerce. Physical-product dropshipping can meet all three criteria for someone who still has a job and fixed financial obligations. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1014.019]
  • Dropshipping with approved suppliers and low-priced products suits a first step with low capital. Choose the model that matches your remote working needs, your starting capital and the time you can give alongside other obligations. [L1014.019, L0345.001, L1283.104]
