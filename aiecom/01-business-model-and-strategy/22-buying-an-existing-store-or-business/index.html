1.22 Buying an existing store or business

Build versus buy

  • Building a store from scratch is generally preferred to buying one (older advice, 2021). A store with roughly 10,000 to 40,000 in profit can often be rebuilt within a few weeks, so buying it is rarely worth the price. [L0247.002]
  • Buying a finished store is usually more expensive than building one, so building and growing the store until it produces income is the more common route (older advice, 2020). [L0967.088]
  • Build online stores from scratch rather than buying existing ones (older advice, 2020). [L0852.005]
  • Buying an existing online business is generally not recommended for most buyers, because e-commerce businesses are easily replicated. Starting a new store is usually a better use of several hundred thousand dollars than buying one (2024). [L0971.100]
  • Buying a profitable store at a high multiple rarely makes sense (older advice, 2019), because a store that sells about one order per day is achievable with focused work and little money. The unit does not state the multiple itself. [L1002.002]
  • A better route is to find which niches and stores are for sale, check their numbers, and rebuild a comparable model yourself (older advice, 2019). [L1002.002]

When buying can make sense

  • Buyers purchase an existing drop shipping store to:
    1. shorten their learning curve,
    2. trade money for time,
    3. use momentum the store has already built, or
    4. put a unique advantage of their own to work. [L0858.002]
  • For high-ticket dropshipping, the choice depends on your resources (older advice, 2021-2022). [L0949.001]

  • Only content sites with established traffic, such as a forum or blog running for around 15 years, are worth buying. Pure e-commerce stores are not (older advice, 2020). [L0852.006]

Who should not buy yet

  • Buyers without prior experience building stores from scratch often overpay and see sales fall soon after purchase, because they cannot judge the quality of the store (older advice, 2021-2022). [L0949.002]
  • Learning to build a store first makes it much easier to evaluate one that is for sale (older advice, 2021-2022). [L0949.002]

Verifying figures before paying

  1. Verify the seller's figures, supplier relationships, supplier lead times and sales trend. Sellers can present numbers that hide problems, so check everything against the underlying data (older advice, 2021-2022). [L0949.003]
  2. Do your own check of revenue and margins. Many small online sellers keep poor financial records, so the figures they report are often unreliable (older advice, 2021-2022). [L0949.004]
  3. Check the length of the sales history before you agree on a multiple. See the valuation benchmarks below. [L1115.093]

Aged domains

  • Buying aged domain names is generally not worth the money in drop shipping. Spending large amounts on several aged domains has not paid off (older advice, 2021). [L0853.021]
  • An established store with an aged domain, existing backlinks and running traffic lets you pull growth levers quickly (older advice, 2021-2022). [L0949.005]
  • An aged domain with authority does not guarantee fast search rankings, so budget time for SEO even after buying (older advice, 2021-2022). [L0949.005]
  • Both statements apply to different things. An aged domain bought on its own has not been worth the money, while an aged domain that comes with a store that already has backlinks and traffic can speed up growth. [L0853.021, L0949.005]

Shopify Exchange and broker listings

  • Stores listed for sale on Shopify Exchange, as seen around its 2019 launch, were often overpriced, had little or no sales history and carried unrealistic valuations. Check the real revenue history and the asking price critically before buying a store there (older advice, 2019). [L0357.067]
  • On a business broker listing for a dropshipping store, the sale includes the domain and all site contents and files. [L0458.062]
  • The URL, Google Analytics access and profit-and-loss statement are visible only after you unlock the listing (2021-2022, older). [L0458.062]
  • Review the listing carefully before buying (older advice, 2021-2022). [L0458.062]

Risks for buyers

  • You inherit processes you did not design and must review them carefully. [L0949.001]
  • You can overpay and watch sales fall, because you cannot judge quality without building stores yourself. [L0949.002]
  • The seller's numbers can hide problems. [L0949.003]
  • Reported revenue and margins are often unreliable when the seller kept poor records. [L0949.004]
  • Small, unproven stores sold for roughly $5,000 to $20,000 are risky for beginners. [L0852.005]
  • An aged domain does not guarantee fast rankings. [L0949.005]
  • E-commerce businesses are easily replicated, which weakens the case for paying for one. [L0971.100]

Learning due diligence

  • The podcast Acquisitions Anonymous has its host vet two businesses a week as if buying them. It is a useful way to learn due diligence before purchasing an online store (older advice, 2021-2022). [L0949.064]
