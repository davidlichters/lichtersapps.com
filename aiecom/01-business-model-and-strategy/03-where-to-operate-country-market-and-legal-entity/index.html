1.3 Where to operate: country, market and legal entity

Choosing the home or target market

Principles

  • Choose markets by buying power, not by cheap traffic. A country where people have little money to spend is a poor place to test a product. A market where people buy a lot online, especially cheap items with fast delivery, is a good candidate. [L0361.052]
  • Dropshipping can run in a country with an established e-commerce market, such as the US, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand or European countries. Where e-commerce is less developed, you can still run the business in the US from abroad, but this adds setup steps and costs. The US is the easiest large market for this. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0458.011]
  • The model works only in a country with an established e-commerce market where Google Shopping is running. In countries with underdeveloped e-commerce, the local store may not work yet. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0771.010]
  • To check whether your country has a real domestic e-commerce market, ask whether you regularly buy online from websites based in your own country. If you do, local suppliers and payment options probably exist. If you mostly buy from overseas sites, a store aimed at local customers will be harder to run. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1405.036]
  • Entering a new country should follow research of that market, not assumptions. Countries such as Australia and the UK were once advised against but later proved successful, so a country like Spain can be a good market if your own research supports it. (Older, 2019.) [L1068.008]
  • The country-specific demand test is an addition to the core product research method, not a replacement. Every other part of the core research method still applies when you target a foreign market. [L1072.012]
  • A core dropshipping blueprint can be run from any country. International sellers should keep most of their effort on the core process and treat country-specific adjustments as small add-ons, not a separate model. [L0587.001]
  • Choose the countries you advertise to before you set prices. The main markets at the time were the US, UK, Australia, New Zealand and Canada. Each market needs its own price in local currency, because a price that looks normal in one currency can look far too cheap or expensive in another. [L0999.070]
  • The local currency for the 800 price minimum is the currency of the country you operate from. If you are based in a country without an established e-commerce market, the reference is USD and the business is set up in the US. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0994.029]

Home-country default in detail

  • For a first high-ticket business, the best default is to run it from the country where you currently live. This keeps shipping, customs and currency complexity low. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0781.006]
  • If your country has an established e-commerce market, you can run high-ticket dropshipping there. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0781.007]
  • If you live in a country without an established e-commerce market, do not start the business there. Choose a different target market instead. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0781.008]
  • When your country has no e-commerce market, the default alternative is the US market or the market of the country where you hold citizenship. The second option only applies if that market is workable for you. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0781.009]
  • For a first store, sell in the country where you live if that country has a real domestic e-commerce market. Local suppliers, local currency and local payment accounts avoid cross-border payment problems. Expansion into other countries can wait until the first business runs well. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1405.001]
  • For a first store, a market in the owner's home country can keep the business simpler when enough suppliers there will sell to dropshippers. Cross-border sourcing from a neighbouring country is a workable alternative, but it adds currency and shipping questions to the pricing. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0905.027]
  • A business can be run across borders, with the business registered in one country while the operator lives in another. Partnerships with owners in different countries, such as Australia and the US, are also possible. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1014.006]
  • Example: a first store in home lighting (chandeliers, pendant lights and wall lights) sourced its products in the same country and sold to local customers. Starting with domestic sourcing and domestic buyers is a simple way to launch a first store in a single market. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1014.007]

Step-by-step: choosing a first market (high-ticket, Source B method)

  1. Determine the country where you currently live. [L0781.013]
  2. Check whether that country has an existing e-commerce market. [L0781.013]
  3. If it does, run your first high-ticket business there with local suppliers, products stocked in local warehouses and local customers. [L0781.013]
  4. If it does not, use the US market or the market of your citizenship country as your default target. [L0781.013]

(Older, 2021-22.)

Examples for this method:

  • A citizen of one country who lives abroad in a market without e-commerce can choose between a business in their home country's market and a business in the US market. For instance, an Australian living in Thailand could run either an Australian-market or a US-market business. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0781.011]
  • Thailand and Singapore are cited as places where residents often lack an established e-commerce market. Someone living there would default to the US market or their citizenship country's market rather than the local one. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0781.014]
  • A UK-based high-ticket seller can source from UK suppliers for UK customers, or possibly from European suppliers. Brexit makes the European sourcing option harder, so UK-only sourcing is usually the simpler starting point. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0781.015]

Country notes

United States

  • Focus primarily on the US market because it is larger. (Older, 2019.) [L0356.011]
  • The United States produced the best results among the big markets tested, so the focus was placed there. Test countries one at a time and keep the strongest market as the base. [L0694.033]
  • The US is the easiest large market for a foreign operator. Australia is hard for non-residents. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0771.011]

Canada

Sources differ on how to combine Canada and the US. Use the version that matches your situation:

  • Source B (2021-22): Canadian residents can sell in Canada, in the US, or in both, since the US is geographically close and easier to reach from Canada. (Older.) [L1405.003]
  • Older Source A advice (2018-2019): make the US the main market and sell to Canada as a secondary market. (Older.) [L1085.081, L0356.011]
  • Older Source A advice (2019): Canadian sellers usually get better results by drop shipping to both the US and Canada rather than only to Canada. (Older.) [L1068.009]
  • A 2025 unit: a store can sell only to Canada, or sell to the US from Canada, and both approaches have worked for operators. The US market was described as more than 10 times larger than Canada's. [L1097.003]
  • Selling into the US from Canada can be harder because heavy items cost more to ship across the border. Many suppliers with US warehouses are therefore chosen for a Canadian business. A US EIN is recommended for doing business in the US. [L0360.084]

United Kingdom

  • The UK market can be started as easily as the US if the owner knows that market better, since members there do similarly well and the time zone is the same. Price research should follow the chosen market. [L0941.006]
  • A UK-based seller can source from UK suppliers for UK customers. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0781.015]

Australia and New Zealand

  • Australia is hard for non-residents. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1405.005]
  • Countries such as Australia were once advised against but later proved successful. (Older, 2019.) [L1068.008]
  • After its home market, a brand expanded into Canada and Australia. For each new country it looked for local micro-influencers and fashion bloggers to build sales there. [L0790.101]

Europe

  • EU markets are easier for a beginner to enter than the US, Canada, UK and Australia, because CPMs are much lower and competition is lower. The top four English-speaking markets carry high CPMs, which makes an unproven single store hard to launch there. [L0660.005]
  • For a German-speaking seller, the expansion order is: test the US or Canada, then Australia and New Zealand. If the product fails there, test Germany, Switzerland and Austria, where you can communicate fluently with customers. [L0549.013]
  • Expanding from North America into the EU is often impractical, because most suppliers have no EU warehouses and shipping can cost around ten times the North American rate. Expansion makes more sense when a neighboring market can be served from existing supply, such as moving from Spain to France. (Older, 2020.) [L1277.009]

Asia

  • The author advises against launching dropshipping into Asian markets such as Thailand for now, because the market is less proven and needs more effort. Local marketplaces such as Lazada show that some companies are willing to take that pioneer risk. Whether to enter depends on goals, risk tolerance and available time. [L0910.003]

Cross-border shipping, sourcing and pricing

  • High-ticket dropshipping is generally a domestic business, because sellers rarely ship products from their home country across borders. Some regions, such as parts of Europe, are exceptions. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0781.001]
  • Cross-border shipping of high-ticket products adds cost through bulkier packages, higher freight rates, customs duties and currency conversion. These costs can significantly raise the price for either the seller or the buyer. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0781.003]
  • Launching a cross-border high-ticket model without pricing in freight, customs duties and currency conversion can leave little or no margin. Check these costs before choosing an international setup. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0781.004]
  • Your customers should be located in the country where your supplier stocks the products, so the supplier's location determines your target market. For example, US-based products are sold to US customers. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0781.005]
  • For a store that targets a country where you do not live, use suppliers located in that country. Shipping and tariffs from abroad are the main reasons this matters. (Older, 2020.) [L0468.014]
  • Dropshipping from a non-US country works if the supplier ships from a US warehouse or distribution center. The store can then be run under a US company set-up. Check the choice of entity with a forum or professional before setup. (Older, 2019.) [L0357.065]
  • Expansion from North America into the EU is often impractical, because shipping can cost around ten times the North American rate. (Older, 2020.) [L1277.009]

Running the business from abroad

  • A seller based outside the US can run a store that sells to US customers without relocating, since the US market has many active sellers. Confirm the legal and tax requirements for your country and the US states you sell into before launch. (Older, 2020.) [L0961.092]
  • A business owner living outside the US can still apply to US suppliers, provided the business is set up in the US. This usually means forming a US company from abroad. Confirm the business structure before applying, so the setup does not block supplier approval. [L0470.100]
  • Running the store from a non-US company can still work with US suppliers, because suppliers receive payment in the same way. The legal side should be checked with an accountant or a community forum. (Older, 2018.) [L1085.082]
  • Sellers from outside the US can follow the same setup process to sell into the US market. Only local market specifics differ by country. (Older, 2019.) [L1275.056]
  • The business can be run from any country, as long as the operation is managed remotely. Many stores run from outside the US successfully. (Older, 2019.) [L1086.053]
  • A store owner can target a country without living there. If a home country lacks enough high-ticket suppliers for a chosen niche, the business can target another market such as the US or UK. (Older, 2020.) [L0967.102]
  • Selling into a larger market from a different country than your base is possible, so the home market is not a fixed limit. (Older, 2021.) [L0968.106]
  • A dropshipping store can run semi-passively from abroad if customer contact, supplier contact and order handling are set up correctly from the start. It still needs regular check-ins, such as email checks several days a week, so it is not fully passive. [L0910.001]
  • Calls, emails and orders arrive in the store's home time zone. A store owner working in a distant time zone should plan coverage for the hours they are asleep. The coverage can come from short routines or from delegated staff. [L0910.002]
  • Slow travel, meaning staying at least one month in each location, reduces distractions and travel admin. It also keeps the daily routine stable enough to work efficiently. [L1441.002]

Market testing and expansion order

Testing

  • Validate a product in a large market such as the USA or Canada first. Then take the same proven product to another market, since the same product can perform very differently across countries. [L0549.012]
  • Breaking ad results down by country showed the United States outperforming all other markets for a Facebook-driven store. The seller concentrated spend on the US for four to five months before testing other markets again. [L1127.009]
  • Worldwide targeting combined with free shipping made profit difficult to manage. The worldwide spend was reduced and budgets moved to the top 5 to 10 countries. Scaling down a weak region can protect margin while the strongest markets grow. [L1127.010]
  • Choose test and expansion markets by buying power, not only by cheap traffic. [L0361.052]
  • Treat the first international order as a milestone that proves the model can scale. Selling the same product outside the home market shows that the business model can grow simply by adding volume and reach. [L1438.005]
  • When a product works in one country, expand to other European markets such as Germany and France with a separate store for each market. Use a separate ad account or agency account to gather those markets' data, rather than mixing them in one store. [L1440.059]

Expansion within one store or separate stores

  • One Shopify store can sell to Australia and Europe by adding those countries to the shipping options, so a separate store is not needed at the start. (Older, 2020.) [L0664.085]
  • Build a separate country store, such as a.com.au domain, only when that market should carry a smaller set of brands, for example 20 of 100. The country store can keep the same logo and company information and list only the products chosen for that market. (Older, 2020.) [L0664.085]

Order of expansion

  • Start with one country and scale within it before expanding abroad. The home market alone can supply enough revenue for most sellers. Countries with about 5 million (New Zealand) and 17 million (Netherlands) inhabitants were cited as workable markets in 2020. (Older, 2020.) [L1277.008]
  • Test countries one at a time and keep the strongest market as the base. [L0694.033]

Small home markets

  • Sellers in a small home market such as New Zealand, Sweden or Sydney can still succeed. Check shipping costs and taxes to nearby markets first, and broaden your niche. Only expand to neighboring markets if shipping and duties are reasonable. (Older, 2020.) [L0468.015]
  • Drop shipping can be started in a country outside the US, but the market and the number of buyers in that country affect the size of the business. A small local market can be a poor fit, so consider the US or other large markets. (Older, 2021.) [L0801.171]
  • A small local market can still work when the store's niche is well chosen. The best choice is often to test a local market alongside the larger US market. (Older, 2021.) [L0801.172]
  • In a small home market, widen the product range so you can find enough suppliers and products to sell. (Older, 2021.) [L0968.106]
  • Small-population markets often lack enough search volume for a narrow niche. In that case, move up one level to a broader category that gets enough traffic. (Older, 2020.) [L1277.010]
  • Start with one country and scale within it before expanding abroad. Countries with about 5 million (New Zealand) and 17 million (Netherlands) inhabitants were cited as workable markets in 2020. (Older, 2020.) [L1277.008]

Business entity types

Sole proprietor

  • A sole proprietorship makes the owner and the business legally the same. Personal and business assets are mixed and exposed to business liabilities. [L0695.001]
  • A sole proprietor is a business run under the owner's personal name with no formal registration. An LLC is formed through formal documents with the state. An S-corp is a tax designation applied to an existing LLC. A corporation is generally not needed for a dropshipping business. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1415.002]
  • You can legally start a US dropshipping business as a sole proprietor. It is wiser to form an LLC from the beginning, because later conversion to an LLC after selling a business adds work. Choose a generic business name, form the LLC in your state, and do not spend long deciding. Any structure works if you commit and move forward. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1415.001]
  • A business can start as a sole proprietor with an EIN, and an LLC is not required at the beginning. Sole proprietor, LLC and S-corp each have trade-offs, so compare them before choosing. [L0970.010]
  • Many beginners start as a sole proprietor, form an LLC and later an S-corp with an accountant once sales come in. A general business name works for several future stores. [L0934.005]
  • Many founders start as a sole proprietor using a personal bank account. They later form a corporation with a business bank account and an accountant once profits arrive. A new business can also set up separate entities from day one if it wants full separation. [L0941.003]
  • The legal entity choice is a personal decision. Many owners start as a sole proprietor because the risk is low, and then set up a corporation once the business is making money. (Older, 2019.) [L1068.014]
  • An LLC is the recommended structure from the start. A sole proprietorship is only a reasonable shortcut for someone with almost nothing to lose who wants to avoid an extra filing. [L0695.002]

LLC

  • An LLC is a separate business entity that shields personal assets from the business. [L0695.001]
  • Outside the US, a dropshipping business usually has two legal structure options: a sole trader or a limited liability company. Names differ by country, but Commonwealth countries use fairly similar structures. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0702.002]
  • The legal structure, whether sole trader or company, is not visible to customers. It affects liability and taxes, not the storefront, checkout or customer experience. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0702.030]
  • Use a generic business entity name that is not tied to one niche. Related niches such as desks and chairs can share one entity, while an unrelated niche such as greenhouses should get its own new entity. [L0970.008]
  • An LLC name does not have to appear on the website. Many stores show only the store name, and the legal entity can stay private. (Older, 2020.) [L0358.050]
  • The LLC itself does not need a website. Websites are built only for sales stores. [L0695.004, L0934.008]
  • A new business name should be the umbrella company that owns related stores. Use a different company name for an unrelated niche. [L0971.032]
  • Each store should have its own generic-named company rather than a store name in the company name. [L1114.009]
  • A business address that is not the owner's home address is recommended. Do not use a virtual address for Google Business Profile, since it is not required and could be a concern for Merchant Center. [L1115.027]
  • Consider using a DBA when the LLC name differs from the store name, so the store is legally doing business under its public name. [L1115.029]
  • A DBA can officially link a store URL to the business entity. The legal entity name is what goes on banking and tax forms and into the business information in Shopify settings, while the store name can differ. [L0970.009]

S-corp

  • For a US business that is in it for the long term, an LLC elected to be taxed as an S-corporation is considered the best structure. Part of the income is paid as a set salary and the rest as distributions, which are taxed at a lower rate. This can save money. [L0970.005]
  • The S-corp tax election can be made from the start of the business rather than only after it has grown. Whether it makes sense depends on the owner's situation, so a tax professional should confirm it. [L0970.006]
  • An S-corp tax election becomes more advantageous as the business earns more profit, so it is typically considered after revenue grows, not at the start. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1415.003]

Sources differ on timing. Use L0970.006 when a tax professional confirms that an early election fits your situation. Use L1415.003 as the default, which is to consider the election after revenue grows.

Co-mingling

  • Mixing personal and business funds (co-mingling) removes the liability protection of a business entity. Even when a personal account is used as a temporary workaround, keep the flows separated where possible. [L0970.007]

Entity grouping for related and unrelated niches

Core rule

  • For related niches (for example office chairs and desks), operate under a single LLC. For completely unrelated niches (for example office furniture and greenhouses), create separate business entities to compartmentalize finances and simplify future sale of individual stores. [L0251.039]
  • Closely related businesses (for example modern office desks and modern office chairs) share one business entity, bank account, and credit card. Unrelated businesses receive their own business entity, bank account, and credit card. Determine relatedness by asking: Would the same customer buy from both? Would I cross-promote them? [L0253.008]
  • Group related stores under one LLC and put unrelated niches into separate LLCs. This keeps money from being mixed, simplifies accounting, and makes it easier to sell related stores as a bundle later. [L0941.002]
  • Each niche should stand as its own business line. Related niches that customers can cross-promote can sit under one parent company, while unrelated niches need a separate entity. This lets stores be sold together as a network. [L0667.075]

Related stores

  • Stores in related niches are grouped under one LLC. This is the name used on supplier forms, the bank account and tax filings, while each store keeps its own brand and domain. [L0695.004]
  • Related stores that are cross-promoted with each other can share one business entity and one EIN. [L0934.009]
  • Example: a trampoline store and a bounce house store, which would be cross-promoted, share one corporation and one EIN. [L0934.010]
  • Closely related stores sell better as a bundle or network, since buyers value a group of stores in one niche. Keep a separate legal entity for each group of related businesses so accounting stays clean and the group can be sold as one unit. (Older, 2020.) [L0468.013]
  • Stores that belong to the same network of related niches should sit under one company. A future buyer of one store usually wants a non-compete covering the whole network. (Older, 2020.) [L1277.003]
  • Sites in related niches that target the same customers can share one LLC. (Older, 2022.) [L0969.027]
  • Stores that are indirectly related, for example through cross-promotion, should be kept under one business entity so they can be sold together later. [L1114.009]
  • One company entity can own several stores with different store names and domains. The company's assets are the domains, the store design, the customer lists and the supplier relationships, so a single entity can hold multiple stores. [L1114.066]
  • One company can own several store domains. Form one company, such as a US LLC, and build each store under it. Each store can run as its own brand under the same legal entity. (Older, 2021.) [L0469.002]
  • One LLC can own several niche stores, but each store should keep its own identity and accounts. (Older, 2020.) [L0358.050]

Unrelated stores

  • Unrelated business areas, such as a store in a different niche, get a separate LLC. This keeps the books and accounting clean and makes it simpler to sell one business later without affecting the others. [L0695.005]
  • A store in an unrelated niche should get its own LLC and EIN, so its accounting can be separated if it is sold later. [L0934.009]
  • Example: a horse saddle store that is unrelated to the trampoline and bounce house stores gets a new LLC and a new EIN. [L0934.010]
  • A store in an unrelated niche should be under a separate LLC, so it can be sold or audited without affecting others. (Older, 2022.) [L0969.027]
  • A new, unrelated niche should go under a separate entity, even if the same owner runs it with the same skills. (Older, 2020.) [L1277.003]

Account separation

Sources differ on how far separation goes inside a shared entity. Apply the one that matches the relationship:

  • Closely related stores share one bank account and one credit card. [L0253.008]
  • Each store keeps its own identity and accounts, even within one LLC. (Older, 2020.) [L0358.050]
  • Each store keeps its own brand and domain under the same entity. [L0695.004]

EINs and US registration

  • A US EIN is recommended for doing business in the US. This applies to a Canadian business selling into the US. [L0360.084]
  • A business can start as a sole proprietor with an EIN. [L0970.010]
  • Related stores that are cross-promoted share one EIN. An unrelated store gets its own EIN. [L0934.009]

Taxes and administration

  • Setting up a company in a country with a favorable tax structure, such as Ireland, can be considered for selling to other markets. The tax benefit must be checked for the specific business before it is relied on. [L0359.007]
  • Confirm the legal and tax requirements for your country and the US states you sell into before launch. (Older, 2020.) [L0961.092]
  • Dropshipping from a non-US country can be set up under a US company. Check the choice of entity with a forum or professional before setup. (Older, 2019.) [L0357.065]
  • Running the store from a non-US company can work with US suppliers. Check the legal side with an accountant or a community forum. (Older, 2018.) [L1085.082]
  • To check whether a home-based business needs a local license after moving to a new state, search for that state's business registration or business license page on a.gov website and call the phone number listed there. Ask your question directly, since the office can give the state-specific answer. [L0970.011]
  • Spending dozens of hours researching where to register a business or how to minimize tax before the business earns meaningful profit wastes time that costs more than the savings. Register it where you already live while income is small. [L1515.016]

Common mistakes

  • Launching a cross-border high-ticket model without pricing in freight, customs duties and currency conversion can leave little or no margin. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0781.004]
  • Mixing personal and business funds removes the liability protection of a business entity. [L0970.007]
  • Spending dozens of hours researching where to register a business or how to minimize tax before profits arrive wastes time. [L1515.016]
  • Choosing a market by cheap traffic alone, instead of buying power, makes a poor test market. [L0361.052]
  • Expanding abroad before the home market is proven. Start with one country and scale within it first. (Older, 2020.) [L1277.008]
