1.7 Launch plan and timeline

Step sequence from niche to live store

Order of decisions

  • Settle the niche before building the store, because product selection, supplier sourcing and store design all depend on it. [L1080.010]
  • Choose the market, the product types and a rough picture of the target customer before building anything. The website and the supplier outreach both depend on these choices. [L1060.001]
  • A weak market or product choice affects every later stage: the website, supplier contact and supplier approval. [L1059.002]
  • Run the path from joining to a live store as one ordered sequence, completing each step before the next. Do not park steps under "come back to this later" when they are required for launch. [L0555.003]
  • Advanced growth tactics, such as negotiating supplier exclusivity, referral and influencer marketing, advanced SEO, advanced email marketing and additional paid ad platforms, are not day-one tasks. Defer them until the basic store is live and has made its first sales. [L0555.001]
  • Studying a long list of advanced topics before the first store is live overwhelms beginners and delays the first sale. Complete the basic setup in sequence first. [L0555.002]

Build sequences by source

Source A (undated), dropshipping store build sequence:

  1. Choose a niche and product type that suits dropshipping and has profit potential. [L0506.001]
  2. Validate the niche ideas with real market numbers that can be gathered free online, cross off weak ideas and favor the niche with more opportunity. [L0506.001]
  3. Build the Shopify store, including a demo store with uploaded demo products, before contacting suppliers. [L0506.001]
  4. Find suppliers and get approved as an authorized retailer through direct outreach using phone and email scripts, without paying for supplier access or supplier directories. [L0506.001]
  5. Optimize the site for conversions once real products are live and before spending on paid traffic. [L0506.001]
  6. Bring in traffic through paid ads and other traffic sources once the site converts. [L0506.001]
  7. Automate and outsource the operation so the business can be managed in about 15 to 30 minutes per day. [L0506.001]

Source A (undated): the program uses a seven-step process for launching each new highly profitable semi-automated store, and niche selection is step one. [L0591.007]

Source B, high-ticket dropshipping (2021-22, older), five-step launch sequence:

  1. Identify your market: a customer group with a shared interest that has several viable high-ticket products. [L0458.058]
  2. Build a professional Shopify store before contacting any supplier. [L0458.058]
  3. Find local suppliers and secure their approval to sell their products. [L0458.058]
  4. Load the supplier product data, tidy the design and optimize the product pages for conversion. [L0458.058]
  5. Launch with a traffic system built on Google Shopping ads, organic search, email and retargeting. [L0458.058]

Source B (2021-22, older), seven-step full business build:

  1. Identify the market and the profitable products to sell. [L0912.005]
  2. Build an ecommerce site step by step on Shopify. [L0912.005]
  3. Get suppliers to approve you to sell their products through your website. [L0912.005]
  4. Get the approved supplier products onto your website. [L0912.005]
  5. Launch the business and set up marketing to drive traffic and sales. [L0912.005]
  6. Manage the day-to-day operations. [L0912.005]
  7. Learn the financial numbers behind running the business. [L0912.005]

Source B (2021-22, older): the steps that must be finished before a high-ticket store can go live are building the website, securing supplier approval and launching the store. Managing day-to-day operations and learning the financial numbers can follow after launch without delaying it. [L0912.013]

Source D (2026), typical sequence for an AI-assisted dropshipping business:

  1. Set the mindset, choose the tools and set up the store. [L1487.007]
  2. Do product research to find products with winning potential. [L1487.007]
  3. Build the store: product listings, branding and conversion optimization. [L1487.007]
  4. Launch paid ads and optimize them with AI-assisted targeting. [L1487.007]
  5. Scale the store once it is profitable. [L1487.007]

Launch criteria

Definition of launched

  • A store launch means officially opening for business to start receiving traffic, sales, questions and data. It is not a one-day event that brings in large cash flow, and it differs from the big launches of established brands. [L1446.004]
  • A new dropshipping store counts as launched only when it is live, connected to working suppliers, receiving traffic and ready to take its first sale. Use these conditions as the finish line for the initial build. [L0555.008]
  • A store is ready to launch once the first two suppliers are onboarded and their products are uploaded. That is enough to open for business, accept payments, start small-budget ads and begin learning how the business works. [L1446.005]
  • Launch as soon as the store is optimized for conversions and has no glaring problems. Further improvements can continue after launch, since the store keeps improving over the life of the business. [L1446.006]

Product count

  • For Google ads, the store should have at least two approved brands and a minimum of about 100 different products listed. Variants of the same product do not count toward the 100. [L0468.006] (older, 2020)
  • In 2022, the view was that a big catalogue is not needed at launch because the store builds out over time. The source does not state whether the 100-product rule applies to launch-day sales. Use the 2020 rule for Google ads and the 2022 view for the first sales. [L0963.119, L0468.006]
  • Baseline product count for a new store: 100 unique products. [L0823.012]

Traffic and sales baselines

  • Baseline traffic for a new store: 60 unique clicks per day. [L0823.013]
  • Baseline sales for a new store: 1 sale every other day, which is about 0.5 sales per day. [L0823.014]
  • Starting baseline, in order (Source A, undated):
    1. Launch the store and run PLDA ads. [L0823.011]
    2. Test the checkout by completing a sale. [L0823.011]
    3. Have 100 unique products. [L0823.011]
    4. Reach 60 unique clicks per day. [L0823.011]
    5. Aim for 1 sale every other day. [L0823.011]

Avoiding a shortcut

  • Do not build a single-product sales page as a shortcut to conversions. Without a track record and a catalogue, the store cannot carry paid traffic. [L0468.007] (older, 2020)

Pre-launch review

  • Run a full pre-launch review before switching on paid traffic, because each missing setup item can cost sales and raises the risk of account suspension. A store can be published without being ready, so this review separates a live site from one that converts visitors. [L1145.001] (older, 2021-22)
  • A site is technically live once published, but meaningful customer volume starts when the paid campaigns are switched on. Plan the final check around that moment. [L1145.002] (older, 2021-22)

Hours required

  • The model works as a lifestyle business, so launch speed depends on how many hours the owner can put in each week. Someone working a few hours a week needs much longer, while someone working evenings and weekends can be live in about two weeks. [L1068.001] (older, 2019)
  • The estimated total effort to finish the initial store setup is roughly 40 to 80 hours, depending on speed and previous experience. This is an estimate, not a fixed figure. [L1068.002] (older, 2019)
  • Building the store requires about one hour a day, or several hours on each weekend day. A 21-day launch plan is unrealistic if only a few hours per week are available. [L1068.003] (older, 2019)
  • When weekly hours are limited, put the time first into getting approved with the top brands in the niche, uploading their products and setting up traffic campaigns. Less important brands can follow later. [L1068.006] (older, 2019)
  • The time from starting training to a live store varies widely: about one week up to about 60 days, depending on the owner's pace. [L0356.014] (older, 2019)

Launch order

Ordered launch steps (Source A, 2024)

  1. Complete the compliance checklist. [L0471.062]
  2. Set up Google Merchant Center and a Google Shopping campaign. [L0471.062]
  3. Set up Google remarketing campaigns. [L0471.062]
  4. Add a small Microsoft campaign. [L0471.062]
  5. Launch Meta ads under the same compliance rules. [L0471.062]
  • Do not start traffic sources before completing the compliance checklist (lesson 6.0.2). [L0969.102] (older, 2022)
  • The 2024 order and the 2022 rule agree: compliance comes before any traffic source. [L0471.062, L0969.102]

Launch in the high-ticket model (Source B, 2021-22, older)

  • Choose the market, the product types and the rough target customer before building anything. [L1060.001]
  • Build the demo website before contacting suppliers. It should be nearly identical to the store customers will eventually see, and the main change before launch is adding products from approved suppliers. [L1060.005]
  • Before launching a product, choose the entry route in advance: a marketplace such as Amazon first, your own website first, or a crowdfunding campaign such as Kickstarter. Smaller products especially need a written launch plan instead of the assumption that the product will sell by itself. [L0768.001]

Store build principles for testing

Speed over polish

  • Copying proven elements from successful competitors is the fastest way to get a test store live, especially for beginners without design experience. Their choices have already been tested by real sales. [L0600.004]
  • Building from scratch without studying competitors slows the launch and forces untested design and copy decisions. Beginners who avoid inspiration often lose weeks before they can start testing a product. [L0600.005]
  • For a first test, spend little time polishing the product page, because its design matters less than getting a live store that can produce data. [L0600.006]
  • In the early testing stage, create a logo fast just to get the store live and the first sale. The full brand work (extensive brand document, customer avatars, unique packaging) comes later, once a product has proven it sells. [L0954.001]
  • Choose the store's own theme by previewing it as a draft in the theme library before making it live. [L0971.079]

Demo store and catalogue

  • Build the Shopify store, including a demo store with uploaded demo products, before contacting suppliers. [L0506.001]
  • Start selling after the first two brands are uploaded, and keep adding suppliers in the background. [L0963.119] (older, 2022)

Entry route for small physical products (Source B, 2021-22, older)

  • For small physical products, either build an on-site sales path in which customers are upsold from cheaper items, or use Amazon, Etsy, eBay or Walmart as a lead magnet to generate traffic. Amazon is named as the most likely option, since it captures a large share of online traffic for physical products. [L0768.019]

General store testing

Test order before traffic (Source C)

Set up a Shopify store with a dummy test order before launch:

  1. Place a test order on the live store. [L0352.054]
  2. Confirm the purchase flow, Apple Pay and payment steps work. [L0352.054]
  3. Check the order flow before sending traffic from TikTok. [L0352.054]

Conversion check before paid spend

  • Optimize the site for conversions once real products are live and before spending on paid traffic. [L0506.001]
  • Launch as soon as the store is optimized for conversions and has no glaring problems. [L1446.006]
  • Run the full pre-launch review before switching on paid traffic, and plan the final check around the moment the paid campaigns start. [L1145.001, L1145.002] (older, 2021-22)
