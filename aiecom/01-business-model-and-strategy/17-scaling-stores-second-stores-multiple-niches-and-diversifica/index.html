1.17 Scaling stores: second stores, multiple niches and diversification

Deciding whether to scale at all

  • Scaling a drop shipping business to $40,000 to $60,000 or more in monthly net profit typically requires about 50 hours of work per week for roughly a year [L0413.002].
  • Scaling is more cash-intensive than running a single store, because traffic and self-branding cost much more money, and it carries a bigger risk [L0413.002].
  • Growing a five-figure monthly sales store tenfold rests on three levers: partnerships, branding and an X factor [L0413.003].
  • Before committing, the owner should decide whether the higher time commitment, cash needs and risk are acceptable [L0413.003].

Scaling the first store before adding another

  • Start with one niche and one store, bring it to stable profit, and only then add a second store. Launching several stores at once multiplies beginner mistakes [L0247.001].
  • Build one store first, make it profitable, and only then build the second and third store. Do not launch several niche stores at the same time [L0359.005].
  • Build and make the first store profitable before building a second or third store (older, 2019). If you complete the first two modules and follow the niche criteria, the first store has no obvious reason not to work, but it still needs real sales and profit first [L0960.057].
  • Once an X factor is found, the best way to improve results is to improve what already works and keep testing new ideas. A store is never finished, and adding new suppliers or products can sharply increase sales [L0413.040].
  • Understanding your industry better than your competitors helps you decide which X factor experiments are worth running. Test one idea at a time and keep learning, rather than running a single campaign and hoping for results [L0413.045].
  • Focus on one store and optimize it as far as possible before opening more stores, because multiple stores tend to be neglected [L1293.006].
  • Grow one niche store until it is maxed out before opening a second store, because growing an existing store is easier than building a new one (older, 2021) [L0671.148].
  • A store that is not actively improved will decline, and the decline can be slow, taking years. Keep adding products and optimizing the store so that it keeps growing [L0826.011].

Maintenance-mode criteria: when a store can support a second one

Sources define maintenance mode with different levels of detail. The versions are listed below as given.

2019 version (older):

  • A store should only expand to a second store when the first is in maintenance mode, meaning all suppliers are approved, products are uploaded and optimized, Google ads are running with biweekly optimization, email sequences are set up, and Facebook remarketing is in place [L0467.054].

2021 version (older), ten-point check before opening store two [L0671.149]:

  1. Are you approved with all gold and silver suppliers in your niche? [L0671.149]
  2. Are all of their products uploaded to your store? [L0671.149]
  3. Are your product pages optimized? [L0671.149]
  4. Is your customer support, by live chat, email and phone, as good as it can be? [L0671.149]
  5. Are your traffic sources diversified across Google, Facebook, Bing and SEO? [L0671.149]
  6. Have you scaled your ad spend? [L0671.149]
  7. Do you have email marketing flows for different scenarios? [L0671.149]
  8. Do you run monthly promotions to your social audiences and email list? [L0671.149]
  9. Do you create content and have a loyal following? [L0671.149]
  10. Are you objectively better than your competitors, with a clearer unique selling proposition and a better buying experience? [L0671.149]

2025 version:

  • Start a second store only when the first one is maxed out: all supplier approvals available at this stage are obtained, all their products are uploaded, product pages are optimized to convert, and ad campaigns are actively optimized for the best return on ad spend [L0966.005].

Comparison of the three versions:

Version Number of conditions Source
2019 (older) Five: suppliers approved, products uploaded and optimized, Google ads with biweekly optimization, email sequences, Facebook remarketing [L0467.054]

  • A store is growing on its own when direct visits, referral traffic, organic search traffic and repeat purchases rise without the ad budget being increased each month (older, 2020). Google search position alone does not show that a store has become the dominant player in its niche [L1111.174].

Exception to one-store-at-a-time:

  • Several stores can be launched and grown at the same time, instead of selling one store before starting the next (2025) [L0966.004].
  • Parallel work is easier in related niches, because the audience of one store can be cross-promoted to the others [L0966.007].
  • Parallel projects divert focus. Optimizing a store that already works is a better use of time than researching suppliers for a new store [L0966.006].
  • In practice, the 2025 source allows parallel launches but treats them as a higher-risk choice, which is why the majority of sources keep one-store-at-a-time as the default [L0966.004, L0966.006, L0966.007, L0247.001, L0359.005].

Related versus unrelated niches

Starting with an unproven market (older, 2019):

  1. Build one store that sells both product lines when neither market is proven as a standalone market. [L0663.013]
  2. Once sales start, track which products sell better. [L0663.013]
  3. Pick the stronger niche and make it the main focus of the site. [L0663.013]
  4. Keep the other products only as complementary items. [L0663.013]
  • Run separate stores for two niches only when each market is already proven as a standalone market with successful stores. Otherwise, combining them in one store is the safer test (older, 2019) [L0663.014].
  • Do not mix unrelated products together just to add variety. A combined store is a temporary test to find the stronger niche (older, 2019) [L0663.015].

Unrelated categories (older, 2022):

  • Products from unrelated categories, for example furniture and home appliances, should run as separate stores, each made profitable on its own. Once established, the stores can cross-link and refer customers to each other using lead value optimization [L0429.003].
  • The 2019 combined-store test and the 2022 separate-store rule fit together: the combined store is a temporary test used while a market is unproven, and separation follows once the markets are proven or clearly unrelated [L0663.013, L0663.014, L0663.015, L0429.003].

Product groups and category authority (older, 2022):

  • Build a separate store for each product group that customers would not buy in the same visit, so each store becomes the authority for its category. Example: paddle boards and surfboards should be separate stores, while paddle boards and their paddles, wax and add-ons can share one store [L0665.002].

Keeping a niche store focused (older, 2021):

  • Keep a niche store focused. If its niche passed all tests, do not expand it with unrelated products. Sell other product types in related stores instead [L0671.150].

Store networks and structure

  • One domain equals one Shopify store. You cannot run multiple stores from a single domain [L0270.024].
  • If high-end brands reject you because your store already carries lower-tier brands, open a separate spin-off store that carries only the higher-end products in the niche (older, 2021). Keep it under the same company but give it its own store name, so the lower-tier store does not hurt your approvals [L0834.112].
  • A second store in a related niche can cross-promote to the audience of the first store [L0807.004].
  • Stores in related niches can cross-promote to each other's audiences, which makes running them in parallel easier [L0966.007].
  • Keep a backup store ready, and switch traffic links to it if the main store is shut down [L0352.040].
  • Once established, stores can cross-link and refer customers to each other using lead value optimization. This applies to stores in unrelated categories (older, 2022) [L0429.003].

Overhead of running several stores

  • Multiple stores create duplicated overhead. Marketing systems, bank accounts, email marketing, VAs, phone numbers and phone systems must be separated per store (older, 2021-22). Running many stores also spreads focus and tends to slow the growth of each store, so growing one store to its full potential is generally recommended before starting another [L0721.027].
  • Starting a second store while the first one is not working doubles processes such as customer service and splits focus. This doubles the problems instead of solving them (older, 2021-22) [L0905.021].
  • Running several stores early splits attention, and mistakes in the first store get repeated in the second (older, 2021-22) [L0905.022].
  • Building several stores at once multiplies the same setup errors. Fixing one mistake on one store before repeating the process is cheaper [L0807.004].

Focus versus many stores

  • The largest stores in the examples generally ran one or two stores, and the highest revenue stores were focused on a smaller number of stores, so concentrated focus is associated with high earnings (older, 2021-22). The choice between many small stores and one large store also depends on the owner's preference, including whether they enjoy building or prefer to build, sell and hold [L0721.029].
  • Running several stores in different niches is possible, but focusing one store more heavily is a sensible longer-term plan. The other stores should still be maintained and improved while the main niche gets more budget [L0693.056].
  • When a broad store is hard to make work, narrow the existing store to one customer segment instead of launching a second store. A second store almost never fixes a struggling first store (older, 2021-22) [L0905.020].

True diversification and risk spreading

  • Real diversification means running different business models, such as e-commerce combined with real estate, car washing, RV rental, affiliate marketing or information products. Multiple stores in the same business or model usually suffer together when demand for that model or e-commerce drops (older, 2021-22) [L0721.028, L0721.037].
  • Selling through a single traffic source or a single supplier is a business risk. Use several traffic sources such as Google Shopping, Google Ads and Facebook, plus many brands, so one blacklisting or closure does not stop the business (older, 2021) [L0801.170].
  • Stable year-round revenue from one store can act as a safety net while you build the next store (older, 2022) [L1113.081].
  • There is no fixed answer on whether to focus on one product, one channel and one market, or to diversify. The seller's approach was to first improve the single product and its Facebook strategy, then differentiate to another channel [L1127.013].

Relaunching a store

  • If an old store never ran ads and its products are back in stock, you can restart in the same niche under the same LLC and supplier accounts, applying the skills you have gained since. Pausing for inventory reasons does not damage supplier relationships (2023) [L0804.001].
  • A previously successful store can be relaunched instead of rebuilt from zero. Rebuild the advertising campaigns from scratch and do not reactivate the old campaigns (2025) [L0966.010].

Lean starts for a new store

  • Once a store runs consistently, a second niche can be launched faster by cloning the site with new logos, names and products from suppliers already approved. This avoids repeating supplier applications from scratch. It should only be done after the first niche is stable (2023) [L1114.007].
  • Keep the full niche list after choosing one niche. An approved niche can later be used for a second store without repeating the research, but recheck its data if enough time has passed (older, 2021-22) [L0490.026].
  • Once the basics of the first store are maximized, grow in this order (no year given) [L1441.010]:
    1. Replicate the same products on another sales channel such as eBay, connected to the same Shopify feed, provided the supplier agrees. [L1441.010]
    2. Once the first store is maximized, start a second store. [L1441.010]
    3. Use a proven store blueprint or evergreen course to replicate the setup quickly. [L1441.010]
    4. Keep the setup updated when platforms such as Google Ads change their interface. [L1441.010]

Examples and outcomes

  • Case, Source C (no year given): A seller built a one-product store on Facebook ads over about one and a half years. They started at roughly $10K in monthly revenue and stayed there for a long time, until the revenue grew to about $100K per month. Image ads did most of the work, video ads did not work for their fashion products, and a Taboola test at the $10K level failed. Later, the daily sales plateaued at about $4.5K to $5K from November onward, even after duplicating the ad set horizontally and then vertically. The seller then hired an agency about 10 days before the call and opened a second store, which reached about $30K in its first month. Overall revenue was about $850K, with about $500K generated in the last five months, and the first store reached about $110K. The seller also reported that CJ Dropshipping caused many undelivered orders and heavy customer service work [L1127.014].
  • The second store in this case reached about $30K in its first month on the Shopify plan used at the time [L1127.015].
  • One early store generated 2,000 to 4,000 USD profit per month, enough to live well in a low-cost city. The owner later sold stores for amounts such as 60,000 USD and used the money to start new ones [L1515.024].
