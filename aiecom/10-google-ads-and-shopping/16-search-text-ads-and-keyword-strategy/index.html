10.16 Search text ads and keyword strategy

Funnel Position and Intent

  • Align search text ads to the prospect's funnel position: awareness and research at the top of the funnel, purchase intent at the bottom, because search keywords and buyer intent vary significantly by stage. [L0074.001]
  • Search text ads perform best on search terms with mid-to-bottom funnel intent, where the intent points to a specific collection page or product page. Judge these keywords as low, medium or high value, the same way as for Shopping ads. [L0751.008] (older, 2021-22)
  • Targeting broad, top-of-funnel search terms with search text ads wastes budget when those visitors convert poorly or slowly. The account can then run at a loss for a long time without any return within the planned timeframe. [L0751.011] (older, 2021-22)
  • Low-value or top-of-funnel search terms can be targeted with search text ads only as an advanced technique, and only once the account is already profitable. [L0880.007] (older, 2021-22)
  • More specific keywords produce more specific clicks and a higher conversion rate. Broad or generic keywords bring visitors who are not yet ready to buy the product. [L1085.054] (older, 2018)
  • Search targeting means bidding on core commercial keywords, often called money keywords, which are the terms that generate sales. It is the first targeting method to prioritize. [L0720.008]
  • Search ads aimed at informational queries such as "buyer's guide" plus a product category can put the brand in front of shoppers who are still researching before they buy. Brand visibility in these searches can raise the click-through rate of the same brand's later ads, so include such queries in the search plan. [L1142.012]
  • Buyer-intent keywords can be found in the search terms of a live campaign. Start a new store with one alpha and one beta campaign, review which keywords bring impressions, clicks and sales, and push more budget to those. [L0470.069]

Role and Placement Alongside Shopping

  • In a search campaign the advertiser types in the keywords and chooses broad, phrase or exact match, which makes it easy to get a lot of traffic that rarely converts. Shopping campaigns therefore remain the primary recommendation, with search campaigns used as a secondary source. [L0373.007]
  • Search campaigns can reach shoppers who never open the Shopping tab, so they add reach. Add them only once the Shopping campaigns are working and the store generates real traffic. [L0373.008]
  • A store can receive traffic from three sources at once: organic Google results, Shopping campaigns, and small search campaigns for best-selling products. A branded search campaign is added on top once the store is profitable. [L0373.020]
  • Search text ads appear on the Google results page for a keyword, usually as up to three ads and sometimes four, above the organic listings. A small block can also appear below the organic results. The top of the page is the most valuable placement. [L0751.001] (older, 2021-22)
  • Running Shopping ads, search text ads and organic listings together can give a store up to three visible spots on the same results page. Seeing the brand several times raises the chance that a shopper clicks one of its listings. [L0751.006] (older, 2021-22)
  • Many shoppers avoid clicking ads, but plenty of others do click them. Search text ads therefore still capture a real share of clicks, even though some users will ignore them. [L0751.007] (older, 2021-22)
  • For high-ticket dropshipping, search text ads are rarely the main ad format because Shopping ads usually deliver better returns. Search text ads still have a place alongside Shopping ads. [L0751.004] (older, 2021-22)
  • In markets with lower Shopping ad usage, such as Australia and the UK, search text ads can outperform Shopping ads for some stores. Even there, Shopping ads tend to be the stronger performer overall. [L0751.005] (older, 2021-22)
  • Ad stacking, meaning multiple ads for the same product, on your best-selling items gives maximum exposure on the search results page. [L0074.002]
  • Once one product sells consistently, show it in as many places as possible, including an exact-match search text ad with the product name so it can dominate the first page for that name. [L0356.051] (older, 2019)
  • A strong-performing keyword can also be pulled into its own search text campaign as an exact match, sending traffic to the same product page that already converts. This is an extra channel on top of the Shopping campaign, not a replacement for the Shopping setup. [L0856.010]

When to Start Search Text Ads

  • Google Shopping is the first campaign type to start with and should take most of the daily ad budget at the beginning. Search text ads come later, after the Shopping campaigns are profitable. [L1086.012] (older, 2019)
  • Do not start with Google search text ads. They become worth considering only once products are proven and the store is highly profitable. [L0247.056] (older, 2021)
  • Traditional Google search text ads should not be started until Shopping campaigns are already highly successful. [L1069.080] (older, 2019)
  • Search text ads should not be started until Shopping is already profitable. [L0855.007] (2023)
  • Do not turn off campaigns that are profitable. Search text ads should not be used until all other campaigns are already profitable. [L0969.058] (older, 2022)
  • Shopping ads must be profitable before you add search text ads. If Shopping ads do not work yet, search text ads are unlikely to succeed, with only a small number of stores as exceptions. [L0880.002] (older, 2021-22)
  • Start search text ads only after your Shopping ads have revealed medium- and high-value search terms. These are terms that show buying intent for your products: product IDs, brand names combined with product types, or very specific long-tail terms for one product or a small group. [L0880.001] (older, 2021-22)
  • Search text ads take longer to set up than Shopping ads, and many products are never searched by name or product code. Build search text ads only for search terms that people actually use for your products or brands. [L0880.003] (older, 2021-22)
  • The search term reports of your Shopping campaigns are the data source for finding keywords that should become search text ads. Shopping ads act as cheap research before you spend setup time on search text ads. [L0880.004] (older, 2021-22)
  • Do not start product-specific search campaigns until Shopping campaigns have shown which keywords sell. Shopping campaigns are expected to keep a better return on ad spend than search campaigns, so the search campaign adds only a small extra source of traffic. [L0373.018]
  • Do not launch Google search text ads until the Shopping campaigns are already profitable and you know your best sellers. Until then, check the Google Ads account at least once a day and add irrelevant search terms to the negative keyword list. [L1086.020] (older, 2019)
  • Before running search text ads for a high-ticket dropshipping store, answer these questions in order: [L0751.013] (older, 2021-22)
    1. When is the right time to start running search text ads?
    2. Which search terms should you target? Start with terms that have high purchase intent and are likely to convert relatively quickly.
    3. What is the best way to write the ad copy, including the headline, the ad text and the extension details?
  • Search text ads that build on a proven product follow this sequence: [L0806.063] (2024)
    1. Identify the best-selling product.
    2. Create a search text ad for it with the product name as a phrase match keyword.
    3. Optimize the product page for SEO.
    4. The result is three spots on page one: the Shopping ad, the search text ad above organic results, and an organic listing.

Keyword Sources from Shopping Data

Procedure: starting search text ads from Shopping data [L0880.016] (older, 2021-22)

  1. Confirm that your Shopping ads are profitable.
  2. Open your Shopping campaigns.
  3. Find search terms that already get traffic and are specific to one product, one model, one brand or a small product group.
  4. Build search text ads around those terms and test them with a small budget.

Procedure: search term mining in Google Ads [L0880.017] (older, 2021-22)

  1. Open the Google Ads account and go to Campaigns.
  2. Skip the high-priority Shopping campaign, which holds low-value search terms.
  3. Open the low-priority Shopping campaign, which holds the high-value search terms.
  4. Optionally open one ad group if its products have better margins and you want to test only those products.
  5. Open the Keywords tab in the ad group and select Search Terms.
  6. Set the date range to about three months if the account has run that long.
  7. Look for very specific terms such as model codes, model names, brand plus model combinations and product-specific wording.
  8. Note close variations of each strong term, since they are needed to build exact match or phrase match targets.
  9. Download the search term report as Excel, Google Sheets or PDF.
  10. Copy the terms you want to target into a central keyword spreadsheet and repeat for the other ad groups, and for the medium-priority campaign where brand-level terms can be found.

Setting: Google Ads path for search term analysis [L0880.018] (older, 2021-22) Campaigns > open the Shopping campaign > open the ad group > Keywords tab > Search Terms. Set the date range (the course uses a custom range of about three months) and use the Download button to export the list.

Procedure: keywords from the Shopping keyword report [L0855.177] (2023)

  1. Open Google Ads and go to the Shopping campaigns.
  2. Pull the keyword report for the Shopping campaigns.
  3. Filter the report to your best-selling product.
  4. Note the keywords that have already led to sales of that product.
  5. Add those keywords to a search text campaign built around that product's page.

Procedure: extracting keywords for a new Search campaign [L0210.038]

  1. In Google Ads, open your Shopping campaign.
  2. Navigate to the Keywords section or the report showing where your ads appeared.
  3. Identify all keywords that generated clicks and sales.
  4. Copy the keywords that drove sales for your target product.
  5. Paste them into your new Search campaign ad group as phrase match keywords (enclosed in quotation marks).

Rules for choosing keywords

  • Keyword selection for search text ads is based on what already sells, not on search volume or competition scores. Keyword volume and competition figures from Google are not used for choosing these keywords. [L0855.012] (2023)
  • Shopping data for individual product pages supports the decision of which products to put on search text campaigns. [L0855.026] (2023)
  • Keywords that already drove sales of a product, and that show a high click-through rate on Shopping ads, are strong candidates for a search text ad test. Start with an exact match on the core term, then add the close variations found in the report. [L0880.020] (older, 2021-22)
  • Model names and model codes are high-intent search terms because the searcher already knows which product they want. Keep every variation of a model name or code in your target list. [L0880.021] (older, 2021-22)
  • A brand-level search term has a lower conversion rate than model-level terms, but in some niches a low enough cost per click makes it viable. [L0880.024] (older, 2021-22)
  • Keep every target keyword in one central spreadsheet, recording the search term, its match type and its performance figures in the same file, so you can reuse the terms without searching the reports again. [L0880.026] (older, 2021-22)
  • Optimize a Google Search campaign that starts with many keywords and search terms, since some pay off and others do not. [L1077.050] (Source C)
    1. Review which keywords and search terms generated sales or add-to-carts.
    2. Keep and scale the terms that convert.
    3. Remove or refine the terms that spend without paying off.

Case: model-level starting point [L0880.022] (older, 2021-22) Starting point: a single-brand ad group in the low-priority Shopping campaign with a max CPC of US$1.20 (enhanced) and a three-month search term report. The report showed many searches for specific models and model codes such as HL400D, HL200SN and HL300SN, which became the candidates for search text ads. The ad group totals for the period were 289 clicks, 4,187 impressions, a cost of US$266.20, a cost per conversion of US$91.36 and a conversion rate of 1.01%.

Case: brand-level benchmark [L0880.023] (older, 2021-22) Brand-level test: a Shopping ad group for a brand in another campaign had a max CPC of US$0.80 (enhanced) and an analysis window of about three months. It produced 112 clicks, 3,404 impressions, a cost of US$130.92, a cost per conversion of US$65.46 and a conversion rate of 1.79%. This was the benchmark for judging whether brand-level search text ads could pay off.

Brand-level terms [L0880.024] (older, 2021-22) Brand-level search terms can be tested in the medium-priority campaign. Their conversion rate is usually lower than for model-level terms, but in some niches a low enough cost per click makes them viable.

Keyword Research and Validation

Setting: Keyword Planner access [L1120.004] Google Keyword Planner is reached via Google Ads > Tools > Keyword Planner. Inside it, select the option Discover new keywords.

Procedure: keyword research in Keyword Planner [L1120.005]

  1. Open your Google Ads account.
  2. Click Tools.
  3. Click Keyword Planner.
  4. Click Discover new keywords.
  5. Enter your niche (for example standing desk), and also enter your own URL or a competitor's URL to find related keywords. Doing both gives more and different keyword ideas.
  6. Click Get results.
  7. Close the refined keywords section, since it is not needed for tracking.
  8. Record the average monthly searches, the competition rank and the top-of-page bid range (low and high).
  9. Use the high range of the top-of-page bid for tracking.

Setting: where to find volumes [L0876.010] Keyword Planner is found at Tools > Planning > Keyword Planner. Set the plan location to the target country (for example United States), set languages to All languages, and use the last 12 months as the date range before reading volumes.

Keyword Planner validation sheet [L0876.011]

Keyword Avg. monthly searches Three month change YoY change Competition Ad impression share Top of page bid (low range) Top of page bid (high range)

Read each AI-generated keyword in the group against these columns. The Keyword ideas, Forecast, Saved keywords and Negative keywords tabs sit in the same plan.

  • Search volume differs widely between closely related terms, so each keyword needs its own volume check. Keep high-volume terms and be careful with niche terms that have very low monthly searches. Apply your own judgement to every term that has real volume. [L0876.012]
  • A validated keyword set shapes everything downstream, including ad groups, ad copy and campaign structure. Build the set carefully before creating campaigns, because it sets the direction for the rest of the account. [L0876.013]
  • Keep ad groups tight so the ad copy matches the keywords in the group. Relevant copy protects Quality Score over the long term. [L0876.014]
  • Google Ads shows keyword search volumes in large ranges (for example 10,000-100,000) until the account is established and has spent more money. Exact numbers appear only with account maturity and spending history. [L0278.010] (2025)
  • Third-party keyword research tools give exact monthly search volumes with country-specific filtering. Google Ads shows broad ranges until the account is established and has spent money. [L0278.045] (2025)
  • Keywords Everywhere is a keyword research tool used to compare search volume and competition for product keywords. [L0788.019] (Source C)
  • For a new store, look for keywords with 1,000 to 10,000 searches per month and a cost per click below $1 with low competition. [L0788.018] (Source C)
  • Golden nugget keywords have moderate search volume but low competition. They often deliver better returns for Google Ads than high-volume, highly competitive terms. [L0788.021] (Source C)
  • Keyword Planner cost-per-click estimates are usually higher than what you will actually pay. Google adjusts the real cost based on click quality and how users behave on the landing page. [L1090.101] (older, 2021)
  • A low top-of-page bid range does not mean zero impressions. Better click-through rate and longer time on site can earn cheaper clicks, so a keyword with a high range can still be worth starting. [L0855.011] (2023)
  • Sources differ on AI-generated and outside keywords. One source says keywords for search text ads come from the Shopping keyword report, not from outside keyword tools or AI brainstorming. [L0855.007] Another builds an AI-generated keyword set and validates every term in Keyword Planner before creating campaigns. [L0876.011, L0876.013] Both apply to the same decision: the Shopping report sets the list for proven products; a validated set is needed when you build a broader list.

Match Types

Table: match types for search ads [L0080.002]

Match Type Syntax Behavior Examples
Exact match [keyword] Appears only for the keyword and close variants such as plurals [car] triggers for 'car' and 'cars'
Phrase match "keyword" Appears when the phrase is present with other words before or after it "car" triggers for 'fast car', 'car parts', 'amphibious car'
Broad match keyword Appears for any search related to the keyword, including synonyms and variations car triggers for 'automobile', 'ferrari paint color', 'car parts', 'truck tires'
Modified broad +keyword +modifier Appears for searches containing the modified terms or close variations +red car triggers for 'reddish car', 'stemming', 'CA'

Table: positive targeting match types [L0880.009] (older, 2021-22)

Match type Syntax Ad shows for Notes
Exact match [keyword] Only the same words in the same order; close variants such as plurals and misspellings are also accepted; no extra words around it Tight control, needs many variations listed
Phrase match 'keyword' in quotation marks The phrase in the same order, with other words allowed before or after it Catches longer searches without predicting every extra word
Broad match modifier +word1 +word2 All words marked with + must appear, in any order, with any other words around them Middle ground between precise and wider reach; recommended by the course
Broad match keyword with no punctuation Any search containing at least one of the words Not useful for e-commerce conversions; only for brand awareness
  • A keyword is what you bid on in Google Ads. A search query is the actual search term that triggers your ad. Your chosen match type determines which search queries will display your ad. [L0080.001]
  • Google Ads keyword match types (broad, phrase and exact) determine how closely a user's search must match a keyword before an ad can show for it. Choosing the match type controls how much irrelevant traffic a campaign receives. [L0844.004]
  • Use exact match with square brackets, for example [trail bike X200]. It triggers only that wording in the same order, while plurals and small misspellings still trigger it. [L0880.010] (older, 2021-22)
  • Use phrase match with quotation marks, for example "trail bike X200". It also triggers "buy trail bike X200 online", but not "X200 trail bike" because the word order changes. [L0880.011] (older, 2021-22)
  • Use broad match modifier with a plus sign before each required word, for example +trail +bike +X200. It triggers any search that contains all three words in any order, so "X200 bike trail shop" triggers it, while "trail hiking boots" does not. [L0880.012] (older, 2021-22)
  • Broad match without punctuation can show ads for unrelated searches that share only one word with the target, such as a generic word that appears in many searches. Forgetting the punctuation when you set keywords is a common cause of ad spend running out before anyone notices. [L0880.013] (older, 2021-22)
  • Prefer exact match, phrase match and broad match modifier for search text ads, and avoid broad match. Broad match modifier gives extra reach over exact match while keeping the core product term fixed, which protects profitability. [L0880.014] (older, 2021-22)
  • Broad match modifier is the recommended match type for specific product searches, because it captures variations without manual updates. [L0262.009] (older, 2021-22)
  • For product-specific searches, target using exact match on the complete product phrase in square brackets, for example [Sunray Cayenne Sauna HL400D]. Exact match typically generates fewer impressions than broad match modifier but may deliver a higher conversion rate due to stronger relevance. [L0262.031] (older, 2021-22)
  • Broad match modifier keywords can include more than two required terms. Start with the most critical product identifiers marked with plus signs (for example '+cayenne +hl400d'). You may optionally add terms like '+sauna' for higher specificity. Add extra terms only when the additional precision is needed to exclude irrelevant searches. [L0262.033] (older, 2021-22)
  • In a phrase-match search campaign, ads can also trigger when the searcher adds words before or after the keyword phrase. Limit the keyword list to phrases that have already sold well in Shopping campaigns so the campaign stays narrow. [L0373.017]
  • Product-level keywords combine the brand with the model number and size, rather than using the broad category name. A TV seller would bid on a brand plus a size and model, not on the word TV, which keeps the cost per click much lower. [L0551.081] (older, 2020)
  • Three keyword types for a high-ticket store should be targeted with search text ads: product ID keywords, brand plus product type, and product type plus specific descriptors that narrow results to one product or a very small number of products. Broader non-branded terms are the last option. [L0880.005] (older, 2021-22)
  • Example for a store selling outdoor saunas: a model code plus brand (for example a brand with model code HX400) points straight to one product page; a brand plus product type (for example a brand plus "infrared sauna") targets a larger group; and a non-branded term with size and type (for example "four person outdoor sauna") needs a well-built collection page with content. [L0880.008] (older, 2021-22)

Bidding and Budget

  • Select the "Manual CPC" bidding strategy when creating the search campaign. [L0262.008] (older, 2021-22)
  • Set the default bid at ad group level to a minimum of $0.50 CPC for search text ads. Google will warn you if your bid is too low to appear on the first page of results. [L0262.005] (older, 2021-22)
  • After launching a search ad campaign, check back within 1-2 days to review bids. If Google displays a warning that your bid is below the first-page threshold, increase it. Starting bids of $0.50 may need to increase to $1.00 or higher depending on the auction. [L0262.020] (older, 2021-22)
  • Case bids: a single-brand ad group in the low-priority Shopping campaign ran at a max CPC of US$1.20 (enhanced); a brand-level Shopping ad group ran at a max CPC of US$0.80 (enhanced). Use these as reference points for the cost per click you can expect. [L0880.022, L0880.023] (older, 2021-22)
  • Source C's new-store target is a cost per click below $1 with low competition. [L0788.018]
  • Daily budget: start with a minimum of $20 per day for search text ads. Budgets below $5 will not generate enough data to optimize effectively. [L0262.002] (older, 2021-22)
  • Test new search text ads with a small budget, for example around US$10 per day, and let them run for a while before judging them. The result to measure is whether the ad's conversion rate supports its cost. [L0880.025] (older, 2021-22)
  • A search text campaign is a new experiment with its own budget, separate from Shopping (Alpha/Beta) budgets. Start with about $5 per day and use tight phrase match keywords, for example the store name only. [L0971.019] (2024)
  • Target CPA is used only for lead generation campaigns, such as buyer guide sign-ups, where a maximum cost per lead can be set (for example 4 USD per lead). [L1092.068] (older, 2022)
  • Low search volume on specific product keyword match types is normal for high-ticket items. This does not mean the keywords will not deliver. Confirm that searches exist by checking your Google Shopping campaign data. [L0262.021] (older, 2021-22)

Campaign and Ad Group Structure

Networks

  • For Search campaigns, uncheck "Search network" and "Display network" in campaign settings to restrict ads to Google search only and avoid wasting budget on search partners. [L0210.014]
  • Uncheck both "Include Google search partners" and "Include Google Display Network". Text-only search ads do not perform well on the Display Network, which uses image-based ads. [L0262.001] (older, 2021-22)

Campaign per product

  • Create one separate Search campaign for each top product instead of grouping multiple products in one campaign. This gives better budget control and prevents high-performing products from being overshadowed. Do not group multiple products in one Search campaign with multiple ad groups, so each product keeps an independent budget and bid control. [L0210.016, L0210.032]
  • Conflicting structure: one source organizes each product as a separate ad group within one campaign. [L0262.024] (older, 2021-22)
  • Campaign structure: a campaign contains multiple ad groups, and each ad group can contain multiple ads and keywords. Naming conventions should clearly identify the product type or targeting strategy. [L0262.023] (older, 2021-22)

Ad group size and ad count

  • Structure each ad group with approximately 10 keywords in Google Ads. [L0080.003]
  • Always run two text ads simultaneously in each ad group to enable continuous performance comparison and testing. [L0085.002]
  • Ad groups should have at least three ads that closely match the theme of their keywords, so Google can test which headlines and descriptions perform better. This applies to lead-generation ad groups. [L0922.017] (older, 2021-22)

Procedure: standard search text campaign build [L0262.008] (older, 2021-22)

  1. Click "New Campaign" in Google Ads.
  2. Select "Create a campaign without a goal's guidance".
  3. Choose "Search" as campaign type.
  4. Enter campaign name (identify product type and targeting clearly, e.g., "Product IDs - Search Text - US").
  5. Under Networks, uncheck "Include Google search partners" and "Include Google Display Network".
  6. Select target location (e.g., United States).
  7. Set language (default English).
  8. Enter daily budget (minimum $20).
  9. Select "Manual CPC" bidding strategy.
  10. Expand "Show more settings" and find Ad rotation.
  11. Change from "Optimise: Prefer best performing ads" to "Do not optimise: Rotate ads indefinitely".
  12. Add ad extensions: callout extensions (minimum 2) for offers like "Free Shipping" or "Price Match Guarantee", and call extension with business phone number.
  13. Skip sitelink extensions for product-specific ads.
  14. Click "Save and continue" to create ad groups.
  15. Name the ad group after the product or product ID (e.g., "Sunray Cayenne Sauna hl400d").
  16. Set default bid to $0.50 minimum.
  17. Enter keywords with match type: for specific product searches, use broad match modifier format (+keyword +keyword).
  18. Enter final URL (product page URL).
  19. Write Headline 1 (30 chars max) with product name and key identifier.
  20. Write Headline 2 (30 chars max) emphasizing product's best unique feature.
  21. Write Headline 3 (30 chars max) with main offer element (e.g., "Free Shipping US Wide").
  22. Create display path including keywords (e.g., "bathvault.com/sunray/cayenne-hl400d").
  23. Write Description 1 (90 chars max) creating curiosity about the product benefit.
  24. Write Description 2 (90 chars max) introducing a secondary benefit or special offer.
  25. Save ad and continue.
  26. Review campaign and publish.

Procedure: product search campaign setup [L0210.017] URL to product page > keywords sourced from your Shopping campaign keyword report (phrases that already convert) entered as phrase match > pin product name as first headline > add benefits (in stock, free shipping) in second headline > include price in description > optionally add image extensions (50% approval rate) > uncheck Search network and Display network.

Naming

  • Search - COLD - [Top Shopping Product] aims to double the visibility of the best-selling products. Its keyword list should be built from search terms that already convert in Google Shopping campaigns. [L1121.009]

Product-Specific Search Campaigns

Procedure: building a product-specific search campaign [L0373.011] This is a long-term step and not for the first days.

  1. Run test campaigns until you can name your five best sellers that sell consistently with good profit.
  2. In the Shopping campaigns, find the keywords that drive sales for those products.
  3. Create a very small budget search campaign that uses phrase match only for those proven keywords.
  4. Write search ads without an image, with the brand name, product name, SKU number and price in the description lines, plus selling points such as fast shipping, free shipping, a price match policy and a one-year warranty.
  • Keywords worth a product-specific search campaign are usually long, specific phrases that combine color, pattern and model number for one product, such as a rug described by color, pattern and model number. They are chosen because they already drove sales in Shopping campaigns. [L0373.019]
  • Start with one best-selling product: build one Google search text campaign named after that product, with a single ad group containing only a few phrase-match keywords in quotation marks. Set a small budget and use an ad that includes the brand name, product name and SKU. [L1112.009] (older, 2021)
  • Start Google search text campaigns only after Shopping has identified your best sellers, and build them one product at a time. Keep the keyword list minimal and phrase match, so each campaign stays specific. [L1112.010] (older, 2021)
  • Setting up search text ads for the best Shopping products: [L0971.020] (2024)
    1. Pull the best-performing products from Google Shopping.
    2. Create a search text campaign for each product.
    3. Start each at about $5 per day.
    4. Keep keywords very tight and targeted.
  • Exact-match campaign for a proven keyword: [L0837.047] (2025)
    1. Find a keyword in your Google Shopping search terms that brings clicks which turn into sales.
    2. Create a separate Search campaign with text ads that runs alongside Shopping, not in place of it.
    3. Give it a very small budget.
    4. Target that keyword as an exact match and add no negative keywords in this campaign, so it only appears for that exact phrase.
    5. Write the ad with the keyword as the headline and the description following the pattern in the matching unit.
    6. Optimize the matching product page with product page SEO so that it can rank organically.
  • A keyword that wins both a Shopping listing and a text ad can reach three positions on the first page of Google: two paid positions and one organic position from product page SEO. Use this when the keyword's clicks turn into sales. [L0837.049] (2025)
  • Once one product sells consistently, add a search text ad for it so that it holds three spots on page one: the Shopping ad, the search text ad above organic results, and the organic listing from product page SEO. Add search text ads for best sellers only after the product is selling profitably. [L1086.022] (older, 2019)
  • For a non-branded search term with no matching product page, build a collection page for that exact term and add real copy and content to it. The page should be specific enough that the ad's landing page matches the search intent, which also supports SEO. [L0880.029] (older, 2021-22)
  • If Google Shopping is not available in your country, set up Google Search text ads instead: [L0471.005] (2024)
    1. List the brand names you are authorized to sell.
    2. Search each brand on Google.
    3. Check which competitors run text ads at the top of the results.
    4. Test search text ads for those brands.

Ad Copy and Formats

Responsive Search Ads (current format)

  • Responsive Search Ads consist of up to 15 headlines (30 characters each) and up to 4 descriptions (90 characters each). Google assembles live combinations of these assets based on audience and context. [L0220.004] (Source E 2025-26)

Older formats (2021-22)

  • New Google search campaigns used responsive search ads by default, which take five headlines and five descriptions. The expanded text ad option was hidden in the interface, and the advice was to avoid responsive ads for this business model. [L0719.009] (older, 2021-22)
  • Steps to create expanded text ads in the Google Ads interface of the time: [L0719.010] (older, 2021-22)
    1. Create the search campaign.
    2. Create the ad group without any ads.
    3. Go back into that ad group.
    4. Create the ad and choose the option labeled to use expanded text ads.
  • A standard text ad in the Google Ads campaign builder has three headline fields (Headline 1 to 3, max 30 characters each), two description fields (max 90 characters each), and two display path fields after the domain (max 15 characters each). Each field shows a live character counter, so check the text against the limit before saving. [L0922.023] (older, 2021-22)
  • Responsive search ads test multiple combinations of the headlines and descriptions you provide, for example all possible combinations of 5 headlines with 5 descriptions. Google selects which combinations to show, which makes true split testing impossible. Use standard text ads to control which ad wins a split test yourself. [L0262.029] (older, 2021-22)
  • Avoid responsive search ads for manual split testing. They test multiple headline and description combinations automatically, so you cannot isolate which change drove an improvement. [L0262.019] (older, 2021-22)

Headline, path and copy rules

  • Google's ad preview warns that ad text may be shortened or not fully shown in some formats, and that specific text can be set to always appear. Put the main hook and the core promise at the start of the headline and the first description so they survive truncation. [L0922.024] (older, 2021-22)
  • Include keywords in the display path (the URL shown under the ad headline) as another relevance signal. Use the format "domain.com/product/keyword-keyword" to reinforce the product match. [L0262.015] (older, 2021-22)
  • Dynamic headline insertion is set up by putting short codes into the headlines of an ad, so the headline changes to match the customer's search while the landing page stays the same. It is optional and only worth testing where a generic headline underperforms. [L0719.013] (older, 2021-22)
  • Unlike Shopping ads, search text ads require you to choose the keywords you bid on and to write the complete ad yourself, because Google does not generate the copy. [L0751.012] (older, 2021-22)
  • A standard search text ad consists of a landing page URL or display URL, an ad headline and a limited amount of ad copy. You enter these elements yourself for every ad you create. [L0751.002] (older, 2021-22)
  • In a paid search text ad, swapping the top line or the bottom line can change click-through rate sharply, and a doubling is possible. Test one line at a time and compare CTR, because more clicks at the same cost lift sales directly. [L0575.032]
  • Product-page copy pattern: pin the product name as the first headline and put the price in the description, as in the product search campaign setup. [L0210.017]

Ad Extensions and Site Links

Principles

  • Ad extensions are one of the simplest ways to raise Quality Score, click-through rate and the volume of relevant traffic to a site, and they can also lower cost per click. Switch on any extension the platform offers, because it gives the ad more space on the results page and a more distinct look than competitors who do not use it. [L0712.001]
  • Callouts and site links take up more space on the results page, which helps even when they do not directly generate clicks. Run an extension whenever the option is available. [L0712.010]
  • Ad extensions make the ad more unique, since competitors who skip them look plainer. Using all available extensions together increases click-through rate and Quality Score, lowers cost per click and sends more of the intended traffic to the website. [L0712.015]
  • Ad extensions (callout, call, price, structured snippets) can increase click-through rate by up to 15%. Use at least 2 callout extensions to highlight offers. Phone extensions enable mobile users to call directly. [L0262.026] (older, 2021-22)
  • Ad extensions can add business information such as a physical location, phone number, product details and reviews. Google only pulls extensions into the ad some of the time, so the layout varies between searches. [L0751.003] (older, 2021-22)

Site links

  • Site links add four additional separate links below the main search ad within a campaign, so the searcher can jump directly to the page they want. For brand searches, useful site links point to new releases, a specific popular product, another popular product type, or a custom-build option. [L0712.006]
  • Choose site link targets from the most visited pages on the site, such as contact, shipping, reviews, or the best-selling product, rather than writing generic links. Site links should match what searchers of the brand or product are most likely to want. [L0712.007]
  • Procedure to create site links in Google Ads: [L0712.008]
    1. Open the search campaign you want to extend.
    2. Click Ads and extensions.
    3. Open the Extensions tab on the ads page.
    4. Click Add a new site link.
    5. Create each site link one by one inside Google Ads.
  • Conflicting guidance on product-specific ads: the 2021-22 build skips sitelink extensions for product-specific ads. [L0262.008] (older, 2021-22) The other sources say to switch on every available extension and use site links for brand searches. [L0712.001, L0712.006]

Callouts and other extensions

  • Callout extensions are short extra lines of text shown under the main ad, alongside site links. They state additional facts about the brand that customers should know. For e-commerce stores the lesson ranks them as the third most important extension. [L0712.009]
  • All search-campaign extension types, including site links, callouts and promotions, are added from one place: open the search campaign, go to Ads and extensions, then the Extensions tab on the ads page. Site links are created one at a time there, and the same tab offers the other extension types available for search text campaigns. [L0712.016]
  • Use call extensions or call-only ads for local service businesses (plumbers, locksmiths) where customers click to call directly from the ad. Use "Calls on website" to track calls from a phone number displayed on your website. [L0234.003]
  • Image extensions have a 50% approval rate and can be added optionally. [L0210.017]

Ad Testing and Optimization

Metrics

  • Text ad optimization focuses on improving both click-through rate (CTR) and conversions. CTR indicates whether the ad reaches the right audience and appeals enough to click. Conversion rate measures whether the landing page persuades visitors to complete the purchase. [L0085.001]
  • When split testing search ads, base the winner selection primarily on click-through rate, which signals relevance to Google. A higher CTR can lower your average CPC through improved quality score. Secondary metrics are conversions and cost per click. [L0262.017] (older, 2021-22)
  • Note the conflict: one source treats CTR and conversion rate together [L0085.001], while the 2021-22 course selects winners on CTR first [L0262.017].

Rotation and duration

  • Select "Do not optimise: Rotate ads indefinitely" instead of the default "Optimise: Prefer best performing ads". This lets you control when split tests end and which ad wins, rather than letting Google decide too quickly. [L0262.004] (older, 2021-22)
  • Run split tests for approximately 2 weeks. After 2 weeks, review metrics to determine the winning ad. [L0262.016] (older, 2021-22)
  • After launching, all ads enter a mandatory approval period before they can generate impressions. Approval typically takes 1-2 days but may complete within hours. Monitor the campaign dashboard; ads will not run until approved. [L0262.028] (older, 2021-22)

Test design

  • Keep a written log of all text ad variations tested, their performance results, and whether each succeeded or failed. Use this record to avoid retesting ineffective variations and to build a reference library of proven winning approaches. [L0085.003]
  • Iterative testing modifies only one element, such as the call-to-action phrase, while keeping all other headline, body copy and display URL constant. This isolates the impact of that single change, so any performance shift reveals what drove it. [L0085.004]
  • Evolutionary testing contrasts two substantially different ad variations, with different headlines, angles or overall messaging approaches, to discover which fundamental frame or emotional angle your audience responds to most. [L0085.005]
  • After a split test win, pause (do not delete) the losing ad so you can reference it later. Create a new ad variation attempting to beat the previous winner, then run a new 2-week split test. Repeat continuously. [L0262.018] (older, 2021-22)
  • Stacking and extensions are tested on the same basis: change one thing at a time. [L0575.032]

Monitoring

  • Check back within 1-2 days to review bids and respond to first-page warnings. [L0262.020] (older, 2021-22)
  • Low search volume on a specific product keyword is not a sign of failure. Check Shopping data before deciding. [L0262.021] (older, 2021-22)
  • Check the account at least once a day and add irrelevant search terms to the negative keyword list. [L1086.020] (older, 2019)
  • Use the search term report to keep and scale converting terms and to remove or refine terms that spend without paying off. [L1077.050]

Lead-Generation Search Ads

  • Search text ad lead generation is an advanced strategy. Start it only once standard search text ads are already profitable and the store is producing valuable content. It costs more and takes longer to show results than normal product-focused search ads. [L0922.001] (older, 2021-22)
  • Lead generation works best for product-specific or product-type-specific search terms that are tightly relevant to one product or a small group of products. Broad or low-value terms are the target for this approach. [L0922.002] (older, 2021-22)
  • Sending top-of-funnel search traffic to a product page or collection page usually fails, because the visitor is not ready to choose a product and the page cannot show which product fits their needs. A content page that explains how to choose the right product works better. [L0922.004] (older, 2021-22)
  • Sending a broad or top-of-funnel search to a single best-selling product page, or to a collection page with many near-identical models, makes the visitor compare the options alone. Without a clear fit, the visitor leaves quickly, so the paid click is wasted. [L0922.025] (older, 2021-22)
  • Procedure for top-of-funnel lead generation: [L0922.006] (older, 2021-22)
    1. Make sure standard product search text ads are already profitable and that a content marketing piece, such as an ultimate buying guide, exists on the store.
    2. Choose the top-of-funnel search term you want to target and set up a standard search text ad campaign for it.
    3. Write several ads that use a headline designed to grab attention and a description that hints at what the reader will learn.
    4. Set a bid and start the campaign.
    5. Send the ad's final URL to the content page, not to a product or collection page.
    6. Make sure retargeting audiences exist for visitors of that content page, for example on Google Display Network or Facebook.
    7. Place an email opt-in form on the content page so visitors can join the email list.
    8. After the visit, run retargeting ads and an email sequence to move the visitor toward a purchase.
  • Lead-generation ads use the standard search text ad campaign setup. Google Ads reports and the campaign builder are used to create the ads, with the Final URL set to the content page or quiz page. [L0922.020] (older, 2021-22)
  • Target CPA is used only for lead generation campaigns, such as buyer guide sign-ups, with a maximum cost per lead (for example 4 USD per lead). [L1092.068] (older, 2022)

When Search Text Ads Add Value

  • A search campaign can bring sales before the Shopping side is ready. A new store with search text ads made its first sale 45 minutes after the first ad went live, while the product feed was still pending [L1437.016].

  • For a new store, running a search campaign alongside Shopping builds early visibility, because search does not depend on the store's existing footprint. Google then also starts recognizing the store on the Shopping side [L1508.019].

  • Search text ads built on product listing ad (PLA) terms show below or beside the product listing ads, which lets the store take up more of the results page. In one example, text and PLA ads together filled most of the page and no organic results were visible [L1203.012].

  • To launch search text ads on profitable terms from PLA data:

    1. Open the search terms report of your product listing ads to see which terms trigger them.
    2. Pick the best-performing terms.
    3. Create a search text campaign built around those terms.

    No extensive keyword research is needed because the best keywords already appear in the PLA search terms [L1203.011].

  • Stacking means bidding on the same keywords with text ads that already win in Shopping, so the text ads sit on top of the product listing ads for those keywords:

    1. Identify the keywords that already convert for you through Google Shopping campaigns.
    2. Bid on the exact same keywords with Google Search text ads.
    3. Expect the combined presence to take up more of the search results page, which generally brings more traffic for those proven terms [L1350.012].

  • For choosing keywords, Google has already classified some terms as having commercial intent. Bidding on these lets you take up more space on the search results page [L1350.011].

  • A search ad for a keyword that already wins in Google Shopping should use the same exact keywords, so the text ad and the product listing appear together. Do not add keywords that have not yet converted in Shopping just because they look similar [L1350.017].

  • Older advice (2019): Run product search text ads only for your top roughly 1 percent of products that sell repeatedly and already max out your Google Shopping budget. Avoid generic keywords for these campaigns [L1275.045].

Why Relevance Matters More Than in the Past

  • Many e-commerce stores fail when they add Google Search text ads to an existing Shopping setup because they run them with the mindset of the early 2000s, when search clicks were cheap and competition was low. Search clicks today are expensive, so the same approach wastes budget [L1350.001].
  • Around the year 2000, nearly all Google search clicks cost less than 50 cents, which let advertisers tolerate very low conversion rates on loosely related queries. This no longer holds, so search campaigns need tight targeting [L1350.002].
  • When clicks are cheap, a broad or semi-relevant keyword can still pay off with a few conversions. When clicks are expensive, irrelevant keywords drain the budget quickly. Keyword relevance therefore matters far more for search text ads today than in the past [L1350.003].
  • A loosely related keyword can bring around 700 clicks and only one or two sales. When clicks cost pennies, that return looked acceptable, but at today's click prices the same pattern drains the budget quickly. Check the search terms report for such queries and exclude them [L1350.013].

Keyword Sources from Shopping Data

  • A keyword is the term you bid on. A search query (also called search term) is the text a shopper actually typed and that triggered your ad. The two are often confused, but they are the same thing in reports only when the match is exact [L1352.001].
  • To find converting search terms, open the campaign, go to Keywords and then Search terms, add the Conversions column, and download the report. Then add the product column in the downloaded data and filter for search terms with more than one conversion [L1352.012].
  • Google Ads does not show the product and the search term that triggered it together in the standard interface. This makes it hard to know which product a converting query belongs to. Workarounds exist, such as a separate ad group per product, but they are time-consuming [L1352.011].

Building a First Search Campaign from Converting Shopping Terms

  1. Open the Shopping campaign, go to Keywords > Search terms and add the Conversions column.
  2. Download the search terms report and add the product column to see which product received the conversions.
  3. Filter the data to search terms with more than one conversion.
  4. Confirm that all conversions point to the same product and note its URL as the landing page.
  5. In Google Ads, start a new campaign and choose Search as the campaign type.
  6. Select Website visits as the goal and enter the website URL.
  7. Enter a descriptive campaign name.
  8. Set the location to the United States and exclude Hawaii, Alaska and Puerto Rico.
  9. Set the location presence option to target people in the targeted locations and exclude people in the excluded locations.
  10. Set the language to English and choose the daily budget.
  11. Keep delivery method on Standard.
  12. Set bidding to manual and keep Enhanced CPC off.
  13. Skip dynamic search ads, audiences, sitelinks and call extensions for now.
  14. Save and continue.
  15. Create the ad group with a descriptive name that states the theme and source of the terms.
  16. Set the default bid to about $2 CPC, taken from the average CPC of the Shopping campaign.
  17. Enter the converting terms as keywords, typing exact match keywords inside square brackets.
  18. Write the ad: final URL, headline and display path. Skip tracking templates unless a third-party tool needs them, and add a mobile URL only if one is used.
  19. Save, continue to the campaign and launch it.
  20. In the campaign, change every keyword from broad match to exact match in one step using Change match types [L1352.013].

The purpose of this campaign is that it competes on the same results page as the winning Shopping ads and sends traffic to the same product URL that already converted. Because the terms already converted in Shopping, they have a high chance of converting in Search as well [L1352.017].

Campaign Settings and Starting Bids

  • Settings for a new Search campaign:
    • Campaign type: Search network only
    • Goal: Website visits
    • Location: United States, with Hawaii, Alaska and Puerto Rico excluded
    • Location option: target the chosen locations while excluding the excluded ones
    • Language: English
    • Delivery: Standard
    • Bidding: manual, Enhanced CPC off [L1352.014]
  • The procedure names Search as the campaign type [L1352.013], while the settings list narrows it to Search network only [L1352.014]. Use the settings list as the stated setting.
  • Bidding should be set manually at the start, with Enhanced CPC switched off, so the seller controls every bid while the campaign collects data. Dynamic search ads, audiences, sitelinks and call extensions can be added later once the basic campaign performs [L1352.015].
  • Use the average CPC from the Shopping campaign for the same product as the starting Search bid, which in the example was about $2 CPC. Adjust it later based on the search terms data [L1352.016].
  • When creating a new Search campaign, Google Ads asks whether the campaign should focus on conversions or on conversion value. Choose the option that fits how the campaign will be measured, and keep Enhanced CPC off while the campaign is still being started [L1352.018].
  • A descriptive campaign name can combine the product, the channel and the target return on ad spend, for example "infrared sauna search text 6x return on ad spend". Naming it this way makes the goal visible in every report [L1352.025].

Keyword Research and Checks

  • Ahrefs and SEMrush are keyword research tools used to measure monthly search volume for categories. Both offer free trials that can be used for a basic keyword check [L1389.015].
  • Relevance check before bidding on a keyword:
    1. Type the target keyword into Google in a private window and note which results show ads.
    2. Read the top ads and check whether the ad text mentions the specific use case or the informational need in the query.
    3. Compare the ads with the organic results below them.
    4. If the organic results match the query more closely than the ads, the ad is not relevant enough. Rewrite the ad and the landing page so they address the query directly, or drop the keyword.
    5. Repeat the check for each keyword before you start bidding on it [L1350.014].

Match Types

Match type Notation What it matches
Exact match [keyword] in square brackets Only the exact term and very close variants (plurals, misspellings, same meaning in word order)
Phrase match "keyword" in quotation marks Searches that contain the keyword phrase in that order, with other words possibly before or after it
Modified broad match +word1 +word2 with a plus sign before each word Close variants of each marked word individually (e.g. +red also matches reddish), but not unrelated words such as pink for +red
Broad match keyword without brackets, quotes or plus signs Synonyms, related searches, word stems and many unrelated but loosely connected queries

[L1352.002]

  • Broad match can show ads for a very wide range of queries, so the same keyword can trigger on surprising searches such as a different quantity or a different product type. Exact match is the most controlled option, phrase match is in between, and modified broad gives more control than broad because each word only expands to its own close variants [L1352.003].
  • Roughly 25 percent of Google searches each month are completely new and cannot be predicted by a seller. Match types exist so that the ad can still appear for these unknown queries [L1352.004].
  • Phrase match on the keyword "jump rope" lets a Search ad appear for long-tail searches such as a jump rope that survives hot summer sun, which a seller could never list in advance. This is why phrase match captures traffic that exact match misses [L1352.019].
  • Broad match on "car" can trigger on synonyms and related searches such as automobile, Ferrari paint color, truck tires and skyline roadster. This shows how far broad match can drift from the seller's product [L1352.020].
  • Modified broad match with +hike can also match its word stem hiking, and +CA can match the abbreviation California. Each marked word expands only to its own close variants, which keeps the reach narrower than broad match [L1352.021].
  • Keywords entered without brackets, quotes or plus signs are broad match, so a new Search ad group can start with broad keywords even when the intent was exact. Check every match type before the campaign goes live and convert the keywords to exact match in one step if needed [L1352.023].

Launch Keyword Mix and Bidding by Match Type

  • Start a new Search campaign with exact match keywords only, using terms that already converted in Shopping. Add phrase and modified broad keywords later at lower bids, and add broad match last at the lowest bids [L1352.005].

  • Launching a new Search campaign with broad match keywords lets the ads appear for many irrelevant queries, which wastes budget before any data is available. Use only proven converting terms at launch [L1352.010].

  • A long, specific term that already converted in Shopping, such as "infrared sauna for three people", should become the first exact match keyword in a new Search campaign. If it were broad match, the same ad could show for unrelated queries such as "infrared sauna for 20 people" [L1352.022].

  • Bid by match type in this order:

    • Most for exact match
    • Somewhat less for phrase match
    • Less again for modified broad match
    • Much less for broad match

    This keeps the budget on the most reliable queries [L1352.006].

  • Long term, the keyword mix of a Search campaign should be mostly exact match, with some phrase, some modified broad and only a small share of broad match. This is a long-term target and not the setup for a new launch [L1352.009].

Ad Group Structure

  • Keep an ad group to about 10 keywords maximum. With more keywords the ad group mixes too many themes, and the ad can no longer match what a searcher is looking for, which lowers relevance [L1352.008].
  • An ad group that mixes several themes, such as cabinet doors with other product types, forces the ad copy to be generic. A generic ad does not speak to a specific query, which lowers relevance and clicks [L1352.024].

Product-Specific Campaigns

  • Build each product's search campaign around that product's own converting terms and its single landing URL, so every conversion points to the product that earned it [L1352.013, L1352.011].
  • A separate ad group per product is the workaround for seeing which product a converting search term belongs to, although it takes more time [L1352.011].
  • Older advice (2019): Product search text ads are for the top roughly 1 percent of products that already max out the Shopping budget [L1275.045].

Search Intent and Funnel Stage

  • Meet the searcher at their stage in the funnel, whether they are early in research or ready to buy. Each keyword's landing page should reflect that stage [L1350.010].
  • Match the landing page to the search intent. A top-of-funnel informational query should lead to an informational page, while a bottom-of-funnel commercial query can lead to a product page. Never send top-of-funnel traffic to a bottom-of-funnel landing page [L1350.005].
  • Sending people who search for information, such as how-to or comparison questions, straight to a product page is a costly mistake because they are not ready to buy. Running traffic this way is essentially paying for awareness and rarely converts [L1350.004].
  • Classify search keywords by commercial intent to decide which funnel stage each keyword belongs to [L1386.013]:
Group Examples from the source
Commercial "buy product", "product free shipping", product-type terms
Midway "product comparison", "product A versus B"
Non-commercial Keywords that describe a problem or a question
  • The sources place comparisons in the midway group [L1386.013], while the list of information-seeking queries that are not ready to buy includes comparison questions [L1350.004]. Treat comparison keywords as midway and do not send them straight to a product page [L1386.013, L1350.004].
  • Build your ad messages from the searcher's exact question or problem, and match the landing page to that stage, because a sale depends on the ad, the landing page and the user's readiness working together [L1386.015].

Informational and Lead-Generation Searches

  • Organic results for informational searches often outperform paid ads. Turning a strong informational organic listing into an ad would likely bring even more traffic. Paid informational ads should therefore point to genuinely helpful content, not a sales page [L1350.008].
  • For an informational query such as asking what makes a good trail running shoe, ads that push free shipping, free returns, or a shop-now message miss the searcher's intent. The searcher wants information, so a sales-driven ad gets fewer clicks than an informational result [L1350.007].
  • An informational landing page lets you capture a lead through an email opt-in, set a tracking cookie, and retarget the visitor later. These benefits make top-of-funnel traffic valuable even when the visitor does not buy immediately [L1350.009].
  • Keywords that do not fit the simple top, middle and bottom split can still be handled:
    • For someone who knows the problem but not the solution, such as "manage business finances", offer a free software trial as a lead generation step and sell the paid sign-up later [L1386.014].
    • For a problem such as "improving focus at work", offer a low-commitment product such as a supplement with the first month free [L1386.014].

Ad Copy Relevance

  • Ads for a specific use case, such as running shoes for hills, fail when neither the ad text nor the landing page mentions that use case. Without this relevance, the ad performs worse than the organic results for the same query [L1350.006].
  • Showing ads for search queries that differ from what the buyer intended pushes the click-through rate very low. Keep keyword targeting and ad text tightly aligned with the actual search intent [L1401.010].
  • The display URL path is free text that can contain a sale word or the target keyword, which signals to the searcher where they will land. It does not have to be the real page address [L1401.013].
  • Putting the price in the ad copy pre-qualifies buyers and stops people who cannot afford the product from clicking. This may lower CTR, but it improves the cost-to-revenue ratio, which matters more than CTR. Shopping campaigns use the same logic by showing prices [L1401.015].
  • Adding buying-intent keywords (such as "buy" or "price") keeps non-buyers from clicking at all. Limiting unlikely buyers before the click usually saves more money than raising CTR [L1401.016].
  • Older advice (2021-2022): The copywriting principles for search text ads apply equally to Bing or any other search engine where the ad is shown. Google is the platform noted for automatically bolding the searched keyword in the ad [L1351.010].

Ad Copy Testing

  • Routine ad copy testing:
    1. Run at least two ads at the same time.
    2. Track which headlines, descriptions and calls to action get clicks and which get conversions.
    3. Keep written notes on what works and what does not, so new tests build on past results.
    4. Compare CTR and conversion rate together before deciding a winner [L1401.005].
  • Ad test steps for finding the strongest versions:
    1. Split test your ads to find the versions with the highest click-through rate.
    2. Use emotion in the ad copy to make people want to click.
    3. Give a clear call to action.
    4. Give people a good reason to click on the ad [L1226.015].
  • Optimize Google text ads for click-through rate, but always check conversion rate alongside it. Run at least two ads in the same ad group at the same time so results can be compared [L1401.004].
  • CTR and cost-to-revenue can pull in different directions. Price in the copy may lower CTR, and the cost-to-revenue ratio then takes priority [L1401.015]. Where that trade-off applies, judge the ad by revenue per cost, not by CTR alone [L1401.004, L1401.015, L1401.016].
  • Older advice (2021-2022): Saving changes to a live ad creates a new version that is sent back for review. Metrics from earlier versions remain visible, so you can compare the old and new ad versions [L1391.021].

First-Sale Timing and Early Results

  • Search ads can produce early sales while Shopping listings are not yet approved. The new store in the example made its first sale 45 minutes after its first search ad went live [L1437.016].
