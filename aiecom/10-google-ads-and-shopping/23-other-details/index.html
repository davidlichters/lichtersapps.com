10.23 Other details

Launch order and first targets

Channel priority

  • Use Google Shopping ads as the first paid channel for a high-ticket store, because they show your products to people already searching for them. Bing Shopping ads can be added as a second search channel. (older, 2021-2022) [L0458.050]
  • A search-engine-based funnel without social media can sell high-ticket products, because customers already search for the product. Social media ads are optional, not required. (older, 2021-2022) [L0458.052]
  • Google Shopping is the largest source of traffic and conversions for these stores. Get Shopping campaigns working well before adding search, display or discovery ads. [L0806.062]
  • In the first months of a new high-ticket dropshipping store, most traffic is paid, so most diagnostic time should go into Google Ads traffic. Organic traffic usually takes longer to build, so the balance shifts toward organic only as the store matures. (older, 2021-2022) [L1456.041]
  • Going live sooner is better, even if training is still in progress. Learn by working in your ad account while watching the training, so the setup is ready when you finish. (older, 2018) [L1109.058]

Launch sequence A: Shopping first, then remarketing (older, 2022)

  1. Launch the alpha beta Google Shopping campaigns first and keep them live. [L0854.039]
  2. Then add Facebook remarketing ads. [L0854.039]
  3. Then add Google remarketing ads. [L0854.039]
  4. Keep 90% of the budget on alpha beta and split 10% between Facebook and Google remarketing. [L0854.039]

Launch sequence B: Shopping, then brand search, then product search (2023)

  1. Launch Alpha and Beta Shopping campaigns with a combined $5 to $30 per day and optimize them using the data. [L0855.005]
  2. Once Shopping sales are consistently profitable, launch a brand search campaign for the store name with a small budget. [L0855.005]
  3. Once specific products are proven sellers in Shopping, build search text campaigns around those product pages using the keywords that already drive their Shopping sales. [L0855.005]

Launch sequence C: New dropshipping store (undated lesson)

  1. Launch the store and get Google Shopping ads running as soon as possible. [L1126.029]
  2. Test the full checkout with your own credit card, then cancel the order. [L1126.029]
  3. Upload at least 100 unique products to act as a broad product test. [L1126.029]
  4. Aim for 60 unique visitors per day from Google Shopping only, without retargeting or Facebook ads yet. [L1126.029]
  5. Expect about one sale every other day even at a low conversion rate, which is the baseline for further growth. [L1126.029]

Baseline targets for a new store

  • 100 unique products, 60 unique visitors per day through Google Shopping, and about one sale every other day. The lesson presents this as the minimum before outside help makes sense. [L1126.030]

Conflict: when retargeting starts

  • Sequence A adds retargeting right after the Shopping campaigns are live (2022, older). [L0854.039]
  • Sequence C holds retargeting and Facebook ads back until Shopping alone reaches the baseline above. [L1126.029, L1126.030]

Launch on Google and Bing (older) (2019)

  1. On day one set up Google Shopping product listing ads, which takes about 20 minutes. [L0356.050]
  2. Set up Bing right after, because Bing can transfer the Google setup with little extra work and syncs with it. [L0356.050]
  3. Hold off on Google search text ads until the store is making money and the best sellers are known. [L0356.050]

Funnel and channel mix

High-ticket launch funnel (older, 2021-2022)

  1. Run ads on Google Shopping, where customers first find the products. [L0771.038]
  2. Make the site available for search engines so it appears in free organic listings. [L0771.038]
  3. Capture visitors' email addresses while they browse, then email them to bring them back to purchase. [L0771.038]
  4. Show retargeting ads to visitors who viewed products but did not buy. [L0771.038]
  5. After the first sale, use email and retargeting to bring the same customer back for a second and third purchase. [L0771.038]

Four channels to run the store

  • Master four channels to run the store: Google Shopping ads, organic search engine optimization for free listings, email marketing and retargeting. These channels are enough to generate large revenue in a good market. (older, 2021-2022) [L0771.039]
  • Keep Google Ads running if they are profitable, but do not treat them as the only source. The framework's minimum set of four traffic channels is Google Ads, organic search, social media and YouTube. (older, 2021-2022) [L0414.027]

Adding social and other ad types

  • Run a brand search campaign, a retargeting campaign and a Performance Max campaign alongside Facebook ads, since the combination adds traffic from several sources. Google's Performance Max shows ads across Google surfaces such as YouTube and websites. [L0871.078]
  • Once a brand has consistent results, adding Google and Facebook ads gives omni-channel presence, so the brand reaches a customer wherever they browse. Visitors who leave without buying can be reached again through Google ads and retargeting. [L1382.064]
  • Traffic sources beyond Google Shopping include Google Discovery, Display, YouTube and search text ads. Facebook dynamic remarketing is the next strongest source after Google, and banner ads or sponsored articles on niche blogs have been consistently profitable for drop-shipping stores. (older, 2021) [L0853.099]
  • Shopping and search can be complemented by other traffic sources, such as transfer-of-influence blog banner ads, for products with color or design searches that do not match SKU and brand queries. (older, 2022) [L0969.068]

Conflict: social ads

  • In a search-only high-ticket funnel, social ads are optional (2021-2022, older). [L0458.052]
  • Where Google Shopping and a brand are already running, Facebook ads are added alongside them (no stage condition given). [L0871.078, L1382.064]

Platform comparison and print-on-demand

  • Google Ads had the highest return of the platforms compared at the time, followed by YouTube ads, TikTok ads and Pinterest ads. Instagram placements performed worse and Snapchat burned money quickly. [L1440.036]
  • For a print-on-demand store that needs its first 1,000 in sales, paid ads are needed. Google Ads was described as the most reliable and consistent channel for this model, and ads with product images are the entry route. [L1438.044]

Scaling into advanced Google Ads types

When to add more types

  • Stores that reach roughly $50,000 to $100,000 per month in revenue and can no longer grow Google Ads spend beyond about 10% extra ad spend are the stage where mixing in additional Google Ads types makes sense. The source wording on the 10% figure is unclear. [L0544.003]
  • Advanced Google Ads types are mostly a testing ground for typical stores. They can get good results but are less proven and less profitable than the core Google Ads setup, so run them as experiments. [L0544.004]

Optimize before changing niche

  • Google Ads optimization comes before a niche change. A new niche is not the answer when a store is making sales. First reduce the budget and reintroduce spend as campaigns become more efficient. [L0836.059]

Budget, bidding and cost signals

Spend and cost reference

  • Example Google Ads spend over 90 days: $100,000. (Source E 2025-26) [L0222.010]
  • Search volume cannot be increased by spending more money on Google Ads, because it reflects what the market searches for. Focus on keyword choice and product listing quality instead. (Source C) [L0788.022]

Competition and cost signals

  • A product with no paid competitors in Google Shopping typically means cheap clicks. (2023) [L0855.022]
  • Third-party click protection services are not worth paying for. Google already blocks invalid clicks, and a click protector did not reduce traffic or improve return on ad spend in one test. (older, 2022) [L0854.053]

Time in the ad account

  • When hours in the ad account are cut back to about 30 minutes for routine data checks and small tweaks, the freed time should go to more important work such as SEO. Extra time in the ad account is justified only for setting up a new account, building new campaigns, or making major structural changes. (older, 2021-2022) [L1514.022]

Campaign setup and account settings

Campaign list filters

  • In Google Ads, open the Campaigns view and set the Campaign status filter to 'All but removed' so that paused and limited campaigns appear alongside active ones. Without this filter, you can miss shopping campaigns that hold useful search term data. (older, 2021-2022) [L0880.027]

  • Hide removed campaigns:

    1. Open the campaigns view. [L0934.066, L0934.124]
    2. Use the filter that controls campaign status or campaign type. [L0934.066, L0934.124]
    3. Select All but removed. [L0934.066, L0934.124]

    Removed campaigns still exist in the account and cannot be deleted, but the red X entries disappear from view. [L0934.066, L0934.124]

Account availability

  • Google Ads may show that Buy on Google is unavailable for businesses registered in Canada. Before concluding that you cannot list products, search Google's documentation for your country and open a live chat or support ticket with Google. (2023) [L0804.111]

Niche research on Google Shopping

Validation thresholds

  • Minimum 10,000 monthly search volume in Google Ads is required for niche validation. (2023) [L0274.007]
  • The niche's own research should decide the niche. The ranking of competitor stores in organic search results does not decide whether a niche is viable. (older, 2018) [L0466.054]
  • Competitor stores that a niche should target as future competitors should rank between 1 and 100,000 on SimilarWeb within the country where the business plans to sell, not the global rank. (2024) [L0836.016]
  • Niche research tool: the source's own site provides a keyword research tool and a competitor research tool with Alexa traffic data for niche validation. (older, 2022) [L0249.062]

Reading Google Shopping results

  • If searches show mostly cheap items for a niche you think is luxury, raise the price filter in the left sidebar of Google Shopping and check whether the brands and volume still appear in your target price range. (older, 2020) [L0852.066]
  • Use Standard Shopping data for keyword and product research only. Sponsored results at the top of Google Shopping show one store per ad, not the full competitor set. (2024) [L1115.051]
  • Standard Shopping data for a product shows up to 20 reviews only if the scraping limit and pagination are set. Check the review count before analyzing product demand. (2024) [L1115.091]

Pricing outliers and sellers

  • Prices on Google Shopping can be skewed by outliers. Include high-priced items only if they are relevant to what you would sell, and exclude them when they are unrelated or obvious pricing errors. (2023) [L1283.029]
  • If a niche's average price is distorted by low-priced results, add a qualifying keyword to the search, such as family size or luxury, to match the products you actually want to sell. Refining the keyword is better than excluding products one by one. (2023) [L1283.030]
  • Exclude Etsy listings from supplier-based calculations because they are unique sellers, but include Etsy results when judging what customers actually see. Include evergreen eBay stores in price averages and exclude one-off auction listings. (2023) [L1283.031]
  • A niche's first-page Google Shopping competition is healthy when more than half of the top results come from ten or fewer stores. If more than half come from many stores, refine the niche before launching. (2023) [L1283.034]

Competitor tracking

  • The Search Monitor tracks competitors' Google Shopping ads, showing which sellers appear for your product searches and at what price. (2023) [L0430.004]
  • The Search Monitor is a dedicated monitoring tool for Shopping campaigns, used to track how Shopping ads appear and perform in search results. (2023) [L1093.002]

Search terms, content and SEO

Using search terms to find content

  • To find content ideas in a Google Shopping campaign, review the search terms report for informational queries that do not fit a product page, such as beginner-level questions. Exclude those terms from the shopping campaign so they stop spending on product ads. Then create a content page for each one and promote it with a Google Search text ad. Move the visitors from that page into the email list and retargeting audiences. (older, 2021-2022) [L0385.024]

Anchor content for organic traffic (older, 2020)

  1. Identify top traffic products in Google Analytics (Content > Pages, sort high to low). [L0245.020]
  2. Customize the top 20% of product pages by traffic with unique descriptions, additional photos, and embedded videos. [L0245.020]
  3. Build 3-5 'anchor blog posts' providing massive value, incorporating quotes/contributions from authority figures in your niche. [L0245.020]
  4. Email anchor posts to your list and share on social. [L0245.020]
  5. Reach out to contributing authorities to request they share the content, expanding reach. [L0245.020]

Tools for the content work

  • Google Page Speed Test measures website loading speed and identifies specific technical errors affecting performance. A score under 75% indicates optimization is needed, which directly affects Google organic search rankings. (older, 2020) [L0245.021]
  • Google Analytics (GA) Content > Pages shows which product pages receive the most traffic. Sort high to low for the last quarter to identify your top 20% of performers for optimization focus. (older, 2020) [L0245.022]

SEO rules

  • Module 6 includes the 'Search Engine Domination' lesson (2.5 hours) covering site speed optimization, SEO implementation, and compliance. Follow this lesson instead of hiring external SEO companies. (older, 2022) [L0272.036]
  • Do not outsource backlinks or backlink services. Google's algorithms advanced significantly. Risk of sandbox penalty and loss of organic traffic. Instead, focus on content and product pages that naturally earn links. (older, 2022) [L0272.037]
  • Content pages attract backlinks much more easily than product or collection pages. Terms that need links to reach page one should therefore be targeted with content pages. (older, 2021-2022) [L0877.023]
  • Shopping ads still show products above AI-generated results on Google, so product searches are less affected by AI tools than content searches. The main impact of AI results is on content and blog visibility. (2023) [L1094.045]

Other channels and marketplaces

Fallback when Shopping is unavailable

  • Where Google Shopping is not available, run Google Search text ads using your supplier names. Also research local shopping comparison engines, which many sellers use as an alternative channel. (older, 2020) [L0961.062]
  • For products Google does not allow, use secondary ad networks. BuySellAds lists websites where ads can be bought, and Bing is generally less strict than Google. (older, 2019) [L0356.052]

Google Search and Bing text ads

  • Google and Bing ads (text and shopping ads) can drive traffic to your Amazon listing, but conversion rates are significantly lower than Amazon-native ads because Google shoppers are researching, not buying. Bing sometimes outperforms Google depending on niche. These channels warrant minimal budget unless you discover an unusually profitable angle. [L0228.032]

Amazon

  • Google Ads to Amazon product listing: direct traffic to ASIN. In the supplement category, this was tested but did not become profitable; it broke even initially, then lost money. Not recommended for all products. Results vary by category and competition level. [L0227.031]
  • As of 2015, Amazon product ads were being discontinued, and Amazon's shoppers could still be reached through the Google Display Network. Do not plan around Amazon product ads as an acquisition channel. [L1328.035]

eBay

  • eBay Promoted Listings uses a percentage slider. Set an ad rate such as 1 percent, and you only pay that percentage when the item sells. Promoted items are placed higher in search rankings. [L1441.041]

Direct site and blog advertising

  • Craigslist as a quick, short-term way to get early sales without ads, in the top two cities where you operate:

    1. Post a photo of your bestseller. [L0429.014]
    2. State that the products are in stock and can be delivered within the city (for example Manhattan, within 3-5 business days). [L0429.014]
    3. Answer the emails that come in to start conversations. [L0429.014]

    This is only a very early tactic and does not replace long-term traffic sources. (older, 2022) [L0429.014]

  • When a site sells ads directly, check its advertising section for separate listing types such as single product listings, multi-product listings and banner advertising, and note the advertising email in the sheet. Banner ads are usually the easiest format to get as a free trial, so start with a trial there. [L0528.028]

  • Outreach template for blog owners:

Hey, we came across your website and love what you publish. We would love to write a guest post for your blog. Or alternatively, we would like to take out a banner ad in your sidebar, since I noticed you are not currently running any ads on the site. If you're open to either, I'd be happy to share more details and discuss pricing.

[L0528.033]

Geo-targeting

  • Geo-targeted ads will not be shown to users outside the target country. Check how your ads appear in the target market using a VPN when you are abroad. Do this only in a separate browser, because logging into your Shopify admin through the VPN can freeze a payout. [L1441.025]

Ad costs, seasonality and market data

Downturns

  • During April 2020 COVID-19 lockdowns, Google ad costs decreased by 20% while Facebook ad costs decreased by 15%, driven by reduced advertiser competition and increased user search/social activity. (older, 2020) [L0245.018]
  • The same fall in ad costs appeared on Google too, though the decrease was smaller than on Meta. [L1407.021]
  • During economic downturns with reduced advertising competition, maintain current ad budgets and shift strategy toward building remarketing audiences for when consumer confidence returns. Higher lead volume at the same ROAS compounds future revenue. (older, 2020) [L0245.019]

Reported market figures (dated)

  • Reported for Q1 2018: paid search ad spend grew 21% year over year, and Q4 saw seasonal spend increases in heavy e-commerce periods. Shopping ads typically grow by double digits each year and drive most non-branded revenue in paid search. (older, 2018) [L1455.009]
  • Reported for Q4 2017 compared with Q4 2016: desktop ad spend in e-commerce search grew about 20% year over year, and mobile shopping was described as rising too. Treat these as 2017 to 2018 figures and check current data before using them for planning. (older, 2017-2018) [L1455.015]

Google Ads interface and changes

  • Google moved its older AdWords interface toward retirement after the 2018 rename, so new campaign work should be done in the current Google Ads interface. Older tutorials and screenshots may show menus and layouts that no longer exist. (older, 2018) [L1455.018]
  • Google frequently runs several interface versions at once and tests changes on subsets of users, so the Google Ads screen may look different from one day or one account to the next. Do not assume a setting has disappeared just because its location or appearance differs. (older, 2021-2022) [L1494.005]
  • Google changes the look of the Google Ads interface often, sometimes weekly to monthly, but changes to how the ads actually work are much rarer. When a setting seems missing, look for it in its new location, since the underlying option is usually still there. (older, 2021-2022) [L1494.006]
  • When the Google Ads screen differs from a guide, focus on the outcome or function you need rather than the exact menu position. The option usually still exists under a new label or placement, and the underlying mechanics of how ads run change far less often than the layout. (older, 2021-2022) [L1494.007]

Store operations and shipping

Heavy items

  • For large heavy products, shipping is usually handled by the supplier's own carrier. About 70 percent of suppliers ship on their own, and about 30 percent require the store to use a carrier such as Freight Center with a bill of lading. (2023) [L0964.066]
  • Freight Center (freightcenter.com) gives shipping cost quotes from supplier location to multiple US cities for pricing calculations. (older, 2022) [L0249.061]

Order and product rules

  • Products that are not quoted with a clear default price should use the default quantity the top competitors use for their own product pages. (2021, older) [L0834.073]
  • Offering cards, invoices, or tracking should use only Shopify default order confirmations. A separate invoice is not sent. (2021, older) [L0834.074]
  • Do not use ad targeting for the word Shopify. Ads that target it can be taken down. (2023) [L0964.067]

Team and hiring

  • Loom records screen, audio and webcam for team training. Recordings can be saved into folders such as team training, order processing or supplier training, and shared with chosen email addresses. (older, 2022) [L1281.087]
  • When choosing which marketing hire to add first, PPC can be prioritized over SEO if the owner wants a quicker return on investment. PPC needs a budget that covers ad spend within the operating expenses allocation. (older, 2021-2022) [L1334.032]

Strategy adaptation

  • Account results depend on the specific market, product and niche, so a strategy must be adapted to your own situation rather than copied exactly. Early results may be weaker than expected while the setup is being fine-tuned on both the ads and the website. (older, 2021-2022) [L1228.012]

Learning and working habits

Learning resources

  • searchscientist.com/blog is a blog on paid search (PPC) topics that can be used for ongoing Google Ads education. [L1078.005]
  • Search Engine Land is a news site that covers Google Ads updates and changes, useful for staying current on platform developments. [L1078.006]
  • PPC Hero is a paid search blog that is widely read by PPC practitioners and is a good source for ongoing Google Ads learning. [L1078.007]

Habits

  • Keep learning continuously through blogs, podcasts, videos and news about paid advertising, because staying curious about how to push campaigns further is part of keeping them profitable. [L0463.008]
  • When stuck on a problem such as Google Ads, book a coaching call, solve that problem, and repeat the practice until it feels comfortable. Then move on to the next problem. [L0832.036]
