10.22 Account suspensions, appeals and Google support

Why Accounts Get Suspended

Merchant Center: Misrepresentation and Data Mismatches

  • Google suspends Merchant Center accounts mainly for five reasons: business information that does not match the website, incomplete product data, missing required business information on the website, incomplete Merchant Center settings, and products in prohibited categories. A suspension is not a deletion; the account is only blocked from use. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0727.002]
  • Suspensions often carry the reason misrepresentation, which means Google believes some information is missing or wrong. Suspension notices arrive by email to the business email address set up for the account. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0727.008]
  • A suspension is a legitimacy check, not a final verdict. Google suspends to protect searchers and to screen out scam or low-quality stores. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0727.025]
  • New accounts are checked most strictly and are often suspended almost immediately. Established accounts can also be suspended after two years or more of trading. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0727.025]
  • Google tightened account security after the e-commerce boom in 2020, so suspensions can also hit legitimate sellers. A suspension can be reversed, so fix the cause and request reinstatement rather than panic. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0731.004]
  • Common causes of suspension are shipping or tax information in Merchant Center that does not match the Shopify store, product prices in the catalog that do not match the store, missing contact information, and a missing privacy policy. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0801.072]
  • Missing or inconsistent contact details can cause a contact-information suspension. The contact page should offer at least two contact methods, such as a phone number, an email address or a contact form, plus a privacy policy. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0801.071]
  • A currency mismatch, missing contact details or a missing privacy policy can flag an account. Fix these and then appeal. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0468.118]
  • A missing or unverified business phone number in Merchant Center is a recurring trigger for misrepresentation. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0247.060, L0962.067, L0429.080]
  • Shipping costs, the shipping policy and the privacy policy URLs in Merchant Center must match what appears in the Shopify store, because Google bots add products to cart to check this. [L0856.035]
  • Footer social links that point to Shopify social pages or to platform home pages, instead of your own profiles, were a known cause of a Merchant Center suspension. Change them to your own profiles or remove them, then resubmit. (Advice from 2023.) [L0934.072]
  • Misrepresentation often comes from missing policy pages and content. Make sure the store has all required policies and pages. (Older advice, 2022.) [L1281.107]
  • Vague or missing wording in shipping and return policies is a common cause of a suspension without a clear reason. Check that both policies are clear and complete. [L0808.025]
  • Never invent a GTIN or enter a third-party GTIN that does not belong to the product. Google treats a mismatched GTIN as misrepresentation, which can lead to suspension. [L1283.038]
  • A missing GTIN alone does not cause a misrepresentation suspension. The problem arises only when a wrong identifier is placed in the GTIN field. [L1283.039]
  • A contact detail or policy that differs even slightly from the Merchant Center entry can trigger misrepresentation. Example: a suite number written differently. Compare address, phone, email and every policy character by character with the store. [L1283.045]
  • A Merchant Center suspension can have many causes. Check the GTIN, verified phone number, shipping, return and privacy policies, and product detail content, and use the full checklist before escalating to support. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0469.095]
  • Installed dropshipping apps alone were not considered a cause of account rejection. The likely trigger is a red flag on the store that a Google bot detects as dropshipping. [L0577.054]
  • Blocking Google's checkout crawler to hide abandoned checkouts also leads to automatic feed suspension. Do not try to block it. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1170.040]
  • Test ads with fake URLs or non-existent websites can get a Google Ads account suspended. Ads must point to the business website linked to the account. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0727.012]

Google Ads: Account-Level Reasons

  • Google Ads accounts are suspended for specific reasons: incorrect business information; multiple Google Ads accounts tied to the same business or personal information, including old dormant accounts after a prior suspension; a payment method flagged as fraudulent or previously bounced; and ads that point to a URL other than the business website or to a website that does not exist. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0727.009]
  • A Google Ads suspension applies to the whole advertiser account and every related account. While suspended, you cannot open new accounts, and ads stop running on Search, search partner sites and the Display Network at the same time. A store that relies on one paid channel can lose all paid traffic at once. [L0380.033]
  • Google can flag an ad account as restricted in a country where the advertiser does not sell or advertise. This is usually an automated error. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0803.103]
  • For suspected bot clicks, contact Google support by live chat for the recommended course of action before relying on a click-blocking tool alone. (Older advice, 2021.) [L1279.068]
  • A Google Ads suspension for suspicious payment activity is usually caused by billing details that do not match the account, which is covered in the appeal steps below. [L0806.067]

Circumventing Systems and Linked Accounts

  • The circumventing systems suspension usually occurs when Google believes a new account was opened after a previous account was closed. [L1283.043]
  • The circumventing flag often appears when a previously suspended account used the same billing address, credit card or website as a new account. Creating a new account to get around a policy triggers this flag. [L0857.014]
  • Google can link new ad accounts to earlier ones through IP address, cookies, name, address and card details. Creating a new ad account after a suspension therefore usually fails. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0963.019]
  • A suspension on one platform does not automatically affect other platforms. Accounts on Microsoft, Facebook, TikTok, Pinterest and Instagram can keep running. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0963.020]
  • Using agency accounts to circumvent bans on Google Merchant Center is not recommended. It can lead to a cycle of banned accounts and costs a lot of money. [L0577.050]
  • Only use.com domains for the store. Do not switch to a.store or.us domain to fix a Google Ads policy problem, because changing domains to get around a flag can look like circumventing systems. [L0857.012]
  • The circumventing flag can be a false flag when a new account was opened after a previously closed account. If you never had an earlier account, appeal the suspension instead of creating a new one. [L0965.007]

Response Procedure After a Suspension

First Steps

  1. Do not reply immediately, and do not reply in anger. [L0727.013]
  2. Read the suspension reason in Google's email. It may be a warning giving 7 days to fix the issue, or an immediate suspension. [L0727.013]
  3. Check that the business information in the account is complete. [L0727.013]
  4. Make sure the website follows the business launch checklist. [L0727.013]
  5. Check product data in the feed for disapproved products and missing attributes. [L0727.013]
  6. Make sure shipping, tax, phone number and other account information match the website exactly. [L0727.013]
  7. Delete old, unused Google Ads accounts. [L0727.013, L0727.009]
  8. Submit a detailed, neutral response through the form or avenue in Google's email, explaining what was checked and changed and asking for a review. [L0727.013]
  9. Expect a review within one to two days, sometimes up to a week. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0727.013]
  10. Unsuspension responses must be neutral and factual. An angry or terse reply makes the problem worse and delays the review. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0727.014]
  11. When a Merchant Center issue is unclear, respond calmly, check the official Google policy pages, and ask an expert or coach rather than attempting a risky fix alone. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0730.018]

Merchant Center Recovery (Current Method)

  1. Read the compliance checklist lesson and confirm every recommended setting is in place. (Older advice, 2021.) [L1279.067]
  2. Log in to Merchant Center and click through all settings, making sure each is complete. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0962.068]
  3. Check the phone number under contact info in Merchant Center settings. [L1279.067]
  4. Check whether the business phone number is verified. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0962.068]
  5. Verify the phone number. Depending on what Merchant Center offers, use the verification by text message [L0247.060], the automated call [L0962.068], or the PIN sent by phone call [L1279.067]. (Older advice, 2021.)
  6. Reply to Google's suspension email and request a review. In the source example, reinstatement came the same day. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0962.068, L0962.067]
  7. Follow up with Google until the account is reinstated. [L1279.067]

Merchant Center: Full Review Request

  1. Log in to Google Merchant Center. [L1094.042]
  2. Add the return policy and make the other changes Google asked for. [L1094.042]
  3. Find the request review option, which may appear in the first email Google sent. [L1094.042]
  4. Submit the request. After the first few reviews, Google may impose a waiting period of about one week before the next submission. [L1094.042]
  5. Before requesting review, correct all mismatches. The first suspension round is usually handled by an algorithm, and a human reviews the store only after a manual review request. [L1283.046]
  6. Read the reason Google gives and fix the mismatched item. [L1283.044]
  7. Check that every setting and policy in Merchant Center matches the store exactly. [L1283.044]
  8. Submit for a review. If suspended again, request a manual review. [L1283.044]
  9. In the manual review request, list each correction you made and state that you checked all Google policies. [L1283.044]
  10. Do not request a review again until the problems are fixed. [L0962.069]
  11. After a Merchant Center suspension is fixed, wait for Google's review result before changing other platforms. Once confirmed, make sure the same errors do not repeat on Microsoft, Bing or Meta. [L0934.073]
  12. Prepare the fix before appealing, so the first appeal succeeds. A failed appeal can be resubmitted. [L0934.074]
  13. Manual review requests for suspended products are typically decided within 24 to 48 hours. Use them when a flag appears to be an algorithm misreading rather than a true violation. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1170.045]

Merchant Center: Standard Template

Use this message when requesting a Google Merchant Center account review after a suspension. Replace each bracketed placeholder with the real information and keep every sentence in the same order:

To whom this may concern,

We are submitting our store for review after our account was initially suspended for [reason] on [date]. We understand that our initial submission was missing [issues]. All of these issues have now been addressed in their entirety. After reviewing all of the Google Merchant Center terms of service, we now believe our site is in full compliance with your policies. [List each change you made to meet compliance.]

Thank you for your time and review.

[L1094.043]

Google Ads: Response Routine

  1. Identify the suspension reason first by contacting support. Do not create a new Google Ads account with the same email, card on file or URL. [L0278.005]
  2. Fix whatever caused the suspension. [L0359.073]
  3. Contact Google Ads support by phone first, or by live chat if phone support is unavailable. [L0359.073]
  4. Ask the support team to reinstate the suspended account. [L0359.073]
  5. Use the Google Ads support page to find the contact options. [L0359.073]
  6. Only if reinstatement fails, create another Google account. [L0359.073] (Note: the 2025 rule in step 1 and the rule against new accounts in the Avoiding New Accounts section both apply before this step.) [L0278.005, L0963.018]
  7. Keep records of every change made. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0801.073]

Recovery Routines by Suspension Type

Suspension for misrepresentation (Google Ads)

  1. Re-check the store's content (business details, product claims, policies) against the policy requirements and fix any mismatch. [L0666.067]
  2. If nothing stands out, ask an outside reviewer to look at the store with fresh eyes. [L0666.067]
  3. Search community posts for the policy name to see what others changed before their account was reinstated. [L0666.067]
  4. Contact Google Ads support and request a review of the store against Google's policy requirements before reinstatement. [L0970.120]
  5. Follow up the compliance lesson, make the policy adjustments, then request a review through support with your store details. [L0855.141]

Suspension for misrepresentation (Merchant Center, with appeal button unavailable)

  1. Follow up with the Google contact you are already waiting on. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0803.048]
  2. Re-check the compliance checklist and fix any gaps. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0803.048]
  3. If the appeal button is unavailable in your account, call Microsoft Merchant Center support, which runs 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. Pacific, and ask for a manual review. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0803.048]

Suspension for the circumventing systems policy, first Google Ads account

  1. Do not change the domain or create a new account. [L0857.013]
  2. Request a review from Google. [L0857.013]
  3. State that this is the first Google Ads account for the store or business, and ask for a human to review it. [L0857.013]

Suspension for suspicious payment activity (Google Ads)

  1. Contact Google Ads support through live chat. This is a different team from Merchant Center support. [L0806.067]
  2. Ask for the specific reason for the suspension. [L0806.067]
  3. Check that the billing address on the card matches the address on the account. [L0806.067]
  4. Ask whether anything else must be submitted, or whether the account can be reviewed again. [L0806.067]
  5. Submit the appeal only after this check. [L0806.067]
  6. A suspended account for suspicious payment activity is usually fixed by contacting support, not by hiring an agency. An agency is not needed if the business model has not changed. [L0806.068]

Google Ads suspended, and no reason given

  1. Use live chat with the platform and keep contacting support until a person states the specific reason. (Older advice, 2021.) [L1279.094]
  2. Fix the stated problem, such as an unverified phone number in Merchant Center contact info, and verify it with a PIN sent by call. (Older advice, 2021.) [L1279.094]
  3. Keep following up with the platform until the account is reinstated. (Older advice, 2021.) [L1279.094]
  4. Ask Google for the specific suspension reason, so you can address it. (Older advice, 2022.) [L1281.106]
  5. If an account is suspended without a clear reason, check that shipping and return policies are clear and complete. [L0808.025]
  6. Explain the situation calmly on live chat, describe what other stores do, and ask for the terms to be checked before the restriction stays in place. (Older advice, 2021.) [L1090.106]

  1. A store received a Google Ads disapproval citing a reason that did not apply, stating the ads were illegal in a country where nothing was advertised. The store contacted support with the details and the issue was resolved in a few hours. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0801.070]

Store restricted in a country it does not sell in

Domain may be linked to a suspended ad account

Reactivated Google Ads account where ads are not running

Video Ads and Evidence

  • Before appealing a suspension for alleged policy circumvention, check that each video ad is an original file and contains no nudity or graphic content. Keep this evidence ready to include with the appeal. [L1214.063]

Appeals and Review Timing

Appeal Rules

  • Google allows a limited number of suspension appeals through the review form. Reaching a person through live chat or phone support is a separate route that can be used in addition to appeals. [L0360.049]
  • Google bots crawl stores and can flag a store wrongly, which leads to a back-and-forth until a human reviews it. A suspension is often resolved by getting a human to look at the store rather than by repeating automated appeals. [L0360.050]
  • Reinstatement sometimes happens when you reach the right person at the right time, so keep following up. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0671.072, L0671.209]
  • A Google Ads account and a Merchant Center account can be reinstated separately. A Google Ads account may stay suspended for months after Merchant Center is reinstated. [L0964.059]
  • A Google Ads appeal for an ad that was not in compliance is a valid route; do not run ads before the store has real products and is open for business. (Older advice, 2020.) [L1111.108]
  • After a suspension, first resolve the issues on the existing account before creating a new business account. [L0965.008]

Avoiding New Accounts

Rules Against Opening a Replacement Account

  • Do not start a new Google Ads account while an existing one is suspended. Creating a second account to get around a suspension is treated as circumventing the system and can lead to a permanent block. (Older advice, 2022.) [L1281.104]
  • After a Google Ads account suspension, do not create a new Google Ads account. A new one is usually suspended as well, and Google may cite circumvention of policy. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0963.018]
  • Opening a new ad account right after a suspension can get it blocked or suspended again within minutes. Work through the appeal on the existing account rather than repeatedly creating replacement accounts. [L1214.062]
  • A new account with a different email and card may still be linked if it is opened from the same computer or IP address. [L0965.008]
  • Creating a second Merchant Center account to get around a disapproval causes more problems. Fix the disapproval and request review instead. (Advice from 2023.) [L0964.058]
  • Do not delete the account and create a new one with the same email, because that is flagged as circumventing policies. (Older advice, 2022.) [L1092.072]
  • Do not run a new account with the same card or domain as the suspended one. [L0278.005] (See the conditional route below.)

Conditional New-Account Routes (Conflicting Sources)

Sources disagree on whether a new account is ever a route. Give each route with its conditions:

  • Current route (2025): If Google Ads is suspended, identify the suspension reason first. To open a new account, use a completely different business entity, new email address, new billing information and new domain URL. Include a VPN only if reusing information from the suspended account. [L0278.005]
  • Older route (2019): One practical route was to open a new account with a new domain and a new credit card. The new account could be flagged too, so persistent calls to Google support were still needed, and reinstatement was generally possible when there was no fraud and no unpaid balance. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1069.089]
  • Merchant Center only (2025): Multiple Merchant Center accounts can be created, but this does not solve Google Ads suspension issues first. [L0278.004]
  • Merchant Center only, reported (2021): Reported experience suggests a suspension of one Merchant Center account does not automatically affect separate Merchant Center accounts. This was not verified and should not be relied on. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0469.096]
  • Untrustworthy promotions, Merchant Center (2020): If unresolved after a week, create a new Merchant Center account with a different email. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0268.037]
  • Google accounts are stricter about opening multiple new accounts than Merchant Center. [L0278.004]

Policy Checks Before Any Ad Account Decision

  • If Google Merchant Center asks for business verification such as proof of address or a verified phone number, wait until you have the requested document and upload it. Trying to work around the request can trigger the circumventing systems policy. [L0857.015]
  • If a domain may be linked to a different ad account that was suspended, ask Google Ads support through live chat, describe the earlier suspension, and keep the written answer on file. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0963.021]
  • Only use.com domains for the store. [L0857.012]
  • After a suspension, first resolve the issues on the existing account. [L0965.008]

Reps, Scams and Unsolicited Offers

  • Callers who say they are Google ad representatives and offer paid ad management are often third-party sellers rather than Google. Even genuine Google reps tend to push higher ad spend, so decline such offers and manage the account yourself. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0468.185]
  • Google reps who call or email about account reviews are sales staff who earn money from higher ad spend, so their advice should not be followed blindly. Google's own sales advice is not a reliable guide for account setup. [L0472.017]
  • If the form route gets no answer after an appeal, ask your Google account rep for help, since phone support pointed to a form. Google ad accounts can be suspended without a clear reason, such as an alleged policy circumvention. [L1214.034]
  • When a Facebook account is blocked, a direct contact at the platform can help recover funds or restore access. Keep an agency or platform rep contact available for these cases. [L1214.035]
  • Be skeptical of agencies offering negative keyword management services, since such services often do not involve the owner's own oversight of campaigns. Hands-on management of campaigns in early stages is a better approach. [L0471.029]
  • If any Google account shows errors or problems, contact the platform support or the community for help instead of making ad hoc changes. Report the error so it can be resolved before the learning phase ends. [L1393.009]
  • Keep requesting live chat with Google through the Ad support center whenever that option appears, and do not give up on the appeal. [L0471.031]

Agencies and Outsourcing

When Not to Outsource

  • Do not outsource Google Ads until the store is already profitable with your own optimization. Only hire for management or scaling once you have proven what works. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0270.025]
  • Do not outsource Google Ads management until the store has a track record of profitable ads and known winning products. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0834.071]
  • Before raising ad spend, verify the account is profitable. Outsourcing ads to an agency is not recommended until the store is profitable and the baseline performance is known, since agencies rarely take a brand-new store. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0854.056]
  • Do not outsource Google Ads early. First prove a baseline on your own account, such as a store live for six months with about 13X return on ad spend over the past four months, before hiring an agency. (Advice from 2023.) [L0934.064]

  • Do not start ads before the store has real products and is open for business. (Older advice, 2020.) [L1111.108]

  • Google Ads agencies typically charge 1,500 to 2,500 dollars monthly and manage one to ten accounts per person, making per-account costs higher than larger platforms. [L0217.017]
  • Google Ads agencies charge low fees (1,500 to 2,500 dollars monthly) because one person manages 1 to 10 accounts simultaneously. The cost of labor is amortized across multiple clients. In-house requires paying for one full-time person dedicated to your account only. [L0217.041]
  • Google Ads agencies tend to be maintenance-focused rather than proactively scaling because the platform requires more campaign setup work per testing cycle. Clarify scaling expectations upfront and map deliverables week by week. [L0217.031]
  • Google Ads return on ad spend is claimed in the source to be strong, around 4x to 7x in typical cases. Outsourced Google Ads management in the source cost about 1,500 dollars per month. [L0637.041]
  • A good agency brief sets a measurable goal: keep a 13X return on ad spend while raising monthly spend from 1,000 to 2,000 dollars, or raise return from 13X to 15X at the same spend. Without a baseline an agency has nothing to be judged against. [L0934.132]
  • Judge the performance of an outsourced Google Ads manager by ROAS trend, not spend alone. A flat or weak ROAS at a relatively small ad budget should be improved before the budget is raised. (Older advice, 2020.) [L1111.110]
  • When an agency is paid a fixed monthly fee, check whether the ROAS justifies it. A 10x ROAS on 2,000 dollars monthly spend was judged acceptable for a new account, but needs to improve as spend grows. (Older advice, 2020.) [L1111.111]

Further details

Handling a Google Merchant Center suspension:

  1. Read the email from Google, which usually names a broad reason for the suspension.
  2. Send that reason and a link to the store to support for a review.
  3. Look for a clear pattern in the flagged stores before changing anything. [L1088.048]
