10.3 Pre-Launch compliance and store readiness

Why Compliance Comes First

  • Google's automated crawlers, not human reviewers, scan the site and flag missing or incomplete elements. Make the store fully compliant before creating the Google Merchant Center and Google Ads accounts. Fixing these issues first helps avoid suspensions, review loops and back-and-forth with Google. [L0167.001, L0724.001]
  • The crawler checks the store before Merchant Center and Google Ads accounts are approved, so the site must be complete, error-free and free of placeholders at submission. [L0167.001, L0724.001]
  • Google crawls the site during setup. Do not proceed if the store has demo products, policies with placeholder text, or any broken links or pages. [L0181.002]

Compliance Checklist Sequence

Work in this order before any account or campaign is created:

  1. Add every required policy and store element to the site. [L0665.131]
  2. Confirm the checklist is complete. [L0665.131]
  3. Only then set up and launch the campaigns. This reduces the risk of a Merchant Center or account suspension. [L0665.131]

Timing rules that apply to the checklist:

  • Complete the Compliance Checklist lesson before creating a Google Merchant Center account. [L0181.002]
  • Before setting up Google Merchant Center, Google Ads or a Facebook account, complete the compliance checklist. Following it reduces the chance that the store gets flagged for misrepresentation. (2022, older) [L0803.047]
  • Run through the store compliance checklist before submitting products to Google Merchant Center. It covers the required pages and elements to add to the store. (2023) [L0934.071]
  • Do not start paid traffic (Shopping, Facebook) before the compliance checklist is completed. (2022, older) [L0969.103]
  • The checklist is Module 6, Lesson 6.0.2, "Compliance Checklist", which outlines all requirements to complete before turning on Google Ads live. Follow it to reduce the risk of account suspension. (2022, older) [L0272.035]
  • Complete the compliance checklist from the traffic module before launching Google Ads. Run Google Ads alone until sales come in before adding other traffic sources. (2023) [L0804.077]

Required Store Elements

Contact Page

  • The contact page must offer at least three ways to reach you: an email address, a contact form and a phone number. (2021, older) [L0671.068]
  • Keep the prices, contact details and page content on your site consistent with your ads. Do not let them disagree. Inconsistencies between ads and webpages are a common cause of account reviews. (2021, older) [L0671.071, L0671.208]

Policies

  • Shopify's policy generators insert placeholder text into refund, privacy, terms and shipping policies. Google treats leftover placeholders as a sign of an incomplete store. Read each generated policy line by line and replace every placeholder before submission. [L0724.025]
  • Merchant Center policy details, such as return and privacy policies, can start with the default text provided by the store and be edited later. The store and Merchant Center must eventually match. (2020, older) [L0664.062]
  • Before submission, the policies must be final, not default text. [L0724.001, L0724.025, L0181.002]
  • Review the compliance section several times before launch. Merchant Center suspensions can come from broken links on the store, filler text such as lorem ipsum, a missing shipping or return policy, or policies entered in Merchant Center that do not match the site. [L0966.083]
  • Check that all legal pages are in place and that shipping times are stated correctly before starting Google Ads. New merchant accounts are often suspended for missing legal pages or incorrect shipping times. (Source C) [L0788.024]

Policy requirements at a glance:

Policy or element Requirement Source
Privacy policy Must disclose that the store uses retargeting. A policy that omits retargeting does not meet the Google Ads requirement. L0421.005
Shipping policy Must state the exact shipping cost, so customers know what they will pay before checkout. If the header or site promises free shipping, the shipping policy must also state free shipping. L0724.021
Shipping policy, freight Freight charges must be disclosed, even if competitors do not show them. L0360.047
Return policy Place a summary table at the top. Google crawlers can pull shipping and return data easily from tables, so it helps Merchant Center read the terms. Use clear, specific language. Vague wording commonly triggers Merchant Center flags. L0724.020
Refund, privacy, terms, shipping Every placeholder replaced. L0724.025
All legal pages In place before Google Ads starts. L0788.024

Shipping, Prices and Checkout Consistency

  • Shipping fees, business name, phone number and tax information in Merchant Center must exactly match the store's website and Shopify settings. Google's bots test the checkout. A mismatch, such as a store charging paid shipping while Merchant Center shows free shipping, leads to suspension. (2021-22, older) [L0727.003]
  • Do not mark products as free shipping on Google Shopping while shipping is charged at checkout. This is a compliance problem. (2024) [L0360.047]
  • Website prices must match the prices submitted to Google Merchant Center exactly. A mismatch is one of the most common causes of a suspension. (2021, older) [L0671.067, L0671.204]
  • If prices, taxes or shipping shown at checkout differ from those in the ads and feed, Google can suspend the entire product feed. Google compares these during checkout. (2021-22, older) [L1170.039]
  • Set up Shopify Markets per country so checkout runs in each buyer's local currency. A shopper from the UK or Australia will not easily buy when prices show in USD. (Source C) [L1315.001]
  • Create shipping profiles per country, or one international profile with free tracked shipping. (Source C) [L1315.001]
  • Showing estimated delivery days in the shipping text is optional, and operators are split on it. It works for some stores and not for others, so test it rather than assume. (Source C) [L1315.002]

Demo Products, Dummy Stores and Broken Links

  • Do not start the Merchant Center setup until the store's compliance requirements are fixed, including removing demo products. Unresolved compliance issues will cause problems with the account. [L0729.003]
  • Do not link Google Ads or other ad accounts while the store has only dummy products, because it can hurt account approval. Add real products first. (2021, older) [L0968.063]
  • Do not proceed if the store has demo products, placeholder policies or broken links or pages. [L0181.002]

Business Information and Addresses

  • Do not hide business information, such as an address, with text that matches the background colour. Google can treat this as misrepresentation, which can lead to account bans. (2026) [L0808.027]
  • Some sellers report that Google flags virtual mailbox addresses as business addresses in Merchant Center. This is anecdotal and not confirmed, so check your Merchant Center before relying on a virtual address. (2026) [L0808.028]

Reviews and Ratings

  • Enabling customer reviews and product ratings in Google Merchant Center is fine when the reviews come from Judge.me, which transfers them automatically. (2022, older) [L0429.059]
  • Do not use reviews copied from other websites on a new store. Google can detect this, and it can lead to account problems. (2021, older) [L0671.070, L0671.207]
  • Reviews without dates: a review without a date is still impactful for the product types sold, but showing dates can help customers. (2023) [L0964.070]
  • Google Trusted Store badge:
    • It requires Google user ratings, about 50 reviews according to the lesson.
    • It must be requested from Google.
    • Reviews alone are not enough. (2023) [L0470.035]

Live Store, Products and Stock

  • Do not set up Google Ads or other paid ad accounts until live products are on the store. Running ads before that is a red flag for Google. (2023) [L1094.037]
  • Do not run ads on a store that is not live. Merchant Center accounts and ads are set up only after real products are on the store and it is ready to take orders. (2020, older) [L1111.109]
  • A site that does not take orders is not eligible for Google Shopping. The product feed links to product pages where a customer can check out, and approval would not last. (2021, older) [L0801.058]
  • Google Shopping ads must link to live product pages that are in stock. Pause a product when stock is short to prevent wasted spend and poor customer experience. (2020, older) [L1111.114]

Products That Are Not Ready

Keep products that are not ready out of the ads and store:

  1. Open the product in Shopify.
  2. Change its status to draft and save. It will not show in the store or in the ads.
  3. When images and descriptions are complete, set the status to active and save. It is then pushed to the Google ad account. [L0857.016]
  • Products that are ready for sale but not fully optimised can stay in the shopping campaigns. [L0857.017]
  • Products missing images or descriptions should be set to draft. [L0857.017]

Launch Structure for Large Supplier Sets

  • If many suppliers are approved, upload all their products. Set up alpha and beta with all products included once the product pages are complete. Turn ads on after the products are ready. (2025) [L0972.062]

Supplier and Product Thresholds Before Paid Traffic

Sources give different supplier minimums. Use the values below and apply the most recent one as the floor.

Source Year Minimum before paid Google ads Notes Id
Source A 2024 At least two supplier approvals with uploaded products Keep uploading in the background afterwards. L0856.030
Source A 2022 At least two suppliers approved and their products uploaded Keep getting approvals with other suppliers in the background. (older) L0963.022
Source A 2020 At least two suppliers on the site After the first campaign runs, use time to get more suppliers approved and add their products. (older) L0967.080
Source A 2021 One or two approved suppliers can start Do not start with a supplier count below the target. Keep getting approvals with the remaining suppliers in the niche. (older) L0962.064
  • Read together, the 2021 source allows a start with one or two suppliers, while the 2020, 2022 and 2024 sources set two as the minimum. Treat two as the floor and the target supplier count as the goal. [L0856.030, L0963.022, L0967.080, L0962.064]
  • Ads should start once at least two suppliers are on the site. [L0967.080]
  • Product count: have roughly 20 products uploaded to the store before enabling Google Shopping ads. (2021, older) [L1112.005]
  • Both thresholds were stated in the sources. Apply the supplier minimum and the product count together. [L0856.030, L1112.005]

One Store, One Google Ads Account

  • Each unrelated store needs its own Google Ads account, registered with a sales email address on that store's domain and a separate phone number. (2025) [L0837.064]
  • Stores that can cross-promote to each other's customers, such as a bounce house store and a trampoline store, can share one account. (2025) [L0837.064]

Prohibited Products and Restricted Categories

  • Google Merchant Center prohibits certain product categories from Google Shopping, including adult products, weapons, vehicles and smoking-related implements. Listing a prohibited product usually leads to suspension, and recovery chances are very low. (2021-22, older) [L0727.007]
  • Check a niche against Google's prohibited products list before building the store and campaigns. A product category on that list can get the Google Ads account suspended even when the business is otherwise legitimate. (2019, older) [L1069.088]
  • Google Ads does not allow advertising for some product categories. A niche in a prohibited category cannot run paid Google ads. (2019, older) [L0467.074]
  • If the core products of a niche cannot be advertised on Google, such as paintball guns, the niche is a strikeout because Google Shopping cannot be used to sell them. Other products in the same niche do not change this result. (2021-22, older) [L0490.008]
  • For restricted or specialty product categories, check Google's policy and enable the extra restriction boxes in the Google Ads setup. These limit which audiences see the ads. (2021, older) [L0247.055]
  • Google's policy on pop-ups and large sticky ads targets elements that block a visitor from using the page. A sticky top announcement bar or homepage banner that does not block content was not expected to break this rule. (2022, older) [L1281.108]

Information Products

  • Information products such as courses can only be advertised through Google Shopping and product listing ads if a physical item is shipped with them, for example a workbook. Without a shipped item, the ad account risks being suspended. (2019, older) [L1002.015]

Health and Diet Products

  • Use factual product attributes, such as low-calorie, gluten-free and seed-oil-free, and lifestyle benefits, such as macro-friendly, diet-friendly and supports low-sugar diets. Avoid absolute health claims or medical assertions. (Source E 2025-26) [L0220.028]
  • Be transparent about ingredient variations. For example, some product flavours may use sucralose while others are naturally sweetened. (Source E 2025-26) [L0220.028]
  • Google's health policies are strict. Ads making unsupported health claims will be disapproved or suspended. (Source E 2025-26) [L0220.028]

Organic Fallback

  • Plan organic traffic from SEO and content from the start. A store can grow through organic search even after losing ad access. (2019, older) [L0467.074]

Ad Consistency

  • Do not let the price, contact details or page content on your site disagree with your ads. (2021, older) [L0671.071, L0671.208]
  • Merchant Center and the store must eventually match on policies and business details. [L0664.062, L0727.003]

Readiness Before Any Ad Account Is Opened

Complete these steps in order before creating a Google Merchant Center, Google Ads or other paid ad account:

  1. Complete the compliance checklist, including every required page and store element. [L0181.002, L0803.047, L0665.131]
  2. Make the store live with real products and the ability to take orders. [L1094.037, L1111.109, L0801.058]
  3. Remove demo products, dummy data and placeholder text, and fix broken links. [L0729.003, L0968.063, L0724.025, L0966.083]
  4. Publish the contact page with at least three contact methods. [L0671.068]
  5. Publish the privacy policy with retargeting disclosed, and the shipping and return policies with exact terms. [L0421.005, L0724.021, L0724.020]
  6. Confirm that prices, shipping fees, tax information and business details match in the store, Shopify and Merchant Center. [L0671.067, L0727.003, L1170.039]
  7. Confirm that the niche and its core products are not prohibited or restricted, and enable the extra restriction boxes where required. [L0727.007, L1069.088, L0490.008, L0247.055]
  8. Confirm the supplier count and product count thresholds are met. [L0856.030, L1112.005]
  9. Set products that are not ready to draft, and keep only in-stock, live product pages in the feed. [L0857.016, L0857.017, L1111.114]
  10. Link Google Ads only after the store is ready, and run Google Ads alone until sales come in before adding other traffic sources. [L0968.063, L0804.077]
