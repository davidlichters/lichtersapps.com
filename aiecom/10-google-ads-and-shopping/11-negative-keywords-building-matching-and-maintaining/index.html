10.11 Negative keywords: building, matching and maintaining

Negative Keywords: Building, Matching and Maintaining

Why Negatives Matter

  • Negative keywords stop Google from showing your ads for irrelevant searches, which shifts budget away from low-converting traffic toward profitable keywords over time. [L0193.009]
  • Negative keywords stop Google from spending budget on the queries you add, so over the first month or two a Shopping campaign becomes more focused and more spend goes to queries that make sales. Starting with a small budget helps you find non-revenue queries sooner. [L0750.028]
  • Roughly 20% of the search terms made on the Google search network each day are unique, meaning they have never been searched before. They cannot be predicted in advance and can only be handled by exclusion. [L1074.002]
  • Negative keyword lists substantially reduce the time needed to manage Google Ads campaigns compared with managing exclusions without them, and make it more likely that campaigns run as intended. [L1074.007]
  • Google matches products to search terms by reading the product feed in its own way. In the first one to three weeks, many search terms that no buyer would use may appear and need negatives. [L0715.007]
  • Google builds Shopping keyword matches automatically from the product feed. It sometimes matches searches unrelated to the store or containing brand names you do not sell, so check the Search terms report regularly and add those queries as negatives. [L0750.027]
  • Google chooses which products to show for generic searches based on feed data, including title, SKU, description and keywords. To reduce impressions for a product, add negative keywords or lower its CPC at product level. [L0803.043]
  • Many advertisers never review the Search terms report, so they do not add new negatives or exclude non-converting terms. The report is where most wasted spend shows up, so check it regularly. [L0876.017]
  • The Search terms report can reveal wasteful searches to exclude, winning searches to scale, comparison or "X versus Y" queries that suggest new keywords, and the split between brand and non-brand searches. Accounts with real spend produce hundreds or thousands of rows. [L0876.016]

Shopping search terms and Gmail or YouTube:

  • Shopping ads that also show on Gmail or YouTube can trigger search terms through audiences. To see clean search term data, set the ads to appear only in Google Shopping results. [L0247.064]

Shared Library and Negative Keyword Lists

  • Negative keywords can be added to a single ad group, to a single campaign, or to a shared negative keyword list. A shared list is the only option that applies one entry to several campaigns at once. [L0744.028]
  • Negative keyword lists are reused across campaigns from the Shared Library. It holds negative keyword lists alongside bid strategies, shared budgets, the audience manager, location groups and placement exclusion lists. [L1074.008]
  • Negative keyword lists are found under Tools and Settings > Shared library > Negative keyword lists. Select the list, choose Apply to campaigns, tick the target campaigns and apply. [L0742.027]
  • To remove a wrong assignment, open the list, go to the campaign list, tick the campaign and choose remove list from selected campaign. [L0742.027]
  • Tools and Settings > Shared Library > Negative keyword lists opens the list overview. It shows each list with its keyword count and number of campaigns, and the plus button creates a new list. [L1074.009]
  • The new list dialog has the fields List name and Add negative keywords, with the hint "Enter or paste your negative keywords, one per line", plus SAVE and CANCEL buttons. A list cannot be saved until it contains at least one negative keyword. [L1074.010]
  • Inside a negative keyword list, the APPLY TO CAMPAIGNS button attaches the list to selected campaigns. The detail page offers DOWNLOAD, EXPAND and ADD FILTER options. Each keyword in the detail view has a Match type column. [L1074.013]
  • The Master Generic Negative Keywords list is meant to grow over time into the largest negative keyword list in the account. It holds every irrelevant search term that your ads should never show for. [L1074.014]
  • Create the negative keyword lists before building a Shopping campaign so they are ready to apply at the right moment. Creating a list does not require applying it to a campaign at that point. [L1074.012]

Setup procedure for the three lists:

  1. Open Tools and Settings in Google Ads. [L1074.011]
  2. Under Shared Library, click Negative keyword lists. [L1074.011]
  3. Click the plus button to create a new list. [L1074.011]
  4. Enter the list name Master Generic Negative Keywords. [L1074.011]
  5. Paste the negative keywords from a ready-made generic starter list into the Add negative keywords field, one per line. [L1074.011]
  6. Click SAVE. The list cannot be saved without at least one keyword. [L1074.011]
  7. Create a new list named Brand Names. [L1074.011]
  8. Enter the names of the brands you sell through your website, using just the brand name, one per line. [L1074.011]
  9. Click SAVE. [L1074.011]
  10. Create a new list named Product IDs, product identifiers or product names. [L1074.011]
  11. Enter the unique identifiers of your products, such as model numbers and model names, exactly as they appear in your product titles or in the manufacturer or supplier documentation, one per line. [L1074.011]
  12. Click SAVE. [L1074.011]
  13. Do not apply the lists to any campaign yet. They are applied when the Shopping campaign is set up. [L1074.011]

Adding Negatives in Shopping Campaigns

Steps for adding negatives in Shopping campaigns:

  1. Log in to Google Ads and click Campaigns in the left navigation, then Campaigns in the dropdown. [L0750.011]
  2. Use the toggle to select Shopping campaigns (the options shown depend on your campaign types). [L0750.011]
  3. Open the beta campaign first, then repeat the same steps for the alpha campaign. [L0750.011]
  4. Do not use the Keywords section for this. It only shows negative keywords already added. [L0750.011, L0750.012]
  5. Expand Insights and reports at the top and click Search terms, checking the beta campaign one at a time. [L0750.011]
  6. Review the list of search terms the ads appeared for, and check off the ones to exclude. [L0750.011]
  7. Click Add to negative keywords. The added terms then appear in the negative keywords list in the Keywords section. [L0750.011]
  8. Switch to the alpha campaign in the Search terms report and add only universal negatives there (see the alpha and beta rules). [L0750.011]

Negative keywords are added in the campaign under Keywords in the sidebar, using the Negative keywords section and its plus button. [L0746.026]

Source A 2025 (Beta location): open the Beta campaign, expand "Audiences, keywords and content" in the sidebar, click "Keywords", then "Negative keywords", and add them to the campaign (Beta Source A merch) rather than to an ad group. [L0743.022]

Entering negatives:

  • Enter negative keywords directly in Google Ads line by line. A spreadsheet is not needed. [L0972.057]
  • Alpha and beta campaigns each need their own negative keyword list. [L0972.057]

Multi-Campaign Setups (High, Medium and Low Priority)

  • Negative keyword work is done in the high priority campaign only, because Google checks that campaign first. If the setup is correct, the medium and low priority campaigns should not show unwanted terms. [L0744.023]
  • A negative keyword added to only one campaign pushes the term into the other campaigns, where it costs more per click without a better chance of conversion. Every negative added at the high priority level must also apply to the other two campaigns. [L0744.026]
  • Add negative keywords to the shared master generic negative keyword list, which applies to all three campaigns at once. This avoids missed campaigns and copy errors when lists are long. [L0744.027]
  • Decide whether to review search terms per campaign or per ad group based on how many brands and product types the store carries. A store with many brands and product types usually needs ad group level review, while a narrow store can be reviewed at campaign level. [L0744.061]

Campaign Moves and Keyword Placement

  • Do not move keywords between campaigns. To keep a term out of a campaign, add it there as a negative keyword. [L0671.012, L0671.290]
  • Example: a main niche term such as pool table is added as a keyword in beta and as a negative keyword in alpha, so alpha does not spend on it. [L0671.013]

Master Negative List

  • Add negative keywords to a master list that applies to all new campaigns. Example list of universal terms that never convert: secondhand, used, wholesale, local, job, training, advice, agency, and similar terms. [L0225.026]
  • Merchant Center is used for universal negatives during cleanup of a campaign with no sales. [L0807.024]

Universal Negatives

What Qualifies

  • Universal negatives are terms that should never trigger an ad, such as unrelated products or topics. They belong in both Alpha and Beta. Brand, SKU and product-name negatives belong only in Beta, because Alpha has to be able to show for those searches. [L0746.009]
  • Universal negative keywords are terms for other brands you do not sell, and for products or niches unrelated to your store. Add them as negatives in both the alpha and the beta campaign. [L0750.014]
  • Universal negatives are only for keywords that never lead to a sale on your store, for example an unrelated product term. Do not use universal negatives for competitor brand names that buyers might search with your product name. [L0804.072]
  • Add terms you never want to appear for to the universal negative keyword list, so they block across all campaigns. [L0745.022]
  • Example: a chandelier store that shows for tanning beds should block tanning beds in both campaigns so no money is spent on it. [L0746.010]
  • If a search term shows another brand you do not carry, add it as a universal negative keyword. Do not exclude generic brand names that could be confused with your own products. [L0276.016]

Starting List and Timing

  • Start with a universal list of negative keywords, plus your brand names and SKU numbers. Then review the keyword reports for the active campaigns every two to three days and add any unwanted search terms as negatives. [L0941.062]
  • Add universal negative keywords to Alpha and Beta campaigns right away, before data comes in. [L0855.009]
  • Universal negative keywords should be added to both alpha and beta campaigns from day one. New irrelevant search terms that appear later should be added as you find them. [L0970.112]
  • Add universal negative keywords as irrelevant terms appear in ad reports. Start with a published list, then keep extending it through regular audits. (Source A, 2021, older.) [L0853.073]
  • Add generic words such as "free", "cheap" and "local" as universal negative keywords on both alpha and beta campaigns. Broader niche terms should only be blocked as exact-match negatives. (Source A, 2022, older.) [L0854.049]
  • Add negative keywords by applying the same universal negatives and campaign-specific negatives from the product feed submission lesson, and update them based on the search terms that bring irrelevant clicks. [L0856.020]

Sources disagree on how much to add at the start. Read together: add the obviously irrelevant universal terms from day one, and hold back sweeping broad terms until search term data exists. [L0855.009, L0970.112, L0274.024, L0972.058]

Early-phase advice that conflicts with the "add now" rules above:

  • Be selective early in a campaign. If traffic is too new to tell what will convert, only block obviously irrelevant keywords. Broad negatives early risk blocking future sales. [L0274.024]
  • Start with a few basic negative keywords, and add more as the search terms that trigger clicks become clear. Adding too many negatives at the start limits reach. [L0972.058]
  • Do not add many negative keywords at the start. Begin with basic terms such as free and cheap, then add more negatives from actual search terms that waste money. (Source A, 2022, older.) [L0969.062]
  • Start with broad keyword coverage and add negative keywords over time, based on search terms that do not match the products. Do not limit the keyword list at the start, because more impressions give more data. (Source A, 2020, older.) [L0468.113]

Match Types

Match Type Reference

Match type Syntax What it blocks Typical use Sources
Broad term, no symbols Searches containing the term; see the conflict below on single generic words Universal terms irrelevant to every product (e.g. free, retail); brand, product and SKU list in Beta [L0746.011, L0961.058, L0743.023]
Phrase "term" Every variation containing the word or phrase Brand names; product-type phrases you never want; long competitor-style product titles in Beta [L0225.004, L0270.016, L0961.058, L0225.029, L0805.014]
Exact [term] Only that exact phrase Specific terms that must be blocked exactly; generic words that must not block product-name searches; suppliers; broad niche keywords taking budget [L0225.004, L0962.058, L0837.012, L0854.050, L0857.001]

Syntax check: exact match negatives are typed in square brackets, for example [competitor name], and phrase match negatives in quotation marks, for example "competitor name". [L0804.071]

Other match-type rules:

  • Negatives in Google Shopping work as broad match, so every variation containing the negative term is blocked, not only the exact phrase. One negative such as course or videos covers many related queries. [L0746.011]
  • Negative keyword match types: broad match blocks related searches, while phrase and exact match are narrower and better suited for most negatives. [L0966.087]
  • Choose the match type for a negative keyword by how closely the term matches the products you want to keep showing. Broad and phrase negatives also block longer searches that contain the term, so a generic word like chandeliers can be added as an exact match negative if specific product-name searches must still appear. [L0626.007]
  • Choose negative keyword match type per keyword. Use exact match for a single term that should always be blocked, and broad match to block variations. In practice, a mix of match types is common. (Source A, 2022, older.) [L0803.038]
  • Check the match type before you add any negative keyword. [L0626.009]
  • Use exact match negatives for generic terms that you want to block. [L0966.076]
  • Exact match negative keyword: enter the term in square brackets in the negative keywords list, for example [table], so only that exact search is blocked. [L0966.077]
  • Negative keywords do not automatically cover close variants, plurals or misspellings the way positive keywords do. Add each variant, such as plurals and misspellings of a blocked word, as its own negative. [L0876.027]
  • Positive match types for search text ads differ from the negative keyword match types used in Shopping campaigns. Do not transfer the negative keyword rules to search text ads. (Source B, 2021-22, older.) [L0880.015]

Broad Match Pitfalls

  • A broad match negative keyword blocks every search that contains that word, including specific product searches such as dining table with model numbers. Use exact match negatives for generic terms that you want to block. [L0966.076]
  • Broad match negatives also block searches that contain the term with extra words before or after it. Use exact match negatives (wrapped in brackets) only when a specific term must be blocked exactly, and not for the brand, product, and SKU list. [L0743.024]
  • A broad generic word such as sinks should not be added to alpha or beta manual campaigns. Broad matching would show ads for any search containing that word, so use exact match only if clicks justify it. (Source A, 2022, older.) [L0963.014]
  • Negative keywords set as broad match block long-tail variants. Blocking broad "standing desk" also blocks "model 123 standing desk", because the system matches the partial phrase. Use exact or phrase match instead. (Source A, 2022, older.) [L0249.095]
  • Do not add negative keywords using broad match. Use exact match to avoid accidentally blocking related keywords. (Source A, 2022, older.) [L0249.057]
  • Add negative keywords as exact match by default, because phrase or broad match negatives can block traffic you want. Example: a broad negative "generators" would also block "brand name generators". Use phrase or broad match only for terms you never want to appear for. (Source A, 2025.) [L0837.012]

Sources disagree on the broad-match default for brand and product lists:

Source Advice Applies when
Source A, 2025 The brand list for Beta is entered as broad match, so searches for these terms are blocked in Beta and handled by Alpha. [L0743.023] Brand, product and SKU list in the Beta campaign.
Source A, 2025 Brand names are added as negatives; the default matching type is broad, which is correct for this strategy. Exact match negatives are optional for later optimization and not required for initial setup. [L0186.009] Initial Beta setup for high-intent searches.
Source A, 2022 (older) Broad negatives block long-tail keywords; use exact or phrase match. [L0249.095, L0249.057] Generic keywords where related long-tail searches must still show.

Read together: broad match suits the brand, product and SKU list in Beta, while exact or phrase match suits generic or niche terms that must not block related product searches. [L0743.023, L0186.009, L0249.095, L0837.012]

Single Generic Words

Sources disagree on how a single generic word behaves:

Source Behaviour described Applies when
Source A, 2021 (older) Single-word negatives use broad match, which blocks any search containing that word. [L0270.016] Single words such as "free" that should be blocked everywhere.
Source A, 2025 Broad match blocks every variation containing the term. [L0746.011] Negatives in Shopping campaigns.
Source B, 2021-22 (older) Adding a single generic word such as sauna as a negative blocks only that word on its own, not longer search phrases that contain it. Use it when the word alone attracts irrelevant searches. [L0744.033] The word alone attracts irrelevant searches.

Test the behaviour in your own account before relying on either version. [L0274.062, L0744.031]

Match Type Examples

  • Exact match example: [infrared sauna portable] blocks only that exact term but allows "infrared sauna portable for cats". Using just [portable] blocks only the word portable and still allows "portable infrared sauna". Use phrase match "portable" to block all variations containing portable. [L0225.005]
  • Exact match example: a negative exact match for blue pool does not block searches for blue pool q. [L0962.058]
  • Negative exact match keywords in brackets block only that exact phrase. Negative phrase match keywords in quotes block every search variation containing that word or phrase, which makes them useful for blocking entire categories like "portable". [L0225.004]
  • For single-word negative keywords, such as free, use broad match (no quotes or brackets) to block any search containing that word. For brand name negatives, use phrase match (quotes) to block exact brand plus variations. For exact blocking, use exact match (brackets) when only one specific phrase must be blocked. (Source A, 2021, older.) [L0270.016]
  • Use broad match for universal negative keywords that are irrelevant to every product, such as free or retail. Use phrase match for brand names and for product-type phrases you do not want, such as an unrelated product term. (Source A, 2020, older.) [L0961.058]
  • Use a broad negative only when the term is irrelevant to every product you sell. Exact match suits any term that could still lead to a sale on a specific product. [L0626.007, L0837.012]

Over-Blocking Risks

  • Double-check the negative keyword list early on, because over-blocking can cut off buyers who search for specific product names. [L0966.078]
  • Do not add a broad negative that blocks a model, product or buying term. Blocking a broad use-case term can also block buyers who search for the same product type. [L0665.120]
  • Add a use-case search term as a negative keyword only when it clearly signals no purchase intent, such as a treadmill search for weight loss. Never block model numbers, brand names or clear buying terms. [L0665.120]
  • Adding a generic keyword as a broad or phrase negative can block the specific product-name searches that convert best. [L0626.009]
  • Ingredient and feature terms that describe your selling points must not be negated, even when an AI lists them as irrelevant. Check the negative list against your full product range and selling propositions. [L0876.036]
  • Some terms that look irrelevant can be real top-of-funnel or B2B demand, such as "how to" or recipe queries, or "wholesale" searches when the brand sells wholesale. Put these in a review list rather than negating them automatically. [L0876.035]
  • Never negate terms that could come from real buyers. When uncertain, place the term in a separate "Review before adding" list rather than the main list. [L0876.031]
  • A negative keyword added by mistake will quietly block real buyers. Read every term in an AI-generated list before adding it to the account, and treat AI suggestions as a head start rather than a final list. [L0876.037]
  • Uploading a large list of negative keywords generated by ChatGPT can block searches you want, such as relevant customer queries. Check each proposed negative keyword against your target customers before adding it. (Source A, 2024, older.) [L0471.028]
  • Negative keywords reduce spend on low-intent searches, but a list built too quickly can limit reach. Build negatives gradually. [L0972.058, L0471.027]
  • Do not block a search term only because it has no clicks yet. Block it when it is not relevant to what you sell, whether or not it is getting clicks. [L0748.011]
  • Do not block a term only because it has no clicks yet, but do not let it wait when it is clearly irrelevant. Waiting before cutting them wastes impressions on unrelated searches. (Source A, 2020, older.) [L0551.079]

Testing for Over-Blocking

Test whether broad negatives are blocking profitable traffic:

  1. Copy all universal negative keywords to a Google Doc. [L0274.062]
  2. Remove most negatives from the campaign (keep only your core niche keyword). [L0274.062]
  3. Run the campaign for one week. [L0274.062]
  4. If results improve, add back only the obviously irrelevant negatives. [L0274.062]
  5. If results worsen, restore all negatives. [L0274.062]

This determines whether negatives are too restrictive. [L0274.062]

Competitor Names

  • Do not block competitor names as negative keywords by default. Let search term data show which terms lose money, and add those as negatives. (Source A, 2021, older.) [L0247.066]
  • Start with exact match on competitor brand names rather than blocking them fully. [L0804.071]
  • Long competitor-style product titles that trigger the ad can be added as negative keywords, entered as phrase match negatives in quotation marks ("term") so that exact searches are blocked and pushed into the other campaign. Source A, 2024, Beta campaign. [L0805.014]
  • If search terms show a competitor that sells direct to consumers and whose products you do not carry, add that brand as a broad negative keyword so you stop appearing for it. [L0837.014]
  • Competitor brand names belong in the negative list only for prospecting campaigns that should not target competitors. Flag them for review because they depend on the category. [L0876.033]

Own Brand and Product Names

  • In the beta campaign, add your own brand names, product names and SKU numbers as negative keywords, so that this traffic is pushed to the alpha campaign. Alpha is meant to show for those brand, product and SKU terms, so never add them as negatives there. [L0750.013]
  • Add branded product names and specific SKUs as negative phrase or exact match keywords in your beta campaign so they automatically funnel to your alpha campaign. For example, if "Lifestyles" is your brand, add negative phrase "lifestyles" to beta to block all related searches. [L0225.029]
  • For Beta campaigns, add brand names you sell, product names you sell, and SKU numbers as negative keywords to prevent your ads from appearing for your own products. [L0193.007]
  • Beta campaign negative list: add all brand names you are authorized to sell (e.g. the five authorized brands), all product names from the store, and all SKU numbers, entered as broad match. Searches for these terms are blocked in Beta and handled by Alpha. Update the list over time as new brands are added. [L0743.023]
  • Brand names for the Beta list are entered in the Brand Names list, using just the brand name, one per line. [L1074.011]
  • Block brand names in the beta campaign, vague short-tail keywords and universal keywords; set up match types (broad, phrase, exact) later based on data. (Source A, 2022, older.) [L0969.063]

Sources disagree on own-brand negatives in Alpha:

Source Advice Applies when
Source A, 2020 (older) Add your brand name as a phrase-match negative in the alpha campaign, together with closely related but different product variants. [L0961.091] Read as the older view; not the current rule.
Source A, 2025 Never add brand, product or SKU terms as negatives in alpha, because alpha has to be able to show for them. [L0750.013, L0746.009] Current rule for Alpha.

Own-brand negatives in a campaign that carries the brand:

  • In a campaign with a brand term, do not add your own brand to the negative keyword list if you carry that brand. Negative keywords should block only terms for product types you do not sell, such as commercial, which can be added as a universal negative. (Source A, 2022, older.) [L0429.049]
  • Use this rule for the brand campaign; the Beta rules above apply to the Beta structure. [L0429.049, L0743.023]

Suppliers and Other Brands

  • Do not add search terms for suppliers you don't carry as negative keywords from day one. Add them only after search term reports show consistent spend without sales, using exact match, e.g. a supplier's brand name only. (Source A, 2022, older.) [L0854.050]
  • If a search term shows another brand you do not carry, add it as a universal negative keyword. [L0276.016]
  • Universal negative keywords are terms for other brands you do not sell. [L0750.014]

Building Negatives from the Search Terms Report

Where to Find the Report

  • To find search terms triggering your ads in Google Ads: navigate to Campaigns → Select your campaign → Insights & Reports → Search Terms. [L0193.006]
  • The Search terms report is found inside a campaign under Insights and reports > Search terms. It shows the actual phrases people typed that triggered the ads, with metrics for each. Use Add filter on the Search term column to narrow the list. [L0876.015]
  • To see which searches triggered your ads, open the campaign in Google Ads, expand "Insights and reports", and select the "Search terms" tab. Review each term for relevance and add irrelevant ones as negative keywords. (Source A, 2025.) [L0837.011]
  • To see which search terms triggered Google Shopping ads: open the Shopping campaign, click Insights and reports in the left navigation and then click Search terms. It shows the terms you appeared for and what you spent on each. (Source A, 2025.) [L0857.010]
  • To see which searches triggered your Google Ads, open the campaign in Google Ads and click Keywords. Review the listed search terms and mark the irrelevant or overly broad ones as negative keywords. (Source A, 2020, older.) [L0551.078]
  • To see which search terms converted: open Google Ads, select the campaign, then click Keywords or Search terms in the left sidebar. Choose the date range; the view shows the terms that triggered ads and the conversions they produced. (Source A, 2022, older.) [L0963.012]
  • To see which search terms trigger a Beta campaign: open Google Ads, go to the Beta campaign, click Keywords, and review the triggering search terms. (Source A, 2024.) [L0805.014]
  • Do not use the Keywords section for this. It only shows negative keywords already added, not the search terms the ads actually appeared for. Use the Search terms report under Insights and reports. [L0750.012]
  • Find which search terms a Shopping product got impressions for: 1) Open Google Ads. 2) Go to your alpha and beta campaigns. 3) Open the keywords view and click the search terms your product appeared for. 4) Look for keywords with many impressions that go to that product. (Source A, 2024.) [L0806.065]

Reading the Report

  • The Search terms view shows the columns Search term, Match type, Added/Excluded, Ad group, Clicks, Impr., CTR, Avg. CPC, Cost and Conversions. Use these to spot irrelevant terms, low click-through terms and clicks without sales. (Source B, 2021-22.) [L0744.031]
  • The Search Terms view can list thousands of terms over 30 days, for example 6,971 terms in an established account. The view can show up to 500 rows per page, and a single ad group view can show 1 to 500 of 2,673 terms. (Source B, 2021-22.) [L0744.030]
  • The Google Ads search terms view paginates its rows, so only a fraction of a large report is visible on screen at once. Export the complete search terms report to a file before analysis instead of reading the on-screen list. [L0876.039]
  • Use the search terms report within each ad group to view exact searches triggered by your ads. Add irrelevant searches as negative keywords at the ad group level to prevent wasted spend. (Source B, 2021-22.) [L0262.022]
  • Shopping campaign search terms: Open the campaign, then the ad group. Look at Keywords and then Search terms. Expand the date range if nothing shows. If a product group reads not set, click it and expand it. (Source A, 2021, older.) [L0247.063]
  • Search terms optimization in Shopping campaigns: sort by clicks to see where most traffic came from. Check that branded terms show up in the campaign that should contain them, since branded terms normally convert very well and should not run in the beta campaign. [L0745.022]
  • Compare reported impressions with the impressions the ad group actually received. The difference (example: 1,500 shown vs 3,800 received, about 2,000 hidden) is the search traffic Google does not report. [L0745.022]
  • Compare the compare-period columns (Clicks, Impr., CTR, Conversions, Conv. rate, Cost / conv., Cost) against the previous period to spot new waste. [L0876.021]
  • Use a negative keyword list built from the search terms report. Add a term as a negative only after checking whether it produced clicks or conversions. (Source A, 2022, older.) [L0963.013]
  • Build negative keyword lists from the search terms report: add terms that get impressions or clicks but make no sense for your product, and terms that spend money without producing sales. [L0857.011]

  1. Open the high priority campaign, click into it to see its ad groups, then open Keywords and choose Search Terms. [L0744.029]

Find the Right Scope Before Changing Anything Else

  • If Google Shopping spend goes to search terms without buying intent, fix keyword targeting and add negative keywords before changing the niche or widening the product range. Buying intent means the searcher is ready to purchase a specific product now. [L0665.119]
  • If search impression share for broad category keywords stays low in a beta campaign set up for brand plus SKU searches, raise the default cost per click so those keywords get impressions. Add the brand name as a negative keyword to keep category searches out. (Source A, 2023.) [L0470.013]
  • When a broad niche keyword takes most of the Google Shopping budget, add that keyword as an exact match negative keyword so spend shifts toward more purchase-ready searches. [L0857.001]
  • Negative keywords reduce wasted spend, lift click-through rate and bring more relevant impressions. If search impression share is low, the ads are often showing for irrelevant search terms. [L0806.064]
  • If visitors click but do not convert, check which keywords generate those clicks and add negatives for irrelevant searches, because the buying intent of the traffic drives conversion rate. Negative keywords reduce spend on low-intent searches and raise the share of buyers in the traffic. (Source A, 2024.) [L0471.004]
  • Adding a negative keyword as a fix for a product that is shown too often: add negative keywords or lower its CPC at the product level. [L0803.043]

AI-Assisted Term Audits

Starter List for a New Campaign

  • For a new campaign with no search history, an AI can build a starter negative keyword list from the store URL alone. This avoids launching blind and spending the first week's budget on irrelevant searches. [L0876.029]
  • Starter list prompt for a new Search or Shopping campaign on an eCommerce store:
You are a Google Ads strategist building a starter negative keyword list for a new Search or Shopping campaign on an eCommerce store.

Store URL: [paste store URL]
Product overview (optional, use only if the URL gives little detail): [one line]

Review the store to determine what it truly sells: product types, categories, price level and the type of buyer it targets. If the URL cannot be read, request the page contents from me instead of guessing.

Then compile a starter list of negative keywords for searches that would waste budget or indicate the wrong intent for this store. Sort the negatives into these buckets:
- Free / cheap intent (for example 'free', 'cheap', 'discount code', 'coupon')
- Job / career intent (for example 'jobs', 'careers', 'salary', 'hiring')
- Non-buyer intent (for example 'DIY')
- Wholesale / B2B / bulk (only if the store is retail or DTC)
- Wrong product or category (adjacent items the store does not carry)
- Competitor brand names (include only if they are obvious from the category; flag each one for my review)
- My own brand names, such as my actual brand name and product names unique to my brand

Rules:
- Include only negatives that clearly would not lead to a sale for this specific store.
- Never negate terms that could come from real buyers. When uncertain, place the term in a separate 'Review before adding' list rather than the main list.
- Format each bucket as a plain list I can copy directly into Google Ads.
- End with one short note naming any buckets I should double-check against my own product range.

Include a note stating that this list is a starting seed and must be replaced with search terms report data once real data is available.

[L0876.031]

  • Discount and promo terms such as "discount code" or "promo code" are often added as negatives. Some accounts target them by mistake and end up competing with large discount websites, so they are usually worth excluding. [L0876.032]

Shopping ads often show a product to people searching for the vehicle or item it fits, not the accessory itself, which creates irrelevant paid clicks. Check search term reports for this mismatch, because those visitors are not in the market for the product and paying for them is wasteful. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1456.007]

Brand and Product-Name Negatives

Brand Names list

The Brand Names list should contain the names of the brands sold through your website, entered as just the brand name. It is a separate negative list from the master and product lists. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1074.019]

Product IDs list

Create a separate Product IDs list, which may also be named product identifiers or product names, containing the unique identifiers of your products. These identifiers can be model numbers, model names or similar codes. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1074.020]

A Product IDs list can mix model numbers such as AM156JDTSZ, BO73D-BLK and BT-0502 with model names such as Baldwin, Barcelona, Barrett, Bergen and Bliss. Such a mix is fine. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1074.021]

If a product has both a model number and a model name, enter both as separate negative terms. Customers may search with either one, so both must be covered. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1074.023]

Match Types and Pitfalls

Match type definitions

The screen describes each negative keyword match type as follows. (Older advice, 2021-22.)

Match type Ad does not show when the search
Broad match Contains all keyword terms, even if they are in a different order
Phrase match Contains the exact keyword terms in the same order
Exact match Contains the exact keyword terms, in the same order, without extra words

[L1074.025]

A broad match negative blocks a search only when it contains all of the keyword terms. A broad positive keyword triggers on any one of the terms. For negative keywords, broad match is often the usual choice. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1074.026]

A broad match negative keyword blocks any search containing its words in any order. Phrase match blocks searches containing the phrase with extra words before or after. Exact match blocks only that exact search. Use broad match for SKU numbers, and phrase or exact match for brand plus product names so you do not block searches for other products that share generic words. [L1115.100]

Negative exact keywords are written in square brackets and block only that exact query. Negative phrase keywords block any query that contains the words. Use phrases for brand names and exact keywords for generic terms you want to keep out of a campaign. [L1328.009]

Use broad match for terms you never want, and phrase or exact match for terms you might want in some cases. [L1285.057]

Use negative keywords to block searches you do not want. A negative phrase keyword blocks every query that contains that phrase, so a negative phrase on a single word blocks all queries with that word. [L1352.007]

Common pitfalls

A broad match negative keyword can also block searches that are relevant to your product, because it blocks every search containing that word. Check which relevant searches a broad negative is removing. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1074.029]

Exact match on a single brand or model word such as Baldwin would only block searches that consist of that word alone, and would miss most related queries. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1074.028]

A negative exact keyword blocks only the exact wording you typed. Plural forms, word-order changes and abbreviations such as "mens" can still trigger the campaign, so add each common variant of a generic term as its own negative exact keyword. [L1328.039]

Add irrelevant search terms as negative keywords and add close variations as broad match modifier, phrase match and exact match negatives. Keep adding negatives over time, because they are the main way to stop wasted spend. (Older advice, 2021.) [L1090.087]

Phrase match blocking can accidentally block searches that already make money, such as a product name that contains the blocked phrase. Before blocking, check whether the term affects profitable search terms and choose exact, phrase or broad match accordingly. (Older advice, 2021.) [L1090.094]

Non-English Terms
