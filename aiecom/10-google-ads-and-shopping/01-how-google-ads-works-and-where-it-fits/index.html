10.1 How Google Ads works and where it fits

Definition and Scope

  • Google Ads is a customizable, pay-per-click, self-serve advertising platform. It includes search ads and the Google Display Network, which reaches many internet users beyond search results. [L0013.002]
  • It is a pay-per-placement platform where advertisers bid to show text, banners, videos, product listings or app install promotions. A store owner pays to be seen by people who are already searching for or browsing related products. [L0714.001]
  • Pay-per-click means the advertiser pays each time a user clicks an ad. It is the paid format used for search and shopping campaigns. [L0713.010]
  • Google Ads reaches people who are actively searching for solutions, so it taps existing demand rather than creating awareness. It places you where people are already looking for what you sell. [L0013.001]
  • The core of Google Ads is showing the right ad to the right person at the right time. Getting these three elements right puts an advertiser well ahead of competitors. [L1078.004]
  • Google Ads gives complete cost transparency. You can see how much revenue each campaign generates, which gives you full control of spending and profitability. [L0013.004]
  • Google Ads fits this model because spend per sale is small relative to the product price. It can work for any product, but it is most effective when the product's margin covers the ad budget. [L1111.106]

Channels, Campaign Types and Targeting

  • Google Ads serves on three main channels: text search results on Google search, Product Listing Ads (PLA) through your Merchant Center with your Google Shopping data, and the Google Display Network, including YouTube, Gmail and many other sites. [L0020.001]
  • Campaign types include Shopping Campaigns, Search Campaigns, Video Campaigns and campaigns on the Google Display Network. [L0020.003]
  • Eight targeting options are available: keywords for search; website and placement targeting for display; demographics; audience interests and life events; topics; YouTube videos and channels; customer match; and remarketing. [L0020.002]
  • The four Google traffic methods work as one integrated system. Organic search provides free high-intent traffic, product listings add free visibility, shopping ads deliver scalable paid traffic with maximum control, and remarketing recaptures interested visitors who did not convert. [L0163.011]
  • Google Organic Search is free traffic from visitors who click non-paid text search results. Most clicks concentrate on the first page of Google results. [L0163.003]
  • Google Ads is one traffic source among several, never the only one. Once the account is running, the next step is to add other marketing channels. [L1065.007]
  • Google Ads should not be the only channel. Use it as the starting point, get campaigns running and producing results, then add other traffic channels that complement the Google Ads account. (older: 2021-22) [L0713.009]

The Auction

How the Auction Works

  • Google Ads runs an auction for every search query. Advertisers place bids to have their ads shown for specific queries, and competing advertisers bid on the same queries at the same time. (older: 2021-22) [L0496.001]
  • The auction decides the order of ads on the results page: which ad appears first, second, third and so on. Ads are ranked for each query individually, so the same advertiser can hold different positions for different queries. (older: 2021-22) [L0496.002]
  • Google decides which search terms your ads are eligible for, based on the product data you supply. [L0739.014]
  • Google's auction is a second-price auction, not a typical auction where the highest bidder pays their own bid. (older: 2021-22) [L0496.003]

Second-Price Logic

  • The winning advertiser pays only slightly more than the next competitor's ranking, not the full amount of its own maximum bid. (older: 2021-22) [L0496.003]
  • Standard cost-per-click formula for the second-price auction: (older: 2021-22) [L0496.004]
actual cost per click = (Ad Rank of the advertiser directly below you ÷ your Quality Score) + $0.01
Ad Rank = maximum bid × Quality Score
  • A higher Quality Score lowers the price you pay for the same position. (older: 2021-22) [L0496.004]
  • Approximate cost per click: Ad Rank of the bidder directly below you ÷ your own Quality Score, plus $0.01. You never pay more than your maximum bid, and the actual price depends on the competitors around you. [L1226.009]
  • Worked example: the bidder with Ad Rank 32 sits below a bidder with Quality Score 10, giving 32 / 10 + $0.01 = $3.21 per click. [L1226.009]
  • Do not assume you pay your full bid per click, as you would at a house or product auction. Your actual cost depends on the competitor below you and your Quality Score, so a high bid with a low Quality Score can still cost more per click than necessary. (older: 2021-22) [L0496.006]

Ad Rank and Position

  • Ad Rank = maximum bid × Quality Score. The highest Ad Rank takes the top position, so a bidder with a strong Quality Score can outrank a higher bidder. [L1226.001]
  • Google earns money when people click ads, not when they buy on the advertiser's site. For this reason, it rewards ads that people want to click with a higher Quality Score. [L1226.002]
  • A higher ad position brings more traffic than a lower one, regardless of competitors. A bidder with a well-optimized Quality Score can therefore get more clicks while bidding less than the competition. [L1226.006]
  • Google and Facebook prefer ads that get clicked, so they reward advertisers whose ads perform well with cheaper clicks. Their quality metrics exist to enforce this. [L1031.015]
  • Google Ads uses Quality Score, Facebook uses Relevance Score, and Amazon also uses a quality score. All three measure how well an ad matches what people want and how often they click it. [L1031.016]
  • A bidder with a very low Quality Score can have the highest bid and still rank low. A bidder who bids too little with a strong Quality Score can miss the top results entirely. [L1226.008]
  • Ad rank historically combined the bid with a bundle of factors known as Quality Score. A competitor with a higher Quality Score can rank above you with a lower bid, while a lower Quality Score forces you to bid much higher to move up. (older: 2021-22) [L0879.009]
  • Ad rank and Quality Score cannot be measured directly in the Google Ads interface. Any change to them has to be tested and judged from indirect results, because no one knows the exact outcome. (older: 2021-22) [L0879.011]

Worked Auction Example

Worked auction example (bids and Quality Scores as stated in the lesson): [L1226.007]

Bidder Max bid Quality Score Ad Rank (bid x QS) Position
Bidder A $3.75 10 37.5 1
Bidder B $4.00 8 32 2
Bidder C $3.45 7 24.15 3
Bidder D not stated 6 not stated 4
Bidder E $5.00 3 15 5
Bidder F $2.50 4 10 6
Bidder G $1.70 5 8.50 not stated
Bidder H $0.50 high high first page
  • Bidder A ranks above Bidder B despite bidding $0.25 less. Bidder E bids the most but ranks fifth because of its very low Quality Score. [L1226.007]

Cost Per Click and Click Quality

  • A high Quality Score gives discounted clicks. A low Quality Score triggers a penalty that raises the cost per click, so Quality Score changes what you pay, not only where your ad shows. [L1226.004]
  • Quality Score affects ad rank, not only the price paid. In the example, a bidder with the highest bid but a low Quality Score was not shown, while a bidder with a lower bid and the strongest Quality Score took the top position. A high Quality Score lets an advertiser win placement with a smaller bid. [L0510.013]
  • Moving from a Quality Score of 5 to 6 gave about a 16% CPC discount. Moving from 5 to 7 gave about a 30% discount. [L0510.012]
  • When Quality Score rises, CPC falls, so an advertiser with a higher score can bid low and still appear first and receive the most clicks. Bidding high is not required to win top placement. [L1031.018]
  • Shopping ads charge per click. Cost-per-click is lowered when your ad gets a higher click-through rate and visitors stay longer on your site than on competitors' sites. (2023) [L0855.021]
  • Google evaluates post-click behavior on the store (bounce rate, time on site, add-to-cart, purchase). Strong engagement raises Quality Score and lets ads show more for less, so the conversion tag must be set up for tracking. (2024) [L0666.039]
  • Shopping ads show the price in the ad, so shoppers who object to the price do not click. This filters out unqualified clicks before they cost money. [L0510.064]

Quality Score

  • Quality Score is Google's rating of how relevant your ads and landing pages are to a search query, based on expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. It affects both the price you pay per click and how competitive your ad is against others for the same query. (older: 2021-22) [L0496.005]
  • Quality Score in Google Ads is based on how relevant the ad is to the searched term, how often people click it, and whether visitors stay on the landing page instead of leaving immediately. A high score lets an advertiser rank well without outbidding everyone. [L0714.004]
  • Quality Score depends largely on click-through rate, along with landing page quality and site experience. Advertisers with a higher Quality Score compete better in the auction, and at equal bids they usually pay less per click within a certain range. (older: 2021-22) [L0744.037]
  • A slow website can lower Quality Score through the on-page experience factor. Search text ads once showed per-keyword quality breakdowns, but Shopping never has, though the same factors still apply. (older: 2021-22) [L0879.014]
  • Google does not publish the exact Ad Rank or Quality Score factors or their weights. The bid is the main lever for search impression share, while Quality Score factors help make your bid more competitive. Google Shopping uses its own algorithm separate from search. (older: 2021-22) [L0879.010]
  • Google Ads shows Quality Score per keyword with change history. It does not show a Quality Score column for Shopping campaigns. [L0510.014]
  • Google has no separate advertiser-level Quality Score visible to the advertiser; only the keyword level is displayed. Improving an existing campaign is generally better than creating a new one, because the campaign history and steady spend help the account. [L0510.027]

CTR as the Main Lever

  • Google measures whether people like an ad mainly through Click-Through Rate (CTR). A high CTR is the main signal of a clickable ad. [L1226.003]
  • CTR = Clicks ÷ Impressions. A higher CTR increases Quality Score and lowers CPC, so a higher CTR gives cheaper clicks. [L1031.017]
  • CTR has the largest influence on Quality Score. If your CTR is below the benchmark, you probably have a Quality Score problem. [L1031.019]
  • Google measures relevance through click-through rate. A low CTR, where people see the ad but do not click, lowers ad rank because Google reads it as weak relevance compared with competitors. (older: 2021-22) [L0879.012]
  • Most paid-traffic problems can be traced back to CTR, so start diagnosis there. [L1031.020]
  • For Shopping, the two ad types worth optimizing most are ad engagement and Shopping campaign CTR. These are the two levers to address first. [L1031.021]
  • When an account has high bids but almost no traffic, the usual cause is that the ads are not clickable, not that the bids are too low. Fix ad quality before raising bids. [L1226.005]
  • Raising CTR, which drives Quality Score, is therefore the main lever for cheaper clicks. [L0510.012]

Benchmark CTR for Shopping Campaigns

  • Google does not show a Quality Score column for Shopping campaigns. Use the Benchmark CTR, which reflects the average CTR of competing advertisers, to estimate the Shopping Quality Score. [L0510.014]
  • If your CTR is below the Benchmark CTR, a Quality Score problem is likely and bidding more will not fix it. In the example, the advertiser had a 0.77% CTR against a 0.94% benchmark, so competitors were paying less per click for the same visibility. [L0510.015]
  • Google shows an ad in more than one position for a query when the ad's CTR is higher than the average for that position. Each position has an average CTR, so an ad that beats its position average gets moved up, which then makes its CTR even higher. [L0510.023]

Funnel Position

Funnel Model

  • The sales funnel is an inverse pyramid where TOFU (top, awareness stage), MOFU (middle, consideration stage) and BoFU (bottom, buying stage) represent declining audience size but increasing purchase intent. Apply this framework when structuring Google Ads campaigns. [L0023.001]
  • The funnel runs Awareness, Interest, Learn, Shop, Buy. Google Ads works well because its campaigns target people at the bottom of the funnel who are ready to buy. The goal is to reach only those at the Buy stage, not people who are only researching or aware of a product. [L0714.009]
  • Paid traffic works when the right ad reaches the right customer at the right stage of the funnel. Most people at the top are only researching, a larger group is comparing options, and only a small share at the bottom is ready to buy now. [L0720.004]
  • Funnel-aligned spending is more effective and profitable because BoFU audiences have much higher conversion rates and require less budget to generate sales than spending on TOFU awareness audiences. [L0023.002]
  • Intent maps onto the sales funnel. Low-value terms sit at the top, medium at the middle and high at the bottom. Google Shopping and search text ads perform best in the middle and bottom of the funnel, and they perform poorly for high-ticket products at the top. (older: 2021-22) [L0739.025]

Matching Message to Stage

  • Match each funnel stage with a different ad message. At the top, reach people who are unaware of the product or the problem with informational content. In the middle, serve people comparing options with webinars, buying guides, comparison guides and detailed video reviews. At the bottom, show direct product ads to people who are ready to buy. [L0720.024]
  • No tool currently matches the right ad to each funnel stage automatically, so audience and ad segmentation by funnel stage has to be planned and set up manually. [L0720.006]

Where Google Sits in the Funnel

  • Google Ads is excellent for middle and bottom funnel work, such as retargeting previous visitors, but weak for top funnel because it cannot reach cold audiences unaware of the product. [L0296.011]
  • Google is mostly a bottom-of-funnel platform (search and shopping ads triggered by product searches). Top-of-funnel testing should be done on Facebook and TikTok first. Test on Google once the product works with branded content. [L0577.049]
  • Google Ads cannot interrupt journeys or show new products to cold audiences. It works best where people already search for solutions or have pre-existing intent. [L0296.010]
  • Google Shopping works best for middle and bottom of the funnel, meaning shoppers already searching for specific products. It performs poorly for high-ticket businesses at the top of the funnel, and spending heavily on top-of-funnel traffic makes the campaign unprofitable. (older: 2021-22) [L0741.016]
  • Google Ads are strong in the middle and bottom of the funnel but weak at the top. Social media is strong at the top, and YouTube works across the whole funnel. Using them together gives full coverage of the customer journey. (older: 2021-22) [L0414.034]
  • Google is strong for retargeting and for buyers who search for the product. Cold traffic from Google is less scalable than Facebook, because customers must already be searching for the product. Use Google to reach people with search intent rather than to find new audiences. [L0694.036]
  • Target Google Ads at search queries with a high chance of leading to a sale rather than broad interest. Expensive products should not be pushed to cold audiences on social media in the first phase. (older: 2021-22) [L0713.005]
  • Cold traffic from Google includes Google Shopping, Google display ads, Google search text ads and YouTube ads. (older: 2021-22) [L0414.028]
  • Google and Bing are the main channels for front-end demand on expensive products, since they reach people already searching. Facebook and Instagram are better suited to remarketing. (older: 2021) [L0247.054]
  • Most high-ticket sales come from Google Shopping ads and organic search, because buyers of expensive items use search engines. Social media is not a main channel in this model and is used only for retargeting. (older: 2021-22) [L0771.037]
  • Google Shopping reaches people who are actively searching for a specific product, while social media reaches people who are mostly not ready to buy. For expensive products, this makes search-based traffic more likely to convert. (older: 2021-22) [L0713.004]

Ad Types and Placements

Overview of Types

  • Four main Google Ads types matter for a dropshipping store: Search Text Ads, Display Ads, YouTube Ads and Shopping Ads. Other ad types exist but are rarely used in this approach. [L0714.002]
  • Google Ads shows ads in several placements: search results (SERP), Shopping (including on mobile), the Display Network on individual websites, Gmail and YouTube. For e-commerce, search and Shopping placements usually deserve the most attention first. [L0720.007]
  • Google Ads and Bing Ads are paid search channels that show ads to people actively searching for products. Use them as main paid traffic sources next to Facebook Ads. [L0597.007]

Search Text Ads

  • Search Text Ads appear at the top of Google search results for a keyword, marked as paid, and are won through a keyword auction where the highest bidder ranks first. Bid amount alone does not decide position because Quality Score also counts. [L0714.003]
  • Google search text ads are the sponsored ads that appear above the organic results. [L0676.005]
  • Search text ads can outperform Shopping in some cases, especially outside the US, so test both formats before deciding. (older: 2021-22) [L0739.001]
  • On a results page, Shopping ads come first, followed by the first block of search text ads, then organic results, then a second block of text ads at the bottom. Shopping results are shown above all other ads when Google has relevant products. (older: 2021-22) [L0741.003]
  • When relevant Shopping ads exist for a search, they are the first results a shopper sees, above search text ads and organic results. On desktop they sit at the top of the page above the fold. Google shows no further Shopping ads lower on the page, only search text ads. (older: 2021-22) [L0739.003]

Shopping Ads

  • Shopping Ads show a product image, product name, price, store URL and a short description for matching searches. They work very well for dropshipping stores and are a core part of the approach. [L0714.007]
  • Google Shopping ads are paid placements that show the product image, store name and price on the front of Google and at the top of the Google Shopping section, making them the most controllable traffic source in this method set. [L0586.008]
  • Google Shopping ads appear in a carousel at the top of the regular Google results page for product searches and in the Shopping tab. They are labeled "Sponsored products" and are charged per click. [L0743.002]
  • Shopping ads display the product image, price and product name directly in the ad, so the user sees exactly what they would be clicking. This makes these clicks purchase-ready, which is why they tend to convert better than generic ads. [L0746.031]
  • Shopping ads were reported to convert about 30% better than standard text ads and to produce about 40% higher average order value. In the same source, Shopping ads made up about 60% of Google ad clicks and about 82% of non-branded clicks. [L0510.011]
  • For high-ticket dropshipping, Google Shopping produces most of the sales that come from Google Ads traffic for about 90% of businesses, usually at the best profitability. Some stores, especially outside the US, get better results from search text ads, so test both formats before deciding. (older: 2021-22) [L0739.001]
  • Google Shopping is the main Google Ads format for high-ticket dropshipping. For most such businesses it generates the majority of ad-driven sales at the best profitability. Search text ads can outperform it in some cases, especially outside the US, and the Display Network is a secondary format. (older: 2021-22) [L0741.001]
  • Shopping ads appear at the top of the results page, where most clicks and sales come from. Free product listings appear lower and require submission to Google Merchant Center. (older: 2021) [L0671.088, L0671.225]
  • Google Shopping ads are the first traffic source for a new store, because they bring in shoppers with clear purchase intent. Facebook ads should follow to re-engage visitors who leave the site. (older: 2019) [L1069.077]
  • Google Shopping is the highest converting traffic source for these stores. Keep it as the core of paid traffic and focus optimization effort there. (older: 2020) [L0664.060]
  • Start conversion work with Google Shopping campaigns, since they usually bring most of a store's traffic, and only then move to remarketing. Judge those campaigns by their own conversion rate, not the store-wide average. [L0626.021]
  • Google Shopping product listing ads are a main traffic source that sends shoppers directly to a product page on the Shopify store. [L0676.005]
  • Shopping ads appear in Google's Shopping tab and in regular search results when a user searches for a brand name, product name or SKU that exists in the product feed. Merchant Center must hold the products and be linked to the Google Ads account before any of this works. [L0746.032]

Product Listing Ads (PLA)

  • Product listing ads (PLAs) are image-based product ads shown in Google search results. They display a product image, title, price, store name and star ratings, which gives shoppers a lot of buying information before they click. [L0762.001]
  • Google Product Listing Ads show products with images and prices directly in Google search results and are one of the main traffic sources in this model. [L0759.006]
  • PLAs reach people who are already searching for a product, unlike Facebook ads, which interrupt users who are browsing their feed without buying intent. This makes PLAs a stronger fit for buyers than interruption-based placements. [L0762.002]
  • Until a store makes more than $50,000 in sales per month, keep focusing the paid budget on Google Product Listing Ads and do not start other paid channels. Google PLAs stay in use even after that point, but they are no longer the only focus. (older: 2018) [L1085.001]
  • PLAs only appear for commercial search queries, meaning searches where the user is looking to buy. Informational how-to queries show few or no product ads, which filters out clicks from people who are not interested in buying. [L0762.003]
  • With Shopping campaigns you do not select keywords yourself. Google decides which searches trigger your product ads, mainly by matching the search against your product data, especially product titles. [L0762.004]
  • Google automatically classifies each search as commercial or non-commercial and only shows product ads for the commercial ones. This classification happens without any keyword setup from the advertiser. [L0762.005]
  • A commercial search such as "infrared sauna" showed many product listing ads, while the informational search "how to install cabinet doors" showed only one product ad next to a knowledge panel. The difference shows how Google separates buying intent from research intent. [L0762.006]

Display Network

  • The Google Display Network places image banners of various sizes on partner websites across the internet. For a new store with a small budget, Display Ads usually have a weak return on ad spend and are not recommended as a starting point. They become more useful as the business grows. [L0714.005]
  • Google Ads serves on the Google Display Network, which includes YouTube, Gmail and many other sites. [L0020.001]

YouTube

  • YouTube Ads appear before or during videos on YouTube, and viewers can skip after 5 seconds. They need extra production work to set up and usually give a weaker return on ad spend than other Google ad types, so they are not recommended for beginners. [L0714.006]
  • YouTube works across the whole funnel, so it is useful at every stage when combined with other channels. (older: 2021-22) [L0414.034]

Performance Max and Other Types

  • Besides brand search, other Google campaign types include dynamic ads and Smart Shopping campaigns. Performance Max is treated as the second priority after a brand search campaign. [L1213.008]
  • Google Ads campaign types include Shopping Campaigns, Search Campaigns, Video Campaigns and Display Network campaigns. [L0020.003]

Placement and Layout

  • Google search results show Shopping ads in the right-hand column and text search ads on the left. Shopping also appears on mobile, where Shopping Spotlight ads present products in a richer format. For e-commerce, Shopping placements often need the most attention. [L0720.021]
  • Google changes how Shopping results are displayed over time, for example as a horizontal row at the top or a vertical block on the side. A different layout on a later check is normal and not a reason to change the campaign. (older: 2021-22) [L0739.004]
  • Shopping ads appear mainly on the Google search results page, but they can also show on other Google properties such as YouTube video overlays and Gmail inboxes. Placement settings control where they run. (older: 2021-22) [L0739.002]
  • Shopping ads appear mainly on the Google search results page and can also show as overlays on YouTube videos and in Gmail inboxes. On desktop they sit above the fold, and Google may change their layout at any time. (older: 2021-22) [L0741.002]
  • Shoppers can click the Shopping tab on the results page to see a larger grid of related products. Clicking a product ad sends the shopper directly to the retailer's product page. (older: 2021-22) [L0739.005]
  • Shopping ads always send traffic to the product page of the promoted item, not to collection pages. This is the default behavior and cannot be changed. (older: 2021) [L0801.059]

How Google Shopping Works

Comparison Engine

  • Google Shopping works as a comparison shopping engine. Shoppers compare the same or similar products across retailers by price, title, retailer name, reviews and delivery without opening each site. Your product page is therefore judged directly against competitors shown beside it. (older: 2021-22) [L0739.006]
  • Customers compare the same product across several retailers on price, brand, delivery, special offers, reviews and seller. As a reseller you are therefore always compared directly with other sellers of the same product. (older: 2021-22) [L0741.004]
  • Google Shopping is a comparison engine by design, and avoiding comparison with other sellers of the same product is not possible within it. If you cannot accept competing with identical listings, this channel is not a good fit. (older: 2021-22) [L1170.016]
  • Shoppers decide mainly on price, product title, retailer name and reviews. When five or more shops sell the same product, the listing also shows delivery and special offers. (older: 2021-22) [L0739.011]
  • Shoppers decide on price, product title, retailer name and reviews, and may also see delivery and special offers when several shops are listed. (older: 2021-22) [L0741.006]
  • Shopping ads place your product alongside other sellers' near-identical listings, so price competition is a race to the bottom. Differentiate with better offers, better content and better images, not with price, and do not compete against the AliExpress listing itself. [L0577.053]
  • Google Shopping only shows "compare prices at X stores" when multiple sellers list the same product. If only one listing exists, only one price appears. (2024) [L0836.030]

Shared Product Pages

  • When several retailers sell the same product, Google groups their listings together, and the price range can be wide. A listing marked as refurbished can show the lowest price, so check the condition label when benchmarking competitors. (older: 2021-22) [L0739.007]
  • Google groups listings with matching product data, such as the same GTIN and brand, onto one shared product page where each seller shows its own price, delivery and offers. Resellers of the same item therefore compete on that page, and price, delivery and offer details decide which seller gets the click. (older: 2021-22) [L0741.020]
  • A seller who lists a refurbished unit under the same identifiers can appear with the lowest price on the shared product page, and the condition is only visible in the details. A retailer that sells only new products must expect such lower prices and compete on other factors such as delivery, offers and page clarity. (older: 2021-22) [L0741.021]

Query Matching and Auction Entry

  • For each search, Google checks whether its product feeds contain a matching product. If so, it runs an auction among the retailers who submitted that product. Entry depends on the bid, whether the advertiser has enough budget to win a click, and any negative keywords the advertiser has set. (older: 2021-22) [L0741.008]
  • Google Shopping entry procedure, as described in the source: (older: 2021-22) [L0739.014]
  1. A customer enters a search on Google. [L0739.014]
  2. Google checks whether a product in the merchant's product feed can match that search. [L0739.014]
  3. If there is a match, Google runs an auction among eligible retailers, considering each one's bid, budget and negative keywords. [L0739.014]
  4. If the bid and budget win the auction and no negative keyword blocks the search, the Shopping ad is generated and shown to the customer. [L0739.014]
  • Google's incentive is to show ads for many searches that carry some chance of a sale, not to filter by intent. The advertiser must build the filtering through bids, budgets and negative keywords, and avoid overexposure to low-value traffic that burns budget. (older: 2021-22) [L0741.014]
  • Google keeps matching your products to a broad range of terms because most of its revenue comes from ads, and it accepts loosely related searches as long as some sale chance exists. The advertiser therefore has to manage intent-based bidding and exclusions. (older: 2021-22) [L0739.028]
  • Search results and ad details depend on the searcher's location, so check how your ads look from the target country before judging them. Local tax and delivery display are part of that check. (older: 2021-22) [L0739.013]

Taxes and Location

  • In the US, Google can show a sales tax column per state, based on the shopper's IP address location. In Australia, the single national GST of 10% is included in the displayed price and is not shown as a separate column. (older: 2021-22) [L0739.012]
  • Some markets show sales tax as a separate column based on the shopper's IP location, while in Australia tax is included in the price and not shown separately because the national rate is a single 10%. (older: 2021-22) [L0741.006]

When Google Suits a Product or Store

Product Fit

  • Google Ads works best for products where people already search for solutions or have pre-existing intent, such as furniture, health and fitness. [L0296.010]
  • Furniture and home decor are good categories for Google Ads because they are high-ticket products with good search volume. Allow one to two weeks to warm up the account so the algorithm understands the audience. (Source C, undated) [L0788.013]
  • For Google ads for clothing, collection and catalogue ads perform best. [L0779.015]
  • For high-ticket products (roughly 1,000 USD and up), Google Search and Google Shopping are the main paid channel to start with. Buyers of expensive items look for solutions via search engines rather than on social platforms like Facebook, Instagram or Pinterest. (older: 2021-22) [L1065.001]
  • For high-ticket e-commerce products, Google Ads (search and Shopping) and organic SEO are the primary marketing channels. Buyers who have a question or a problem to solve type their query into a search engine, so search is the place to meet them rather than social feeds where they are not actively looking for such products. (older: 2021-22) [L1065.008]
  • Google Shopping campaigns for expensive products require a different structure than campaigns for low-priced items such as deodorant, toothbrushes or 30 USD t-shirts. Follow the high-ticket approach step by step rather than applying generic low-ticket setups. (older: 2021-22) [L1065.005]
  • Google Shopping campaigns for high-ticket dropshipping need a more advanced setup than basic campaigns. Expect the setup and ongoing management to take more time to learn. (older: 2021-22) [L0713.011]
  • A good traffic source combines traffic volume, affordable cost per visitor and a good chance of conversion. Google Ads is a suitable starting point because it offers all three together. (older: 2021-22) [L0713.006]
  • Google Shopping is a sensible first traffic source for a new high-ticket store, and it reaches people who are already searching for the product. (see next subsection for conflicting views) (older: 2021-22) [L0713.001]

Store Readiness and Timing

  • For a new high-ticket dropshipping store, paid Google Ads and Google Shopping are a sensible first traffic source because they deliver traffic almost immediately. Organic search traffic takes much longer to build. (older: 2021-22) [L0713.001]
  • Paid search ads can be switched on immediately and bring traffic within a day, whereas SEO typically needs several months before measurable results appear. Paid ads therefore suit the early launch phase of a new store, but they should not be the only traffic source over time. (older: 2021-22) [L1065.002]
  • Early paid traffic lets a new store collect performance data, customer feedback and customer service experience. These show what buyers want, what they reject, and whether the website fits the market. (older: 2021-22) [L0713.002]
  • Start paid traffic with Google Ads before other paid channels, because the Google Ads supply of qualified shoppers is large enough to justify mastering it first. (older: 2018) [L1109.057]
  • Google Ads works best for a store that already gets roughly 1,000 to 5,000 monthly site visitors, with Google Analytics and the pixel connected. Without existing traffic and tracking, Google campaigns have little data to learn from. [L1213.004]
  • Google Ads for drop shipping does not make sense as a first test, since Google is expensive at the top of the funnel. Use it only once the product is proven and you have a branded store, and then it is a good source of conversions. [L0577.055]
  • Google Ads is suitable for beginners only if the product is already generating sales (proof of concept) from another platform, or if the product has inherent search intent. [L0296.023]
  • Revenue from Google Ads differs strongly by market. Some high-ticket stores generate multiple millions of dollars per year from Google Ads alone, while in other markets the same channel may generate only about $500,000 per year. (older: 2021-22) [L0713.007]

Starting Point: Conflicting Views

The sources disagree on whether Google should be the first paid channel. Each applies under different conditions:

  • Google Shopping as the first traffic source for a new store, with Facebook following to re-engage visitors. Source from 2019, so older. Applies to a new store with clear purchase intent. [L1069.077]
  • Keep the paid budget on Google PLAs and do not start other paid channels until the store makes more than $50,000 in sales per month. Source from 2018, so older. Applies below that sales level. [L1085.001]
  • Google Ads and Google Shopping as a sensible first source for a new high-ticket store, because traffic arrives almost immediately. Source from 2021-22, so older. Applies to high-ticket products with search demand. [L0713.001]
  • Google as a later step, not a first test, because it is expensive at the top of the funnel. Applies to drop-shipping products that have not been proven and have no branded store. [L0577.055]
  • Google as a beginner channel only when the product already sells elsewhere or has inherent search intent. Applies to beginners. [L0296.023]
  • Test top-of-funnel on Facebook and TikTok first, and test on Google once the product works with branded content. Applies to products that need awareness testing. [L0577.049]

Scaling and Competition

  • A single product on Google Ads has a scaling ceiling based on search volume and user intent. Scaling depends on how much search demand exists, unlike Meta or TikTok, where account capacity allows higher scaling. [L0296.014]
  • An established competitor's edge in paid Shopping comes from campaigns that are better optimized with more data, and usually from higher bids, not from store age. A new store can match or outrank them quickly in paid results by optimizing its campaigns and bidding more. (older: 2022) [L0963.154]
  • Competition in Google Shopping is measured per product, not per store. Count the stores that sell the same specific product, not the total number of stores in the niche. (2024) [L0856.004]
  • If more than 10 suppliers advertise the most popular product in Google Shopping, do not bid on that product with Shopping ads. Rely on free organic listings and traffic instead. (older: 2022) [L0963.010]
  • Google Ads is well suited to this model because spend per sale is small relative to the product price. (older: 2020) [L1111.106]
  • Google Ads has been the most profitable traffic source across niches for over a decade, so do not abandon it after weak early results. Refine campaigns before moving to other channels. (older: 2022) [L0803.033]

Control and Automation

  • The main advantage of paid traffic is control over who sees an ad, based on demographics, age, household income, search behavior and other signals. A billboard reaches a broad crowd and makes it hard to tell which exposures led to a sale, while targeted paid ads can be measured and adjusted. [L0720.001]
  • Google Ads gives you control over where and when your ads appear. When evaluating new features or options, be skeptical of anything that removes your control, because this trades away the ability to target the right audiences precisely and prioritizes visibility over conversions. [L0020.004]
  • Avoid Google Ads features that take away control over bidding, targeting or ad creation, because they tend to optimize for visibility rather than direct conversions. Google's former AdWords Express bundled automated bidding, automated targeting and automated ad creation and is the example of this kind of setup to avoid. [L0720.002]
  • Creating a campaign without goals guidance gives more manual control over settings. More control over campaigns tends to produce a higher return on ad spend than letting Google automate by fitting into its boxes. [L0734.002]
  • Google's Shopping campaign setup has changed little in about a decade. Google does not publish how ad rank or Quality Score work, so be skeptical of anyone claiming hidden shortcuts. Once the known levers are handled, further account optimization usually brings diminishing returns. (older: 2021-22) [L0879.042]

Interface-Change Mindset

  • Google Ads implements significant changes almost monthly, so it requires regular monitoring of the official Google Ads Blog, Google Ads Updates and Google Ads Support to maintain campaign performance. [L0017.001]
  • The foundational principle for Google Ads campaigns, right message, right person, right time, remains valid regardless of interface or feature changes. [L0017.002]
  • Google Ads changes its features almost every month, and many changes affect core functionality, not just colors or layout. Check the account and its settings regularly, because a setup that worked a few months ago may behave differently now. [L0489.001]
  • AdWords was renamed Google Ads and its interface was fully redesigned. Features may have moved to other menus or may work differently, so an older tutorial can send you to settings that no longer exist. [L0489.002]
  • Google has posted over 2,000 changes to its official AdWords blog in recent years. Follow the official Google Ads announcements instead of relying only on older course material or forum answers. [L0489.003]
  • Build large libraries of campaigns, keywords, audiences and email lists so you can rebuild quickly when Google changes its tools. A large stock of tested assets is a hedge against platform changes. [L0489.007]
  • Older Google Ads course instructions for conversion tracking setup often no longer match the current interface and menu names. Before following a guide step by step, check the actual menus and settings in the live account and compare them with Google's current official documentation. [L0489.012]
  • Platform changes in Google Ads were mainly visible in YouTube campaigns, so they had limited impact on a standard Shopping setup. Adjust only when you actually see a change in your own account. (older: 2021) [L1090.103]
  • Google Ads changes are not a reason to change a working campaign by themselves. Shopping layouts shift over time, and a different display on a later check is normal. (older: 2021-22) [L0739.004]

Skepticism Toward Google Recommendations, Reps and Agencies

Google's Recommendations

  • The Google Ads optimization score should not be treated as actionable advice. Following Google's recommendations usually means raising budgets and opening up targeting, which can distort the numbers. (older: 2022) [L0429.030]
  • Google will call and push budget increases and wide targeting. Its recommendations generally mean spending more money, so they should be weighed against your own data. (older: 2022) [L0429.073]
  • Google's advice is shaped by its goal of increasing advertiser spend, which may differ from your goal of profit. Evaluate every recommendation against your own margins and results before acting on it. [L0489.010]
  • If a single keyword wastes about $100 per week without producing sales, the usual fix is to pause that keyword. Google may instead suggest moving it into a Display campaign, which would keep spend flowing, so judge that suggestion against your own results. [L0489.011]
  • Applying Google Ads recommendations automatically is risky, because they are generic, auto-generated and not specific to your business. Only apply a recommendation when you can see a business reason for it. (older: 2021-22) [L0879.016]
  • Do not follow Google's automatic recommendations (such as the one-click auto-apply suggestions) by default. Apply them only when the account data supports them. (older: 2022) [L0969.061]
  • Do not use automatically applied Google Ads recommendations or follow advice from Google account representatives who call or email the account, because these often add automated spend-raising settings. (2024) [L0856.013]
  • Avoid following Google's advice without checking it. Changes that raise spend are the default direction of its suggestions. [L0429.073]

Google Reps

  • Google reps often push higher spend, so treat their recommendation as a sales pitch. Test changes before committing a large budget. (older: 2021) [L0801.077]
  • Google account reps tend to recommend higher budgets, broad targeting and their own suggested campaigns. These increase spend without improving sales, so treat their recommendations with caution. (2023) [L0804.079]

Agencies

  • Reject any agency proposal that departs radically from a proven high-ticket Google Ads approach, such as running broad top-of-funnel targeted search text ads, because such strategies will not work for high-ticket dropshipping. (older: 2021-22) [L1053.013]

Core Principles Summary

  • Google is a comparison engine and a cost-per-click platform, so your product page is judged against competitors on price, title, reviews and delivery. [L0739.006, L0741.004]
  • Relevance and CTR drive Ad Rank and cost per click, so clickable ads come before higher bids. [L1226.005, L1031.019]
  • Google is strong at the bottom and middle of the funnel and weak at the top, so treat it as a demand-capture channel. [L0296.011, L0741.016]

What Google Ads Is

  • Google Ads is a self-serve pay-per-click platform. Anyone can open an account, set up ads, and change or delete anything without contacting Google. It was called AdWords until July 2018, when it was renamed. [L1455.001]
  • With paid search you pay only when someone clicks, and you control bids, the search terms you appear for, and how your ad looks. This makes results easier to track and control than organic or unpaid content. [L1455.005]
  • Paid search reaches people who are actively looking for a product or solving a problem, unlike passive browsing of social feeds. Search intent is therefore the key reason to buy clicks for purchase-ready keywords. [L1455.006]
  • Google paid traffic is generally higher quality and more consistent than social media traffic, because shoppers actively search for products. Once an account is set up and working, its performance tends to be more predictable and reliable. [L1251.019]
  • Google works more like a pull channel than Meta, Instagram or TikTok. Those platforms mostly push products to people who did not ask for them, which favors impulse buys. On Google, shoppers already look for the product, so results depend on matching existing demand. [L1508.002]
  • Google Ads and Google Shopping are the paid channels used to attract buyers who search for the specific high-ticket products being sold. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1481.020]
  • Target customers who are actively searching for the exact product type sold, because they are ready to buy when they reach the site. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1481.021]

Scale of the Platform (older figures)

  • In the 2018 lesson, the Google Display Network reached about 80% of US internet users. Google's ad revenue for 2017 was close to $100 billion, showing that pay-per-click advertising is its main revenue source. (Older, 2018.) [L1455.011]
  • In 2018, on most search result pages for e-commerce keywords, ads took over more than 50% of the visible screen, often with only one organic placement shown. This indicates Google prioritizes shopping and ad placements for product-related searches. (Older, 2018.) [L1455.008]

Google Ads Versus Other Paid Channels

  • Google Ads is generally cheaper over the long term than Facebook and TikTok, which face frequent regulation, suspensions and account issues. This makes Google a stronger choice for a steady paid channel. [L1251.020]
  • Google Ads has a higher knowledge requirement at the start than Facebook Ads. Beginners should give themselves time to learn it, because rushing in without that learning often leads to frustration. [L1251.021]

The Auction: Price and Position

  • Your cost per click usually lands slightly below your own bid and above the bid of the advertiser directly below you. Some advertisers in neighboring positions pay less than their own bid, so the result can vary. [L1226.011]
  • Ads are shown in positions one to eight on the results page. Bidders whose Ad Rank is too low to reach that range do not appear on the page at all. Ads shown off the page receive no clicks, so a low Ad Rank means lost traffic even when the budget is available. [L1226.023]

Worked auction example [L1226.022]:

  1. Bidder A has a bid of $4.00 and Quality Score 8, giving Ad Rank 32.
  2. The bidder directly below has a bid of $3.45 and Quality Score 7, giving Ad Rank 24.15. This bidder sets the price.
  3. Divide 24.15 by 8 and add one cent: about $3.03 per click, close to the stated $3.02.

Low-bid example [L1226.010]: A bidder at $0.50 with a very high Quality Score in a low slot paid $0.19 per click, because the bidder just below was bidding $0.20. A high Quality Score can keep costs low even at a small bid.

Quality Score and CTR

  • CTR is the dominant component of Quality Score, so it is the first thing to improve. Landing page relevance is a pass-or-fail check: it only needs to be relevant enough to pass. [L1226.013]
  • Keyword relevance means the keyword must match the ad, and the ad must match the other keywords in the same ad group. All keywords and the ad should relate closely to each other. [L1226.014]
  • In an account, Quality Scores below 6 are a major problem that should be fixed first. Such scores usually mean the ads are paying penalties on every click. [L1226.017]
  • Higher CTR lowers your cost per click, because Google treats more-clicked ads as more relevant. That raises return on ad spend. (Older, 2018.) [L1274.062]

Case: client account, Google Ads, June 29 to July 19 [L1226.016]. The team did not change the landing page or anything else. It only worked on raising CTR.

Metric June 29 July 19 Change
Quality Score 4.7 7.5 up 60%
CTR 5.6% 11.65% roughly doubled
Cost per click $1.70 $1.29 down 24.6%

The same traffic cost much less. The source states the 24.6% drop. The rounded prices ($1.70 and $1.29) give about 24.1%, so the stated figure probably comes from unrounded values. [L1226.016]

Funnel Position: Bottom, Middle and Top

  • Pay-per-click ads should send the right traffic, at the right time, at the right price to your website. This applies to every paid traffic source, including Google Ads, Facebook ads, banner ads and Pinterest ads. Ads do not have to produce the conversion themselves. (Older, Source B 2021-22.) [L1507.001]
  • The right traffic is a person who is in the market for your product at some point and matches your ideal customer, even if they are not ready to buy today. In Google Ads, this means a search with relevant intent toward the products you sell. Not every searcher is the right traffic. (Older, Source B 2021-22.) [L1507.004]
  • Right time refers to the customer's position in the sales funnel, not your own schedule. Top-of-funnel users do not yet know what they need, so buy-focused ads aimed at bottom-of-funnel users will not make sense to them. Match the ad message to where the person is in the funnel: top, middle or bottom. (Older, Source B 2021-22.) [L1507.005]
  • Google Shopping works well for prospects who are most aware of their need or already know the product. It is poorly suited to prospects who are only aware of their problem, because it usually sends visitors to a product page, which often does not move a problem-aware visitor forward. (Older, Source B 2021-22.) [L1392.007]
  • Paid search text ads can become too expensive to generate meaningful volume from completely unaware or problem-aware audiences. They are better suited to prospects who are actively searching for a solution. (Older, Source B 2021-22.) [L1392.009]
  • If targeting is weak, the offer and site presentation are much less likely to convert, even if they are strong. Targeting does not have to hold the highest single share of conversion chances to be critical. Getting the audience right is necessary for the rest of the funnel to succeed. (Older, Source B 2021-22.) [L1507.008]

Campaign Build Order

Campaign build order for a paid search funnel, from highest to lowest priority [L1386.012]:

  1. Audience-based search campaigns, starting with bottom-of-funnel priorities.
  2. Shopping ads with stacked targeting, including retargeting, serving shopping ads to people who previously visited the website.
  3. Search text ads with stacked targeting on the winning keywords learned from shopping campaigns, so shopping results inform the search text ad keywords.
  4. Display retargeting: dynamic retargeting and general retargeting.
  5. Search campaigns without audiences (cold traffic), including shopping campaigns to cold traffic.
  6. Middle-of-funnel audience search text campaigns to cold traffic, targeting people looking for information related to the products, such as buyer's guides or comparison guides.
  7. Top-of-funnel cold traffic on display, YouTube and Gmail, targeting in-market users and demographics that resemble website visitors and customers, sent to a non-commercial page.

Search Campaigns: Conflicting Advice

  • Source C advises against running standard Google search campaigns for a product store, because cost per click is very high even though search generates a lot of traffic. Use shopping-based campaigns instead. This applies to the early setup of a product store. [L1251.022]
  • Source A (2022, older) says search campaigns have a place once a store is scaling, but not in the first stage. Start with Shopping and Performance Max only after the data supports it. [L1281.103]
  • Source A's build order places search after shopping and retargeting, and cold search without audiences comes at step 5. [L1386.012]

Both sources agree that search is not the first step. Source C's warning applies to standard search in a product store's early setup. Source A's sequence applies once shopping and retargeting data exist.

When Google Suits a Product or Store

  • Before adopting a Google Ads strategy, check which business type it was built for. A tactic that works for one model, such as a law firm or a high-margin service, may not work for a low-margin high-ticket dropshipping store. Always weigh the price of the product against what each click costs. (Older, Source B 2021-22.) [L1228.001]
  • Some Google Shopping tactics work for low-ticket products because buyers face little risk and purchase quickly. The same tactics often fail for high-ticket products. Using the wrong tactic for your business type wastes both time and ad budget. (Older, Source B 2021-22.) [L1228.002]
  • For a high-ticket dropshipping store, Google Ads is usually the first paid traffic source to build, ahead of other channels, and it remains a core part of the overall marketing system afterward. (Older, Source B 2021-22.) [L1494.001]
  • A typical Google Ads setup for a high-ticket dropshipping store combines Google Shopping campaigns, search text ad campaigns and display network retargeting campaigns. (Older, Source B 2021-22.) [L1494.003]
  • Business buyers search Google the same way consumers do, so target them with the same Google Ads setup. Also place banner ads on trade and industry websites that business buyers visit. (Older, 2021.) [L1279.097]
  • Shoppers compare Google Shopping listings that show near-identical photos and descriptions, so price is often the only factor for the first click. Matching the lowest price is needed to earn that click. Undercutting causes a price war where everyone loses. [L1293.008]
  • Competing with Amazon works best with non-US stores. Amazon has about 80% market share in the US, and local markets where Amazon is weaker offer more room. A non-US store requires more effort to find suppliers, often about two extra weeks. [L1293.019]
  • Before choosing a Google campaign type, check whether you are building your own brand or running a dropshipping-style store. That decides how much differentiation the product needs. Brand-building stores should move to their own designs and photos once a tested product shows demand. [L1508.037]

Mindset: The Ad Is Not the Sales Tool

  • Thinking that a good ad alone causes sales is a common error. It leads to lower profitability and wasted spend. Evaluate ads by the clicks and visitor quality they produce, and by whether the site converts that traffic profitably. (Older, Source B 2021-22.) [L1507.012]

Skepticism Toward Google's Recommendations

  • Google account reps push more spend and services, such as search campaigns, that are not needed at the start. Follow the account data and the blueprint instead of their upsell advice. (Older, 2022.) [L1281.102]

Agencies, Reps and Outsourcing

  • When judging a PPC agency or freelancer, assess three areas: activity (current account work and recent results), communication (response speed and clear, regular output), and technical expertise (depth of paid search knowledge). Use these as a screening checklist, not as a guarantee of results. [L1262.001]
  • Google ranks agencies on performance and customer care, so a Google Partner status with a top-tier standing is one signal of an agency's recent activity. Verify the status directly in Google's partner directory before relying on it. [L1262.002]
  • A WordStream study ranks advertisers on performance, customer care and account activity. A top 5% placement is cited as a benchmark for a strong account. The figure is a vendor claim, so confirm it against the study itself before using it as a hiring criterion. [L1262.003]
  • A PPC-only agency tends to have deeper technical expertise than a generalist agency, because the team builds its strategies from years of paid search work. When vetting one, ask how long they have run PPC and whether paid search is their only service. [L1262.005]
  • When screening a PPC agency for a store, ask whether they have managed PLA campaigns specifically, not just general AdWords accounts. [L1262.009]
