10.17 Brand search and competitor protection

Why Brand Search Matters

  • Competitors bid on your store name, so you have to pay for branded search to keep your ads at the top. One brand spent $10,000 to $30,000 per month on AdWords and Bing, because organic results alone were not enough [L0790.041].
  • A store with existing brand searches tends to rank in the top three or four organic positions for those searches. For a store that drives traffic from other platforms, Google Ads works well as a brand search and retargeting channel [L0788.023].
  • Social posts often lack clickable links, so shoppers who see an influencer post search for the brand on Google. Branded search ads capture that traffic, and the site's pixel can then collect their email [L0790.042].
  • A branded search ad should send people who search the store name or domain straight to the store as the first result. This keeps returning customers from being pulled away by competitors bidding on the store name [L0373.021].
  • Competitor ads protect the store from the loss of returning customers. Branded campaigns have rarely produced a negative return on ad spend [L0373.010].
  • Brand searches become a growing traffic source as a business matures, especially for high-ticket products with repeat visits (older, 2021-22) [L1391.002].
  • Even when your homepage ranks first organically for your brand name, a competitor's paid ad on that name can push you down. Your own branded search ad keeps the top position and reduces the effect of competitor ads (older, 2021-22) [L1391.003].
  • Brand search is worth running even for stores with only about 3 to 5 or 5 to 10 sales per day. It captures shoppers who already know the store but have not bought yet, so you do not lose them to competitors [L1213.006].
  • Brand searches usually deliver good cost per conversion and rarely become high-traffic campaigns unless the business is very large, so budget risk is low (older, 2021-22) [L1391.010].
  • Brand search is the simplest first Google Ads campaign for a store. It shows the store at the top when people search for the brand or product name and protects it from competitors bidding on that name [L1382.063].

When to Start

Sources disagree on timing. Use the rule that fits your store stage.

Store stage Guidance Source
Beginner, first Google Ads campaign Start with brand search; simple to create, runs on $5 to $10 per day [L1213.005, L1382.063]
Beginner after brand awareness Most effective after brand awareness is built through Meta or TikTok campaigns [L0296.032]
Store with real traffic and profit Run branded search targeting store name and domain [L0373.009, L1436.074]
Competitors start bidding on your name Run the campaign once competitors begin bidding, since that is when they start taking traffic [L0855.027, L1275.044]
  • Brand search campaigns only start after the store is live and profitable, because competitors begin bidding on the store name at that point to take traffic (2023) [L0855.027].
  • Once a store is profitable, start a Google brand search campaign first, so your brand name ranks at the top and competitors cannot take your brand searches [L1436.074].
  • Once profitable, run a search text ad campaign on your store name and domain. Your ad appears first above the organic result for your own name, which keeps competitors from taking brand searches (older, 2019) [L1086.021].
  • Once competitors start bidding on your store name, run a search text ad campaign covering your company name and all its variations (older, 2019) [L1275.044].
  • Early stores can start with brand search at a small budget and no keyword research, since the store name is already known [L0855.028].

Campaign Setup

Naming and structure

  • Name the campaign so it is clearly branded. An example format is "Search - COLD - YourStore.com", which targets searches for the store or brand name. Its goal is to capture those searches before competitors start taking customers who look for the brand [L1121.008].
  • Keep brand and generic terms in separate campaigns. Generic terms like "dropshipping" can bring competitors whose ads appear beside yours (older, 2021-22) [L0719.045].
  • Create one ad group containing your business name as keywords (older, 2021-22) [L1391.007].

Setup A: Source A setting (no year given)

  1. Create a brand name Search campaign.
  2. Set the bid strategy to Impression share, with the target Absolute Top of Page Impressions at 100%.
  3. Set max CPC to $5.
  4. Use phrase match keywords, with quotes around brand variations, for example "Source A merch" and "Source A merch".
  5. Set the URL to the homepage.
  6. Write headlines led by the store name, with a call-to-action and benefits such as free shipping and 30-day returns.
  7. Uncheck the Search network and the Display network.

[L0210.015]

Setup B: Source A 2022 procedure (older, 2022)

  1. Create a search text campaign for your store name so you appear above competitors bidding on your brand.
  2. Later, create product-specific phrase-match campaigns for products that convert well in Google Shopping.
  3. Do not run generic niche-keyword campaigns, because they do not convert.

[L0429.050]

Setup C: Source B procedure (older, 2021-22)

  1. Create a search campaign named with your business name, for example "Brand + Branded + Search + USA".
  2. Create one ad group containing your business name as keywords.
  3. Add a phrase match and an exact match keyword for the business name, plus common variations you can think of.
  4. Set a bid high enough to be competitive. The example used a max CPC of about $1.55.
  5. Write the ad with your business name in Headline 1.
  6. Add a call extension and up to four site-link extensions.
  7. Set a daily budget of around $10 and review performance.

[L1391.007]

Setup notes

  • The brand campaign can be set up in one ad group with a small number of brand keywords (2023) [L0855.006].
  • Setup A and Setup C differ in bid strategy and budget; see Bidding and Budgets below.

Keywords and Match Types

  • For the store name, add two keywords: the store name written without spaces, and the store name as a phrase match in quotation marks with its normal spacing (2023) [L0855.006].
  • Target the store name and domain, not generic product terms [L0373.009].
  • Phrase match keywords with brand variations in quotes are used for the brand name campaign [L0210.015].
  • Add a phrase match and an exact match keyword for the business name, plus variations you can think of (older, 2021-22) [L1391.007].
  • Keyword choice for brand search does not depend on any Google keyword statistic, because it targets the store's own name (2023) [L0855.028].
  • In the observed example, the exact match keyword had a 31.01% CTR and the phrase match keyword about 13.33%. Conversion rates were roughly 1.28% and 2.00% respectively (older, 2020 data) [L1391.009].
  • Exact match converted at about one third of the cost per conversion of phrase match in the same example (older, 2020 data) [L1391.017].

Bidding and Bid Strategy

Two example settings exist. They differ, and each fits a different goal.

Setting Bid strategy Max CPC / bids Use when Source
Impression share (Source A) Target Absolute Top of Page Impressions at 100% Max CPC $5 You need the top position on your own store name at all times [L0210.015]
CPC (enhanced) (Source B, older 2021-22) Enhanced CPC, ad group default Max. CPC US$1.00 Brand keywords bid at US$1.55 You want keyword-level bids that stay competitive on your own brand term [L1391.018]
  • Keyword-level bids can be set separately from the ad group default. The bid should be high enough to stay competitive on your own brand term (older, 2021-22) [L1391.018].
  • In the Source B example with a 1.55 USD max CPC, the observed average CPC was 0.14 USD, because no competitor bid on the brand term. Cost per click stays low when you are the most relevant result for your own brand name (older, 2021-22) [L1391.008].
  • Bidding on a brand search keyword needs only a small budget (2023) [L0855.028].

Budgets

  • Start with a budget of $5 to $10 per day (Source A) [L0373.009].
  • Brand search can run for $5 to $10 per day (Source C) [L1213.005].
  • A daily budget of about $10 is enough for a trademark campaign. It is often not fully spent (older, 2021-22) [L1391.011].
  • The budget can be very small and should scale with how many people search for your name (older, 2019) [L1275.044].
  • Early brand campaigns reached about 5x to 6x ROAS. The budget cannot grow much, because only a small number of people search for a specific brand name [L1213.007].

The $5 to $10 range (Source A, Source C) is the starting point. The $10 daily figure (older, 2021-22) is the upper end for a trademark campaign.

Ad Copy for Brand Defense

General rules

  • The brand ad should lead with the store name, followed by a call-to-action and benefits such as free shipping and 30-day returns [L0210.015].
  • A branded search ad usually gets the highest Quality Score and first position for your own brand term, because the landing page directly matches the searched keyword. It is hard for competitors to outrank you there (older, 2021-22) [L1391.005].
  • Competitors may target your brand name as a keyword in a search text ad, but they may not write your brand name into the ad text, because Google policy forbids that (older, 2021-22) [L1391.001].

Brand-focused responsive search ad template (older, 2021-22)

A brand-focused responsive search ad follows a fixed layout you can adapt to your own store. The first headline (30-character limit) includes your company name, for example in a phrase like 'Browse Our Store: [Company Name]'. The second headline (30-character limit) points to a main advantage, such as free delivery combined with hassle-free returns. The third headline (30-character limit) states a price-match pledge or guarantee, for example 'We'll Undercut Any Price'. Description 1 (90-character limit) reads: 'Looking for the right [product]? Reach out to our staff today.' Description 2 (90-character limit) reads: 'Spot a cheaper offer online? Give us a ring and we will beat it.' Place a call extension under the ad so people on mobile can phone you directly with one tap.

[L1391.014]

Brand-defense ad pattern (older, 2021-22)

  • Put your exact business name in Headline 1, so the searcher immediately sees they are in the right place.
  • Use the remaining headlines and descriptions to present an offer, a guarantee, or a call-to-action aimed at returning visitors who may still be comparing options.
  • Example pattern:
Shop [Brand] Official Store
Free Returns On All Orders
Questions? Call Our Team Today

[L1391.015]

Competitor-conquest ad pattern (older, 2021-22)

  • Target a rival's brand name as a keyword, avoid writing the rival's name in the copy, and lead with a clear differentiator.
  • Example pattern:
Looking For A Better Platform?
Start Your Store With Free Trial
Lower Fees, Faster Setup

[L1391.016]

Extensions

  • A maximum of four site-link extensions can be shown per ad. Choose the four most common collections or products that visitors want to reach directly (older, 2021-22) [L1391.012].
  • Site-link extensions are especially useful for brand-name searches. Searchers often do not know which part of your site they want, and direct links bring them closer to conversion (older, 2021-22) [L1391.013].
  • Add a call extension so mobile users can phone you with one tap (older, 2021-22) [L1391.014].

Competitor Bidding and Trademark Rules

Two positions exist. Apply the one that matches your product's trademark situation and your store's stage.

Position 1: Competitor bidding can be legitimate

  • Bidding on competitor brand names is a legitimate strategy. Check whether a competitor runs an ad on its own brand name. If it does not, you may run a search text ad targeting that name with a compelling offer (older, 2021-22) [L1391.006].
  • If competitors are not running Google Ads, bidding on their brand keywords can capture searchers who would otherwise look for alternatives. Google ads appear above organic results, so the advertiser ranks first [L0637.039].
  • Some operators bid on a large competitor's brand name to capture its searchers, so protecting your own brand term with a brand search campaign is necessary [L1382.077].
  • A seller who does not yet run Google ads can capture search traffic for a larger competitor's brand name by bidding on that name as a search keyword, so the seller's listing appears when shoppers search for the competitor. Check trademark rules before running brand-name keywords this way [L1436.093].

Position 2: Avoid competitor brand bidding; use custom audiences

  • Bidding on competitor brand names gives many clicks but low conversion rates. Brand owners can block that bidding through trademarks. Use competitor names only to build custom audiences, not as bid keywords [L0941.071].
  • Bidding on brand names you are authorized to sell usually converts well and is allowed. Bidding on another company's trademarked brand name is often rejected by Google once the owner files a trademark, and it tends to bring clicks with low conversion. Use competitor names only to build custom audiences [L0941.104].
  • Do not add competitor brand names as keywords in Google Shopping campaigns, because Shopping keywords come from your product pages and feed. Competitor names belong only in a search text campaign, and only for best-selling products after the store is already profitable (older, 2021) [L1090.098].

When each position applies

  • Position 1 applies where the competitor does not advertise on its own name and you hold no trademark conflict. Check the trademark situation first [L1391.006, L1436.093].
  • Position 2 applies where the brand is trademarked by someone else, or where the store is not yet profitable. Use custom audiences in that case [L0941.104, L1090.098].
  • Competitor ads on a product search may target a broader term, such as the product type or a platform keyword, rather than your exact product name. Their ad may use dynamic headline insertion to look more relevant, so their presence does not prove they bid on your exact term (older, 2021-22) [L0719.012].

Competitor ad examples (older, 2021-22)

  • Shopify runs a paid ad on its own brand name. Wix runs a paid ad on the keyword "shopify" without mentioning Shopify in its copy. Shopify's own branded ad keeps it in the first position above Wix's ad [L1391.004].
  • Competitor-conquest pattern is in Ad Copy for Brand Defense above [L1391.016].

Shopping and Brand Restrictions

  • Google Shopping is not keyword-based. A brand that bans bidding on its brand name in search text campaigns does not automatically block Shopping ads. Ask the brand or supplier whether its products may appear on Google Shopping (2023) [L0934.039, L0934.128].
  • A Google Shopping keyword brand term with high traffic but no sales should not be bid aggressively. A brand name often ranks higher than a store for brand-only searches, while product-page keywords can outrank brand sites (2024) [L0856.031].

Brand Contamination and Negative Keywords

  • Brand contamination in a non-brand campaign means branded searches are using the prospecting budget. Use the brand split to decide whether a dedicated brand campaign is needed or whether branded terms must be excluded from prospecting campaigns (Source E, 2025-26) [L0876.023].
  • Own brand and product names should be added as negatives only when branded searches are being forced into a separate dedicated brand campaign. Otherwise they stay out of the negative list (Source E, 2025-26) [L0876.034].
