21.13 Preparing for supplier calls

Why phone beats email

  • Phone communication works better than email because people connect through voice, so a call allows real-time discussion and problem-solving that the back-and-forth chains of email cannot provide. (Older: 2021-22.) [L0235.002]
  • Call suppliers rather than relying on email, because a phone call gets a clear answer far more often. The lesson reports approval rates above 60% when calling suppliers, and email follow-up alone was less effective. (Older: 2021-22.) [L0458.038]
  • Calling gives an immediate yes, no or call-back answer, which speeds up approvals. (Older: 2021.) [L0469.109]
  • If a call is not possible, use email and follow up persistently. (Older: 2021.) [L0469.109]
  • Use phone calls to supplier contacts as the default approach, because emails are easy to ignore and calls build a stronger connection. (2023.) [L0970.155]
  • Call first and email second, because a phone call gets a yes or no faster than email. (Older: 2022.) [L0803.022]
  • Call the supplier first and make your case by phone rather than email, because a call cuts through the noise and gets you to the decision maker faster. [L0928.016]
  • Calling is faster than emailing for getting approved, especially when you need a direct answer. Pair calls with follow-up emails, since many suppliers only respond after a second contact. (Older: 2021.) [L1090.138]
  • Phone calls to suppliers are the most efficient route to approval. (2024.) [L0836.057]

Vetting suppliers

  • Vet a supplier before calling: search the company name for reviews and reputation, and confirm they are sold on competitor sites. The call then only confirms the details. (Older: 2022.) [L0963.079]

Building the call list

  • Keep building the supplier list and collect all contact details during the holiday period. (Older: 2020.) [L0551.020]
  • Build a contact list of at least 20 suppliers before you start calling. Not all of them will approve you from the first call. [L0933.031]
  • Some suppliers need education about the business model, and approval can take many months. (Older: 2018.) [L1085.091]
  • Prepare your own pitch script and expect a dialogue rather than a single call. (Older: 2018.) [L1085.091]
  • Suppliers with strong brands are usually the hardest to get approved. (Older: 2018.) [L1085.092]
  • Starting with silver-tier suppliers and building traffic and sales makes it easier to return to them later. (Older: 2018.) [L1085.092]

Holiday-period plan (older: 2020)

Source procedure, kept complete:

  1. Keep building the supplier list and collect all contact details during the holiday period. [L0551.020]
  2. Finish the demo store so it looks complete before you start calling. [L0551.020]
  3. Start supplier calls on the first Monday after the holidays, when companies return to normal business. [L0551.020]
  4. Call with the supplier sheet ready to go. [L0551.020]

Step 3 is tied to the return to normal business after the holidays. Timing for calls outside that window is covered under Timing and time zones below.

Reviewing the supplier's website

  • Before calling a supplier, review the supplier's own website and speak back to what it highlights about its brands, as if preparing for a job interview. (2023.) [L0855.160]

Timing and time zones

  • All supplier calls must occur during the supplier's business hours, even if this requires calling at inconvenient times from your location. (Older: 2021-22.) [L0235.007]
  • When calling suppliers, call early on weekdays in the supplier's time zone, for example at 8:30 a.m. (2025.) [L1097.042]
  • If no one answers, call back at 9:00 a.m. This was the fastest way to get through. (2025.) [L1097.042]
  • Use a phone-based process for supplier contact: call first thing in the morning and follow up by phone or email. (2023.) [L0835.104]
  • Contacting suppliers early in the day gives you a better chance of reaching the decision maker. (2023.) [L0835.104]
  • Calls to suppliers, especially early in the morning, get through to decision makers more often than emails or calls at busy times of the day. (2023.) [L0970.157]
  • If no replies come in, change the timing and test again. (2023.) [L0970.157]

Where the timing sources differ

  • The 2020 holiday plan starts calls on the first Monday after the holidays. It applies to the post-holiday restart. [L0551.020]
  • The 2023 and 2025 rules recommend early weekday morning calls in the supplier's time zone. They apply to routine calling. [L0835.104, L0970.157, L1097.042]
  • Where the supplier's business hours and your location conflict, the business-hours rule takes priority. [L0235.007]

Mindset before the call

  • Fear of calling suppliers often comes from treating one deal as make-or-break. [L0701.036]
  • Having several options lined up makes each call feel less critical, and the calmer energy that results makes others more willing to work with you without changing your words. [L0701.036]
  • Present yourself as a partner who will bring them sales, not as a pestering retailer. Approach the call with the expectation that they are glad to hear from you. (Older: 2022.) [L0665.111]
  • Supplier calls are normal conversations, not scripted interviews, so questions should be treated as a discussion and not as objections to overcome. [L0933.029]
  • Only some of the questions covered here will come up on any given call. [L0933.029]
  • Mistake: do not overthink supplier questions or read them as rejections. [L0933.030]
  • Preparing answers in advance is useful, but a call should stay natural and confident. [L0933.030]
  • Supplier calls are relaxed, informal conversations rather than hiring interviews, and no salary is offered. (Older: 2021.) [L0968.002]
  • Prepare by learning about the company and expect questions about your business, but treat the call as a conversation rather than a test. (Older: 2021.) [L0968.002]
  • Many new sellers get stuck calling suppliers. (2023.) [L0804.103]
  • Making the calls in a single block, recording notes on each call and starting with the top of the list helps overcome the reluctance. (2023.) [L0804.103]
  • Nervousness with senior decision-makers often comes from seeking their approval as if you had to beg for it. [L1206.045]
  • Ask whether you would physically behave that way in the same situation. If you would not, the posture is a belief you can test and drop. [L1206.045]

Nervousness and accent

  • Accent or non-native English is not a barrier to getting supplier approvals. Suppliers want business relationships that benefit both sides. (Older: 2021.) [L0962.017]
  • When making cold calls to suppliers, a non-native accent or nervousness can be named openly as part of your background, which reduces tension and builds trust. [L0832.057]
  • Presenting this trait honestly often works better than hiding it. [L0832.057]

Rehearsal and coaching practice

  • Rehearse hard tasks like supplier cold calls with a coaching call before doing them alone. [L0832.035]
  • This reduces nervousness and builds the skill over time. [L0832.035]
  • Rehearse the script and practise on less important suppliers first. (2024.) [L0836.057]

Mental rehearsal procedure (Source C)

  1. Before a sales call, list the questions and objections the prospect is most likely to raise. [L1206.044]
  2. For each one, work out a clear, specific answer in your mind. [L1206.044]
  3. Rehearse the full conversation in your imagination, including the prospect's objections and your replies. [L1206.044]
  4. Repeat the rehearsal, especially for difficult objections, until your replies come easily before using them on real calls. [L1206.044]
