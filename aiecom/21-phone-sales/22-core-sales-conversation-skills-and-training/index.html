21.22 Core sales conversation skills and training

Conversation flow and qualification

Run the sales conversation in this order

  1. Get the prospect's attention first. Nothing else works without it. [L0378.037]
  2. Find out about the prospect. Ask which provider or solution they use now, whether they like it, what is missing, and whether they care most about price, quality, or having the best option. [L0378.037]
  3. Present a custom offer that matches what you learned, walking through the options from highest to lowest. [L0378.037]
  4. Close by doing the math for them, starting with the highest figure that fits the argument. [L0378.037]

Qualify prospects

  • Start with people who are unhappy with their current solution. [L0378.038]
  • If they are satisfied, look for one missing feature that could matter to them. If nothing does, move on. [L0378.038]

Build know, like and trust first

  • Build know, like and trust before presenting an offer. Help people first, learn about them, and then move to the offer, because trust makes buyers far more likely to purchase. [L0378.044]
  • Moving a prospect to a second location during a conversation makes the interaction feel longer and builds trust faster. Use this when it fits the context. [L0378.045]
  • A sales call or customer chat should always end with a clear offer or next step. [L1439.043]

Price reveal order

Reveal prices top-down

  • Reveal prices in a fixed order, from the top option down to the cheapest, one step at a time. Showing the cheapest option first removes the chance to anchor on the top price. [L0378.039]
  • Showing the highest price first creates a moment of concern that the buyer cannot afford it. Showing cheaper options afterward releases that tension and makes the buyer more willing to commit. [L0378.040]

Doing the math for the buyer

  • Do the math for the buyer instead of expecting them to calculate. Lay out logically why the purchase makes sense, using figures that are believable and tied to their situation. [L0378.043]
  • Use the following closing template, filling in the placeholders with real figures:
So you pay [ONE-TIME COST] once and add [MONTHLY GAIN] to your bottom line every month. Is that worth it for you?

An extra [MONTHLY GAIN] a month adds up to [ANNUAL GAIN] over a year. If someone offered you [ONE-TIME COST] today in exchange for [ANNUAL GAIN] at the end of the year, what would you say?

[L0378.042]

Objections

Anticipate objections

  • Top closers anticipate objections before they are raised and address them openly. [L0378.046]
  • Highlighting a product's real weaknesses, framed honestly, keeps trust and prevents later doubt. [L0378.046]

Handle objections in person

  1. List every objection the prospect raises on a pad before answering any. [L1370.104]
  2. Keep asking if there is anything else until the prospect has no more objections. [L1370.104]
  3. Answer each objection. [L1370.104]
  4. Ask a conditional closing question, such as whether the prospect will go ahead if all these concerns are resolved. [L1370.104]
  5. Once the prospect agrees, the close is in place. [L1370.104]

Print copy cannot replicate this live feedback, so the letter must answer the full list in advance. [L1370.104]

Emotional buyers

  • Some buyers, such as passionate sports fans, are much less sensitive to price for products tied to their team or interests. Identify the emotional attachment first and build the offer around it. [L0378.041]
  • Sales psychology matters. Understanding emotions, impulse and how people decide is the core of online selling, and studying human behaviour helps a store owner predict how a customer will respond. [L1440.056]
  • Customers, not payment processors or platforms, pay the business, so the owner must understand how people think and decide to buy. Understanding persuasion principles helps predict what people will spend money on. [L1513.016]

Composure and not appearing desperate

  • Sales conversations work best when the seller is not desperate for the deal, because the hardest deal is the one you visibly need. Taking a position of not needing the deal removes pressure and keeps buyers engaged. [L1370.105]

Training and role-play drills

Train sales skills in this order

  1. Role-play different customer types, each with a different objection. [L0378.047]
  2. Drill the pricing sequence and numbers until they come out in the right order without thinking. [L0378.047]
  3. Keep a fixed close and offer block on printed cards for field use. [L0378.047]
  4. Improve the opening and the middle of each pitch, which is where creativity matters most. [L0378.047]

Reference case: a commission-based field seller

  • Starting point: a new salesperson sold TV subscriptions face to face, earning about $125 per installation, with the commission paid only when the service was installed. [L0378.048]
  • The first week was used to learn a four-step process. [L0378.048]
  • In week two, the salesperson moved from a busy discount warehouse to a quieter electronics store, where meeting buyers in aisles allowed building a relationship before pitching. [L0378.048]
  • He asked about current providers, identified what buyers wanted, presented options from the highest price down, and closed with a clear calculation. [L0378.048]

Books and learning by doing

  • Read recommended books on sales and persuasion. Dale Carnegie's How to Win Friends and Influence People is recommended for handling clients, and Alex Hormozi's 100 Million Offers for building offers. [L0440.059]
  • Robert Cialdini's Influence is recommended for learning persuasion principles. [L1513.016]
  • Time spent in calls and real-world conversations is the most effective way to learn sales, because the mistakes made in calls show what to do better next time. [L0440.060]

Written sales presentations

  • Sell in print the way you sell in person. A sales letter is a sales presentation on paper, so write it as if speaking directly to one prospect at a kitchen table or living room floor. [L1370.106]
  • Invite an immediate phone call and tie it to a specific time the reader can act on before the deadline. A time-limited request removes the delay of a letter's slow reply. [L1369.111]
  • Formula: invite an immediate phone call tied to a specific time that the reader can act on before the deadline. [L1369.111]
  • Examples:
    • "Call me before noon today, and I can have your order confirmed and the first shipment booked for tomorrow." [L1369.111]
    • "Call the office before 5 this afternoon, and we will hold the discounted rate for your team until Friday." [L1369.111]
