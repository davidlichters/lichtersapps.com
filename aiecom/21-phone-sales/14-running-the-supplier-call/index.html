21.14 Running the supplier call

Before the call

  • Prepare a short script before the first call. The conversation feels less daunting when you have one. [L0771.054] (older advice, 2021-22)
  • Keep the core call simple: pick up the phone, introduce yourself and your business, build a little rapport, and ask whether you can sell their products through your website. [L0771.054] (older advice, 2021-22)
  • Plan the framework, not the words. Know the purpose of each part (introduction, planned launch, reason for choosing them, request for the application process) and phrase it in your own words on each call. [L0928.017]
  • Suppliers are busy and receive more requests as e-commerce grows, so the call must be ready before you dial. [L0929.004] (older advice, 2021-22)

Reaching the right person

  • Call the supplier using the phone number on their website to start the approval conversation. [L1085.088] (older advice, 2018)
  • Use this first-contact procedure:
    1. Introduce yourself briefly and say you want the person who handles working with online retailers. [L0968.001] (older advice, 2021)
    2. Wait for their reply. [L0968.001] (older advice, 2021)
    3. If they are not that person, ask who you should speak to. In very small companies the owner often answers and is the right contact. [L0968.001] (older advice, 2021)
  • When calling a supplier for an account or referral, mention the existing supplier relationship and ask for the specific contact who handles approvals. [L0468.178] (older advice, 2020)
  • Use this procedure for account requests (Source B):
    1. Call the supplier's phone line and ask for the person who handles new retail or dealer accounts. [L0458.037] (older advice, 2021-22)
    2. Introduce yourself and your business, and say that you have a website built for their products. [L0458.037] (older advice, 2021-22)
    3. Ask whether you can sell their products through your website and whether they drop-ship to customers. [L0458.037] (older advice, 2021-22)
    4. Ask for the online dealer application or product information, and note the name and direction given. [L0458.037] (older advice, 2021-22)
    5. Send a follow-up email after the call to confirm the conversation, and record the date and outcome in your tracker. [L0458.037] (older advice, 2021-22)
  • When you get no decision maker, ask for a sales representative. Keep the call short and make sure your message is clear each time. [L0941.097]
  • Handle a gatekeeper this way (older advice, 2021-22) [L0929.013]:
    1. Briefly state that you want to talk about becoming a retailer or reseller for their products. [L0929.013]
    2. Let them direct you to the right person, such as the sales contact who handles new retailer requests. If that person is not available, ask for their mobile number. [L0929.013]
    3. If you reach the right person, let them tell you what they need next and respond to that. [L0929.013]
    4. Do not give a long pitch to a gatekeeper, since they cannot approve you. [L0929.013]
  • Supplier approval calls should explain why the niche fits the supplier's brand, ask about their products, and ask who handles approvals. [L1111.166] (older advice, 2020)

Opening the call

Use this opening script, filling in the placeholders (Source B, older advice, 2021-22):

Hi, it's [Your name] calling from [Your company name]. How are you?
[Wait for their reply, then continue:] We are an online retailer of [your niche or product category]. We have looked at your products, we like them, and we would like to talk with you about becoming a retailer for them.
[Stop talking and let them respond. Do not fill the silence.]

[L0929.012]

Explaining the model without the word dropshipping

  • Avoid the word dropshipping on calls because some people have negative views of it. Describe the model in plain terms such as direct-to-customer or warehouse-direct-to-customer instead. [L0929.015] (older advice, 2021-22)
  • Some suppliers avoid working with dropshippers because of bad experiences. Do not use the term in the first contact. Present yourself as an additional sales channel that brings new customers. [L1085.090] (older advice, 2018)
  • Use this model explanation, filling in the placeholders (Source B, older advice, 2021-22):
Say: Look, we're an online-only retailer and we work on a direct-to-customer basis. If you are at your computer, you can have a quick look at [your website] while we talk. Here is how it works: we build and maintain our website, upload your products to it, handle all the marketing and customer service, and generate the sales. When we make a sale, we send you an order for that product, we pay for it upfront, and you ship it directly from your warehouse to the customer.
[Stop talking after this and let them respond.]

[L0929.014]

  • Spelling out the model prevents confusion, shows you will not buy in bulk, and sets the basis for the request. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0929.016]
  • Mentioning upfront payment in that explanation lowers the supplier's risk immediately. A zero-risk offer is the easiest way to win agreement. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0929.016]
  • The same call approach works in any country; it is not specific to one market. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0929.011]

Stating the launch date

Sources give slightly different windows. Use the table to choose:

Source Recommended window Upper limit Note
Source A, pitching a new store [L0928.019] Roughly two to four weeks away, or up to about one month Never more than two months A longer timeline makes the supplier treat the store as not relevant right now
Source B, opening call (older advice, 2021-22) [L0929.017] About one month away None stated Launching a few days earlier or later is fine
Source A, script guidance [L0928.017] A few weeks to about one month ahead Never more than two months Applies to the window the supplier should expect
  • Use a real launch date, not a vague one. [L0928.019]
  • Use the window in the table above; the shorter windows fit most calls, and the two-month ceiling is the hard limit. [L0928.017, L0928.019, L0929.017]
  • If the store is almost ready, describe it as being in its final touches before launch, not in beta testing. [L0855.161]
  • Present the store's future potential, not its current results. Do not present a store as having sales or achievements it does not yet have. (older advice, 2020) [L1111.167]

Positioning a store with no business history

  • If this is the first store, say so honestly. Then explain the niche opportunity, the search demand, the brand quality, and why you want to sell that supplier's products. [L0855.159]
  • Use the pronoun I when you run the business alone, instead of we. Use whatever you are comfortable with, but do not claim a team you do not have. (older advice, 2022) [L0803.091]

Checking US distribution

Use this script for a supplier with unknown US distribution (Source A, older advice, 2022). Replace the brackets and keep the question about shipping:

Hello, my name is [name] from [store name]. I noticed your products are listed on many US websites. Do you have distribution in the United States, a warehouse, or a fulfillment partner, and how does your shipping to US customers work?

[L0854.108]

Building rapport

  • Ask casual questions about the brand's history, goals, and best products. These questions build a relationship and give you data for your marketing. (older advice, 2021) [L0801.163]
  • Build a relationship rather than pitching only price. (older advice, 2020) [L1111.166]

Making the first request

  • Your only request at this point is the product data: the price list, product images, and any useful technical specifications. Ask how soon they can send it to you. [L0929.018]
  • Accept any delivery method for the product data, such as a shared cloud folder link, emailed files, a login to a dealer portal, or downloading images from their website. Confirm you will get access; do not insist on a format. [L0929.019] (older advice, 2021-22)

Tone

  • Sound genuinely excited. A flat or disinterested tone makes the supplier less likely to approve you, but do not overdo it. [L0928.006]
  • Stay calm, friendly, and respectful even if the supplier does not say what you hoped. Treat them with the same courtesy you would expect yourself. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0929.005]

Brevity

  • Get to the point quickly and say as little as needed. Long rambling calls and long emails waste the supplier's time and weaken your chances. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0929.004]
  • Keep the sales call short, concise, and tight. Follow the framework by introducing yourself and your company, then stating which brand you want to carry. [L0936.007]
  • When calling suppliers, keep the call short and make sure your message is clear each time. [L0941.097]

Using scripts as guides

  • Scripts are guidelines, not text to read aloud. Use the same key points on every call, phrased naturally. [L0928.017, L0928.018]
  • Reading a supplier script word for word sounds unnatural and makes the call feel scripted. [L0928.018]
  • Use the same framework in your own words instead of reading a call script word for word, which sounds unnatural and robotic to suppliers. [L0936.008]
  • Use the script as a guide, not word for word, but cover the same key points concisely. Let the supplier do most of the talking, hear what they need, and then respond to it. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0929.033]
  • Use the supplier call script as a guide and speak naturally. Keep the questions consistent across calls. (older advice, 2022) [L0963.080]

After a yes

Follow this procedure (older advice, 2021-22):

  1. Thank them and confirm when they will send the product data. [L0929.021]
  2. Exchange contact details if not already recorded. [L0929.021]
  3. Record the outcome in your supplier sheet. [L0929.021]
  4. Follow up if the data does not arrive by the date they promised. [L0929.021]
  5. Move on to the next call. [L0929.021]
