21.17 Other supplier channels: forms, email, events and in-person pitches

Online dealer forms and follow-up

Older advice, 2021-2022 (Source B) and 2022 (Source A). Check against current supplier practice before use.

  1. Submit the online form on the supplier's website. [L0235.009]
  2. Wait 24 hours. [L0235.009]
  3. If you receive no response, make a phone call. [L0235.009]

When following up by phone after a form, use this opening (older source, 2021-2022):

I'm John from Oz Off Grid. I submitted your online application form about a day ago and wanted to reach out to follow up and discuss it with your team.

[L0235.010]

Start supplier contact with the online dealer application form when it is available, and call during free time. The online form takes less time and can be completed in the evening. (Older source, 2022.) [L0665.113]

Form or phone first: when each applies

These two sources give different first steps. Both are older (2021-2022). Use the phone when a number is available, and the form when calls are not possible.

Situation First step Source
Online form available, you can call in free time Form first, then call after 24 hours if no response [L0665.113, L0235.009]
Phone number available Call first, because direct conversation is more effective than email [L0665.114]
Calls not possible Fill out the online form [L0665.114]

Call a supplier rather than only submitting an online form. A call lets you explain your store, ask for the dealer application, and get the contact moving quickly. (Older source, 2021.) [L0801.161]

Introduction emails

When first contacting a supplier by email (older source, 2022), a short introduction is enough to get a referral to the right contact if it:

  • names your store,
  • says you want to sell their products, and
  • asks for the person who opens accounts for internet retailers. [L0969.116]

Pitching in person or on a call

Older source, 2018. Follow these steps:

  1. Think about two things first: what the supplier cares about and how they can trust you. [L1085.086]
  2. Be honest about where your store is. You can say you are learning from a training program and are just getting started. [L1085.086]
  3. Explain the marketing you will use to bring them sales: Google Product Listing Ads, retargeting, exit-intent capture, a follow-up email sequence, YouTube and industry-specific platforms. [L1085.086]
  4. Make clear that customer service is your first priority and that you are building a long-term business. [L1085.086]
  5. Keep the focus on how you will bring the supplier more sales, not on the money you expect to make. [L1085.086]

Use this opening for a first meeting, adapted to your situation (older source, 2018):

I appreciate you meeting with me. I am [your name] and I am starting an online store focused on [niche]. I am still early in the process and I want to be honest about that. I am learning e-commerce from [training program or mentor name], and I plan to bring you more sales by using [traffic channel 1] and [traffic channel 2] to reach new customers. Customer service is my first priority, and I want to build a business that customers come back to for years.

[L1085.087]

Trade events

Older source, 2020. Follow these steps:

  1. Attend supplier trade events and meet the representatives in person. [L1111.168]
  2. Explain why your store is a good fit. [L1111.168]
  3. Ask questions about their products. [L1111.168]
  4. Identify the person who can make approval decisions. [L1111.168]
  5. Follow up with a short email after the conversation. [L1111.168]

Competitor calls

Calling a competitor by phone is a quick way to check whether it has a showroom or store. Ask for the opening hours and whether a visit is possible. (Older source, 2020.) [L0551.021]
