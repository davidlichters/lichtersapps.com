21.16 Supplier follow-up cadence and record keeping

Day-by-day contact plan

  • Start with a phone call on day one whenever the supplier has a phone number. Calls were the quickest route to a yes compared with waiting for email replies. [L0936.005]
  • The day one call has two goals: find out the supplier's application process, and identify the decision maker at the company. [L0936.006]
  • Day three of the contact plan is another phone call. [L0936.012]
  • The full sequence runs to day seven as a one-week follow-up plan that mixes calls and emails. [L0936.012]
  • Keep following up using the plan until you get a clear yes or no. You can stop early if you reach the right person on the first call. [L0936.013]
  • Do not stop outreach after one unanswered call or email. Suppliers often respond only after repeated follow-ups, so continue through the full sequence. [L0936.014]

Call first, then email: sequencing

  • Call the phone number listed for the supplier first. [L0929.002]
  • Send a follow-up email confirming what you discussed. [L0235.004]
  • When a supplier does not answer your emails, call instead, because a phone call is the most reliable way to be heard and to get a point of contact. [L1110.006]
  • Older advice (2019).
  • Procedure for an email that went unanswered:
    1. Call the supplier. [L1110.006]
    2. Mention the earlier email and roughly when you sent it. [L1110.006]
    3. Follow the call script from the course. [L1110.006]
  • Keep following up by phone and leave voicemails, because many brands return calls later. [L0857.068]
  • If a pitch script gets no results, make it specific by explaining how you will drive sales for the brand and how you will handle its customer service. [L0857.068]

Repeated calls for unanswered suppliers

  • Procedure for a supplier that has not replied:
    1. Keep calling if nobody answers, since a persistent caller usually reaches someone. [L0803.023]
    2. If there is no reply after a few days, send a follow-up in the same email thread so the messages stack. [L0803.023]
    3. If still no response, find supplier relations staff on LinkedIn and contact them there. [L0803.023]
  • Older advice (2022).
  • When suppliers do not answer calls, keep following up. Suppliers with limited stock may later accept new dealers, so approved products should be uploaded while contact continues. [L0963.078]
  • Older advice (2022).
  • Supplier approvals often need repeated calls because companies get busy. Keep following up by phone and log each conversation's notes in a tracking sheet. [L1279.085]
  • Older advice (2021).

Follow-up scripts

Follow-up call for a supplier that has not replied

Hi, this is [your name] from [your store name]. I sent an email yesterday to the dealer contact at [supplier domain] about becoming a retailer for [brand name], and I just wanted to make sure you received it. Would you have a few minutes to talk about how we could work together and what the next steps are for approval?

[L0470.020]

Follow-up call for a supplier that said it is too busy

Hi [Supplier contact name], this is [your name] from [store name]. We spoke [timeframe] ago and you asked me to follow up. Our store launched on [date] and we are now showing up for the main keywords in [niche] on Google Shopping. We are selling for [brand A], [brand B] and [brand C], and business is going well. We would like to add you to our lineup so you get this exposure too. When would be a good time to talk?

[L0665.112]

Older advice (2022).

Follow-up call on an application

Hi [supplier contact name], this is [your name] from [store name] calling about your application. I wanted to follow up on [previous step, e.g. the application sent on date]. We have [new progress, e.g. grown traffic and a larger email list] and are excited to list your products. What do I need to do next to move forward, and when can we get started?

[L1279.084]

Older advice (2021).

Hesitant suppliers and callback dates

  • Procedure for a supplier who is hesitant but has not said no:
    1. Do not push for an immediate decision. [L0929.022]
    2. Ask them to think it over for a few days. [L0929.022]
    3. Agree on a specific callback day and say you will call then. [L0929.022]
    4. Send a follow-up email confirming the conversation and including your contact details. [L0929.022]
    5. Note the callback date in your supplier sheet and in your calendar. [L0929.022]
    6. Call back on that date. If they ask you to end the call, end it respectfully and move on. [L0929.022]
  • Older advice (2021-22).
  • Persistence pays. A supplier who believes you will call back is more likely to decide, and a follow-up email keeps you in front of them. Always make the callback you promised. [L0929.024]
  • Older advice (2021-22).

Final offers and follow-up dates

  • When a supplier declines, make a polite final offer and ask for a follow-up date. Recording that date in your notes gives you a reason to return with results. [L0801.162]
  • Older advice (2021).

Notes and tracking sheets

  • Keep notes during or right after every supplier call. You will make many calls and cannot remember what each supplier said, and calling back to ask again makes you look unprofessional. [L0929.003]
  • Older advice (2021-22).
  • Procedure for each supplier call:
    1. Record the date of your request call. [L0929.002]
    2. Record what they said about supplying their products, their pricing and anything else specific to them, during or right after the call. [L0929.002]
    3. Record the name of the person you spoke to; this person becomes your contact. [L0929.002]
    4. Record any direct email, direct line or mobile number they give you for follow-up. [L0929.002]
    5. Mark the supplier as Approved (Yes) or not (No). [L0929.002]
    6. Move on to the next supplier. [L0929.002]
  • Older advice (2021-22).
  • Log each conversation's notes in a tracking sheet. [L1279.085]
  • Older advice (2021).
