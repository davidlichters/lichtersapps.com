21.1 Deciding whether phone sales belong in your store

Price bands where calls matter

Price level What to expect Source ids
Up to about $1,000 without phone capacity Keep the average price in a lower range, around $1,000. This is older advice from 2019 and 2021. L0356.010, L1112.071
About $1,000 to $2,000 Products generally convert with phone support, so have a team or staff ready to answer or return calls quickly. L0667.073
About $1,000 to $2,000 (listing) High-ticket items above this range are worth listing, because a short phone call can close the sale. L0934.108
Above about $1,500 to $3,000 Expect many inbound calls that check the business is legitimate before buyers order. L0667.073
Above roughly $2,000 Many buyers call before ordering, so expect inbound sales calls. List these products and return calls quickly. L1081.018, L0934.184
  • The sources give different thresholds, so apply the one that matches your situation. The 2025 source puts conversion with phone support at $1,000 to $2,000 and expects heavy inbound calls above $1,500 to $3,000 [L0667.073]. The 2023 sources put the listing threshold at $1,000 to $2,000 [L0934.108] and at roughly $2,000 [L0934.184]. Older advice from 2019 and 2021 points to around $1,000 as the ceiling without phone support [L0356.010, L1112.071].
  • Use the lower end of each range if you are unsure whether you can handle calls, and the higher end only when you have capacity to answer them [L0667.073, L0934.108, L0934.184].

Phone as a trust signal

  • A visible phone number tells customers they could call if needed, even when few customers actually call [L0359.105].
  • Phone calls matter for trust even when few customers call, and the few who call usually buy [L0359.105].
  • Keep a working phone number on the store and answer customer calls promptly, because phone responsiveness affects conversions for higher-priced items. This is older advice from 2020 [L0468.176].

Inbound calls as a buying signal

  • A phone call on the store is usually a buying signal, not a waste of time [L0910.007].
  • Over about 2.5 years, more than 90 percent of calls either led to a sale or came from existing customers [L0910.007].
  • Many shoppers call a store before buying, and those who get an answer tend to buy from that store [L0966.095].
  • Answering the phone quickly and reliably is a competitive advantage [L0966.095].
  • Answering the phone or returning calls generates significant sales volume that competitors miss. Customers are often surprised that a human answers and frequently place orders as a result. For established businesses, this represents millions in annual revenue [L0251.027].
  • Phone calls before purchase are a normal part of selling products above about $1,500 to $3,000, because buyers check that the business is real [L0667.074].
  • Calls decrease as the store builds reviews and credibility over time [L0667.074].

Capacity you need before taking calls

  • A store with high-priced products needs someone who can answer customer phone calls. Without that capacity, keep the average price in a lower range [L0356.010].
  • For high-ticket products, the store owner should be willing to handle phone calls and extra emails, since that is part of how higher order values are closed [L0359.106].
  • Only enter the $1,500 to $3,000 price range if you are willing to take these calls [L0667.074].
  • Return calls from high-ticket buyers quickly [L0934.108, L0934.184].
  • Respond to or call back high-ticket callers quickly [L0934.108].

High-ticket offers and live selling

  • Changing the selling environment is needed for high-ticket offers, because few people will buy a very expensive product from a sales letter alone [L0523.064].
  • Move high-ticket offers to a phone call, direct mail or a live event [L0523.064].
  • An 8,000 USD buy button is unlikely to be clicked from an email, while a booked phone appointment makes the sale far more likely [L0523.064].
  • A phone conversation gives live feedback, so the salesperson can answer objections and help the prospect decide on the spot [L0523.065].
  • Listeners pay closer attention to a voice than to a page [L0523.065].
  • Use phone sales for offers that are too costly to close online [L0523.065].

Decision: phone work or lower prices

  1. Check whether you can answer and return calls quickly and reliably. If yes, you can consider products in the higher bands above $1,000 [L0667.073, L0966.095, L1112.071].
  2. If you cannot, keep prices in the lower band around $1,000 and do not move into high-ticket niches [L0356.010, L1112.071].
  3. If you want a hands-off business with no phone sales, do not pick niches with an average price above roughly $2,000 [L1081.018].
  4. If you enter the $1,500 to $3,000 range, expect calls from buyers checking legitimacy and accept that they decline as reviews grow [L0667.073, L0667.074].
