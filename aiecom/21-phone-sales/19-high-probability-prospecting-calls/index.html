21.19 High Probability prospecting calls

Where prospecting fits in the approach

  • The approach has three phases, in this order: High Probability Prospecting, High Probability Selling, and High Probability Closing. [L0770.074]
    • Prospecting: make a short phone contact with a clearly stated offer, let prospects disqualify themselves, and set a meeting only with someone who wants the offer and intends to buy. [L0770.074]
    • Selling: first build trust and respect in a relationship phase, then ask the discovery and disqualification questions in fixed order, letting the prospect end the process at any point. [L0770.074]
    • Closing: define the prospect's Conditions of Satisfaction, obtain a commitment to buy if they are met, and only then present a proposal or demonstration. [L0770.074]
  • There are usually far more prospects than a sales team can visit, but at any given time only a small share are ready to buy. Contact as many prospects as possible, briefly, to find that small group, and spend meeting time only on them. [L0770.076]

Writing the opening offer

  • Keep the offer under 45 words, including greeting and introduction. Write it in your own words so that it makes sense to you. [L0770.013]
  • Deliver it in a neutral, unemotional tone, because a convincing tone creates resistance even when the words are right. [L0770.013]
  • Benchmarks from practice:
    • One seller's offer went through three rounds of trial calls and settled at 34 words. It was later sharpened further based on prospect responses. [L0770.014]
    • Another seller cut a 59-word offer to 42 words and saw better results. Shorter offers generally perform better. [L0770.014]
  • Opening offer template for a cold call, filled in and kept under 45 words:
Hello, this is [your name] from [company name]. We [concrete description of what you provide, with one specific benefit or measurable result, e.g. the product type and what it does for the buyer]. Is that something you want for [the prospect's product line, business or situation]?

Deliver it in a neutral tone and take the prospect's answer as it comes. [L0770.015]

  • Formula for a cold-call offer:
    1. Name yourself and your company.
    2. State concretely what you provide.
    3. Add one specific benefit, coverage detail, or measurable result.
    4. Ask a yes-or-no question about whether the prospect wants it. [L0770.016]
  • The question keeps the prospect in control and makes a clear no easy. [L0770.016]
  • Own examples of the formula:
    • This is Mark from Cleanline Office Services. We clean commercial kitchens after hours, which in our clients' experience cuts grease-related inspection failures roughly in half. Is that something you want for your kitchen? [L0770.016]
    • This is Lena from Pressgrid Print. We produce custom labels in runs of 500 to 5,000 with a proof sent the same day. Is that something you want for your product line? [L0770.016]

Running the call

  • Follow this sequence on a High Probability prospecting call: [L0770.012]
    1. State your offer clearly in no more than 45 words, counting the greeting and your introduction. [L0770.012]
    2. Check whether the prospect wants what you are offering. [L0770.012]
    3. If he says yes, ask whether he would like to set up a meeting aimed at doing business. [L0770.012]
    4. If he is not ready at that point, book a new call for a time he chooses, and get his commitment to move forward then. [L0770.012]
    5. End the call on any negative answer. Arrange a callback only if he states he will be a qualified prospect at a specific later time, and make clear that it is completely fine for him to say no whenever he hesitates. [L0770.012]
    6. Disqualify anyone who does not want your offer or will not buy it from you. [L0770.012]
  • Two signs that you have slipped into selling on a prospecting call: you feel stress during the call, or the call runs longer than three to four minutes. When either happens, stop pitching and return to asking questions. [L0770.018]

Qualifying questions

  • Collect the following from the prospect before any appointment, in this order: [L0770.019]
    1. Does he want this product?
    2. Does he want to arrange an appointment?
    3. If not now, when?
    4. If not now, does he want you to check back later?
    5. Why does he want to meet at this time?
    6. What would he like to accomplish at the meeting?
  • Add further disqualifying questions when the situation calls for them. [L0770.019]

Handling vague and negative answers

  • If a prospect answers with "that sounds interesting", "maybe", "I don't know", "I don't think so" or "I'll consider it", reply in a flat tone: [L0770.020]
Does that mean you want what I'm offering, or not?
  • Treat a no as a clear answer and close the call politely. Do not argue, add benefits, or sell harder. [L0770.020]
  • If a prospect asks a question you cannot answer well yet, such as why he should switch when he is happy with his current supplier, use one of these replies in a flat tone: [L0770.022]
I don't have a satisfactory answer to that yet. What answer would make sense to you?
It doesn't sound like this is a good fit for you. Is my offer something you want, or not?

Choose the reply that fits the situation and let the prospect answer. [L0770.022]

  • A statement that does not answer your question, such as "I'm not interested", is noise rather than a real answer. Do not respond with a sales pitch. [L0770.023]
    • Ask a clarifying question he can answer, such as whether he is the person who decides on this, and keep the conversation on your questions. [L0770.023]
    • Disqualify him only when he clearly says he does not need, want, or can afford the offer. [L0770.023]
  • Interest has no sales value because it is not a commitment. Enthusiasm and direct, quick answers are better signs of a qualified prospect than polite interest. [L0770.024]
  • Ask yes-or-no questions that include the option of no, such as whether something is what he wants or not. A clear option to decline reduces resistance and makes the prospect responsible for his own choice. [L0770.025]

Information requests and materials

  • If a prospect asks you to send information, reply in a neutral tone: [L0770.021]
When someone asks for information, it often means the conversation is ending politely. If that is your intention, just tell me and that's fine. If you do want the details, tell me what you would need to decide so I can see whether it makes sense to send them.
  • Send materials only when the prospect wants the offer, needs the information, and is likely to buy after reviewing it. [L0770.021]
  • Do not send unsolicited brochures or samples to cold prospects. Keep unsolicited mail for a mass mailing sent two or three times a year, and send individual material only to prospects who have confirmed interest. [L0770.069]

Finding the right contact

  • When you need the right person at a company, first ask the front desk for whoever handles the product area, such as packaging design. [L0770.067]
    • Many switchboards only release the marketing or sales manager's name. Ask for that person and let them point you to the correct contact. [L0770.067]
    • If you still cannot reach the decision-maker, the sales department is a usable alternative route. [L0770.067]
  • In companies with several product lines, each line's product manager often owns packaging or purchasing decisions for that line. Identify the manager of each line separately instead of relying on one central contact. [L0770.068]

Scheduling the meeting

  • Never try to sell the appointment. Ask whether he wants to meet to see if your offer fits his needs. Then let him propose the time, and ask him what he wants to accomplish, since a prospect who does not commit to a meeting has disqualified himself. [L0770.017]
  • Scheduling a sales meeting by phone: [L0770.070]
    1. Set a tentative date for the meeting, usually a few weeks ahead if the prospect names that timing. [L0770.070]
    2. Ask what the meeting should accomplish and whether the decision-maker should attend. [L0770.070]
    3. Call the day before to confirm the time and the attendees. [L0770.070]
    4. If the prospect does not confirm, treat the appointment as disqualified and do not chase it. [L0770.070]

Ending a call with a disqualified prospect

  • Close politely and do not argue. Say you will only call again if new developments fit his stated needs, and do not try to convert him. [L0770.081]
