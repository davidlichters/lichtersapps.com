21.7 Selling high-ticket and B2B buyers by phone

Qualifying high-ticket buyers

  • Use this sequence on a qualified lead call for products with an average order value of $5,000 or more [L0268.044, L0268.071]:

    1. Ask why they want the product, where it will go, when they need it, and what they will use it for [L0268.044, L0268.071].
    2. Decide whether they are a real buyer by checking for a confirmed need and a real timeline [L0268.044, L0268.071].
    3. Offer service enhancements such as expedited delivery or white glove delivery instead of discounts [L0268.044, L0268.071].
    4. Show that you want to earn the business through service, not price cuts [L0268.044, L0268.071].
    5. If call volume becomes unmanageable, hire a VA to handle calls [L0268.044].

  • Expect more pre-sale questions and inbound calls for high-ticket items, such as those priced at $10,000 and above or at $1,000 to $2,000. These products can still be sold, but expect more contact before the sale [L0808.043].

  • Before pitching a high-ticket B2B lead, qualify in this order [L1315.063]:

    Step Question Purpose
    1 Who holds the authority over the budget? Identify the economic buyer
    2 What is the need the buyer is trying to meet? Establish the need
    3 Where are they now? Establish the current state
    4 Where do they want to be? Establish the target state
    5 What is the impact of not reaching that goal? Establish urgency and cost of inaction

Avoiding discount tactics

  • Do not use loss-leader pricing or a discount-focused strategy for products of $5,000 or more [L0268.084].
  • Discounts signal desperation to customers with serious buying intent [L0268.084].
  • Focus on service enhancements such as white glove delivery and expedited shipping [L0268.084, L0268.044].
  • Verify genuine customer need and timeline, and show business credibility [L0268.084].
  • Price questions from buyers often close the sale, but this requires constant commitment and fast responses. Older advice from 2019 (Source A) states that high-ticket products of about $5,000 and above can sell well when someone answers live chat and the phone quickly and responsively [L1110.031].

Phone as a sales channel

  • Selling by voice on the phone is an effective way to close high-ticket sales. The chance of a sale rises significantly when customers can speak to the business [L1371.039].
  • Plan staff capacity for phone sales before entering the high-ticket or bulk segment. Calls will reveal product questions that you can then answer on the website [L1277.114].

Concierge-style selling

  • High-ticket buyers are often over 50 and sometimes in their 60s or 70s. They value personal attention and clear answers to their questions. A concierge-style approach works best with this group [L1472.044].
  • Concierge-style phone sale for an expensive product [L1472.046]:
    1. Answer every question the buyer has about the product, and pay attention to their specific needs [L1472.046].
    2. Create a draft order in your system and send it to the customer, so they can pay without going through the normal checkout for the first order [L1472.046, L1472.047].
    3. For repeat orders, keep the conversation going by email, text or voice memos, and send another draft order when they are ready [L1472.046, L1472.065].
    4. Keep the buyer updated on any back-order delays until the product arrives [L1472.046].
  • A personal concierge approach can turn a single high-ticket buyer into a steady source of revenue over time. Draft orders avoid friction in checkout for buyers who prefer a personal sale [L1472.047].

Case: concierge sale with draft orders

  • A store sold products priced between $2,000 and $9,000, with the most expensive variant at $9,000 [L1472.048].
  • One buyer called with many questions about her purchase. The store answered each one attentively and sent a draft order, so she did not have to go through checkout for the first order [L1472.048].
  • Two months later she placed a second order after only a few emails of prior contact, with no draft order needed. Over those two months she brought in about $13,000 in sales [L1472.048].
  • A separate $9,000 order was back-ordered for three months. The store kept her updated on the status until it was delivered [L1472.048].

Multi-person B2B buying

  • High-ticket B2B purchases involve several people [L1315.064]:

    Role Description
    Economic buyer Controls the money
    Lead decision maker or product owner Leads the decision on the product
    Functional buyers For example, head of purchasing

  • Get in front of the key decision maker and build relationships with the others [L1315.064].

  • Expect the buyer to need strong proof and security for the purchase [L1315.064].

Long sales cycles

  • High-ticket products can require around 20 touch points before purchase. Expect a long sales cycle and plan a sequence of follow-ups rather than expecting a sale after one contact [L0949.033].

Direct mobile numbers and repeat business

  • Giving a high-value buyer your direct mobile number can turn a one-time purchase into repeat business. The buyer may later text or send voice messages to request a new draft order [L1472.065].
  • Accept the channel the buyer prefers, including voice memos, and respond reliably [L1472.065].
