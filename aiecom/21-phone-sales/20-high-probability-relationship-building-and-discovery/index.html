21.20 High Probability relationship building and discovery

Relationship phase: purpose and sequence

  • Do not discuss your product during relationship building. Your purpose is to learn who the prospect is, what motivates him and how he reached his current role, so you can decide whether you trust and respect him. If you do not trust him, leave politely, even if you cannot yet say why. [L0770.030]
  • The relationship phase usually happens in the first meeting, and there is no fixed script or question list. The point is to let the conversation produce the information you need, so the exact questions depend on what the prospect says. [L0770.035]
  • Prospects also judge the salesperson during relationship building, and trust shows through how you behave rather than what you say. A relationship of trust and respect gives you an advantage over competitors, because most buyers prefer to deal with people they trust. [L0770.034]
  • Do not go on with a long relationship phase when the prospect has not yet shown trust or respect. Leaving early costs little, while investing time in someone you do not trust wastes the rest of the process. [L0770.045]

When the prospect wants to skip the relationship phase

Use this in a flat, unemotional tone when a prospect in the relationship phase wants to jump straight into business:

Before we talk about my product, I like to learn how my customers operate, because that tells me how to serve them. I also need to find out whether I trust and respect the people I work with, and you have the same chance to judge me. So, are you willing to continue this kind of conversation, or would you rather I leave?

If he chooses to leave, end the visit politely and treat him as a low-probability prospect. [L0770.071]

Opening the relationship

  • To open a relationship, make a neutral observation about something visible or known, such as the building, the location or the company, and attach a question to it. Then base each follow-up on the prospect's last answer rather than a fixed list. [L0770.031]
    • Example: The loading area here is unusually wide. Was it built for this kind of product flow? [L0770.031]
    • Example: You mentioned the new site opened last spring. What made you move the team there? [L0770.031]
  • Use open-ended questions while building the relationship, such as why, what did you do, how did you handle that, and was that something you wanted to do. Open questions let the prospect explain himself instead of answering yes or no. [L0770.032]
  • Prospects tend to answer almost any authentic question truthfully when they sense you genuinely want the answer and are not manipulating them. Sincere interest cannot be faked, and prospects will cut short a conversation if they sense you are only going through the motions. [L0770.033]
  • Answering a personal disclosure with an unrelated small-talk question, such as about family or hobbies, signals that you are not really interested and damages trust. Respond with a follow-up on what he just said, such as asking why it happened or how he handled it. [L0770.079]

Listening and note-taking

  • Ask a question, then listen to the full answer before thinking about your next question. If an answer is incomplete, ask a follow-up to clarify it for yourself and sometimes for the prospect. [L0770.027]
  • Keep your own talking to about 25 percent of a meeting. If you are talking more than that, the meeting is probably going badly; ask why the prospect is unresponsive, and end the meeting if you cannot fix it, unless he is naturally quiet. [L0770.028]
  • Take complete notes at every contact, recording the prospect's facts and opinions in his exact words. Exact wording is valuable later when you define his requirements, and it shows him that you value what he says. Missing details from a first meeting are hard to recover later. [L0770.026]

Handling negativity

Deal with anger, sarcasm, threats, abuse or any other negativity before anything else. Speak in a flat, neutral tone, without judging and without sympathy.

  1. Say neutrally that he seems upset. [L0770.029]
  2. If he is still upset after explaining why, repeat the observation in the same flat tone, without stressing any word. [L0770.029]
  3. If he is still upset, suggest that you discuss the matter at another time. [L0770.029]
  4. If he calms down, continue the conversation. If he becomes upset again, return to step 1 and keep your tone even. [L0770.029]
  5. If he stays upset, tell him you are not willing to meet right now because it would not serve either of you, and ask whether he wants to schedule another appointment or not. [L0770.029]
  • Do not respond with anger to a low-probability prospect. He is simply not a fit, so recognize it, end the meeting politely and move on to the next prospect. [L0770.044]

Discovery and disqualification questions

Use the wording below in this fixed order, swapping "this product" for your own product or service, and do not mention your company by name. [L0770.036]

  1. Need: Why do you need this product? [L0770.036]
  2. Want: Do you want it? If yes, ask why; if no, end the meeting. [L0770.036]
  3. Financial: This product will cost about [price]; are you prepared to spend that? [L0770.036]
  4. Time: If you decide to proceed, when would you want this to start, and what happens if it doesn't start then? [L0770.036]
  5. Decision-makers: When you make a decision like this, who do you usually talk it over with? Then ask whether he'll let you speak with them before you prepare a proposal. [L0770.036]
  6. Authority: If you went ahead, who else would have to agree? Handle this the same way as decision-makers. [L0770.036]
  7. Commitments: What would happen if you don't buy this product? [L0770.036]
  8. Brand preference: If you had to decide right now, without talking to anyone, which brand would you buy? [L0770.036]
  9. Supplier preference: Is there someone you'd rather do business with? [L0770.036]
  10. Internal procedure: What is your process for issuing a purchase order? Have him write it down and read it back before you move on. [L0770.036]
  11. Personal motivation: What would it mean to you personally if you didn't purchase this product? [L0770.036]
  12. Personal prejudices: Is there any reason you wouldn't want to do business with me? Anything we haven't covered yet, or any emotional reason? [L0770.036]
  13. Hidden obstacles: What could be lurking in the background that would prevent this from happening? [L0770.036]
  14. Close: Once questions 1 to 13 are answered satisfactorily and he hasn't disqualified himself: if I can meet all your criteria for this product, what will you do? [L0770.036]
  • Discovery questions are general, not about your brand, so the answers describe the prospect's own needs. Ask each question in the exact same words every time and write down the answers precisely, since the wording and tone of his answers can matter later. [L0770.037]
  • The prospect can disqualify himself at any of the fourteen discovery questions. Each answer he gives without disqualifying himself builds the relationship and the mutual commitment, and each problem you handle early prevents a deal-breaker later. [L0770.038]
  • Do not disqualify a prospect after an objection such as a price limit without first asking why he holds that position. The reason may be solvable, and asking why is better than guessing at his thinking. [L0770.043]

Decision-makers and approvers

Request to speak with the decision-maker or approver before preparing a proposal:

I need to talk with [name or role] before I prepare a proposal. Are you willing to arrange that?

If he refuses, reply in a flat tone:

That's not the way I work. I won't prepare a proposal without understanding their concerns and objectives, because I don't want a proposal rejected over something we could have handled up front. What would you like to do?

If he still will not arrange it, disqualify him. [L0770.039]

Unclear, evasive and conflicting answers

When a prospect's answer is unclear or evasive, do not move on until you are fully satisfied.

  1. Ask for clarification, for example by saying you do not understand what he meant and asking what he means. [L0770.040]
  2. If he is still unclear, state that his answer seems evasive and ask whether there is a problem. [L0770.040]
  3. If he still avoids answering, tell him you will not move on until the question is answered. [L0770.040]
  4. Carry on only when you have a clear answer, because an unresolved issue usually returns later as a problem. [L0770.040]
  • When a prospect gives conflicting timing, such as urgent on the phone and six months away in the meeting, ask him directly about the discrepancy before going further. Then either reach a clear commitment for the timing or set a specific return date that he chooses. [L0770.041]
