2.15 Competitor intelligence and offer analysis

Competitor research comes first

  • Competitor research is required before conversion work. Without knowing the competition, you cannot position your business as unique, and blue ocean opportunities are often found through this analysis. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0414.010]
  • Study competitors thoroughly, including their claims, prices and offers, and collect everything published about the product category. Competitive intelligence should come before you set your positioning. [L1261.034]
  • Competitive weakness analysis reveals specific gaps in the market where you can differentiate and capture sales by delivering superior solutions. [L0040.014]
  • Beyond initial research, analyse the top three competitors both as a consumer and as an e-commerce operator. Look for what they do not do, because each weakness can become your opportunity. (older advice, 2018) [L1109.017]
  • If a product is new to you, the market for it usually already exists and a competitor is selling to it. Competitor sites and social accounts show whether that demand is real and where the gaps are. [L0497.028]
  • Traffic already exists, so you do not need to invent it. Find where competitors' audiences already gather and redirect that attention toward your offer. Creating traffic from scratch is slower and costlier than borrowing existing traffic. [L0523.040]
  • Early product research method: look at the stores making the most sales in the chosen niche, list them, and compare them with each other to find trending products. Competition is acceptable if it shows demand and is not overwhelming. [L0999.050]
  • Study brands in other verticals for inspiration. For example, a commoditised fragrance sold at about $40 next to a designer bottle at about $400 shows how a large price gap can be used as a marketing angle. [L1016.012]
  • Find a product by looking at competitors on several platforms, not only TikTok. Check competitor websites and ads to see what they promote, how their sales message is framed and which channels they use. Where large competitors sell mainly through retail partners and do not advertise on Facebook, that gap can be a channel opportunity. [L1077.008]

Finding and selecting the competitor set

  • Before building a store, find the product in a research tool such as Kalodata, then collect five to ten other stores that sell the same product. Treat these stores as the competitor set you study for offers, layout and content. [L0600.001]
  • Choose the competitors to analyse from the top-ranked sites by traffic rank found during product research. Use the two or three highest-ranked sites for each niche. [L0894.004]
  • Monitor the top two or three organic Google results for your niche and main supplier brands, since Google already treats them as authorities. Watch their pricing and product pages most closely. (2023) [L0804.028]
  • For organic research, focus on Google organic results (the free listings below the sponsored carousel), not the sponsored carousel at the top. The carousel shows premium or featured products; organic results show the mainstream competition you will face in your price range. (2023) [L0251.055]
  • To research a niche, review the websites of established competitors, including product pages, about pages, content pages and blogs, and watch relevant YouTube videos. This shows how they group products, their typical price points and which items are their best sellers. Use these findings to decide whether the niche is worth pursuing. (older advice, 2020) [L1088.090]
  • Competitor check for a niche: if most sellers are general stores rather than specialist brands, and few run ads or build a following, the niche is open for a focused brand. Check the Meta Ad Library, filtered to ads running for at least a few months, to see which ads have lasted and what they promote. Ads without a click-through or with no following are weak signals. [L0450.011]
  • Choose the number of competitors by purpose. For a store or product check, five to ten stores is the set (Kalodata workflow) [L0600.001]. For a Facebook or TikTok product, list five to six competitors (see the procedure below) [L0895.018]. For brand-level research, list at least 10 to 15 direct competitors and 7 to 10 indirect alternative categories (see the procedures below) [L0613.007].
  • Indirect competitors sell different products to the same demographic, and their websites can reveal new placements and ad formats you would never find otherwise. Analysing one profitable indirect competitor can open a new traffic source worth thousands per month. Include them in your research list. [L0523.038]
  • Competitor research should not take hours. Use a few tools for a short, focused check instead of spending five or six hours searching for competitors. [L0708.041]
  • Use sites that look professional and trustworthy as the design benchmark, since a buyer would trust a store that looks like that. Filter competitor stores by how credible they appear before using them as a source. [L0600.016]

Procedure: quality check for competitor stores (Source D 2026)

  1. Open each competitor store found through Ad Library.
  2. Judge whether it looks professional and loads quickly. If it looks cheap, scammy or slow, discard it.
  3. Check whether it uses a shared dropshipping theme or template, such as Atlas, and note it as a dropshipping store.
  4. Keep only the professional stores as design benchmarks for your own store.

[L1249.011]

  • Do not copy stores that look cheap, unfinished or scammy. They are often supplier sites run by Chinese manufacturers rather than dropshipping competitors, and copying their look or claims can hurt your store's credibility. [L0600.015]
  • A competitor with a weak-looking website is an opportunity, because a product can sell even on a poor store and a better store can outperform it. Analyse the store's offer and copy before deciding to test the product. [L1173.003]
  • A strong landing page is a positive sign for a product, because sellers invest in pages only for products that sell. A very poor landing page may still run, but it can also indicate weak competition or a seller who is not yet scaling. [L0638.030]

Checking that competitors really sell

  • Competitor sales cannot be verified. Estimate them from traffic rank and the countries the traffic comes from, not from reviews, which can be fake. (older advice, 2022) [L0963.108]
  • Sales data from a tracker outweighs ad activity. A competitor with many active ads who makes no sales is not a valid signal to copy. [L1177.014]
  • Do not conclude a product is a winner from the store's surface sales or a single claimed total. Hidden products and sales on other products may sit behind a store's listing, and the public product list can be misleading. [L1385.011]
  • A store may show only one product on its public pages while other winning products exist and can be reached only by editing the URL. When researching a store, check for product URLs that do not appear in the navigation. [L1385.012, L0372.017]
  • Some competitor stores hide their product URLs so visitors cannot browse the full catalogue. Competitors may also run several accounts linked to the same domain, so check related accounts before judging a store's traction. [L0372.017]
  • A large order count for a competitor product may be carried mainly by paid ads, so check whether it is advertised heavily before treating its demand as organic. [L1186.004]
  • An older, established store with a consistent brand and several months of history often indicates a store that has been reused for ongoing sales. A new account launching a long-running store is less likely to show that stability. [L1385.013]
  • Check how old a store is against its traffic. A store launched about one year earlier with around 2.6 million visitors signals strong marketing and copywriting skill. Use the monthly traffic graph to see whether visits grow month over month. [L1164.009]
  • A store that has run for four years and generated about 2.7 million in revenue within 27 months shows that its products really sell. Copying the same product or a very similar one from such a store is a faster path than testing from scratch. [L0549.005]
  • A store that began as a dropshipping store and now invests in its own branding for a product can signal that the product is worth testing. Such a shift suggests the product has shown enough demand to justify building a brand around it. [L0708.052]
  • Watch how long a competitor has been posting and whether it is still uploading. Continuous posting over a long period is a sign that the product is still selling. [L0657.005]
  • When monitoring a competitor's TikTok account, a pause of about 24 hours in posting is not a warning sign. Concern becomes justified only when the account has gone silent for several days. [L1429.053]
  • Check whether a competitor is still active before entering a product. [L1444.009]
    1. Open the store link from the competitor's TikTok or Instagram profile.
    2. If the store link is gone, the competitor has most likely stopped the product, which is a sign the product may be dead.
    3. If the competitor still uploads and keeps the store active, the product is more likely still viable.
  • Pick brands with a clear following rather than brands with few posts. A brand with a following of about 78,900 Instagram followers in a hair-removal category is worth studying, because its comments and posts show real audience language. [L0450.032]
  • A competitor's product description reflects how the seller sees its customer base, but testimonials can be fabricated. Do not treat either as proof of real demand. Verify demand through social accounts and genuine customer comments. [L0450.012]
  • Competitor pages showing out-of-stock products are not automatically a bad sign. Stores often keep discontinued or backordered items listed without running ads, to catch organic search traffic. (older advice, 2020) [L1277.023]
  • Do not compare prices for a competitor whose price is shown as N/A, because the price was not found. Re-check the store before entering a price into the comparison. [L1159.012]

Store tracking and traffic tools

Tool conflict: can store sales still be tracked?

  • Store-tracking tools were described in one lesson as no longer possible because Shopify restricts them. That is a 2023 statement; check current platform rules before relying on any tracking method. (older advice, 2023) [L1219.046]
  • Other lessons describe tracking tools as working: Sales Tracker, Shop Hunter and Dropship.io are named as ways to verify competitor sales (see the tool list below) [L1382.010]. The Peekster tracker is described as working, though a 2023-era approach is said to work less well now [L1177.012, L0892.009].
  • Use the older or unverified method only as a rough signal, and confirm with a second source before deciding. [L1382.010, L1219.046]

Tools and their use

  • Trackers (Peekster, Shop Hunter, PP Spy, Dropship.io, PeakStar store tracking) estimate sales for a store to confirm that an ad is producing revenue. [L1382.010]
  • Peekster sales tracker: paste the competitor's store link to see estimated daily and monthly revenue. Tracker accuracy is typically around 50 to 70 percent. Stores that are not on Shopify may return no data. Shop Hunter is named as another tracker, and Ad Library tools are considered accurate. [L1177.012]
  • Example of a successful store found through sales tracking: about $5,000 daily income and roughly $200,000 monthly revenue. The lesson treats sales as the most important signal, because ad activity counts for nothing without sales. [L1177.013]
  • SimilarWeb is used to look up the traffic rank of a competitor store. Its free access is limited to a short trial (7 days), then it is paid. Alexa rankings no longer exist. (2024) [L0806.012]
  • A competitor's traffic score is a quick way to judge how popular a store is in its niche. Use it together with your own reading of what the store does well or poorly. (older advice, 2021) [L0853.086]
  • BuiltWith shows which platform and which apps a competitor store uses. Enter the competitor's website, click Look up, and read the results for platform and apps. (2024) [L0806.013]
  • The Meta Ad Library shows a launch date for each ad, so you can see how long a competitor has been running it. Use this to judge which competitor stores are stable enough to learn from. [L0600.002]
  • Explore Shops store explorer filters: Monthly revenue, Avg. product price, Monthly Visits, Based in, Product Count, Shopify Theme, Creation date, Language, Visitor Country, Currency, Traffic Growth and Trustpilot. Quick filters are All Shops, Highest Revenue Shops, Whale, Recent Scalers and Brands. [L0892.007]
  • Use the one-month Traffic Growth filter in the store explorer to find Shopify shops whose traffic rose by 30 percent or more in the last month. Such shops are a starting point for finding rising competitors. [L0892.008]
  • The Store Tracker takes the URL of any store and shows its estimated sales. The estimate is produced by an AI model fed with traffic and several data points, not by a revenue-per-visitor calculation. The lesson says the older Shop Hunter approach works less well now. [L0892.009]
  • The Sales Tracker details page shows store creation date, number of other users tracking the store, connected ad libraries, pixels, socials, monthly traffic, traffic share by country, an estimated revenue range, similar shops and SimilarWeb global and country rank. [L0892.010]
  • Example: a supplement store created in March 2024 had about 2.6 million monthly visits, up 48% month over month, 67 active ads and 49 products in 5 collections. Its estimated revenue was $11,745,000 to $17,617,000, and about 53% of its traffic came from the United States. [L0892.011]
  • Milled.com is a free resource where you enter a competitor's name to find the marketing emails they send, which reveals the offers they run. [L1404.011]

Procedure: order-number check for store sales

  1. Place a test order at a fixed time of day.
  2. Place another order at the same time 24 hours later.
  3. Compare the order numbers to estimate how many orders the store took in that day.

[L1219.045]

Procedure: count the store's domains and traffic (Source D 2026 validation)

  • A brand running several subdomains or domains at once often sends ads to several stores to test which funnel converts best. Count the domains, their product pages and their product counts to see how wide the test is. [L1164.010]
  • Check the traffic by country. [L1164.003]
  • Check the Trustpilot site that matches the store's main market before comparing numbers. Trustpilot can redirect visitors to a regional site with different ratings and review counts. [L1164.014]

Validating a product against competitors (step by step)

Procedure: product validation workflow (Source D 2026)

  1. Find the product in the Meta Ads Library and open its brand in Brand Tracker to see personas, USPs and angles.
  2. Open the store in Winning Hunter's Sales Tracker and check the launch date, monthly visits and estimated revenue bracket.
  3. On Trustpilot, check the star rating, review count and AI review summary.
  4. Filter the Trustpilot reviews to 1, 2 and 3 stars.
  5. Copy all low-star reviews into a CSV file.
  6. Give the CSV to an AI assistant such as Claude or ChatGPT and ask for a full gap analysis of the complaints.
  7. Count the store's subdomains and domains.
  8. Check the traffic by country.
  9. Open the store's best sellers and every similar product page.
  10. Check the pricing and offer tests (bundles, free items, price changes).
  11. List the apps the store runs.
  12. List the installed pixels and tracking tools.
  13. Open the top ads sorted by ad rank.
  14. Review the similar stores and competing products.
  15. Open the brand's ad copies in Brand Tracker and study its offers, proof and urgency.

[L1164.003]

  • Open every best seller and every similar product, because a brand's best sellers show which offers and angles already sell well. [L1164.012]
  • Check the Similar stores and similar products sections to build a list of competitor brands to analyse next. [L1164.013]
  • High traffic at an established competitor shows that demand exists, so it is not an entry barrier. You can still enter by offering a clearly better formulation, improved packaging or a new design. [L1164.011]

Procedure: competitor check before launching a product

  1. Search the product keyword in Google.
  2. Open the top competitor stores that appear.
  3. Note their bonuses, guarantee length, bundles and free add-ons.
  4. Choose a bonus or angle that none of them offers.

[L0577.076]

Procedure: early and alternative competitor checks with spy tools

  1. Remove the spy filters in the tool you used for product research.
  2. Add 5 to 10 keywords related to the product, such as the product name or broader terms.
  3. Run the same keywords in spy tools such as Peak Star and Droppy Spy.
  4. Search the same keywords in the Facebook Ad Library.
  5. Count the competitors, their engagement, and look at their landing pages before deciding.

[L0708.036]

Procedure: competitor check without spy tools (2024)

  1. Browse their product pages and categories like a normal customer.
  2. Watch their remarketing ads on Facebook and on sites showing Google display ads.
  3. Join their email list with a personal email address to see email frequency and offers.
  4. Search your product on Google to see their shopping results.

[L0471.008]

  • Before listing a product, check the price competitors charge for it on their product page. If the price looks fair and the competitor's description is complete, use it as the price reference for your own listing. [L1171.056]
  • If a competitor appears to be selling in the same niche, that overlap is normal. Copying their content is not allowed. Enter the niche only with a unique value proposition and a community code of ethics in mind. (2024) [L0806.010]
  • Competing with other members in the same niche is usually fine. The real problem is copying another store, so build a unique version of the store instead. (older advice, 2021) [L0968.093]

Competitor ads, creatives and landing pages

  • Study competitors' ads, creatives and landing pages to see which pain points and angles they use. Check whether their landing page matches the message of their ad. [L1194.006]

  • Ripping ads without understanding the messaging behind them burns budget on someone else's creative. Understand your ICP and USP before testing. [L1195.017]

  • Copying a winning competitor's store, product, offer and creative does not guarantee success. The competitor may have seasoned their pixel earlier, caught the trend wave earlier, or have a more mature pixel with better data. Copying is a good starting point for beginners but not a guaranteed success path. [L0067.035]

  • If copying a competitor's approach gets zero sales:

    1. Review their targeting strategy.
    2. Consider that they may have a first-mover advantage or a more mature pixel.
    3. Try a different marketing angle or audience segment.
    4. Target a slightly different audience with the same product, but a different context or problem emphasis.

    [L0067.036]

  • Competitor stores often reuse the same layouts and offers, so the layouts themselves are not a unique advantage. Your differentiation should come from your own product angle, brand and customer feedback that appears once you have sales. [L0600.035]

  • A competitor's proven store structure can be used as a base for a test, but it should be differentiated with your own angle, offer or market. Copying exactly the same page gives no advantage over the original seller. [L1185.007]

  • Copying a store word for word, including its typos, is a sign that a competitor is cloning the same seller's store rather than building an original one. [L0656.010]

  • Ads that work for others: when you find competitor ads that other brands have tested, save screenshots, the targeting reason, the ad creative and the ad copy in a document. Testing proven ads is cheaper than writing and testing untested ones from scratch. [L1243.011]

  • Influencer marketing: before committing to it in a niche, check whether competitors already use it. If no one does, that is an opportunity. If many do, the product itself must stand out. [L0790.008]

  • Creative and copy research for a Facebook or TikTok product:

    1. List 5 to 6 competitors.
    2. Pick the 2 to 3 with the best landing pages and strongest engagement, which suggests they are scaling.
    3. Take paragraphs from each, mix them, add your own style, and build your own page.
    4. Ask a freelancer on Fiverr to recreate the winning competitor video or hook when a creative is used in 10 or more ads.

    [L0895.018]

  • Procedure for analysing a competitor's product:

    1. Open the competitor's video ads and landing page.
    2. Note the price, colours, offer structure (for example buy-one-get-one at 50% off) and product description.
    3. Add the product, store link and notes to the shared product sheet.

    [L1185.006]

  • Competitor category menus often split a main category into sub-categories by product type. For example, surfboards split into hybrid, longboard, mid-length, pro series and shortboard. Filtering a competitor's category by product can reveal the same breakdown, giving a ready list of sub-collection names for your own store. [L1312.017]

  • Find marketing ideas for a product on Amazon:

    1. Take the exact product name you want to sell.
    2. Search for that name on Amazon.
    3. Open the first result, which is often the same product even if it is sold under a private label.
    4. Study the order of its images and what each image shows, such as use cases, dimensions and benefits.
    5. Note how the listing presents its badges and benefits, then write your own version and do not copy the images.

    [L1346.006]

  • Brand and product analysis, in order:

    1. State the specific desire or problem the product solves.
    2. Decide whether it is a superior product or uses identity marketing.
    3. For a superior product, document at least 3-5 specific mechanisms that make it better. For an identity product, document detailed identity markers.
    4. Describe how it positions itself against direct and indirect competitors.
    5. List the technical and emotional mechanisms that deliver results.
    6. Record the exact price relative to alternatives.
    7. Document the brand voice with at least 10 example phrases, and collect at least 20 key benefit and problem phrases.
    8. Note whether it is a new mechanism or a better version of an existing one, and how it claims to fix failures of previous solutions.
    9. Record visual identity elements, the awareness-to-purchase journey, and guarantees or risk reversal.

    [L0613.004]

  • Competitor research, in order:

    1. List at least 10-15 direct competitors with their websites, and analyse their hooks and headlines (with quotes), ad visuals, positioning, longest-running ads (likely the most successful), pricing and value propositions, site structure and sales approach, trust signals, how claims are substantiated, and which emotional narratives they use or miss. Identify at least 7-10 categories of indirect alternatives, and for each analyse how it meets the same desire differently, its advantages and disadvantages against the product, its language, price points, whether its share is growing or shrinking, and its emotional connection to the audience.
    2. For both groups, document recurring ad creative approaches, channels, audience segments, common objections addressed, guarantee and risk-reversal tactics, how success stories are used, and emotional gaps that represent opportunities.

    [L0613.007]

Reverse engineering a competitor's campaign

The five variables of a successful online campaign

Variable What to find out
Demographics Age, sex, education, location, income level, race, language, political affiliation and any other traits that define the target group
Offer What is sold, at what price, and every upsell and downsell in the funnel
Landing page The page visitors see right after clicking, such as an opt-in page or a sales page
Traffic source The websites, networks and media (banner, social, email, video) that deliver the traffic
Ad copy The headline, body text, images and video used in ads that get clicks

[L0523.035]

  • Enter a niche only when you know at least four of the five variables: demographics, offer, landing page, traffic source and ad copy. One unknown can be guessed, but two unknowns mean you should keep researching or choose another market. Never build offers, pages and ads with two blanks. [L0523.034]

Procedure: reverse engineer a competitor's campaign

  1. List direct competitors (selling the same thing) and indirect competitors (selling something else to the same demographic) with their landing page URLs. If you do not know them, search your keyword phrases on Google and click the paid ads, usually shown on the right side.
  2. Enter each URL into a traffic research tool such as SimilarWeb to see paid traffic sources, visitor demographics, the sites where ads run, when each ad was first seen and how long it has been running.
  3. Note the ads that have run longest, since long-running ads usually convert well. Collect their copy and images, and record which landing pages receive most traffic.
  4. Buy the competitor's product, and record every upsell, downsell and follow-up email, including how many emails are sent and how often they sell.
  5. Save everything in a swipe file of ideas to model before building your own funnel.

[L0523.036]

  • Spend freely on competitor research. Buy each of their products and take careful notes on sales videos, emails and the number of follow-up messages. The first visible offer is usually an entry point, while the real revenue sits further down the funnel. Mapping the full sequence matters more than the first sale. [L0523.045]
  • For a long-term business, buying a competitor's product is worth the cost, because it gives you their post-purchase email sequences and advertising funnels. A small purchase can reveal a full retention system. [L1243.009]

The Facebook Pixel events to check on a competitor site

  • The six events are Page View (usually the homepage), View Content (usually the product page), Add to Cart, Initiate Checkout, Add Payment Info and Purchase. Each event marks a different stage of the buyer journey. [L1243.007]

Procedure: competitor retargeting analysis with the Facebook Pixel Helper

  1. Open the competitor's front page and click around until the Page View pixel fires.
  2. Wait seven days, then check Facebook and Instagram during that time and record which retargeting ads you receive.
  3. Open a competitor product page until the View Content pixel fires.
  4. Wait seven or more days and again record which retargeting ads you receive.
  5. Save the screenshots and the reason each ad appeared, so you can compare the competitor's funnel timing and offers.

[L1243.008]

Offers, bonuses and pricing

  • The price of a competitor selling below your wholesale cost suggests they bought in bulk and secured a much lower per-unit cost. You can check Alibaba for 1,000-unit bulk pricing to understand their cost structure. [L0067.033]

  • Some good products are poor entry products. Choose the front-end offer by what the market wants most urgently, and then sell higher-value products after the first purchase. [L0378.015]

  • Bundles and offers, such as buying one item and getting a second at 50% off, or buy two and get one free, help a store win customers over competing sellers. [L1184.025]

  • Compare offers element by element. List the competitors' offers for the same product, compare them on each element, and identify where yours is weaker. Work through the competition research questions from the foundation stage to find the gaps that need fixing, so your offer ends up at least as good, ideally better. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0408.009]

  • Record the competitor's offer, how visible its return policy is, its shipping approach, the proof it gives for trust, and what customers say about it. Review this tracker before rewriting your own offer so each change is compared against real competitor data. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0411.041]

  • Map your offer as a CURVE offer:

    1. Review your competition research, including what competitors offer and what customers say about them.
    2. Compare your total offer against each direct competitor.
    3. Map how you can turn your offer into a CURVE offer.
    4. Record the results in the CURVE offer builder spreadsheet, including a section for marketing messages.

    (older advice, 2021-22) [L0411.033]

  • Research competitor offers:

    1. Search a competitor name, the term BOGO or other offer keywords in ad and email tools.
    2. Open the saved landing page to study how they present the offer, if it is still live.
    3. If the brand has removed the site or landing page, use the Wayback Machine to view archived versions.

    [L1404.013]

  • Check product pages for bonuses or premiums, such as freebies or content upgrades. [L0893.010]

  • Check the footer and policy pages for the returns policy and any other risk reversal, such as price-match guarantees. [L0893.010]

  • Before pausing ads on a product whose competitor breaks MAP, search that competitor by name and read its reviews. If customers complain about slow answers, late shipping or payment trouble, the violator will likely lose customers, so it can be sensible to keep your ads running. (2023) [L1094.120]

Manufacturers that also sell direct (checking MAP and MSRP)

  1. Check whether the manufacturer sells directly to the public. If it does not, there is no direct price conflict.
  2. If it sells direct, check its public price. If it sells at MSRP, you can list the product at a better price. If it sells at MAP, you will have to match its price.
  3. If it sells at MAP, judge its website. Outdated design, one image, little product information, and no phone number or live chat mean you can outsell it with a well-built store.
  4. If its site is as strong as yours, do not run paid traffic to the product. List it only for organic traffic or skip it.

(older advice, 2019) [L1110.018]

Competitor emails, retargeting and retention

Procedure: competitor email research

  1. Identify your closest competitors, at minimum the nearest ones in your market.
  2. Visit each competitor's website and look for opt-in forms, newsletter subscribe boxes, pop-ups and opt-ins on their blog.
  3. Subscribe to each list using a dedicated research email address.
  4. If no opt-in is visible, add a product to the cart, enter the research email at checkout, and abandon the checkout so you enter their abandoned checkout flow.
  5. Review the emails you receive to see what they promote, how they present offers and which topics they avoid.

(older advice, 2021-22) [L0609.012]

  • Use a separate, throwaway Gmail account under a neutral name for competitor sign-ups. This keeps the main inbox clean and prevents competitors from recognising the research address. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0609.013]
  • If competitors are not running email marketing, the inbox is an uncontested channel in that market. This creates an opportunity to build a dominant presence in customers' inboxes, which is hard to achieve in search or social feeds. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0609.014]
  • Frequently promoted products or messages in a competitor's emails usually indicate what works for them. Topics they never mention may reveal openings, but competitor emails should be used for inspiration, never copied outright. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0609.015]
  • Competitor email research also helps identify market gaps, since older competitors may not email at all or may avoid certain subjects. Those gaps can be used for your own email topics. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0609.016]

Procedure: website analysis of a competitor (Source B course, 2021-22)

  1. Record the competitor's website URL(s).
  2. Note whether they sell the same or similar products.
  3. Identify traffic sources: search for your product types on Google and check whether the competitor appears in paid ads. If you have never seen them running Google ads, paid search is likely not a traffic source for them.
  4. Enter the competitor's domain into an SEO research tool to estimate organic monthly traffic. Estimates are approximate, since some traffic sources take time to detect.
  5. Check for social media presence, such as Facebook, and record it even if it looks light.
  6. Check for email marketing: look for opt-in forms on the homepage and on content pages.
  7. If no opt-in exists, add a product to the cart, enter your name and email at checkout, then leave the checkout and close the tab so it looks like an abandoned cart.
  8. Use a dummy email inbox (separate from your main inbox) to subscribe to all competitors' lists, then review what they send, including special deals, content and any abandoned cart sequence.
  9. Record the USP from the homepage.
  10. Record proof points shown on the site.
  11. Check product pages for bonuses or premiums such as freebies or content upgrades.
  12. Check the footer and policy pages for the returns policy and any other risk reversal, such as price-match guarantees.
  13. Record price and terms: is their price the same, lower or higher than yours, and are there extra sales terms?
  14. Record the hook, or leave it as none if there is no one-liner.
  15. Record the primary marketing promise.
  16. Record features and deliverables they mention.
  17. Check whether they have content, such as guides, how-tos and comparisons, and judge its quality by length, depth, images and product links.
  18. Add comments on gaps and opportunities.

(older advice, 2021-22) [L0893.010]

Case: competitor analysis of an Australian online caravan parts retailer (older, 2021-22)

  • Range: about 7,000 products.
  • Traffic: organic traffic of tens of thousands to around 100,000 visits per month, with SEO as the main traffic source, little Google Ads and light Facebook activity.
  • Email: no opt-in on the homepage or content pages, and no abandoned cart email received after a test checkout.
  • USP: fast delivery, a large range, specials, free shipping, guides and live stock levels.
  • Proof points: a 4.97 out of 5 rating from 58,005 reviews, and over 200,000 items shipped in 12 months.
  • Offer: no bonuses on product pages. A 30-day change-of-mind policy with a store credit and a 10% restocking fee.
  • Messaging: hook "simple", primary promise "great parts delivered fast".
  • Content: dozens of guides with mixed content quality.

Range and trade checks (older, 2021-22)

  • When analysing a competitor's range, count the listed products on each category page and add the category counts together to estimate total catalogue size. Record the largest categories too, since they show where the competitor concentrates its inventory. [L0893.038]
  • Check a competitor's navigation and footer for a trade or wholesale section and for a downloadable price list. These pages indicate a separate customer segment or pricing channel that should be noted in the competitor sheet. [L0893.039]

Competitor comparison checklist (older, 2021-22)

Answer these questions for each primary competitor and keep them in a spreadsheet (for example a Google Sheet) for regular updates:

Question Purpose
What is their unique selling proposition? Find positioning gap
What is the marketing message for their products? Compare messaging
How do they price their products? Price benchmark
What other value or benefits do they provide with purchase? Find added-value gap
Where and how do they generate their traffic? Find traffic sources
What do they do to retain and elevate existing customers? Find retention gap
What search terms do they rank for organically? Find SEO keywords
Are they present at each stage of the customer awareness pyramid and journey, and how? Find stage coverage gap

    1. Check the manufacturer documentation for the product's features and attributes.
    2. If the mechanism is not clear there, list the competing products that customers would consider buying.
    3. Write down the features and attributes of each competing product.
    4. Compare them with the features and attributes of your product.

Reviews, complaints and Trustpilot

Procedure: Trustpilot complaint check (Source D 2026)

  • To see only low-rated reviews on Trustpilot, open the company's review page and add the URL parameters stars=1, stars=2 and stars=3 (for example trustpilot.com/review/[domain]?stars=1&stars=2&stars=3). This filter shows 1 to 3 star reviews, so complaint themes are quick to collect. [L1164.048]
  • Copy the filtered low-star reviews into a CSV file and ask an AI assistant for a full gap analysis of the complaints. [L1164.003]
  • Trustpilot does not allow companies to offer incentives for reviews or to pay to hide them, so a company's review record is a relatively hard-to-fake research signal. Still check the review count and the recency of reviews before judging a product from its rating. [L1164.049]
  • Check how competitors list a supplier's brand. Many stores order brands by sales, and a brand near the top of the brand menu or sorted-by-bestseller view is likely selling well. Review counts also signal volume: for example, a product with over 3,000 reviews is likely a strong seller. (2026) [L0808.014]
  • Check the product's review count and rating as a signal of volume, alongside the sales data. [L0808.014, L1164.049]

Checking whether two stores belong to the same seller

Procedure: checking whether two TikTok accounts or stores belong to the same seller

  1. Compare the store domains. Different domains for the same store design can indicate one seller running several stores.
  2. Check the Terms of Service, Privacy Policy and Contact Us pages for missing or identical content.
  3. Compare the email addresses on the contact pages. Identical emails are the final confirmation that the same person runs both stores.
  4. Compare the posting times and the uploaded clips. Identical times and identical content point to the same operator.

[L0656.009]

  • A seller with a new second account may be reacting to a viral success on the first account. Opening a second store under a different domain can be a marketing move to run ads through the other store. [L0656.011]

Brand-level and channel-level signals

  • Successful sellers rarely reveal their winning products, and competitors quickly copy ads that work. Do not expect a direct product answer from people who are already profitable, and assume a visible winning ad will be copied within a short time. [L0690.045]
  • Competitor sales of winning products are not visible to you directly, so use signals (traffic, ad longevity, store age, activity) together. [L0523.036, L1164.009]
  • Analysing a competitor's product for landing-page message match and offer structure helps you spot gaps. [L1185.006, L1194.006]
  • Use a competitor's pain points and angles as input for your own message, not as a copy. [L1194.006, L0609.015]
  • Monitor direct and indirect alternatives to see whether they are growing or shrinking, and note their emotional connection to the audience. [L0613.007]
  • Competitor analysis of an earlier market check, from the Source A course: check whether a competitor's ads and pages are consistent over time before treating them as a model. [L0523.036]

Gaps, differentiation and what to avoid

  • Competitor gaps can be turned into your angle: the gaps you find become the reason a buyer picks you. [L0040.014, L0414.010]
  • Low competition in SEO means building links can help you outrank stores that rank without strong effort. This is an advantage when competition is low. (older advice, 2022) [L0963.109]
  • If a niche has few SEO-focused competitors, invest in links and content to take those positions. (older advice, 2022) [L0963.109]
  • Competitor shifts in brand focus (from dropshipping to own brand) indicate the product has shown enough demand. [L0708.052]
  • Do not copy another store's content or store word for word. Build an original version, with your own value proposition. [L0806.010, L0968.093, L0656.010]
  • Do not treat a competitor's winning ad as proof that the product is a winner, since the ad may run on paid traffic without sales. [L1186.004, L1177.014]
  • Do not treat a single claimed total as proof. Verify with multiple signals. [L1385.011, L0963.108]
  • A product with many competitors selling it is not automatically a poor choice. It shows demand, but the product itself must stand out. [L0790.008, L0600.035]

Sourcing and product selection from competitor best sellers

  • Study a competitor's best sellers to decide what to test first. [L1164.012, L1285.008]

Procedure: find best sellers from competitors (2024)

  1. Search Google for your top future competitor stores.
  2. Open the category or collection that matches your product, such as standing desks.
  3. Sort by best selling or most popular.
  4. Note the top listed products from brands you also carry, because they already sell well.
  5. Use that short list as the core of your best-seller campaign.

[L1285.008]

Procedure: find likely best sellers on a future competitor's store (2023)

  1. Open the collection pages of the competitor store.
  2. Check the default sort, which is often recommended or best-selling, and switch to sort by best selling if available.
  3. Note which products sit at the top of each collection.
  4. Use these products as the starting set for your first campaigns.

[L0470.028]

  • On a competitor's brand filter, the brands shown first are usually the best sellers, not the alphabetically first brands. Check the full brand list and the top brands in each subcategory to find brands worth prioritising. (2023) [L0470.029]

Procedure: select the best sellers from a large supplier catalogue (2024)

  1. Visit future competitor stores and open their Best Sellers or Most Popular sorting in each category.
  2. Match those products to your supplier catalogue.
  3. Submit only those products in the Merchant Center feed for the initial campaign.
  4. Add a testing budget for the remaining products after the initial period.

[L0471.012]

Procedure: find best sellers on a competitor's site (Wayfair example, 2024)

  1. Go to a top competitor store (for example, Wayfair).
  2. Navigate to your product categories.
  3. Sort by "Recommended" or "Best Selling" (these are top sellers).
  4. Start uploading the best-selling products first.
  5. Alternative: ask suppliers directly which products are their best sellers.

[L0253.003]

  • Competitor menu structure: successful stores list the most popular categories first, reading left to right and top to bottom, so a sub-niche placed at the far right or bottom suggests weak demand. (2021) [L0469.014]
  • Competitor check before a product with a hidden or cloned competitor: "reviews" and "testimonials" are not proof. Verify demand through the store's own record of sales and activity. [L0450.012, L1177.014]

Competitor installation, service and channel checks

  • When researching how competitors handle installation, contact them from an email address that is not your business email. Ask which installer they use in your area, to see whether they rely on one national service or many local contractors. (2023) [L0835.143]
  • Use this to decide whether a service-based product needs a national or local installer network. [L0835.143]

Product viability and early research

  • Check competitor sites and social accounts to see whether a product is still viable before investing, as a stopped store link or silent account can signal a dead product. [L1444.009, L0657.005]
  • Before listing a product, use the competitor's price, description and offer as a reference, then adjust for your own angle. [L1171.056, L0577.076]
  • Study what competitors sell and how, then decide whether the niche is worth pursuing. [L1088.090, L0999.050]
