2.9 Brand, product and supplier count validation

The 20 Brands and 100 Products Rule

  • A niche needs at least 20 brands or suppliers and at least 100 products before you commit. Both thresholds apply to the niche as a whole, not to a single store. [L0551.003, L0961.042, L0972.004, L0667.071, L0852.015, L0964.004, L0275.001]
  • Beyond these minimums, the number of suppliers and products should be as high as possible. Some niches have hundreds of brands and thousands of products, and that is acceptable. [L0667.071, L1068.026]
  • Count products across all suppliers combined, not per supplier. Twenty suppliers with two or three products each (about 60 in total) fail the 100-product threshold. [L1097.009, L1277.011, L1277.012]
  • Treat the 100-product minimum as a firm rule. A large catalogue from many suppliers reduces the risk that one supplier's stock, prices or survival can sink the store. [L0934.019]
  • Twenty brands leave room for a low supplier approval rate, and 100 products give Google Shopping enough variety to show. [L1275.001]
  • Aim for around 20 brands because not every supplier approves you right away. Even with several rejections, the remaining approved brands still give a sizable catalogue. [L1277.013, L1109.014]
  • The 100-product minimum gives ads enough different searches to run against and generates the data needed to learn. A small range raises the risk that one product must succeed. [L1109.015]
  • Experienced operators can work with fewer suppliers and products. [L1109.009, L1085.021]
  • Successful stores have carried more than 3,000 SKUs. There is no upper limit on products if they come from quality suppliers and fit the niche. [L1109.013]
  • A niche is not disqualified because no store sells that exact niche. A broader store that carries the category is acceptable evidence, as long as the subcategory meets the 100-product and 20-brand minimum. [L0664.042]
  • Finding only a few stores that sell the niche is acceptable if the 20-brand and 100-product thresholds are met. Competitor stores that mix the niche with three or four other categories are fine if they carry the products you want to sell. [L0551.004]
  • Brands owned by the same parent company each count separately toward the 20-brand minimum, as long as each is a distinct brand. [L1279.095]
  • Different colours of the same product type count as separate products. An orange surfboard and a blue surfboard are two different items in your product count. [L0036.002]
  • Store-level counts: a store should have at least 100 products, although stores with 5,000 or more products exist. [L0356.002]
  • Accessories, carrying cases and free add-ons do not count toward the 100 core products. Google ads run for core products, and accessories are used as free bonuses or cross-sells. [L1110.014, L1110.015]
  • Only brands that supply the core products you would sell count as real suppliers. Sellers of add-ons are useful only as cross-sell ideas. [L0931.022]

Product-Count Warning Levels

  • If the niche has fewer than 20 to 30 styles across all suppliers, it is too narrow. Broaden it to the next category level. [L0268.079]
  • If a narrow niche cannot reach 100 products, broaden it one level, for example from brown leather chairs to all leather chairs, until the count is reached. [L0356.002, L1275.002]
  • The counting procedure for a niche (older, 2018):
    1. Count the suppliers and the distinct products available for your niche.
    2. If there are fewer than 100 products, move one level up to the broader category.
    3. Count again and repeat the step up until you reach at least 100 products and enough suppliers.
    4. Stop at the level where both thresholds are met. Do not go broader than needed. [L1109.010]
  • Illustrative counts (older, 2018): 20 products for a very specific item, 80 at the category level, and 150 at the broader table level. The broader level is the one that meets the 100-product minimum. [L1109.011]
  • Worked example (older, 2019): a narrow niche with 20 brands but only 50 products fails. A broader category with 25 brands and 150 products passes. [L1275.003]
  • Products and brands must both be counted; a niche that passes one but not the other fails. [L0356.003, L0960.017]

Counting the Niche: Procedures

  • Validation procedure (older, 2018): [L0466.048]
    1. Check that at least 20 brands sell the product.
    2. Check that at least 100 products exist in the niche.
    3. Check that the product is evergreen and not a short-lived trend.
    4. Check that the price point works for a profitable store.
    5. Check the demand for the niche with Google Keyword Planner.
  • Validation procedure (older, 2019): [L1275.002]
    1. Confirm the niche passes all validation criteria.
    2. Count the brands you can find; you need at least 20.
    3. Count the products across those brands; you need at least 100.
    4. If the product count is too low, broaden the niche one level (for example from brown leather chairs to leather chairs) and count again.
    5. Only launch once both thresholds are met.
  • Before committing to a niche, find at least 20 brands and 100 products that could be sold in it. These are minimum research thresholds. [L0972.004]
  • A niche should offer at least 20 suppliers or brands and at least 100 products before you commit to it. It must pass every niche criterion. If it falls short, move up one level to a broader related category. [L0671.005, L0671.161]
  • Choose a niche only after finding at least 20 possible brands that can be approved. If fewer exist, either research a different niche or move one level broader. [L0962.081]
  • Before committing, you need at least 20 potential brands to sell for. If you find fewer, widen the niche sideways (for example from chandeliers to all home lighting) or up one level. The catalogue should reach about 100 products across those brands. [L0834.004]
  • Make the niche as specific as possible, but only as long as at least 20 suppliers and 100 products are available. If the minimum cannot be met, broaden to the next category level, for example from chandeliers to all lighting. [L1068.015]
  • A niche should have enough supply to build a store: a rule of thumb is at least 20 suppliers and over 100 products. (Foosball example, 2022, older: one product type needed 20 or more makers and over 100 products.) [L0803.005]
  • Before building a store, aim for at least 20 suppliers or brands and at least 100 core products. [L1110.014]
  • A niche with fewer than 20 brands can still be pursued if it is close to the threshold and the store is already built. The threshold is a guideline, so weigh the remaining supplier count against the work already done. [L0801.019]
  • Niche validation thresholds (2024, older): find at least 20 brands on dropship-friendly websites and at least 100 products across them, and find 2 to 3 future competitor stores (or more) with a traffic rank under 1 million. [L0805.037]
  • Niche validation (2023 and later, current wording): a niche needs at least 100 products and at least 20 dropship-friendly brands to pass. [L0964.004]
  • Validation of the niche on a single dropship-friendly threshold: a niche needs at least 20 brands with contact information. [L0931.019]

Minimum Brand Counts and What Counts

  • A niche requires at least 20 suppliers to be acceptable for dropshipping. A strong niche has 100 or more. [L0050.008]
  • Require at least 20 separate suppliers or brands, not 20 products from one source. This gives a pool large enough to approach several suppliers for account approval, so you are not left with too few options if some decline. [L1079.008]
  • Confirm that more than 20 brands in the niche can be found with their contact information accessible. [L1080.005]
  • Count each supplier that carries even a few matching products toward the 20. [L0960.017]
  • A supplier with only a few products in your niche can still be approved and listed. Two or eight relevant products are enough to start if the supplier is otherwise a good fit. [L0934.026]
  • Find at least 20 potential suppliers within a niche before committing. If only about 10 are available, widen the product range with related items. [L0966.013]
  • Target at least 20 potential suppliers per niche. If a niche has only 5 to 10 suppliers, broaden it rather than building on so few. [L0804.008]
  • A niche with at least 20 brands is more useful than one with a few popular items. Gather brands from future competitors' stores until the list reaches 20 or more. [L0963.103]
  • The number of brands and products matters more than having many suppliers in a single product line. A sub-niche with only three manufacturers is not worth pursuing even with high prices and good volume. [L0468.030]
  • If a niche has only a few manufacturers, widen the category to find enough brands. Fewer than 20 different brands is a warning sign. [L0801.003]
  • If one manufacturer supplies most of the brands, the concentration risk is high. Aim for at least about 20 brands from different manufacturers. [L0247.011]
  • Brands from different manufacturers are needed; the count must reflect distinct brands, not one source. [L0247.011, L0801.003]
  • Within a chosen niche, list supplier candidates across every product category the niche covers (for example air purifiers, dehumidifiers and humidifiers for an air quality niche), not just the first product type searched. (Source B, 2021-22, older.) [L0932.006]
  • A dropshipper should aim to carry many brands and options within a product category rather than rely on one brand, since the brand customers prefer is unpredictable and unexpected brands, including low-margin ones, often sell best. A new store will naturally start with few brands and should keep adding suppliers over time. (Source B, 2021-22, older.) [L1456.017]

Competitor-Based Supplier Counting

  • Competition matters at the product and supplier level, not the niche level. Count how many brands you can get approved with and how many products those brands make available. [L0852.014]
  • To judge competition, count how many brands the niche-specific stores sell and how many of those brands appear in Google Shopping ads. If niche stores exist but few brands are advertised, the niche is workable. Exact thresholds are defined in the research module. [L0941.008]
  • Brands a competitor sells are the main source of supplier names. [L0963.114, L0930.011]
  • Finding suppliers by researching the brands that competitors sell takes one to three hours, and paid supplier lists rarely do better. (2022, older.) [L0963.114]
  • The source for supplier names is the future competitor list for each winning niche. It records every competitor's URL, store name, traffic rank and niche. Only competitors already known to dropship are used as sources, because their brand filters show suppliers that work on dropship terms. [L0930.011]
  • Competitor research in two passes (2020, older):
    1. During niche research, list the likely future competitor stores for the niche.
    2. For each competitor, identify the brands and suppliers it sells, then add them to the supplier list. [L0967.098]
  • Competitor research prioritisation (2019, older):
    1. Find competitor websites for the niche, including online-only stores, retailers and warehouses.
    2. Note the brands each site sells.
    3. Prioritise brands sold by online-only stores with no physical presence, because those brands have likely approved other online sellers.
    4. Treat brands that appear on both an online-only site and a physical-store site as the most likely to approve you.
    5. Keep the remaining brands as later contacts, since approval and pricing are not guaranteed. [L0357.005]
  • Brand-count procedure for reseller sites (Source B, 2021-22, older):
    1. Search Google for competitor stores in your niche.
    2. Open a store and look for a Brands page or a brand list.
    3. If there is no brand page, check the product titles, the sidebar filters and the category navigation for brand names.
    4. Scroll through the full brand list and note the brands you see.
    5. Repeat on a second, third and possibly fourth site, adding any new brands found.
    6. Stop once you can confidently count 20 or more distinct brands in the US, or 10 or more elsewhere. [L1484.005]
  • Reseller sites often do not list brands in the same place. Search the store's menus, filters and sidebars. Brand names frequently appear in product titles or category links, and the lack of a brands page is normal. (2021-22, older.) [L1484.006]
  • At the validation stage you only need to count how many distinct brands exist in the market. Record no details or contact information for each one. Whether the reseller dropships or holds stock is irrelevant to this count. (2021-22, older.) [L1484.007]
  • Validate the brand count across multiple competitor websites rather than one site. Brands that appear on several sites are reliable signals. If the count is still uncertain after three or four sites, continue searching Google results. (2021-22, older.) [L1484.008]
  • Record only the supplier count (for example 20 or more) in the tracker's Suppliers column for each market idea. Complete the same check for every idea before moving to the next stage. (2021-22, older.) [L1484.010]
  • Run the brand-count check for five market ideas, not just one. Write the supplier count for each idea into the tracker before moving on. (2021-22, older.) [L1484.016]
  • A first pass only gives a starting list and can understate the real number of suppliers. If a niche looks promising but has few suppliers, go deeper: open more competitor sites, search their categories for more brands, and look for other online-only sellers. [L0459.018]
  • Large multi-category retailers can be used to find suppliers in your category by collecting the brands they sell in that category. Do not copy their full range, and do not treat their high traffic ranking as a model for a niche store. (2021, older.) [L0801.014]
  • If most Google Shopping competitors in your niche are big-box retailers, check whether many brands in that niche are sold outside those retailers. If many are, the niche is a go-ahead for a small store. (2018, older.) [L1274.082]
  • Prefer mid-sized, specialised, up-and-coming brands that are not sold at big-box stores such as Home Depot or Lowe's. Being sold at big-box stores is acceptable only if enough brands in the niche still come from drop-ship-only suppliers. (2018, older.) [L1085.024]
  • Check the category section of a general competitor. For a general water sports store rather than a specialist, the category section should contain at least 20 brands and at least 100 products. If the category exists only as part of a mixed range, do not build a store around only that category. (2019, older.) [L0357.007]
  • A competitor's full brand list is a starting point and needs filtering before use. This keeps the validation count honest. [L0931.022]
  • Brand concentration: a single retail site in the same niche can carry many brands. (Example: in the generator niche, brands found on Google Shopping included Honda, Champion, Firman, Generac, DuroMax, Westinghouse, Briggs & Stratton, Yamaha, Cummins and Onan. A single retail site in the same niche carried many of these brands, which was taken as enough suppliers.) [L0458.020]

Google Shopping Counting and Validation

  • The sellers in Google Shopping for a product are both your future competitors and a ready list of brands you could approach as suppliers. Record their brand names while you count competitors. (Source B, 2021-22, older.) [L0458.061]
  • Count the products for a niche by going through the Google Shopping results for the niche keyword. [L0801.007]
  • Google Shopping research (Source B, 2021-22, older): search the generic product term, click the Shopping tab, and filter by Price (Up to $500, $500-$1,000, $1,000-$3,000, Over $3,000) and by Brand. Page through the results to list brand names and sellers. Open the "compare prices from X stores" link for each product to count competitors. [L0771.018]
  • Validation procedure through competitor brands in Google Shopping (2019, older):
    1. List all brands you found in the niche.
    2. Search each brand in Google Shopping.
    3. Count how many stores pay to advertise each brand.
    4. Get approved with all the brands, but run Google ads only for brands sold by 10 or fewer stores. Example: 30 brands with 15 of them sold by 10 or fewer stores means you move forward, get approved with all 30, and advertise the 15. [L0467.025]
  • Verify each brand extracted from a competitor list by searching it on Google Shopping and checking the competitor's site. Confirm the brand sells the products you plan to offer before contacting it. (2021, older.) [L0247.012]
  • Check how competitors price a brand's products on Google Shopping before contacting the supplier. If the brand has no price policy, a wide spread of prices across sellers is normal. (2020, older.) [L0468.027]
  • Brands with weak pricing discipline can still be sold if other stores in Google Shopping show healthy margins. Check which stores sell the brand and at what markup before dropping it. (2022, older.) [L0963.107]
  • If a category on Google Shopping shows only Alibaba-type sellers, search the organic Google results for US-based stores with US suppliers that do not use Shopping ads, since some suppliers are only visible there. (2021, older.) [L0834.007]
  • Sellers in Google Shopping can be both competitors and a source of brands, but only sellers that dropship are useful outreach targets. [L0458.061, L0930.011]

Finding the Real Brand Behind a Product

  • Find the original source of a product photo (2021, older):
    1. Right-click the image in Google Chrome.
    2. Choose Search Google for image.
    3. Review the websites where the image appears.
    4. Look for a brand name, SKU or supplier that a retailer has left out of its listing. [L0962.149]
  • Find competitor supplier names via image search (2020, older):
    1. Right-click on competitor product image on their website.
    2. Select "Search Google for image."
    3. Review visually similar results to see which retailers carry the same product.
    4. Check which retailers display the brand name on their product page to identify the supplier. [L0268.067]
  • To find a supplier's real brand when a search returns only resellers, right-click the product image in Google Chrome and choose "Search image with Google." This finds visual matches on other websites that may show the manufacturer or another brand name. (2023, older.) [L0964.010]
  • If a future competitor sells products without naming the brand, check whether it is private labelled. You can also right-click a product image and use Google Image search to find the same image on other sites that name the brand. (2024, older.) [L0805.039]
  • Partner suppliers that say they do not have a manufacturer role: check the About us and Contact us pages of the site. The word "partner" does not always mean the company makes the products. (2024, older.) [L0805.046]
  • Products already run by brands with logos should be avoided or deprioritised in favour of other candidates. Dropshippers running the same product are acceptable, but established brands indicate high competition. (Source C.) [L0708.040]
  • A manufacturer that sells only direct and has no retail program does not make the product a bad choice. It only means you cannot sell that brand, so look for other suppliers. (2019, older.) [L1002.011]

Brand Verification and Brand Extraction Hygiene

  • Remove private-label brands and products exclusive to a single marketplace. Focus only on suppliers that operate independently and sell through their own channels. [L0056.008]
  • Before committing to a brand, confirm it sells the products you plan to offer. [L0247.012]
  • A niche with many brands should still be checked for brands that are only sold on marketplaces. Marketplace listings on Walmart or Target are not reasons to avoid a niche. [L0970.024]
  • Brands found selling in the niche on other dropship online-only stores are the best outreach targets. Brands whose products go only through distributors with warehouses usually do not dropship. (2023, older.) [L1094.123]
  • Products in a niche that are sold on other dropshipping stores are more likely to come from brands that will approve retailers, so brands found this way are a better outreach target. (2023, older.) [L1094.024]

Keeping Your Count Honest: Exclusions

  • Avoid sourcing from overstock-style liquidators that sell leftover or previous-season stock at a discount. That is not a reliable supply model. [L0970.024]
  • Retailers such as Wayfair and marketplace listings on Walmart or Target are not reasons to avoid a niche. [L0970.024]
  • Wayfair and Houzz are primarily brand-authorised retailers and advertising platforms, not direct competitors. When you see brands on Wayfair, those suppliers likely dropship and accept new retailers. Search the brand name on Google to find the brand's official store and determine whether they sell direct to consumers. (2024, older.) [L0253.046]
  • A supplier that sells only to brick-and-mortar stores should not be pursued for an online-only store. The initial list should consist of brands sold by online-only future competitors. (2024, older.) [L0666.045]
  • Do not contact the competitor stores themselves. Contact the brands. (2024, older.) [L0276.046]
  • Repeated brand and supplier searches keep the list valid, because new brands and new competitor stores appear over time. (2025, older.) [L0857.036]
  • A supplier that refers you to another company to open an account is usually a distributor. Distributors should be avoided. (2020, older.) [L1277.027]
  • If a supplier sells at the same price as its retailers on a direct-to-consumer site, it gives retailers no price advantage and should not be approached for approval. (2023, older.) [L0835.009]

MAP Checks

  • MAP (minimum advertised price) enforcement: check whether a supplier enforces MAP before contacting them.
    1. Find the supplier's bestselling product.
    2. Search for that product on Google Shopping.
    3. Check whether all sellers show the same price. If they do, the supplier likely enforces MAP. (2023, older.) [L0804.020]
  • Check MAP quickly with Google Shopping (2022, older):
    1. Take the most popular products from each brand on your list.
    2. Search each product name on Google and click Shopping.
    3. Compare the prices of the stores. Example: four stores at 1,000 and one at 1,100 means MAP is likely 1,000. Five stores where one sells at 800 means a MAP violation or no MAP. [L0963.104]
  • Check whether a supplier enforces MAP before approval by searching several of its products on Google Shopping and comparing what stores advertise. If prices are nearly identical, the supplier is likely MAP-enforcing. If some sellers undercut the others substantially, it likely is not. (2023, older.) [L0964.015]
  • Test whether a competitor is violating MAP on Google Shopping (2023, older):
    1. Search the product and read the prices of all listings, including free ones, from three or more stores.
    2. Check the seller of each listing.
    3. If most sellers show one price and one or two show a much lower price, the common price is likely the MAP and the lower one is a violation.
    4. Check the price against the supplier's MAP policy before acting on it. [L1094.119]
  • MAP enforcement reality: [L0856.045, L0856.047]
    • A store with supplier approvals from a MAP-enforcing supplier can still work if the niche has many shops.
    • For non-MAP suppliers, most traffic must come from organic sources. Paid traffic is harder when competitors undercut price.
    • A store with only non-MAP suppliers should sell mostly through organic traffic and should seek MAP-enforcing suppliers as the main fix.
  • Non-MAP suppliers can be used only with caution. Check the linked video on profiting with non-MAP suppliers before proceeding. (2023, older.) [L0964.014]
  • Non-MAP suppliers sell products that competitors can undercut. [L0856.047]
  • Be cautious with some baby product sub-niches, such as strollers, where suppliers may give large retailers lower MAP tiers. That makes smaller stores undercut each other or fall below expected prices. Verify MAP is uniform before building. (2021, older.) [L0469.017]
  • Before committing to baby strollers, check the pricing structure. Hearsay suggests major brands give retail stores better wholesale and MAP pricing than online-only sellers, and online-only margins can disappear under such pricing. (2019, older.) [L0960.023]
  • In some niches, such as car seats, physical stores get preferred wholesale pricing and can sell at a different MAP than online-only sellers. If a competitor sells a brand well below the others on Google Shopping, check that brand's MAP policy before advertising. (2021, older.) [L0968.094]
  • Inventory delays from port backlogs affect only a minority of brands, but the risk is real. Sell for many brands rather than two or three, so one supplier's stockout does not sink the store. (2021, older.) [L1090.010]

Quality and Reputation Checks

  • Before contacting a brand, research its reviews and check for repeated complaints about product quality, shipping or packaging. Brands with frequent negative feedback on these points tend to cause returns, so skip them. (2023, older.) [L0970.025]
  • Check product reviews on competitor sites before choosing a supplier. Repeated reports of damage or poor quality for a product are a reason to avoid that supplier. (2021, older.) [L0962.093]
  • Check supplier product quality before onboarding (2023, older):
    1. Search the supplier company name together with the word "reviews."
    2. Look at product reviews on future competitor sites.
    3. If you see repeated complaints like breakage or quality issues, treat it as a warning. [L0804.025]
  • Research a brand's reputation before contacting it (2020, older):
    1. Search the brand name on Google.
    2. Search the brand name followed by the word "reviews."
    3. Look for quality complaints on competitor websites that sell the same brand.
    4. Use findings to decide whether to contact the brand or skip it. [L0468.026]
  • Find suppliers on competitor websites that have a traffic rank within the top one million. That indicates real traffic and suggests the site is converting visitors into sales. (2023, older.) [L0970.023]

Supplier Tiers and Supplier Types

  • Use supplier tiers to decide which brands to contact. Gold and silver brands are contacted; bronze brands are excluded. The tier is based on the brand's online reputation and how it deals with resellers. (2020, older.) [L0468.025]
  • To tell a gold supplier from a silver one, check the supplier's where-to-buy page. A gold supplier links to several online stores and will send them traffic and sales, while a silver supplier does not. Silver suppliers made up nearly all of the revenue in 2020, so a silver supplier is not a problem. (2020, older.) [L1277.025]
  • Profitability with silver-tier suppliers is possible during the first year. (2022, older.) [L1281.119]
  • Traditional dealers and brick-and-mortar suppliers are acceptable if their products appear at online-only stores. Suppliers already found selling through online-only stores should go on the outreach list. (2023, older.) [L0835.010]
  • B2B (business-to-business) opportunities can be a source of products that consumer competitors do not sell. [L0920.016]
  • Products already in a niche on other dropship stores point to suppliers worth contacting. [L1094.024]

Sourcing Channels

  • Trade show exhibitor lists can be used to find brands to contact. The TSNN site (tsnn.com) lists trade shows by country and industry. The exhibitors on each show's website are potential suppliers. (2019, older.) [L0357.009]
  • Finding suppliers through trade shows (2019, older):
    1. Open tsnn.com.
    2. Choose the country in the search field.
    3. Choose an industry or enter keywords for your niche.
    4. Open the website of each relevant trade show.
    5. Find the exhibitor list and collect the companies to contact. [L0357.010]
  • If a trade show directory has no results for your niche in a country (2019, older):
    1. Search Google for your niche plus "trade show" plus the country.
    2. If that finds nothing, search a broader term such as home interior or interior design plus "trade show" plus the country.
    3. Collect exhibitors from the trade shows you find. [L0357.011]
  • Find brands through trade show exhibitor lists on TSNN.com (2022, older):
    1. Enter your niche keyword and search.
    2. Select the country where you do business.
    3. Open the websites of the listed trade shows.
    4. Go to the exhibitors list and search for your product terms.
    5. Contact the exhibitors, who are often manufacturers or brands renting booths. Attending the shows in person is not required. [L1113.003]
  • To find US-based brands, search crowdfunding sites such as Kickstarter for funded projects in the niche over the past five to seven years. Brands found this way are often US-based. (2022, older.) [L0963.106]
  • High-ticket suppliers (Source B, 2021-22, older):
    1. Research which high-ticket product types exist and which markets they belong to.
    2. Choose products with an average price of about $1,000 or more.
    3. Identify all local manufacturers, distributors or importers of those products in the country where the business will sell.
    4. Contact each supplier to ask for approval to resell their products through an online store.
    5. Once approved, place the supplier's products on the store website and build marketing around them. [L1481.011]
  • Premium cookware from a well-known brand such as Le Creuset can work only if enough brands can be found to build a range. Whether a category works is subjective and requires research. [L0766.020]
  • Prefer ranking suppliers using metrics such as search volume when you have dozens of new candidates. [L1357.018, L1441.008]

Outreach Targets and Order

  • Use this order to approach suppliers:
    1. Identify at least 20 brands selling on online-only stores in your niche.
    2. Contact those first for supplier approvals.
    3. After approval with online-only retailers, circle back to brands sold by online-plus-offline stores. Their approval rate is lower.
    4. Use the niche verification tool in the members area for external validation before launching. (2020, older.) [L0268.005]
  • Suppliers that do not enforce MAP: keep contacting new suppliers. Continue reaching out to more suppliers in the list until approvals come through. A list of over 200 suppliers still has room to expand. (2024, older.) [L0856.046]
  • Brands found selling in the niche on other dropship online-only stores are the best outreach targets, because they are more likely to approve new retailers. (2023, older.) [L1094.123]
  • Suppliers to contact (2023, older): products that already appear on other dropship stores are a better outreach target. [L1094.024]
  • Contact the brands that competitors sell, not the competitors. [L0276.046]
  • Contact procedure (2024, older):
    1. Find online stores selling in your target niche.
    2. Identify all brands those stores carry.
    3. Search for and contact those brands directly.
    4. Request authorisation to resell their products.
    5. Do not contact the competitor stores themselves. [L0276.046]
  • Check supplier price-to-retailer relationship: if a supplier sells direct and at the same price as its retailers, do not approach it for approval. [L0835.009]
  • Look beyond the standard supplier criteria only after standard research is exhausted. [L1125.011]

Refusals, Short Counts and Next Steps

  • When a supplier will not accept a new reseller, keep working through the supplier list you already built. Return to the future-competitor and supplier research stages to find more suppliers to contact. (2023, older.) [L0835.002]
  • If fewer than 20 brands with contact information are found, go back to the earlier research, find more future competitors and extract their brand names. Repeat until the minimum is reached. Do not move forward with a niche below this count. [L0931.020]
  • If you identify fewer than 20 suppliers, return to the previous lesson and research additional competitor websites. Collect contact information for at least 20 suppliers; finding more is ideal. [L0056.001]
  • A niche passes validation when at least one future competitor has a traffic rank under 100,000 and at least 20 brands in that niche have contact information. Both conditions must be met before the niche is taken further. A niche that fails this test is not pursued. [L0931.018]
  • The count is about contactable brands, not the total number of brands extracted. Twenty is the bar to move forward with confidence. [L0931.019]
  • Traffic-rank thresholds disagree across sources:
    • At least one future competitor under 100,000 (required for validation). [L0931.018]
    • 2 to 3 or more future competitor stores under 1 million (2024, older). [L0805.037]
    • Supplier-site traffic within the top one million (2023, older). [L0970.023]
  • A niche that falls short on suppliers is not built on until the count is met, but a niche with fewer than 20 brands can still be pursued if it is close and the store is already built. [L0801.019]
  • Suppliers that refuse a reseller are a normal outcome; a niche is never guaranteed approvals in advance. [L1283.102]
  • Demo-store supply problems (2021, older): if the demo store is built but suppliers in your niche have supply problems, keep contacting suppliers and review results after about 10 to 20 contacts. Then decide whether to start a second niche in parallel instead of switching early. [L1112.085]
  • A niche should be tested for supplier availability and competitor count before committing. Supplier willingness to approve a retailer cannot be checked before choosing a niche. (2023, older.) [L1283.102]
  • Do not sell a product without checking that it is sold by enough suppliers and passes the pricing test, even if a single supplier looks strong. [L1281.119]
  • If two or more similar order values exist, choose the niche with more suppliers (for example about 60 rather than 20). [L1115.019]
  • The minimum product count is a guideline to be weighed, not an automatic cut-off, when a store is already built. [L0801.019]
  • Concentration risk in suppliers: a niche with only about 15 products from a few suppliers can still work but carries much higher risk. If one supplier with 10 of those 15 products goes out of business or raises prices, the business is badly hurt. (2023, older.) [L0934.020]

Judging Whether a Category Works

  • A category is worth pursuing only when the 20-supplier and 100-product thresholds are met for the niche. [L0961.042, L0960.017]
  • Products across 20 to 30 styles: if fewer than 20 to 30 styles exist across all suppliers, the niche is too narrow. [L0268.079]
  • Whether a category works is subjective and requires research. [L0766.020]
  • A sub-niche with three manufacturers is not worth pursuing, even with high prices and good volume. [L0468.030]
  • Do not build a store around one category from a mixed range if the category alone fails the thresholds. [L0357.007]
  • A niche with limited variety, such as chandeliers, may stock only a few dozen options at big-box retailers. Its supplier pool is narrow. A niche like wallpaper, where patterns, colours and textures are nearly unlimited, offers far more supplier and product room. (Older, 2021.) [L1125.016]

Going Beyond Standard Criteria

  • Sticking to the standard criteria gives the highest probability of success. Use expansion methods as a later step when you need more suppliers, more products or less competitive products. [L1125.011]
  • Once a store is running with suppliers and traffic, sourcing from outside the standard criteria is a next step for growth. It can add suppliers and products, reduce competition, and increase traffic and sales, and it is worth doing even when the store is already performing well. [L1125.015]
  • Be careful with categories where a brand's products are not typically sold to retailers. Consider whether the item is sold more widely across multiple sources. [L1125.016]
  • A dropshipper's growth comes from adding as many products as possible from as many suppliers as possible and keeping them updated. A product can take off unexpectedly, so the catalogue should always grow. (2019, older.) [L1068.026]

Keeping the Supplier List Current

  • Pre-made supplier lists are never fully current because new suppliers appear every year. The most current list comes from checking which brands the top competitors use. (2022, older.) [L1092.028]
  • Keep the supplier list current (2022, older):
    1. Identify the top competitor sites in the target niche when building the store.
    2. Record which suppliers those competitors sell through.
    3. Add any supplier not yet on your list.
    4. Repeat the check every six months to a year and add newly appeared suppliers. [L1092.029]
  • Repeat the brand and supplier search from time to time. [L0857.036]
  • Look for new brands and products through B2B opportunities. [L0920.016]
  • Repeat the validation of the niche when the supplier list changes. [L0857.036, L1092.029]

Within-Niche Breadth

  • Within a chosen niche, list supplier candidates across every product category it covers. [L0932.006]
  • Brands across many categories should be considered before the niche is judged too narrow. [L0932.006, L1456.017]
  • Make the niche as specific as possible, but only as long as the 20-supplier and 100-product minimums are met. [L1068.015]
  • A niche should have at least 20 brands and at least 100 products across them. Some niches have hundreds of brands and thousands of products, which is also acceptable. [L0667.071]

Measuring Risk and Diversity

  • Sell for many brands rather than two or three so one supplier's stockout does not sink the store. (2021, older.) [L1090.010]
  • Inflation was expected to push wholesale and retail prices higher in the coming months (2021, older). Having many suppliers protects margins and availability when costs rise. [L1090.011]
  • A large catalogue from many suppliers reduces the risk that one supplier's stock, prices or survival can sink the store. [L0934.019]
  • Require at least 20 suppliers so that a few rejections do not leave too little to build on. [L1109.014, L1079.008]

Further details

  • Time allocation: 5-10 minutes per competitor to extract brand names; 20-25 minutes total to research and enter supplier contact information; complete the entire task in under 30 minutes. [L0056.014]

  • Complete this task when you have at least 20 suppliers with contact information from real competitor websites, and your niche has passed the pricing, demographic, brand loyalty, seasonality, dropship-friendly, and competitor traffic tests. [L0056.015]

  • Find online-only retailers in the niche and note the brands they list, since those brands already supply online-only stores. If no such brands appear for a niche, move to a different niche. [L0247.007]

  • Minimum criteria: at least 20 dropship-friendly suppliers and at least 100 products per niche. [L0249.002]

  • Minimum 100 unique products (different SKUs and brands) required in a niche; if fewer, niche up to broader category. [L0268.003]
