2.2 Brainstorming niche ideas

Targets and Minimum Counts

  • Write at least 10 ideas yourself before using any AI tool, then expand the list with AI to 50 or more. [L0010.002, L0024.001, L0159.001, L0591.001]
  • If fewer than 10 manual ideas exist, pause and brainstorm more before going on. [L0024.001, L1179.022]
  • The 10-idea minimum gives the AI enough context and keeps its suggestions from being purely generic, without your market intuition. [L0159.003]
  • Your manual brainstorm captures market intuition and unique angles, while AI finds systematic variations that meet your explicit criteria. [L0159.011]

Principles of the Brainstorm

  • The brainstorm only collects candidates; you do not need to be sure any idea is the best one. Profitability is checked later, where weak ideas are set aside. [L1082.002]
  • Brainstorming produces candidates only, and every candidate must then be validated. [L0468.022, older 2020]
  • Brainstorm many ideas before choosing, because most will be weak and only one to three will fit you. Discarding weak ideas is a normal part of the process. [L1040.001, older 2021-2022]
  • Generate ideas freely without filtering them against criteria during brainstorming, because filtering early kills ideas before they are developed. The goal of this stage is volume. [L0996.009, older 2021-2022]
  • Add any idea that might work, even if it seems unlikely; strict filtering happens later in market research against defined criteria. [L1056.017]
  • Sources differ on timing. One view says the research criteria must be set before brainstorming, because a list of a hundred niche ideas can still be all bad if you do not know what to look for. [L0506.002] The other says do not filter while generating; filtering comes after. [L0996.009, L1056.017] Apply both: know your criteria first, and apply them only after the list is written.
  • Brainstorming is a habit. Opportunities for product ideas are everywhere once you look for them, and the idea list grows faster with practice. [L1082.013]
  • Aim for a long idea list now, because the later action tasks ask for many ideas. Keep all ideas in one spreadsheet so they are ready for research. [L1082.014]
  • Record product types rather than single brands or models, for example electric mountain bikes, car rims or chandeliers. Product types are broader and easier to research. [L1082.015]
  • A market idea can be a product type or a market defined by its audience or interest. Both forms are valid, and you can use whichever is clearer to you. [L0996.011]
  • A product-type idea names one specific item, such as a gear category like wetsuits or dive equipment. An audience-type idea names the interest group and leaves the product range open, such as scuba divers who buy a range of diving gear. [L0996.012]
  • Narrow a broad category, such as home furniture, into specific product types, such as sofas, dining room tables, patio furniture or hammocks, before researching niches. [L0359.009]

Step 2: Personal Environment and Purchase History

Brainstorm order (keep in order)

  1. Look around your home and your workplace and write down physical products you see or own, keeping the $200 threshold in mind. [L1082.004]
  2. Open your credit card statements, find past purchases over $200, and add every physical product to your list. [L1082.004]
  3. Ask close friends and family which products they have bought, then ask a wider group, for example with a Facebook post to your connections. [L1082.004]
  4. Go through your hobbies: note products advertised in magazines and blogs, products mentioned or advertised in podcasts, product-type questions in Facebook groups about your hobbies, sidebar ads and product reviews on blogs you follow. [L1082.004]
  5. Observe products in everyday places such as work, the gym, the shopping mall and the doctor's office, and add anything that could become a product idea. [L1082.004]
  6. Write every idea into column A of the niche research worksheet. [L1082.004]

  • A single living room contains more than 17 product categories that can be niches: chandeliers, wall lighting, table lamps, crown molding, wallpaper, coffee tables, sofas, fireplaces, mirrors, vases, bookshelves, dining tables, dining chairs and wood flooring. Look around for product types. [L0988.009, L1082.011]
  • Items to list from one living room photo include chandeliers, wall lighting, crown molding, lamps, sofas, coffee tables, wood flooring, dining tables and chairs, fireplaces, mirrors and bookshelves. [L1082.011, L0988.009]
  • Product ideas can come from everyday observation, such as outdoor items, pools, fire pits, garden structures and large smokers seen around a home or on a walk. (older, 2020) [L0967.003]
  • Niche ideas can come from everyday objects around you, such as furniture in your living room. Test each idea against the validation criteria before you commit to it. (older, 2019) [L1275.005]
  • Niche ideas can come from everyday observation, such as background products in movies or shows. Test each idea against the normal validation criteria before committing. (older, 2021) [L0853.042]
  • Everyday personal needs, such as children's play equipment, can suggest niche ideas that are worth checking against price and shipping criteria. [L1179.018]

Principles for personal sources

  • Hobbies are a source of product inspiration, not a requirement. You do not have to build a business around a hobby you are passionate about to use it for niche ideas. [L1082.008]

Step 4: Hobbies, Interests and Magazines

Hobby brainstorm procedure (keep in order)

  1. List things people do, love or are passionate about, such as hobbies, sports and interests, and not things they only have to do. [L0771.013, older 2021-2022]
  2. Use Google searches for lists of hobbies, activities and interests, and check books and specialist magazines for more ideas. [L0771.013, older 2021-2022]
  3. Write the ideas into a spreadsheet or on paper, aiming for about 20 ideas. [L0771.013, older 2021-2022]
  4. Do not evaluate them yet; the brainstorm is only the list that is researched next. [L0771.013, older 2021-2022]

Market-idea procedure (keep in order)

  1. Open the research tracker and work only in the Market Idea column. [L0996.008, older 2021-2022]
  2. Write down ideas without judging them against any criteria. [L0996.008, older 2021-2022]
  3. Each idea can be a product you could sell, a customer group you could serve, or an interest you could serve with products. [L0996.008, older 2021-2022]
  4. If you are stuck, take ideas from outside sources such as online searches, marketplace categories, magazines, library books, or hobby lists. [L0996.008, older 2021-2022]
  5. Keep adding ideas until you have at least 20 market ideas. [L0996.008, older 2021-2022]
  6. Only then move on to assessing the ideas against the other criteria. [L0996.008, older 2021-2022]

Interest-group procedure (keep in order) (older, 2021-2022)

  1. Brainstorm at least 20 market ideas, using paper or a spreadsheet, based on hobbies and interests you, your friends and family, Google hobby lists, and books or magazines. [L0458.014]
  2. For each interest group, list the high-ticket products its members would need or want to buy. [L0458.014]
  3. Run each idea through the six market criteria using Google Shopping and a keyword tool. [L0458.014]
  4. Mark each criterion yes, no or maybe in your tracker. [L0458.014]
  5. Prioritise ideas with yes on all six criteria; keep the others on the list in case they are needed. [L0458.014]

Google hobby-list procedure (keep in order) (older, 2021-2022)

  1. Search for 'list of hobbies' in Google. [L0996.021]

Hobby sources and examples

  • Products that people buy for their own hobbies, see on special interest TV shows, or that friends and family use are good starting points. People who already buy expensive products for a pastime are a strong signal for a high-ticket market. [L0996.017, older 2021-2022]
  • Special interest magazines are useful because their advertisers are typically companies selling products for that interest. Flipping through a magazine's ads shows which product types are sold to that audience. [L0996.018, older 2021-2022]
  • Ads in a hobby magazine or blog for a new type of product can be added to the list directly. A mountain biker who sees ads for electric mountain bikes in biking magazines or forums would add electric mountain bikes as an idea. [L1082.009]
  • One hobby list was described as containing 549 hand-picked hobby ideas, which gives a large pool to screen. This is one example of how a single search can deliver hundreds of candidate interests. [L0996.022, older 2021-2022]
  • A Google search for a list of hobbies returns Wikipedia's list, a compiled list of about 549 hand-picked hobby ideas, and hobby category sites such as Discover a Hobby. Together these give a large pool of candidate interests from a single search. [L0996.032, older 2021-2022]
  • Hobby lists can be used from any country, because the hobbies are not tied to one region. A hobby that is popular locally but not in the target market should still be recorded and checked later. [L0996.033, older 2021-2022]
  • Not every hobby contains products worth selling. Hobbies that require equipment, supplies or specialised gear are promising, while hobbies that involve only giving advice or performing a skill often have no products to sell. [L0996.023, older 2021-2022]
  • Beekeeping implies equipment purchases, and commercial t-shirt pressing may involve expensive machinery, so both are worth recording. A handyman trade or giving advice typically has few products, so those are weaker candidates. Interior design usually involves many relatively high-priced home products. [L0996.025, older 2021-2022]
  • An activity such as horse riding can be recorded as a market idea, or a specific expensive product linked to it, such as horse saddles, can be recorded instead. Product ideas that look expensive can be written down now and researched later. [L0996.026, older 2021-2022]
  • On a Sports & Outdoors hobby page, bodybuilding, cardio workouts, cycling, golf, horse riding and camping are examples of hobbies that involve purchasable gear. Each can be recorded as a market idea or narrowed to a specific product such as horse saddles. [L0996.034, older 2021-2022]
  • Water hobbies such as kayaking and canoeing are worth recording because quality kayaks and canoes tend to be expensive. Water sports is a broader alternative label that can be used instead. [L0996.035, older 2021-2022]
  • Crafts such as engraving and car detailing should be screened even though the craft itself is not a product. The tools and consumables used in these crafts can be the products to research. [L0996.036, older 2021-2022]
  • A list of over 1,000 hobbies can be scanned for niche ideas. Not every hobby is a niche or a product category, so look for the ones that stand out. [L1056.018]

Step 5: Pitch-a-Niche and Reflection Exercises

Pitch-a-niche (keep in order)

  1. Write down every niche you think is a good fit for dropshipping. [L0831.015]
  2. For each niche, write down the reasons behind your choice. The purpose is to bring out the underlying 'what for' motives that drive the choice. [L0831.015]

Timed first-reaction brainstorm (keep in order)

  1. Set a two-minute timer. [L1513.027]
  2. Write down as many possible niches as you can in that time. [L1513.027]
  3. Note the first thoughts that come up, such as worthiness, fear or excitement. These thoughts reveal the programming that will influence later decisions. [L1513.027]

Timing rule

  • Complete the first brainstorm within 15-20 minutes. [L0012.007]

Sold Stores, Listings and Marketplaces

Business brokerage and sold-store research

  • Business brokerage websites list online businesses for sale, showing asking price, business age and net profit. Browsing their online and technology section reveals which products real stores sell. Use these sites for idea research only, not for buying a store. [L1056.004]
  • The online and technology section of one brokerage site listed 1,368 businesses for sale at the time of recording. [L1056.006]
  • A two-year-old Shopify store selling premium wall paneling was listed for almost $1.5 million and made about $600,000 net profit per year. Premium wall paneling is therefore a niche to add to the list. [L1056.005]

  • Empire Flippers is a marketplace for selling online businesses, and its e-commerce section shows stores for sale with details. Use the listings for research: look up what the store sells and which products it carries. A store listed for almost $3 million selling health, fitness, personal care and medical products is one example. [L1056.009]
  • Sold-store and listing research can include content sites rather than pure e-commerce stores. A content-driven site that sells gear still shows product categories, such as fishing, camping and hiking equipment, that are worth adding to the list. [L1056.026]

  1. Open the section that shows businesses already sold. [L1056.007]
  2. Select the category e-commerce. [L1056.007]
  3. Note the names of the sold e-commerce companies. [L1056.007]
  4. Google each company name to see which products it sells. [L1056.007]
  5. Add the product types to the niche list. Not every sold store will be a winner. [L1056.007]

Warnings for pre-made and public sources

  • Do not start niche brainstorming by searching online for best dropshipping products or products to sell this year. Those searches mostly surface middlemen suppliers and make it very hard to find something that works. Generate ideas from your own purchases, surroundings and daily life instead. [L1082.001]
  • Ready-made lists of top-selling products or niches are shared with thousands of people, so the top items are usually too competitive. Do not start from such a list; generate your own market ideas. (older, 2021-2022) [L0458.013]
  • Do not rely on pre-made lists of approved niches. When many people pick the same few markets, competition rises and those markets stop being profitable for everyone. Find market ideas through your own research. (older, 2021-2022) [L0996.013]
  • In dropshipping communities, asking other sellers for their winning niche is considered taboo. Generate niche candidates from your own research instead. [L1513.029]

Stalled Brainstorming and Outside Sources

  • When brainstorming stalls, use outside sources to find ideas, such as internet searches on passions and hobbies, marketplace category browsing, special interest magazines, library books, and hobby lists. (older, 2021-2022) [L0996.016]
  • When a category produces a promising product, use that category name as a new search term in other sources, such as Google, magazines or libraries. A single category can lead to a broader or more refined market idea. (older, 2021-2022) [L0996.030]
  • Stuck-brainstorm procedure (older, 2019):
    1. Go through a list of hobbies and mark the ideas that stand out. [L1002.007]
    2. Run each marked idea through the niche verification tool. [L1002.007]
    3. If an idea passes all checks, start building the store right away instead of delaying. [L1002.007]
  • Note on the 2019 procedure: it moves straight to building the store once an idea passes its checks. Current guidance requires at least 50 niche ideas before narrowing to one niche for the first store. [L1002.007, L0532.005, L0591.001]

Narrowing and Filtering the List

Quick checks (keep in order)

  1. Brainstorm a broad initial list of niches. [L0766.023]
  2. Filter the list using the quick checks: electronics, emotional attachment, strict deadlines, personalized items that must be seen or felt, and seasonality. [L0766.023]
  3. Verify the remaining niches against size, price and target demographic before committing. [L0766.023]

Connection, passion and market size

  • Do not limit yourself to one niche at the start. If you have a personal connection to a niche, start there; otherwise choose by problem and market size. Sub-niches can be built later within the same problem area. (Source C) [L0871.006]
  • Pick a niche by listing the brands you would like to resemble and studying the customer base they already attract. If your brand differs clearly from those brands, build your own audience instead of copying theirs. (Source C) [L1438.010]
  • Passion is set aside at the first brainstorm stage, where price is the only criterion. [L0012.007]
  • Conflict: the first brainstorm disregards passion and interests [L0012.007], while the Source C approach starts from a personal connection when one exists [L0871.006]. Use the price-first list for the initial 50, and apply the personal-connection rule when choosing where to begin among the survivors.

Other filters

  • Hobbies that require equipment, supplies or specialised gear are promising; hobbies that only involve advice or skill are weak candidates. [L0996.023, older 2021-2022]
  • Do not start with a single niche: scoring, validation and elimination happen in Module 2. [L0024.008, L0034.001]

Common Mistakes

  • Sources warn against starting from a short list of pre-approved niches, against broad labels, and against cheap hobbies. [L0458.013, L0996.013, L1056.010, L0996.024]
  • A list of a hundred niche ideas can still be all bad if the research criteria are not set before brainstorming. [L0506.002]
  • Avoid searching for best dropshipping products or products to sell this year at the brainstorm stage. [L1082.001]
  • Do not request winning niches from other sellers; generate candidates from your own research. [L1513.029]
