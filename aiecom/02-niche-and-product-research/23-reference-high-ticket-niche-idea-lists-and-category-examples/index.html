2.23 Reference: high-Ticket niche idea lists and category examples

Using the category pool

  • The 500 high-ticket niche ideas are a brainstorming pool of product categories, not a finished verdict. Every candidate still needs checks for demand, supplier availability, margin and shipping before a store is built around it. [L0546.002]
  • The list is sorted into B2B, family, fitness, hobbies, kitchen, living room, medical, office, outdoors, pets and sports. Use it to spark ideas and get into the right mindset, not to copy entries one by one. [L1056.003]
  • Each top-level category points to a buyer: Family for parents, Medical for patients and caregivers, Office for workplaces, Pets for pet owners, and Outdoors or Sports for homeowners and athletes. Define the buyer before choosing a product. [L0546.004]
  • Choosing a product only because it appears on the list skips the supplier check. The list gives no sourcing, cost or shipping data, so these must be found separately for each candidate. [L0546.007]
  • Some products appear in more than one category: Baby Rockers is listed twice under Family, Greenhouses appears under Hobbies and Outdoors, and Coffee Tables appears under Living Room and Office. Count each product once when comparing categories. [L0546.008]
  • The list contains spelling variants such as Break Makers (most likely bread makers) and Childrens Desks & Chairs. Search suppliers and marketplaces with the corrected term, or you will miss results. [L0546.009]
  • Some entries overlap heavily, such as Treadmills, Anti-gravity Treadmills and Aquatic Treadmills. Group overlapping items into one sub-niche before comparing demand between them. [L0546.012]
  • The example products in a pricing lesson illustrate a math pattern and are not product recommendations. Many new sellers copy the same popular niches, so a store should look for its own angle within a chosen niche. [L1147.026]
  • A product that passes all the niche and product tests can work even if it sounds unusual, such as dining room tables. Run the product through the full criteria before deciding. (Older source: Source A, 2022.) [L1281.022]
  • Typical product types for the gimmick-led model include LED galaxy projectors, portable mini printers, unusual kitchen tools and unique pet toys. These are deliberately extreme examples used to illustrate the category, not a verified shortlist. [L1211.008]

Home, living room and furniture

  • High-ticket niche categories to consider include rugs, stand-up desks, leather club chairs, ergonomic office chairs, horse saddles, 3D printers, espresso machines, Smith machines and Pilates reformers. [L0012.008]
  • Rugs and furniture are strong dropshipping categories because they offer extensive variety, can be personalized to customer specifications, and customers expect delivery time. [L0028.003]
  • Furniture is generally a good niche at face value, but it must still pass the checks for size, price and target demographic. Some furniture items will fail the price check even though the category is good. [L0766.012]
  • Cribs and bedroom furniture sets are good niches because buyers do not need to see, feel or touch them before delivery. If the range has many colors and sizes, buyers are usually willing to wait for a delivery. [L0766.014]
  • A refrigerator niche is weak because most buyers choose from four or five brands, while a wine refrigerator niche has many styles and brands and may suit online selling. (Older source: Source A, 2021.) [L0962.095]
  • Product categories that tend to sit at higher price points and are worth testing as niches include 3D printers, laser engraving machines, CNC machines, horse saddles, ergonomic office furniture, stand-up desks, leather sectionals, swing sets and backyard playgrounds. Their actual prices and margins still need to be verified. [L1179.017]
  • Additional higher-ticket candidates to price-check include Persian rugs, leather club chairs, leather sofas and espresso machines. Intuition suggests the price points may be good for most of them, but espresso machine pricing was explicitly left unverified and needs checking. [L1179.023]
  • Stand-up desks and leather club chairs were judged possible candidates for the upper middle class. Neither showed clear signs of appealing to wealthy or low income buyers in the review. [L1517.014]

Rugs

  • Rugs are a good niche because there are many expensive options and a wide range of sizes, colors and patterns. Buyers cannot easily find a specific size or pattern at local home improvement stores, so they search online, but the price must still cover the cost of shipping large, heavy items. [L0766.008]
  • Sub-niches found for the broad rug category after an AI query include:
    • hand-knotted or hand-woven rugs [L1517.012]
    • rugs with premium materials such as wool, silk or wool-silk blends [L1517.012]
    • traditional heritage rugs such as oriental and Persian [L1517.012]
    • larger area rugs [L1517.012]
    • collectible or antique rugs, which were judged not relevant [L1517.012]
  • Persian and oriental rugs suit older upper middle class households, while collectible and antique rugs were judged not relevant. [L1517.012]
  • The rug price and profit figures for Persian, woven and oriental rugs are listed under Price and profit illustrations below. [L1081.011, L1081.012, L1081.013]

Lighting, home improvement and kitchen

  • Lighting and home improvement niches work well with sufficient product diversity and natural customer acceptance of waiting periods. [L0028.004]
  • Lighting and home improvement products are considered good niches. [L0766.015]
  • A home seller in Australia using eBay and Facebook found small kitchen and kids' items sold well and shipped cheaply, including cookie cutters, kids' clothing and spiralisers. Such products fit a low-cost, low-storage start, but they are examples to check against current demand, not a fixed shortlist. [L1437.039]

Air purifiers (Source B, 2021-22; older source)

  • Some air purifier brands use no filters and rely on a patented air sterilization technology, which means no filter maintenance costs. Such units suit small rooms but are not recommended for serious gas or odor removal or for reducing household dust. [L0937.023]
  • Airpura air purifiers are modular, so a buyer can reconfigure the unit later when needs change, and they are priced fairly for their feature set. Weak points are filter changes that need a screwdriver and turning the unit upside down, and the lack of filter change monitoring. [L0937.025]
  • Alen air purifiers suit general buyers as an all-purpose unit at a moderate price, with a lifetime warranty, very easy filter changes, quiet operation and low energy consumption. They are not recommended for serious gas and odor removal. [L0937.026]
  • Austin Air is popular with allergy-focused buyers because it spends its budget on filtration rather than extras, with above-average particle filtration and very good gas and odor filtration. Its filters are hard to change, requiring a screwdriver and turning the unit upside down. [L0937.027]
  • Blueair's HEPASilent technology combines electrostatic, carbon and HEPA-type filtration, giving high filtration with no ozone. Its units carry the highest CADR certified by AHAM in the category, come in sizes for different rooms including quiet bedroom models, and newer models add filter life monitoring and pre-filters. [L0937.028]
  • QuietPure air purifiers use HEPA filtration to remove dust, allergens and VOCs while running very quietly, and they are the most budget-friendly of the brands in that comparison. Their range includes a basic Home model, a Home plus Smoke model and a Whisper model. [L0937.029]
  • Winix air purifiers combine a space-saving design, easy filter changes, automatic air quality monitoring and Energy Star compliance with above-average filtration at a low price. They are not recommended for serious gas and odor removal. [L0937.030]

Sport, outdoors and hobbies

  • A competitor surfboard category was split into hybrid, longboard, mid-length, pro series and shortboard. This breakdown is a useful starting list for subcollections. [L0371.011]
  • Snowboard niche case: the audience was split into beginners struggling to get started and intermediate to advanced riders in different situations. Targeting snowboarders specifically, rather than general sports, gave a more focused message that tied to each rider's level and problem. The specific message led to much better marketing performance and allowed the brand to scale massively with one of those audiences. [L0450.019]
  • Fishing was named as a niche with strong results in the US and Canada. Treat such claims as a hypothesis and test popular hobby markets with similar products. [L0388.028]
  • Fishing accessories such as fishing rods were an underrated, profitable drop-shipping niche with strong seasonal sales in summer, cited for 2018 and 2019. Other small-community niches, like canoe accessories or niche extreme sports, follow the same logic. (Older sources: 2018 and 2019.) [L0561.021]
  • Greenhouses appear under both Hobbies and Outdoors, so count them once when comparing categories. [L0546.008]
  • Treadmills, anti-gravity treadmills and aquatic treadmills overlap and should be grouped into one sub-niche. [L0546.012]

Fashion and clothing

  • The most popular clothing styles to consider as a niche are streetwear, casual wear, athleisure and punk. [L0779.007]
  • Women's clothing and fashion for Australia or Europe is a suggested starting niche, especially during cold weather. Choose items that need little explanation so simple image ads can do the selling. [L1316.023]

Health, beauty and wellness

  • A health product example was reviewed with these facts: it sold over 745 units, but its ads were heavily bottom-funnel and lacked a mix of content, so it was skipped. The revenue chart was slowly rising, which was noted as a mild concern about whether the product was stable. [L1187.013]
  • A pain-relief supplement brand had no clear audience at first and little traffic. Its team reverse engineered similar supplements and found competitors advertising on diabetic websites, survival websites and natural health websites, audiences the team had not known would respond. After targeting these new demographics, the product scaled over two months from about 20,000 USD per month to over 500,000 USD per month. [L0523.041]
  • Essential oils have traditionally been marketed to women, so a line aimed at men fills an underserved gap. The marketing, website design and imagery must be built for that audience rather than copied from mainstream essential oil brands. (Source: Source E, 2025-26.) [L1016.002]
  • Two brands in the same hair-care niche sold nearly identical products. KYIR sold a Fuller Active Hair Serum in 50 ml with 30, 90 and 150-day supplies, 20 percent off the 90-day tier and a 90-day money-back guarantee. Divi sold a Daily Growth Care System with a 30 ml Scalp Serum and hair growth vitamins, a monthly subscription at about 86.36 euros (save 10 percent) versus about 96 euros one-time, and a 100 ml Scalp Serum at 41.95 euros one-time. Two brands selling nearly identical products signals a validated direction worth researching further. [L0772.016]
  • A skincare brand, Lumera, sold a Microcrystal Serum PM Duo for 39.99 USD on the ad card, with a 4.7 rating over 2,847 reviews and a 90-day guarantee. Its ad with a problem-first hook about acne scars and microcrystal technology had been running since about March 2026. The Meta Ad Library page showed 130 results, while Winning Hunter counted 124 active ads. The brand was added on May 6, 2026, and data was still filling in. The brand has validated ads worth recreating, and the weak point is that it sends paid traffic straight to a product page, which leaves room for an advertorial that agitates the problem further. [L0772.036]
  • A hair-removal shaver sourced from AliExpress at about 10 USD or less was sold at 29.99 USD to match competitors, with a 60% profit margin. This case is listed under Failed and stopped products below. [L1444.002]

Spirituality, gifts and family

  • Spirituality, astrology and healing stone products were reported as fast-growing sellers during a fulfillment partner call. Treat such reports as hypotheses to test in your own market. [L0388.027]
  • A store selling healing-stone-style products found that its best audience was women aged 45 and over. The seller combined that niche with a second interest into an ebook, offered it at 19 USD, and ran ads before the book existed. The early click-through rate was described as quite good, while sales were still unknown at the time of the call. [L1212.052]
  • A customized message gift priced at $60, aimed at fathers buying for their sons, showed about 120% progression. The same personalized structure can be reused for other gift relationships with a different copy angle, without copying the competitor's page word for word. [L1185.040]
  • The first winner was a kids LED skirt sold in Q4 2020 in the gifting niche, which earned about 10,000 euros in one month. Ads targeted grandparents and parents with a retirement-related angle. The founders realised the product was seasonal to Christmas and that it would fade afterwards. (Older source: Q4 2020.) [L1440.005]
  • The second product, a problem-solving item with viral potential, was chosen after about three months of saving and new research. It brought in about 16,000 euros in its first month at a 26% profit margin, and it later moved to custom labeling and inventory in addition to dropshipping. [L1440.006]
  • A draw-following train toy (where a toy train follows a line drawn on paper by a child) previously sold very successfully during seasonal testing. [L0209.021]
  • A teach-your-child-to-read book, Teach Your Child to Read in 100 Easy Lessons by Siegfried Engelmann, was first published in 1983, built on the DISTAR method, and reported by the presenter as over one million copies sold. Its product page for the last 30 days showed the figures listed in the Market signals section below. [L0354.015]
  • A Halloween slides product was priced at $25 with $5 shipping. The seller who scaled it first uploaded a video organically, then used spark ads, and the videos reached about 17 million and 18.4 million views. The store was a single product page with no homepage, only one product, no downsells, and sizes in US format, and it reported about $134,000 in sales from September 15 to September 26, roughly 11 days, with order number 6368 and about 5,367 orders. The store had a 60 percent off banner and urgency text, and it was built before Halloween. [L0352.033]

Pets

Home services and bulky goods

  • Pool tables need slate installed, leveling and felt attached, and most sellers left customers to handle it. A seller who dominated this market dropshipped the table, posted local ads for installers in the customer's city and hired freelance contractors who met the curbside delivery and installed the table in the home. The seller could charge a premium and made many sales, because the extra steps were the differentiator. (Older source: Source A, 2018, describing a magazine article about a decade earlier.) [L1274.021]
  • Adding product types can broaden a niche: stand-up paddle boards alone gave eight suppliers, adding kayaks gave about 12, and adding kite boarding brought the total to 25 suppliers, which passes the minimum. (Older source: Source A, 2022.) [L1281.020]
  • A print-on-demand store with eight years in business was reported at about £6,000 profit per month, a 44% profit margin and annual revenue around £188,000. Verify such figures on the store listing before relying on them. [L1214.026]

Wayfair was checked first: it is a very large online retailer that dropships most of its products (about 99% by the lesson's estimate), its brand filter showed more than 100 brands, and the estimate was set to 100. Home Outlet Direct had no address and its About Us page confirmed dropshipping, so it was also a future competitor. Tractor Supply had a large physical store and was eliminated. [L0459.024]
  • Outdoor kitchens result: DS Friendly = Yes, over 100 suppliers, three future competitors, marked green. The lesson's figures for this niche were about $4,900 average profit per sale against a $1,200 base and 74,000 monthly searches. [L0459.024]
  • Outdoor kitchens validation: the supplier list ended with more than 40 suppliers that had contact information, well above the 20-brand minimum. One competitor met the traffic rank condition under 100,000. The niche therefore passed validation and the next step was choosing which niche to pursue first. [L0931.021]
  • Chandelier dropship check. The niche had passed the earlier niche checks. A seller with a corporate office and no warehouse listed 251 First with 936 chandeliers among its brands. [L0460.023]
  • Chandelier final result: three of the first five sellers checked were online-only and sold for many brands, which confirmed the chandelier niche as dropship friendly and marked it yes. [L0460.023]

Price and profit illustrations

  • Persian rugs: grade A, average price $2,319.13, average profit about $580 per sale, passes demographic, brand loyalty and seasonality tests, 49,500 monthly searches. [L1081.011]
  • Woven rugs: grade A-, average price $186.16, average profit only $47 per sale, passes the three tests, demand of 5,400 monthly searches. This is about 10% of the demand for Persian rugs and roughly one tenth of the profit per sale. [L1081.012]
  • Oriental rugs: grade A, average price $484.08, average profit $121 per sale, passes the other three tests, demand of 22,200 monthly searches. [L1081.013]
  • The top 5 niches in the example all have a minimum profit of $854 per sale, pass the price point, demographic, brand loyalty and seasonality tests, and have demand above 10,000 monthly searches. [L1081.017]
  • CNC machines: average price $11,000, average profit about $2,700 per sale, most rated A+ by the research tool, with pass results for the tests in the lesson. The high price means pre-selling by phone is usually needed before the order, so the niche only suits someone willing to take phone calls. [L1081.019]
  • The CNC starting product in the validation example was priced at about $1,900, while the average price benchmark here is $11,000. Both figures come from different data sets, so apply each to the price range it describes. [L0459.023, L1081.019]
  • Massage chairs: average price $3,400, average profit $854 per sale, 110,000 monthly searches. Some buyers may still call before ordering because the price is above $2,000, but the niche looks strong. [L1081.020]
  • Rooftop tents: price about $2,000, profit about $500 per sale, 33,000 monthly searches. Buyers at this price usually purchase online without calls, making it a fit for a hands-off business. [L1081.021]
  • Budget dining table: a dining table sells for $500 with free shipping. A big, heavy table of this kind costs about $250 to ship on average, and the seller's COGS is also $250. Before customer acquisition, card fees, the seller's time and traffic costs, net profit is $0, and once those costs are added every sale loses money. The conclusion is not that dining tables are a bad niche but that this price point is too low for the item's size and weight. [L1180.007]
  • Premium dining table: a solid wood dining table sells for $1,200 with free shipping. Because it is heavier, the average shipping cost is $300, and the COGS is $600, which is 50% of the selling price. Total costs are $900, so net profit is $300 per order before other expenses. The $300 shipping figure is a high-end assumption that applies to roughly the top 2 to 3 percent of orders, since most orders ship for less. If supplier and customer are both in California, shipping drops to $150 while COGS and price stay the same, raising net profit to $450 per order. Even a cross-country shipment at $300 still nets $300 per order. [L1180.008]
  • Gun safes: a large, heavy safe priced at $200 cannot be shipped profitably with free shipping. Such safes are viable only in the roughly $800 to $1,000-plus price range, where the margin covers COGS and shipping. [L1180.011]
  • A liver detox supplement example was judged promising with these figures: 46,000 dollars revenue in the last 30 days, 1,008 to 1,100 units sold, a product price of 34.18 dollars, a shipping fee of 5.00 dollars and a 30 percent commission rate. Its revenue graph went up and down, and its ads were mostly bottom-funnel with strong editing and a varied set of avatars. [L1187.014]
  • The liver detox example was checked further over the last 90 days: the revenue chart peaked at about 3k and then started to slow down. Before committing, the trend was reviewed again, and the decision was to customize the ads rather than copy them. [L1187.015]

Market signals: views, sales and ad counts

  • Benchmark case: a Korean calcium multi-balm stick sold about 50k per day and about 1.5 million in the last 30 days in the United States, with a sold item count of about 68k in the same window. Its average unit price was about 22.27 dollars, while the displayed product price was 23.00 dollars. [L0707.002]
  • The teach-your-child-to-read book showed these figures for the last 30 days (05/25 to 06/23 on screen): a price of 32.90 USD, a commission rate of 15%, a shipping fee of 5.99 USD, ship from local, and an earliest recorded date of 10/13/2025. Revenue was 991.29 USD (141.81 USD per day), of which video revenue was 566.58 USD (80.94 USD per day) and product card revenue was 424.71 USD (60.67 USD per day), with live revenue at 0.00 USD and an average unit price of about 33.04 USD. The presenter planned to list the book on a Shopify product page, run ads and check whether demand held for the next two to three days. The result was not shown in the lesson. [L0354.015]
  • The leather jacket single-niche store in the Netherlands and Belgium was cited as doing about 20k per day with Facebook ads, even with some refunds. Currency was not stated in the source. [L0660.010]
  • A TikTok account sold a showerhead with a vortex effect, and the product was also listed on Amazon. The account grew mainly through organic posts, reaching about 20 million views on one video and about 43,000 followers. Once organic sales were coming in, the brand had enough revenue to fund branding, paid ads and sponsorships from upcoming TikTok creators. The brand became well known, but the account later stopped posting for a long time, so a product needs repeated organic reach in the millions, and consistent posting matters more than a single hit. [L0706.059]
  • A seller used a proven product found through a community call and added it to an existing general Shopify store in about three hours by reusing the store design and adding the product link to TikTok. The product generated about 100 orders in its first three days, after the seller had tested four earlier products without a clear winner. [L0372.048]
  • A product that won on the fifth or sixth test was run with three creatives: one or two videos and one or two pictures, all edited from supplier content. The product kept working for nearly two years afterwards. A different product later produced about $2,000 in sales but could not be scaled, so it was stopped and a new product was tested. [L0349.034]

Viral and challenge tests

  • A TikTok challenge account for an instant ice cream product that the team was given, not chosen, was tested late in the summer season. About six videos were drafted. In the first two days the checkered red-and-white background looked messy and results were weak. A blue juice video using a corn trend reached only about 7,000 views, while comments kept mentioning Breaking Bad. The team posted a video reply to a top comment built on that Breaking Bad reference, which reached about 1.1 million views and went viral. [L0764.021]
  • On day three the team changed the background and switched the whole account to Breaking Bad themed videos using dialogue and theme songs with on-screen text. Four videos passed 1 million views, and the account reached about 8 million views in two weeks (nearly 10 million total as later stated). It had around 400,000 likes, but only about 1% converted to followers, against an expected ~40,000 followers. The Instagram version reached about 500 followers after late posting and did not convert sales. [L0764.021]
  • Uploads were 3 per day, 4 on the viral day, posted around 12:00, around 16:00 and between 18:00 and 19:00. Sales did not reach the team's sales-per-million-views target, so the challenge was treated as a views exercise and uploads stopped when it ended. Ad spend was zero; the only costs were the product, which was returned for a refund, and food. [L0764.021]

Ad and audience signals

  • Lumera-style evidence: a filtered search for ads running at least seven days is a starting point for finding validated ads. The approach is covered under the skincare and hair cases above. [L0772.036]
  • The product of a leading seller with a validated product and a strong audience was a gift relationship case; see the customized message gift above. [L1185.040]
  • A health or supplement brand can gain audiences it did not expect; see the pain-relief supplement case above. [L0523.041]
  • A necklace store began as a one-product store, targeting African Americans and Africans in the US. The operator sourced the same design from an AliExpress supplier, which offered a golden necklace for every African country. (Older source: 2019-2020.) [L0519.022]
  • The operator started dropshipping in June 2019 with a first store built from YouTube tutorials and ran Facebook ads himself for six months without success, because he did not understand CTR and CPM. He joined a mentorship in January 2020 and tested five to seven products for about 2000 USD, with no profitable result. His eighth product, an Africa map necklace, was found in late March or early April 2020 and recovered all earlier losses. (Older source: 2019-2020.) [L0519.021]

Failed and stopped products

  • A cheap hair-removal shaver sourced from AliExpress at about 10 USD or less was sold at 29.99 USD to match competitors, with a 60% profit margin. The product was tested by its own team for two weeks with consistent uploads and got about 8 million views, with one video at about 5 million views. Sales reached about 5,000 USD, of which about 3,000 USD was kept after costs. Views and sales did not match: 5,000 USD on 8 million views is about 625 USD per million views, below the 1,000 USD per million target. Quality problems (the product worked only for 3 to 5 uses, caused skin redness and burns, and was awkward to use) led to exposure, and the team stopped the product. Competitors had used one consistent female model and reused the same intros until their views dropped. [L1444.002]
  • The paintball niche was rejected because its guns cannot be advertised on Google and its average price fell below the minimum. [L0490.022]
  • A health product that sold over 745 units was skipped because its ads were heavily bottom-funnel. [L1187.013]
  • The liver detox example was not committed to until its 90-day revenue slowdown was reviewed. [L1187.015]

Seasonal and short-term products

  • Trampolines and motorcycle helmets both showed strong seasonality and failed the evergreen test, so both were moved to red. [L0451.011]
  • Halloween and gifting products with a holiday angle are seasonal and will fade after the event. [L0352.033, L1440.005]
  • A seasonal product should be checked against its sales window before committing to a store around it. [L1440.005]
