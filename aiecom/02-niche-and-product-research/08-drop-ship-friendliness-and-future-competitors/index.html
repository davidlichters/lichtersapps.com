2.8 Drop-Ship-Friendliness and future competitors

Scope: which niches enter the test

  • Run the dropship-friendly test only on your top 3-5 niches: those that already passed the earlier tests and have the best price points. Three to five niches is enough at this stage, not the whole worksheet [L0459.001].
  • Those niches must already have passed screening for pricing, profit margins, demographic match, brand loyalty and seasonality [L0050.002].
  • Do not spend time on niches that already failed an earlier step [L0460.024].
  • Testing the entire niche list at once overwhelms the research. Concentrate on the top candidates to keep focus and quality [L0050.004].
  • Avoid niches with no competition, because no one has proven demand or supply for them [L0459.003].
  • A niche with only a few dropshipping competitors can be a first store if it passes all criteria and shows signs of future dropshipping competitors [L0359.015].

Definitions: what counts as a future competitor

  • A future competitor is an online-only store that sells multiple brands in your niche, without physical retail locations, public showrooms or owned warehouses [L0051.001].
  • Future competitors show that people already buy these products online. They validate that real demand exists and that the niche is large enough for new entrants [L0051.002].
  • A niche is dropship-friendly when existing online-only retailers successfully sell multiple brands in it without physical retail locations or public showrooms. This proves the model works in that category [L0050.001].
  • A niche is dropship-friendly, and so confirmed, when competitors operate without warehouses, showrooms or retail stores, which indicates they sell entirely through online dropshipping [L0060.001, L0060.003].
  • "Online-only" means no warehouse, no showroom and no retail store. A plain office is acceptable [L0460.001].
  • A niche counts as dropship-friendly when online-only retailers sell products in it, because suppliers can approve these retailers [L0460.001].
  • Only sellers that carry products from several third-party brands count as future competitors. Their brand count also feeds the supplier estimate for the niche [L0459.007].
  • A seller whose site and products carry only its own brand is a direct-to-consumer brand and is not a future competitor. Confirm brand ownership from product images, the store name and the footer logos [L0459.006].
  • A store with only its own brand, such as a customisation service with no manufacturer names on products, neither confirms nor rejects the niche. Close the tab [L0460.017].
  • A competitor may sell a wider catalogue than your niche. It is acceptable as long as the niche itself still meets the supplier and product minimums [L0356.004].
  • A seller can sell beyond the niche and still count. A furniture and decor store that also sells chandeliers qualifies for the chandelier test [L0460.019].
  • A store that sells several niches is still a competitor if it sells the niche you want to enter. Do not remove it because it carries other product types [L0891.007].
  • A store that carries related niches can reveal secondary niches you can expand into later [L0687.010, L0687.016].
  • Parent companies or affiliate programmes do not disqualify a competitor [L0804.017].
  • An online-only store that is the e-commerce division of a company with physical stores still counts as a future competitor. Its supplier relationships may make some brand approvals harder to get [L0460.020].
  • Competitors should be in the country where you will sell. Overseas competitors are a disqualifier [L0855.091].
  • A store that only ships within a country you will not sell in cannot count as a future competitor for your store [L0460.028].
  • Stores that sell niche products with no dedicated niche store are not a reason to reject the niche [L0551.005].

Signs of real online-only dropshipping

  • True dropshipping competitors show clear signs: notes about delayed shipping in product descriptions, shipments from supplier warehouses, or mentions of multiple suppliers or distribution partners [L0050.007].
  • A footer with only phone, live chat and email suggests an online-only store [L0460.010].
  • A returns page that does not mention returns to a warehouse supports an online-only, dropship model [L0460.012].
  • A seller with no address anywhere in its footer or contact details is a likely online-only store. This is only a signal; confirm it on the About Us page before counting the seller [L0459.031].
  • A store name containing the word "Direct" is a hint of dropshipping. Verify it on the About Us page and in the footer before counting it [L0459.021].
  • A store that lists many different brands in the niche category is a strong indicator of dropshipping. Count the brands and the products per brand, since a spread across brands shows the store is not making its own stock [L0460.018].
  • A competitor on a default myshopify.com address often indicates a weak business. Check its traffic rank before treating it as a real competitor [L0806.011].
  • Stores that ship directly from China often have long delivery times, inconsistent product quality and customer issues that are hard to resolve. They also depend on one style of Facebook ads, which creates a famine-and-feast revenue pattern that concerns buyers [L0554.019].

Exclusions: what does not count

  • Direct-to-consumer sites, where all products carry the company's own brand, do not validate the dropshipping model and are excluded [L0050.005].
  • Exclude any site with physical retail indicators in its footer, About page or Contact page: store locator pages, "Find a store" links or listed physical locations [L0050.006].
  • A multi-brand seller with physical stores, a store locator or a pick-up option is not a future competitor, even if it sells many brands. Physical retail disqualifies it for this test [L0459.010].
  • A company with a warehouse, showroom or retail store does not count as a future competitor, even if it also sells online. Only pure online sellers count as evidence for the niche [L0460.003].
  • Remove any research-list entry that has a physical presence such as a showroom, retail store or warehouse [L0891.001].
  • Exclude large chain retailers with physical stores, such as Home Depot, Lowe's, Walmart and Tractor Supply. Exclude general sites that are not specific to the niche, such as broad restaurant supply sites [L0459.019].
  • Count only online-only stores that sell the niche. Exclude big-box chains such as Home Depot or Lowe's, because they cover too many categories [L0358.019].
  • Skip sites you already know have warehouses or physical stores, such as sellers owned by large retail chains, marketplaces with fulfilment centres, or big home-improvement chains. Do not open them for research [L0460.007].
  • A competitor with a physical store but thousands of products should not be used as a future competitor. Instead, search its brand names on Google and add the online-only brands you find as potential suppliers [L0805.040].
  • A single product listed at one store often signals a direct-to-consumer seller. Verify the brand before counting it, and do not assume a store is a dropshipper from its name or a single listing [L0459.020].
  • A seller that shows another brand's logo in its footer or on its products, while selling only one brand, is a single-supplier store and should be eliminated. Do not confuse a brand's footer logo with a multi-brand range [L0459.008].
  • A store that only shows a street address is not proof of a physical showroom. Check the address before counting the store as a real retailer [L0551.001].
  • Do not use a competitor that shows no Alexa data as validation. Find competitors with available data that show real traffic [L0245.075]. (older: 2020)

Physical presence: where the rule applies and where it does not

  • For competitor validation, retailers with warehouses or showrooms are not true competitors. They may compete for some customers, but only online-only stores count toward the minimum [L0853.030].
  • For supplier sourcing, the presence of a retail store or warehouse is not in itself a reason to avoid a supplier [L0357.006].
  • Suppliers that have retail stores are often unwilling to drop ship. Check competitors' retail presence and product listings before you choose a niche [L0671.154]. (older: 2021)
  • Avoid contacting brands that are sold primarily through companies with physical showrooms or retail locations, because they are less likely to approve dropshippers [L0270.057]. (older: 2021)
  • Source suppliers from online-only retailers rather than from retailers with showrooms. Showroom retailers often carry brands that are not suitable for dropshipping, so some overlap can still happen [L0852.023]. (older: 2020)
  • Niches where competitors hold their own stock can still work. Suppliers usually need more convincing before approving a dropshipper and may offer different pricing terms than to stocking retailers. Treat such a niche as a second store once the first one produces steady cash flow [L0356.005].
  • Items priced at $2,000 to $5,000 or more can still be sold online. Buyers usually contact the seller before ordering, so expect a pre-sale conversation [L0357.006].
  • If most suppliers in a niche have a showroom or warehouse, check how many products each competitor website stocks. Stores often list many brands but keep only a few in stock, which is normal. If no suitable online-only option exists, broaden to a category sold by online-only stores and check which brands they list [L1068.018]. (older: 2019)
  • A product category does not have to be sold mostly by online stores to be viable [L1069.031]. (older: 2019)

Finding sellers: Google search

  • Search the niche with the string buy [niche keyword] online in google.com and submit with Enter. Open the text ads and organic results in new tabs [L0460.005].
  • Search for competitors with buy [your niche] online on Google and visit the linked websites [L0060.002].
  • Take the top organic results, not the paid ads [L0963.097].
  • Review only the first page of organic Google and Google Shopping results. Do not view multiple pages or use "View More" [L0270.038]. (older: 2021)
  • To research competitors, search the niche on Google and open the top 3 organic results. Treat those as future competitors. If no store sells the exact niche, move one level up. For example, stand-up desks is the narrower term and desks the broader fallback. Then study their brands, SKUs and product pages [L0941.007].
  • Ignore the sponsored ads and open the top five or so organic results in separate tabs [L0806.001]. (2024)

Finding sellers: Google Shopping

  • Google Shopping, the Shopping tab in Google search results, is used to find sellers of a niche's products. Its All products section shows more sellers than the subcategories do [L0459.005].
  • Google Shopping seller entries often show no website URL. Find the website by searching the seller name on Google in a new tab [L0460.009].
  • The Google Shopping seller list only shows some of the more popular online stores, not every seller [L0460.008].
  • When a product shows only one or a few sellers, click the more stores option to list the other sellers offering the same item. This reveals sellers you have not yet checked [L0459.028].
  • Price filters in the Google Shopping sidebar are not the right tool for narrowing a niche. Use keywords that describe the target product tier, so the results already sit in your price range [L0806.007].
  • Price filters: one 2021 source suggests adding a price filter (for example 200 dollars and up) on the left side to remove cheap products for competitor research, but not for the popularity test, because it hides the most popular products and their average prices [L0801.015]. (older: 2021) Another 2021 source says not to apply price filters when researching a category, because they skew the picture. Check what Google shows by default as the most popular products [L0834.006]. (older: 2021)

Finding sellers: popular searches, brand lists and marketplaces

  • On a competitor's site, check the popular searches list for the niche keyword. It can surface additional products and brands that help find more online-only sellers [L0459.030].
  • The header menu of a seller's website may contain a "Brands" item. It opens an alphabetical page of all brands the store sells, which is a quick alternative to the category brand filter [L0460.029].
  • On a niche category page of a large marketplace such as Wayfair, open the left navigation, click Brand, then click show more to expand the full brand list [L0459.027].
  • A store with a brand that has a Wayfair subcategory for your niche counts Wayfair as a competitor, even if it stocks few products directly. Potential customers may shop there [L0803.014]. (2022)
  • A large online marketplace that already dropships most of its catalogue can confirm a niche as dropship-friendly before smaller sellers are checked. Its brand filter gives a rough supplier count, which can be recorded as a rounded figure when the list is very long [L0459.026].

Checking each seller on its website

Procedure: ordered checks for each seller

  1. Count the products and brands on the category page, and hover over product images to see whose logo they carry. [L0459.009]
  2. Open the footer menu and look for Find a store, store locations, showroom, pick-up options or a warehouse. [L0459.009]
  3. Open the About Us page, then the Contact Us page, which are the two most useful pages for stores, warehouses and fulfilment. [L0459.009]
  4. If an address is listed, copy it into Google and open it in Google Maps Street View to see what the building is. [L0459.009]
  5. Check the brand count in the category sidebar filter. [L0459.009]

Checks in order of priority

  • Check the header menu first for a "Stores" link or a store locator, since these signal physical locations. If nothing appears in the header, check the footer for an address [L0460.010].
  • On the About us page, look for stores, warehouses or a physical location in the US beyond an office. Mentions of design, production or tech teams in other countries do not by themselves show a physical store in the US [L0460.011].
  • Check the returns page for returns sent to a warehouse [L0460.012].
  • Check the footer for a physical address. A footer with only phone, live chat and email suggests online-only [L0460.010].
  • Check the About us page for mentions of a store, warehouse or physical location in the US beyond an office [L0460.006 step 12].

Verifying an address

Procedure A: Google Maps check (2024)

  1. Go to Google Maps. [L0253.026]
  2. Search for the business address. [L0253.026]
  3. Click Street View. [L0253.026]
  4. Observe whether it is a warehouse, commercial space, or mailing service. [L0253.026]
  5. Google the address to see if multiple other businesses use it. [L0253.026]
  6. If many businesses share the address, it is likely a mail-forwarding service, not a warehouse. [L0253.026]

Procedure B: address check in Google Maps

  1. Copy the address shown in the footer or on the Contact us page. [L0460.013]
  2. Open a new tab and go to google.com. [L0460.013]
  3. Paste the address and search. [L0460.013]
  4. Open Google Maps from the results or the Maps link to see the location on the map. [L0460.013]
  5. Use Street View to see the building. A private home suggests the business runs from home and is not a warehouse. An office suite address in an office building is most likely not a warehouse. [L0460.013]
  6. If the address shows a store, a showroom or a warehouse, the seller is not a future competitor. [L0460.013]

Procedure C: verify before counting (2023)

  1. Copy the address from the competitor's site. [L0804.018]
  2. Paste it into Google Maps. [L0804.018]
  3. Check street view if available, otherwise the overhead view, to see whether it is a warehouse or other business. [L0804.018]

Procedure D: address check with fallback call (2020)

  1. Copy the address shown on the competitor's website. [L0551.002]
  2. Search the address on Google Maps and open Street View to see the building. [L0551.002]
  3. If the building is a shipping store (for example a UPS store) or a mailbox forwarding service, treat the competitor as a dropshipper. [L0551.002]
  4. If the address does not appear on Maps or cannot be seen, call or email the competitor and ask whether it has a showroom and what its opening hours are. [L0551.002]

Reading the building

  • Google Maps with Street View shows what kind of building an address is: a home, an office or a warehouse. A suite number in an office building is a sign that the address is an office, not stock storage [L0460.014].
  • A street address alone does not prove a physical showroom. Verify the building first [L0551.001].

Counting brands

  • Brand filter on a category page: click "More filters" and choose "Brand". Some sites label it "Manufacturer". The dropdown lists all brands with the number of products per brand in that category [L0460.015].
  • On sites without a "More filters" brand option, look in the sidebar under "Types" for "Brands", click to expand it, then click "More" to see the full brand list with product counts per brand in the category [L0460.016].
  • Count how many brands a store sells and how many products each has in the category, since the split across brands shows it is not making its own stock [L0460.018].
  • Only multi-brand sellers count, and their brand count builds the supplier estimate [L0459.007].
  • A seller that lists a logo from another brand but sells one brand is a single-supplier store. Do not count it as a multi-brand range [L0459.008].

Dropship-friendly test: complete procedures

Variant A: worksheet procedure (14 steps)

  1. Select one of your top 3-5 pre-qualified niches from your worksheet. [L0050.009]
  2. Go to Google.com. [L0050.009]
  3. Paste the niche name into the search box. [L0050.009]
  4. Click the Shopping tab. [L0050.009]
  5. Scroll to "All products." [L0050.009]
  6. Open multiple unfamiliar store listings in new tabs. [L0050.009]
  7. For each store, check if it sells multiple brands; exclude stores selling only their own brand. [L0050.009]
  8. For stores with multiple brands, check the footer, About page, and Contact page for physical retail indicators. [L0050.009]
  9. Exclude any sites showing physical locations, store locators, or "Find a store" links; focus only on online-only operations. [L0050.009]
  10. For remaining online-only stores, look for dropshipping signs: shipping delays, warehouse shipments, or mentions of multiple suppliers. [L0050.009]
  11. Navigate to product category pages within qualifying stores. [L0050.009]
  12. Use brand filters or brand lists to count the distinct brands offered. [L0050.009]
  13. Verify the niche has at least 20 suppliers (100+ indicates strong potential). [L0050.009]
  14. Update your worksheet: enter "Yes" or "No" in the "DS Friendly" column and record the estimated supplier count in the "Estimated Suppliers" column. [L0050.009]

Variant B: Google Shopping procedure (6 steps, plus the seller checks)

  1. Copy the niche name from the worksheet. [L0459.004]
  2. Search it on google.com and click the Shopping tab. [L0459.004]
  3. Scroll past the subcategories to the All products section. [L0459.004]
  4. Right-click each product from a seller you do not know and open it in a new tab. [L0459.004]
  5. Skip sellers you already know, large marketplaces such as eBay, and sellers whose products are clearly their own brand. [L0459.004]
  6. Work through the sellers one by one using the checks in the seller-check procedure above. [L0459.004]

Variant C: worksheet tab procedure (19 steps)

  1. Open the niche research worksheet and find the column "DS Friendly". [L0460.006]
  2. Go to google.com and search: buy [niche keyword] online. [L0460.006]
  3. Right-click each top text ad and organic result and choose "Open link in new tab". [L0460.006]
  4. Skip sites you already know have warehouses or physical stores (for example chains owned by large retailers, or marketplaces with fulfillment centers) and do not open them. [L0460.006]
  5. Open the remaining relevant results in new tabs. [L0460.006]
  6. Go to Google Shopping and search the niche keyword alone. [L0460.006]
  7. In the sidebar, scroll to the bottom to "Seller" and click "More". [L0460.006]
  8. Open the listed sellers in new tabs, skipping the ones you already know have stores or warehouses. [L0460.006]
  9. For sellers without a URL in the list, copy the seller name, open a new tab (Command+T on Mac), search the name on Google and open its website. [L0460.006]
  10. On each site, check the header menu for stores, locations or a store locator. [L0460.006]
  11. Check the footer for a physical address. A footer with only phone, live chat and email suggests online-only. [L0460.006]
  12. Check the About us page for mentions of a store, warehouse or physical location in the US beyond an office. [L0460.006]
  13. Check the returns page for returns sent to a warehouse. [L0460.006]
  14. If an address is found, copy it and check it in Google Maps (see the Maps procedure). [L0460.006]
  15. Check whether the site sells for multiple brands in the niche category (see the brand filter setting). [L0460.006]
  16. Close tabs of sites with a physical presence or single-brand sites. [L0460.006]
  17. Keep open the online-only, multi-brand sites for future reference. [L0460.006]
  18. If online-only, multi-brand sellers are found, mark the niche "yes" in the DS Friendly column. [L0460.006]
  19. Repeat for all remaining winning niches. Niches marked "no" move to the bottom of the list. [L0460.006]

Variant D: fast test (2024, 6 steps)

  1. Search the target niche on google.com. [L0806.001]
  2. Ignore the sponsored ads and open the top five or so organic (non-sponsored) results in separate tabs. [L0806.001]
  3. Check each store for a physical location: look first in the footer for an address, then on the contact page for a home base or local pickup option. [L0806.001]
  4. If an address appears, search it on Google to see whether it is a retail store or warehouse. [L0806.001]
  5. Stores with a physical location or stock are not treated as future competitors. [L0806.001]
  6. Keep only online-only stores that sell for multiple brands. If the niche has such stores, it is drop ship friendly. [L0806.001]

Competitor limits: how many to track

  • Go deepest into the top three competitors rather than spreading thin across many [L0801.011]. (older: 2021)
  • Identify the top three future competitors by traffic using a competition tool [L0247.008]. (older: 2021)
  • Identify the top two or three online-only competitors, or the first page of Google results [L0969.018]. (older: 2022)
  • Select at least three competing stores, or five to six if more are available [L0963.097]. (older: 2022)
  • A niche with only a few dropshipping competitors can be a first store if it passes all criteria [L0359.015].
  • The niche is only proven when several qualifying online-only, multi-brand sellers are found. Keep checking sites until there are enough to contact for supplier approval [L0460.021].
  • One online store is not proof that a niche is dropship-friendly. It may be a special case, so look for several online-only, multi-brand sellers [L0460.027].
  • Keep only those competitors that fit the online-only and niche criteria [L0468.023 step 4]. (older: 2020)

Traffic rank check

Which rank to read: country, not global

  • Always record the country rank for the country where you plan to do business, never the global traffic rank. A competitor is only relevant through its traffic in your target market [L0687.005].
  • Using a global rank instead of the country rank misstates how big the market is in your country and can make a niche look stronger or weaker than it is [L0687.006].
  • A country traffic rank is a position among all websites in that country. A rank of 91 means the site is the 91st most visited website there. A low number is good [L0687.007].
  • In SimilarWeb, use the country rank for the target market, for example the US rank for US sales [L0836.017, L0891.011].
  • For a dropshipping business that targets one country, the country rank matters more than the global rank, because global traffic from other countries does not help [L0891.013].
  • Read competitor rank from the result for the target country, not the global result [L0247.009]. (older: 2021)
  • A competitor with a good global ranking but no ranking in the target country is probably getting little traffic and is not a top competitor [L0962.091]. (older: 2021)
  • SimilarWeb shows a country rank only for countries where the site is popular. To check another market, such as the UK or Australia, search the same URL and read the rank listed for that country. No rank there means little traffic in that market [L0891.019].
  • If Alexa shows only a global rank and no US rank, the site has very low traffic in the United States [L0960.076]. (older: 2019)

Benchmarks for rank

  • A traffic rank of about 4,000 is excellent. Anything under 100,000 is acceptable. For a site that sells a wide range of related items, aim for competitors around 30,000 to 40,000 [L0963.098]. (older: 2022)
  • Treat the lowest number as the strongest player when comparing future competitors in SimilarWeb. There is no true number one, since platforms like Facebook or Google usually hold the top spots [L0965.014]. (2024)

Collecting the rank in SimilarWeb

  • SimilarWeb shows traffic and ranking data for any website. The free version, used without an account, is enough to collect the country rank for competitor research. A paid plan is not required [L0891.009].
  • SimilarWeb ranks websites by traffic within the country where they are most popular, with 1 as the highest. A lower number means more traffic. The rank is only a comparison tool for the country you plan to sell in [L0891.012].
  • The traffic ranks collected here are used later to choose the niche and to identify the most popular sites within it. At this stage they are only gathered, not yet interpreted [L0891.016].
  • Do not spend time on other data SimilarWeb shows at this stage. Only the country rank is needed, and other data can easily lead to a time-consuming detour [L0891.015].
  • For a free account as of July 18, 2024, click "skip this step" during registration to bypass the trial offer and unlock the limited plan [L0061.002].

Caveats on traffic data

  • Use the traffic rank of a competitor as a rough guide only. Estimates from tools such as Alexa are no longer reliable in 2026 and must be checked against current data [L0468.029].
  • A competitor's traffic rank is a signal of demand. Check that the rank tool used is one option among others [L0468.024]. (older: 2020)
  • Supplier websites are showcase pages and are not expected to have high traffic. Judge the competition by the retail stores that sell in the niche [L0801.012]. (older: 2021)
  • If the niche-level stores show low traffic, broaden the category one level, find the top stores there, and check their traffic. Use those larger stores to judge demand [L0801.013]. (older: 2021)
  • A niche with zero Alexa rank but other data available usually indicates a tool glitch rather than a real lack of traffic. Check the ranking by testing other large websites before excluding the niche [L0961.044]. (older: 2020)
  • Competitors with no Alexa rank usually mean little traffic [L0664.043]. (older: 2020)
  • Competitors with no rank because the tool counts zero when no country is listed. A site with no country listed counts as zero [L0359.018].

Older Alexa-era rules (2019-2021)

  • An Alexa rank under 1 million (as of 2020) is the threshold for competitor sites in a niche. A niche whose competitors have no Alexa rank usually means little traffic, so look for at least one ranked store in a wider category that includes the niche [L0664.043]. (older: 2020)
  • A niche with weak competitor Alexa rankings can still be pursued if other criteria pass: enough brands and products, and enough search traffic in keyword tools. Abandoning it is not automatic [L0664.044]. (older: 2020)
  • If a niche's competitor sites rank only globally, the niche is generally not viable. Similar sites that sell related products and rank within the top 100,000 on the Alexa US ranking can still justify going ahead [L0961.043]. (older: 2020)
  • Alexa.com can be used for free to look up competitor sites. Scroll to the bottom of the page and use the "browse all sites" search field to enter a competitor's domain. No paid plan is needed [L1002.009]. (older: 2019)

Using the list

  • A competitor's category list shows how broad the store is. A store that sells many related categories, such as a large home retailer, may reveal secondary niches you could later build a separate store for [L0687.016].
  • Once a first store in one niche is profitable, look at the related categories of a broad competitor and consider building a second store in one of those categories [L0687.011].
  • Note every category a competitor sells beyond your target niche. A broad store that sells related categories can reveal secondary niches for a second store later [L0687.010].
  • Before counting a competitor in the sheet, check the address and whether it is online-only [L0804.018, L0460.013].
  • Supplier websites or competitors with no rank: record them, but they do not count toward a pass [L0687.008].

Worked cases

  • CNC Machines. Multiple direct-to-consumer manufacturers were found, one online-only future competitor was found, and only 8 total suppliers were estimated. Result: marked dropship-friendly but weak because the supplier count is too low [L0050.010].
  • Outdoor Kitchens. Wayfair and Zoro sold over 100 distinct brands, with strong demand, pricing and profit margins. Result: marked as a strong niche candidate [L0050.011].
  • Three validated future competitors. Wayfair (traffic rank 91 in the US, sells everything home), Zoro (2,350, sells tools, restaurant supplies and furniture), and Home Outlet Direct (50,116, sells appliances, grills and outdoor products). All three rank under 100,000, confirming strong market demand [L0051.009].
  • Three US competitor ranks, recorded at the time of the lesson. A furniture store with a lighting category had a US country rank of 38,442. A lighting store had 84,612. A home decor and lighting store had 21,829 [L0891.014].
  • Failing example. Three competitors in a target country with ranks of 200,000 and 500,000, and a third site showing no rank because its traffic is very low. None falls below 100,000, so this is a bad sign and the niche fails this test. Return to the list and look for more competitors [L0687.009].
  • Marketplace example. A large online marketplace that dropships most of its catalogue can confirm a niche before smaller sellers are checked [L0459.026].
  • Benchmarks for scale. A furniture and decor online store with over 300 brands and over 350,000 products, a lighting seller with over 100,000 lights, and a chandelier category with 1,855 products from one store. These figures show what an online-only multi-brand seller looks like [L0460.022].
  • Build-your-own case. If a niche has 20 or more brands and no dedicated niche store exists, you may build that niche store yourself. A missing competitor store is not a reason to reject the niche [L0551.005]. (older: 2020)

Where the sources disagree

  • Amazon and Wayfair. In 2019, sources said to exclude Amazon, Wayfair and other warehouse-based operations from validation [L0243.045]. (older: 2019) Another 2019 source accepted Amazon and multi-category marketplaces as competition research [L0243.037]. (older: 2019) From 2022 on, Wayfair counts as a competitor through its subcategories [L0803.014] and a large marketplace can confirm a niche [L0459.026]. Use the marketplace to confirm the niche, but count only pure online-only sellers for validation [L0460.003].
  • Number of competitors. One 2019 source requires one to three online retailers [L0243.037]. A 2021 source requires at least three online-only stores [L0853.029]. A 2022 source says two or three are enough [L0969.018]. A 2023 source says two in range are enough [L0964.005]. The current test requires at least one online-only, multi-brand competitor [L0459.017]. Use the strictest source that applies to your decision, and check the newest guidance.
  • Traffic threshold. The current test uses 1-100,000 [L0051.006, L0687.008]. A 2023 source uses 1-1,000,000 [L0964.005]. A 2021 source accepts 169,000 [L0247.008]. Use 100,000 for the pass decision; use the 1,000,000 figure only as a broader screen when the niche is otherwise strong.
  • Supplier count. The worksheet bar is 20 suppliers, with 100+ strong [L0050.009]. The transcript also names 100 suppliers as the bar to move to the next research stage [L0459.017]. Check the stricter threshold before advancing.
  • Price filter. One 2021 source recommends a price filter for competitor research [L0801.015]. Two other sources say not to use it [L0834.006, L0806.007]. Use keywords for the price tier instead of a sidebar filter.
  • Parent company. A physical-retail seller is excluded even if it is multi-brand [L0459.010]. An online-only store owned by a company with physical stores still counts [L0460.020, L0804.017]. The distinction is the site itself: a store locator or pick-up option on the site excludes it; an online-only site owned by a physical company is counted, with a possible harder approval.
  • Physical presence for supplier and competitor. A warehouse or store is not a reason to avoid a supplier [L0357.006]. It excludes a competitor from validation [L0853.030]. Both apply to different decisions.
  • Country versus global rank. Competitor validation uses the country rank [L0687.005, L0891.011]. Supplier extraction in 2023 used a global rank between 1 and 100,000 [L0855.088]. Use country rank for validation; the 2023 rule applies only to finding suppliers.
  • Column order. The sheet layout in one source lists Store name, URL, Traffic rank, Niches sold [L0687.015]. Another lists URL, Store Name, Traffic Rank, Niches Sold [L0543.003]. The SimilarWeb procedure puts the rank in column C [L0891.010]. Use the layout your worksheet already has and keep the rank column consistent with that.
  • Stand-up desks example. The source phrases the narrow and broad levels as "desks to stand-up desks is the narrow case", while also calling a broader category like desks the fallback [L0941.007]. The consistent reading is that stand-up desks is the narrower term and desks the broader fallback.

Common mistakes

  • Testing your entire niche list at once [L0050.004].
  • Treating a direct-to-consumer site as proof of the dropshipping model [L0050.005].
  • Confusing a brand's footer logo with a multi-brand range [L0459.008].
  • Counting a single product listing as a dropshipper without verifying the brand [L0459.020].
  • Assuming a store is a dropshipper from its name or a single listing [L0459.020].
  • Opening every search result or every seller in Google Shopping, which adds clutter and wastes time [L0460.026].
  • Treating one online store as proof that a niche is dropship-friendly [L0460.027].
  • Using a competitor's global traffic rank instead of its country rank [L0687.006].
  • Spending time on the rest of the SimilarWeb page at this stage [L0891.015].
  • Excluding a niche because of an Alexa tool glitch without testing other large sites first [L0961.044]. (older: 2020)
  • Keeping a default myshopify.com store as a real competitor without checking its traffic rank [L0806.011].
  • Relying on a store that ships directly from China: long delivery times, inconsistent quality, and a famine-and-feast revenue pattern [L0554.019].

Decision: pass, fail, or loser list

  • Mark the niche "Yes" in the DS Friendly column when online-only sellers are found. If only sellers with a physical location (warehouse, showroom or retail store) are found, mark it "No" and move the niche to the loser section [L0460.004].
  • Niches marked "No" move to the bottom of the list [L0460.006 step 19].
  • A niche passes when it has at least one online-only, multi-brand competitor with no physical stores or warehouses, and at least 20 suppliers [L0459.017].
  • A niche passes the traffic check when at least one future competitor has a country traffic rank between 1 and 100,000. Otherwise, reject it or keep working the list [L0051.007, L0687.008].
  • A niche that passes the worksheet test: update the worksheet with "Yes" or "No" in the DS Friendly column and the estimated supplier count [L0050.009].
  • A niche with a pattern of a few online-only sellers that carry many brands, confirmed by the About Us or footer pages, stays in play [L0459.025].
  • A niche with a few dropshipping competitors can be a first store if it passes all criteria [L0359.015].
  • A niche that still stocks its own products can be a second store, after the first produces steady cash flow [L0356.005].
  • A niche with weak competitor rankings is not automatically abandoned if other criteria pass [L0664.044]. (older: 2020)
  • If a niche has no dedicated niche stores but has 20 suppliers and at least 100 products, it is not a reason to reject it [L0960.016]. (older: 2019)
  • A niche in which all top competitors are broad physical retailers, with no strong sub-niche sites, is a warning sign [L0358.021]. (older: 2020)

Identifying Online-Only Multi-Brand Sellers

  • A future competitor is an online store that sells to end customers in the niche. Suppliers are not future competitors and are not tested on traffic rank. [L1097.010]
  • Build the candidate list from Google results, not from ads:
    1. Search your niche on Google.
    2. Look only at the top organic results, not sponsored ads.
    3. Check each site's traffic rank. The highest-ranked and most niche-specific sites are your top future competitors. [L1279.007]
  • Online-only stores that sell a broader category can still count, if their exact sub-niche holds at least 20 brands and more than 100 products within it. [L1094.023, L1094.122] (2023)
  • Competitors with a traffic rank under about one million are the most useful to study for suppliers, conversion approaches and promotions. Stores on pages three and four of search are less useful sources. [L1283.065] (2023)
  • When you research a brand, check that the websites that carry it are online-only retailers. This shows the brand already works with online-only stores. [L1277.026] (2020, older)
  • One established store that dropships and carries about 25 brands is enough evidence that a niche is dropship-friendly. Its position in the search results does not matter. [L1275.008] (2019, older)
  • Before you build on a niche, check the supplier list for online-only stores that already sell the products. If none exist, the supplier may refuse to work with a store that has no physical location, or may offer better prices to physical stores. Check this risk first. [L1114.014] (2023)
  • Industry knowledge does not replace the dropship test. Products that fail the test should not be used because you know the industry. [L1114.015] (2023)

Excluding Warehouses, Showrooms and Physical Stores

  • Online-only competitors that hold warehouses or stock are not counted as future competitors. You cannot assume their suppliers are dropship-friendly. [L1279.011] (2021, older)
  • Future competitors with a physical showroom, store or warehouse are not counted. Suppliers can still be extracted from the pure online dropship stores among them. [L1113.004] (2022, older)
  • A future competitor should not have its own warehouse and should be based in your country of operation. A single showroom is acceptable. A nationwide chain of showrooms is a poor sign for a supplier. [L1281.024] (2022, older)
  • In a niche where most brands sell through physical showrooms, the niche is usually not dropship-friendly, even if the showrooms are temporarily closed. Brands that lost retail sales may still approve new online sellers, so contacting them can be worth a try. [L1279.013] (2021, older)
  • The units give no separate rule for DTC sites. Apply the end-customer test and the warehouse and showroom tests above to them. [L1097.010, L1279.011]

Verifying Addresses

  • A supplier or competitor site that shows no showroom, retail store or warehouse address is treated as dropship-friendly enough to source from. [L1113.001] (2022, older)

  • To check an unclear address:

    1. Paste the address into Google Search.
    2. Open the Maps result.
    3. Zoom in and check Street View.
    4. Decide whether it is a real building, such as a warehouse or office, or a virtual address, such as a shipping-store mailbox or PO box.

    If no address is shown at all, the site can still be used for supplier sourcing. [L1113.002] (2022, older)

  • Google Maps and Street View show whether a competitor or supplier has a warehouse or showroom. An office without an attached warehouse still counts as an online-only business. [L1279.012] (2021, older)

Traffic-Rank Tools and Procedures

Tool status

  • SimilarWeb is now used for traffic data and replaced the Alexa rank. The research tool site still shows the same traffic data through its first tool in the toolbar. [L1092.033] (2022, older)
  • SimilarWeb's free market research allows 15 free searches per week. The free version does not require an email address. It shows traffic data for competitor and niche analysis. [L1083.003]
  • Do not start a SimilarWeb free trial to get market data. Some users reported that the signup flow asked them to link an Amazon seller account. Use the free search allowance instead, which gives up to 15 searches per week without an email address. [L1083.016]

Key figures

  • In market research, use the Global Rank and Country Rank from the extension to compare competitor websites. Use the extension to find these two ranks, not to read every data point. [L1332.005]

Extension procedure

  1. Open the target website in Chrome.
  2. Click the SimilarWeb icon in the toolbar.
  3. Accept the terms the extension asks for. Its panel opens on the right side of the page.
  4. Scroll down the right-hand panel to about halfway, to the ranking section.
  5. Read the Global Rank and the Country Rank. The other traffic data is further down the panel. [L1332.003]

Country-rank test for a future competitor

  1. Confirm the site is a store selling to end customers in the niche, not a supplier.
  2. Open the competitor's website and click the SimilarWeb Chrome extension.
  3. Open the ranking section and read the Country rank for your target country.
  4. Count the competitor as a qualifying future competitor if its country rank is 1,000,000 or lower. [L1097.012]

Setting and caveats

  • When you check a competitor's traffic rank in the keyword tool, enter the full URL, including https:// and www. If every competitor shows zero, the URL is probably incomplete. Test the tool with a known large site, such as wayfair.com, to confirm it works. [L1279.005] (2021, older)
  • A product with very few competitors and an unusually high-traffic sample does not necessarily have low traffic only because of low ad investment. Low traffic can also come from weak SEO or from too few people searching for the product. [L1115.024] (2024)

Traffic Thresholds and Fallbacks

Alexa-era rules (older)

  • For a valid niche, the Alexa ranks of the leading competitor sites should fall between 1 and 100,000. Ranks far above that signal too little traffic to support a store. [L1111.001] (2020, older)
  • Example Alexa ranks of about 4,000, 35,500, 42,000, 55,000 and 94,000 were judged acceptable under the 100,000 threshold. A rank close to 100,000 still passes. [L1111.002] (2020, older)
  • A competitor without an Alexa US ranking has traffic below the threshold at which Alexa displays a rank, so its traffic is smaller than that of ranked sites. [L1069.038] (2019, older)

When the traffic is too low

  • If competitor sites in a niche have too little traffic to measure, widen the niche one level and check again. Example order: brown leather chairs, then leather chairs, then chairs. [L1111.003] (2020, older)
  • If niche competitors show zero in a small market such as the UK, look at a related, broader category where the top sites have measurable rankings. [L1277.019] (2020, older)
  • A competitor with a zero US Alexa rank but a high global rank can still be studied. Prefer competitors with a higher US rank as the benchmark, because they show that demand is active. [L1111.017] (2020, older)
  • Competitors with a zero US rank and a high global rank are weaker benchmarks, because their traffic does not come from the target market. Use them for ideas, not for volume estimates. [L1111.018] (2020, older)
  • Use the highest-ranking organic competitor sites in your niche as benchmarks. Check their traffic rank in a tool such as SimilarWeb, and aim for sites ranked roughly between the top 1 and 1 million. [L1115.022] (2024)

Competitor Counts and Limits

  • A niche should show at least two to three future competitors ranked within the top 100,000 on SimilarWeb. These should be online-only brands without showrooms. [L1287.012] (2025)
  • Submit the niche with its listed future competitors for manual verification. The reviewer checks that they really compete with you. [L1287.012] (2025)
  • At least one future competitor must have a country traffic rank of 1,000,000 or better in the country where you plan to sell. The other future competitors can rank lower and still count. [L1097.011] (2025)
  • A store in a broader category counts as a competitor if its exact sub-niche holds at least 20 brands and more than 100 products. [L1094.023, L1094.122] (2023)
  • One established dropshipping store with about 25 brands is enough to confirm a niche is dropship-friendly. [L1275.008] (2019, older)

Filling the Future Competitors Sheet

Working order

  1. Search the niche on Google and take the top organic results. [L1279.007]
  2. Confirm each site sells to end customers and is online-only. Exclude suppliers. [L1097.010, L1279.011, L1113.004]
  3. Check the address and Street View for warehouses and showrooms. Exclude competitors with a warehouse, and exclude competitors with more than a single showroom in the country. [L1113.002, L1279.012, L1281.024]
  4. Get the Country Rank for your target country and record it. [L1097.012, L1332.003]
  5. Check the count and thresholds for the niche. [L1287.012, L1097.011]
  6. Record each site in the sheet. [L1159.010]

Sheet columns

  • Store each competitor's website in the WEBSITE column. [L1159.010]
  • Label each competitor as BRANDED or DROPSHIPPING. Use the label to judge whether it is a real brand or a dropshipping store. [L1159.010]
  • Rate each competitor GOOD, OK or BAD. [L1159.010]

Further details

  • Competitors that sell with physical locations are not reliable signs that brands work with online retailers; only online-only competitors show drop-ship-friendly brands. [L0969.019]

  • To check whether a product can be drop shipped, do the following. 1. Find competitors who sell it online. 2. If none exist, broaden the search one level. 3. Check large multi-brand retailers such as Wayfair to see whether many brands make the product. [L1068.019]

  • A niche can be drop-ship friendly even when the sites selling it also carry other niches or product types. A niche-specific store is not required if the supplier and product thresholds are met. [L1068.081]
