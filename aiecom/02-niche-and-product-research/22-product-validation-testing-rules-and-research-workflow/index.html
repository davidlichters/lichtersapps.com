2.22 Product validation, testing rules and research workflow

Research Volume and Shortlist Size

  • Research at least 10 products using the course framework, enter all of them into the Product Research Sheet, and validate each one before advancing. Progression is blocked until all three conditions are met: 10 products researched, all 10 entered in the sheet, and each validated with the framework. [L0038.001, L0038.002]
  • Build a research list of at least 10 product ideas, not just one. [L0280.004]
  • Research at least 15 product ideas before submitting anything for approval. A shortlist of 10 to 15 ideas is the minimum before narrowing down. [L0491.011]
  • Aim for 10 to 12 candidate products per research session, each backed by its active ad count, targeted countries and store data. [L1172.014]
  • One research session can surface about 15 to 20 products, and the research itself should take about three to five days. [L1436.010]
  • Use one or two research tools that together give at least 10 to 15 candidate products. If a tool lacks the filters you need, switch to a free method, such as the burner account method or a Chrome extension method for Facebook. [L0363.002]
  • Research 10 to 15 potential products across different niches rather than committing to one niche at the start. For each product, record the margin, whether it is new, whether it solves a problem, the value it gives the customer, and how many marketing angles it offers. [L0363.006]
  • Build a sheet of 10 to 20 candidate products before committing any budget. [L0639.044]
  • Build a Facebook product research list of at least 10, ideally 10 to 15, products so they can be ranked later. Add a column for the Facebook page creation date next to the ad library link in the research sheet. [L1183.003]
  • Keep a list of 5 to 10 solid, validated products ready at all times, because individual products fail and the next one must already be prepared. Product research is time-consuming, so start it early. [L0693.003]
  • Before competitor analysis, build a shortlist of roughly 10 to 15 product candidates that already meet basic criteria, such as being unique and not seen before in the market. [L0895.001]

Shortlists and Scoring

  • Refresh the product research list regularly, for example every two weeks, because active ad counts and trends change quickly. A product that was strong last month may lose active ads and need to be dropped. [L1172.058]
  • If a competitor's store page or ad link is not loading, do not add the product to the test list yet. Keep it on a watch list and recheck it once the page works. [L1172.060]
  • Score each viral product candidate from 1 to 10 on separate criteria, such as comment demand, product appeal and store quality, instead of one overall impression. A candidate with buyer comments scored 10 for demand can still be worth a test even if the product itself scores around 7. A candidate scoring 4 or 5 overall should be dropped. [L0530.029]
  • Rate each competition criterion on a numeric scale in the Facebook competitor scoring sheet, with 7 or higher marked as a red flag, then add the ratings into a total score to rank products. This method is a custom sheet, not a standard tool. [L0895.012]
  • Rank each product against the number of validation criteria it meets, since products that meet more criteria deserve a higher rank. Products that fail critical checks should be dropped or deprioritized. [L0708.021]
  • Rank final product candidates by how many marketing angles they have, how well they fit your criteria, their quality, and how little competition they face. The top-ranked product is the best candidate to launch. [L0636.019]
  • Use the Weighting column to rank candidate products against each other after the four phases are complete. Products with better engagement, lower competition and a workable break-even ROAS should receive higher weights. [L1159.016]
  • Score the product list in a spreadsheet with a checkbox for each criterion. Products that miss criteria drop to the bottom with a rank of 1 out of 5, while products that meet them rise with a higher rank. [L1194.005]
  • Use a 1 to 10 opportunity scale when comparing niches. Low-scoring opportunities (around 1 to 5) rarely become profitable regardless of effort, so pursue niches scoring around 8 to 10. [L0924.003]
  • Rank new brands and products using the same analytics you use for your current products. Search volume is generally a good metric for this ranking. [L0920.018]
  • Use one consistent product name or search term for all research steps, because switching terms makes the data incomparable. Related terms can be used later in ads. [L0970.021]
  • Check the research tools for product ranking and sales data using the same method every time, so results stay comparable. Reliable niche decisions depend on consistent measurement, not on a single data point. [L1279.017] (Older source: Source A, 2021.)

Screening Criteria and Product Filters

  • Define validation criteria before choosing a product. The main failure for beginners is spending money on untested products until they give up. Use the results of each tested product to decide the next one. [L1440.008]
  • Do not select products based on personal intuition or random AliExpress browsing, because beginners cannot afford such guesses. Back each research step with data showing that the product is currently selling for others. [L0708.019]
  • Only promote products you have tested yourself and that actually do what they claim. Genuine belief in the product makes it easier to create convincing videos and sell it to customers. [L0657.004]
  • Before choosing products, check the price point, the number of available suppliers, and the competition on the first page of Google Shopping results. [L0671.022] (Older source: Source A, 2021.)
  • Check the product, its type, and its seasonality with this sequence:
    1. Find candidate products in a product research tool (Peak was used). [L0577.031]
    2. Check the product is legitimate and does what it claims, e.g. by reading the reviews on Amazon. [L0577.031]
    3. Check the product type: entertainment products that attract parents, such as activity products for children's summer holidays, suit seasonal selling. [L0577.031]
    4. Consider seasonality: pick products that fit the upcoming season, e.g. summer products such as water dispensers for offices and barbecues, or Apple Watch straps, which sell well in summer. [L0577.031]
    5. Check the platform fit: decide whether the product works on Facebook and TikTok. [L0577.031]
    6. Ask family, friends or partners whether they would buy the product, instead of relying on personal taste. [L0577.031]
    7. Test with a budget of about 200 dollars and see how it performs. [L0577.031]
  • Product selection order: first check whether the product has a lot of competition and whether it can be sold profitably, e.g. at a 3x markup on a cheap item. Then check that real demand exists in the market, since a very small niche makes scaling difficult compared with a large market like pets. [L0690.007]
  • Before testing a candidate, define a customer avatar, list several marketing angles for it, and estimate the total addressable market. Products with a clear audience, several angles and a large market are the ones worth testing. [L0363.079]
  • Judge the market before entering. If the product is new and ads run by others are working, check the problem size, the number of use cases, the store and the margin. These checks decide whether to proceed. [L1436.015]
  • Complete customer research, product research and primary text research before testing, so that the product page and ad copy are dialed in. Testing without them makes it much harder to know why an ad works. [L1476.051]
  • Check TikTok Shop traction before building a product page: a strong creator conversion ratio, steady revenue across weeks, and a concentration rate that shows sales are spread over several creators rather than one. Compare these numbers with the price and commission rate to see whether the product is worth testing. [L1188.027]
  • Keep a fixed set of criteria for every product, for example a product criteria checklist of six to seven points that defines the kind of product you want. [L1382.001]
  • Set clear criteria for the team, for example upsell potential and a maximum supplier cost of 30 USD on AliExpress. Clear criteria let the assistant screen products before the owner picks one. [L1431.005]
  • Products that sold well organically on TikTok do not automatically work with paid ads. Once a proof of concept exists, test horizontally within the same niche to find a product that converts with ads. [L0819.009]
  • Keep the full research order in mind. It runs: product criteria checklist, TikTok ad spy search, AliExpress supplier quality, competition and sales check, competitor store verification, and product and store quality check. [L1382.001]

Four-Phase Research Process and Sheet

  • Thorough product research happens in four phases, and each phase weeds out weak candidates before the next one begins. Use this four-stage sequence in order, because each stage filters out products before the more time-consuming checks begin. [L0636.001, L0636.002]
  • Work through product research in four phases, in this order: [L1159.003]
    1. Phase 1, Initial research (criteria based): enter the product name, the ad link, the competitor store link and the AliExpress supplier link (Ali).
    2. Phase 2, Competitor analysis: record the ad engagement (likes) and the competition level for the product.
    3. Phase 3, Market validation: record the market and mark whether the product should be tested (TEST?).
    4. Phase 4, Decide: enter COGS, selling price, competitor price, break-even ROAS (BE ROAS), mentor notes, your own notes and the weighting score.
  • Work through the product testing sheet in phase order. First research the product and review competitor ads, then fill in the competitor analysis (phase 2) with engagement (high or low) and competition (low, medium or high) plus your findings. Next set the market size as small, medium or large (phase 3), and finally record the yes/no decision on testing the product (phase 4). [L1162.010]
  • Mentor review of a shared product list lets two people compare candidates and pick the test product together, with a comment left per product. [L1162.009]

Demand Validation Before Testing

Signals and Their Limits

  • Beginners often overthink validation and ask whether a product is guaranteed to win. There is no guarantee, so testing is required. [L0347.010]
  • For validating a product before testing, look for active ads, recent creatives, and multiple advertisers. These signals are enough to justify a test. [L0347.011]
  • Look only at products already advertised on the ad platform, not untested items. [L0335.003]
  • Confirm product demand from several sources, such as supplier sales counts, the follower count and views of organic accounts, and ad activity. Do not base a decision on a single signal. [L0706.026]
  • Validate a product with data, such as sales and engagement signals, instead of choosing it by guessing. [L0914.005]
  • Only scale a product after validation shows it sells consistently. One lucky sale is not validation. [L0914.006]
  • Validate niche ideas with actual numbers and real market data gathered free online. When time is limited, choose the niche with more opportunity over the weaker one. [L0506.003]
  • Do not judge a product by one viral video or one spike in sales. Check that revenue holds across the Last 30 Days window and that the product has a rising trend before you commit. [L1479.032]
  • A product page with only about 10 reviews after a large viral spike is weak evidence of quality, and such reviews may be fake. Do not judge demand from review count alone; combine it with real order checks. [L0655.029]
  • A common product research guideline is a minimum of 300 orders before a product is considered proven. Treat this as a guideline and not an absolute rule, since some products with strong potential sell below this level. [L0644.040]
  • Low AliExpress order counts are not a reason to drop a product if the ads show strong engagement, such as several thousand likes on one ad. Check the CJ listing for the same product and test the product anyway. [L0661.007]
  • Test product variants separately instead of assuming a whole category works. In one example, one version of a door-lock style product sold while another version of the same product did not. Track each variant's sales on its own. [L0706.062]
  • Research the product category before launching videos for it. A lack of category research slows the first days of filming, because the creator has to learn what to show and how. [L0764.043]

Saturation and Trend Checks

  • Before testing, check when the first ad for the product started running. Look for viral ads already run for the product; if there are many, the market may be saturated. [L0502.035]
  • Search TikTok for old trending videos. If trends from several years ago are still circulating, the trend has likely passed. [L0502.035]
  • Check the product in ad-spy apps for old ads. [L0502.035]
  • Mark which candidates look saturated or heavily competed. Get an experienced outside opinion on saturation for the marked candidates, then test the ones that remain. [L0639.044]
  • If you are unsure whether a product is saturated, test it with a new TikTok account and no store. Building the store takes significant time, so skipping it removes much of the cost of a failed test. [L1444.010]
  • Before investing in a product from scratch, validate demand with a market test on Facebook, TikTok and Instagram. A small group of friends or testers cannot show whether a product can reach millions of buyers. [L0760.017, L0760.024] Test with an audience of roughly one thousand to tens of thousands of people on these platforms before committing money to inventory or custom product development. A small group of friends or testers cannot show whether a product will work at scale. [L0760.024]
  • Pushing a product that the market does not respond to leads to poor ad performance on social platforms. Founders should not rely on intuition alone and must let the market react. [L0760.018]
  • Test demand on Facebook and TikTok first, not Google. Product testing is about spending time and money efficiently. [L0577.037]
  • Check the trend with Google Trends and news, and collect marketing angles from Amazon reviews. [L0708.022]
  • For a product that is too new to check with Amazon reviews, the goal is proof of concept and demand, not quality certainty. Start with a small test spend. If the first two or three sales come in, stop or scale based on results, because the cost of failure stays small. [L0552.007]
  • Validate a product with a small amount of sales on your store before spending a large budget. The goal is proof of concept, not a large test. [L1219.047]
  • Before buying a large quantity of a custom-brand product, get approval of the concept by testing it. Scaling before the concept is validated increases chargeback and cash-flow risk. [L0351.021]
  • Dummy test order refunds: competitors rarely accept refund requests for test orders, and a cancellation request is often refused with a claim that the order already shipped. Plan for losing the order cost. [L0501.028]
  • Do not rush into buying a product or spending on it all at once after a single positive check. Test in small steps and compare multiple reference points, because a store's age and account history can change the reliability of dummy orders. [L1385.016]
  • Check the supplier or CJ shipping cost and the sourcing cost before testing. [L1176.001]

Conversion Depends on the Product

  • Conversion from views to sales depends heavily on the product. A good, unique product that solves a clear problem sold 5 units from under 50,000 views, while some other products got one sale or none from a million views. [L0764.025]
  • When a video or account does not convert, the cause could be the audience, the product, the videos or a mix of all three. Diagnose which one is responsible before changing everything. [L0764.026]
  • A 500 to 600 views per post result over three weeks at two to four posts per day, across several accounts and devices, suggested the product or the video angle was the cause rather than the account itself. [L0820.025]
  • Bottom-of-funnel ads work for market-aware products: universally understood items (phone cases, water bottles), or products already validated and run by competitors. For niche products that need explanation (specialized supplements), warm audiences with video ads first, then revisit bottom-of-funnel once you have repeat customers or brand awareness. [L0316.023]
  • Attention rate and click-through rate can be lower for a relevant audience that buys, so a low soft-metric ad that makes sales can still be profitable. Retargeting the people who clicked can also build a useful audience later. [L0995.037]

Research Cadence and Sources

  • Start product research while still going through the training rather than after it. Also check TikTok Shop, Kalodata and competitor stores for demand signals. [L0491.010]
  • The research approach is to launch small tests, look for a little demand and some sales, and only then go deeper. Deeper research uses sources such as Reddit, Amazon and emotion valence exports, which are fed into an AI tool like Claude for analysis. [L1175.006]
  • The Market Research habit is a daily 30-minute block whose purpose is to understand the target audience better through a structured research process. Doubling or more of that time is encouraged when possible. [L0456.015]

Testing Approaches and Product Counts

Choosing an Approach

  • Product testing falls into three approaches: [L1158.002]
    • Spam testing: many products tested quickly with little thought.
    • Continuous testing: a steady flow of products checked against fixed rules.
    • Branded testing: building a brand around one product with custom content and quality control.
    • Spam testing is not recommended, because it depends on luck and needs a large budget to test 50 to 60 products. [L1158.002]
  • Spam testing means testing roughly 3 to 5 products per day or 10 to 15 per week, with a hit rate of about 2 to 3 winners out of 10 tested products. [L1158.003]
  • Continuous testing with rule sets means testing about 1 to 2 products per week against defined criteria, rather than being tied to a single product or niche. [L1158.004]
  • The recommended approach combines rule-based continuous testing with a light branded approach, adding brand elements without investing heavily upfront. [L1158.006]
  • Product tests should be good enough rather than perfect, and should not consume much time or money, since a test can fail and the time spent should still produce learning. [L0349.005]
  • Early on, test products aggressively, since you cannot yet tell which products are good. Once you have a viral win and know how to create virality, shift to strict product selection. [L0372.013]
  • Early tests on weak products are useful for building the skill of going viral. Before investing heavily in a product, confirm it can produce sales. [L1429.037]
  • Product research has no shortcut. A product's potential is only known after spending money on it. Learn audience research as the core skill rather than relying on hiring freelancers for research. [L0690.012]
  • No single product research method is perfect, so a researcher should adapt a method that works for them rather than follow one rigidly. A method is worth keeping only when it has produced winners for that person. [L1382.002]
  • Product testing is about spending time and money efficiently. [L0577.037]

Time Budgets for Research

  • Spend about 6 to 8 hours on initial market research per product before testing. This is considered a reasonable investment. [L0363.009] (Source C figure, per product.)
  • Budget time per niche: about one hour at most, not days for the whole sheet. Doing the full check on an outdoor kitchen niche took about 15 minutes using this method. [L0459.022] (Source A, per niche.)
  • Spend only the research time that a niche needs. After the first few niches, you start to recognize patterns and can drop weak niches quickly, while the niches that need more work get extra time. [L0667.072] (Source A, 2025.)
  • The two figures measure different units: 6 to 8 hours covers one product in depth, while one hour covers a whole niche screen. Use the hour for screening niches and the 6 to 8 hours for products you are about to test. [L0363.009, L0459.022]

Stop Rules

  • If an ad has a good CTR but no sales after spending a lot (about $300 spent across several offers, creatives, and audiences), move on to the next product, since the data is no longer good. Continue only when the data is good. [L0650.021]
  • When a store has a good CTR but poor sales, test a different offer before moving on to another product. A good CTR means the ad is doing its job, and the offer is the likely problem. [L0650.022]
  • A loss of around $300 with sales still coming in can justify stopping, while a good CTR with few offers tested means more offers should be tested first. [L0650.023]
  • If the first two or three sales come in, stop or scale based on the results. [L0552.007]
  • If a product generates some sales but cannot be scaled, stop it rather than forcing it, and move the budget to the next product test. Scaling potential matters as much as early sales when choosing which product to keep. [L0349.035]
  • Use a rule set for every product: if the CTR is good, continue; if it is bad, try new creatives; if it is still bad after new creatives, move to the next product. [L0502.035]
  • Switch immediately to the next product when the CTR is bad. [L1184.022]
  • Products that miss criteria are dropped, not tested further. [L1194.005]

Sales Proof

  • A common product research guideline is a minimum of 300 orders before a product is considered proven. Treat this as a guideline and not an absolute rule, since some products with strong potential sell below this level. [L0644.040]
  • The 300-order guideline is for judging a product as proven. Early stages need only a few sales to justify continuing, as set out in the stop rules above. [L0644.040, L0552.007, L1219.047]

Diagnosing a Test: What to Change

  • Check a strong CTR before changing anything else. If a store has a good CTR and poor sales, test a different offer before moving on, because the ad is doing its job. [L0650.022]
  • Diagnose the audience, product, videos or a mix before changing everything. [L0764.026]
  • Test many products rather than a few, but change one variable at a time when diagnosing a test so you know what caused the result. [L0764.026, L0361.011]
  • Keep one fixed core audience, such as women over 40, and test every product against it, so the ad data stays comparable from product to product. Consistent audience data makes it faster to tell which products work. [L1184.067]
  • Test with ripped creatives: the test measures the product and the offer, not the quality of the creative. Creative quality is improved only later. [L1252.005]
  • A creative test uses at least 12 videos from the last 30 days, with a range of 12 to 24 videos. [L0704.002]
  • For a collection of many similar products, test sending ads to the collection page or a landing page built from it, not only to individual product pages. A collection landing page can add a hero section, testimonials and a product list. [L1315.038]
  • Test several designs or products, and send traffic both to collection pages and to individual hero products. [L1217.033]
  • Give a product new ad data before changing it. A product that does not perform after three to four days is replaced with a new product page. [L1412.062]

Google Shopping Diagnosis (Older)

  • Diagnosing high impressions with low clicks on Google Shopping: [L1085.030] (Older source: Source A, 2018.)
    1. In Google Ads, check which search terms and keywords trigger your ads. [L1085.030]
    2. If the keywords are very broad, accept a low click-through rate and add negative keywords to cut irrelevant searches. [L1085.030]
    3. Compare your price, promotions (such as free shipping), product images and ad position with the competitors showing for the same keywords. [L1085.030]
    4. Search the keyword yourself as a customer and fix what makes your listing less clickable. [L1085.030]
  • Diagnosing a 50% impression rate with only a few clicks per day: [L1085.031] (Older source: Source A, 2018.)
    1. Open Google Keyword Planner and check the monthly search volume of your keyword. [L1085.031]
    2. If search volume is low, people are not searching for the product, so reconsider the keyword or product. [L1085.031]
    3. If search volume is high, search the keyword on Google as a customer and compare your listing with the others. [L1085.031]
    4. Check whether your price, your image or the listing itself makes people skip your product. [L1085.031]
  • Google tests are for established products, while new product tests run on Facebook and TikTok. [L0577.037, L0920.011]

Scaling, Stock and Commitment

  • Track clicks and early traction on a product to decide whether to scale it, with profitability checked alongside demand. A trending product is only useful if the margin can still work at higher volume. [L1508.027]
  • A trending product list is used this way: [L1508.025]
    1. Research which products are trending in the niche. [L1508.025]
    2. List products that are not your own to test whether they get clicks. [L1508.025]
    3. If the products get clicks and traction, adjust the pricing. [L1508.025]
    4. Check that the product can be profitable at scale, for example by lowering cost through bulk shipping or a faster shipping method. [L1508.025]
    5. Go back to the manufacturers, replicate the winning product, and expand into its category. [L1508.025]
  • Once sales or sign-ups are coming in, you have proof the product sells, so you can justify the supplier order. [L1017.028]
  • For a new product, run ads and collect sales or pre-order sign-ups. [L1017.028]
  • If the product does not ship on time, email the buyers, explain the situation, and either refund them or give them the expected delivery window if they are willing to wait. [L1017.028]
  • Stock procedure for confirmed winners:
    1. Test several designs or products and send traffic both to collection pages and to individual hero products. [L1217.033]
    2. Check at least two weeks of sales data to find the top sellers and the best-selling variant of each. [L1217.033]
    3. Confirm that the best seller keeps selling consistently for about three weeks. [L1217.033]
    4. Order stock for about one month of sales for each confirmed top product. [L1217.033]
    5. During the stock order, keep ad parameters and performance unchanged, and watch sales for the next month. [L1217.033]
    6. If performance stays consistent or improves, scale the ad budget and order stock in proportion to the increase. [L1217.033]
  • Scaling potential matters as much as early sales when choosing which product to keep. [L0349.035]
  • Only scale after validation shows the product sells consistently. [L0914.006]
  • Spot-check your top 10 to 20 selling products regularly. For each product, check the Google Ads results, the Bing Ads results, and the top organic search results. [L0920.011] (Source A, no year given.)
  • Optimize the top products rather than the whole catalog. A supplier catalog with about 1,000 SKUs is updated first on the top 20%, which can be identified with the 80-20 rule. [L0272.005] (Older source: Source A, 2022.)
  • In a catalog of about 2,000 products, typically 200 to 300 products are the best sellers, so use a 200-product group as the starting point for paid campaigns. [L0471.011] (Source A, 2024.)

Catalog Optimization Procedure (Older)

  • Upload the supplier's full product catalog (e.g., 1,000 SKUs) with only SEO modifications: product name and meta descriptions. Do not rewrite product descriptions. [L0272.005] (Older source: Source A, 2022.)
  • Monitor Google Analytics and Google Ads data for 3+ months to identify which products gain traction. [L0272.005]
  • Apply the 80-20 rule: identify the top 20% of products (200 from 1,000) generating impressions and clicks. [L0272.005]
  • Further optimize only those top 200 by improving descriptions, enhancing pages, and increasing ad spend, while leaving others as they are. [L0272.005]
  • Continue until performance plateaus, then repeat for the next product tier. [L0272.005]

Relaunching and Revisiting Products

  • Do not abandon a niche that already shows sales and buyer trust, such as about 11,000 USD in two months. First optimize the existing ads and products within it. [L0836.018] (Source A, 2024.)
  • Expect the plan and the niche to change as sales data comes in. Adjust rather than hold to the first idea, and revisit projected sales regularly to see which direction works. [L0832.027] (Source A, no year given.)
  • If a niche was chosen using the blueprint criteria and is not profitable, do not switch niches right away. Troubleshoot the supplier, product pages, traffic and conversion elements first, because a new niche will often show the same problems. [L1086.007] (Older source: Source A, 2019.)
  • After passing the brand and product count test, continue with the evergreen demand test and price point tests. A niche that passes the count test still needs demand over time and workable price points. [L0357.008] (Older source: Source A, 2019.)
  • Systematic process for finding more products after a winner: [L1440.009]
    1. After every test, review each funnel stage: the ad, the store and the product, and find the weak point. [L1440.009]
    2. Use a strong click-through rate as proof that an ad angle works, and replicate that angle with other products. [L1440.009]
    3. Research products in the same niche continuously. [L1440.009]
    4. Study competitors: what they sell and which marketing angles they use. [L1440.009]
    5. Look for angles others are not using, for example in Amazon reviews. [L1440.009]
    6. Revive older products with new marketing angles. [L1440.009]
    7. Use customer reviews and feedback to improve the product, working with agents and manufacturers if needed; this improvement took about one to two months. [L1440.009]
  • Revisit a product once a new marketing angle or a new product page is ready. A product that failed a test can be retried with a new page, as set out in the three-to-four-day rule. [L1412.062, L1440.009]
  • Lessons from each failed product test should carry into the next test, so product page, copy and creatives improve from test to test instead of starting from zero each time. Product selection should become stricter as the operator gains experience. [L0349.004]
  • If a product that does not perform is revived, keep the same core audience so the data stays comparable. [L1184.067]

Delegating Product Research

  • Do product research yourself across several products, niches and countries before handing it to a team. Without knowing which niche works for you, a team returns a huge generic product list that is hard to use. [L1358.004]
  • Delegating product research (short version): [L1358.006]
    1. Give the team member access to your product research software. [L1358.006]
    2. Show them exactly which product criteria and customer problems you are looking for. [L1358.006]
    3. Have them send you a list of products they believe will work at the end of each week. [L1358.006]
    4. Scan the list, choose the products you like, and start testing them. [L1358.006]
  • Delegating product research (full version): [L1360.007]
    1. Develop your own product research skill first and test multiple products, niches and target countries. [L1360.007]
    2. Define the target audience and the problems you are looking for, based on what has worked in your past stores. [L1360.007]
    3. Teach the team member your exact research methods and give them access to your research tools, for example Peeksta. [L1360.007]
    4. Show them exactly which problems and needs to look for. [L1360.007]
    5. Ask for a list of products at the end of each week. [L1360.007]
    6. Scan the list, choose the products you like and start testing them. [L1360.007]
  • Delegating without a defined target audience leads to a huge, general list of products that may or may not work. Team members do not know what to look for unless the owner has already identified the best niche and audience. [L1360.008]
  • Give product research criteria to the team, for example upsell potential and a maximum supplier cost of 30 USD on AliExpress. Clear criteria let the assistant screen products before the owner picks one. [L1431.005]
  • A team member with a personal interest in a niche suggested it for a second store, which then converted better than the first store. Letting people who know a category choose products can beat the owner's own guesses. [L1437.017] (Source A, no year given.)
  • Every research step can be split into smaller tasks, and each task with clear instructions can be taught to a person or an AI agent. The quality of the result depends on how well the instructions are written and which best practices are applied. [L0464.013] (Source E 2025-26.)

End-to-End Research Process

Phase Timeline

  • Phase 2, Product Research and Validation (Days 15 to 30): identify a proven product worth building a real brand around, not just testing for quick sales. [L0199.004]
  • Research and tests begin early, run in parallel with training, and keep running after the first winner. [L0491.010, L1184.001]

Niche Stage

  • Niche selection and market research, in order: [L0393.004, L0532.004] (Source A; the two sources give the same five steps, with slightly different wording for step 2.)
    1. Review the niche selection material. [L0393.004, L0532.004]
    2. Brainstorm at least 50 niche ideas and list them in a niche worksheet. Add 50 or more niche ideas to the niche worksheet. [L0393.004, L0532.004]
    3. Begin the market research tests. Start the research tests from the market research material. [L0393.004, L0532.004]
    4. Complete all market research tests. [L0393.004, L0532.004]
    5. Choose the niche for your first store. [L0393.004, L0532.004]
  • You do not need to research all 50 niches. If 2 to 3 feel strong and meet criteria, research those. Most lists contain multiple winners, and thorough research is only needed on the top candidates. [L0274.058]
  • Research every niche only until a winner is found. It is not necessary to search suppliers for every niche on a list of 50. [L1069.039] (Older source: Source A, 2019.)
  • Niche checks procedure (Source A, 2020):
    1. Confirm the niche has at least 20 brands that sell the products through online stores. [L0468.020]
    2. Confirm there are at least 100 different products available across those brands. [L0468.020]
    3. Check that demand and interest in the niche exist. [L0468.020]
    4. Run the seasonality test and avoid niches that collapse for part of the year. [L0468.020]
    5. Search Google Shopping and review the first page of results to see the average selling price. [L0468.020]

Product Stage

  • Product research procedure, in this order: [L0335.003] (Source C.)
    1. Look only at products already advertised on the ad platform, not untested items. [L0335.003]
    2. Check proof of concept on competitor ads: likes, comments and especially shares. [L0335.003]
    3. Find early winners: ads launched in the last 1 to 2 weeks, with about one month as the widest window. [L0335.003]
    4. Check the Facebook page creation date of the competitor. [L0335.003]
    5. Confirm scaling: count active ads and new ads per day on the competitor's page. [L0335.003]
    6. Verify sales with at least two tools. [L0335.003]
    7. Check product criteria: at least 3x markup, longevity, quality rating, and a problem-solving or wow factor. [L0335.003]
  • Product research process: [L0637.033]
    1. Use spy tools to find products. [L0637.033]
    2. Compare 10 to 15 candidate products. [L0637.033]
    3. Check the pros and cons of each niche and product. [L0637.033]
    4. Look at the value the product gives, including whether it solves a problem. [L0637.033]
    5. Find marketing angles competitors are missing. [L0637.033]
    6. Pick the products that meet the most criteria, with a clear competitor gap and room for better creatives and websites. [L0637.033]
  • Product selection approach with the Peak tool: see the seven-step procedure in the screening section above. [L0577.031]
  • Product research routine for a batch of 8 to 10 products: [L1176.001]
    1. In AfterLib, find trending products and sort them by percentage change in ad count, viewing up to 80 ads per page. [L1176.001]
    2. Open each product's store and note its price, shipping offer and target countries. [L1176.001]
    3. Copy the store link into the research sheet. [L1176.001]
    4. Open the Facebook Ad Library, check the number of active ads, the ad copies and the age of the Facebook page. [L1176.001]
    5. Remove products whose ads are inactive, because nobody is selling them right now. [L1176.001]
    6. Check the supplier or CJ shipping cost and the sourcing cost before testing. [L1176.001]
    7. Keep the products that pass and test them one at a time. [L1176.001]
  • Product research order (Source C):
    1. Go through a product criteria checklist of six to seven points to define the kind of product you want. [L1382.001]
    2. Search TikTok ads in a spy tool (PeakStar or PP Ads) with the filters for that tool. [L1382.001]
    3. Check supplier quality on AliExpress by reading ratings and reviews of multiple listings. [L1382.001]
    4. Check the competition and whether a product is already heavily sold. [L1382.001]
    5. Verify sales of competing stores with a store-tracking tool. [L1382.001]
    6. Check product quality, the size of the problem it solves, the number of use cases, how the store looks, and whether the margin is sufficient. [L1382.001]
  • Product selection approach shown in the lesson: see the seven-step procedure in the screening section above. [L0577.031]
  • Competitor analysis: check how many competitors run the same or a related product, and how strong they are. [L0708.022]
  • Product quality check: review ratings on AliExpress and Amazon and, if possible, order a sample. [L0708.022]
  • Product spy research: find products that are currently selling, using filtered spy tools. [L0708.022]
  • Five success factors for profitable product research, in order: [L1158.007]
    1. Use a solid product criteria list of validation points to judge potential winners. Adapt it to your own process, since no single process fits everyone. [L1158.007]
    2. Learn to be a differentiator: find how you can stand out from competitors. [L1158.007]
    3. Understand why a customer buys from you and not from a competitor, Amazon, or another store, by critically evaluating your own landing page and branding, ideally with outside feedback asking for negative points as well. [L1158.007]
    4. Understand your exact audience by stepping into their shoes. [L1158.007]
    5. Combine points 2, 3 and 4 to turn a regular product into a stronger potential winner. [L1158.007]
  • Women's products and other audience-led categories, tested through the Jungle Scout route: see the women's product test procedure in the stop rules above. [L1184.022]
  • Fashion dropshipping research workflow:
    1. Create a free AdSpy account. If the free page limit is used up, create a new account with a different email address. [L0661.002]
    2. Set the gender filter to female, the age filter to 40 to 65+, and the country filter to United States. [L0661.002]
    3. Type a product keyword into the ad text filter, for example shoe, sneakers, boot or slippers. [L0661.002]
    4. Scroll through the results and note ads with many likes, comments and shares, and check the ad creation date. [L0661.002]
    5. Open the AliExpress app on your phone, use the camera search on a photo of the product, and open the matching listing. [L0661.002]
    6. If the AliExpress order count is low, check whether the seller fulfills through CJ and look at the CJ listing. [L0661.002]
    7. Watch how the ad uses copy, video type and images, and use that as inspiration for your own product page. [L0661.002]
    8. Test the product in a market where it is not yet saturated, such as Australia, New Zealand, Germany, Switzerland or Austria. [L0661.002]
  • Use the 4-stage sequence in order, because each stage filters out products before the more time-consuming checks begin. [L0636.002]

Daily and Weekly Cadence

  • Keep product research running in parallel with testing, and check the list every week. [L1184.001, L1172.058]
  • Test the sales hypothesis against the rule set each week rather than on a fixed guess. [L1158.004]

Ads, Pages and Variant Tests

  • Test a few products in one segment rather than all products at once, so each product gets enough attention. [L0995.045, L0999.052]
  • For a product test, use the full research order, then ad testing, then store and offer checks. [L1382.001, L0502.035, L0650.022]
