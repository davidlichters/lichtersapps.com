2.18 Finding products on TikTok and social platforms

Where to Search and Why

  • TikTok shows products while they are already viral, which is faster than waiting for research tools to list them. The trade-off is the time you spend browsing every day. [L0372.011]
  • Find product ideas on TikTok before paid research tools, because many winning products appear there first. Products that performed well in Facebook ads can also be tested organically. [L1380.044]
  • Use TikTok to spot trending products, then check that the product fits the target audience's needs. A trend alone is not enough. [L0492.009]
  • Going viral is the first step. It is harder and more expensive to achieve through ads than organically. Once viral reach exists, the time spent finding a winning product is better spent. [L0764.044]
  • Facebook users are usually not in active buying mode, so targeting and creatives carry more weight there. Problem-solving products generally perform better on Facebook than on TikTok. [L0708.017]
  • Test a product on both Facebook and TikTok before dismissing it. Facebook ads are not limited to older users, because they also run on Instagram, which has a younger audience than Facebook itself. [L0995.043, L0995.044]
  • Match the platform to where your audience asks questions rather than where most ads run. Test with a small budget before scaling. [L0637.066]
  • Products recommended by YouTube channels are often not saturated, because most viewers only watch and never implement. About one in ten viewers actually runs ads on the recommended product. [L1184.020]
  • Reverse-engineering the TikTok feed is a method of its own. [L1219.038]

Reverse-engineering TikTok for products

  1. Shape the TikTok feed toward dropshipping content.
  2. Note products that perform well there but have not been widely used on Facebook or Instagram.
  3. Test those products on Facebook and Instagram.
  4. Reuse the TikTok videos as UGC where possible. [L1219.038]

Finding Candidates on TikTok

Searches and hashtags

  • Search "TikTok Made Me Buy It" in the TikTok search bar. Use the "Top" tab, not the hashtag tab, because the hashtag tab does not let you filter out recent uploads. [L0706.011]
  • The hashtag "Amazon Finds" works like "Made Me Buy It" and can surface AliExpress products that can be dropshipped. Filter results by time range as in other TikTok product searches. [L0706.015]
  • Giveaways and similar viral content attract a young audience, and TikTok can keep showing your videos to younger viewers. Those viewers rarely buy, which can lower conversion. [L0706.052]
  • Watch daily which products get unusually high views on other accounts. When a product shows this traction, act quickly and move to testing before the trend fades. [L0821.002]
  • Look for decoration items with a "wow factor". They have viral potential, and niche products such as viral lamps achieve significant traction alongside paid advertising. [L0209.027]

Made Me Buy It: filter procedure

  1. Search "TikTok Made Me Buy It" in the TikTok search bar.
  2. Select the "Top" tab, not the hashtag tab, because the hashtag tab does not let you filter out recent uploads.
  3. Open the filter settings and set the time range to "This month", "This week", or "Last 3 months". The 3-month range can reveal older products that are still selling.
  4. Sort by "Most liked" only as an option, because good products can have few likes.
  5. Use the phone app for the filter, since the filter is not available on desktop. [L0706.011]
  • Do not rely only on the most-liked results. Strong products are often posted with few likes, so review lower-engagement posts too. [L0706.012]

Free discovery with a research account

  • Use a new TikTok account created without your main account to search for terms such as "dropshippers", "Etsy finds" and "Amazon finds". This is the free method; spy tools are the paid alternative for the same goal. [L1382.003]
  • Searching exposing-dropshipper content can reveal products that have already gone viral with lower visibility. Those videos often get modest views, but the product videos themselves can reach millions. [L1384.043]
  • Engage with every product video in your feed so the For You Page shows more organic product content over time. [L1384.042]

Free discovery procedure (exposing-dropshipper search)

  1. Search TikTok for keywords such as "Amazon finds", "TikTok made me buy it", or "exposing dropshippers" to find product videos.
  2. Filter results by upload date to see the most recent week or month, which surfaces newer products.
  3. Open the product videos, follow the tagged product link or search the product name on AliExpress.
  4. Engage with every product video in your feed so the For You Page shows more organic product content over time. [L1384.042]

Dummy research account procedure

  1. Create a second account on the same phone. You do not need a new device.
  2. Do not upload any videos from this account.
  3. Search for keywords such as "exposing dropshippers" and "Amazon finds".
  4. Like and comment on the product videos that appear.
  5. Repeat for a few hours to a few days until the For You page shows mostly product content and dropshipping ads.
  6. Scroll this feed during product research to spot products that show up. [L0706.021]

Free product search (broad method)

  1. Use a mobile phone, preferably, or a computer.
  2. On TikTok, search for these phrases: "TikTok made me buy it" / "Amazon finds" / "50% off".
  3. While browsing the feed, look for videos showing products that have more than 5 million views.
  4. Search the product name on TikTok and identify the biggest accounts posting about it.
  5. Search for those accounts on Instagram, or go to their website and find their Instagram link.
  6. Check whether the product has gone viral on Instagram or whether those accounts post on Instagram. If neither is true, that is a good sign.
  7. Search for the product on AliExpress using the names people use online, so you can source it. [L1367.004]

Personal research account

  1. Create a personal TikTok account that is set up only for product research.
  2. Search TikTok directly for the product or product idea.
  3. Check whether any account has had a viral video with it, since a viral result from up to a year ago still shows that the product can work.
  4. If you find only paid ads and no organic viral examples, treat the product as unproven. [L0372.010]
  • A product uploaded a year ago that got little attention is not automatically dead. A product that has never shown viral traction anywhere on TikTok deserves more caution. Treat previous attempts as information, not as a final verdict. [L0372.016]
  • Older products with no dominant account can be revived if the video quality is improved. A product from a few months or a year ago can still be worth testing. [L1380.045]

Seasonal and gift products

  • Use the TikTok search for "mother's day gift" or "shop now", open the hashtag page, and filter to this week. [L1379.025]
  • Seasonal gift products can be found by checking products that performed well in past seasons on TikTok, and by using the hashtags "link in bio" and "shop now" to find trending products. [L1379.026]
  • A novel, hard-to-demonstrate product such as a glowing toy can struggle alone. Comparison videos, such as comparing it with a well-known competitor, performed well in this case. [L1379.027]
  • A proven seasonal video format can be reused for a new holiday by keeping its structure and hook and changing only the theme and product visuals. [L1186.009]
  • For a gift-focused test, consider following only gift-related content on a new account, since the feed fills with product posts within about a day. [L1379.025]

Hashtag and Instagram sourcing

  • Target products that already sell well as short videos on TikTok but have not yet gone viral on Instagram. Focus first on fashion, beauty, entertainment and sports products, and read the comments under winning videos to spot trends and community demand. [L1367.001]
  • Search Instagram by hashtag, for example #product-name or #weighted-hula-hoop, to discover real user photos and videos. Filter for clear product shots, authentic user context and good lighting. [L0321.045]
  • On Instagram, search several variants of the product term, such as "pet hair cleaner" and "dog hair removal", because different wording returns different accounts. Keep accounts with real followings and posts, and skip those with few followers or no content. [L0450.028]

Creative Center

TikTok Creative Center procedure

  1. Open TikTok Creative Center from TikTok Business Center.
  2. Scroll to the trending hashtags and choose an industry filter that matches your product, for example "Sports & Outdoors" or "Travel".
  3. For trending songs, set the time range to "Last 30 days" and the country to "United States" if you are targeting the US.
  4. Scroll past the songs and click "View more" to reach the top-performing ads.
  5. Choose an industry such as "Apparel & Accessories" and set the duration to "Last 30 days" or "Last 7 days".
  6. For e-commerce ads, select "E-commerce", choose "Non-app", and select all.
  7. Scroll through the ads and note products that match AliExpress dropshipping listings. [L0706.017]

Kalodata

Access and data coverage

  • Kalodata's Claude integration runs through the third-party tool 2Suite, which costs 39.99 USD per month and does not allow CSV export. An enterprise Kalodata subscription gives API access and can be integrated with an AI tool directly. [L0354.001]
  • Kalodata's enterprise data covers 365 days, so trends can be selected for the last 30, 90, 180 or 365 days. [L0354.005, L1187.030]
  • Kalodata product pages show an earliest record date for their data (07/25/2025 in one example). Trends for a product cannot be checked further back than that date. [L0333.020]
  • Toosuite is a third-party platform that bundles several research tools, such as spy tools and CapCut-style editing tools, and is used alongside Kalodata. Kalodata remains the main data source for the product research described here. [L1175.001]
  • Kalodata is a TikTok Shop product research tool that shows revenue, units sold, growth rate, creator count, commission rate, shipping fee and video ads for each product. Its Explore Category, Top New Products and Video & Ad views are used to find and inspect candidates. [L1187.001]

Product detail page: metrics

  • The product detail page shows the product image, price, commission rate, shipping fee and rating. In the example, a $17.99 book had a 15% commission rate, a $0.00 shipping fee and a 4.7/5 rating. The same page can be used to pick a product and copy its image for cover editing. [L0333.001]
  • The product detail page shows the category path, the number of product SKUs, a "View on TikTok" button and a "Generate Promo Video" button. For books the category path can read Books, Magazines and Audio, then Literature and Art, then Literature and Fiction. [L0333.023]
  • The product detail page shows revenue and revenue per day, items sold, video revenue, product card revenue, live revenue, creator count, creator conversion ratio, concentration rate, commission rate, shipping fee and the earliest recorded date. [L0354.005]
  • Kalodata is used to see a product's revenue, items sold, average unit price, review count and rating, plus the video and ad items linked to it, filtered by country and a 30, 90 or 180 day window. In the sample page the rating was 4.2 out of 5 from about 3,400 reviews, and the category was Beauty and Personal Care > Skincare > Moisturizers and Mists. [L0707.003]
  • To check a TikTok Shop product's performance, use the product detail page with a chosen window, such as the last 30 days: revenue, items sold, creator conversion ratio, average unit price, live revenue and concentration rate. It also shows commission rate, shipping fee, rating and linked videos. [L1188.024]
  • Kalodata's product view shows revenue, items sold, launch date, revenue growth rate, average unit price, creator number, revenue split by source (video, ad, livestream), highest-revenue videos and revenue trend. Filters for category, date, country, revenue, growth rate and price narrow the list. [L1175.002]
  • The product Overview splits total revenue into Live Revenue, Video Revenue, Product Card revenue and Interactive Mode revenue. In the NAD+ drink mix example, live revenue was far above video revenue over 30 days, so check which channel actually sells before choosing which ad style to copy. [L0901.023]
  • The product page shows "The earliest date recorded", which tells you how long the product has been tracked on TikTok. An older date means a longer selling history in the data. In the drink mix example it was 01/10/2025. [L0901.025]

Product detail page: tabs

  • Product pages are split into tabs: Overview, Creator, Sales Strategy, Reviews, Video & Ad and Live. Use Video & Ad for the library of ad videos and Overview for revenue and items sold. [L0901.022]
  • Product detail pages are split into tabs for linked Video & Ad content, Creators, Sales Strategy and Review Insights. Check these tabs to see how a product is marketed before you commit to building a store. [L1249.021]
  • Listings for TikTok Shop products can carry a "Ship From Local" label, which indicates the product is stocked in the local market. Treat it as a signal of faster delivery when comparing products for a store in that market. [L1249.022]

Filters, date windows and item-sold buckets

  • Date presets for product metrics are Yesterday, Last 7 days, Last 30 days, Last 90 days, Last 180 days and Last 365 days. The Item Sold filter offers the buckets All, under 100, 100 to 1k, 1k to 10k and Max. [L1187.030]
  • The date selector for a product offers Yesterday, Last 7 Days, Last 30 Days, Last 90 Days and Last 180 Days. Use the same window when comparing products. [L0901.021]
  • The Video & Ad list shows each ad with views and sales, with a time filter of Last 30 Days, Last 90 days and Last 180 days. Use Last 30 Days to find current winners. [L0704.003]
  • Kalodata's Explore view has tabs such as "Top New Products" and "High Potential Affiliate". Product lists can be sorted by revenue, items sold, revenue growth rate and average unit price. Each product has an "Is Affiliate Product" column that shows whether it can be promoted through affiliates. [L0333.018]

Category and market structure

  • Kalodata product pages list a category path and a price range, for example Health > Nutrition & Wellness > Beauty Supplement for the NAD+ drink mix. Use the category to find related products whose ads you can study for hooks and testimonials. [L0901.020]
  • On a category page, read Revenue Growth Rate, Top 3 Shop Revenue Ratio, Top 10 Shop Revenue Ratio, Number of Shops and Average Revenue per Shop together. In the Beauty & Personal Care L1 example for the last 30 days, the top 3 shops held 10.46% and the top 10 held 19.80% of revenue. Low shares suggest many sellers share the market; high shares suggest a few shops dominate. [L0901.026]
  • The "Save selected filters" button on a category page keeps your filter combination for reuse. [L0901.027]

Health product filter procedure

  1. In Kalodata, change the category to Health.
  2. Set the revenue range to 10,000 to 45,000 dollars and press Apply.
  3. Set Revenue Source (Content) to Video and Revenue Source (Channel) to Affiliate.
  4. Set Revenue Growth Rate to at least 30 percent.
  5. Press Submit.
  6. Skip the first five result pages and start the product review on page 6.
  7. Check the Last 30 Days and Last 90 Days revenue graphs and the video ads for each product that passes the filters. [L1187.002]
  • Filter panel values for this Health research: Category = Health; Revenue = 10k to 45k dollars; Revenue Source (Content) = Video; Revenue Source (Channel) = Affiliate; Revenue Growth Rate = minimum 30 percent; date range = Last 30 days. A Creator Conversion Ratio filter with a threshold above 30 percent is also available in the same panel. [L1187.003]
  • Products that rise steadily without dips are less interesting than products whose revenue graph goes up and down. A fluctuating curve with recovering peaks suggests real, repeated demand rather than a single spike. [L1187.004]
  • Skipping the first five pages of filtered results is a deliberate way to avoid the most obvious, heavily tested winners and find less crowded products. Review results from page 6 onward. [L1187.005]
  • Products whose ads are TikTok Shop videos that rely on the TikTok Shop platform are not suitable to run as Meta ads and should be skipped. [L1187.006]

Other Research Tools

  • Fastmoss is an AI-driven product research tool used to spot viral products early. [L0914.003]
  • Catalog Data is an AI-driven tool used together with Fastmoss to identify viral products early in product research. [L0914.004]
  • The TikTok Shop explorer in WinningHunter lists products with revenue, revenue growth, sales growth, price, units sold, creator count, launch date, product score and review count. It can be filtered to Yesterday, 7 days or 30 days, with the default set to Last 30 days. [L0892.001]
  • The TikTok Shop product list has the tabs All Products, Best Sellers, High growth and Top New Products. Each product row offers a "Find Shopify competitors with AI" action, and the list has Export and Refresh buttons. [L0892.002]
  • PPAds (about 1 USD for a 3-day trial with a virtual card, cancelled before the trial ends) and Seller Count are paid TikTok research tools. Their product search shows direct competitors and ad impressions for each product. [L0895.013]
  • PP Ads has the largest TikTok ad library with strong filters, and its 3-day trial for $1 suits a short research sprint. Menia is a TikTok ad spy tool with a TikTok research section. Afterlib can be used for similar product discovery. [L1183.010]
  • Menia filter settings for product research: Date set to creation date within the last 14 days; Likes at least 200; Plays at least 10,000; E-commerce platform Shopify; Countries United States, Canada, United Kingdom and Italy. PP Ads supports the same filters. [L1183.011]
  • The Chrome extension VidIQ shows the reach and engagement graph of a competitor's video, revealing whether views drop off after the video starts running paid ads or Spark Ads. [L0819.011]
  • My Ad Finder is a free browser extension that shows only ads in the Facebook feed, so you can spot products with strong engagement and positive comments. Record promising products in a spreadsheet. [L1107.001]

Free competitor research on TikTok

  • Search the product name in the TikTok app and count the organic videos. Many videos, or a few with tens of millions of views, signal saturation, though paid tools are more precise. [L0895.016]
  • A competitor whose first and last ad dates are recent and still running shows the product is working now. If the first and last ad dates are old with no recent ads, the product has likely faded. [L0895.032]

Recency Rules

  • For TikTok product research, filter for posts created in the last 2 weeks. For Facebook Ads research, the window can go up to 1 month. [L0363.003]
  • A recent TikTok post with strong engagement signals a new product, while an older product that someone has only just launched may still qualify. If it is unclear whether a product is new, check with an experienced mentor. [L0363.004]
  • Note that Menia's filter uses a 14-day creation window, and TikTok's "Made Me Buy It" filter offers "This month", "This week" or "Last 3 months". The sources give different windows for different tools, so match the window to the tool you are using. [L0363.003, L1183.011, L0706.011]

Likes and Engagement Thresholds

  • Use the number of likes on trending videos over the last month as a product signal. About 300 likes in a month was cited as a starting threshold. For a six-month window, extrapolate it (3 times 300 gave about 1,500 likes). [L0639.035]
  • Menia's search filter used a minimum of 200 likes for ads created in the last 14 days. This is a search filter setting, not the same threshold as the 300-per-month starting point, so apply each one only in its own context. [L1183.011]
  • Products with weak video quality can still be good products. Strong hooks can draw attention to weak content, while good products with plain videos get overlooked. Judge the product itself, not the production quality of the ad. [L0639.043]

Vetting Viral Accounts

Account health and timeline

  • Check the seller's account timeline. A product that went viral on day one, with no or very few new uploads afterwards, is a warning sign, especially when the account is fresh and the views are very high. [L0530.002]
  • A healthy viral product account often posts a new viral video roughly every one to two weeks, or about one per month for a strong product, and keeps uploading steadily. [L0530.003]
  • A genuinely organic creator with a viral video usually keeps uploading many videos. An account with tens of millions of views and only a few uploads is a warning sign that paid promotion or a second account is involved. [L1385.003]
  • A seller who stops posting after a viral hit is not always failing. They may be holding back to avoid disrupting a video that still converts, or the hit may have come from one region only. Treat these as explanations to verify, not proof that the product sells. [L0530.032]
  • Judge a seller's momentum by the views of the single relevant video, not the lifetime view count of the account, because old posts can inflate the total. [L1186.002]
  • When many unrelated creators post the same branded product at once, the seller is likely running creator-driven campaigns, which suggests active selling. Check which accounts are pushing it rather than assuming it is organic. [L1186.003]

Ad origin and fraud

  • Use the TikTok ad profile link as a signal. If clicking it shows no account, the ad is a regular paid ad with only a logo and no profile attached. Spark Ads, by contrast, are linked to a creator profile. [L0372.008]
  • A missing account link does not always mean the product failed, because the video may later have been removed or its sound muted. Check the product again on TikTok before dropping it. [L0372.009]
  • Check whether a viral video is the seller's own content. Copied ads run by a different account with a different name, no real TikTok account and zero comments are signs of ad fraud or stolen creative. [L0530.009]
  • A spike in views on a very new account can come from Spark Ads (boosted organic posts). This explains missing uploads but does not guarantee sales. [L0530.012]
  • A video with zero comments on an ad is a red flag. Sellers often disable comments on ads, and negative feedback can be hidden, so do not judge viability from ad content alone. [L0530.010]

Repeat and re-upload checks

  • Before treating a viral video as proof of sales, check the seller's account for a repeatable pattern. Several videos above 1 million views spread over weeks suggest a product that keeps working. Mostly low-view uploads with one hit suggest a single lucky moment. [L0530.030]
  • If a viral video was re-uploaded and the second copy gets far fewer views than the original, the original hit is the only real signal. Compare the view counts of both uploads before deciding that the product sells. [L0530.031]

Competitor exposure

  • When competitors expose a product as dropshipped, the effect is usually small. 100,000 views on a video that has 10 million views is only about 1 percent of the audience. Exposing accounts often reveal the supplier name and search terms for AliExpress, which can be useful. [L0372.012]

Case: a bunion product account

  • A viral account selling a bunion (hallux valgus) product gained about 51M, 32M and 8M views on its first videos, all uploaded from 6 September, with only 8 uploads in roughly 3 to 4 weeks. It claimed about $800,000 in lifetime sales. [L1385.001]
  • Reviewers found a second account with the same website domain and very similar follower counts. Both posted a video on the same day, which pointed to split testing or paid promotion rather than purely organic growth. [L1385.001]
  • A dummy order on the website showed large sales, but a second dummy order placed 24 hours later added only about $3,000. [L1385.001]
  • Reviewers ran the product themselves. A student's product video passed 10M views and produced only about 3 sales in a few hours, then a slow trickle. A separate account with about 12M views produced 10 orders, and a newer account with about 10M views in 3 days produced only about 10 sales. [L1385.001]
  • The store listed one product, while the PPAds tool revealed sales on two other products reachable only by editing the product URL. [L1385.001]
  • The account then deleted or made private its videos, changed its name and domain while keeping the same website, and switched to a bunion product, which reviewers judged to be a weak converter that had misled many sellers. [L1385.001]

Your Own Accounts

  • Use a new TikTok account for every product tested, unless the new product is in the same niche as an existing account. Only then is an older account acceptable, because a new account carries the new-account advantage. [L0501.008]
  • Staying in the same niche makes the account's audience transition smoother. Changing niche is possible, but the new audience will be confused at first and views will start low. [L1383.036]

Judging Virality Against Real Sales

Views do not prove sales

  • Millions of views do not prove sales. A viral video can be a reaction or entertainment piece with no conversion, so judge the product by comments, store quality and pricing too. [L0530.005]
  • A product with a viral video can still fail to convert if its audience is mostly curious rather than buying. Product quality and the match between product and audience determine conversion, so a high view count alone does not validate a product. [L1077.009]
  • A high view count on a viral video does not prove that a product sells. Before copying a viral product, check actual sales per view, because very large view numbers can coexist with only a handful of orders. [L1385.002]
  • A product with a few hundred thousand TikTok views, priced around $27 to $30, had a listing with only 17 orders. Because of the low order count it was not pursued, even though the views were high. Views alone did not prove sales. [L0706.034]
  • Treat high view counts and high sales on a video as proof that it is a viral, working ad. The sales figure is the main quality indicator. [L0704.004]

Signals to check

  • Comments: many buyer questions such as "where can I buy this" from different people are a strong sign of demand. Comments can be more reliable than the video itself. [L0530.001]
  • Reach graph: use VidIQ to see whether views drop off after paid ads or Spark Ads begin. [L0819.011]
  • Ad dates: recent first and last ad dates that are still running show the product works now. [L0895.032]

Screening a Candidate Product

Checklist

  • Use a checklist for product screening: comments showing buying intent, store quality, price range, recency of uploads, and whether the video is original. Proceed only when the overall picture checks out. [L0530.013]
  • Choose a product that has broad appeal and where competitors have gaps. Highly niche audiences such as woodworkers, teachers or nurses are harder to start on TikTok. [L0637.047]
  • Choose products that look appealing on video and solve a visible problem, because the video must show the product working. Products with a clear demonstration are easier to make convert. [L0657.006]
  • Before choosing, check that a product is already selling: prefer products already working in the market, and products with fresh results are easier to act on. [L1380.045, L1134.017]

Seven-criteria procedure (TikTok product research)

  1. The product must solve a problem big enough that people act and buy immediately (e.g., acne rather than dog bad breath).
  2. The product must have broad appeal across many demographics, not a tiny niche.
  3. The product must show instant results or a clear transformation within a 15 to 20 second ad.
  4. Pre-existing TikTok videos of the product must exist that can be reused for custom ads.
  5. The product needs a unique selling angle versus competitors (better price, higher quality, more options in colors or sizes, or a stronger offer).
  6. The selling price must be low enough for impulse purchase, ideally under $50.
  7. The product must have at least a 3x markup on landed cost (product plus shipping) before ad costs. [L1183.004]

Niche and visible-result principles

  • Broad-appeal niches such as makeup, beauty, health and fitness, wellness, life hacks and fun gaming products suit TikTok ads better than narrow niches like knee pain for men over 50. Narrow audiences raise CPMs, are hard to target with TikTok's algorithm, and are too small to scale. [L1183.005]
  • Products that show a visible result within about 10 to 15 seconds of ad time, such as life hacks or beauty transformations, perform well on TikTok. Products that need 30 to 50 days to show results require more social proof and tend to trigger outside research, which reduces impulse purchases. [L1183.006]
  • Products that solve a clear problem or have a strong wow factor tend to perform best on short video, so prioritise these. [L0820.003]
  • Short videos with strong sound, an aesthetic background and a satisfying look can earn high watch time and repeat views, so visual and audio quality matter when judging a viral candidate. [L0530.011]
  • Products that are unhygienic, messy, or need an awkward use step (such as being pushed into a drink and leaving the room sticky) are unlikely to convert, even when the video gets views. [L0530.004]
  • A product with a clear emotional or identity appeal, such as car owners showing passion for their vehicle, can sell well to a targeted audience even if it is niche. [L0530.006]
  • A product tied to a clear identity or passion can convert within a small niche. The operator should understand and relate to that audience before judging its appeal, and should research further if they do not. [L0530.033]
  • Choose the product by its problem, not its niche label: a surprising product paired with a strong audio or visual hook works only if the usage is practical, meaning the product solves a real problem clearly and without a messy routine. [L0530.028]
  • Products in very specialised niches such as fishing, camping or horse riding perform poorly on TikTok ads. Big broad niches such as beauty, fashion and kitchen fit the platform better. High-ticket products also underperformed on TikTok even with good content. [L1376.018]
  • Products that are priced high do better on Facebook. Facebook still has more user data for targeting high-priced products ($200 to $300), while TikTok is cheaper and newer and does not yet find very specific customer groups well. [L1376.019]

Price rules

  • Products priced above $50 only make sense in beauty, health and wellness niches. For TikTok ads with a youth audience, the best-performing price point is under $50. [L1183.009]

Niches that worked on TikTok

  • Niches that worked well on TikTok include life hacks and problem-solving products (such as wallets and fidget spinners), beauty (described as evergreen), health and fitness, pet products (strong recently), and home improvement or home decor (including diffusers). A product outside these niches can still work, since the TikTok algorithm is improving. [L1382.012]
  • Products reported as consistently strong on TikTok included jewelry, home, kitchen, entertainment and beauty items. Relationship jewelry was a niche where a viral product was found first and a better paid-ads product was then found within the same niche. [L0819.010]

Choosing a niche with conflicting advice

  • Broad niches are the safer default for TikTok: highly niche audiences are harder to start with. Identity-driven niches can still convert when the operator understands the audience. [L0637.047, L1183.005, L0530.033]

Influencer marketing

  • Influencer marketing tends to work best for products that solve a visible problem, such as pain relief, and for jewelry, beauty care and fitness products. [L1379.023]
  • Clothing is a saturated market for influencer marketing and usually needs a very unique product to work. Influencer results also depend heavily on the individual creator. [L1379.024]

Traffic and Product Choice

  • When a test product gets views but no sales, look at the video quality and hook before deciding the product is wrong, because a weak first five seconds can cause the failure. [L0820.024]
  • A product that was used to drive traffic to a separate fitness clothing store was easier to make viral than the clothing itself. The creator did not care about buying the demo product, only that viewers visit the store. [L0820.028]
  • A viral product can be used as a traffic vehicle. Promote a product that is easy to make go viral and link to your main store or marketplace listing (for example an Amazon FBA page) to bring visitors who may buy other items. [L0820.029]
  • Products with high convenience, a chance to go viral, and a current trend on TikTok are the ones to commit to. Products that performed well on paid ads may still be worth testing on organic traffic. [L1384.045]
  • Before running paid ads on a product found on TikTok, check whether it already has strong organic performance, such as several thousand to tens of thousands of views on organic TikTok posts. Organic traction is a signal to test with a small paid budget, then keep posting organic content if sales come in. [L1212.006]
  • Testing speed matters: reuse existing viral TikTok content (for example videos with 500,000 to 1 million+ views) rather than ordering and filming for each product before knowing it is a winner. This saves time and money and lets you test more products faster. [L1183.007]
  • Do not start TikTok ads before the store reaches about 50,000 dollars in monthly revenue and other channels are dialled in. This is advice from 2022 (older) and reflects an earlier store-level rule. [L0969.025]
  • Products whose revenue is growing and which can be tested with a small paid budget can be scaled once organic sales confirm them. [L1212.006]

Platform Fit: TikTok

  • A product found on TikTok can be a test for Facebook: test on Facebook and TikTok before one is dismissed. [L0995.044]
  • Products sold in a niche with large audiences can be tested on TikTok when Facebook targeting does not reach the intended younger age group. [L0995.043]

Platform Fit: Facebook

Facebook feed procedure

  1. Scroll the Facebook feed and find dropshipping products shown as ads.
  2. Like the post and comment on it.
  3. Open the product page and add the product to cart.
  4. Go to checkout.

The platform then learns that you are interested in this type of product and shows more of them. [L0361.015]

My Ad Finder procedure

  1. Install the My Ad Finder extension so only ads appear in the feed.
  2. Scroll normally and note products with good engagement and positive comments.
  3. Add them to a spreadsheet.
  4. Run every candidate through the written product-selection criteria checklist (Word document).
  5. Choose the best-fitting product to test. [L1107.002]
  • Find candidate products by running keyword searches on Facebook for a category, such as baby products, and studying the posts that stand out. Look for products that already attract strong engagement. [L1251.002]
  • For Facebook Ads, focus on problem-solving products rather than broad niches. Niches that performed well include pets, kids, tech, work from home and beauty, with beauty especially strong on Instagram stories and reels. [L0788.041]
  • Facebook's ad algorithm reads the text, the video context and the visual content of an ad to decide who sees it. An ad must be relevant to the audience it targets. Narrow targeting combined with a message and visuals that match the audience helps the algorithm find the right people. [L0999.008]
  • Consider the browsing context: people see feed ads while distracted, for example while standing in a queue, so the message must land quickly. Many viewers also watch without sound, so video should carry subtitles. [L0997.012]
  • Products that have worked on Facebook can be moved to a different brand and audience. The same back-stretcher product was scaled on Pinterest by a store with different branding and a different audience, while the Facebook version ran a separate pitch. Targeting a new platform can find a new customer base for an identical product. [L0871.011]

Platform Fit: Instagram

  • Instagram, which Facebook ads also reach, has a younger audience than Facebook itself. [L0995.043]
  • Search Instagram by hashtag and by product-term variants to find real accounts and images. [L0321.045, L0450.028]

Reliability and restrictions

  • The Pinterest algorithm is weaker than on TikTok or Facebook, and only a few niches reliably sell. Random products outside these niches often fail, so check niche fit before spending on Pinterest ads. [L0788.057]
  • Pinterest is cheap and underused by e-commerce sellers compared with Facebook or TikTok. It allows most traditional dropshipping products, with rare bans. Account restrictions are often lifted within one to two days, or a few hours via support. [L1134.019]
  • Pinterest ad restrictions: weight loss claims and before-and-after images are the main risk. Keep claims simple and not bold. Other beauty claims such as glowing skin or wrinkles were not seen as problematic, and rejected ads can usually be fixed quickly by editing. [L1134.020]
  • Pinterest works best for niches like home decor, fashion, beauty, health, home improvement and nails. Pet niches were also reported to work recently. [L1134.016]
  • Pinterest product research follows the same logic as Facebook or TikTok research: find products already working in the market, especially in strong niches, and use trend tools filtered by weekly winners. The speaker used the Mania tool; Pin Spy or Pinterest spy tools were also mentioned. [L1134.017]

Launch settings

  • Launch timing: set the campaign to run on specific days starting the next day and launch at 12:00 midnight UTC, because Pinterest uses only UTC time, not local time zones. Avoid abrupt launch times, since first-day data fluctuates strongly. [L1134.013]
  • Campaign creation settings: name the campaign after the product and launch date, select the country (US is a large market; Germany and the Netherlands also work), set status to Active, set the campaign budget to a daily budget of $5, and choose "run continuously" or "run on specific days". [L1134.014]
  • First-day Pinterest data is unreliable: CPMs are high and CTR is low on day one. The account stabilises by day two or three, so spending more at the start mostly wastes money. [L1134.015]

Pinterest research procedure

  1. Find products that already sell in the market.
  2. Filter for the niches home decor, fashion, beauty and health.
  3. Use a Pinterest spy or product research tool filtered by weekly winners to see which products run as Pinterest ads.
  4. Prioritise the newest products that are working on Pinterest. [L1135.014]

Platform Fit: YouTube and Quora

  • Products recommended by YouTube channels are often not saturated, because most viewers only watch and never implement. About one in ten viewers actually runs ads on the recommended product. [L1184.020]
  • Quora ads tend to fit business products and work-from-home products, where users actively search for answers to problems. Match the platform to where your audience asks questions rather than where most ads run. Test with a small budget before scaling. [L0637.066]

Collecting and Organising Product Assets

  1. Open the product detail page in the research tool with the date range you want.
  2. Go to the Video & Ad section and find the top videos by views.
  3. Right-click each video and choose "Save video as", then save it to a dedicated product folder.
  4. Save the product thumbnails and screenshots in the same folder.
  5. Rename files in a consistent pattern so they can be found later.
  6. Check that the saved images do not show identifiable people before using them, or blur or replace them. [L1188.028]
  • Save and repurpose images from Instagram hashtag searches on your product page, with attention to copyright if using them directly. [L0321.045]
  • Reuse the TikTok videos as UGC where possible. [L1219.038]

Platform Selection and Ground Rules

  • Niche is a guideline, not a rule: a product outside the recommended niches can still work because the TikTok algorithm keeps improving. [L1436.024]
  • Products that solve a daily problem or save time (wallets, fidget spinners and similar life-hack products) have worked well on TikTok. Niche categories are not ranked in priority order. [L1436.016]
  • Beauty is an evergreen niche on TikTok because women buy frequently from TikTok ads. [L1436.017]
  • Health and fitness products work consistently on TikTok. [L1436.018]
  • Pet products have been performing very well on TikTok recently. [L1436.019]
  • Home improvement and home decor products, including diffusers, work well on TikTok. [L1436.020]
  • Beauty and health products aimed at women are riskier on Facebook, which often bans these niches if the ad account profile is not solid. [L1440.011]
  • Products that worked well on paid Facebook ads can be tested organically on TikTok and sometimes perform strongly. Test products from several sources rather than relying only on TikTok discovery. [L1444.007]
  • Creators should watch which content users choose to see more or less of, because platform feedback controls now shape the audience a brand reaches. Planning content around these controls can help attract the right viewers and creators. [L1504.025]

TikTok Product Research Workflow

  1. Define the product type with the product criteria checklist (six to seven points). [L1436.003]
  2. Search PeakStar's TikTok ads section with the country set to US, likes set to 500+, sorted by newest, and the keyword filters below. [L1436.003]
  3. Search PPAds with the filters in the table below. [L1436.003]
  4. Check AliExpress listings for the candidate product (see the quality rule). [L1436.003]
  5. Check the competition: look for ads launched in the last 7 days that perform well, and verify sales with PPSpy, Shop Hunter, Dropship.io or PeakStar's store tracking. [L1436.003]
  6. Confirm the product is new (not seen in the last six months), check the quality, the size of the problem it solves, the number of use cases, how the store looks and whether the margin works. [L1436.003]

Kalodata and FastMoss Research

  • FastMoss scans TikTok Shop and Shopify data and shows trending products with real sales numbers. It replaces hours of manually scrolling TikTok to find what sells in a niche. [L1459.006]
  • Keep product research to two tools: Kalodata to find products with rising revenue, and Meta Ad Library to confirm that other advertisers run ads for them. Use only the filters you need, such as Revenue Uptrend and Category, rather than stacking many filters. [L1479.028]

Kalodata Setup

  • In Kalodata, set Dates to Last 30 Days and the region to United States. Then add the Revenue Uptrend filter under Revenue Filters to show products whose revenue is growing, and use Category to compare niches. [L1479.002]
  • Sort Kalodata's video list by Publish Date (New) to surface recently published videos. This shows which creatives are working now rather than what worked months ago. [L1479.031]
  • Kalodata's video lists show GPM next to Revenue and Views. GPM is revenue per 1,000 views, so compare videos by GPM when their view counts differ widely. [L1479.030]

Kalodata Product Research Procedure

  1. Set the region to United States and Dates to Last 30 Days. [L1479.005]
  2. Add the Revenue Uptrend filter under Revenue Filters to surface growing products. [L1479.005]
  3. Browse the Category list to narrow the niche you want. [L1479.005]
  4. Do not stop at the first page of results. Check pages 4 to 5 for products that are selling but have fewer sellers. [L1479.005]
  5. Open the product detail and check Avg. Unit Price, Creator Number, Commission Rate, Shipping Option and Launch Date. [L1479.005]
  6. Open the top videos of the product and study the creatives that already sell. [L1479.005]
  7. Confirm the product has active ads on Meta before you commit to it. [L1479.005]

Reading Rankings

  • The first page of a ranking usually shows saturated products that many sellers already run. Look at the first pages only to see what works, then go deeper for products with a growing trend, people actively searching for them, and fewer advertisers. [L1479.006]

Viral Account Vetting

  • Two accounts that share a website domain, have similar follower counts and post on the same day suggest split testing or paid ads on both. Look for linked accounts before assuming one account's results are organic. [L1385.004]
  • A viral account can pivot: the account may privately hide or delete its old videos, change its name and domain and switch to a new product while keeping the same website. Track these changes before assuming the product that went viral is still the one selling. [L1385.014]

Viral Account Verification Procedure

  1. Find the viral account and note its website domain and product. [L1385.005]
  2. Compare its upload history: count total uploads, check dates and look for uploads that do not fit a normal pattern. [L1385.005]
  3. Search for a second account that links to the same domain, has similar followers or posts on the same dates. [L1385.005]
  4. Place a dummy test order on the website and record the order number and the sales it implies. [L1385.005]
  5. Place a second dummy order about 24 hours later and compare the sales estimate with the first. [L1385.005]
  6. Check the store for other products and look for products that are reachable only through a direct URL. [L1385.005]
  7. Buy from another account running the same product, so you have at least two reference points. [L1385.005]
  8. Only then decide whether the product is worth testing, and do not commit budget before the checks are complete. [L1385.005]

Facebook Feed Research

  • Beauty and health products aimed at women are riskier on Facebook, which often bans these niches if the ad account profile is not solid. Test such niches with open targeting from the start and read the gender and demographic breakdown in Facebook Ads Manager to decide whether to narrow the audience. [L1440.011]
  • Use Meta Ad Library to confirm that other advertisers run ads for the product before committing. [L1479.028, L1479.005]

Pinterest and YouTube Research

YouTube Keyword Discovery

  • For a surfboard seller, YouTube auto-suggest for the core term 'surfboards' returned variations by skill level (beginners, intermediate), fin setup (two fins, long fins), use case (roof racks, big waves, small waves), body type (big guys), and reviews. Each variation can be a separate video target because people already search for it. [L1511.023]
  • Brand-based YouTube keywords combine the brand, the product, and a review term, such as '[brand] [product] review' or '[brand] [product] reviews'. If you stock a specific brand, a review video targeting these phrases matches searches from people already interested in that brand. Add these phrases to the keyword research tracking spreadsheet alongside the generic terms. [L1511.024]

Virality Versus Real Sales

  • Viral views can cause sellers to believe a product works when it does not. The lesson recommends checking sales data across several accounts before copying a viral product, since many sellers reportedly lost time and money on a weak converter. [L1385.017]
  • Products that worked well on paid Facebook ads can be tested organically on TikTok and sometimes perform strongly, so test products from several sources rather than relying only on TikTok discovery. [L1444.007]
  • Sort by recent publish date and compare GPM rather than raw views to judge which creatives convert now. [L1479.030, L1479.031]
