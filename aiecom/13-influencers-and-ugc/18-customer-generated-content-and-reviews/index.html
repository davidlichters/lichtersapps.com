13.18 Customer-Generated content and reviews

Principles: why buyer content matters

  • Video reviews are a trust format that few competitors use in many niches, so publishing them can make your trust element stand out. Collect the largest number of reviews in your category as well, and state the count next to any claim about it. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0411.039]
  • Loyal repeat customers can become advocates who bring in new traffic, add credibility, and raise the brand's status in its market. Treat advocacy as a deliberate growth channel, not as a side effect of good service. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0414.054]
  • User-generated content is material created by site visitors, such as reviews and customer questions. It adds unique text to product pages and shows search engines that the store is a trusted source for those products. [L1190.025]
  • Customer photos can replace generic supplier images on a product page and make the store look unique. Three customer photos of the same product are enough to start using this on the site and can raise the conversion rate. [L0417.011]
  • UGC-style photos of real-looking customers holding the product act as social proof next to reviews. Rotating several variations on the product page or review platform keeps the set fresh. [L0794.047]
  • Collect UGC-style reviews from real customers instead of borrowing them from other sites. Real customer photos, videos and text are more trustworthy than copied material. (Older advice, Source A 2020.) [L1111.117]
  • Paying customers $2 to $15 in refunds for content can replace creator content that costs $50 to $100 or more per piece. Customer-made content looks raw and native, so it often performs better in ads than polished content ordered from creator platforms. [L0605.037]
  • A review without a review badge is still better than no review at all. Use the reviews you can collect even if they do not carry the badge. [L0965.075]

Setting up the request system

  • Set up a video review request system and include it in every review request. Video reviews are ideal, but image reviews are also a strong form of social proof. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0399.014]

  • Brands that use both request types usually ask for a photo first and send a review request afterwards, so the same buyer gets two separate post-delivery emails. The choice between them still depends on the product type and what buyers are willing to share. [L0417.025]

  • Ask for testimonials several times and in different formats, such as text, photo and video. More emails and more request types give a higher chance of collecting them. [L1076.058]

  • Collect testimonials by following up after each first order, and turn satisfied buyers into sources of quotes. Each new testimonial becomes proof for the next mailing. [L1369.036]

  • Collect customer reviews after each sale:

    1. Contact every buyer by phone or email.
    2. Ask for a review.
    3. Ask for a product photo taken with their phone if they are comfortable sharing it.
    4. If not, accept a text-based review.
    5. Place the reviews on the product pages.

    [L0806.074]

  • Use a post-purchase review service to collect unique store-level reviews for social proof. Yotpo, Shopify Reviews and Judge.me are recommended, though Yotpo was noted as expensive at the time. (Source A, undated source.) [L1357.036]

Request email templates

Use this template for the photo request after delivery:

Subject: Enjoying your [Product Name]?

Hi {{ first_name }},

We noticed your order of [Product Name] has been delivered, and we hope you are loving it!

We would love to see it in action. You can reply to this email with a photo of your [Product Name] in use, or post your photo on Instagram or Facebook using #[StoreHashtag] and tag our page @[StoreHandle]. Photos from our customers may be featured on our website.

Thank you for being part of the [Store Name] community!

[Your Name]
[Store Name]

[L0417.009]

  • Use a branded hashtag built from the store name, for example #[StoreName], and ask buyers to tag the store page handle, for example @[StoreHandle]. The hashtag and tag give friends who see the post a direct path to the store. [L0417.010]

Outreach to customers who have not reviewed

  • Write one or two sentences in the body asking for feedback on the product and their experience buying from your store, without a link or a photo request. [L0965.073]
  • When a customer replies, add the product-specific review to Judge.me as if it came from the customer. If the reply is about the store rather than the product, add it to the general store reviews area in Judge.me. [L0965.073]
  • Do not ask customers directly to leave a Google review. If a customer's reply is positive and you want a Google review, thank them and ask them to post the same message on your Google business profile, then link them to it. [L0965.074]

Collecting photos from buyers

Procedure for an automated photo request:

  1. Wait a few days after the customer's order has been delivered before contacting them.
  2. Send an automated email asking the customer to share a photo of the product in use.
  3. Let the customer reply to the email with the photo or post it on social media with the store hashtag and a tag of the store page.
  4. Collect the photos and use them on the website as product images.

[L0417.008]

  • Photo requests need the product in the customer's hands, with the face visible and smiling. Reward the photo with a refund or cashback, because this shows the customer is happy with the result. [L0871.054]
  • Create at least three different UGC variations for review photos, each with a different person, angle and setting. Varied people holding the product read as real customers and give the review set more credibility. [L0794.046]
  • Share customer photos on Facebook and Instagram with the customer's first name, location, the product name and the store name. Posts can be scheduled, for example once per month. (Older advice, Source A 2019.) [L1086.049]
  • When customers have sent photos of themselves with the product, use those images in preference to studio shots. Customer images show the product in real use. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1398.011]
  • Recruit creators or customers, for example in gyms, to film first-time product use reaction videos. Prioritize volume of UGC, because it both builds trust and fills the creative pipeline. [L1225.051]

Fashion niche: Instagram customer photos

Procedure for the fashion store:

  1. Build out the Instagram page for the fashion store, which is essential in the fashion niche.
  2. Post your own photos of the products.
  3. Ask customers to submit their photos of the products they bought.
  4. Add a 'shop Instagram' link in the store header so visitors can buy directly from the posted photos.

[L0838.017]

  • Customer photos on social media provide social proof and are a major part of the strategy of successful stores. Collecting as many real customer photos as possible is especially valuable in the fashion niche. [L0838.018]
  • An Instagram page with about 4,500 genuine followers drove sales when customers were asked to tag the brand. A post from an influencer with about 127,000 followers also brought some sales. [L1439.027]

Video reviews and video content

  • Only ask for a video review from customers who sound very positive during the call. Video requests are not for every customer. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1011.009]
  • Customer video reviews are user-generated content that the business gets to keep and reuse. They can also generate organic traffic when posted to a YouTube channel, which may rank in Google search. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1012.020]
  • Customer video reviews can run as Facebook video ads and as YouTube video ads. They are rarer to collect than written reviews, but they are worth using when available. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1398.012]
  • Video reviews are a trust format that few competitors use in many niches, so publishing them can make your trust element stand out. [L0411.039]

Procedure for turning customer feedback into video content:

  1. Ask existing customers for a video review.
  2. Collect the videos with a tool such as Video Peel.
  3. Edit a video only if it needs editing.
  4. Publish it on YouTube and link to the product in the description.

(Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1424.029]

Post-engagement testimonial campaign

Procedure:

  1. Create a post and run it as a post-engagement campaign, which does not show on the store's page feed but can be used in ads.
  2. Ask commenters to upload a photo of themselves with the product, or to write a short review if photos feel too hard.
  3. Connect the campaign to Minichat so each commenter is sent a gift automatically, such as free shipping or a small coupon.
  4. Reuse the post with its comments and photos as social proof in later campaigns.

[L1243.041]

  • A store ran a video post as a post-engagement campaign for under one week with a budget of a couple of hundred dollars. The post reached 17,000 views and earned 120 likes, almost 200 comments and 23 shares. The result was presented as strong social proof that could keep working as an evergreen post. [L1243.042]

UGC generator flow

Basic version:

  1. Create a JotForm where customers submit their custom content and grant you marketing rights.
  2. After the order is delivered, send an email asking the customer for UGC, linking to the form.

[L0604.011]

Full flow, in this order:

  1. Trigger the flow when the tracking app reports the order as delivered.
  2. Wait 1-2 days after delivery, so the customer has used the product.
  3. Send an email saying you want to showcase their happiness to other customers, and ask for a photo, selfie or video testimonial.
  4. Offer a reward, preferably a refund on their order, since an extra free item takes longer to deliver.
  5. Collect submissions through a form such as Jotform, with a category choice, an email field, a file upload and a consent checkbox.
  6. Use reviews directly, but ask for permission before using any content in marketing.

[L0605.035]

  • UGC Jotform form structure: first a category field to pick the submission type (product photo, selfie with product, video testimonial), then an email field so submissions can be verified, then a file upload field for the content, and finally a consent checkbox before submission. The form can be cloned from a template and the store name replaced. [L0605.038]
  • For a customer selfie with the product, use the caption formula [customer's situation] + [product] = [solution or benefit], and use it as the UGC form's prompt text. It works because the customer sees their own face in a solved situation, which makes them more willing to submit. Examples: Your cozy evenings + our blanket = warmth without the bulk and Your dog's morning walk + our leash = hands-free control. [L0605.051]

Consent and marketing permission

Consent checkbox text for using customer content in marketing:

Your positive feedback and experience with [STORE NAME] have been invaluable to us. We believe featuring your content on our website and social media channels can show other customers the value and benefits of our products. Tick the box below if you agree that we may use your submitted photo, selfie or video for marketing purposes.

[ ] Yes, I agree that [STORE NAME] may use my submitted content for marketing.

[L0605.039]

  • Use reviews directly, but ask for permission before using any content in marketing. [L0605.035]

Supplier review permissions

  • Ask the supplier before using their reviews, including paid or influencer reviews. If they approve, add any disclaimer the supplier requires on your store. (Source A 2024.) [L0471.018]

Template for asking a supplier to reuse their reviews (older advice, Source A 2020):

Hi [Supplier name],

I noticed your website has reviews for [product name or product number]. We don't have any reviews for your products yet. Would it be okay if we displayed these reviews on our website? Thank you for your help.

Best regards,
[Your name]

[L0551.022]

Procedure for sourcing supplier and customer reviews (older advice, Source A 2021):

  1. Check the supplier's website for reviews or testimonials already published, and ask the supplier if you may reuse them.
  2. After approval, ask suppliers for reviews and images they can share for your product pages.
  3. Ask customers for reviews after purchase through a follow-up sequence, and incentivize them to leave one.

[L1279.049]

Importing reviews

Procedure for importing AliExpress reviews into LUX Reviews:

  1. Install the LUX Reviews app, choose the Beginner plan (upgrade to Growth or Unlimited when scaling), start the trial and select take beginner instead.
  2. Set store primary language (English), choose star color (brand or gold).
  3. Set review request timing to 28 days and the 15% picture-review discount.
  4. Drag the LUX import bookmark to the bookmarks bar.
  5. On AliExpress open the product, click the bookmark and import to the store product URL.
  6. Filter to four stars and up, all countries, translated to English.
  7. Import reviews one by one after checking text and picture, then click finish importing.
  8. Check the reviews and the product page show the imported count and rating.

[L0477.012]

  • Import only supplier reviews rated four stars or higher, from all countries, translated to English, and review each before importing to check the text and pictures look good. [L0477.013]

Q&A on product pages

  • The SMPS Product Question and Answer app lets shoppers ask questions on product pages. The free plan is enough for new stores, and paid plans add an FAQ page. The same developer also offers a Google Shopping feed app for sending products to Google Merchant Center. [L1190.026]

Procedure for handling a product question in the Q&A app:

  1. A shopper submits a question on the product page with a name and email address.
  2. The store owner gets a notification that a question was asked.
  3. Write the answer in the app dashboard, or delete the question if it is not suitable.
  4. Publish the answer, which appears on the product page and is emailed to the shopper.
  5. Use the dashboard to see the number of questions, the most-viewed questions, and pending questions.

[L1190.027]

  • Enter questions that customers ask by phone, live chat, or email into the Q&A app yourself and answer them. Publish those answers on the product page when they would help future shoppers, because the answers also support SEO. [L1190.028]

Pinterest user content

  • About 70% of brand engagement on Pinterest is user-generated, meaning it happens without the brand posting anything itself. Stores should therefore make it easy for visitors to share their products. [L1136.006]
  • Customers who save and share products from a store's site can generate traffic and sales without any action from the store owner. A visible Pin It button is the simplest way to enable this passive sharing. [L1136.007]
  • Entering pinterest.com/source/ followed by your store domain, for example pinterest.com/source/www.yourdomain.com, shows all pins that users have saved from your website. It is a free way to see who is already sharing your products. [L1136.012]
  • Once you have found the users who have pinned your products, comment on their pins, follow their accounts and start a conversation with them. These users have already shown interest in your product, so they are warm potential customers. [L1136.013]
