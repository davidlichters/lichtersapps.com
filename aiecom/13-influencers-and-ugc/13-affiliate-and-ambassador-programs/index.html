13.13 Affiliate and ambassador programs

Recruiting Affiliates

Where to Find Affiliates

  • Start with your Dream 100 prospect list and look for potential affiliates there. [L0202.007]
  • Search Google directly for additional affiliates in your market. [L0202.007]
  • Evaluate whether each candidate is relevant to your market and products. [L0202.007]
  • Reach out by email (using templates) or by phone call. [L0202.007]
  • Present your affiliate program offer. [L0202.007]
  • Affiliate partners sometimes run offers as banner ads, which you can find during Dream 100 outreach or through searches on forums and Google. [L0288.001]
  • A prospect who already runs affiliate deals on a site, YouTube channel or other platform can be added to your affiliate program. They earn a commission per sale and gain new offers to share with their audience. [L0525.010]
  • Use an affiliate or commission plan to reward influencers and buyers who promote the product with a referral code, to keep fans engaged after the first purchase. [L0789.052]
  • Wholesale accounts such as hotels, cafes and shops can share the product with their own followers, which is a free channel for reaching buyers. Ask each new wholesale partner to promote the product and to let you show their logo on the website. [L1016.076]

Finding Content Creators via Google

Principle

  • Affiliate content creators produce material for the information-gathering stage of the buyer journey (reviews, how-to guides, top-10 lists) rather than commercial-intent keywords, because substantial content performs better with research-phase audiences. [L0215.004]

Search procedure

  1. Search Google using research-stage keywords: "best [product type]" and "top 10 [product type]" to locate content creators reviewing or comparing products in your category. [L0215.005]
  2. Examine the results for review and comparison guide websites. [L0215.005]
  3. Click through to these sites to identify which products they recommend and whether they use affiliate links. [L0215.005]

Mapping the full landscape

  • Test multiple search term variations on Google, such as "best [product]", "top 10 [product]" and related modifiers, to identify all affiliate review and comparison sites active in your product category and geographic market. This comprehensive mapping reveals the full landscape of potential affiliate partners. [L0215.013]

  • To map the landscape, search with these variations:

    1. "best [product type]"
    2. "top 10 [product type]"
    3. "[product type] reviews"
    4. "[product type] comparison"

    For price-sensitive categories, also search "best [product type] under $[price]" (e.g., "best headphones under $200"). Review the first 2-3 pages of results for each search to identify all major affiliate publishers in your niche. [L0215.020]

Evaluating candidates

  • Look for reviews comparing multiple products (typically 7 or more), with detailed feature breakdowns, score ratings, and pros/cons for each product. High-effort, comprehensive content indicates a serious publisher worth approaching; thin comparison pages are lower priority. [L0215.014]
  • Major media publications (e.g., New York Times, PC Magazine) frequently rank in "best [product]" searches but typically do not use visible affiliate offers due to editorial policies. Focus outreach on independent review sites and content creators where you can confirm active affiliate links, rather than spending effort on large publishers. [L0215.015]

Confirming an active affiliate link

  1. Hover your mouse over the "See Price" button or product link and read the URL shown in the bottom-left corner of the browser. [L0215.016]
  2. If the URL is unclear, right-click the link and select "Inspect" or "Inspect Element". [L0215.016]
  3. In the HTML code, locate the href= attribute containing the full destination URL. [L0215.016]
  4. Look for an affiliate tag parameter in the URL (e.g., "tag=rtings" for Amazon or a similar pattern for other platforms). [L0215.016]
  5. If a tag parameter is present, the link is active. If the URL ends at the product ID with no additional parameters, it is not an affiliate link. [L0215.016]
  • Affiliate links contain a tag parameter in the URL that identifies the affiliate partner and tracks commissions. Amazon affiliate links use the format tag=storeidentifier. Links without this parameter are not affiliate links. [L0215.006]
  • In Amazon affiliate links, the text after tag= is the affiliate's identifier and activates the affiliate commission tracking system (e.g., tag=rtings). [L0215.007]

Outreach template: message to a potential affiliate [L0215.017]

Hi [Name],

I came across your [article title/topic] and was impressed by your thorough analysis of [number] products in this category.

I noticed you're currently promoting products through [their affiliate program, e.g., Amazon Associates]. I sell [product category or specific products from their review], and I can offer you commission rates that are competitive with or better than what you're earning through [their current program].

[Add one of these based on your situation:]
- I carry the exact same products you reviewed.
- We offer similar high-quality products that fit perfectly with your audience.
- Our products align with the criteria you outlined in your review.

Would you be open to discussing a partnership? I'm happy to share details about commission rates, cookie duration, and partner support.

Looking forward to connecting.

[Your Name]
  • When contacting a content creator you have identified, propose a partnership by mentioning that you sell the same or comparable products and offer affiliate rates that may be better than or competitive with their current Amazon or alternative program rates. [L0215.008]

Negotiation

  • After pitching a potential affiliate, expect negotiation. They may accept your terms, request adjustments to commission or cookie duration, or decline. Treat each conversation professionally regardless of outcome, since affiliate relationships matter and a rejection today may become a partnership later. [L0215.019]

Recruiting in Facebook Groups

Procedure

  1. Go to Facebook and search for groups focused on affiliate marketing. [L0215.010]
  2. Join established affiliate-marketing communities that permit merchant recruitment posts. [L0215.010]
  3. Post your affiliate offer in the group. [L0215.010]
  4. Respond to interested affiliates who comment or send direct messages. [L0215.010]
  5. Share your affiliate program details with qualified partners. [L0215.010]

  • Before posting, check the group's pinned posts or description to confirm that merchant recruitment posts are allowed. Some groups restrict posting frequency, require specific formatting, or prohibit direct links; violating these rules can result in post removal or removal from the group. [L0215.021]

Post template [L0215.018]

Hi everyone,

We're recruiting affiliates to promote [product category] to their audiences.

**Affiliate offer details:**
- Product category: [Specific type, e.g., over-ear headphones, fitness trackers]
- Price point: $[price range]
- Commission: [X]% per sale
- Cookie duration: [X] days
- Tracking platform: [Affiliate software name, e.g., Refersion, Impact]

If you think your audience would be interested, send me a DM and I'll get you all the details to get started.

Thanks!
  • When posting an affiliate offer in a Facebook group, include: the product type or vertical, the retail selling price, the affiliate commission rate you are offering, the cookie duration and tracking system details, and a clear call-to-action directing interested affiliates to message you for program information. [L0215.012]

Affiliates Who Buy Traffic

  • Some affiliates buy their own paid traffic and treat the program as a business. They judge a program by whether the commission leaves them profit on offers that convert, not by the size of the catalog. [L0719.048]

Commission Rates and Cookies

Rate Ranges and Benchmarks

Source A (2020, older) 3-7% Used by some stores; affiliate programs were used rarely and depended on the store's profit [L1088.087]
Source A (2023) 5% or 7% Per sale made through the partner's link [L0528.012]
Source A (2023) Up to 30% Can be considered, but affiliates are hard to win early because a new store has no reputation [L0934.052]
Source A (2022) About 8-10% plus a free product Needed to recruit influencers or sites through an affiliate app, because a link alone generates no sales [L1113.068]
Source C 10-15% of revenue Per sale from a unique link; track with Shopify Collabs or another affiliate platform [L1213.050]
  • Where sources disagree, the values apply to different situations: 5-10% (2020) and 5-7% (2023) fit standard programs, 8-10% plus a free product (2022) fits recruiting through an app where a link alone brings nothing, up to 30% (2023) fits only a case where an affiliate is won with a strong offer, and 10-15% (Source C) fits a revenue-based ambassador setup. Source A's 2020 and 2022 advice is older. [L0961.070, L0528.012, L0934.052, L1113.068, L1213.050, L1088.087]

Tiered Rates by Partner Status

  • Affiliate commission structure: 6% for new affiliate partners, 7% for long-term partners who have consistently performed. [L0226.007]
  • Long-term influencer partners earn passive revenue: articles they wrote years ago continue generating income through affiliate commissions and ad placements. Paying 7% commission to experienced partners versus 6% to newcomers acknowledges the compounding value from evergreen content. [L0226.044]

Setting the Rate by Promotion

  • Set affiliate commissions based on how the affiliate promotes you, such as email to their list, sidebar placements, articles, social media or a footer link. The more prominent the promotion, the higher the justified rate. [L0961.071]

Cookie Duration

  • Cookie duration is stated in every offer (e.g., "Cookie duration: [X] days") and must be included in outreach and group posts. [L0215.018, L0215.012]

Timing

  • Affiliate programs work best once a store has steady sales and history. Early affiliate traffic is usually a small share of total traffic. [L0970.124]
  • Expect few positive responses from bloggers and YouTube creators until the store has an established sales history. [L0970.123]
  • Affiliates are hard to win early because a new store has no reputation; paid sponsored placements, such as a blog post or sidebar image on relevant sites, tend to get results faster in the beginning. [L0934.052]

Payment Logic

  • The store pays nothing until link clicks turn into sales. [L0528.012]

Affiliate Software and Setup

Refersion

  • Refersion integrates directly with Shopify and can run affiliate and influencer programs. It has been used successfully for this purpose. [L0803.079]
  • Affiliates who log into Refersion can browse all available banners in the Creatives section, select whichever they want at any time, and test different versions to see what drives more clicks. They do not need to request new assets or wait for custom creation. [L0288.011]

Referson / Shopify app

  • The Referson app on Shopify runs an affiliate program, where you choose the commission percentage and cookie duration. It works well when your margins allow commissions. [L0961.072]

Shopify Collabs and other platforms

  • Track affiliate and brand-ambassador deals with Shopify Collabs or another affiliate platform. [L1213.050]

Shopify listing partners (complementary stores)

  • Shopify stores that are not direct competitors but sell complementary products can list your product for you, with no product data required from you. You then fulfill the order through your supplier and pay them an affiliate-style cut, so this works best for products where you can afford an affiliate fee. [L0869.010]

Tracking links and tags

  • Use the tag parameter in affiliate URLs to identify the partner and track commissions (see Finding Content Creators via Google above). [L0215.006, L0215.007]

Banners and Creatives

  • Partners with banner ads place image-based advertisements across website headers, sidebars, or other locations. [L0288.001]
  • Create banner ads either tied to specific products or as general business-wide promotions. General business banners work well for affiliate placements where partners earn a commission whenever their visitors buy anything from you. [L0288.005]
  • You create and provide all banner creatives. Affiliate partners do not create banners for themselves; they expect ready-made, professional options they can immediately use on their sites. [L0288.008]
  • Affiliates can choose and test banners in the Refersion Creatives section at any time, without requesting new assets. [L0288.011]

Tiers and Brand Ambassadors

Affiliate and Ambassador Commissions

  • Affiliate or brand-ambassador deals pay about 10% to 15% of revenue from each sale generated through a unique link. Track these with Shopify Collabs or another affiliate platform. [L1213.050]
  • Formula: Commission = 15% of the revenue from orders placed through the influencer's link. Example: 100K followers with 2% buying gives 2,000 buyers whose purchases form the revenue base. [L1213.051]

Upgrading Tiers

  • Upgrade affiliates from a Gold to a Platinum ambassador tier once they reach a sales threshold. The upgrade motivates them to bring more sales. [L1213.052]

Micro-Influencer Affiliate Program

  1. Find small influencers who are active on TikTok or Instagram. [L1439.025]
  2. Gift them one product. [L1439.025]
  3. They promote it on their platforms. [L1439.025]
  4. They receive a commission for every sale made through their channel. [L1439.025]

Referral Programs

  • A referral program lets existing customers refer family and friends, who both get a discount such as a set percentage off. It works better early on than recruiting affiliates. [L0804.058]
  • Referral programs can provide customers with ready-made social posts or emails to share with friends and followers. This turns existing customers into a channel that spreads the offer. [L1234.007]

Brand Ambassadors and Loyalty

  • The hourglass funnel turns new buyers into brand ambassadors who are happy to recommend the store. Their referrals bring higher quality traffic back to the top of the funnel, which converts into more customers. [L0676.008]
  • Use an affiliate or commission plan to reward influencers and buyers who promote the product with a referral code, to keep fans engaged after the first purchase. [L0789.052]

Affiliate Apps and Incentives

  • An affiliate app only works when you recruit influencers or sites with a strong offer, such as a free product and a commission around 8 to 10 percent, because a link alone generates no sales. [L1113.068]

Affiliate Partnerships with Complementary Brands

  • Outreach to magazines, blogs and authority websites you already have a relationship with: invite them to become affiliates and ask whether they would write a review as an affiliate. [L0768.018]
  • Affiliate partners can be asked to add your product to their recommended product list, not only to write a review. This placement on existing lists of trusted sites is a separate step from the review request. [L0768.025]
  • Find indirect competitors in your niche (e.g., drum lessons if you sell drum kits): [L0251.026]
    1. Identify indirect competition products related to your niche. [L0251.026]
    2. Search Google for "best [related product/service]." [L0251.026]
    3. Visit top results and look for "Partner Program," "Affiliate Program," or a similar link in the footer. [L0251.026]
    4. Email them directly with your store information and ask about affiliate terms. [L0251.026]
    5. Negotiate the commission structure (fixed dollar or percentage per sale). [L0251.026]
    6. Get a unique affiliate link and promote it on relevant product pages. [L0251.026]
