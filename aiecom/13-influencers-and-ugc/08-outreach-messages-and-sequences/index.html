13.8 Outreach messages and sequences

Channel: DM or Email

  • Send outreach as a direct message where possible, since DMs are faster, get higher response rates, and reach the creator directly instead of a manager who may slow the reply. Use email only when DMs are not possible. [L0782.005, L0792.002]
  • Use email as the first contact when the creator lists an email address, and follow up with a DM. Instagram DMs are often buried and rarely read. [L1379.005, L1379.007]
  • Use email for influencer outreach because it looks more professional. A DM can work only when the sender has a verified account with a large follower base; otherwise lean toward email. [L0783.002]
  • Which rule applies:
Situation Rule to follow Source
A DM route exists and the email address is not listed DM first L0782.005, L0792.002
The creator lists an email address Email first, then a follow-up DM L1379.005, L1379.007
The sender has a verified account with a large follower base DM is acceptable L0783.002
The sender has no verified large account Email L0783.002
  • Outreach emails should come from a personal-style address on a separate outreach domain. Details are in the sender section below. [L0789.012]

Platform Choice

  • Outreach works best on the platform where a creator is least active or has the fewest followers, because they reply more often there. [L1214.046]
  • If you find a creator on one platform, check their other accounts and message the one with the smallest following. [L1214.046]

Sender Identity and Outreach Domain

  • Send outreach from a personal-style address on a separate domain, formatted as firstname.lastname@ followed by a separate outreach domain. [L0789.012]
  • Sign with a title such as Marketing Officer and the company name. [L0789.012]
  • The source describes this setup as significantly raising open and reply rates. [L0789.012]

Vetting Before Outreach

  1. Build a list of influencers that fit the criteria: one country or language, one niche, follower range, minimum 5 percent engagement rate, and no own product yet. [L0789.015]
  2. Verify engagement rate and follower trend with a second analytics tool, and check placement history. [L0789.015]
  3. Send the first outreach email from a personal-style address with a marketing-officer signature, using an automated sequence. [L0789.015]
  4. Once there is a positive reply, send a due-diligence template asking the influencer for audience countries, age split, gender split, and store reviews, to confirm the analytics data. [L0789.015]
  5. Move the conversation to a call, since influencers avoid direct partnership pitches by email. [L0789.015]
  6. Agree a deal structure and calculate what you can pay before sending a promotion brief. [L0789.015]

Personalisation

  • Tailor every outreach message to the individual creator. Reference one specific piece of their content they did well. [L0782.007]
  • Never send the same copy-pasted message to many influencers. Each outreach should pick out something specific the creator did well and explain why that content fits your brand and campaign. [L0782.007, L0792.004]
  • Address each creator by name and mention a specific aspect of their work that matches your brand, because creators can immediately tell a personal message from a mass one. [L0783.003]
  • Mass emails with unfilled merge fields in the greeting, such as a placeholder where the creator's name or handle should be, are recognised at once as copy-paste outreach and are sent straight to spam by creators. [L0783.004]
  • Each outreach message should name a specific reason the creator fits, such as their style or feed, instead of generic praise. This makes the request look personal and gives the creator a reason to respond. [L0791.003]
  • Clearly state how the collaboration fits your brand and also how it helps the influencer reach their own goals. This alignment is a key reason creators accept. [L0792.006]
  • A handwritten note from a founder or CEO that refers to a specific recent video of the creator makes a PR package far more persuasive and increases the creator's willingness to work with the brand. The note works because it shows real interest in what the creator makes, not only in the product. [L0783.005]

Opening Lines

  • An opener such as "we would love to work with you" without any details gets an immediate rejection from creators. [L0783.008]
  • Opening formula (Source E): the creator's name, one concrete reference to a recent video or post, and the single reason the brand fits their audience. It works because it proves the message was written for that particular person. [L0783.006]
    • Example 1: Hi Maya, the drawer-organization video you posted last week made me rethink how we lay out our product range, and I would love to talk about a collaboration. [L0783.006]
    • Example 2: Hi Jonas, the way you broke down your marathon training plan in your last series is exactly how our running-gear customers think, so I would like to discuss a campaign with you. [L0783.006]

Core Message Formulas

  • Short DM formula (Source C): one sentence introducing yourself and your brand, one specific sentence praising a particular piece of their work, and one sentence explaining why they fit your current campaign and how the collaboration serves both sides. Keep the message brief and clear. [L0782.006]
    • Example A (fitness supplement brand, 2026): Hi Lena, I'm Marco from Northfield Nutrition. Your morning-routine series on creatine timing was the clearest thing I've seen on the topic. Our new electrolyte launch fits your audience of home-workout viewers, and I think a paid collaboration would work well for both of us. [L0782.006]
    • Example B (pet accessories store): Hi Tom, I'm Sara, founder of Pawline. Your slow-motion clips of your rescue dog on the trail are great, especially the one with the harness. We're launching a reflective leash in March that suits your outdoor content, and I'd love to talk terms if you're open to it. [L0782.006]
  • Four-part DM formula (Source C): a very short message with (1) a brief introduction of you and your brand, (2) a specific expression of admiration for their work, (3) a clear explanation of why they fit your current campaign, and (4) a simple next step. The influencer can read it in seconds and sees that you actually looked at their content. [L0792.003]
    • Example 1: Hi Lena, I'm Sam from a home-kitchen brand. Your slow-motion knife-skills video made me rethink how we show product texture. We're launching a cookware set for home cooks next month and your audience is a close match, so would you be open to a short call about a paid collaboration? [L0792.003]
    • Example 2: Hey Marco, I run a small outdoor gear shop. Your honest review of a beginner trail pack stood out because you showed what failed too. We're building a spring campaign for first-time hikers and I think your style would carry it well. Can I send you the details? [L0792.003]
  • Ask for one clear action per message: ask whether they are interested, or request their media kit after they have shown interest. Several requests in one message make the reply harder. [L0791.004]
  • Eight-step structure (Source C):
    1. Find the creator's contact details; an email address in the bio is preferred because it feels more professional. [L0788.005]
    2. Open with praise for a specific piece of their work, in two or three lines. [L0788.005]
    3. Pitch your product and explain how it helps their audience. [L0788.005]
    4. Mention the collaboration and the benefit it gives them. [L0788.005]
    5. Use the headline brand collaboration opportunity so the message feels like a big opportunity to them. [L0788.005]
    6. If there is no reply, follow up by email. [L0788.005]
  • Outreach message pattern (2021-2022, older): the first message should be short and personal, not a sales pitch. It should name something specific you admire in their work, state the shared space or audience in one line, mention that you see possible joint opportunities, and ask directly whether they would join a short call. [L0525.024]
    • Example: Hi Sam, I enjoyed your piece on home coffee gear, and our customers overlap a lot. I think there is a good way for us to work together. Would you be open to a 20-minute call next week? [L0525.024]
  • Outreach procedure (2021-2022, older):
    1. Reach out in the channel you already use with the prospect, such as email if you have their address, a Facebook message to a group admin, or an Instagram direct message. [L0525.023]
    2. Send a short introduction message that says you like their work, that you are in the same space, that you see possible opportunities to work together, and asks whether they would be open to a call. [L0525.023]
    3. If they agree, agree on a time for the call. [L0525.023]
    4. Hold the call and explore how you can work together. [L0525.023]
  • Collaboration discussion steps (2021-2022, older):
    1. Use email templates to reach out when relevant. [L0202.005]
    2. Initiate a discussion about collaboration opportunities. [L0202.005]
    3. Frame it as finding mutual benefit. [L0202.005]
    4. Ask directly: "How can we work together?" [L0202.005]
    5. Identify specific collaboration possibilities during discussion. [L0202.005]

Comment-Based Openers

  • Expect that most influencers will not accept a proposal from the first direct message. Plan to contact a large list, because a low response rate is normal. [L1379.004]
  • Comment-based procedure (Source C):
    1. Send an email to the creator if an email address is listed. [L1379.007]
    2. Send a follow-up DM on Instagram or TikTok. [L1379.007]
    3. If there is no email or DM option, comment on the creator's most recent video with a short collaboration message. [L1379.007]
    4. Be among the first commenters, because creators tend to reply to the first 10 to 15 comments and to new messages. [L1379.007]
  • Comment opener template:
Check your DM, let's work together! (Alternative openers: 'Collaboration?' or 'Do you take sponsors now?')

[L1379.008]

  • Post this opener as a comment on the creator's newest video so it is among the first comments. [L1379.008]
  • Reply template for a creator who commented on your post:
Thanks so much for commenting! We would love to send you [PRODUCT] for free if you'd like to promote it on your socials. Reply here if you're interested and I'll send you the details.

[L1379.009]

Offer Messages: Gifting, Affiliate, and Podcasts

  • Gifting template (product under a value cap or a specific product):
Hey there, I'm a big fan of your videos. I'm writing on behalf of my company, [brand name]. Should you be open to it, we'd like to ship you [a product under $800 in value, or the specific product I want featured]. In return, you'd produce a video, publish it on your YouTube channel, and give us a shout-out saying you got the item from [brand name] and that [brand name] is a strong place to shop for anyone in your audience who wants [product].

[L0528.031]

  • Affiliate template (commission-based, no upfront cost to the creator):
Hello, I'm reaching out on behalf of [brand name]. If it interests you, we run an affiliate program: you share a link to our shop and collect [5% or 7%] of the sale for each purchase that comes through it. Payment happens only when clicks convert into orders, so trying it costs you nothing.

[L0528.030]

  • Podcast pitch: pitch with specific episode ideas that match what the audience cares about, such as market trends or a new brand, and offer to appear as a guest. Each accepted appearance sends traffic to your store. [L0528.009]

  • Influencer outreach agents are inexpensive to hire, and more outreach generally produces more promotions, more posts, and more sales. Even if the owner is better at picking talent, outreach volume still drives deals. [L1129.007]

Terms and Timing of the Deal Conversation

  • Do not negotiate terms in the first outreach message. Start the terms discussion (compensation, content, timelines, contract details) only after the influencer has replied and shown real interest in working with you. [L0782.015]
  • State the core terms in the first message: deliverables, timeline, and whether the campaign is paid. Compensation can be left for the negotiation stage, but everything else should be open from the start, since creators receive many emails daily and avoid vague messages that need several exchanges. [L0783.007]
  • Both rules apply together: name the deliverables, timeline, and paid status in the first message, and hold the compensation negotiation until the creator has replied. [L0782.015, L0783.007]
  • Case: a technology brand launched a wireless mechanical keyboard and mouse set in a special colour edition and wanted creators to promote it as a social activation. [L0783.012]
    • The agency's casting producer emailed a creator with the product link, a description of the campaign, and a request for the creator's content ideas. [L0783.012]
    • The offer listed the deliverables: 1 TikTok post with a paid media push, plus a 6-month usage term across the brand's channels with worldwide territory and all digital formats. [L0783.012]
    • The timeline was: product and content brief sent as soon as possible, first edit on 4 Nov (pending product arrival), client feedback on 5 Nov, and content live on 9 Nov. [L0783.012]
    • Because the email was complete and clearly a legitimate inquiry, the creator responded immediately, and rates were negotiated afterwards. [L0783.012]
    • Knowing the brand beforehand helped but was not required. [L0783.012]

Follow-Up Sequence and Timing

  1. Send the first outreach mail introducing yourself, your brand, and the niche, and asking whether the creator is interested. [L0791.001]
  2. If the creator replies positively, send the second mail with the collaboration brief and ask for their media kit. [L0791.001]
  3. If there is no reply after 3 days, send the first follow-up. [L0791.001]
  4. If there is still no reply 2 days after the first follow-up, send the second follow-up. [L0791.001]
  5. The sequence has no step after the second follow-up. [L0791.001]
  • Timing: wait 3 days after the first outreach mail before the first follow-up, then wait 2 days after the first follow-up before the second follow-up. [L0791.002]
  • Keep the same thread so the creator sees the earlier messages. [L0791.002]

Response-Rate Expectations

Measure Value Source When it applies
Typical response rate for influencer outreach 2% to 3% L0788.006 General planning; contact volume must be high
Replies from a list of 100 contacts About 10 reply; 3 to 4 agree to your terms L1213.045 Prospecting session with 100 influencers
Outreach emails to meetings and partnerships About 1,000 emails lead to about 8 meetings and 1 profitable partnership L0789.016 Full funnel from email to profitable deal
Accepted paid influencer offers (cold, general) From 30 influencers contacted, expect 1 to 2 to accept; plan for rejection as the baseline L0226.003 Baseline for cold influencer outreach
Trial-offer method for paid advertising Works 9 out of 10 times L0183.004 Standard trial offer method for paid placements (blog, site, channel, event)
  • The figures above measure different steps (replies, agreements, meetings, accepted offers), so read each against its own step. [L788.006, L1213.045, L0789.016, L0226.003, L0183.004]
  • Each careful research session takes about two to three hours. [L1213.045]
  • Expect that most creators contacted early will not reply, and that roughly the first 20 outreach messages may produce few or no responses. Keep building the list rather than judging the campaign by the first few contacts. [L1379.052]
  • Early outreach to creators was manual: the team searched Instagram by category and sent individual emails with short, specific subject lines. Reply rates were higher in the early years and dropped as competition for creators grew. [L0790.099]

Volume and Split Testing

  • Split-test outreach templates because a template that works in one market may fail in another. Keep the first email short and aimed at getting any positive reply, such as a request for more information or a yes. [L0789.013]
  • Contact a large list when prospecting, because low response rates are normal and volume drives results. [L1379.004, L1213.045, L1129.007]
  • Each message should contain one clear call to action, so split tests should vary one element at a time. [L0791.004, L0789.013]
