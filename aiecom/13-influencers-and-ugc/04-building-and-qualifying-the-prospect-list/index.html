13.4 Building and qualifying the prospect list

Setting Up the Tracker and Channel Mix

Follow these steps to set up the tracker. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0202.001]

  1. Create a tracker document to organize Dream 100 prospects.
  2. Identify prospects across multiple channels (social media, email, communities, forums).
  3. Follow them on their platforms.
  4. Subscribe to their email newsletters.
  5. Join their Facebook groups and online forums.
  • Target platforms for Dream 100 are blogs and websites, online forums (including the forum and its owners or moderators), Facebook groups, Instagram, YouTube, and LinkedIn for B2B businesses only. Other channels relevant to a niche, such as Pinterest for interior design, can be added. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.008]
  • Facebook pages with large followings are excluded from Dream 100 targets, because page posts reach under 10% of followers and page audiences are largely unengaged unless you run ads. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.009]
  • Facebook group admins are valid targets, because their posts reach at least around 50% of members as notifications. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.009]
  • A creator who posts on several platforms can be entered more than once, and a single person can have several entries. Some niches only have YouTube, Instagram and Facebook groups, which is acceptable. Aim for a reasonably balanced spread across channels when the market allows it. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.010]
  • Instagram reach is moving closer to Facebook reach but is still much better than Facebook pages. LinkedIn and YouTube also reach more of their followers than Facebook pages, so an active creator on these platforms is more valuable. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.026]
  • A large forum can be split into subsections. When one section matches your target market better than the whole forum, enter that subsection as its own tracker row next to the parent forum. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.023]

Pure Creators and Sources Without a Website

  • Pure creators with no website or other online presence usually earn from ad revenue, sponsorships, viewer donations or membership platforms. They typically form a single entry in the matrix with only a YouTube or podcast property. This is acceptable, because the entry still shows where your buyers watch. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0478.033]

Sourcing Experts and Creators

  • Experts in a niche include anyone with a large audience there, such as large YouTube channels, large blogs, or pro athletes with big followings. Identifying them is useful for ad targeting and for partnership opportunities. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0872.021]

  • Example: a seller of running shoes would search Google for the top runners with large followings, look up lists of experts in the running niche, and search YouTube for running channels that have many followers. The creators found this way go into the Experts Followed field. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0872.022]

  • Build a list of potential influencer and authority partners:

    1. Search Pinterest or Google for people already using or showing your product.
    2. Note each person's name and category, such as social media influencer, actor, artist, celebrity or doctor.
    3. Record each person's social media handle next to their name.

    [L1268.009]

  • Creator discovery and contact procedure:

    1. Find one well-known creator in your niche as a starting point.
    2. Study the accounts that like and comment on that creator's posts to find more creators with the same audience.
    3. Search Google for the creator's name plus email to find contact details.
    4. If the bio has no email, send a direct message; keep the first message short and specific because high-profile creators receive many messages.
    5. Use sales CRM tools that scrape contact information as a supplement to manual search.
    6. For high-level creators, contact their manager or agency.

    [L0790.062]

YouTube Checks

  1. Open the Videos tab and check upload dates to confirm regular posting.
  2. Check the view counts and comments on recent videos.
  3. Open the About section for the creator's name, website, email and social links.
  4. Note the channel's subscriber count and whether it is focused on your niche.

Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.014]

  • Quick credibility check on the About page: note the join date and the lifetime view count in the Stats section. Combine these with the subscriber count and recent video views before adding the channel to the tracker. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.028]
  • Upload frequency: the ideal is weekly uploads. A channel that uploads roughly monthly can still be a valid target if its recent videos keep consistent view counts. Check the upload dates on the Videos tab to judge this. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.027]

Forum and Website Checks

  • Judge engagement by checking whether recent posts receive answers and views. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.016]

  • A blog with many posts but no comments generally has a weak audience. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.016]

  • Check monthly traffic with a domain tool. Aim for more than 10,000 visits per month as a safe level. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.016]

  • Domain traffic tools for checking a website's monthly visits when researching Dream 100 targets:

    • Ubersuggest
    • Ahrefs
    • Semrush

    Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.017]

  • A Dream 100 target who is a moderator or owner of a forum or Facebook group is often only visible after joining, so the admin's name can be filled in later. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.019]

Facebook Group Checks

  • Facebook group search results show whether a group is Public or Private, its member count, and its posts per day.
  • In a private group you cannot see the discussion before joining. Read the group rules and judge activity from the listing stats first. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.021]

Finding Contact Details and Advertising Pages

  • For a blog or website, look for the owner's name on the About Us page and any contact details on the Contact page, then record them in the tracker. If the site has no about or contact information, the name can be left blank for now. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.024]

  • On a blog or media site's contact page, look for a separate podcast, media, advertising or partnership email before using the general contact address. Send guest and partnership pitches to that role-specific address, because it usually reaches the person who handles those requests. [L0528.048]

  • Look for advertising pages on websites and forums, labelled such as Advertise or Advertise with us. They often list banner ads, sponsored listings or business listings. Record the advertising page URL in the tracker, because advertising can be a future traffic source alongside the relationship. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.018]

  • Forums and websites often have an advertise page listing:

    • banner ads
    • single product listings
    • affiliate or multi-product store listings
    • spot or location pages
    • business listings

    Record the advertising page link and the types of placements offered in the tracker. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0527.022]

Verifying AI-Generated Lists

  • AI-generated partner lists can contain broken websites, wrong or missing emails, and names that do not match. Every entry must be verified manually before outreach. [L0528.024]
  • One AI run returned only 30 rows, and a rerun asking for 100 rows returned 42. If the output is short, keep the usable rows and build the rest through manual research. [L0528.025]
  • Prompt for generating the list:
As the proprietor of [STORENAME], you are assembling a list of 100 potential partnership targets: public figures, media properties, independent publishers, video creators, course developers, industry events, social media personalities, podcast producers, and professional organizations whose audiences overlap with your [PRODUCTS] customer base. Record this list in a spreadsheet with four columns: (A) the category describing the partner type (Public Figure, Media Property, Writer, Video Creator, Course Developer, Event, Social Media Creator, Podcaster, or Professional Group), (B) the person's or organization's name, (C) their web presence address if available, and (D) their contact email if obtainable. Complete the list with 100 entries.

[L0183.005]

  • Verification procedure for each AI-listed entry:

    1. Open each URL the AI listed. If the site is broken or flagged unsafe, search the person or business name to find the working site.
    2. Confirm the site matches the name and note the audience location, for example a site based in one country with a US audience.
    3. Find the contact page or email, using Cmd+F (Ctrl+F on Windows) to search the page for contact.
    4. If there is no email address, record the contact form or an Instagram account for direct messages.
    5. Check blog posts for named outside authors. If none appear across several posts, mark guest posts as no.
    6. Check the sidebar, footer and blog for current ads. If none are running, mark paid ads as NA and note any empty ad space.
    7. Look for an advertising or media section and for emails shown as images, which AI tools cannot read. Copy those addresses manually.
    8. Enter the contact details, dates and notes in the sheet.

    [L0528.023]

Expanding the List and Qualifying Partners

  • Before outreach, open the partner's about or partners page and look for other companies they already work with. Add those linked companies to the list as new rows, since they are likely to be relevant to your niche too. [L0528.047]
  • If a prospect gets annoyed when you respectfully disagree with a specific point in their content, treat that as a sign they may be a poor long-term partner. Creators who care about delivering value to their audience usually welcome honest, constructive feedback. Older advice, 2021-22. [L0453.021]
