13.16 Commissioning UGC Creators

Sourcing creators on marketplaces

  • Four platform routes appear in the sources: Upwork, Fiverr, the TikTok Creator Marketplace and Billow. An agency that sources creators on incense.com is a fifth route. [L0637.044, L0644.031, L1402.010, L1402.012, L1402.015, L1127.030]
  • The marketplace named Billow is spelled "Billo" in one source and "Billow" in another. [L0995.052, L1402.015]

Hiring a UGC creator on Upwork

  1. Post a job describing the new e-commerce project and the number of creatives needed.
  2. Send all applicants a Google Form with questions on expectations, salary and experience.
  3. Shortlist the answers, which removes about half of the applicants.
  4. Interview the rest via Zoom.

[L0637.044]

Working with a freelancer on Fiverr

  1. Find a UGC creator on Fiverr with relevant experience, such as a journalism background.
  2. Send the product and allow about a week for the creator to receive it before filming.
  3. Send the creator a script or a clear steer on the angle before filming starts.
  4. Review the footage and test it against the existing hooks and angles.

[L0644.031]

  1. Search for UGC on Fiverr.
  2. Scroll through the freelancers offered.
  3. Choose the freelancer whose style fits your product, reviewing their past work.
  4. Start working with them.
  5. If the result is good, continue the collaboration long term.

[L1402.012]

Sourcing on the TikTok Creator Marketplace

  1. Open creatormarketplace.tiktok.com and create an account to get full platform access.
  2. Create a campaign and enter the store and product information.
  3. Wait for creators to apply to the campaign.
  4. Approve the creator you want to work with.
  5. Ship the product to the creator.
  6. Receive the UGC video.

[L1402.010]

Ordering a video on Billow

Billow offers a video ad, TikTok short, product showcase or honest review, priced at about $119 for one quoted order. [L0995.052]

  1. Create an account on the Billow website.
  2. Click Get a video.
  3. Select an existing product you used before, or create a new product.
  4. Choose the video type: video ad, TikTok short, product showcase, or honest review.
  5. Choose how many creators should work on the product.
  6. Check the final price, which updates after every change, and adjust the settings.
  7. Choose the format: portrait, landscape, vertical, or square.
  8. Choose whether to add subtitles, animations, or your own pictures.
  9. Set the preferred creator age so it matches your target customer.
  10. Set the preferred creator gender; for a product for women only, deselect male and keep female.
  11. Choose the creator interests that fit the product.
  12. Add additional notes for the creator.
  13. Click Next step and complete the payment.
  14. Within 2-3 days, creators apply to the task.
  15. Filter the applicants and choose the creator you like most.
  16. Within 1-2 weeks after the creator receives the product, you get the finished video.

[L1402.015]

Billow order options:

  • Get a video
  • Product (existing or new)
  • Video type (video ad, TikTok short, product showcase, honest review)
  • Number of creators
  • Format (portrait, landscape, vertical, square)
  • Subtitles
  • Animations
  • Added pictures
  • Preferred creator age
  • Preferred creator gender (deselect a gender to exclude it)
  • Creator interests
  • Additional notes
  • Next step (payment)

Using an agency

  • An agency can research the product and store, write scripts and send them to creators found on incense.com. Creator fees are paid separately from the agency fee. [L1127.030]
  • An agency can write briefs or scripts for creators, who then shoot the ad videos, so the store owner only reviews the finished videos. This moves content production out of the owner's hands once the store is scaling. [L0694.049]

Writing the brief and script

  • Give ad creators a detailed, well-written ad script before outsourcing video. The script should surface the problem, give the solution, educate, create curiosity and build desire. Fiverr creators are often generalists rather than ad copywriters, so a great script gets great videos and a poor one wastes money. [L0308.026]
  • Send the script before the product ships. A creator needs about one week to receive the product before filming, so writing and sending the script during that wait lets filming start as soon as the product arrives. [L1426.023, L1426.051]

Raw scene clips

  • Ask creators for raw 3-5 second scene clips, not a finished 60-second video. Each scene is quick to record, which makes the work cheap for creators and gives you more material to assemble. [L0398.046]

Creative brief math:

  • One 60-second brief is split into 12 scenes of about 5 seconds each: 3 hook scenes, 3 product-introduction scenes, 3 benefit scenes and 3 CTA scenes. [L0398.047]
  • Choosing one of 3 hooks, one of 3 intros, one of 3 benefit clips and one of 3 CTAs gives 3 x 3 x 3 x 3 = 81 combinations. [L0398.047]
  • About 20 high-quality videos can be picked from these combinations, so a $60 creator fee works out to about $3 per video. [L0398.047]

Raw clip request template:

Subject: Raw clips for [PRODUCT NAME]

Hi [CREATOR NAME],

We would like to work with you on [NUMBER] short scene videos for [PRODUCT NAME]. Please do not send a finished 60-second video. Instead, record separate raw clips of about 3-5 seconds each, one scene per clip, with no editing and no music.

Please record these scenes:
- 3 hook scenes: [HOOK ANGLE 1], [HOOK ANGLE 2], [HOOK ANGLE 3]. Keep the first moment strong and simple.
- 3 product-introduction scenes: the product in your hand, an unboxing or first use of [PRODUCT NAME].
- 3 benefit scenes: show [BENEFIT 1], [BENEFIT 2] and [BENEFIT 3] in use. Speak or show the benefit clearly.
- 3 call-to-action scenes: you holding the product and saying [CTA LINE, e.g. shop now or check the link].

For each scene, use a plain background and keep the phone steady. Send all clips separately so we can combine them ourselves.

Thank you,
[YOUR NAME]

[L0398.048]

  • Some UGC creators now charge more for raw clips, because the brand does the editing and the creator's output becomes more valuable. Agree on a clear fee for the number of raw clips before the shoot. [L0398.049]

Matching creators to the audience

  • Audience demographics matter in UGC because people relate to people who look like them. If you target Gulf countries, consider whether the creator's ethnicity and accent match the audience. Test both approaches and measure performance. [L0308.027]
  • Match the creator's age and gender to the target customer so viewers can relate to the person showing the product. For a female-only product, exclude male creators. [L1402.020]
  • When the product cannot be sourced in your own market, hire creators in the target country so the content looks local and relatable. Creators in the same market are easier to brief for raw scenes. [L0398.050]
  • Hire creators from the country you are targeting and use a local accent or voice. In one lesson, UK-accented UGC for UK customers brought most sales. [L0773.054]

Case: a UK-accented creator for a UK audience

A store where about 90% of buyers came from the UK added TikTok UGC made by a creator with a British accent, and sales rose sharply. The seller then offered a simple digital user guide, made with ChatGPT and one or two images, as a free gift with purchase, listed at 19.95 so the free gift looked valuable. Earlier buyers who had missed the guide bought it once they heard about it, and the seller then gave it to them free as extra service. Reviews were five stars, and the seller had at one point reported a conversion rate of 11.11%. The seller planned to run the same ads on Facebook and to add picture ads. [L0774.054]

  • For organic growth on Instagram or TikTok, hire a creator to produce several videos you can post on your own account. Relatable people using the product help the audience connect with the store. [L1402.004]

Vetting creators

  • Freelance sites such as Fiverr often produce poor-quality UGC. Vet candidates with a sample or test video before committing, and expect many misses before you find a good creator. [L1077.031]
  • Reviews alone do not guarantee video quality. A freelance video seller with thousands of five-star reviews delivered three unusable videos. Test a seller with a small order before committing. [L1107.021]

Contracts, volume and rights

  • Set UGC work as a daily output over a month, for example at least three videos per day, so the creator can calculate total volume and quote a price. Negotiate after the first month based on quality. [L1214.049]
  • Hire creators to make videos you post on your own accounts, since their own feeds may be weak. Talented creators with low views can be better value than popular ones. [L1214.050]
  • Hire a mediocre creator, train them for a few weeks, then work with a top creator at roughly a quarter of the price. Long-term contracts usually bring better rates. [L1214.051]
  • When paying influencers for video, commission clips you can run as paid ads, not one-off posts on their own channel. Secure the right to use the footage in your own campaigns before the video is made. [L1017.042]

Group chats, content calendars and audits

  • Create a group chat for UGC creators who deliver on a regular schedule, for example two videos per month each. The group lets creators network with like-minded creators, which makes the partnership more valuable to them. [L0576.024]
  • JotForm can serve as a content calendar and creator management system. Creators submit content through a form that is open only on set days, such as Tuesdays and Fridays, so submissions stay organised. [L1457.014]
  • Run a content audit before outsourced content goes live, so that off-brand posts, weak messages or compliance risks are caught. Start new hires by having them produce test TikToks, review them, then give them posting rights. [L1077.032]
