12.13 Social ad copy by platform

Facebook and Instagram primary text: principles

  • Primary text is the most critical element of a Facebook ad because users see it prominently and it must look organic. Most advertisers fail by writing it in marketing language, which makes the ad read as corporate promotion. Getting it right matters more than perfecting headlines or descriptions. [L0205.012]
  • Write it as a first-person narrative in three parts: the problem you experienced, trying the product, and the positive result. Conversational phrases such as "I've been noticing," "switched to," "honestly," and "seriously loving" make it sound like a real person sharing an experience. [L0205.011]
  • It must appear organic and natural, like a personal recommendation or story, not marketing copy. [L0205.004]
  • Include the closing phrase word for word: "click below to get yours here." [L0205.004]
  • The first one to two lines carry most of the attention, so open with a bold claim, a benefit-led headline, or a question to the viewer that names their pain point. [L0647.024]
  • On mobile News Feed, only the first three lines show before the "see more" link (older advice, 2021-22). Put the key message and the reason to click in the opening lines. [L1342.033]
  • Keep paid social copy short, direct and bullet-pointed, with a clear call to action and an offer. Very long text reduces readability on feeds such as Facebook. [L0515.023]
  • Short, simple copy still performs well for proven products, so competitor ads with minimal text are a valid model. Judge performance by ad activity rather than copy length. [L1172.041]
  • Long and short copy should both be tested, because on many niches and products longer copy often outperforms very short copy (older advice, 2021-22). [L1238.010]
  • The opening line of a long Facebook ad must be interesting enough to make people click to read more, because text beyond the visible start is truncated. [L1238.010]
  • Ad copy for a proven video should use a formula: open with a surprised first-person line that names the buyer's hope, introduce the product by name as the answer, promise one result without fuss, then remove the main objection about feel or texture. The surprise stops the reader, the named product makes the offer specific, and the objection removal answers doubt before it forms. [L0707.032]
    • Example one: "Thought I would never find a moisturizer that sinks in fast. Meet [PRODUCT], my go-to for softer skin without the greasy feel." [L0707.032]
    • Example two: "Tired of serums that pill under makeup? Meet [PRODUCT], my secret to a smooth base without the heavy layer." [L0707.032]
  • Pattern for a home decor ad: an emotional benefit statement plus a call to the customer to build a space. It sells the feeling of the product rather than only its features, which fits the ad's job of attracting clicks. [L0642.029]
    • Example one: "Turn any room into a place you love with pieces that feel personal." [L0642.029]
    • Example two: "Add warmth to your space with decor picked for everyday comfort." [L0642.029]

Facebook copy generation procedure

  1. Identify your product name and the main customer problem it solves (for example: acne problem, focus problem, skin dryness). [L0205.010]
  2. Open ChatGPT and use the Primary Text prompt, filling in [INSERT PRODUCT HERE] and [NAME MAIN PROBLEM CUSTOMERS HAVE]. [L0205.010]
  3. Generate 2 primary texts from ChatGPT. [L0205.010]
  4. Copy one or both primary texts and paste into your Facebook ad's primary text field. [L0205.010]
  5. Add your website URL to the primary text field in the ad. [L0205.010]
  6. Create two headlines: use the first headline for your offer (for example: "Buy 1 Get 1 Free Ends Midnight"), and generate the second headline using the Headline prompt in ChatGPT. [L0205.010, L0205.005]
  7. Use the Description prompt in ChatGPT with your product name and customer problem. [L0205.010]
  8. Copy one description from ChatGPT into the Facebook description field, ensuring it is shorter than the headline. [L0205.010, L0205.006]
  9. Review all three elements before launching the ad. [L0205.010]

  • Example for an organic hyaluronic acid serum powder addressing skin dryness:
    • Primary post: "I've been noticing my skin getting drier and duller lately, especially with the seasons changing. Switched to this organic hyaluronic acid serum powder, and my skin's honestly never felt more hydrated and smooth. Seriously loving the natural glow it's giving me! Click below to get yours here." [L0205.009]
    • Headline: "Finally found something organic that actually keeps my skin hydrated all day. No more dry patches or dullness! Click below to get yours here." [L0205.009]
    • Support text: "My skin used to feel so dry and tight, especially in colder weather. Ever since I started using this organic hyaluronic acid serum powder, it's been noticeably softer, hydrated, and glowing naturally. Seriously, mixing it fresh each morning has been a game-changer." [L0205.009]

Facebook headlines

  • Keep headlines short enough to read fully on a phone. Place an emoji only at the end, and only when it matches the event and brand tone, because an unrelated emoji adds noise instead of attention. [L0547.012]
  • Create two Facebook headlines with different purposes: one contains your specific offer (such as "Buy 1 Get 1 Free Ends Midnight"), and the other is generated with the headline prompt in ChatGPT. [L0205.005]
  • Check each ChatGPT-generated headline for the phrase "click below to get yours here." If it appears, remove it. This closing belongs only in the primary text; headlines must stand independently as compelling statements or offers. [L0205.013]
  • The headline is the most visible text, so include either a trust factor (a number of happy customers, or a star rating such as 4.8 out of 5 from reviews) or an offer such as free shipping or buy one get one free. [L0647.029]
  • Headline formula: [trust element or offer] + [short supporting detail]. Shoppers look for proof that the brand is trustworthy or for a concrete deal, so one of these must be visible immediately. [L0647.030]
    • Own examples: "Over 2,500 five-star reviews" and "Free returns on every order." [L0647.030]
  • Headlines in a Facebook ad are shown only on Facebook; the headline does not show on Instagram. [L0865.045]
  • Social proof for the description field, in the format "[Rating] out of 5 by [number] happy customers":
    • "4 out of 5 by 15,000 happy customers" [L0204.021]
    • "[Decimal rating] out of 5 by [number] happy customers," for example "4.8 out of 5 by 15,000+" [L0204.021]
    • "[Number] happy customers worldwide," with no star rating [L0204.021]

Facebook descriptions

  • The description is shorter than the headline because the platform display requires brevity. [L0205.006]
  • The description sits under the headline in small type and should carry an offer or a scarcity signal, such as "Limited time only" or "Get yours today." [L0647.031]
  • Description formula: [urgency or scarcity phrase] + [action to take now]. A short urgency line gives the viewer a reason to click immediately, so keep it to a few words. [L0647.032]
    • Own examples: "Only 48 hours left at this price" and "Order before this batch sells out." [L0647.032]
  • Keep the description very short, with one benefit, feature or trust line such as free shipping or 24/7 support, because longer text is cut off. [L0648.025]
  • Conflict on whether the description is shown: Source E states the description is never shown on Facebook or Instagram and does not need to be filled in. [L0865.045] The Source C source treats it as a visible line that carries the offer or social proof. [L0647.031, L0204.021] Check the Advanced preview for each placement before deciding whether to fill it. [L0865.050]

Facebook ad copy structure

Use this structure in order.

  1. Bold headline: a pain point, a controversial statement, a question to the customer, or a big claim such as a rating or ranking. The product name can also work as headline. [L0648.025]
  2. Product introduction line. [L0648.025]
  3. Benefits: two benefits and one feature. [L0648.025]
  4. Trust factor: a customer count or the accepted payment methods. [L0648.025]
  5. Star rating: four star icons followed by a rating such as 4.8 out of 5 from 1000+ customers. [L0648.025]
  6. Offer plus scarcity: a discount with a limit, such as 50% off for the next 50 orders. [L0648.025]
  7. Call to action: Shop now, with the link. [L0648.025]

Trust labels and call to action

  • Trust labels: in the ad, open Edit info and click Create new label to add lines such as free shipping, free returns, PayPal accepted, Klarna, Google Pay or Apple Pay. These labels appear under the ad before the customer clicks. [L0648.026]
  • Call to action button: Shop now. [L0647.033]
  • Destination: the product page URL, not the home page, because the product page holds the product information the viewer needs after clicking. [L0647.033]
  • Use Shop Now as the call-to-action button for standard sales ads. [L0865.046]
  • Shop for product retargeting is the CTA for product retargeting, though it does not appear in every placement; use it where it shows. [L1342.035, older advice 2021-22]
  • Add a strong offer such as 40% off near the start of a video to grab attention. [L1077.018]
  • Ad copy for a proven video's link card: a bestseller label in the headline, a Shop now button, and a time-limited discount line such as "50 percent off today." These three elements together push the click at the end of the ad. Use a real sales rank or real offer. [L0707.031]

Ad setup in Meta Ads Manager

  • Creative setup: under Set up creative, add the headline in the Headline field (the Add headline option is available for more variations), and add the Primary text in its own field. The ad preview shows how the ad will look in Feeds, Stories and Reels. [L0614.014]
  • Media picker: under Ad creative, the source tabs are Media from you and Media from AI, and the account's Business, Instagram and Page media appear under the Account and Page tabs. Only one image or video can be selected per ad, and a search field filters the media library. [L0614.023]
  • Text section: Primary text (a line explaining what the ad is about), Headline, Description (extra detail, optional) and a Call to action button. [L0614.024]
  • Several headline options let Meta show the version it predicts will perform best. [L0614.024]
  • Creative testing can compare up to 5 creative versions, which is not needed for a warm-up. [L0614.024]
  • Text & Links fields: Primary text (main copy), Headline (short, optional), and Description (additional details, optional). The Destination is Website, and the Website URL must be the landing page. The display link is the URL shown on the ad and must match the website URL. [L1342.034, older advice 2021-22]
  • Settings > Ad > Languages: an ad can be created in several languages, and Facebook shows each viewer the version that suits them best. Add a language version when the store serves more than one language. [L1342.037, older advice 2021-22]
  • Multiple text options: the primary text and headline fields can hold several text options, and Meta shows the option it predicts will perform best. [L0865.067]
  • If full control over the copy is wanted, add only one primary text and skip Meta's generated variations. [L0865.067]
  • Facebook's AI-suggested copy variations are optional and worth using only once they look good on their own. [L0647.028]
  • Ad preview: the preview on the right shows the ad as it will look on Facebook, including the catalog below it when enabled. Advanced preview shows every placement the ad may run on and should be checked before publishing. [L0865.050]

Placements, crop, video length and previews

  • Crop: in Ad > Creative > Crop, choose 1:1 or 9:16 for the placements that need it. If Original is kept, the image may be cropped automatically, but this is unreliable. To control the crop, upload a separate 9:16 version with Replace in the vertical slot. Horizontal formats are rarely shown and can be ignored. [L0865.044]
  • Primary text position: on Facebook the primary text appears above the ad and on Instagram it appears below the ad. [L0865.045]
  • Carousel: in a Meta carousel ad, the primary text appears above the product cards on Facebook and below them on Instagram. One primary text entry therefore serves both placements, and the preview shows how it appears on each. [L1029.033]
  • Carousel closing card: the destination URL entered in the final ad setup step is also used for an extra closing card at the end of the carousel. A click on that card takes the viewer to the store. [L1029.034]
  • Ad copy length on Meta: keep ad copy to three lines because Meta shows only three. Use emojis, a clear offer and a direct link. [L0649.033]
  • Video length cap: some placements, such as Instagram, cap video length at 15 seconds. A 30-second video still publishes but simply will not show in that placement, so make shorter cuts and both vertical and landscape versions. [L0649.037]
  • Story aspect ratio warning: if Facebook or TikTok Ads Manager shows a story aspect ratio warning under automatic placements, it can be ignored when the video follows the format from the tutorial. [L0363.050]
  • Copy space by platform: Google Search ads have limited character space and need short, concise copy. Facebook ads provide substantially more space, allowing longer paragraphs, detailed benefit explanations, and customer review quotes. Tailor copy length, depth of explanation, and proof elements to each platform's constraints. [L0241.013, older advice 2021-22]

Facebook ad testing structure

  • Inside one ad set, create several ads with different images and text so the creatives can be split tested against each other. Each ad holds its own image, headline and link text, while the ad set keeps the audience and budget. [L0641.022]
  • During testing, use one ad copy version per ad. [L0647.028]
  • Product test structure (Source D): read the 5 headlines and 5 primary texts generated by the prompt, and do not use all five. Pick the two headlines and two primary texts that feel strongest. [L1193.021]
  • Product test entry (Source D): enter the two headlines and two primary texts into the ad, add one description, paste the landing page URL as the website URL, and select Shop Now as the CTA. [L1193.021]
  • Only one description is needed per ad in a product test. Add it alongside the two headlines and two primary texts. [L1193.023]
  • Conflict on copy per ad: the Source C rule of one copy version per ad [L0647.028] applies when each copy version must be read on its own. The Source D product test [L1193.021, L1193.023] puts two headlines and two primary texts into one ad. Choose the structure that matches the test's goal. Note that a single primary text gives full control and skips Meta's selection. [L0865.067]
  • Image ads and carousel ads can perform as well as video ads on Facebook, so test them as a separate format next to the winning video creatives. Image ads with a short text overlay and an emoji were reported to work well for a brand. [L0774.056]
  • When a proven video ad is the source, keep the proven video and add a call to action, urgency text and other conversion elements; change only what is needed for conversion. [L1429.050]

Facebook audience-matched copy

  • Facebook reads the text and the context of the video or image, including detected objects in the visual, to decide who sees the ad. Creatives should be relevant to the target audience, and a narrow interest may need messages that clearly match it. [L0995.029]
  • Creatives should speak to the target audience intimately by naming who they are or the specific problem they face. Facebook uses the creative's visual context and text to match the ad to the right audience. [L0999.035]
  • Pattern for niche creative and copy: name the specific audience and its specific problem or situation in the ad, and show that situation in the video or image. A broad message with a broad audience performs worse than one relevant to a narrow group. [L0995.028]
    • Example for a self-defense product: "Heading out on a late-night walk? Keep a personal alarm in your pocket." [L0995.028]
    • Example for a pet hair brush: "Shedding dog on your bed every morning? This brush pulls out the thick German Shepherd undercoat." [L0995.028]
  • Interest targeting and broad targeting: when budgets are small, test a specific interest (e.g. camping, snowboarding, pets, home and garden), reduce the audience size, pick an interest, and make sure the ad clearly matches it. In the case described, broad targeting was not working and interest-specific targeting performed better. [L0995.030]

Retargeting copy

  • Retargeting Facebook copy works when the primary text reminds the visitor of the exact product they viewed, adds one concrete reason to return now, and the headline names the next action. [L1342.036, older advice 2021-22]
    • Example 1, primary text: "Still thinking about the [product name] you looked at? It is in stock and ships in [X] days." Headline: "Get Yours Before It Sells Out." [L1342.036]
    • Example 2, primary text: "Your [product name] is still waiting. Order today and get [offer] on your first purchase." Headline: "Complete Your Order." [L1342.036]
  • Retargeting copy prompt for Meta and Google Display (use the email text as the foundation):
Great, thanks. I'm also launching retargeting ads on Meta and the Google Display Network. Use the email text above as the foundation for this ad copy. Meta needs main body text, a headline, and a call-to-action button labeled Shop Now. For Google Display, write one headline and one description per ad, with each ad matched to the email it follows, covering the opening two days, the middle days, and the final days of the promotion.

[L1287.050]

  • Google remarketing ads allow only short copy, so the text must be brief there. [L1238.010, older advice 2021-22]

TikTok ad copy

  • Creatives must show what is being sold. On TikTok, users need a clear hook and an explanation of the offer. [L0577.059]
  • Good TikTok creatives explain product features through sound and visual effects, with no on-screen text, no talking presenter, and no sales argument. This satisfying, fun style makes viewers feel entertained rather than sold to. [L1374.004]
  • Native pattern: a newspaper-style analogy shows that traditional bold ad formatting gets skipped, while an advertorial that looks like useful content holds attention until the viewer realizes it is an ad. Make the ad match the native look and feel of the feed and provide value first. [L1374.003]
    • Example for a kitchen gadget: a 10-second clip of a slicing-hack sequence with sound and no text, showing the product working. [L1374.003]
    • Example for a pet accessory: a quick "what your dog does when you leave" scene that reveals the product as the answer at the end. [L1374.003]
  • Ad copy pattern: a four-to-five-word phrase taken from how a customer would describe the main benefit, phrased in the customer's own words. It matches the native, conversational tone and names the concrete result the buyer wants. [L1376.020]
    • Examples: "My back finally stopped aching" for a posture corrector; "Sleep feels different now" for a sleep mask. [L1376.020]
  • Offer-led text: a clear discount plus an urgency phrase, such as a percent-off message and a limited-time notice. [L1377.036]
    • Example patterns: "Save 40% today only" or "Free shipping ends tonight." [L1377.036]
  • Short video ad text: name the product, add a short offer with its price, and keep the call to action simple. The price sits in the text so viewers understand the offer in one line. [L1378.021]
    • Examples: "Portable blender for only 39.99" and "Foot massager for only 49.99." [L1378.021]
  • Ad copy formula: an offer line that states the discount and a call to action, such as a percent-off figure followed by "get yours today" or "ending soon." The discount and urgency are the first things a TikTok viewer reads, and the line fits the ad text limit. [L1382.027]
    • Examples: "40 percent off, shop today before the sale ends" and "50 percent off this week, get yours now." [L1382.027]
  • Tone: use a fun, relatable tone in ad text and reactions, such as a funny quote or a customer reaction after receiving the product, instead of only a discount message. Offer messages still work well and are common. [L1377.038]
  • Thumbnail: a human face smiling in the ad image or thumbnail has worked better than a product image or store graphic in tests. The face need not be tied to the product, and the brand name and a logo should stay visible. [L1377.037]
  • Reuse across platforms: a TikTok ad creative that works on TikTok can usually be reused on Facebook and Instagram placements, but a Facebook ad generally does not perform on TikTok. Use TikTok-native creatives as the source when expanding to other platforms. [L1374.005]

TikTok duplicate content and freshness

  • TikTok reduces the reach of duplicate content, and ads are treated as ordinary videos on the platform. Creatives that were already widely posted or running for a long time lose reach, so new or freshly uploaded videos are preferred. [L1382.021]

TikTok Spark Ads

  • Spark ads on an organic video can lower that video's organic reach, so use videos that are roughly 20 to 30 days old or older. Recent viral organic posts are better left out of paid tests. [L1377.026]
  • Spark Ads performed better than regular TikTok ads in the tests, so test both formats on the same product. Spark Ads are the preferred option when running ads from an organic account. [L1381.030]
  • Spark Ads kept the comment filters set on the account, while comments on traditional TikTok ads are harder to monitor. Filter negative comments on the account before running ads. [L1381.029]
  • After a Spark Ad was rejected by TikTok, organic views on the linked account dropped below 600 and stayed low. Rejected ads may coincide with a drop in organic reach, so review the rejection before testing more ads from the same account. [L1381.014]

Instagram Reels and Trial Reels

  • For Instagram Reels repurposed from TikTok, add a call to action such as Shop Now, and make the text more ad-like near the end of the video. The owner reported no account penalty on Instagram, while TikTok was more sensitive to reports. [L1077.018]
  • Trial Reels works as a low-risk testing ground for content styles and can be removed from the profile grid afterwards, so a polished brand page keeps its look. Creators also use it to test many hooks before pushing an ad on Facebook. [L1397.006]
  • When Instagram is a main source of income, the ad can link to the Instagram page instead of the store, since the goal is for customers to check out the brand page first. This is an exception to the usual approach of linking directly to the store. [L1439.024]

Pinterest audience and targeting

  • Pinterest audience size: 400+ million users. [L0296.016]
  • 80% of Pinterest users have previously purchased items on the platform. [L0296.017]
  • Pinterest's primary audience is women age 30 and older; this demographic is the majority and the best fit for products targeting this group. [L0296.018]
  • Pinterest requires 7-15 days to optimize; expect very high CPMs and poor results in the first 2-3 days as the platform tests. [L0296.019]
  • Not all products work on Pinterest; the platform works best for niches related to DIY, crafts, and inspiration content. [L0296.020]
  • Audience size: use stacked interests, selecting seven to ten (or five to twenty) specific sub-interests related to the product and target audience, not only broad parent categories. Keep audience size between 5 and 10 million as the sweet spot; up to 30 million is acceptable, and above 30 million is too big. [L1134.021]
  • Select individual sub-interests rather than only the parent interest, because Pinterest reports which interest drove sales only for individually selected interests; the breakdown view then shows which interest is performing. [L1134.023]
  • Demographics: if only women fit the product, select female only; an age range of 21+ is good, and 25+ was also reported to work well. Location can be all US or a specific country; language and device restrictions are not needed. [L1134.022]
  • Optimization and delivery: select Checkout as the conversion event and automatic bidding. [L1134.022]
  • Keep keywords and interests in separate campaigns. Start with interest-based targeting, and test keyword targeting only after the product has been confirmed as a winner. [L1134.024]
  • Keywords: Pinterest changed its search results so that keywords appear as separate, segmented items, which makes the platform more advertiser-friendly. Promoted pin targeting is built on these keyword segments, so choose keywords that match the exact terms users search for. [L1136.039]

Pinterest creative

  • Pinterest creatives should be TikTok-style vertical video in 9:16 format, 15 to 20 seconds long (20 seconds is seen as best), with quick transitions within one to two seconds. Show the product in use within the first three to five seconds; in the speaker's tests such clips reached over 3 percent CTR. [L1134.026]
  • Video outperforms static images on Pinterest in the speaker's tests across beauty, health, home decor and home improvement; fashion may be an exception where images can work. [L1134.027]
  • Creative can be reused for six or seven months because there is little saturation on Pinterest. [L1134.027]
  • TikTok-style videos produced the best CTR in the lesson's tests, and simple image pins and standard pins performed worse. [L1135.041]
  • Pinterest users recognize product images immediately, so the product must be visible. [L0577.059]

Pinterest ad setup and copy

  • Creating a Pinterest ad: click Select pins or Create pin (upload a video from the computer). Use the same headline as on TikTok ads as the title, and add a short description of three to four lines that names the pain point and the benefit and ends with a call to action, such as "flawless skin is just a click away." The description can be generated with ChatGPT. [L1134.028]
  • Creating a video pin at the ad level:
    1. Click Select pins. [L1135.043]
    2. If you have no existing pins, click Create pin. [L1135.043]
    3. Upload the video from your computer. [L1135.043]
    4. Enter the ad headline in the title field (the same one used for TikTok ads). [L1135.043]
    5. Enter the ad text in the description field. [L1135.043]
  • Pattern for Pinterest ad description copy: write three to four lines that name a pain point, state the product benefit, end with a call to action, and optionally finish with a short benefit-driven line. [L1135.044]
    • Example one: "Tired of frizzy hair after every wash? This comb smooths in one pass, so tap now for salon-smooth results at home." [L1135.044]
    • Example two: "Sore feet at the end of the day? These insoles add cushioning under every step, so shop today and walk easier tomorrow." [L1135.044]

YouTube ads

  • YouTube ads need studio-quality creatives with a hook that grabs attention within the first six seconds. Videos that work on TikTok or Facebook often do not perform the same way on YouTube. [L0788.037]
  • Production polish is not a prerequisite. YouTube video ads can be produced cheaply, even with a phone and a plain setup where the presenter stands in front of the product and talks. [L1510.001]
  • Authenticity can outperform high production values. A phone-shot video of a person talking in front of a product, with a near-zero production budget, can become one of the most-watched YouTube ads of its kind. [L1510.002]
  • Simple YouTube ad formula: a product video with product promotion aimed at a retargeting list that has not purchased, plus a top-of-funnel video aimed at competitor fans. [L1510.009]

Demand Gen ads

  • Informational images should be paired with a headline that matches the image, since Demand Gen combines headlines with images. The image's job is to create curiosity, not to give the answer. [L0882.024]

Native ad networks

  • Native ad networks such as Taboola place advertorial-style ads on large news sites. This is a separate channel from Meta ads. [L0691.003]
