12.22 Creative testing structure

Why creative testing comes first

  • Creative testing is one of the largest parts of scaling paid ads, whether it is done in-house or outsourced. Make many creatives and test them continually, because only a small share will work. [L0999.034]
  • A weak creative can make a product look like a failure even when the product works, so test new creatives before deciding that a product is not viable. [L1038.016]
  • Structured testing frameworks during the ads phase keep decisions based on results rather than opinion. [L0914.011]
  • Testing many creatives early matters more than the size of the budget. For example, test about double the number of creatives on the first day, then add budget only to the creative that looks promising. [L1379.032]
  • Creatives are usually tested for the first time around week three. A creative that fails is information about what to change, not a final verdict on the product. [L1445.004]
  • Smaller budgets do not give enough data to judge an ad. Decide quickly on what performs early, since waiting for a month of data at 50 per day is not practical for most sellers. [L0950.039]
  • Creative testing often requires enough spend for the algorithm to learn. At very low daily budgets such as 5 per day, controlling spend manually gives faster, clearer results. [L1426.041]
  • Simple homemade or cropped creatives can outperform professionally shot ones, so appearance is not a reliable predictor of performance. Decide which creative stays by the data and the sales it brings. [L0562.013]
  • Unconventional or aesthetically imperfect designs can outperform polished creative. Test unusual aesthetics even when they do not match typical advertising polish. [L0316.044]
  • Test across different concepts such as humour, education, before-and-after and shock, and let audience results decide. Launching quickly and iterating on feedback costs less than planning one polished ad and launching it late. [L1077.016]
  • Test creatives aimed at different audience types, because it is not possible to know in advance which audience will respond. Find the creative that works, then scale it. [L1016.017]
  • Combine educated guesses about why an ad should work with variation tests of different angles, because psychology alone cannot predict results. Buyer context often only becomes clear once an ad is live, so launching and testing usually beats waiting for a perfect creative. [L1348.027]
  • Test formats on your own product before committing budget to one type. In one fashion store, image ads generated most of the revenue while video ads did not work. [L1127.028]
  • A product that splits its audience can be tested with creative that deliberately provokes a clear reaction. The mix of positive and negative responses shows which buyers lean toward the product. [L1315.053]

When to start testing

  • Start creative testing when click-through rate (CTR) or cost per click (CPC) is poor, since weak CTR signals a need for new creatives immediately. [L0902.003]
  • Also start once a product is a consistent seller and has been profitable for more than two to three weeks. [L0902.003]
  • Start creative testing only after two weeks of consistent profitable results, with two or three winning audiences. Early in a product test, focus on finding winning audiences first. [L1177.033]
  • Before launching a test, have a finished landing page, 4 to 5 creatives (images, videos or carousels) and ready ad copy. [L0648.003]
  • Move to hook testing only after you get consistent profitable sales with ripped creatives. At that point you have a validated angle, avatar and product. [L1252.016]
  • Hook testing is the step after ripped creatives prove profitable, and its aim is to raise CTR. Starting it earlier mixes the creative variable with the product test. [L1252.017]
  • Start creative testing when a product is already a consistent seller, and use the audience stage first to find where it sells. [L0902.003, L1177.033]

Test structure: audiences first, then creatives

  • Stage 1, audience testing for a new product, works as follows. [L0398.025]
    1. Produce three to four videos for the product. [L0398.025]
    2. Run them in an audience testing campaign across several audiences, such as interests, countries and age groups. [L0398.025]
    3. Goal: find two to three winning audiences whose performance shows the product sells and the message reaches the right people. [L0398.025]
    4. Once two to three winners are found, move to the creative testing campaign described below. [L0398.025]
  • Creative testing campaign setup, using ABO with the Sales objective. [L1335.023]
    1. Create an ABO campaign (Sales objective) for creative testing. [L1335.023]
    2. Add three ad sets, each targeting a proven winning audience (for example one winning interest, one lookalike winner, one further consistent winner). [L1335.023]
    3. Place the same three video variations in each ad set; the variations differ only in the first 3 to 5 seconds (the hook) while the rest of the video is identical. [L1335.023]
    4. Run the test for 2 to 3 days to collect enough data. [L1335.023]
    5. Choose the winner by ROAS first; if ROAS is equal, use CTR; if CTR is also equal, use average play time. [L1335.023]
    6. Use the winning creative in audience testing campaigns, CBO scaling campaigns, and wherever new audiences are tested, and reuse it for audiences that are dying down. [L1335.023]
  • Separate campaigns: run an interest testing campaign (winning creatives, new interests) and a creative testing campaign (winning interest, new creatives), so a new creative gets its own budget. [L0640.026]
  • Creative testing in a separate campaign, with a new CBO or ABO campaign. [L1107.022]
    1. Pick two or three interests that currently perform well. [L1107.022]
    2. Place them in a new CBO or ABO test campaign with two ad sets. [L1107.022]
    3. Test three new creatives in each ad set. [L1107.022]
  • Duplicate the winning ad set so that its audience and placements stay identical, then test the new creatives in the duplicate. [L1335.054]
    1. Duplicate the winning ad set so its audience and placements stay identical. [L1335.054]
    2. Put the duplicate into a new creative testing campaign. [L1335.054]
    3. Change only the launch date and replace the videos with the new creatives. [L1335.054]
    4. Keep the targeting unchanged, so any performance difference comes from the creative and not the audience. [L1335.054]
    5. Run the test and compare the hook or creative variants inside the same audience. [L1335.054]
  • Test new creatives on a winning audience. [L0774.035]
    1. Take the winning ad sets with the best audience, age, gender and interest. [L0774.035]
    2. Create a separate campaign with the new creatives and compare CTR. [L0774.035]
    3. Move the best-performing creatives into the running ad sets. [L0774.035]
    4. Turn off ads that spend 20 to 30 USD without sales and keep the ones that produce sales. [L0774.035]
  • Keep new creatives out of ad sets that contain the old winner. Facebook and TikTok send most budget to the creative with the most data, which hides how well new creatives would perform. [L1177.031]
  • Facebook creative testing structure. Keep the winning audience and copy, change only one variable at a time (hook, creative or creator), and put each new creative in its own ad set, or put several new creatives together in one ad set to compete. Do not mix them with the old winner. [L1177.032]
  • TikTok launch structure. One winning ad set and winning creative can be duplicated into a new campaign, while new creatives are tested in a separate ad set to avoid budget going to the old winner. [L1177.034]
  • Standard Pinterest test structure: one campaign, one ad group with stacked interests, and three creatives (ads). Let it run for at least five to seven days, and let the platform optimise. [L1134.025]
  • Structure each test as one campaign, one ad group and three creatives, because one creative may be rejected and two others still give a valid test. [L1135.040]
  • Creative testing setup based on a winning interest. [L1212.012]
    1. Find the winning interest or audience first. [L1212.012]
    2. Duplicate that ad set (for example three times) with the same interest. [L1212.012]
    3. Keep only one ad (creative) in each ad set. [L1212.012]
    4. Compare which creative has the highest CTR. [L1212.012]
    5. Only increase the budget once a winning audience is found, then scale the winning campaign by raising the budget. [L1212.012]
  • Keep each angle in its own column, and do not mix problem types inside one column. Testing angles separately shows which problem drives clicks and purchases, and empty columns are kept for future angles. [L0392.009]
  • Full setup for a Meta CBO test ad. [L0348.007]
    1. In the ad set, select Advantage+ creative / ad format Single image or video and leave Multi-advertiser ads unchecked. [L0348.007]
    2. Upload 6 to 10 creatives (a first round of about 9 creatives across ad sets is the target). [L0348.007]
    3. Choose the Facebook Page and the Instagram account. [L0348.007]
    4. Confirm the website URL points to the correct product page for that product. [L0348.007]
    5. Press Next, then use the regenerate option on the primary text and headline generated by Meta to draft copy. [L0348.007]
    6. Paste the copy from your external prompt into the ad primary text and headlines, and check the spacing and line breaks. [L0348.007]
    7. Press Publish. [L0348.007]
  • Ads per ad set: keep the number small (about three to four creatives) so that spend per creative is controlled and each result can be read. Facebook is good at choosing the best creative for a specific audience, so several options give more chances to work. [L0646.039]
  • Reuse the same set of three to four ads across ad sets with the same goal. Meta then tests them and spends on the best one, so a single ad is never enough. [L0649.032]
  • Full setup needs about 13 ads in total across the campaigns. Lead generation and promotions need 3 to 4 each, the goodwill campaign needs one video, and each sandbox test needs two ads. [L0649.038]
  • For a clean creative test, place each of four creatives in its own ad set with about 5 to 15 dollars of spend per ad. This costs more but shows which creative wins on CTR and CPC. In one ad set with four ads, one creative can take most of the budget, so the result is harder to read. [L0646.062]
  • Do not blindly trust Facebook's spend allocation. If a creative receives little spend, duplicate the ad set into its own ad set and force spend onto it for a test period (for example three days) before deciding it is weak. [L0640.027]
  • Standard test per product uses about three creatives: one or two videos and one or two pictures. [L0349.010]
  • Test one interest set at a time... see section on budgets for per-ad-set amounts. [L1177.059]
  • Test each new creative with about 10 to 20 dollars of spend in its own single-creative ad set to read its performance on its own. Combine creatives in a shared ad set only after each one has shown its results separately. [L1177.059]

Isolating variables: one change per test

  • Test one variable at a time. Start with the hook, because it is the most important variable at the start. Thumbnails, overall style and ad copy come later. [L0398.027]
  • Keep each level above the one being tested fixed, so that only one variable changes at a time. [L0902.027]
  • Creative testing works as a chain: an ad angle becomes a concept (the storyline), the concept becomes one script, the script gets three hook variations, and those hook variations run as ads. [L0902.027]
  • Static ads mean image ads and carousel ads. A concept can be built as either a video or a static ad, because the concept is the angle or script the test is based on, not the format. [L0902.028]
  • Keep a control ad with unchanged interest, copy and hook, and change only one element (such as the mid-section), so any lift is attributable to that change. [L0640.025]
  • During creative testing, keep the offer and ad copy identical across all ads and change only the video, clip or creator. This makes the creative the only variable. [L1382.074]
  • Only change one variable at a time: in creative tests keep the targeting identical to the proven winning audience, so the performance difference comes from the creative and not the audience. [L1335.024]
  • Test one variable at a time: keep audience, creative, copy and targets identical and change only the offer, so a conversion uplift can be attributed to the offer. [L1315.026]

Hook, copy, thumbnail and angle testing

  • Each new video should come with about three hook variations, where only the first five seconds differ. Meta then optimises the combinations. [L0398.028]
  • Hooks should be tested by making one video with three different first 3 to 5 seconds, keeping the rest identical. Ideas can be taken from what is trending on TikTok. For image ads, make three images around the same marketing angle. [L1335.027]
  • Ad copy angle testing: keep the winning video and hook identical and change only the ad copy, so each version speaks to a different motivation within the same winning audiences. [L0398.060]
    • Formula: one copy per audience motivation, with the video held constant. [L0398.060]
    • Example 1: for a camping product, one copy focuses on night-time visibility and another on gadget features. [L0398.060]
    • Example 2: for a fitness product, one copy focuses on saving time and another on feeling confident during workouts. [L0398.060]
  • Ad copy matters more once several audiences need different angles. Test ad copy only after spending more than about 1,000 per day on testing, and keep the video and hook identical while you test it. [L0398.033]
  • Thumbnails and ad copy are tested separately from the video. [L0902.025]
    1. Keep the same winning video and vary only the thumbnail (thumbnail 1, 2, 3) to find the thumbnail winner, since thumbnails are the first thing customers see and can raise CTR. [L0902.025]
    2. Keep the same video and thumbnail while varying the ad copy (ad copy 1, 2, 3) to find the ad copy winner. [L0902.025]
  • Testing a new angle on a video that did not get the expected views. [L0795.016]
    1. Pick a good video idea that did not perform. [L0795.016]
    2. Add a hook that has already worked for you in the past. [L0795.016]
    3. Publish the new version and compare its results with the previous one. [L0795.016]
  • Find the strongest marketing angle without a large budget. [L1218.033]
    1. Pick one marketing angle. [L1218.033]
    2. Make a creative for that angle alone, or use only its first 5 to 10 seconds as the hook, and add UGC-style clips. [L1218.033]
    3. Test each angle's creative separately on Facebook. [L1218.033]
    4. Once one angle clearly wins, add a landing page section for it and move the other sections down. [L1218.033]
  • Creative testing by splitting a winning ad. [L1315.045]
    1. Split a winning ad into separate features or benefits, one per creative. [L1315.045]
    2. Run them for a few days. [L1315.045]
    3. Pick the best-performing problem or angle. [L1315.045]
    4. Create new creatives around that problem. [L1315.045]
    5. Keep iterating on existing ads and test new concepts continually. [L1315.045]
  • Creative testing as a test of four creative types against interest audiences. [L0642.030]
  • A winning angle is tested in video before it is scaled. Launch VSL (video sales letter) ads first and static image ads afterwards, because VSLs convert better and test the full message. The winning angle then gets iterated into static image ads. [L1019.038]
  • Replace a Google text ad's single element with one change at a time. Iterative text ad testing isolates single variable changes to identify which specific word or phrase your audience prefers. Only one element varies while all other copy remains identical; performance shifts directly show what caused them. Examples: Join the Community versus Become a Member (different verb, same offer), or Learn More versus Discover More (same action, different tone). [L0085.006]
  • Evolutionary text ad testing uses fundamentally different messaging frames, such as leading with a customer pain point versus leading with the solution benefit, to discover which overall approach converts best. Examples: Tired of Back Pain (pain-point focus) versus Professional Spinal Support (solution-forward), or Don't Get Left Behind (urgency) versus Trust the Industry Leader (authority and quality). [L0085.007]
  • Iterative testing changes only one element per test and keeps everything else identical, for example changing the call to action from Buy now to Shop now. It is worth doing, but it produces only small differences. [L1401.007]
  • Evolutionary testing compares two very different ad styles, such as a question (Struggling with acne?) against a direct statement (The acne solution). On Google text ads, evolutionary tests usually produce bigger gains than tweaking single words, so new concepts should be tested more often. [L1401.008]
  • Test sales copy on real members of the target market before launch. [L0340.077]
    1. Give the copy to 3 to 5 people who fit the buyer profile and do not know the product. [L0340.077]
    2. Ask each reader to say in their own words what the product does and what they should do next. [L0340.077]
    3. Mark every sentence where the reader hesitates, misreads, or asks a question, and rewrite those sentences with simpler words and one claim each. [L0340.077]
  • Free pretest for a sales letter, in order. [L1370.058]
    1. Read the letter aloud and fix any tongue twisters or awkward phrases. [L1370.058]
    2. Read it to several people who match the target customer, possibly in a casual setting, and watch whether they ask how to buy, which signals that the approach works. [L1370.058]
    3. Have a young reader read it aloud and change any words or phrases they struggle with. [L1370.058]
    4. Check whether typical customers have questions the letter leaves unanswered, and if so add a page that answers them. [L1370.058]
  • Testing a voiceover against text-only overlays before committing. A voiceover reaches people who cannot read the screen, and text reaches people who cannot hear, and many text-only ads perform well. [L1427.051]
  • Case: a text-only ad with rain overlays and heavy effects reached about 2.5 ROAS and was treated as a failure. A simple UGC-style ad with plain text overlays and a voiceover reached 4 to 6 ROAS. [L1427.052]
  • Subtitles: when a video from abroad is strong but not in the local language, add subtitles if the creative is good; otherwise create or hire a similar one. Image ads were the main format used for testing new products. [L1108.020]
  • Testing AI presenters for a proven product. [L1479.016]
    1. Take an ad angle and offer from a brand whose ads have run for a long time. [L1479.016]
    2. Create AI presenter versions of the same ad, each with a different avatar type (for example a doctor, an everyday woman, a man). [L1479.016]
    3. Keep the product, script and offer identical so that only the presenter changes. [L1479.016]
    4. Let the versions run and compare their results. [L1479.016]
    5. Scale the avatar that performs best and stop the rest. [L1479.016]
  • Test platform-specific hooks: write two or more strong hooks, titles or captions for the same video and publish each variant on a different platform. [L0784.017]
    1. Write two or more strong hooks, titles, or captions for the same video. [L0784.017]
    2. Publish each variant on a different platform, for example variant A on TikTok, variant B on Instagram, and a third variant on YouTube. [L0784.017]
    3. Compare the results per platform. [L0784.017]
    4. Adjust the messaging for each platform based on what performed best there. [L0784.017]

Offer testing

  • Keep the offer out of the ad creatives while testing, because the offer changes often. Once a winning offer is found, add it to the ads; every offer change then requires updating the ads as well. [L0502.025]
  • Test offers with simple static creatives, such as buy one get one free, a free gift or 40 percent off, to see which offer responds best across markets and products. [L0341.022]
  • Image creatives can be built fast from templates and tested before video creatives are made. [L0343.002]

Format choice: static before video

  • Video takes longer to produce, so use it once a static layout has shown it converts. [L0343.002]
  • When product footage is limited, run quick image ads first to see which market responds, and only then produce a full video for the winning market. If footage is good enough for a clear narrative, use it, but the hook and offer come first. [L0344.014]
  • Use simple image ads with product imagery in an AI tool, Photoshop, or a freelance graphic designer to test quickly before committing to video. [L0950.052]
  • Test formats on your own product before committing budget to one type. [L1127.028]
  • Swipe file creatives: the full testing workflow. [L0343.040]
    1. Choose a product and find a matching swipe file layout, such as Them vs Us, testimonial, benefit callout, meme or offer. [L0343.040]
    2. Replace the text with your branding, product benefits and offer. [L0343.040]
    3. Swap in your product cut-out and adjust colours and gradients. [L0343.040]
    4. Publish the variants as ads and compare which layout performs best for each product. [L0343.040]
    5. Scale the winning layout and build video versions only after the static test shows a clear winner. [L0343.040]
  • Concepts that do not show the product at all, such as a close-up of two faces contrasting a problem and a solution, can be tested when competitors use this style successfully in search. Test such images against product-visible versions rather than replacing them. [L0882.015]
  • Replacing the image of a working Shopping listing without a split test can lose performance that was already earned. Duplicate the listing first so the new image competes against the original. [L0882.032]
  • Study competitors' ads in the Meta Ad Library and note recurring hooks, ad copy and story structure. [L0639.024]
  • Reused TikTok-style video pins on Pinterest held a CTR of roughly 3 to 4 percent for about a month, which is well above the 1 percent day-three minimum used as a go or no-go gate. [L1135.061]
  • Simple video formats: a new Spark Ad test on a new account. [L1381.028]
    1. Choose the best organic video. [L1381.028]
    2. Use no interest targeting, only an age range such as 18 to 45 depending on the product. [L1381.028]
    3. Let about 20 dollars of spend run completely. [L1381.028]
    4. Do not reopen Ads Manager for the account afterward, since the account was seen to get an organic boost once the spend finished. [L1381.028]
  • Creative testing: find the strongest format. Testing many creatives early (for example an image test and a video test in one ad set) lets the platform tell you which format responds. [L1379.032]

Pre-launch checks

  • Before launching, have a finished landing page, 4 to 5 creatives, and ready ad copy. [L0648.003]
  • Check the spacing and line breaks of ad copy before publishing. [L0348.007]
  • Check that the website URL points to the correct product page for that product. [L0348.007]

Thresholds and stop rules

  • A clear winner is a video creative with at least 3 sales and a positive ROAS above break-even. One sale can be luck, so wait for more data before calling a creative a winner. [L0398.030]
  • To pick a winning creative, read hard metrics such as purchases once the ad has spent around its target cost per acquisition. If it has not yet spent that much, use softer signals such as attention and CTR to decide which ad deserves more budget. [L0646.059]
  • A reused creative with 3 to 4 percent CTR above a 1 percent day-three minimum passed the go or no-go gate. [L1135.061]
  • Turn off ads that spend 20 to 30 USD without sales and keep the ones that produce sales. [L0774.035]
  • Stop a product if metrics do not show promise after break-even spend. [L0640.028]
  • A hook and creative combination counts as a clear winner only when it performs best in every ad set of the test, not just one. A result that wins in one audience but loses in the others should be retested before it is scaled. [L1335.052]
  • Start creative testing when CTR or CPC is poor. [L0902.003]

Declaring winners

  • Choose the winner by ROAS first; if ROAS is equal, use CTR; if CTR is equal, use average play time. [L1335.023]
  • A winner must perform best in every ad set of the test. [L1335.052]
  • Decide on the winner with the hard metric once the spend passes the target cost per acquisition; before that, use CTR and attention. [L0646.059]
  • Once one angle clearly wins, add a landing page section for it and move the other sections down. [L1218.033]
  • Keep the three best performers after the test. [L1219.028]
  • Compare the highest CTR creative in each ad set. [L1212.012]
  • Scale the winning layout and video version only after the static test shows a clear winner. [L0343.040]
  • Decide which creative stays by the data and the sales it brings, not by how polished it looks. [L0562.013]
  • Let the platform decide which performs best, then turn off the weak ones. [L0562.012]
  • Decide quickly on what performs early; waiting a month at 50 per day is not practical for most sellers. [L0950.039]
  • Keep creative testing going: the winning creative is reused in audience testing campaigns, CBO scaling campaigns, and new audience tests. [L1335.023]

Batch naming and benchmarks

  • Testing creatives in batches works by naming ads by batch number, not by individual creative number. The best-performing creative becomes the benchmark, and each new variation is tested against it. Any variation that beats the benchmark moves into a scaling campaign. [L0819.006]
    • Example pattern: Batch 12, Creative B vs Batch 11 benchmark: move to scaling if it beats the benchmark's cost per purchase. [L0819.006]
    • Second example: Batch 7, skit hook vs Batch 6 benchmark: scale only if it beats benchmark ROAS for three days. [L0819.006]

Testing cadence and weekly planning

  • Testing creatives is a weekly job: plan 6 to 9 videos per week from 2 to 3 concepts, and test a minimum of three concepts per week. [L0298.048]
  • Creative testing frequency: 2 to 3 times per week at small scale, 10 to 15 times per week at higher scale. [L1335.026]
  • Angle tests are cycled: after a winning angle is found, keep testing new concepts continually while iterating on existing ads. [L1315.045]
  • A creative test that starts with a lot of spend will show a high CPM at first. [L0298.029]
  • For new creative tests, expect initially high CPM regardless of creative quality, because new ads lack social proof. CPM reduces as real user engagement accumulates. [L0298.029]
  • Creative testing begins on a small budget of about 5 per day per ad set to see whether an audience responds. [L1077.015]

Common mistakes

  • Changing many things at once makes results unreadable. Keep a control ad and change one element. [L0640.025]
  • A single creative inside a shared ad set can absorb most of the budget. Test each new creative in its own ad set first. [L0646.062, L1177.059]
  • Forgetting to test a new creative separately lets the winning creative hide new results. [L1177.031]
  • Judging an ad before it has spent enough. Wait for the target spend before reading hard metrics. [L0646.059, L0640.027]
  • Launching a single polished ad late. Launch many creatives quickly and iterate. [L1077.016]
  • Changing the offer and the creative in the same test. Keep the offer fixed while testing creatives. [L0502.025, L1382.074]
  • Starting hook testing before ripped creatives have proven profitable. [L1252.016, L1252.017]
  • Blindly trusting platform budget allocation. Force spend onto a creative before calling it weak. [L0640.027]
  • Declaring a product dead after a weak creative. Test new creatives first. [L1038.016]

Further details

  • Test ads for a product in the US first, but if CTR stays low with the same product, test it in another market such as Canada or Australia. Australia is named as a good test market. [L0513.032]

  • Keep testing ad creative and the wording of customer messages, because small changes in phrasing can have large effects on sales. Spending money on testing was a core part of this seller's approach. [L1437.019]
