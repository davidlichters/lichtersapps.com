12.17 Video production and editing

Phone filming: camera and resolution

  • A modern smartphone is enough to shoot Facebook ad video. Learn the basics of lighting, camera movement and shot technique from free online tutorials or a low-cost course of about $50 before you outsource production. [L0349.030]
  • A recent smartphone with a 4K camera can produce product footage good enough for organic and paid video. Shoot on the phone and edit on a computer. Expensive cameras are not needed at the start. [L1077.048]
  • TikTok compresses uploads to at least 1080p, so a back camera with good light is enough. 4K is not needed for phone uploads. [L1403.017]
  • Video must be at least 1080p, with 4K preferred. Older advice (Source B, 2021-2022): do not overthink production. A modern smartphone can produce video as good as a DSLR or a lower-end video camera, and the video does not need depth or movement to work as an ad. [L1238.011]
  • Both rules apply: 1080p is the floor in both sources. Use 4K if the phone offers it, but do not pay for it. [L1238.011, L1403.017]
  • Older (2021-2022): an iPhone X records 4K at up to 60 fps, which is enough for product shoots, unboxings and talking-head videos. A mid-range recent smartphone (used in 2020) is a sufficient starting camera. [L1424.016]
  • Older (2021-2022): the main difference between a smartphone and a dedicated video camera is the lens. Telephoto and other interchangeable lenses can cost a lot on their own, while current phones already produce high-quality video. [L1424.038]
  • Older (2021-2022): about 80% of the instructor's videos were recorded on a phone. Footage was moved from phone to Google Drive, then downloaded to the computer for editing. [L1238.012]

Lighting

  • Lighting has the biggest effect on video quality. Shoot indoors with artificial light or next to a window rather than outdoors in direct sunlight. Direct sun produced weaker quality even on an older phone. [L1383.050]
  • Light matters more than the camera. Control the light on the subject and let the phone camera handle the rest. [L1403.005]
  • Light sources and softening:
    • A ring light, clip-on light or phone light adds light to the subject.
    • A diffuser (umbrella filter) over a light softens it so it is less harsh.
    • If your body blocks daylight, compensate with an extra light source. [L1403.006]
  • Do not rely on the back camera's low quality when the light is poor. The light setup decides the result more than the camera. [L1403.028]
  • Older (2021-2022): dim lighting turns viewers off quickly. Natural daylight in a room is the best light source, but avoid direct sunlight. Without enough natural light, use affordable artificial lights. [L1424.011]
  • Older (2021-2022): for a simple talking-head ad, place the speaker so daylight from a window lights the face. Natural light was enough for good results without a studio. Avoid large or expensive lighting kits as a starting point. [L1238.017]
  • In the smartphone UGC workflow, set up lighting first: a ring light or a cheap LED light, plus daylight from a window. [L1403.003]

Production workflows

Choose the workflow that matches the type of video.

Smartphone UGC production, in order [L1403.003]

  1. Know the product, its unique features, its benefits and the audience. [L1403.003]
  2. Write the script covering each feature and benefit. [L1403.003]
  3. Set up lighting first (a ring light or a cheap LED light, plus daylight from a window). [L1403.003]
  4. Film the A-roll (the talking-to-camera voiceover) first, using the teleprompter app if needed. [L1403.003]
  5. Film the product showcase shots: hook, pan, zoom and parallax. [L1403.003]
  6. Film the feature and benefit footage that matches each script line. [L1403.003]
  7. Film context and lifestyle shots (establishing shots, props, people using the product). [L1403.003]
  8. Assemble the footage over the A-roll audio in an editor such as CapCut. [L1403.003]

Short footage-led ad, in order [L0343.031]

  1. Write the script first and decide what each line says. [L0343.031]
  2. Find footage that matches each line of the script. [L0343.031]
  3. Cut the footage to the script with plain cuts and remove dead time. [L0343.031]
  4. Add text overlays that support the key benefit. [L0343.031]
  5. Add transitions only after the narrative works. [L0343.031]
  6. Add sound effects, music and polish last. [L0343.031]

Presenter ad with A-roll and B-roll, in order [L0344.009]

  1. Pick the background music and the mood first, since the atmosphere shapes the rest of the edit. [L0344.009]
  2. Write the script and decide the main talking or presenting footage (the A-roll). [L0344.009]
  3. Cut the main footage into the final narrative order and remove every mistake, pause and filler word. [L0344.009]
  4. Lay supporting footage (B-roll) over the main track, and only then add product demonstrations and close-ups. [L0344.009]
  5. Add the music bed, set its volume and fade it out at the end. [L0344.009]
  6. Generate auto captions, delete the unwanted ones and style the remaining text. [L0344.009]
  7. Add transitions, animations, effects, sound effects and keyframe motion last. [L0344.009]
  8. Check that all text and graphics stay inside the safe zone for each placement. [L0344.009]

Note: the footage-led ad [L0343.031] and the presenter ad [L0344.009] order the work differently (music first in the presenter ad). Use the footage-led order when the ad is built from found footage, and the presenter order when a person speaks to camera.

Principles for the order of work

  • Decide the order on purpose: script and footage decide the ad, text comes after cutting, and effects come last. Adding text before the cut is final wastes time, because wording and timing will still change. [L0344.012]
  • Place the main talking footage on the main track and put supporting product footage above it, so the spoken narrative stays readable. [L0344.010]
  • Record the A-roll first, then match each script line to footage, so you know exactly where each scene belongs. Footage can be layered over the voiceover later. [L1403.014]
  • A-roll can be faceless. The voiceover can be recorded by you, a script writer or an AI voice, as long as the script is recorded before the B-roll is filmed. [L1403.015]
  • Start uploading right away. While waiting for equipment such as a ring light, upload drafts and experiment with phone footage, because consistent uploads are needed to learn what performs. [L1381.037]
  • UGC-style video can look rougher than polished brand content and still work for ads. The core requirement is that the shots clearly show what the product is, its features and its benefits. Beginners can start with this rougher style, and high polish is optional. [L1403.001]

Traditional TikTok-style ad from a reference video [L1381.031]

  1. Edit the video in Splice. [L1381.031]
  2. Screen-record the on-screen text and speech of a reference TikTok video. [L1381.031]
  3. Add matching text and only non-copyrighted sounds in Splice. [L1381.031]
  4. Export the video to the computer. [L1381.031]
  5. Upload it in TikTok Ads Manager and run it as a traditional ad. [L1381.031]

Shot list and footage

  • Write a shot list before filming that matches the story the ad tells. [L0561.053]
  • Film several angles of the product being made and of the product in use. [L0561.053]
  • Film more angles of the product than you think you need: a static shot, a slide or pan across a surface, and slow zooms. Keep the product centred and the camera level, so it does not look skewed when cut in as B-roll. [L0344.011]
  • Main shot types for UGC and ads:
    • Product showcase.
    • Close-up when discussing a specific feature.
    • Use-case shot showing the product in action. [L1425.022]
  • Shot types and their effect:
    • A wide shot shows the setting and context. A close-up isolates a detail or shows facial emotion.
    • Upward-angled cowboy shots make a subject look more powerful; downward-angled shots make it look smaller. [L1427.014]
  • B-roll is secondary footage layered over the main narrative to support it, for example a product being made while the presenter talks. Text and shapes can sit on the upper layers too. [L1427.025]
  • Establishing shot: open with a wider shot of the setting (for example the bath) before zooming in on the product, so the viewer understands the context. [L1403.022]
  • Props should fit the product's context (for example rubber ducks in the bath). Avoid props that distract from the product, such as bath bombs that create foam or colour. [L1403.020]
  • Test the product's claims on camera, such as waterproofing, by actually doing it, then film that test as a proof shot. [L1403.021]
  • To show built-in lights:
    1. Film the product lit in a normal room. [L1403.031]
    2. Switch off the room lights and film the product glowing in the dark. [L1403.031]
    3. Cycle through each light mode as its own shot. Keep the phone steady and use a slow zoom or parallax move for each state. [L1403.031]
  • To prove a connectivity claim such as Bluetooth pairing, start a screen recording on the phone, pull down the quick-settings panel (Android) or Control Centre (iPhone), and open the device connection menu while the product connects. Add this clip next to a close-up of the product's pairing mode. [L1403.032]
  • For a floating product, film a movement shot by following it across the water surface at the same speed as the product drifts. This shows buoyancy in motion rather than in a static frame. [L1403.033]
  • If the home has no suitable location, film in a public place such as a shop aisle, handling the product the way a customer would, or at a pool or beach for water products. Shoot when the location is quiet so the shot stays clean. [L1403.036]
  • Supplier warehouse footage: filming in the supplier's warehouse makes a small brand look larger and differentiates it from competitors selling the same product. Owner-filmed content shows the business as real and established. [L0921.032]
  • Demonstrations work for physical products: live product demonstrations, how-to tutorials and entertaining presentations. Video is effective for showing benefits and differentiating from competitors, even where it is under-tested in some categories. [L0227.042]
  • ASMR-style close-ups of products, such as bottle caps or drops, are popular on TikTok on a small budget. 10 to 15 second videos are also popular. [L0561.040]
  • When a product must be shown in use but you do not want to appear on camera, hide your face with a mask, film only the body or the back of the head, or use a full costume. [L1186.014]
  • Video backgrounds strongly affect conversion. A clean, aesthetic, branded background looks more professional than a plain wall and can make the same product convert noticeably better. Use better backgrounds when starting out, even if the product is simple. [L0821.015]
  • Check the background before uploading. A messy or busy background, such as a checkered red-and-white pattern, made a video look old and interfered with the camera. Use a clean, plain setup. [L0764.019]

Camera moves and framing

  • Turn on the 3x3 grid in the smartphone camera settings to frame shots. If the grid is unavailable, keep the product in the exact centre of the frame, because the camera is sharpest at the centre. [L1403.007]
  • Do not film the product in a way that hides its main features or leaves it off-centre. Off-centre footage loses sharpness and clarity on the key feature. [L1403.025]
  • Choose the aspect ratio by platform before filming: square or 4:5 for Facebook feed ads, and vertical portrait for TikTok and Instagram Stories. Leave enough space in the frame for text and overlays. [L1403.023]

Focus lock [L1403.009]

  1. Press and hold the screen on the product until the focus lock appears. [L1403.009]
  2. Move the camera without the focus shifting. [L1403.009]

Pan shot with a towel slider [L1403.008]

  1. Place the phone on a towel laid across a surface so it can slide smoothly without sticking. [L1403.008]
  2. Tap and hold the screen on the product to lock focus. [L1403.008]
  3. Slowly drag the phone across the product in one fluid movement. [L1403.008]

Level pan [L1403.010]

  1. Turn the phone upside down on a flat surface. [L1403.010]
  2. Slide it along the base, keeping the lens low so the product is filmed straight-on at a consistent height. [L1403.010]

Slow zoom [L1403.011]

  1. Keep the product centred in the 3x3 grid. [L1403.011]
  2. Zoom in slowly on the product while holding the camera steady. [L1403.011]

Parallax shot [L1403.012]

  1. Walk slowly around the product with the feet placed wide and the body braced. [L1403.012]
  2. Keep the product centred in frame as you move. [L1403.012]
  3. Practise before the final take. It is the hardest shot to keep stable. [L1403.012]

Reveal technique [L1403.030]

  1. Open the clip with the presenter reacting to the product close to the lens. [L1403.030]
  2. Move the phone or body out of the frame to reveal the product as a transition. [L1403.030]
  3. Alternatively, start the reveal in darkness and move into light as the product comes into view. [L1403.030]

Common framing and movement mistakes

  • Avoid sloppy handheld pans and zooms. Move slowly, lock focus first, and use a support (towel or the phone base) to keep movement fluid. [L1403.026]

A-roll and script recording

A-roll recording [L1403.013]

  1. Set the phone to the back camera with good light and frame yourself in shot, or flip to the front camera. The back camera is lower quality but usable when the light is good. [L1403.013]
  2. Use a teleprompter app to read the script. [L1403.013]
  3. Record the full voiceover. [L1403.013]
  4. Write down which footage should go over each line of the script. [L1403.013]
  • For scripted videos, write the full script word for word beforehand. Read it while recording, looking at the script and then at the camera. The editor cuts out the glances at the script. This also removes hesitations and filler words such as "um" and "ah". [L1424.012]
  • Teleprompter options:
    • A free online teleprompter on a computer or tablet screen, placed right next to the camera, costs nothing.
    • A paid teleprompter attached to the camera, or an iPad held near the lens, are other options. [L1424.013]
  • Record narration separately when the person does not need to appear on camera, so the audio can be combined with images or other video later. Reading the script or blog post aloud works well for this. [L1424.014]
  • Mistakes on camera are normal and should not stop a UGC shoot. They are removed in the edit. [L1423.008]

Cutting and trimming

Cutting the A-roll [L1423.007]

  1. Drag the footage onto the timeline and find the start of the speech. [L1423.007]
  2. Use B to cut at each unwanted word, pause, "um" or "ah". Then use A to select the unwanted segment and delete it with Delete or Backspace. [L1423.007]
  3. Trim clip edges by dragging their sides to shorten them. [L1423.007]
  4. Go through the whole script, then play the result to check the flow. [L1423.007]

Trim and cut clips [L1427.029]

  1. Drag the start or end of a clip to shorten it. [L1427.029]
  2. Use the razor tool to cut out a section and delete it. [L1427.029]
  3. Hold Shift and click several clips to select them, then move them together. [L1427.029]
  4. Close any gap left behind by deleting a section so the cut flows. [L1427.029]
  • Cut B-roll to fit the spoken words. Each B-roll clip must be cut so it does not carry unwanted audio. UGC does not need high polish, but a few clean product shots (for example slow pans, zooms and close-ups) make the ad look credible. [L1423.009]
  • Cut dead footage that repeats what viewers already saw. In one example, a 9 to 10 second pouring segment was removed because it was too long. [L1383.043]
  • Shorten a decent video by trimming dull moments, removing unnecessary frames or speeding up sections. This is a valid way to repurpose it. [L0795.015]
  • Keep the middle of the video fast-paced by changing the clip roughly every 3 seconds, so viewers do not lose interest. The middle carries the education, solution, benefits and features. [L0777.007]
  • On-screen text should animate in and out quickly, so viewers cannot read it all in one pass and must replay the video. This pushes rewatches, but only works when the story itself is compelling. [L1384.021]
  • Before overlaying B-roll, lower the clip's own volume to zero in the Tools panel. Sound recorded with a shot, such as a phone scratching across a desk, would otherwise play under the ad. [L1423.035]
  • Mistake: dragging a clip onto a higher track in CapCut pushes the clips below it along the timeline, which shifts the whole edit. Duplicate the clip onto the upper track and trim the copy instead, so the main timeline stays in place. [L1423.040]

Captions and on-screen text

Safe zones and placement

  • Canva safe zone for a 4:5 video ad: set up the 4:5 ratio at 1080 × 1350 px. Place critical text such as the offer in the safe zone where Facebook will not crop it. Stories show text at the top and bottom; the main feed may cut off bottom text. Design accordingly. [L0308.014, L0777.002]
  • Source conflict: the Canva note gives 1350 × 1920 px for 4:5. That frame is not 4:5 (1350 ÷ 1920 ≈ 0.70, while 4:5 = 0.80). Use 1080 px wide × 1350 px tall for 4:5, the vertical size that matches the ratio. [L0308.014, L0777.002]
  • Use 4:5 (1080 × 1350 px, vertical) because the feed is viewed mainly on mobile and 4:5 fills more of the screen. [L0777.002]
  • Mobile viewing drives most sales for social video ads, so design creatives for the phone screen first. The source claims about 90% of sales come from mobile. [L0777.003]
  • Create two versions of each video: one format for the Facebook and Instagram feed, and a separate one for Reels and Stories, because the two placements display content differently. [L1347.010]
  • Test video ad dimensions and aspect ratios. Some videos may appear differently sized than expected, which affects ad performance and reach. [L0209.035]
  • Place the on-screen call-to-action text high enough in the video so it stays visible and is not hidden by platform UI elements. A low position was noted as a weakness in the example. [L0655.009]

Why captions matter

  • Feed viewers often watch without sound, so the caption carries much of the message. Put a benefit-driven line of 4-5 words on screen for each clip, with an emotional trigger. [L0398.008]
  • Viewers are often distracted, for example waiting in line or in a crowded place, so messages must be understood quickly. Add subtitles, because many viewers cannot hear the sound. [L0998.016]
  • Consider how people browse the ad. A person scrolling while queuing in a store has split attention and may have no sound on. Design the creative to work silently, for example with subtitles on videos. [L0993.011]

Caption tools and steps

  • Auto captions in CapCut (desktop and mobile) create a text layer from the spoken words, which can then be edited or deleted. Generate captions only after the voiceover is final, otherwise you will have to regenerate them. [L0344.019]
  • Auto captions in CapCut:
    1. Select all clips with the voice track. [L1423.016]
    2. Go to Text and click Auto captions at the top. [L1423.016]
    3. Check the caption language setting, since the auto-detected language can be wrong. [L1423.016]
    4. Click Create. [L1423.016]
    5. Review the captions track above the timeline and correct any misheard words in the captions list. [L1423.016]
    6. Use the captions list to split or merge caption lines and adjust their length. [L1423.016]
  • Vide.io: upload the video, open the subtitles function and let it generate the text automatically. Then choose the subtitle style (plain or in a box), set the size and select the font. [L0888.018]
  • On CapCut mobile, the auto-caption step asks which caption animation to apply. The Pro list includes an animated caption background that changes colour as each word is spoken. This was popular for TikTok-style captions at the time, but it may not suit every ad, and the desktop version did not offer it during the demo. [L0344.045]

Caption styling

  • Style captions in the text panel: add strokes, add a background, and align text left, middle or right. For a TikTok-style look, use a rounded rectangle background and increase its height and width for padding. [L0344.020]
  • Caption styling in CapCut, under Text > Basic:
    • Use a readable font. The system font is recommended for UGC.
    • Set the text colour.
    • Either add a stroke for a simple TikTok or Instagram look, or switch on the background, set it to a rounded rectangle with white fill and black text, and increase the height and width to round the corners.
    • Tick Apply to all, then increase the line height if it looks cramped. [L1423.018]
  • Every text layer on video needs a background, a stroke or a drop shadow, because light and dark areas of the footage can make plain white text disappear. Glow and drop shadow are other options in the text settings. [L1423.019]
  • Text over video needs readability treatment. On a video with white text, place a gradient behind it or add a black stroke. On TikTok, the native text style uses a black stroke over white text. [L1348.013]
  • Avoid CapCut's template text styles for UGC. They look overdone and not like user-generated content. [L1423.020]
  • To style one caption differently, untick Apply to all in the caption settings, then move, resize or restyle that caption independently. [L1423.020]
  • Text animation duration is set under animation at the bottom of the timer. A value of 0.4 seconds gives a quick entrance for short-form ads. Use the same short duration across lines so the rhythm stays consistent. [L0344.021]
  • Split a long claim into several short text lines, stacked or staggered, so each benefit appears on its own. For TikTok keep text short and punchy; on Facebook you can use more varied title styles. [L0344.022]
  • When text layers are stacked and move together, untick the Apply to all checkbox so each layer can be moved independently. Once unlinked, each line can be positioned and timed on its own. [L0344.023]
  • To give different lines of one multi-line text layer different colours in CapCut, select the specific line in the text layer and change its colour in the text section of the control panel. Each line can be styled separately without creating a new layer. [L0344.048]
  • Duplicate a styled text layer with Ctrl/Cmd+C and Ctrl/Cmd+V to keep the same font, stroke and size, then edit only the words. This is faster than styling each bullet from scratch. [L1423.039]
  • Blinking text effect: a blinking text effect can lift CTR, especially for seasonal promotions such as Black Friday. It is a small change, but creative is a major factor in results. [L1194.024]

Benefit overlays and bullet graphics

  • Benefit list overlay: while the speaker names a product's benefits, short text lines appear on top of B-roll one after another, in sync with the words. Formula: spoken claim, then one benefit per line, timed to the moment each benefit is said.
    • Example 1: "Calms the mind and helps you unwind" appears while the speaker says "calm evenings".
    • Example 2: "Lasts up to 60 hours" appears while the speaker says "long-lasting burn time". [L1423.010]
  • Bullet-point graphic:
    1. Create a text layer for each benefit with the same style. [L1423.021]
    2. Pick a checkmark from the Stickers tab or download a free check icon, and place it beside the text. [L1423.021]
    3. Position all items so they fit together on screen. [L1423.021]
    4. Select the group and drag each line onto the timeline at the moment the speaker mentions that benefit. [L1423.021]
    5. Resize the items so they are legible on a phone. [L1423.021]
  • Add icons, ticks, arrows and emojis next to claims to emphasise them, and make them appear at the same time as the text they support. Keep these graphics smaller than the main text so they do not compete with it. [L0344.024]

Transitions, zooms and attention cuts

Transitions

  • Transitions are built into CapCut and can be added between two cuts. A zoom or pull-in suits an introduction of the product, and a duration around 1.2 seconds was used as a starting point. Drag the transition onto the cut point and adjust the duration in the control panel. [L0344.025]
  • Adding a transition between two clips in CapCut:
    1. Transitions can only be placed between two clips, so duplicate the clip and place the copy on a track above. [L1423.026]
    2. Trim the top copy so the cut lines up with the transition point. [L1423.026]
    3. Open the Transitions tab and drag a transition, such as Slide down, between the two clips. [L1423.026]
    4. Preview the transition to confirm it works. [L1423.026]
  • Use a transition every time the scene or subject changes, so the viewer stays engaged. Effects such as colour overlays, graphics and filters are for style and should be used sparingly. [L0344.027]
  • Use transitions sparingly and match them to camera movement: pan or slide when the camera moves sideways, and zoom when the shot pushes in. Panning and zooming transitions are the most useful. Overusing transitions makes the ad look chaotic. [L1423.027]
  • Reading L0344.027 and L1423.027 together: a transition belongs at each scene or subject change, with the type matched to the camera movement. Do not add transitions where nothing changes, and keep colour overlays and filters to a minimum. [L0344.027, L1423.027]
  • Clip animation in the video controls can animate a clip in, out or both, using options such as swing or slide. Avoid slide animations that bring in a black background, because they look unpolished on a product ad. [L0344.028]

Attention cuts and zooms

  • Zoom into the subject with a keyframed scale:
    1. Select the clip you want to zoom into. [L0344.029]
    2. Move the playhead to the frame where the zoom starts and click the keyframe button next to Scale in the video controls at normal size. [L0344.029]
    3. Move the playhead to the frame where the zoom ends. [L0344.029]
    4. Increase Scale to the target size, which creates a second keyframe. [L0344.029]
    5. Play the section to check that the zoom moves smoothly between the two keyframes. [L0344.029]
  • Cut-in zoom: split the clip with the blade at the moment of the action (for example a wink), select the second part, go to Video > Scale and increase the scale so the shot zooms in at that moment. Pairing the zoom with a short sound effect like a ding adds emphasis. [L1423.022]
  • Keyframe zoom in CapCut:
    1. Select the clip and open the scale property in the Tools panel. [L1423.023]
    2. Click the keyframe diamond to set a keyframe at the start. [L1423.023]
    3. Move the playhead to a later point. [L1423.023]
    4. Change the scale, which creates a keyframe automatically. [L1423.023]
    5. Drag keyframes to adjust timing. [L1423.023]
    • A slow zoom over the whole clip builds engagement, and a fast zoom in the first seconds grabs attention. [L1423.023]
  • Jump cut: blade just before a small action such as a wink, then scale the new clip in so the eye line stays where it was. Pair jump cuts with a short sound effect at the moment of the cut to grab attention. [L0344.030]
  • The two main attention cuts are a slow zoom into the subject and a quick jump cut. Both can be layered with sound effects. Use them across the ad, not only at the start. [L0344.031]
  • Cuts, zooms and motion keep attention and are used throughout the ad. Zoom in during speech to add emphasis, and zoom out to make a cut out. Both help retention. [L1423.025]
  • Cut types:
    • A straight cut joins two shots back to back and is the main cut for basic ads.
    • A jump cut skips time or zooms in between shots.
    • A J-cut starts the next shot's audio before its picture.
    • An L-cut keeps the previous shot's audio playing after the cut. [L1427.015]

Motion, easing and blur

  • Things in motion accelerate and decelerate, so a linear keyframe move looks mechanical. Apply Ease Out to the first keyframe and Ease In to the second, or use a custom speed graph, to make motion look natural. [L0343.037]
  • Motion blur on a fast zoom: increase the shutter angle in the motion blur settings. A high shutter angle creates visible blur during quick movement, which makes the motion look more natural. [L0343.039]
  • Motion blur and easing make movement look natural. Without motion blur, fast-moving objects look harsh. At the time of this demo the desktop version did not offer motion blur, so check whether your version has it before planning moving text. [L0344.033]
  • Any property with a keyframe diamond can be animated over time, including scale, position and colour. Position keyframes can move a product or hand in from off screen, and text can be animated the same way. [L1423.024]

Overlays, cut-outs and compositing

  • Before placing an emoji or graphic over footage, check that its PNG has a transparent background, because a solid background covers the video underneath. If it does not, find another PNG or remove the background before placing it. [L0343.043]
  • Arrows and graphics are found in the Stickers tab and dragged onto the timeline. Move, resize and rotate them with the rotation handle in the Tools panel, and add position keyframes to slide them in from off screen. [L1423.037]
  • Chroma key: use the chroma key option in the Cut out panel to pick a colour and remove it from a clip, which is how green-screen footage is cleared. Auto cut out can isolate a person from the background so the person can be placed in front of an overlay. [L0344.038]
  • Removing the background with auto cut-out:
    1. Duplicate the clip with the person on the timeline. [L1423.029]
    2. Open the Tools panel, go to Cut out and choose Auto cut out. [L1423.029]
    3. Wait for processing to finish; the person is isolated from the background. [L1423.029]
    4. Place graphics such as wings behind the cut-out layer so they appear behind the person. [L1423.029]
  • Chroma key on green-screen footage:
    1. Place the green-screen clip on the timeline above the background. [L1423.030]
    2. Open Cut out and choose Chroma key. [L1423.030]
    3. Use the colour picker to select the green background. [L1423.030]
    4. Raise the strength of the chroma key until all green is gone. [L1423.030]
    5. Turn off any shadow so the keyed element blends cleanly. [L1423.030]
  • Blend mode overlays:
    • Multiply keeps the dark parts of the overlay and removes the light parts.
    • Screen or brighten keeps the light parts and removes the dark parts.
    • Soft light is a gentle overlay that blends an effect into the footage without overpowering it.
    • Example 1: a soft light glow over a candle video.
    • Example 2: a multiply texture over a dark background. [L1423.031]
  • Animated social proof workflow (pop-up comments or testimonials):
    1. Design the static graphics, such as comment cards or testimonial frames, in Canva. [L1348.031]
    2. Export them as image files. [L1348.031]
    3. Import the images into a video editor and animate them, adding pop sound effects and motion. [L1348.031]
  • CapCut is a quick editor for animated social proof because it includes built-in pop sound effects. [L1348.018]

Sound design and music

Background music

  • Set the music volume low enough that the voiceover is clearly audible. A starting point of 12 to 16 in the volume control of the panel worked in the example, then adjust by ear. Music should sit in the background and create atmosphere. [L0344.017]
  • Background music setup in CapCut: go to Audio, pick a track by searching a mood keyword such as "lo-fi", drag it onto the timeline, trim it to start at the beginning, then lower its volume in the Tools panel so the voice is clearly audible. Pick music that fits the product's mood, for example calm lo-fi for home decor. [L1423.014]
  • Pick the track that fits the mood of the ad, not the most popular one. Envato Elements provides stock video, music and sound effects searchable by keyword, for example "home decor music". [L0344.016]
  • Fading music: select the music clip and drag the small circle at the clip's start or end to fade the music in or out. Fade it out at the end of the ad. [L1423.015]
  • Auto fade out: use the auto fade out switch on the audio track so the music fades slowly at the end instead of stopping abruptly. The fade belongs at the end of the ad, after the closing call to action. [L0344.018]
  • Polished background music in a short product video can make a simple, low-cost product feel more premium. A minimal video with a refined soundtrack can present a premium look better than a busy, text-heavy ad. [L0692.051]

Sound effects and cues

  • Sound effects for transitions and emphasis: open Audio > Sound effects, pick a sound such as a whoosh for a transition or a ding for a wink, drag it onto an audio track aligned with the action, and lower the volume so it is subtle. [L1423.028]
  • Use sound effects like whooshes to support transitions, aligned with the cut, and turn them down so they stay in the background. A sound effect should never be the main element of the piece. [L0344.026]
  • Sound design has two purposes: ambient sounds that match what is on screen (for example train noise for a train), and stylistic cues tied to a visual moment (for example a ding on a wink or a whoosh on a transition). Stylistic cues are in CapCut's sound library at no extra cost. [L1423.038]
  • Audio cues that fall on the same beat as a visual cut make the edit feel intentional. Layer cues sparingly so they guide attention rather than distract. [L0344.043]
  • Shocking visuals, animations or sounds can hold attention, but use them only when they fit the brand and the audience. Test them against a plain version before committing. [L0524.049]
  • Ads should avoid silence. Continuous sound and visual movement hold attention, unless a pause is used deliberately for effect. [L0344.010]

Trending and recreated sounds

  • Use trending sounds and seasonal trends to attract viewers, but only if they fit the product. Examples mentioned: a major sports event that was still running, and the US holiday travel trend around Christmas and New Year. [L0657.025]
  • Viewers must be watching or listening at every moment. Text-to-speech over a strong satisfying sound distracts from the sound, so use plain on-screen text there instead. [L1383.040]
  • For a satisfying product sound, use plain text instead of text-to-speech so viewers can hear the sound. For an ordinary sound, text-to-speech and on-screen text can run together. [L1383.041]
  • Keep the original trending sound at a very low level, around 5, so viewers hear the recreated sound and the environment rather than the trending track. [L1383.042]
  • Recreated sounds usually sound better than originals, because you control the recording. Re-record a sound and layer it in place of the original. [L1383.046]
  • Find a trending sound on the For You page:
    1. When a sound plays on the For You page, tap the sound circle in the bottom right corner. [L1383.047]
    2. Note how many videos already use the sound. [L1383.047]
    3. Open the original video. If there is no original, open the most popular video using the sound. [L1383.047]
    4. Check when the sound was first used. [L1383.047]
    5. Look at view counts of other recent videos that use the sound. [L1383.047]
    6. Use the sound only if recent videos using it still get large views. Skip sounds that are about six months old or whose videos are old. [L1383.047]
  • Recreate a product sound and raise its volume in Splice:
    1. Record the real product sound on a phone with the Voice Memos app, using a second cup and water if needed. [L1383.045]
    2. End the recording, then share the audio to Splice. [L1383.045]
    3. In Splice, tap Audio, then Music, then import the recording. [L1383.045]
    4. Detach the audio from the video at the point where the sound should play. [L1383.045]
    5. Split the audio where it starts and where it ends. [L1383.045]
    6. Open Volume and raise the level. Volume starts at 100; set it to about 180 to 200 so it plays louder without distorting. [L1383.045]
    7. Review the result and keep the recreated sound on the cue point. [L1383.045]

Editing tools

CapCut

  • CapCut is a video editing app good enough for beginners to make their own ad videos. Learning to produce creatives yourself also lets you brief outside creators more precisely. [L1107.018]
  • CapCut is the editor used to cut TikTok Shop mentions, Orange Cart mentions and non-matching offers out of a copied video. [L0704.016]
  • Edit simple problem-and-solution ads in CapCut using existing footage from AliExpress or YouTube, arranged as a short narrative: problem first, then solution. [L0950.051]
  • Editing for UGC-style content can be done with free tools such as CapCut, which allows text overlays and short-form edits. Voice-over can be added to filmed product footage. [L0561.033]

Other tools

  • Splice is used for the traditional TikTok ad workflow above and for recreating product sounds. [L1381.031, L1383.045]
  • Final Cut Pro, to make a TikTok version:
    1. Open the project settings and change the format from YouTube to TikTok. [L0888.019]
    2. Move the character to the left so the speaker stays visible in the vertical frame before exporting. [L0888.019]
  • Opus Clip can cut one long customer video into several shorter clips and add an AI voiceover. Use it to turn existing UGC into many ad variations for split testing. [L1225.088]
  • Basic editing skills, such as cropping out unwanted text and adding simple transitions, are enough to repurpose footage for ads. Advanced production is not required to start testing. [L0644.030]
  • Freelance editing: if editing is needed, send the footage and shot list to a freelance video editor on Fiverr or Upwork. A story and footage must exist before outsourcing the edit. [L0561.053]

Repurposing across platforms

  • Repurposing can mean re-uploading an identical video on the same platform, or moving content from TikTok to Instagram Reels or YouTube Shorts to reach more audiences. [L0795.006]
  • Repurposed content is valuable across platforms. A strong TikTok video can be reused as an Instagram Reel, and a successful Reel can be reposted. Use this to capture more reach from one production effort. [L1077.036]
  • Reuse winning TikTok videos on Facebook in the same format, or have an editor cut them into Facebook formats. Test organic TikTok first, then use the video that goes viral for Facebook ads. [L0773.039]
  • Adapt each video to its platform in format and tone. Trends and jokes that work on TikTok may not work on Facebook Reels, which has an older audience. [L0784.016]
  • Compilation method:
    1. Pick three videos that performed well. [L0795.013]
    2. Extract only their highlights. [L0795.013]
    3. Combine them into one clip of about 10 to 20 seconds. For example, three 3-second highlights make a 9-second video. [L0795.013]
  • Cross-platform workflow:
    1. Edit the video once in a mobile editor such as InShot. [L0784.018]
    2. Upload it to Instagram and add the overlay text using Instagram's own text tool, with the platform's font. [L0784.018]
    3. Upload the same video to TikTok and use TikTok's text tool and font for the overlay, keeping the same thumbnail. [L0784.018]
    4. Upload to YouTube Shorts and use YouTube's own text tool and font for the overlay. [L0784.018]
    5. Use the same wording on each platform, but match the text formatting to the native style. [L0784.018]
  • Example: a DIY phone tripod hack was filmed on a camera, edited in InShot, then posted with the same thumbnail. The overlay "DIY phone tripod hack" was added with each platform's native text feature, which the lesson says boosts algorithm performance. [L0784.019]
  • Mistake: uploading one video to every platform with the same unchanged text styling misses platform-specific optimisation. Use each platform's native text features and fonts, because format is part of what the algorithm rewards. [L0784.020]
  • Watermarks: the animation tool's watermark (bottom left) and the subtitle tool's watermark (top) remain on the video unless covered. Cover them with a screenshot or overlay, and add your own logo in the bottom left corner. [L0888.026]

Thumbnails and hooks

  • The thumbnail must grab attention, stop the scroll and show the product being used. It is the first visual impression of the ad before the video plays. [L0777.010]
  • Use the thumbnail to speak to the main pain point of direct customers. Keep the same thumbnail with a matching opening clip, since that combination can be a winning pattern. [L0871.067]

Quality bar and mistakes to avoid

  • The quality of the video is the most important factor. A good video can be re-uploaded or repurposed later and may perform again, while a bad video will not succeed regardless of hashtags or posting tricks. [L0764.038]
  • Clips must match what is being said. Low-quality or pixelated footage should not be used, because poor visual quality makes an ad look cheap. [L0693.045]
  • Basic production is enough to start: a modern smartphone, a plain setup and consistent uploads. Film, edit and test before buying more. [L0349.030, L1381.037]

Build the cut before the polish

  • Build the cut structure first, then add transitions, sound effects and voiceover. Working in that order keeps the narrative clear before polishing it. [L1427.030]

Voiceover

  1. Write the script first. [L1427.054]
  2. Record the voice on the phone's recorder app, reading the script over the edit. [L1427.054]
  3. Upload the recording to cloud storage and download it to the computer. [L1427.054]
  4. Import the audio into the Audio bin, place it under the visuals and apply the dialogue preset. [L1427.054]

Text and graphics in Premiere Pro

  • Style the text with a fill color, a stroke (outline) and a background. [L1427.034]

Speed changes and motion

Speed ramp in Premiere Pro with keyframes:

  1. Add a speed keyframe at the start of the section you want to change. [L1427.041]
  2. Keep the first part slow. [L1427.041]
  3. Add a second keyframe where the action should speed up and set that section to fast. [L1427.041]
  4. Play it back to check the timing matches the cut. [L1427.041]
  • Ease in and ease out the start and end of a movement or transition. A movement that starts and stops abruptly looks unnatural, while easing makes it feel smooth. [L1427.044]

Transitions

  • In Premiere Pro's Effects panel, the video transitions folder contains presets such as Dip to Black, which fades a clip out to black. [L1427.071]
  • Drag the preset onto the cut point between two clips to apply it. [L1427.071]
  • Apply easing to the start and end of transitions as well as movements. [L1427.044]

Captions

  • Animated word-by-word captions suit YouTube Shorts engagement, but they did not perform well for ads in the tests described. [L1427.069]
  • Test animated word-by-word captions against standard captions before using them for paid ads. [L1427.069]

Color and filters
