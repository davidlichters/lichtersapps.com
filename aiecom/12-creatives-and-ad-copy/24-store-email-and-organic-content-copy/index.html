12.24 Store, email and organic content copy

Product page copy: persuasion order

  • Work through product copy in this order:
    1. List the problems and pains the product solves, and the problems current and potential customers feel now, which may have brought them to the page. [L1157.007]
    2. For each problem, identify the opposite desire: the pleasurable outcome the buyer wants. [L1157.007]
    3. Describe the end result the product delivers in the buyer's life, for example at home, in the car, or wherever they use it. The product itself is not the ultimate pleasure; the result it creates is. [L1157.007]
    4. Write the copy so it shows that you understand the buyer's pain and that the product delivers the pleasure they seek. [L1157.007]
  • Marketing means educating the market that a business can solve problems, fill gaps or achieve goals better than any other business. Product copy has the same purpose: show the buyer that this product solves their problem in a way no other product does. (older, 2021-22) [L1157.005]
  • The ultimate pleasure for a buyer is not owning the product but what the product does for them. Describe this outcome rather than the ownership itself. (older, 2021-22) [L1157.008]
  • Use this order for product copy (older, 2021-22):
    1. Name the customer's pain or problem back to them so they start to feel the negative emotion it causes. [L0611.004]
    2. Paint a picture of the pleasure and positive end result the product brings, so they feel the emotion they would have when the product is working for them. [L0611.004]
    3. Tell a story that makes the reader imagine the situation and feel something, to create emotion and a sense of connection. [L0611.004]
    4. Show proof, either through demonstration or through testimonials from customers who describe what they loved about the product. [L0611.004]
    5. Only then give logical support: features and specifications such as size, weight, speed and colour options, plus manuals or other documents and social proof such as reviews. [L0611.004]
  • Use pain before pleasure and pleasure before features. Talking about pain alone produces negative emotions, so follow it with the positive outcome the product delivers. (older, 2021-22) [L0611.005]
  • Emotion-before-logic pattern (older, 2021-22): open with a pain point, move to the desired outcome, support it with a short story or proof, then close with specs and reviews that justify the choice. [L0611.006]
    • Kitchen knife, pain and pleasure: "Tired of slicing dinner into uneven chunks every night? Imagine prepping in half the time with a blade that glides through tomatoes like butter." Then add: "A 20 cm forged steel blade, 180 g, with a 5-star rating from 2,300 buyers." [L0611.006]
    • Dog harness, pain and pleasure: "Walks that end with a yanked neck and a frustrated owner? Picture every walk calm and enjoyable, like the one Maria shared after a week with her rescue dog." Then add: "Padded chest strap, reflective stitching, sizes XS to XL in four colours." [L0611.006]
  • A story in product copy does not have to be fiction. A real-sounding scenario the buyer can picture creates emotion and a sense of connection with the brand, and makes the benefit feel real rather than abstract. (older, 2021-22) [L0611.007]
  • Product copy should open with emotional hooks (unique mechanism, power bullets, benefits) before proof points. Proof confirms an emotional decision with logic; it does not start the persuasion. (older, 2021-22) [L0295.002]
  • Product copy should lead with how the product makes the customer feel and how it fits into their life, not with generic claims such as high quality or shipping in two days. [L0774.022]
    • Pattern: name the situation the buyer cares about (a family gathering, a gift moment), show the outcome they want, then give one concrete benefit. [L0774.022]
    • Example one: 'Make your next family dinner easier and bring everyone together with this product.' [L0774.022]
    • Example two: 'Give your partner a gift that feels personal and arrives ready to use this week.' [L0774.022]
  • Common elements developed for product page copy (benefits, unique mechanisms, problem statements) can be reused directly in emails and ads without rewriting from scratch. (older, 2021-22) [L0241.003]
  • Before writing ad copy from scratch, audit the website's existing copy for benefit-driven lines, bold headlines and strong claims. Adapt these proven elements for Facebook and TikTok ads so the language already resonates with the audience. [L0204.019]

Product page copy: headlines and claims

  • Primary promise formula (the opening headline of a product description): [verb promising an outcome] + [everyday benefit] + [in the setting where the customer lives] + [superlative claim for the category]. It names the desire directly; the superlative hooks the reader but creates a claim the rest of the copy must prove. (older, 2021-22) [L0396.017]
    • Example 1: 'Keep your coffee hot all morning with the most leak-proof travel mug available at this price.' [L0396.017]
    • Example 2: 'Sleep cool every summer night with the most breathable cotton bedding in its price range.' [L0396.017]
  • Desire headline: name the outcome the buyer wants, using the words from your customer research, and tie it to the product's use. [L0567.022]
    • Example: 'Gear that keeps your longest weekend ride comfortable' [L0567.022]
    • Example: 'Stylish kit that holds up on every climb' [L0567.022]
  • Scarcity lines: name what the shopper loses if they leave, such as stock running low or a price ending. Use one only if it is true. [L0567.024]
    • Example: 'Only a few units left in your size' [L0567.024]
    • Example: 'This colourway is sold out soon, so hold it now' [L0567.024]
  • Shipping claims: do not include 'Free Shipping' in product page copy if Google Merchant Center flags it as redundant. Put the shipping offer in the meta description instead, or rely on Shopify free shipping settings. (2023) [L0274.054]

Product page structure

  • Source E pattern, product description in three blocks (2025-26):
    • 'What it is' paragraph: material, use case, colours, size range. [L1331.018]
    • 'How it works' list: three features, each tied to a function. [L1331.018]
    • 'What you will feel' list: short sensory benefits. [L1331.018]
    • Why it works: it sells the product at the top, keeps the buy button high, and keeps the details easy to scan. [L1331.018]
    • Example pattern: 'Water-resistant upper' followed by 'helps keep damp floors out'; 'Foam insole' followed by 'steps feel softer by the door'. [L1331.018]
  • Structure that worked in one Source B example (older, 2021-22):
    1. Unique mechanism.
    2. A bridging paragraph that introduces the extra benefits.
    3. A bulleted list of extra features, with each benefit shown as a short paragraph.
    4. The call-to-action paragraph.
    • The primary promise may be replaced by a convenience-based promise when a product is health-related and a health promise is restricted. [L0461.010]
  • Use a clear visual difference between the bridging paragraph and the feature list, such as an optional features heading, if it helps readability. Headings are optional. (older, 2021-22) [L0461.016]
  • Product page conversion depends on a clear, irresistible offer and on a product with real demand. A great product page cannot save a product with no demand or heavy competition. [L0690.033]
  • Study high-performing brand product pages (for example Blendjet and hoodie brands) to see how they structure copy, and compare them with weak product pages. Frameworks such as AIDA are a starting point. [L0690.034]

Product page proof and reviews

  • Include 3 to 5 customer reviews directly within the product page copy, at the proof section. Extract them from review widgets and place them into the sales copy. (older, 2021-22) [L0295.003]
  • Reviews can be embedded as text, images or video. Select reviews from existing review widgets and integrate them into the copy. (older, 2021-22) [L0295.004]
  • A video walkthrough showing the product in actual use demonstrates the claimed functionality and works as proof where features can be shown visually. It is not viable for every product type. (older, 2021-22) [L0295.005]
  • Reference media coverage when a product has been featured in recognised outlets: news, magazines, newspapers, current affairs shows, reality-based TV shows, blogs or online publications such as Buzzfeed. Include optional links to the original coverage, because audiences perceive media-featured products as high quality. (older, 2021-22) [L0295.009]
  • Pair proof for claims with the product page. Testimonials, clinical studies and statistics used in abandoned cart emails should stay accessible from the product page or a pop-up, not be linked externally, because external links break the purchase flow. [L0261.022]
  • Authorised retailer badges, procedure (2024):
    1. Add brand logos or text to the home page as a 'Brands We Carry' section. [L0253.044]
    2. Add 'Authorized Retailer for [Brand Name]' text to product descriptions. [L0253.044]
    3. Create a 6th product image in Canva showing an 'Authorized Dealer' badge. [L0253.044]
    4. Place this image at the end of the product image gallery, after images 1-5. [L0253.044]
    5. This works across the home page, collection pages and product pages. [L0253.044]

Product descriptions at catalogue scale

  • Run the product description prompt product by product for the whole catalogue. A short, fixed format keeps the work fast and avoids outsourcing product copy. (2025) [L1116.056]
  • If a supplier has no product description, write one before the product goes live. Writing many descriptions by hand takes a long time; for example, 500 products require a large amount of focus, so outsourcing is an option. (older, 2021-22) [L1163.021]
  • Prioritise unique product descriptions by traffic (2021):
    1. Pull a monthly report in Google Analytics. [L0671.268]
    2. Open the Content section. [L0671.268]
    3. Sort pages by traffic. [L0671.268]
    4. Add unique copy to the highest-traffic pages first, then work down the list. [L0671.268]
  • Beat Amazon with unique, benefit-led product descriptions rather than generic supplier text. Amazon's own descriptions are short, so a longer, story-driven description with a call to action is a clear advantage. [L1293.021]
  • For product page images and copy, reuse supplier assets when they are allowed. Logos, product images and descriptions from the supplier can be used directly if no restrictions apply. Customise only for SEO where needed. (older, 2022) [L0272.040]
  • Add GIFs cut from the product video to the product page and ad creatives as a low-effort test of a richer description. Test them against the current description. [L1173.014]
  • Source E pattern for organic product posts is covered under social content; for product page copy use the structure above. [L1331.018]

Product description prompts

Direct-response copywriter prompt, use with the product name or URL filled in (2024):

Adopt the persona of a direct-response copywriter and draft a product description for [product name and/or URL] following this arrangement: a headline, a short description of one to three sentences, a features-and-benefits block, and bulleted points. Base everything solely on the supplied product information and avoid fabricating specs, measurements, materials, or assertions. Before release, reword the draft into a natural, human-sounding voice while keeping the same arrangement.

Product videos and media

  • Add videos to product pages only if the supplier provides high-quality video assets that genuinely demonstrate the product. Do not source or create videos for every product. If the supplier sends videos, embed them from YouTube rather than uploading them directly to Shopify. (older, 2022) [L0272.068]
  • Short product videos placed directly at the point of sale can answer the buyer's last questions, such as unboxings, ease of use, education and common FAQs. They work in place of or alongside a written description, so the shopper gets the answer without leaving the purchase decision. (2025-26) [L0951.003]
  • Short product videos of 15 to 30 seconds, modelled on the style used by Zappos, can be made for each product even without access to the physical item. [L1293.024]
    • Show the product, state the top benefits (such as easy-adjust knobs or easy to clean), add a call to action such as 'Buy today', and mention authorised dealer status if it applies. [L1293.024]
    • Example: a 20-second video for a best-selling product, where a presenter says the three strongest benefits and ends with 'Buy today'. [L1293.024]
    • Example: a green-screen clip of a presenter describing a kitchen gadget's ease of use and ending with 'Order now'. [L1293.024]
  • To reuse supplier product videos on a store YouTube channel, follow these steps in order (2025):
    1. Ask the brand for permission to upload its product videos to your own channel. [L0667.089]
    2. Create a YouTube channel for the store. [L0667.089]
    3. Upload each supplier video with a correct file name and the right tags. [L0667.089]
    4. In each video description, add a link to the matching product page on the store. [L0667.089]
    5. Embed each video on its matching product page. [L0667.089]
  • Asking viewers a question in a YouTube description can prompt replies and engagement, which can help the algorithm and conversion. Choose questions that push the viewer to respond. [L0655.010]
  • Customer videos and photos are valuable user-generated content. Use them as social proof on the site and in ads, and repost them on YouTube, where they can rank in Google because Google often places video results above other organic results. (older, 2021-22) [L1012.019]

Store structure and landing pages

  • Traffic distribution for ecommerce stores: send 90 to 95 percent of traffic to the product page and only 5 to 10 percent to the home and collection pages. Optimise the product page first; home and collection pages need minimal attention. [L0321.014]
  • When a store sells several items in a niche with a similar theme, a home page featuring all products together can work. Each product should still link to a dedicated product page. Keep visitors on the landing page where possible and include add-to-cart options directly on it. [L0918.020]
  • Hero section principle: above the fold, a landing page hero should carry a big benefit call-out, striking visuals, trust, social proof and risk reduction, so the visitor understands the offer without scrolling far. [L0353.010]
  • Hero section workflow with AI:
    1. Know the hero section's job: visitors should immediately see what the product is, what the offer is and how it helps them. [L0353.009]
    2. Give the AI the product and the problem it solves, then ask for a big benefit statement. [L0353.009]
    3. Ask for trust elements such as social proof and authority. [L0353.009]
    4. Ask for copy that reduces risk, such as guarantees. [L0353.009]
    5. Combine the outputs into the hero section. [L0353.009]

Email: role, length and format

  • Email copy and website copy have different jobs. The email's main job is to get the reader to click through to the website, not to make the full sale. The sales pitch belongs on the product page or destination page. (older, 2021-22) [L0568.001]
  • Inbox attention spans are short, and readers receive more emails per day than they did five or ten years ago, so an email has very little time to hold a reader. Keep it brief rather than covering every selling point. (older, 2021-22) [L0568.002]
  • Long copy in an email is often not read. Product images, videos, full copy, price and the complete sales argument work better on the website, which presents them far better than an email can. (older, 2021-22) [L0568.003]
  • When an email links to a blog post, it does not need to prove the post's merits. It only needs to make the reader click the link, so it should not try to prove the post is great. (older, 2021-22) [L0568.004]
  • Image-heavy emails are more likely to land in the Promotions or Spam tab. Simple text emails that use a name and read like a normal message tend to reach the inbox and get opened more. Split test plain text against image plus text. [L0605.015]
  • Promo block for a time-limited sale, as a reusable structure. Keep the same structure for every promo so readers learn where to look (2025-26):
    • A headline with the sale name, for example 'Flash deals'.
    • A time window line, for example '24-hour flash deals'.
    • An extra discount on sale items, for example 'Extra 15% off sale items'.
    • One or two sentences that tell the reader why the sale matters.
    • A SHOP NOW call-to-action button.
    • Full block as a reusable structure:
Email newsletter promo block for a time-limited sale, as a reusable structure: a headline with the sale name (for example 'Flash deals'), a time window line (for example '24-hour flash deals'), an extra discount on sale items (for example 'Extra 15% off sale items'), one or two sentences that tell the reader why the sale matters, and a SHOP NOW call to action button. Keep the same structure for every promo so readers learn where to look.

  • Problem-focused subject and teaser formula (Source C):
    • '[Emotion word] because [common problem]'
    • 'Entrepreneurs: [time-based guilt problem]'
    • 'Students: [achievement-limiting problem]'

Abandoned cart and recovery messages

  • Second abandoned checkout email: write the draft directly in Shopify, copy the complete text, paste it into ChatGPT or another AI tool, request improved clarity and tone, then replace the original draft in Shopify with the AI-improved version. [L0149.010]
  • Proof belongs on the product page, not in the cart email. See the proof rule above. [L0261.022]
  • Cart-recovery email prompt, Gary Bencivenga style (Source E, 2025-26). Use with the brand name filled in:
Write a cart-recovery email for [brand] that reads like a Gary Bencivenga piece. It should bring a shopper back to checkout by speaking to the strongest want they have. Keep the voice chatty, as if one person is talking to another, and take tone, values and word choice from the brand profile supplied earlier in this chat. This is the first message this shopper receives from us, so leave out the subject line and preview text, and offer no discount. Sell only what finishing the order gives them. Structure: (1) a brief, punchy headline naming our single most valuable selling point, preferably one that also answers a common hesitation, such as fast delivery, a 30-day guarantee, or Australian-owned. (2) A sub-headline built on their desire. (3) Wording in the target market's own language that says 'you left this behind'. (4) A picture of the items they abandoned. (5) A short call to action reading either 'Complete Your Order' or 'Return to Cart'. (6) A scarcity or urgency line suited to the audience, invented fresh rather than borrowed from any example, such as one about low stock after a viral moment. (7) A short opening centred on their desire that invites them back to the cart. (8) Persuasive answers to the three most common customer reservations, never using the word 'objection'. (9) A line saying help is available at [email]. (10) A strong closing call to action. (11) A sign-off. Aim for high burstiness and perplexity: original phrasing, varied sentence length, emotional pull. Adapt language to the target market. Keep it short enough to scan, put spacing between every block, and never repeat the same claim in two blocks.

Lead magnet ebooks

  • Lead magnet offer formula: '[Product category benefit] solved with [number] proven [format] in [time]. [Social proof or scarcity]. Normally [original price], get it free. [Scarcity limiter, e.g. only 100 copies available].' This frames free content as premium while creating urgency. [L0307.015]
    • Example: 'Seven best relaxation techniques to relax at home in less than 5 minutes. Usually $19.99, download free. Only 100 copies available.' [L0307.015]
    • Example: 'Five energy-boosting rituals for busy entrepreneurs in under 10 minutes. Usually $29, free today only. Limited to first 50 subscribers.' [L0307.015]
  • Ebook cover line pattern: a named brand or expert presenting a solution, followed by the author's name, for example 'Your mentor presents [solution], written by [author name]'. This makes the cover read as a credible guide to a specific problem. [L0513.049]
  • Ebook title formula: a number or timeframe, a concrete promised result, and the topic or audience. The title should promise a clear, specific outcome that matches the product. (2026) [L0860.025]
    • Example 1: '5 Desk Stretches To Ease Stiff Shoulders' [L0860.025]
    • Example 2: '30-Day Starter Plan For Beginner Runners' [L0860.025]
  • AI chat tools can contain factual or wording mistakes, so review the full ebook text before publishing it to customers. Health-related guides should carry a short educational disclaimer and point to a qualified professional. (2026) [L0861.033]
  • An ebook used as a free gift should be 15 to 25 pages with 4 to 6 chapters. The product is mentioned only in the closing section. The ebook gives real value first and supports the product second. (2026) [L0861.035]
  • Creating a free ebook gift with AI (2026):
    1. Use the ebook prompt, replacing the target audience, the product name and the problem with your own. [L0862.027]
    2. Check the generated text for placeholder text and for any leftover call-to-action placeholders. [L0862.027]
    3. Ask the AI to turn the text into a stylish, complete HTML file with the product name, and ignore the CTA link. [L0862.027]
    4. Review the HTML and the design notes. [L0862.027]
    5. Ask the AI to convert the HTML into a PDF. [L0862.027]
    6. Download the PDF and upload it to the Digital Downloads app as an asset. [L0862.027]
    7. Attach the e-book product to the gift position in the bundle. [L0862.027]
  • Structure of a free-gift ebook, in order (2026):
    1. Title and subtitle. [L0862.028]
    2. Welcome note or short author intro that explains what the reader will get. [L0862.028]
    3. Four to six chapters, each with real, practical content, subheadings, short paragraphs and bullet points. [L0862.028]
    4. An actionable exercise in the chapter where it fits. [L0862.028]
    5. A closing section with a single soft next step. [L0862.028]
    6. An educational disclaimer at the end. [L0862.028]
  • Example of an actionable exercise: a 30-second self-check at night with a flashlight and a mirror, followed by a single simple observation. It gives readers a concrete task without asking them to change their routine. (2026) [L0862.029]
  • Choosing a lead magnet topic (Source A):
    1. Pick the sites, communities or influencers where you want to advertise. [L1076.007]
    2. Look at their homepage, feature articles and what is trending or popular there. [L1076.007]

  • Post one to two times per week on both Facebook and Instagram, without exception. Inactive pages whose most recent post is months or years old signal to customers that the business may no longer be operating. [L0157.002]
  • Basic organic posts, about a couple per week, add credibility but do not drive early revenue. Posts can be scheduled weeks or months ahead, and AI can help draft them. (2025) [L0972.101]

  • Test organic posts first, then move the best performers into paid ads. Running organic posts as ads without adjustment produced no sales in one brand's test over three months. (2025-26) [L1225.055]
  • Share the product images and pins you create for Pinterest on Facebook and other social networks too. Visual content performs well there, so one set of images can serve both platforms. [L1136.035]
  • Keep most reels evergreen, meaning still relevant months later. A common split is about 70 percent evergreen content and about 30 percent trend-based content. (2025-26) [L0796.021]
  • Keep posts professional and straightforward rather than chasing viral content. Announce new authorised supplier relationships, highlight specific products you recommend, or create product spotlights that explain one to three key reasons why customers should benefit from each item. [L0157.003]
  • Use AI to generate 30 weeks of weekly content ideas if you need guidance on post themes. Keep the focus on posts that build business credibility rather than chase viral engagement. [L0157.016]

  • Share diverse content types that resonate with your audience: blogs, recipes, stories, photos, videos and memes. [L0237.008]
  • Start with content that is funny or educational, because humour is the easiest way to earn engagement early. Test formats until one earns shares, and keep making content people want to share. [L0487.039]

Organic social: captions, polls and interaction

  • Write the way you would text a friend, in captions and replies. Humour, voice notes and voiceover make content feel real, and the more casual the tone, the better it resonates. (2025-26) [L0576.004]
  • Questions in captions invite comments and act as free market research, showing what the audience thinks and wants. They also make followers feel more connected to the brand. (2025-26) [L0576.005]
  • Question-and-reply procedure:
    1. End the post caption with an open question that invites an opinion or a verdict. [L0576.006]
    2. Check the comments on the post. [L0576.006]
    3. Reply to each answer, ideally with a follow-up question about what the commenter meant. [L0576.006]
    4. Keep the dialogue open for as long as people keep responding. [L0576.006]
  • Caption question pattern: end with a short, open question that asks for a verdict, an opinion or a shared experience. It gives people a reason to respond and turns the post into a conversation. [L0576.007]
    • Example 1 for a kitchen gadget: 'Did this actually save you time, or is it just a gimmick?' [L0576.007]
    • Example 2 for a skincare brand: 'Have you ever had a breakout before a big event? What helped you?' [L0576.007]
  • Asking a question and then not replying to the answers wastes the engagement the question created. Reply to comments so people see the brand is listening. [L0576.008]
  • Instagram's polls-in-captions feature is newer and actively promoted by the platform, and creators use it to boost engagement. Newer features a platform pushes are generally worth adopting early. (2025-26) [L0576.009]
  • Polls in captions: write the caption first, then add a poll below it. Viewers can vote while they watch the reel, without leaving the caption or writing a comment. (2025-26) [L0576.010]
  • Two-option poll pattern: pose a light, fun question about what the audience prefers next, such as behind-the-scenes content against a tutorial. Keep it quick to answer, so people vote in seconds. A single tap is an easy ask, and the result shows which content the audience wants more of. [L0576.011]
    • Example 1: 'Coffee at home or at the café?' [L0576.011]
    • Example 2: 'Morning routine or packing hacks next?' [L0576.011]
  • Polls can also use emoji voting, where each choice is represented by an emoji. Low-effort formats like polls and emoji votes are the easiest way to start audience engagement. (2025-26) [L0576.012]
  • Instagram Stories interactive stickers include polls, question boxes and quizzes. Use them repeatedly in a recurring series so the audience expects them and returns to the same format. (2025-26) [L0576.013]
  • Emoji sticker on Instagram Stories: place a heart (or another emoji) sticker with a short prompt, for example asking viewers to tap it if they have been following for a while. Tapping sends an immediate reaction back to the story, a one-tap way to take part. [L0576.014]
  • Offer low-barrier ways to take part, such as tapping a sticker, double tapping or dropping one emoji, instead of asking for a full comment or direct message. Lower effort brings more participation. (2025-26) [L0576.015]
  • Add new interactive elements to content followers already know and love, such as the regular weekly stories, so engagement becomes part of an existing habit. [L0576.016]
  • Polls in Instagram captions help the algorithm and give the brand insight into what its audience wants. A poll below the caption is a quick way to get engagement and feedback from the same post. (2025-26) [L0576.027]
  • Pair a quick poll with an open question. Use the poll for a one-tap yes-or-no or either-or choice. Ask a more in-depth open question in the comments for people who want to write more. Low-effort and high-effort participants then have separate ways to respond to the same post. (2025-26) [L0576.028]

Viral follow-up and organic-to-paid

  • Organic video conversion rules differ by stage. Two values apply to two situations:
    • Default organic videos: no explicit buy or shop call to action [L0820.010].
    • After a viral video: the next video can be more product-focused, with a clear call to action and a direct offer, because converting viewers often needs an offer more than entertainment [L0706.044]. Follow-up videos can be more toned down and professional, with more calls to action and some traditional ad tactics [L1077.019].
  • Once one organic video goes viral, the next should be more toned down and professional. Viewers who come from the viral video then see a calmer follow-up, which builds credibility and encourages them to visit the store and buy. [L1077.019]
  • Extend a viral video's effect and turn views into sales:
    1. Upload four to five good-quality videos while the viral effect is still active, spaced about two hours apart. [L0706.045]
    2. Once the viral effect slows, boost the video with TikTok coins. [L0706.045]
    3. Alternatively, run Spark Ads on the video to reach more viewers. [L0706.045]
    4. Use an urgency call to action at the end of new videos to convert viewers. [L0706.045]
  • Case: a slip-resistant slipper product went viral with a video of about 44 million views, posted September 14, with no paid ads. The video quality and audio were poor, but the clip opened with a slipping risk, showed the slippers solving that problem, and repeated the demonstration. The account had uploaded several times a day, and this one video drove most of the result. The store was later no longer available. [L0657.030]
  • Organic-only success can be judged by the absence of repeated ad-style videos or paid-boost patterns on the account. The hosts believed the slipper account was fully organic because no other video showed signs of paid promotion. [L0657.031]

Trend-driven organic content

Viral Reels production and posting

  • Produce and post viral Instagram content for a product. [L1367.009]

    1. Order the product from Amazon to your own address so you can check it firsthand.
    2. Do not post any reels until you have produced 3 weeks' worth of content.
    3. Study the largest pages and videos that made content for your product.
    4. Copy their video style, change elements based on comment feedback, and add something dramatic or crazy.
    5. Produce 2 to 3 videos per day. Make the full 3 weeks of content before posting. After 1 to 2 weeks, raise the daily count by 1 to 2 videos to avoid shadow bans for spam.
    6. Once all 3 weeks of content are ready, move on to the posting routine.
    7. Post on a strict schedule of 2 to 3 times a day for 3 weeks, for example at 2 pm EST, 6 pm EST and 10 pm EST.
    8. After 1 week, change posting times if you spot time-of-day trends.
    9. Post content immediately, sticking to your content strategy.
    10. Use the hashtags with the highest volume and those your competitors use. Write hashtags inside short sentences that describe the audience and the look, for example a shark slides product tagged with a university students group and a fashionable look.
    11. Read the feedback and comments on every video and look for recurring comment themes.
    12. Reply to as many comments on your videos as possible.
    13. Aim for 10k views in the first 5 days to build momentum.
    14. Use boost post on early content to add initial momentum to your account or to gain followers.

Email and SMS copy

  • Email copy and ad copy use the same persuasion mechanisms as product copy, but they are formatted differently. Writers should apply the same building blocks to these formats, adapting length and layout to each. (older, 2021-22) [L1420.010]
  • For welcome emails, the offer must be clear and easy to understand, because strong products sell themselves and a simple discount drives the click. Long product copy is less important than a clear offer line. [L1470.019]

Content mix and formats

  • Content should be produced in a mix of content types rather than one format, and the format itself is not fixed: the same idea can be a blog post or a video. Video content for stores

  • Video content for an online store works best when it is evergreen, meaning it keeps delivering traffic for years after publishing, rather than being posted only for a short social media feed where the algorithm stops showing it after a while.   • Video formats that suit a store include unboxings, how-to videos, listicles, product reviews, customer video reviews and FAQ videos. Unboxing videos filmed from above are a simple format that can attract searches for a popular product. (older, 2021-22) [L1424.005]

  • A reusable content strategy is to write a blog post first and then turn the same content into a video by recording narration that reads the post aloud, publishing the video on the store's content page and on YouTube so both the text and the video can rank and bring traffic.
