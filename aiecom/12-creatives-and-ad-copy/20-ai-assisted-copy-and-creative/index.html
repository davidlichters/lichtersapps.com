Briefing the AI before prompting

  • Before you ask an AI tool for ad copy, know the product's desire, its features and the benefits those features give the customer, the target persona and the problem it solves. The AI only amplifies the inputs it gets. Weak inputs produce weak copy. [L0353.001]
  • Copy generated without product inputs comes out generic. Always give the AI the unique selling points, the target audience and the framework before asking for headlines or scripts. [L0524.060]
  • Generic AI copy written without knowing the target audience stays generic. Give the model the audience, the problem, the market position and the unique features first. Check every output for accuracy before you publish it. [L0552.033]
  • A general chatbot such as ChatGPT, used to write ad copy without best-practice training or explicit instructions, usually gives weak results. Teams that skip this training often decide AI does not work and go back to human copywriters. Write detailed instructions and best-practice rules first, then judge the output. [L0464.024]
  • Keep one brief in a spreadsheet: the desire, features and benefits, persona, awareness and sophistication stages, and principles. All of these feed the headline and body copy. With the brief kept up to date, you can generate Facebook ad copy, landing page copy and video scripts from the same inputs. [L0516.052]
  • Start with one long, complete brief covering the product, desire, features and benefits, audience and chosen principles, ideally dictated by voice. Then refine with a few short corrections. In a worked example, strong copy took about five rounds. The number of prompts is not the goal. [L0353.054]
  • Use short prompts with minimal wording and refine in several rounds. In one comparison, a single long voice prompt followed by a few corrections was faster than many separate prompts. Read this together with the long first brief in L0353.054: the brief comes first, then short corrections. [L0353.039]

Ad copy workflows

Basic AI-assisted ad copy workflow (Source C)

  1. Dictate the product, the problem, the desire, the features and their benefits, and the target audience into the chatbot in one long prompt. [L0353.004]
  2. Ask for three headlines and three body copy texts for the Facebook and Instagram ad. [L0353.004]
  3. Check whether the headline names the problem and hints at the solution. If the market is crowded, ask for the unique feature to appear in the headline. [L0353.004]
  4. Paste the list of psychological principles and ask for the copy to be rewritten with the best ones. [L0353.004]
  5. Ask for bullet points and emojis to format the ad. [L0353.004]
  6. Refine in several short follow-up prompts instead of starting over. [L0353.004]

Paid-plan ChatGPT workflow (Source C)

  1. Describe the product in plain words, its main target audience, the situation the audience struggles with, and its unique features. [L0515.021]
  2. Send the psychological principles, desire, benefits and persona you wrote. [L0515.021]
  3. Ask for three headline examples and body copy. [L0515.021]
  4. Review the output. Remove generic phrases (for example "experience the magic of") and tighten the benefit and the unique mechanism. [L0515.021]
  5. Give the market awareness and sophistication stage with the competitors' claims, and ask for a rewrite that features the unique mechanism in the headline. [L0515.021]
  6. For paid social ads, ask for a shorter version with bullet points, a clear call to action, a limited-time offer and fear of missing out, plus emojis if wanted. [L0515.021]
  7. Ask for variations of a headline using a named principle, for example how to intensify a headline. [L0515.021]

Primary text process (one chat per product)

Generate Facebook ad copy for a product using the following process. Input: the product page URL, the ad creative description (the hook, the visual and the angle), and the brand name. Output: 3 headlines and 3 primary texts. Headline 1 (Problem-Aware): a headline about the offer. Headline 2 (Solution-Aware): a curiosity gap that makes the reader want the mechanism. Headline 3 (Transformation): an identity shift that makes the reader see themselves already using the product. Primary text V1 (Problem-Aware): open with the reader's pain point, show you understand their struggle, hint at what is possible, create curiosity about the solution, plant a seed of urgency, end with a soft call to action; tone empathetic, understanding, hopeful. Primary text V2 (Solution-Aware): acknowledge they have been looking, address why most solutions fail, show what is different about this product, introduce the mechanism, stack proof and credibility, create urgency, end with a confident call to action; tone knowledgeable, trustworthy, confident. Primary text V3 (Transformation and offer): paint a picture of life after using the product, show the exact transformation, state the core benefits, show what is included in the offer, make the offer undeniable, create urgency to act now. Use the current price, original price and discount percentage from the product page, and keep each text short, scannable and free of unsupported claims.

Meta primary text and headline prompt (alternative format)

This prompt sets a stricter layout (12 words per paragraph, four emoji lines). Use it when you want a fixed structure. The process prompt above (L0348.008) produces three headlines and three primary texts with V1 to V3 labels. Use the process prompt for product pages with full offer details, and this prompt for quick high-intent variations.

Act as a direct-response copywriter who focuses on high-converting Meta ads. Your job: review the product or creative you're given, pinpoint its central marketing angle, and then produce three alternative high-intent primary texts plus three headlines.

Input fields: Brand or product name: [Insert brand or product name here]. Audience notes: [Insert any notes about your audience here, or leave blank so the AI infers them]. Creative attached: [Yes or No; refer to the file name if relevant].

Stage 1 - Creative and avatar review. Write a brief analysis in three sections:
1. Marketing angle: the hook (the instant visual, emotional, or vanity payoff) and how it naturally moves the reader toward the functional, long-term, or technical advantage of the product.
2. Avatar: the buyer's core pains and desires, and the precise reason this creative and angle will halt their scrolling.
3. Awareness level: the single main awareness stage this ad aims at (Unaware, Problem Aware, Solution Aware, Product Aware, or Most Aware).

Stage 2 - Primary text. Produce 3 Meta-compliant primary text variations for the brand or product, each following this exact layout:
- Paragraph 1: an arresting hook centered on the main benefit or pain point (12 words at most).
- Paragraph 2: a transition linking the hook to the product's distinctive mechanism, quality, or solution (12 words at most).
- Benefit stack: exactly 4 standalone lines. Every line opens with one fitting emoji, followed by a short, punchy benefit written as [Benefit name].
- Paragraph 3: a clear call to action or a closing value proposition that is hard to dispute (12 words at most).
Hard rules: no paragraph may go over 12 words, so count each paragraph's words before you output it. Keep emojis out of the paragraphs; they belong only at the start of the 4 benefit-stack lines. Do not make any claim the product page fails to back up.

Stage 3 - Headlines. Write 3 headlines: an offer headline aimed at problem-aware readers, a curiosity-gap headline for solution-aware readers, and an identity-shift headline for readers focused on transformation. Where it fits, use the current price, original price, and discount as displayed on the product page.

Deliver the Stage 1 analysis first, then the three primary text variations, then the three headlines.

Principles for AI-assisted creative

  • AI has changed ad creative more than any shift since social media took hold. It lets a team produce ideas, variations and finished assets much faster, with more variety and far less manual effort. [L0498.001]
  • Using AI to generate ads without knowing how to direct it usually produces low-quality ads that perform worse. The tool alone does not create good creative. The operator's strategy decides the result. [L0498.002]
  • Brands that keep winning with AI-assisted creative follow a small set of underlying principles, not a single tool or prompt trick. Understanding those principles lets an advertiser adapt when platforms change their rules. [L0498.004]
  • Input quality decides the result of ChatGPT concept work, and a human must check the output. Overreliance makes the work weaker, so write the core copy yourself and check it against customer pain points. [L0646.042]
  • Adjust copy and creative direction to match your brand voice and the customer psychology insights from your research. [L0221.012]

Ebook generation prompt

Act as a seasoned ebook writer and content strategist. Produce a finished, high-grade ebook of 15 to 25 pages to serve as a free giveaway for [TARGET AVATAR: who they are and the problem they have], linked to [PRODUCT NAME], which assists with [PRODUCT PROBLEM]. Pick a widely applicable, timeless theme suited to this readership, for example productivity, mindset, health routines, personal finance basics, or something similar. Execution counts for more than the subject: the piece should demonstrate what a well-crafted free-gift ebook looks like in practice.

Deliver the complete ebook from start to finish, containing:
- An attention-grabbing title and subtitle
- A short welcome note or author introduction
- 4 to 6 chapters of substantial, truly practical material, with no placeholder text
- Practical takeaways or exercises wherever they fit
- A closing section offering one clear, gentle next step

Quality benchmarks:
- A warm, direct, engaging voice, never stiff or generic
- Brief paragraphs, subheadings, and bullet points where they improve readability
- Each chapter provides genuine value rather than padding
- The tone should read like a thoughtful present, not a sales pitch

Fictional spokesperson for a Facebook whitelisting page

Two versions of the same spokesperson prompt are kept in full.

Act as a top-tier brand strategist for a seven-figure online store. Attached are my customer research files and product materials. Within ten minutes, invent a fictional spokesperson for my Facebook whitelisting page and produce the materials I need to set it up.

Phase 1, review the research: identify the product and the problem it fixes; the core buyer's demographics (age, gender, stage of life, income); who that buyer is, what they want, and what they fear; which kinds of people they already put faith in (a peer, a coach, a parental figure, a quiet expert, a former professional); and the main pain the product tackles. Phase 2, select the archetype: choose the persona category that lines up with what the purchaser already believes in. Possible options (the list is not exhaustive) include a coach, trainer or former athlete for fitness, supplements and performance; a mom or dad of a child for babies, parenting and family; a beauty enthusiast or editor for skincare and cosmetics; a wellness advocate with a nurse-like manner for health and self-care; a pet parent or rescue owner for animal products; a home organizer or 'clean-girl' type for home and cleaning; or a retired professional or hobbyist for products tied to identity. Phase 3, build the character: provide a complete sheet covering a casual, realistic first name; a page title in the style of 'Coach Sam', 'Mom of 3 Kate' or 'Doc Ellie'; an age range; an ethnicity and physical look kept loose enough for image generators; three to five personality words; a personal backstory of two to three sentences explaining why they entered this field and how they lived the problem themselves; and one sentence on what they help people with.

4a. A copy-ready profile picture prompt for ChatGPT, DALL-E or Midjourney. Exclude corporate headshot style, stock-photo polish and over-processed looks. No text overlays, logos or product shots.
4b. A copy-ready cover image prompt. Use a wide 3:1 frame showing a lifestyle setting from the archetype's world (gym, kitchen, park, home office and similar), with a warm, aspirational, editorial mood and a color palette that fits the character's surroundings. 4c. A Facebook page bio of 60 to 100 words in first person. Begin with a hook drawn from the backstory, connect it to the product's problem area, and close with what the person shares or helps with. It should read like a real individual rather than a company, with no hashtags, no emojis and no sales language.

Deliver everything in copy-ready form, in this order: the three-line research recap, the archetype choice with its rationale, the profile picture prompt, the cover picture prompt, and the page bio. Start immediately.

Full workflow: niche to vertical video

Procedure for a talking-character video:

  1. Choose a niche and a specific angle that has little existing content in the target language.
  2. Generate a frontal portrait of the character in Midjourney, create variations, pick the best one and download it.
