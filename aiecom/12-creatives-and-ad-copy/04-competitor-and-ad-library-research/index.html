Finding winners: run time, spend and scale signals

Run time as a shortcut

  • Ads that have run for a long time are usually profitable, so run duration is a useful shortcut for spotting winners. Model the headline, image, video and copy of ads that have been running for months. [L0523.042]
  • Any ad running for months has been validated as a successful winner in the free Meta Ad Library and can be swiped for analysis. [L0039.019, L0039.013]
  • In the Meta Ads Library, the ads that have run longest are usually the ones with the best return on ad spend. Scroll to the bottom of the results to find them. Use them for inspiration on structure and copy, but do not copy them. [L0667.010]
  • Caution: do not assume the longest-running ad is the winner. Some advertisers keep ads running without sales to mislead competitors. Check running days together with duplicates and traffic before copying an ad. [L0374.031]
  • Running time and duplicates together give a stronger signal than either alone. Where an ad appears as many separate variations, treat it as proven. [L0707.001]
  • Look for a creative that runs in many ads at once; the lesson uses 8 or more ads as the scaling signal. [L0398.021]
  • Recent-launch check: the Brands tracker counts new ads per week, and 3 to 5 new ads per week signals active scaling. [L0374.032]

Spend and brand size

  • Prioritize big brands that spend heavily on Facebook, for example around 1 to 5 million USD per month, because they test creative constantly. [L0398.021]
  • Study what large brands run and copy their layout, fonts, colors and ad formats. They have spent heavily on testing, so their proven choices are a useful starting point. [L0692.016, L0646.041]
  • Check the Facebook Ad Library for the ads large brands in your niche keep running, because those brands test heavily and keep what works. Use the winning styles as a starting point, then test them for your own product and niche. [L0646.041]
  • Research direct competitors that sell the same product and, above all, large brands in the niche, because large brands spend heavily on testing creative ideas and marketing angles. Save their ads and landing pages in one sheet for reference. [L0902.011]

Recency and engagement

  • Find competitor creatives from the last two weeks by searching 5 to 10 product keywords and related hashtags on TikTok. Recent videos are less likely to have been shown to your audience. [L1377.027]
  • A good creative to test is one uploaded recently, not yet used by many competitors, and already getting a good like rate on PP Ads. A recent competitor video with a strong like rate shows real engagement. [L1382.022]
  • On TikTok, compare a creative's likes with its ad impressions. Strong impressions and a good like count mean the creative resonates and is worth replicating. [L0895.033]
  • Kalodata's Video & Ad list shows each ad with revenue, view count and post date. Use these figures to pick hooks from ads that already sold, not ads picked at random. It has an Export button for the list. [L0901.024]

Stopped ads

  • If a competitor stopped a creative for a product, the cause may be account issues rather than poor performance. You can still test the product, but check results after a few days and change the creative, offer or market if sales do not come. [L1219.048]

Searching the Meta Ad Library step by step

Competitor research in the Ad Library

  1. Open the competitor's Facebook page. [L0649.063]
  2. In the right column, click Page transparency. [L0649.063]
  3. Click See more to view the page creation date, name changes and the number of people managing the page. [L0649.063]
  4. Click the Ad Library link, or the button to see all ads, to view active and past ads. [L0649.063]
  5. Check the start date, format and copy of each ad, and switch the country to see regional ads. [L0649.063]
  6. Revisit the page regularly to see which ads they keep running. [L0649.063]

Finding and replicating winning creatives

  1. Open the Meta Ad Library and search your niche and the competitors and large brands in it. [L0398.021]
  2. Prioritize big brands that spend heavily on Facebook (see spend guidance above). [L0398.021]
  3. Look for creatives that run in many ads at once; 8 or more ads is the scaling signal used in the lesson. [L0398.021]
  4. Compare the creatives of the same brand and note the hook that repeats in the first seconds while the later clips change. [L0398.021]
  5. Replicate the repeated hook in your own ads and test it against your own video. [L0398.021]

Using the Ad Library for wording and downloads

  • Search the Facebook Ad Library for the niche, such as home decor, to find wording ideas for primary text and headlines. [L0642.051]
  • Search the product name in the Meta Ad Library with All ads selected, Active status filter on and country set to United States. [L0707.001]
  • Check whether the same video runs many times as separate ad variations, since many active variations signal a proven ad. [L0707.001]
  • To download a video from the Ad Library: play the ad, right-click it, choose Save video as, and type the file name with the extension.mp4. If the file is saved with another extension, download it and rename the extension to.mp4 so it opens as a video. [L0638.045]
  • Meta Ad Library lets you download a competitor's ad creative for review. Save the files and study the hook, pacing and format before building your own test version. Do not copy the footage itself. [L0335.063]
  • Download the video creatives of shortlisted products from the Meta Ads Library and save them in the shared research folder. Use them as a reference when briefing your own test ads, so the team works from the same examples. [L1173.046]

AdvertSuite ad research (5 steps)

  1. Define a S.M.A.R.T. objective for what ad types you want to find and why. [L0196.003]
  2. Select the 'Keywords' search mode and choose 'Ad Text', 'Comments', or 'Landing Page Text' as the search field. Filter by country, date range, ad position, demographics and technology if needed. Sort by 'Longest Running' or by engagement (likes, comments, shares). [L0196.003, L0196.055]
  3. Start with broad keywords related to your industry, then add filters. Examine the results to see what is working now. [L0196.003]
  4. Capture each ad in a Trello board: [L0196.003, L0196.056]
    • screenshot the full ad from source;
    • copy the text to a Google Doc and link it;
    • add a link to the landing page;
    • screenshot the landing page (use FireShot Chrome extension for long pages) [L0196.057];
    • screenshot the AdvertSuite details page;
    • capture relevant comments.
  5. Analyze each ad by asking 30 questions, including: who the advertiser targets, what the short-term goal is, which features and benefits are shared, what the core attention grabber is, what mood is conveyed, which objections are addressed, and which calls to action are used. [L0196.003]

Trello as the research database

  • Create columns (lists) by keyword or category. Add cards with ad details, labels (platform, ad type), description (AdvertSuite search settings) and comments. [L0196.056]
  • AdvertSuite lets you filter by country, date range, ad position (newsfeed vs. sidebar), gender, language, age and technology used. [L0196.055]
  • FireShot is a Chrome extension that takes full-page screenshots of pages longer than the screen, used to capture complete landing pages for analysis. [L0196.057]

Tracking competitors in the brand tracker

What the tracker shows

  • A tracked brand's detail page has these tabs: Overview, Ad Copies, Ad Headlines, Ad Hooks, Landing Pages, Personas, Concepts, Angles, Desires, Emotions, Awareness Stages and USPs. Each tab breaks down that element across the brand's ads, so you can check which hooks, angles or desires a competitor uses most. [L0772.044, L0892.014]
  • Together these tabs show how a competitor is positioning and selling right now, based on its live ads. [L0892.014]
  • A competitor's top personas, concepts, angles, USPs, emotions and desires come from its live ads, so they show what it is actually running. Copying or varying the strongest of these is faster than guessing the strategy. [L0892.015]
  • The tracker's concept categories are Demo / How-It Works, Before-And-After, Educational Explainer, Social Proof / Reviews, Promotion / Discount, Problem-Agitate-Solve, Bundle / Multi-Pack Offer and Social Proof Emphasis. Check which of these a competitor scales most. [L0374.011]
  • The tracker's angle categories in the example included Problem-Agitate-Solve, Customer Before-And-After Reveal, At-Home Microneedling Substitute, Budget Comparison, Comparison Vs Microneedling, Side-By-Side Comparison and After Demonstration. Each angle is tracked with the number of ads that use it. [L0374.012]
  • In the awareness-stage view, the sort control offers Usage over 7 days or 30 days and Longest Running. Hooks and landing pages that appear across many ads or stay live for months are the strongest candidates to study first. [L0892.037]

Procedure: analyse a competitor brand

  1. Open the brand and read the overview cards: top landing pages, personas, concepts, angles, desires, emotions, awareness stages and USPs. [L0374.032]
  2. Count the new ads uploaded each week; 3 to 5 new ads per week signals active scaling. [L0374.032]
  3. Check ad attribution by format; if almost all ads are video, the brand is scaling video. [L0374.032]
  4. Open the Ad Copies tab and identify the copy with the most attributed ads; that is the main winning copy. Then check the headlines and hooks. [L0374.032]
  5. Open the Landing Pages tab, note the number of pages and the share of ads sent to each. [L0374.032]
  6. Open Personas and Concepts to see which audiences and formats the brand uses. [L0374.032]
  7. Open Angles, Desires, Emotions, Awareness Stages and USPs. [L0374.032]
  8. Open the top ads, read the ad details (running days, duplicates, landing page, price) and generate the transcript. [L0374.032]
  9. Download the top video for frame-by-frame analysis. [L0374.032]

Reading a top ad's transcript

  • In a tracked brand's overview, opening a video ad from the list gives you its transcript. Read the spoken hook and script word for word before rebuilding a similar ad. [L0772.045]
  • Video ads in a brand's list can be opened for transcripts, speakable scripts, and framework or pattern analysis. Use these to see how a competitor's ad is structured before writing your own. [L0892.017]

Export hooks and generate variations

  1. Open the brand and go to the Ad Hooks tab. [L0892.016]
  2. Click Export to download the hooks as a CSV file. [L0892.016]
  3. Import the CSV into an AI tool. [L0892.016]
  4. Ask the AI for more hooks similar to the exported ones. [L0892.016]
  • Pro tip: exporting top hooks, personas and angles from a brand and feeding them into an AI tool such as Claude can surface further personas and angles beyond those the tool lists. [L0892.018]

Worked case: acne-scar serum brand

  • Case: an acne-scar serum brand was on the tracker with 133 active ads, tracked since 6 May 2026, and had been added 22 hours before the data was pulled, so counts were still filling in. [L0374.041]
  • It uploaded 3 to 5 new ads per week, attributed almost only to video, with no image or carousel ads. [L0374.041]
  • 133 ads ran the same main ad copy, whose headline reached 23,000+ people. [L0374.041]
  • The top persona was people with acne scars, followed by budget-conscious skincare shoppers and adults with acne scars. [L0374.041]
  • The top desire was fading acne scars, the top emotions were hope and relief, and the top USPs were a 90-day money-back guarantee, a microcrystal technology as the main mechanism, and bundle pricing under $40. [L0374.041]
  • Traffic went almost only to product pages: 125 ads (81.2%) to the main product page and 8 ads (5.2%) to a second page, with no listicles, advertorials or pre-sell pages. [L0374.041]
  • The analysis showed the brand lagging on its funnel, so a similar product with a stronger funnel and a better offer could compete. [L0374.041]
  • The top video was downloaded, transcribed, analysed frame by frame in Gemini, and its script adapted to a new product. [L0374.041]

Worked example: supplement brand

  • A supplement brand with 67 active ads had Promotion / Discount as its top concept, Fast and Free Shipping as its top USP, and a limited-time deal as its top desire. [L0892.039]
  • This combination shows a brand that relies on urgency and price incentives. It can be copied directly or varied with a different angle. [L0892.039]

Reverse-engineering a winning video in Kalodata

Procedure

  1. In Kalodata, open the product page and set the region to United States and the date range to Last 30 Days. [L0707.001]
  2. Check that the product sells at scale in Core Metrics: Main Revenue, Item Sold and Avg. Unit Price. [L0707.001]
  3. Open the Video & Ad tab and pick the video ad with the highest views and revenue in the period. [L0707.001]
  4. Watch the video and note the hook, the problem named, the testimonial structure, the solution, the benefits, the social proof and the call to action. [L0707.001]
  5. Search the product name in the Meta Ad Library (see Ad Library settings above). [L0707.001]
  6. Check whether the same video runs many times as separate ad variations. [L0707.001]
  7. Use the findings to build your own version of the structure, not a copy of the video. [L0707.001]

Reference numbers

  • Winning reference ad: about 8 million views and about 165k revenue in the last 30 days, with the video running about 1 minute 57 seconds on the player. [L0707.006]
  • Kalodata's Video & Ad view lists competitor video ads for a product, and the Storyboard Export lets you export an ad's storyboard for analysis. Highest Revenue Videos and their conversion rates show which ad creatives generate the most sales. [L1187.024]
  • Good ad editing and detailed bottom-funnel creatives indicate a product's ads are worth studying and possibly copying in a customized form. Look for creatives that show the product in use or in a result-focused way. [L1187.023]

Worked case: calcium multi-balm stick

  • Case: a Korean calcium multi-balm stick, sold in the United States and on TikTok Shop, reached about 50k revenue per day and about 1.5 million in the last 30 days. [L0707.020]
  • Its best ad, a phone-filmed creator testimonial, reached about 8 million views and about 165k revenue in the same period. [L0707.020]
  • The ad ran about 1 minute 57 seconds and opened with a woman naming the product as a Botox-like balm to stop the scroll. [L0707.020]
  • She then showed a before and after image, named smoker lines and under-eye darkness, told a story about an aunt who smoked for over 20 years, and added her own insecurity. [L0707.020]
  • She then claimed she is a nurse, listed adenosine, glutathione and a patented calcium-vitamin D ingredient with about ten benefits in a row, cited five-star reviews and a four-week wait from stock shortage, and ended with urgency to act fast. [L0707.020]
  • The same video ran as a repeated Meta ad, with about 6,500 keyword results in the Ad Library. [L0707.020]

Selecting and editing test videos from Kalodata

Procedure

  1. Open the Video & Ad list in Kalodata and set the time filter to Last 30 Days. [L0704.001]
  2. Look for videos with high view counts and strong sales, which signal a viral, trending ad. [L0704.001]
  3. Choose 12 to 24 videos for a test, starting with at least 12. [L0704.001]
  4. Take half of the selected videos (about six) from page 1 and the rest from pages 2 and 3. [L0704.001, L0704.012]
  5. Keep only videos that show a real person; drop abstract or product-only videos. [L0704.001]
  6. Drop videos whose hook or demonstrated outcome does not match what your product delivers, even if the hook itself is strong. [L0704.001]
  7. Drop videos whose spoken language is not English, since you cannot check or match their message to your store. [L0704.001, L0704.011]
  8. Do not spend much time judging production quality; the sales data is the main signal. [L0704.001, L0704.005]
  9. In CapCut, cut every part that mentions TikTok Shop or Orange Cart. [L0704.001]
  10. Cut every offer that is not on your landing page, including buy one get one free if your website does not show it. [L0704.001]
  11. Make no other changes: add no new hooks and no extra edits to videos that already sell. [L0704.001]

Why this selection works

  • Do not judge a video mainly by how polished it looks. If a video shows strong sales, it is already working, so its quality matters less than its results. [L0704.005]
  • Pulling some winners from deeper result pages targets ads that fewer competitors are looking at, which gives the test fresher material than only the top results. [L0704.013]
  • Spending a long time deciding whether a video is good wastes time. Use the view and sales data first and sort the videos on that basis. [L0704.015]
  • Kalodata's result list offers a Download Video (Without Watermark) option, which gives a clean file to edit before running the ad. [L0704.017]

Analysing the psychology of winning ads

Prompt: psychological pattern analysis of a swipe file

Use this prompt unchanged when you have a collection of successful ads:

When you receive a collection of successful ads, study each one to uncover the psychological pattern underneath the wording. For every ad, identify which emotion it activates (fear or desire), the core logic that makes its promise credible, the specific opening technique that halts scrolling, and the sequence of beats it follows. Then group the ads by shared emotional target and persuasion approach, identify fears the avatar likely holds that none of these ads address, and classify where the audience sits on Schwartz's five-stage awareness model (Unaware through Most Aware).

[L0039.003]

Swipe file size

  • Gather 5 to 10 winning ad scripts to analyze as your swipe file. If using Kalodata, take the top performers. If using the free Meta Ad Library, any ad running for months is validated as a proven winner. [L0039.013]

Ad style analysis

  • Always study what competitors are doing in their ad copy and improve on what is already working. Use competitive copywriting as inspiration for your own differentiation, not as a template to replicate. [L0206.005]
  • Ad style analysis compares each pair of styles with at least 10 examples each for the first pair, and tracks pattern changes over time. Pairs: [L0613.016]
    • professional vs user-generated content;
    • emotional vs logical appeals;
    • benefit-focused (bright side) vs problem-focused (dark side);
    • simple vs complex explanation;
    • short-form vs long-form;
    • serious vs humorous tone;
    • male vs female presenters;
    • solo presenter vs group;
    • studio vs real-world setting;
    • text-heavy vs image-focused;
    • technical vs lifestyle framing;
    • identity-led vs benefit-led.
  • Also note outliers that succeed despite going against the trend. [L0613.016]
  • Model proven landing pages and ads instead of inventing new ones and hoping they work. Find what competitors already run, such as opt-in pages, sales pages or quiz pages, and build something similar before testing refinements. Following a proven model saves a lot of time and money. [L0523.043]
  • Copying one source closely is imitation that reads as a knockoff, while borrowing from many sources is research that produces something new. Copy the reasoning behind a style, such as why an ad hook works for its audience, not just its surface look. [L1343.003]
  • Copying a competitor's ad without understanding why it works, and without setting expectations, is a common cause of failure. Competitor research is useful only to extract transferable lessons, not to clone their ads. [L1348.002]

Analysing hooks, visuals and comments

Hooks and visuals

  1. Analyze 20 to 25 of the most successful hooks from TikTok, Instagram and Facebook ads, identify the patterns that work most often, and note which hooks have run longest. [L0613.011]
  2. Analyze visuals: picture of the benefit, picture of the problem, picture of the product, product in action, environments, people shown, what visuals create belief, production quality (professional vs authentic UGC), color schemes and text overlay styles. [L0613.011]
  3. Analyze at least 100 comments on viral posts, grouped by questions, objections, enthusiasm and testimonials. Note sentiment, 'wish I had known' and 'I need this because' comments, tagging behavior, and identity-resonance comments. [L0613.011]
  4. Study emotional storytelling: transformation versus feature-benefit ads, character arcs, before-state portrayal, vulnerability moments, and at least 10 ads that tap identity-level emotion. [L0613.011]
  5. Map content structures (see table below) and note the balance of emotion versus logic, typical ad length, objection handling and narrative patterns. [L0613.011, L0613.013]

Problem and solution messaging

  • Problem-and-solution messaging on the product page and in the ads is an effective way to explain why a product is worth buying. Check how a competitor presents the problem and solution before writing your own. [L1172.044]
  • Competitor analysis on the landing page: [L0708.039]
    1. Open each competitor landing page you found.
    2. Note the marketing angles, the copywriting and the offers they use.
    3. Identify weaknesses such as brand colors or layout that you could improve.
    4. Use these findings to create a stronger version and beat the competition.

Interrupt-style ad models

Collect interrupt-style ad models:

  1. Search Google Images for tabloid newspaper headlines. [L0523.033]
  2. Note the unusual or shocking images used to grab attention. [L0523.033]
  3. Note how headlines stay short and make the reader curious. [L0523.033]
  4. Adapt these patterns to your own product and audience in your ads. [L0523.033]

Finding gaps and building differentiation

  • Gaps in your analysis are fears or mechanisms that the avatar likely has but that none of the winning competitor ads address. These are white-space openings worth testing. [L0039.034]
  • Identifying gaps means recognizing unaddressed emotional pain, unmet anxieties or unstated objections in the customer data that the swipe file does not hit. These signal potential differentiation angles. [L0039.034]
  • Before analyzing your audience, do competitor creative research first. Understanding how competitors frame problems and structure messaging simplifies the audience investigation, because you already know which angles and language resonate in your market. [L0258.027]
  • Competitor creative research process: [L0258.005]
    1. Identify your 3 most important competitors.
    2. For each competitor, find their 3 most commonly used creative types (UGC, lifestyle images, high-production video, memes, etc.).
    3. For each creative type, document: the concept or angle (testimonial, unboxing, advertorial, problem-solution), hooks used in the first 3 seconds, problems and desires addressed, benefits, features, pain points, objections overcome, target character attributes and age, scenery, and slang or buzzwords.
  • Start every market analysis by checking how many similar products have already advertised to the same prospects. Competitors' ads are the best evidence; a few hours of research should gather samples of every competing ad. [L0330.048]
  • For a new competitor that scales, the ad is usually the main reason it works, not the website. The product matters most, but the ad hook and visual approach are what sell it. [L0335.044]
  • Afterlip shows trending products and features beyond Dropispy. Check that the products found are not oversaturated before testing them. [L0349.013]
  • Review competitors' Facebook Ad Library and identify the ads they run for a long time, because repeated ads signal winners. Analyse their storyline and marketing angle, then replicate or adapt that angle for your own product. [L0902.016]

TikTok research

Finding a winning hook on TikTok

  1. Open the TikTok Creative Center. [L1374.006]
  2. Select your niche. [L1374.006]
  3. Look for products being sold with paid traffic in that niche. [L1374.006]
  4. Identify the hook, structure, and creative style that repeat across the advertisers that appear to be working. [L1374.006]
  5. Replicate the same structure and hook with your own product, adapting sound, visuals, and presentation. [L1374.006]

Value and aspiration

  • Competitor analysis for TikTok means identifying what value competitors offer their audience through education, entertainment or aspiration, and then providing that same kind of value. Aspiration works through people wanting to be like a respected figure who uses the product. It is typically used in influencer-led categories such as beauty and cosmetics. [L1374.007]
  • Study TikTok videos of similar products, log why they work, then reproduce the style rather than copying a single post. [L0561.041]

Researching before filming

  1. Search for other sellers of the same product. [L1381.036]
  2. Find videos for that product with decent views. [L1381.036]
  3. Study how they made theirs, including their text and sound, and replicate the approach in your own videos. [L1381.036]
  4. Save viral sounds to favorites for later use. [L1381.036]

Idea boards and inspiration in the editing app

Swipe files and boards

What a swipe file is

  • A swipe file is a digital or analog archive of ads, creatives, headlines, page layouts and other material you have saved from others. Open it when you need ideas and reuse the patterns, not the exact content. Photos taken with a phone are enough to build one. [L1343.006]
  • Keep a swipe file of brands that inspire you and review it regularly, for example every other day. This shows how similar products are marketed in your niche. [L1194.007]

Building a swipe file

  1. When you see an ad, landing page or creative worth reusing, save it to your swipe file immediately. [L1343.007]
  2. Keep the file in a form you actually use, digital or analog, and add to it regularly. [L1343.007]
  3. When you need inspiration for a new ad or creative, open the file and look for patterns that match your product and audience. [L1343.007]
  4. Adapt the pattern to your own offer rather than copying it directly. [L1343.007]

Boards in Foreplay and similar tools

  • Foreplay is a swipe file tool where brands save ads. Use it to collect proven examples, such as Black Friday ads from e-commerce brands, before producing your own creatives. [L0692.050]
  • Foreplay is a paid ad inspiration tool, about 50 USD per month. It saves ads from the Facebook Ad Library into boards through a Chrome extension. It works as a mood board of ideas rather than a performance tracker, because it does not show metrics. [L1348.010]
  • Collect ad inspiration in this order: [L1348.011]
    1. Find ads in the Facebook Ad Library and check how many days each has been running, as longer-running ads suggest performance.
    2. Save ads that look like good performers or show good design practice into a board in an ad collection tool such as Foreplay, using its Chrome extension.
    3. Follow industry experts who post their ads and build a swipe file from them.
    4. Analyze saved ads for the psychological principle, layout and spacing that make them work.

Reverse-engineering a saved ad

  1. Pick a product, ad or brand you admire. [L1343.035]
  2. Write down what it leaves out or could have done better. [L1343.035]
  3. Create your own version that fills one of those gaps. [L1343.035]
  4. Compare your version with the original to see which choices are your own tendencies. [L1343.035]

Automating competitive ad intelligence

  • Set a trigger for when a competitor uploads a new ad to their ad library. The automation downloads the ad, optionally uses an AI step to transcribe it, feeds it to Claude to rewrite it for your brand's voice and offer, and saves the adapted version to Google Drive or Google Docs. This builds a continuously updated competitive creative reference library with zero manual effort. [L0287.018]

Older advice (2018)

  • Older advice (2018): competitor promo text, such as an offer of free shipping, and better product images can lift CTR. Test similar promo text or images if you can afford the offer. [L1274.067]
