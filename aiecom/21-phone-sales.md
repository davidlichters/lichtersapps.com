# 21. Phone sales

## Deciding whether phone sales belong in your store

### Price bands where calls matter

| Price level | What to expect | Source ids |
|---|---|---|
| Up to about $1,000 without phone capacity | Keep the average price in a lower range, around $1,000. This is older advice from 2019 and 2021. | L0356.010, L1112.071 |
| About $1,000 to $2,000 | Products generally convert with phone support, so have a team or staff ready to answer or return calls quickly. | L0667.073 |
| About $1,000 to $2,000 (listing) | High-ticket items above this range are worth listing, because a short phone call can close the sale. | L0934.108 |
| Above about $1,500 to $3,000 | Expect many inbound calls that check the business is legitimate before buyers order. | L0667.073 |
| Above roughly $2,000 | Many buyers call before ordering, so expect inbound sales calls. List these products and return calls quickly. | L1081.018, L0934.184 |

- The sources give different thresholds, so apply the one that matches your situation. The 2025 source puts conversion with phone support at $1,000 to $2,000 and expects heavy inbound calls above $1,500 to $3,000 [L0667.073]. The 2023 sources put the listing threshold at $1,000 to $2,000 [L0934.108] and at roughly $2,000 [L0934.184]. Older advice from 2019 and 2021 points to around $1,000 as the ceiling without phone support [L0356.010, L1112.071].
- Use the lower end of each range if you are unsure whether you can handle calls, and the higher end only when you have capacity to answer them [L0667.073, L0934.108, L0934.184].

### Phone as a trust signal

- A visible phone number tells customers they could call if needed, even when few customers actually call [L0359.105].
- Phone calls matter for trust even when few customers call, and the few who call usually buy [L0359.105].
- Keep a working phone number on the store and answer customer calls promptly, because phone responsiveness affects conversions for higher-priced items. This is older advice from 2020 [L0468.176].

### Inbound calls as a buying signal

- A phone call on the store is usually a buying signal, not a waste of time [L0910.007].
- Over about 2.5 years, more than 90 percent of calls either led to a sale or came from existing customers [L0910.007].
- Many shoppers call a store before buying, and those who get an answer tend to buy from that store [L0966.095].
- Answering the phone quickly and reliably is a competitive advantage [L0966.095].
- Answering the phone or returning calls generates significant sales volume that competitors miss. Customers are often surprised that a human answers and frequently place orders as a result. For established businesses, this represents millions in annual revenue [L0251.027].
- Phone calls before purchase are a normal part of selling products above about $1,500 to $3,000, because buyers check that the business is real [L0667.074].
- Calls decrease as the store builds reviews and credibility over time [L0667.074].

### Capacity you need before taking calls

- A store with high-priced products needs someone who can answer customer phone calls. Without that capacity, keep the average price in a lower range [L0356.010].
- For high-ticket products, the store owner should be willing to handle phone calls and extra emails, since that is part of how higher order values are closed [L0359.106].
- Only enter the $1,500 to $3,000 price range if you are willing to take these calls [L0667.074].
- Return calls from high-ticket buyers quickly [L0934.108, L0934.184].
- Respond to or call back high-ticket callers quickly [L0934.108].

### High-ticket offers and live selling

- Changing the selling environment is needed for high-ticket offers, because few people will buy a very expensive product from a sales letter alone [L0523.064].
- Move high-ticket offers to a phone call, direct mail or a live event [L0523.064].
- An 8,000 USD buy button is unlikely to be clicked from an email, while a booked phone appointment makes the sale far more likely [L0523.064].
- A phone conversation gives live feedback, so the salesperson can answer objections and help the prospect decide on the spot [L0523.065].
- Listeners pay closer attention to a voice than to a page [L0523.065].
- Use phone sales for offers that are too costly to close online [L0523.065].

### Decision: phone work or lower prices

1. Check whether you can answer and return calls quickly and reliably. If yes, you can consider products in the higher bands above $1,000 [L0667.073, L0966.095, L1112.071].
2. If you cannot, keep prices in the lower band around $1,000 and do not move into high-ticket niches [L0356.010, L1112.071].
3. If you want a hands-off business with no phone sales, do not pick niches with an average price above roughly $2,000 [L1081.018].
4. If you enter the $1,500 to $3,000 range, expect calls from buyers checking legitimacy and accept that they decline as reviews grow [L0667.073, L0667.074].

## Choosing a business phone number and provider

### Why a separate business line

- Business numbers signal professionalism to suppliers and customers and keep business calls apart from personal calls. [L0122.001, L0664.084]
- A dedicated business number receives less spam than a personal cell number, and business calls are easier to recognise and prioritise. [L0122.002]
- Use a business phone line and business address on the store, not personal details. A business line also allows a phone team to handle inbound and outbound calls. [L0852.111]
- Each store needs one phone number that belongs only to the business. Show it on the contact page or in the header. [L1092.088]

### Toll-free numbers

#### Prefixes and rules

| Country | Prefix | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| US | 800, 888, 877, 866, 855, 844, 833 | Each prefix is assigned by a Responsible Organization (RespOrg) under FCC rules. [L1198.004] |
| Australia | 1300 | Equivalent of the US toll-free number. [L1198.003] |

- Toll-free numbers in the formats 800, 855 or 866 look more professional on store websites and work especially well for supplier-facing stores. [L0122.006]
- Older advice (2019) lists 888 or 855 for customer calls. A phone line may be a Google number or a toll-free number. [L0960.043] *(older advice, 2019)*
- For callers in the same country, a toll-free number is free. International callers pay charges. [L1198.003]
- The provider forwards calls to any mobile or landline you choose, so callers hear an ordinary phone call. [L1198.003]
- Toll-free numbers are portable. You can move one to a different provider without changing the number. [L1198.004]

#### Vanity numbers

- Do not pay extra for a memorable or vanity number if the number only appears on your website and in supplier emails. The digits after the prefix do not matter for this use, and a plain number with a toll-free prefix looks just as professional. [L1198.010]

## Setting up the line: voicemail, greetings, hours and routing

### Routing calls by time of day

- The phone number is directed to different people at different times of the day, based on the business time zone. [L0404.004]

### Overflow to voicemail or personal phone

- Unanswered calls overflow automatically to voicemail or the owner's personal phone, so no manual switching is needed. [L0404.004]
- Staff must check voicemail regularly during their answering periods. [L0404.004]

### After-hours handling

- Outside advertised business hours, the phone line plays a prerecorded message and goes to voicemail. [L0404.005]
- Voicemail left after hours is checked in the next business period. [L0404.005]
- Alternatively, outside advertised business hours the call diverts to the owner's personal phone. [L0404.005]

### Staff answering rules

- Staff must always state their first name and the business name when answering a call. [L0404.005]

### Ringing several devices

- Google Voice can be installed on multiple phones logged into the same Google account so that calls ring on several devices. [L0971.073]

### Answering service

- Older advice (Source B, 2021-22): Most number providers offer an answering service. When a call is missed, it plays a pre-recorded greeting that names your business and asks for a message. The service then notifies you of the message by email or app so you can call back as soon as possible. [L1198.005]

### Voicemail transcripts

- Quo's automatic voicemail transcripts convert incoming messages to text, enabling faster responses to customer and supplier inquiries without listening to full recordings. [L0122.007]
- Example (Quo Starter plan): The Quo Starter plan includes voicemail transcripts, which turn each voicemail into text. A caller asking whether a product is in stock can be answered from the transcript later, so a sale is not lost while the owner is busy. [L0338.007]

## Showing the number and using phone calls to action

### Why phone contact matters

- For expensive products, a phone-based call to action can convert better than the buy-now button for many buyers, because they want to speak to a sales or customer service person before committing. Test "Call today" with the phone number in the copy against the buy-now button. Keep the buy-now button as the default option. [L0461.014]
- Business buyers often want to call before they order, to confirm stock or negotiate a volume discount. If you sell B2B, put a phone number on your website. [L0769.006]
- Visitors who call before ordering are usually close to buying. Leaving the number off can lower conversion. [L1088.085]
- A visible phone number builds trust, and callers are often ready to buy. [L0960.042]
- Older advice (2019 to 2022): Source A (2019) recommends a phone number at any price point, including products with an average order value around $500. Source B (2021 to 2022) limits the rule to expensive products. Use both: for high-ticket products, show the number prominently; for lower-priced products, the number still builds trust, so it does no harm to include it. [L0960.042, L0461.014, L1371.038]

### Showing the number on the site

#### Header and contact page

- Show a business phone number in the website header with a "Call us today" heading. High-ticket buyers want to speak to someone, and Google Shopping and similar marketing channels require a visible phone number. [L1371.038]
- Keep the phone number on the website even if you cannot answer every call. [L0934.107]

#### Trade (B2B) page

- B2B buyers usually want to talk on the phone and ask for bulk pricing. Make a phone contact visible on the trade page. [L0961.016]

### Handling calls you cannot answer

Follow these steps when the store cannot answer every call:

1. Keep the phone number on the website. [L0934.107]
2. Set up a voicemail greeting that collects the caller's details. [L0934.107]
3. Return every missed phone call on the same day. [L0961.016]
4. Outsource phone support later, when the store earns enough to justify it. [L0934.107]

### Offering a choice of channels

- When customers can choose between writing, calling or visiting a store, the phone option is the one most people use. In one test, 70 percent of inquiries for a sample came by telephone. [L1261.052]
- Offer the phone option as a primary response channel, since people find it easier than writing. [L1261.052]

### Testing phone-based calls to action

| Element | Control | Test variant |
|---|---|---|
| Call to action | Buy-now button (default, stays) | "Call today" with phone number in the copy |
| Product type | Expensive products | Test first here [L0461.014] |

Run the test on expensive products first. Keep the buy-now button as the default option whatever the result. [L0461.014]

## Answering inbound calls

### Greeting and first contact

- Answer every inbound call with a person, not a machine. Do not use AI to answer sales calls. A person should answer, even if they do not know every product answer right away. [L0808.039]
- Use this phone greeting, adjusting the wording to match how formal your company is. Use the same structure for opening chats, and make sure the staff member knows the next question to ask after the greeting. [L1396.009]

```
Hello, thank you for calling [company name], this is [your name]. How can I help you today?
```
[L1396.009]

- Staff with little phone experience should practice this greeting until it feels natural. [L1396.009]

### Understanding what callers want

- Inbound callers usually want to confirm that the business is legitimate, or check whether a product is in stock before ordering. The phone line therefore also answers pre-purchase questions. [L0700.003]
- Most incoming calls concern stock or shipping times. The real question is usually whether the store can be trusted. [L0910.008]
- Most early calls to a store are simple checks, such as "is this a real business" or "is this product in stock". [L1092.089]
- Answers should reassure the caller that a real person will help if something goes wrong. [L0910.008]

### Building trust on the call

- A phone number builds trust, and calls often convert to sales. [L1092.089]
- Ask what the caller's specific concerns are first. [L0253.049]

Trust-concern script (Source A, 2024, older advice):

1. Ask what their specific concerns are.
2. Respond: "We are an authorized retailer for [Brand Name]. We have been since [year]."
3. Ask which product they're interested in.
4. Say: "Our website is hosted on Shopify. All of your payment information is completely secure."
5. Offer: "You can also purchase directly on our website without speaking to us if you prefer. Or, if you ever have questions, you can reach me directly at [your email]."
6. Your goal: build confidence by proving you are legitimate and authorized. Most callers are satisfied once someone picks up the phone.

[L0253.049]

### Answering questions about physical locations

- If a customer asks whether they can visit a store or pick up in person, answer as follows (Source A, 2022, older advice):

```
We do not have physical locations. We ship directly from our suppliers' warehouses to customers, and those warehouses are not open to the public.
```
[L0803.032]

### Handling missed calls and voicemail

- Calls to a store should always be answered by someone who can handle the common questions, because an unanswered call can mean a lost sale. [L0910.013]
- Check voicemail and return calls promptly. [L0910.013]

### Treating callers as real prospects

- Treat each inquiry as a real prospect of a salesperson, and give them the full story rather than cutting the conversation short. [L1261.054]
- A prospect who has already shown interest is worth more effort than a cold one. [L1261.054]

## Callbacks, unknown answers and call logging

### Promising accurate callbacks instead of guessing

- When you do not know a product detail, do not guess. Tell the customer you will check with the manufacturer team and call back with accurate data. [L0934.105]
- Answer unknown technical questions by promising a callback with accurate data, not by inventing an answer. This keeps trust and lets you collect the caller's contact details for follow-up. [L0934.185]
- A correct answer given hours later is better than an instant guess or no answer. Callers accept a follow-up within about 12 hours when they receive a correct answer. [L0808.041]

### Collecting caller details

Three phrasings for the moment you cannot answer at once. Each is a complete script from a different year.

Source A, 2026:

```
Thank you for calling [Company Name], this is [Your Name]. That is a great question, and I want to make sure I give you the correct answer. Could I get your name, phone number and email address? I will get back to you with the answer as soon as possible. [Optional: I will follow up by [email/text] within [timeframe].]
```

[L0808.040]

Source A, 2024:

```
Thank you for calling, that is a great question and I do not have the answer right now. Let me contact our supplier and confirm the answer with you, and I will get back to you as soon as possible. Can I have your name, phone number and email address, please?
```

[L0805.067]

Source A, 2023:

```
Good question, [customer name]. I don't know the answer at the moment, so I'll consult our team and return your call as quickly as possible. May I have your phone number and email address?
```

[L0934.104]

### Handling unknown product questions

Pre-sales product question, 2024 procedure (Source A):

1. Ask for the customer's name, phone number and email.
2. Tell them you will check and call them back.
3. Call the brand contact about the product detail.
4. Call the customer back with the answer.
5. Send the answer by email and add the new detail to the product page.

[L0971.042]

Phone first, email second: call suppliers for an answer and follow up by email only if they do not answer. [L0971.043]

Phone or chat question you cannot answer, 2020 procedure (Source A, *older advice*):

1. Take the caller's name and phone number or email address.
2. Tell them you don't know yet, but will find the answer and follow up as soon as possible.
3. Ask the supplier the question.
4. Add the answer to the website, so future visitors also see it.
5. Contact the lead with the answer and follow up for the sale.

[L1277.113]

Note: the 2020 version does not name a channel for asking the supplier. The 2024 version calls the brand contact first and uses email only as the fallback. [L0971.042, L0971.043, L1277.113]

### Stock questions

Stock question you are not sure about, 2022 procedure (Source A, *older advice*):

1. Tell them you need to confirm with the warehouse before answering.
2. Take their name and phone number and write them down, even if the call drops.
3. Check inventory through the weekly stock sheet, the dealer portal or by contacting the supplier.
4. Call the customer back with the answer as soon as possible.

[L1113.038]

### Evening and gap-time callbacks

*Older advice (Source B, 2021-22):*

- In most countries it is considered acceptable for businesses to call customers until about 8 p.m. Evening callbacks are therefore a practical option when daytime calls are not possible. [L0909.010]
- At the start you may only have about two or three callbacks to return each day. A single half-hour block in the evening is usually enough to clear them. [L0909.011]
- Short gaps such as lunch breaks, a few minutes away from work, or a commute can be used to make callbacks. Calling while commuting is possible even on public transport. [L0909.012]
- A callback in the evening often feels to the customer like extra attention, which can build trust and improve the chance of a sale. Unexpected out-of-hours service is a useful way to create a memorable experience. [L0909.013]

Callback timing at a glance:

| Option | When to use it | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Evening block, until about 8 p.m. | When daytime calls are not possible; about 2-3 callbacks a day at the start, one half-hour block | [L0909.010, L0909.011] |
| Gap time: lunch, short breaks, commute | To make short callbacks during the day | [L0909.012] |
| Within about 12 hours | After you have a correct answer for the caller | [L0808.041] |

### Logging unclosed calls

- Log every inbound sales call that does not close with the call date and a follow-up date. Many callers who promise to call back never do, so the follow-up must come from you. [L0949.030]
- A Google Sheet with columns for call date, emailed or not, and next follow-up date is enough to track early leads without a CRM. *Older advice (Source B, 2021-22).* [L0949.032]

### Daily follow-up review

- Review the follow-up list every morning, either yourself or through a team member, and contact each lead on its scheduled date. This recovers many leads that would otherwise be lost. *Older advice (Source B, 2021-22).* [L0949.031]
- Check the sheet daily to see who needs a reply. *Older advice (Source B, 2021-22).* [L0949.032]
- When a prospect calls in response to an offer, they have already been persuaded enough to take action, so follow up the inquiry quickly while interest is fresh. Delay and forgetting reduce the value of each inquiry. (Source C) [L1261.053]

### Feeding answers back into product pages

- Use the answers you learn from customer calls to update the product pages on your store, so future visitors get the information without calling. [L0934.106]
- Add the new detail to the product page as the last step of the 2024 procedure. [L0971.042]
- Add the answer to the website as step 4 of the 2020 procedure, so future visitors also see it. *Older advice (Source A, 2020).* [L1277.113]

## Selling high-ticket and B2B buyers by phone

### Qualifying high-ticket buyers

- Use this sequence on a qualified lead call for products with an average order value of $5,000 or more [L0268.044, L0268.071]:
  1. Ask why they want the product, where it will go, when they need it, and what they will use it for [L0268.044, L0268.071].
  2. Decide whether they are a real buyer by checking for a confirmed need and a real timeline [L0268.044, L0268.071].
  3. Offer service enhancements such as expedited delivery or white glove delivery instead of discounts [L0268.044, L0268.071].
  4. Show that you want to earn the business through service, not price cuts [L0268.044, L0268.071].
  5. If call volume becomes unmanageable, hire a VA to handle calls [L0268.044].
- Expect more pre-sale questions and inbound calls for high-ticket items, such as those priced at $10,000 and above or at $1,000 to $2,000. These products can still be sold, but expect more contact before the sale [L0808.043].
- Before pitching a high-ticket B2B lead, qualify in this order [L1315.063]:

  | Step | Question | Purpose |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | Who holds the authority over the budget? | Identify the economic buyer |
  | 2 | What is the need the buyer is trying to meet? | Establish the need |
  | 3 | Where are they now? | Establish the current state |
  | 4 | Where do they want to be? | Establish the target state |
  | 5 | What is the impact of not reaching that goal? | Establish urgency and cost of inaction |

### Avoiding discount tactics

- Do not use loss-leader pricing or a discount-focused strategy for products of $5,000 or more [L0268.084].
- Discounts signal desperation to customers with serious buying intent [L0268.084].
- Focus on service enhancements such as white glove delivery and expedited shipping [L0268.084, L0268.044].
- Verify genuine customer need and timeline, and show business credibility [L0268.084].
- Price questions from buyers often close the sale, but this requires constant commitment and fast responses. Older advice from 2019 (Source A) states that high-ticket products of about $5,000 and above can sell well when someone answers live chat and the phone quickly and responsively [L1110.031].

### Phone as a sales channel

- Selling by voice on the phone is an effective way to close high-ticket sales. The chance of a sale rises significantly when customers can speak to the business [L1371.039].
- Plan staff capacity for phone sales before entering the high-ticket or bulk segment. Calls will reveal product questions that you can then answer on the website [L1277.114].

### Concierge-style selling

- High-ticket buyers are often over 50 and sometimes in their 60s or 70s. They value personal attention and clear answers to their questions. A concierge-style approach works best with this group [L1472.044].
- Concierge-style phone sale for an expensive product [L1472.046]:
  1. Answer every question the buyer has about the product, and pay attention to their specific needs [L1472.046].
  2. Create a draft order in your system and send it to the customer, so they can pay without going through the normal checkout for the first order [L1472.046, L1472.047].
  3. For repeat orders, keep the conversation going by email, text or voice memos, and send another draft order when they are ready [L1472.046, L1472.065].
  4. Keep the buyer updated on any back-order delays until the product arrives [L1472.046].
- A personal concierge approach can turn a single high-ticket buyer into a steady source of revenue over time. Draft orders avoid friction in checkout for buyers who prefer a personal sale [L1472.047].

#### Case: concierge sale with draft orders

- A store sold products priced between $2,000 and $9,000, with the most expensive variant at $9,000 [L1472.048].
- One buyer called with many questions about her purchase. The store answered each one attentively and sent a draft order, so she did not have to go through checkout for the first order [L1472.048].
- Two months later she placed a second order after only a few emails of prior contact, with no draft order needed. Over those two months she brought in about $13,000 in sales [L1472.048].
- A separate $9,000 order was back-ordered for three months. The store kept her updated on the status until it was delivered [L1472.048].

### Multi-person B2B buying

- High-ticket B2B purchases involve several people [L1315.064]:

  | Role | Description |
  |---|---|
  | Economic buyer | Controls the money |
  | Lead decision maker or product owner | Leads the decision on the product |
  | Functional buyers | For example, head of purchasing |

- Get in front of the key decision maker and build relationships with the others [L1315.064].
- Expect the buyer to need strong proof and security for the purchase [L1315.064].

### Long sales cycles

- High-ticket products can require around 20 touch points before purchase. Expect a long sales cycle and plan a sequence of follow-ups rather than expecting a sale after one contact [L0949.033].

### Direct mobile numbers and repeat business

- Giving a high-value buyer your direct mobile number can turn a one-time purchase into repeat business. The buyer may later text or send voice messages to request a new draft order [L1472.065].
- Accept the channel the buyer prefers, including voice memos, and respond reliably [L1472.065].

## Taking orders over the phone

### Staff authorisation and payment limits

- Staff are authorised to take customer orders over the phone and place them on the website for the customer. [L0404.003]
- Phone orders are limited to credit card or bank deposit payment. [L0404.003, L0404.002]

| Rule | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Who takes phone orders | Staff | L0404.003 |
| Payment methods allowed | Credit card or bank deposit | L0404.003, L0404.002 |

### When phone sales matter

- Older advice (2021): most sales come through the website rather than the phone. [L0834.094]
- Older advice (2021): phone calls are typically used to close bulk orders, for example 10 items for an office. A discount is offered, and the sale is then placed over the phone. [L0834.094]

### Taking the call

1. Answer the customer's product or stock question. [L1283.062]
2. If they want to buy, add the item to cart in the store. [L1283.062]
3. Collect the customer's name, shipping address, billing address and payment method. [L1283.062]

## Calling abandoned checkouts

### Why speed decides the outcome

- Contacting a lead within minutes of abandonment, instead of 30 minutes after, makes contact about 100 times more likely, because memory and task focus degrade quickly. [L0281.001]
- Contacting within 5 minutes, instead of 30 minutes after cart abandonment, makes lead qualification about 21 times more likely. [L0281.002]
- Immediate contact, within minutes, lets the customer assume a legitimate checkout issue. They are less likely to perceive aggressive marketing and more likely to complete the purchase. [L0286.034]

### Catching abandoned checkouts in real time

- **Mistake:** Checking abandoned carts manually in Shopify Admin (Orders > Abandoned Orders) every few hours or days. Compared with immediate real-time notification, this delay cuts contact likelihood by 100 times and qualification likelihood by 21 times. [L0281.013]
- **Principle:** Orders valued at $2,000-$3,000 or higher nearly require a phone conversation to close. Use automation to identify these abandoned carts and send immediate alerts to the sales team. [L0286.017]
- **Slack alert:** A notification goes to the phone team as soon as a new abandoned cart arrives, so calls can start quickly. [L0737.015]
- **Slack alert content:** Customer name, products left in the cart, and phone number. [L1092.086] *(Older: 2022)*
- **Sheet:** The same data also lands in the tracking sheet, where the team logs results. [L0737.015]

### Capturing the phone number at checkout

- Roughly half or more of abandoned checkouts can yield a phone number, so the checkout should ask for one. Capturing it is what makes the follow-up call possible. [L1522.024] *(Older: 2021-22)*
- Handle abandoned checkouts in this order: [L1522.025] *(Older: 2021-22)*
  1. Ask for and capture the shopper's phone number during checkout.
  2. Call every abandoned checkout with a phone number as soon as possible.
  3. Send the abandoned checkout email flow to all abandoned checkouts, including those without a phone number.
- When you have the customer's phone number, combine email follow-up with a call or text. Many carts can still be converted into sales this way. [L1285.066] *(2024)*

### Timing the call

- Contact every abandoned checkout as soon as possible, ideally straight away, by phone. The call aims to find and fix the problem that stopped the sale. [L1522.023] *(Older: 2021-22)*
- Call customers who abandoned checkout to ask why they did not finish the order, except on Sundays. [L1090.139] *(Older: 2021)*

### Working each row (phone team routine)

### Thank-you and satisfaction calls

#### Purpose and timing of the post-sale call

- The first call is a post-sales support call, not a review request. Opening this way feels natural to the customer, and most businesses do not follow up at all, so the call builds goodwill before the request is made. [L1012.014]
- Schedule a post-purchase call to every customer at the same point the automated review email would go out, typically around 7 to 14 days after the order or after the usual delivery time. For example, if most orders arrive within 7 days, call at day 7; if the review email goes out at 2 weeks, call at 2 weeks. [L1012.010]
- Call new customers after purchase to thank them and confirm the order. Calls cost nothing if done with your own time or on commission, and take about 10 to 20 minutes each. [L1076.046]
- Make an upsell only when a product truly fits the customer's purchase. [L1076.046]

#### Goals of the review call

- A personal thank-you.
- Gauging satisfaction with the business and product.
- Resolving any problems.
- Securing at least a text review.
- Requesting a photo of the customer with the product.
- Requesting a video review via VideoPeel. [L1012.017]

#### Structure of a post-sale call

1. Open by asking a series of questions about whether the customer is getting the value they expected from the product and whether anything is wrong. [L1011.006]
2. Answer any questions the customer has. [L1011.006]
3. At the end of the call, ask the customer to leave a review. [L1011.006]

- Use a call script as a guide to the content only. Callers should speak in their own natural language rather than reading the script word for word. [L1012.015]

### Review call procedure

#### Step-by-step review call

1. Call the customer at the scheduled point after the order (usually 7 to 14 days). [L1012.013]
2. Open with a personal thank-you for the order and say the call is a check-in to make sure everything arrived fine. [L1012.013]
3. Ask whether the customer is happy with the product. [L1012.013]
4. If the customer is satisfied, explain that reviews help the business serve customers better and ask whether they would leave a quick two-minute review. [L1012.013]
5. If the customer is unhappy, listen and try to resolve the problem on the call, since a resolved issue often produces a stronger review. [L1012.013]
6. Request a text review at minimum. [L1012.013]
7. Offer the option of a video review (via VideoPeel) and/or a photo of the customer with the product. [L1012.013]
8. Close the call and follow up with the email sequence that goes with the script. [L1012.013]

### Finding unhappy customers

- Callers must find out when a customer is unhappy, because customers often will not say so themselves. [L1012.016]
- A problem that gets resolved on the call can turn into a more credible five-star review that mentions the good service. [L1012.016]
- Ask whether the customer is happy with the product before asking for a review, so unhappy customers are identified first. [L1012.013]

### Starting new staff on calls

- Do not start new customer service staff on live calls straight away if the product is complex or technical. [L1012.012]
- Start new staff with outbound scripted calls to existing customers. This lets them learn the product and customer language first. [L1012.012]

## Upselling and cross-selling on calls

### Where phone upselling fits

- Phone upselling and cross-selling can increase store profit substantially, but it is optional and is a different style of selling from the core approach. A store with a net margin of about 30% can run without it. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0834.095]

### Upsell planner for agents

- Give every customer service agent the full upsell planner: the list of core products and their related upsells. This lets agents know which products to offer with which core product. If you handle service alone, the planner applies to you too. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1409.021]

### Answer first, then offer

- Answer the customer's question first, then offer the related upsell product. For example, answer a shipping question and then mention the related product that goes with the item. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1409.022]

### Relevance over frequency

- Offer upsells only where they fit the customer's purchase. Relevance matters more than offering every time, so skip the offer when it does not fit. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1409.026]

### Phone and chat upsell steps

Steps for customer service agents:

1. Answer the customer's question first. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1409.023]
2. Mention the related upsell product that pairs with the core product the customer is buying. [L1409.023]
3. Ask whether the customer wants to add it to the order. [L1409.023]
4. If the customer agrees on the phone, create the checkout for them and send it, or walk them through finding the product on the site, particularly when a deal applies. [L1409.023]
5. If the customer is in online chat, send a direct link to the product page or checkout. [L1409.023]
6. If a discount or special offer applies, give the customer the discount code or apply the offer. [L1409.023]

### Agent scripts and training

- Provide agents with a written script and instructions covering when and how to offer upsells. Build the upsell step into the customer service process or SOPs. Agents need practice before the upsell becomes routine. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1409.024]
- Upsells in customer service fail when agents have not been trained or when the step is not part of the service process. Practice the script with agents and make the upsell step a standard part of their workflow. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1409.025]

### Post-purchase calls and offers

- A post-purchase call should add a helpful offer only when a better product or benefit makes sense for the buyer. Calling a customer just to tell them they should have bought something else damages trust. [L1076.048]
- Case: a business owner with a sales background called every buyer after purchase for a few months, after selling the store.
  - The owner offered a different product, extended warranties and faster shipping over the phone. The owner had previously offered these on the site.
  - The owner saw sales climb steadily during that time.
  - The approach needs a team member or owner who is comfortable on the phone, and it takes little time. [L1076.049]

## Handling supplier questions, objections and negotiation

### Opening supplier calls and pitching your store

- Lead with a benefit to the supplier, not a request. You bring traffic, exposure and sales; they only ship orders and answer emails. Keep every conversation pleasant and avoid negativity. [L0962.016]
- Differentiate with a specific reason. Suppliers choose between many callers, so a general claim about being reliable is weaker than a named product attribute. Use this formula:
  1. Name a specific attribute of the supplier's product that stands out from copies in the market.
  2. Show that you researched the brand.
  3. State how your store will serve their customers with care.
  - Furniture example: "Your outdoor sofas use weather-resistant frames that most competitors copy without the same build quality, and we want to show that quality to buyers who need durable outdoor furniture."
  - Skincare example: "Your formulas avoid the fragrance additives that most shelf brands use, and our store targets shoppers with sensitive skin who are looking for exactly that." [L0933.035]
- Show credibility and make the pitch easy to act on. Some suppliers hold back a large amount of their trust until an account is set up, so offer flexible shipping arrangements to reduce the barrier to working with you. [L1441.032]
- Prepare a short paragraph describing the marketing channels you will use to sell their products, and be ready to read it out when asked how you will market them. [L0929.028]

### Handling hesitation and refusals

- Never accept the first no. Politely ask why, because refusals are often quick exits, a busy moment or a need for more time, and the underlying objection can often be resolved. Hanging up without digging can cost you your best supplier. [L0929.010]
- Do not pressure for approval on the spot. Pushing damages a relationship you want to last for years. Many buyers want a day or so to think, and pressure makes them less likely to agree. Give them an easy way out of the call so they do not feel trapped. [L0929.023]
- Where a supplier is not taking new online retailers right now, follow this procedure:
  1. Politely ask why there is a limit, for example a supply shortage, limited product volume or a temporary issue.
  2. If the reason is temporary, offer to set up now but delay your first sales by about a month, so their current supply flow is not disturbed.
  3. If they still refuse, thank them, say you would like to work together later and leave your contact details.
  4. Ask them to contact you if a retailer spot opens up, and offer to start selling straight away.
  5. Record a callback date in your supplier sheet (for example three months later) and make that call. [L0929.025]
- Look for an arrangement that works for both sides rather than insisting on a rigid setup. Starting small and proving sales lets you ask for better terms later, while a hard line at the start can end the relationship before it begins. [L0929.032]

### Deposits, upfront payment and pre-purchase requests

- When a supplier asks for a deposit or pre-purchase, tell them you need to see sales first and propose a test. This reduces risk and keeps the deal open. [L0801.164]
- Upfront payment removes the supplier's financial risk. With credit terms they could be left chasing an unpaid invoice 30 days later, so zero risk is the most effective way to get a yes. [L0929.009]

When a supplier asks what is in it for them, offer these three benefits:

| Benefit for the supplier | What it gives them |
|---|---|
| Payment for each order upfront | Reliable cash flow and no late payments to chase |
| Access to new online customers | Their products reach new buyers and their market share grows |
| Fast route for excess stock | A quick way to move excess or discontinued stock |

- Suppliers often offer up to 40% off excess stock if you promote it actively. [L0929.030]

### Wholesale discounts, MAP and margins

- Do not ask for a discount in the first contact with a new supplier. Ask about pricing tiers and let the supplier name its own volume-based pricing before offering anything. [L1281.044]
- On a first call, after receiving the supplier's price list, follow this sequence:
  1. Thank them and say everything looks fine.
  2. Explain that you always look for the best prices so you can put the most money into advertising for every brand you sell.
  3. Ask whether they offer tiered pricing, such as lower rates above a certain dollar amount or a set number of units per month, and what the pricing would be at those levels.
  4. Stop and let the supplier answer.
  5. If the supplier has no tiers, add: most brands you work with have arrangements like that, so would they be open to discussing terms or incentives for selling more of their products? [L1281.043]
- When asked for a wholesale discount, explain the MAP margin reality and ask whether wholesale can be negotiated so that wholesale is half of MAP, rather than asking only for cheaper pricing. [L0665.115]

```
[Your name]
[Company name]
```

## Cold calling local businesses and agency clients

### Niche choice and target selection

- Choose a niche with healthy budgets and no shortage of money, such as dentists. [L1185.036]

### Call procedure

1. Send a cold email first to introduce the agency. [L0440.054]
2. Call the business. If they are busy, remind them that the email was sent. [L0440.054]

5. Present how running ads can help reach those goals. [L0440.054]

## High Probability prospecting calls

### Where prospecting fits in the approach

- The approach has three phases, in this order: High Probability Prospecting, High Probability Selling, and High Probability Closing. [L0770.074]
  - Prospecting: make a short phone contact with a clearly stated offer, let prospects disqualify themselves, and set a meeting only with someone who wants the offer and intends to buy. [L0770.074]
  - Selling: first build trust and respect in a relationship phase, then ask the discovery and disqualification questions in fixed order, letting the prospect end the process at any point. [L0770.074]
  - Closing: define the prospect's Conditions of Satisfaction, obtain a commitment to buy if they are met, and only then present a proposal or demonstration. [L0770.074]
- There are usually far more prospects than a sales team can visit, but at any given time only a small share are ready to buy. Contact as many prospects as possible, briefly, to find that small group, and spend meeting time only on them. [L0770.076]

### Writing the opening offer

- Keep the offer under 45 words, including greeting and introduction. Write it in your own words so that it makes sense to you. [L0770.013]
- Deliver it in a neutral, unemotional tone, because a convincing tone creates resistance even when the words are right. [L0770.013]
- Benchmarks from practice:
  - One seller's offer went through three rounds of trial calls and settled at 34 words. It was later sharpened further based on prospect responses. [L0770.014]
  - Another seller cut a 59-word offer to 42 words and saw better results. Shorter offers generally perform better. [L0770.014]
- Opening offer template for a cold call, filled in and kept under 45 words:

```
Hello, this is [your name] from [company name]. We [concrete description of what you provide, with one specific benefit or measurable result, e.g. the product type and what it does for the buyer]. Is that something you want for [the prospect's product line, business or situation]?
```

  Deliver it in a neutral tone and take the prospect's answer as it comes. [L0770.015]

- Formula for a cold-call offer:
  1. Name yourself and your company.
  2. State concretely what you provide.
  3. Add one specific benefit, coverage detail, or measurable result.
  4. Ask a yes-or-no question about whether the prospect wants it. [L0770.016]
- The question keeps the prospect in control and makes a clear no easy. [L0770.016]
- Own examples of the formula:
  - *This is Mark from Cleanline Office Services. We clean commercial kitchens after hours, which in our clients' experience cuts grease-related inspection failures roughly in half. Is that something you want for your kitchen?* [L0770.016]
  - *This is Lena from Pressgrid Print. We produce custom labels in runs of 500 to 5,000 with a proof sent the same day. Is that something you want for your product line?* [L0770.016]

### Running the call

- Follow this sequence on a High Probability prospecting call: [L0770.012]
  1. State your offer clearly in no more than 45 words, counting the greeting and your introduction. [L0770.012]
  2. Check whether the prospect wants what you are offering. [L0770.012]
  3. If he says yes, ask whether he would like to set up a meeting aimed at doing business. [L0770.012]
  4. If he is not ready at that point, book a new call for a time he chooses, and get his commitment to move forward then. [L0770.012]
  5. End the call on any negative answer. Arrange a callback only if he states he will be a qualified prospect at a specific later time, and make clear that it is completely fine for him to say no whenever he hesitates. [L0770.012]
  6. Disqualify anyone who does not want your offer or will not buy it from you. [L0770.012]
- Two signs that you have slipped into selling on a prospecting call: you feel stress during the call, or the call runs longer than three to four minutes. When either happens, stop pitching and return to asking questions. [L0770.018]

### Qualifying questions

- Collect the following from the prospect before any appointment, in this order: [L0770.019]
  1. Does he want this product?
  2. Does he want to arrange an appointment?
  3. If not now, when?
  4. If not now, does he want you to check back later?
  5. Why does he want to meet at this time?
  6. What would he like to accomplish at the meeting?
- Add further disqualifying questions when the situation calls for them. [L0770.019]

### Handling vague and negative answers

- If a prospect answers with "that sounds interesting", "maybe", "I don't know", "I don't think so" or "I'll consider it", reply in a flat tone: [L0770.020]

```
Does that mean you want what I'm offering, or not?
```

- Treat a no as a clear answer and close the call politely. Do not argue, add benefits, or sell harder. [L0770.020]
- If a prospect asks a question you cannot answer well yet, such as why he should switch when he is happy with his current supplier, use one of these replies in a flat tone: [L0770.022]

```
I don't have a satisfactory answer to that yet. What answer would make sense to you?
```

```
It doesn't sound like this is a good fit for you. Is my offer something you want, or not?
```

  Choose the reply that fits the situation and let the prospect answer. [L0770.022]

- A statement that does not answer your question, such as "I'm not interested", is noise rather than a real answer. Do not respond with a sales pitch. [L0770.023]
  - Ask a clarifying question he can answer, such as whether he is the person who decides on this, and keep the conversation on your questions. [L0770.023]
  - Disqualify him only when he clearly says he does not need, want, or can afford the offer. [L0770.023]
- Interest has no sales value because it is not a commitment. Enthusiasm and direct, quick answers are better signs of a qualified prospect than polite interest. [L0770.024]
- Ask yes-or-no questions that include the option of no, such as whether something is what he wants or not. A clear option to decline reduces resistance and makes the prospect responsible for his own choice. [L0770.025]

### Information requests and materials

- If a prospect asks you to send information, reply in a neutral tone: [L0770.021]

```
When someone asks for information, it often means the conversation is ending politely. If that is your intention, just tell me and that's fine. If you do want the details, tell me what you would need to decide so I can see whether it makes sense to send them.
```

- Send materials only when the prospect wants the offer, needs the information, and is likely to buy after reviewing it. [L0770.021]
- Do not send unsolicited brochures or samples to cold prospects. Keep unsolicited mail for a mass mailing sent two or three times a year, and send individual material only to prospects who have confirmed interest. [L0770.069]

### Finding the right contact

- When you need the right person at a company, first ask the front desk for whoever handles the product area, such as packaging design. [L0770.067]
  - Many switchboards only release the marketing or sales manager's name. Ask for that person and let them point you to the correct contact. [L0770.067]
  - If you still cannot reach the decision-maker, the sales department is a usable alternative route. [L0770.067]
- In companies with several product lines, each line's product manager often owns packaging or purchasing decisions for that line. Identify the manager of each line separately instead of relying on one central contact. [L0770.068]

### Scheduling the meeting

- Never try to sell the appointment. Ask whether he wants to meet to see if your offer fits his needs. Then let him propose the time, and ask him what he wants to accomplish, since a prospect who does not commit to a meeting has disqualified himself. [L0770.017]
- Scheduling a sales meeting by phone: [L0770.070]
  1. Set a tentative date for the meeting, usually a few weeks ahead if the prospect names that timing. [L0770.070]
  2. Ask what the meeting should accomplish and whether the decision-maker should attend. [L0770.070]
  3. Call the day before to confirm the time and the attendees. [L0770.070]
  4. If the prospect does not confirm, treat the appointment as disqualified and do not chase it. [L0770.070]

### Ending a call with a disqualified prospect

- Close politely and do not argue. Say you will only call again if new developments fit his stated needs, and do not try to convert him. [L0770.081]

## High Probability relationship building and discovery

### Relationship phase: purpose and sequence

- Do not discuss your product during relationship building. Your purpose is to learn who the prospect is, what motivates him and how he reached his current role, so you can decide whether you trust and respect him. If you do not trust him, leave politely, even if you cannot yet say why. [L0770.030]
- The relationship phase usually happens in the first meeting, and there is no fixed script or question list. The point is to let the conversation produce the information you need, so the exact questions depend on what the prospect says. [L0770.035]
- Prospects also judge the salesperson during relationship building, and trust shows through how you behave rather than what you say. A relationship of trust and respect gives you an advantage over competitors, because most buyers prefer to deal with people they trust. [L0770.034]
- Do not go on with a long relationship phase when the prospect has not yet shown trust or respect. Leaving early costs little, while investing time in someone you do not trust wastes the rest of the process. [L0770.045]

### When the prospect wants to skip the relationship phase

Use this in a flat, unemotional tone when a prospect in the relationship phase wants to jump straight into business:

```
Before we talk about my product, I like to learn how my customers operate, because that tells me how to serve them. I also need to find out whether I trust and respect the people I work with, and you have the same chance to judge me. So, are you willing to continue this kind of conversation, or would you rather I leave?
```

If he chooses to leave, end the visit politely and treat him as a low-probability prospect. [L0770.071]

### Opening the relationship

- To open a relationship, make a neutral observation about something visible or known, such as the building, the location or the company, and attach a question to it. Then base each follow-up on the prospect's last answer rather than a fixed list. [L0770.031]
  - Example: The loading area here is unusually wide. Was it built for this kind of product flow? [L0770.031]
  - Example: You mentioned the new site opened last spring. What made you move the team there? [L0770.031]
- Use open-ended questions while building the relationship, such as why, what did you do, how did you handle that, and was that something you wanted to do. Open questions let the prospect explain himself instead of answering yes or no. [L0770.032]
- Prospects tend to answer almost any authentic question truthfully when they sense you genuinely want the answer and are not manipulating them. Sincere interest cannot be faked, and prospects will cut short a conversation if they sense you are only going through the motions. [L0770.033]
- Answering a personal disclosure with an unrelated small-talk question, such as about family or hobbies, signals that you are not really interested and damages trust. Respond with a follow-up on what he just said, such as asking why it happened or how he handled it. [L0770.079]

### Listening and note-taking

- Ask a question, then listen to the full answer before thinking about your next question. If an answer is incomplete, ask a follow-up to clarify it for yourself and sometimes for the prospect. [L0770.027]
- Keep your own talking to about 25 percent of a meeting. If you are talking more than that, the meeting is probably going badly; ask why the prospect is unresponsive, and end the meeting if you cannot fix it, unless he is naturally quiet. [L0770.028]
- Take complete notes at every contact, recording the prospect's facts and opinions in his exact words. Exact wording is valuable later when you define his requirements, and it shows him that you value what he says. Missing details from a first meeting are hard to recover later. [L0770.026]

### Handling negativity

Deal with anger, sarcasm, threats, abuse or any other negativity before anything else. Speak in a flat, neutral tone, without judging and without sympathy.

1. Say neutrally that he seems upset. [L0770.029]
2. If he is still upset after explaining why, repeat the observation in the same flat tone, without stressing any word. [L0770.029]
3. If he is still upset, suggest that you discuss the matter at another time. [L0770.029]
4. If he calms down, continue the conversation. If he becomes upset again, return to step 1 and keep your tone even. [L0770.029]
5. If he stays upset, tell him you are not willing to meet right now because it would not serve either of you, and ask whether he wants to schedule another appointment or not. [L0770.029]

- Do not respond with anger to a low-probability prospect. He is simply not a fit, so recognize it, end the meeting politely and move on to the next prospect. [L0770.044]

### Discovery and disqualification questions

Use the wording below in this fixed order, swapping "this product" for your own product or service, and do not mention your company by name. [L0770.036]

1. **Need:** Why do you need this product? [L0770.036]
2. **Want:** Do you want it? If yes, ask why; if no, end the meeting. [L0770.036]
3. **Financial:** This product will cost about [price]; are you prepared to spend that? [L0770.036]
4. **Time:** If you decide to proceed, when would you want this to start, and what happens if it doesn't start then? [L0770.036]
5. **Decision-makers:** When you make a decision like this, who do you usually talk it over with? Then ask whether he'll let you speak with them before you prepare a proposal. [L0770.036]
6. **Authority:** If you went ahead, who else would have to agree? Handle this the same way as decision-makers. [L0770.036]
7. **Commitments:** What would happen if you don't buy this product? [L0770.036]
8. **Brand preference:** If you had to decide right now, without talking to anyone, which brand would you buy? [L0770.036]
9. **Supplier preference:** Is there someone you'd rather do business with? [L0770.036]
10. **Internal procedure:** What is your process for issuing a purchase order? Have him write it down and read it back before you move on. [L0770.036]
11. **Personal motivation:** What would it mean to you personally if you didn't purchase this product? [L0770.036]
12. **Personal prejudices:** Is there any reason you wouldn't want to do business with me? Anything we haven't covered yet, or any emotional reason? [L0770.036]
13. **Hidden obstacles:** What could be lurking in the background that would prevent this from happening? [L0770.036]
14. **Close:** Once questions 1 to 13 are answered satisfactorily and he hasn't disqualified himself: if I can meet all your criteria for this product, what will you do? [L0770.036]

- Discovery questions are general, not about your brand, so the answers describe the prospect's own needs. Ask each question in the exact same words every time and write down the answers precisely, since the wording and tone of his answers can matter later. [L0770.037]
- The prospect can disqualify himself at any of the fourteen discovery questions. Each answer he gives without disqualifying himself builds the relationship and the mutual commitment, and each problem you handle early prevents a deal-breaker later. [L0770.038]
- Do not disqualify a prospect after an objection such as a price limit without first asking why he holds that position. The reason may be solvable, and asking why is better than guessing at his thinking. [L0770.043]

### Decision-makers and approvers

Request to speak with the decision-maker or approver before preparing a proposal:

```
I need to talk with [name or role] before I prepare a proposal. Are you willing to arrange that?
```

If he refuses, reply in a flat tone:

```
That's not the way I work. I won't prepare a proposal without understanding their concerns and objectives, because I don't want a proposal rejected over something we could have handled up front. What would you like to do?
```

If he still will not arrange it, disqualify him. [L0770.039]

### Unclear, evasive and conflicting answers

When a prospect's answer is unclear or evasive, do not move on until you are fully satisfied.

1. Ask for clarification, for example by saying you do not understand what he meant and asking what he means. [L0770.040]
2. If he is still unclear, state that his answer seems evasive and ask whether there is a problem. [L0770.040]
3. If he still avoids answering, tell him you will not move on until the question is answered. [L0770.040]
4. Carry on only when you have a clear answer, because an unresolved issue usually returns later as a problem. [L0770.040]

- When a prospect gives conflicting timing, such as urgent on the phone and six months away in the meeting, ask him directly about the discrepancy before going further. Then either reach a clear commitment for the timing or set a specific return date that he chooses. [L0770.041]

## High Probability commitment, conditions and closing

### Sequence of the Sale

- Do not present a product, quote, or proposal until the prospect has committed to buy if the product meets his stated conditions. The order is: questions, agreement on conditions, commitment, and only then the demonstration or proposal. [L0770.052]
- Selling in this approach means reaching a series of agreements with prospects who first acknowledge that they need, want, and can afford the product, and who commit to buying at a set time if their conditions are met. It is not about persuading anyone to act. A deal exists only when both sides have agreed. [L0770.075]

### Closing Through Mutual Commitment

- Closing means negotiating mutual commitments, never asking for the order. The prospect chooses yes or no, and you are equally satisfied with either answer, because your commitment is to the process rather than to making a sale. [L0770.050]
- Ask only questions whose answers you need. Avoid rhetorical or trial closes, including small either-or choices meant to push a sale. Prospects recognize these tactics and resent them, and they undermine trust. [L0770.051]
- Traditional selling offers a choice of yes or yes: the seller argues for a sale no matter the prospect's view. High Probability selling offers a choice of yes or no and accepts either answer, which is why it produces commitments that hold. [L0770.066]
- Example of a real decision: "Does a one-week delivery window work for you, or not?" Compare "Shall we get you set up?", which only invites a reluctant yes. (Own illustration.) [L0770.066]

### Defining the Conditions of Satisfaction

Open the conditions phase with this template after discovery is complete:

```
It is time to define your Conditions of Satisfaction. I would like us to write down exactly what you require, every requirement, because that is how I determine whether we can provide it. Are you willing to do that?

After he agrees, read back what he said during discovery and ask whether it is correct and whether he wants to add anything.
```
[L0770.072]

Establish the Conditions of Satisfaction in this order:

1. Confirm that the discovery questions are fully answered and that every decision-maker and approver is involved. [L0770.053]
2. Tell the prospect that you now need to define exactly what he requires, and ask whether he is willing to do that. [L0770.053]
3. Ask what he will do if you return with a proposal that meets his conditions. [L0770.053]
4. If he does not give a clear commitment, tell him that you will only prepare a proposal with a commitment to buy if the conditions are met, and disqualify him if he refuses. [L0770.053]
5. Once he commits, read back what he said during discovery and ask whether it is correct and whether he wants to add anything. [L0770.053]
6. Ask for each condition in his exact words and record them. Raise any conditions he has not mentioned that need addressing. [L0770.053]
7. Specify exactly what he will and will not accept. Agree how each condition will be measured, such as a timed test or a signed sample. [L0770.053]
8. Tell him clearly what you can and cannot provide, and offer alternatives where possible without hiding the negatives. [L0770.053]
9. Negotiate any conditions you cannot meet. If an unmet condition cannot be negotiated, disqualify him. If you agree on conditions you can meet, you have a deal. [L0770.053]
10. At the next meeting, present a proposal that shows you meet all conditions, and complete the transaction. [L0770.053]

### Making Conditions Measurable

- Make each condition measurable so both sides can check it. "Easy to set up" becomes a time limit for a shipping clerk to set up the package, including taping. "Vivid four-color art" becomes a signed sample of the minimum quality the buyer will accept. [L0770.054]

### References

When a prospect wants references from existing customers:

1. Ask how many references he wants. [L0770.073]
2. Ask whether he will call those references while you are present. [L0770.073]
3. Ask what he will do if the references give positive endorsements. [L0770.073]
4. Record the agreed answers in his exact words as a condition to be met. [L0770.073]

### Budgetary Quotes

- When you present a budgetary quote to a prospect who has not committed, ask in the same breath what he will do if the price is acceptable. This ties the estimate to a real commitment instead of sending a free number he can shop around. [L0770.077]

### Testing the Commitment

- After a prospect commits, test that commitment by asking whether he is sure and why he wants to proceed. His reasons often surface, and the answer strengthens his commitment. Testing also brings hidden doubts into the open before you leave. [L0770.057]

### Handling Requests You Cannot Meet

When a prospect asks for something you cannot deliver, such as a finish you do not offer:

1. State plainly that you do not offer it, and give the reason in one sentence. [L0770.042]
2. Offer your available option and ask whether he will accept it. [L0770.042]
3. If he insists on the unavailable option, end the meeting instead of hoping he changes his mind. [L0770.042]

Use this template for follow-up replies to question 14, delivered in a flat tone, depending on his response:

```
Follow-up replies to question 14 are delivered in a flat tone, depending on his response. If he answers 'What do you mean?', reply: What do you think I mean? If he says he will seriously consider it, reply: I'm not willing to prepare a proposal unless I have a commitment that we will do business if I meet your criteria. What would you like to do? Unless he clearly states that he will give you his business if you meet his criteria, disqualify him.
```

### Pushback and Disqualification

- Commitments tend to hold when they are freely made. A prospect who says yes will usually honor it, while an order that rests on pressure is likely to be cancelled. [L0770.058]
- A prospect who breaks a commitment should be disqualified in most markets. In markets with few buyers, you may have to keep working with him. [L0770.058]
- When a prospect pushes back hard against being disqualified, still disqualify him if you have real doubts about his commitment. A serious buyer rarely accepts disqualification without a struggle, but the struggle alone does not prove that he is qualified. [L0770.078]

### Common Mistakes

- Do not move from discovery straight to the prospect's requests without first asking for commitment. Otherwise the prospect takes the lead, you end up doing what he asks, and the process loses its structure. [L0770.062]
- If you find yourself answering the prospect's questions instead of asking your own, return to asking questions and asking for commitment. It is harder to regain control once you have lost it, but a commitment request can still restore it. [L0770.063]
- Do not assume the sale is made because discovery went well or the prospect seems friendly. Until he commits in writing or aloud to the conditions, he has not committed, and acting as though he has leads to lost orders. [L0770.064]
- Do not try to convince a prospect to change his mind after he has declined a commitment or set a limit you cannot meet. Ending the meeting saves the effort, and trying to talk him out of his conditions creates resistance. [L0770.065]

### Worked Cases

- **Toy manufacturer, testing the commitment:** A seller for a large toy manufacturer reviewed the buyer's conditions, then asked what the buyer would do if a demonstration on the following Tuesday met all of them. After the buyer pushed back, the seller held his position calmly, and the buyer then committed to an order if the demonstration met the conditions. The seller asked whether he was sure, then asked why. The buyer gave his reasons and said there was no doubt that he would order if the conditions were met. [L0770.059]
- **Packaging supplier, existing buyer for Star:** A packaging supplier with an existing buyer for a brand called Star learned that a rival supplier was failing on another brand, Sun, which had 17 packages needing new artwork. The seller did not pitch. He asked about the problem and what the buyer needed.
  - The product manager wanted four packages within five weeks and said the other 13 could come later, with an expected lead time of 8 to 15 weeks.

  - The product manager was not going to cancel his existing order. He promised at least half of his packaging requirements if delivery was met. He required top quality, on-time delivery, no shortages, and no more than 2 percent overage.
  - The buyer's purchasing manager signed the first order the next day, after the product manager approved the quote. [L0770.060]
- **Advertising manager, point-of-sale packaging:** A seller who had established a relationship with an advertising manager asked whether the packaging would be worth it if it gave attractive point-of-sale advertising for about one dollar more per unit. ## Core sales conversation skills and training

### Conversation flow and qualification

#### Run the sales conversation in this order

1. Get the prospect's attention first. Nothing else works without it. [L0378.037]
2. Find out about the prospect. Ask which provider or solution they use now, whether they like it, what is missing, and whether they care most about price, quality, or having the best option. [L0378.037]
3. Present a custom offer that matches what you learned, walking through the options from highest to lowest. [L0378.037]
4. Close by doing the math for them, starting with the highest figure that fits the argument. [L0378.037]

#### Qualify prospects

- Start with people who are unhappy with their current solution. [L0378.038]
- If they are satisfied, look for one missing feature that could matter to them. If nothing does, move on. [L0378.038]

#### Build know, like and trust first

- Build know, like and trust before presenting an offer. Help people first, learn about them, and then move to the offer, because trust makes buyers far more likely to purchase. [L0378.044]
- Moving a prospect to a second location during a conversation makes the interaction feel longer and builds trust faster. Use this when it fits the context. [L0378.045]
- A sales call or customer chat should always end with a clear offer or next step. [L1439.043]

### Price reveal order

#### Reveal prices top-down

- Reveal prices in a fixed order, from the top option down to the cheapest, one step at a time. Showing the cheapest option first removes the chance to anchor on the top price. [L0378.039]
- Showing the highest price first creates a moment of concern that the buyer cannot afford it. Showing cheaper options afterward releases that tension and makes the buyer more willing to commit. [L0378.040]

### Doing the math for the buyer

- Do the math for the buyer instead of expecting them to calculate. Lay out logically why the purchase makes sense, using figures that are believable and tied to their situation. [L0378.043]
- Use the following closing template, filling in the placeholders with real figures:

```
So you pay [ONE-TIME COST] once and add [MONTHLY GAIN] to your bottom line every month. Is that worth it for you?

An extra [MONTHLY GAIN] a month adds up to [ANNUAL GAIN] over a year. If someone offered you [ONE-TIME COST] today in exchange for [ANNUAL GAIN] at the end of the year, what would you say?
```
[L0378.042]

### Objections

#### Anticipate objections

- Top closers anticipate objections before they are raised and address them openly. [L0378.046]
- Highlighting a product's real weaknesses, framed honestly, keeps trust and prevents later doubt. [L0378.046]

#### Handle objections in person

1. List every objection the prospect raises on a pad before answering any. [L1370.104]
2. Keep asking if there is anything else until the prospect has no more objections. [L1370.104]
3. Answer each objection. [L1370.104]
4. Ask a conditional closing question, such as whether the prospect will go ahead if all these concerns are resolved. [L1370.104]
5. Once the prospect agrees, the close is in place. [L1370.104]

Print copy cannot replicate this live feedback, so the letter must answer the full list in advance. [L1370.104]

### Emotional buyers

- Some buyers, such as passionate sports fans, are much less sensitive to price for products tied to their team or interests. Identify the emotional attachment first and build the offer around it. [L0378.041]
- Sales psychology matters. Understanding emotions, impulse and how people decide is the core of online selling, and studying human behaviour helps a store owner predict how a customer will respond. [L1440.056]
- Customers, not payment processors or platforms, pay the business, so the owner must understand how people think and decide to buy. Understanding persuasion principles helps predict what people will spend money on. [L1513.016]

### Composure and not appearing desperate

- Sales conversations work best when the seller is not desperate for the deal, because the hardest deal is the one you visibly need. Taking a position of not needing the deal removes pressure and keeps buyers engaged. [L1370.105]

### Training and role-play drills

#### Train sales skills in this order

1. Role-play different customer types, each with a different objection. [L0378.047]
2. Drill the pricing sequence and numbers until they come out in the right order without thinking. [L0378.047]
3. Keep a fixed close and offer block on printed cards for field use. [L0378.047]
4. Improve the opening and the middle of each pitch, which is where creativity matters most. [L0378.047]

#### Reference case: a commission-based field seller

- Starting point: a new salesperson sold TV subscriptions face to face, earning about $125 per installation, with the commission paid only when the service was installed. [L0378.048]

- In week two, the salesperson moved from a busy discount warehouse to a quieter electronics store, where meeting buyers in aisles allowed building a relationship before pitching. [L0378.048]

### Books and learning by doing

- Read recommended books on sales and persuasion. Dale Carnegie's *How to Win Friends and Influence People* is recommended for handling clients, and Alex Hormozi's *100 Million Offers* for building offers. [L0440.059]
- Robert Cialdini's *Influence* is recommended for learning persuasion principles. [L1513.016]
- Time spent in calls and real-world conversations is the most effective way to learn sales, because the mistakes made in calls show what to do better next time. [L0440.060]

### Written sales presentations

- Sell in print the way you sell in person. A sales letter is a sales presentation on paper, so write it as if speaking directly to one prospect at a kitchen table or living room floor. [L1370.106]
- Invite an immediate phone call and tie it to a specific time the reader can act on before the deadline. A time-limited request removes the delay of a letter's slow reply. [L1369.111]
- Formula: invite an immediate phone call tied to a specific time that the reader can act on before the deadline. [L1369.111]

  - "Call me before noon today, and I can have your order confirmed and the first shipment booked for tomorrow." [L1369.111]

## Staffing, hiring and delegating phone coverage

### Decide when to answer the phone yourself and when to hire

- Handle calls yourself first so you learn them, then reinvest profits into hiring someone to take over. Do not outsource phone sales before the business is profitable. (Older advice, 2018.) [L1109.064]
- Missed calls from customers mean lost sales. Whoever answers needs a set schedule and a quiet place to take calls. If that person is running errands, they carry a notebook to capture leads. [L0832.005]
- Customer service calls can interrupt the owner about every 10 minutes, which makes focused work almost impossible. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1247.005]
- Whether you need coverage depends on whether the calls make money:

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Calls from customers turn into revenue | Have someone answer the phone while you are away. [L0808.038] |
| Calls do not generate revenue | You do not need to cover them. [L0808.038] |

### Timing the first hire

Sources give different triggers. Use the one that matches your situation:

| Trigger | Source | Applies when |
|---|---|---|
| The site has traffic | Older, 2019 [L0467.065] | You need someone to cover live chat and phone calls during hours you cannot. The cost should be recovered quickly from the sales they handle. [L0467.065] |
| The business is profitable | Older, 2018 [L1109.064]; older, 2020 [L0468.177] | You are handing phone sales over to someone else, with profits funding the hire. [L1109.064, L0468.177] |

- Phone support must still be answered while you travel. If you work from another time zone, hire a virtual assistant rather than leave calls unanswered, because sales drop when calls go unanswered. (Older, 2019.) [L1002.070]
- The first hire is usually a customer service assistant. [L1000.031]

### Virtual assistants and remote coverage

- Once the business is profitable, a virtual assistant can handle customer phone calls so the owner is not tied to the phone while travelling. (Older, 2020.) [L0468.177]
- For short absences, such as a week away, have a friend or family member answer by forwarding calls to them. [L0808.042]
- For ongoing phone coverage, hire a full-time person through onlinejobs.ph, a Filipino job board. [L0808.042]

### Who to hire for phone work

- Phone support staff should sound natural to the local market. When selling in the United States, for example, they should have no accent, because an accent can lower the conversion rate. Email and live chat do not carry this accent problem, so the person's location matters less for those channels. [L1430.006]
- Phone staff are usually more expensive than staff who handle email, live chat and order processing, especially at the start. That is why the two roles are often separated when you start out, although one person can do both. [L1430.007]

### Vetting and interview tests

Hiring and vetting a phone-support virtual assistant (older, 2019):

1. Post a job on onlinejobs.ph for someone with phone and email sales or support experience. [L1002.071]
2. Review resumes and prefer candidates with experience at large, well-run companies who are still employed there and looking for a better opportunity. [L1002.071]
3. Test the hire by calling your own business at least a couple of times per week, from a withheld number and at unusual hours. Confirm they answer and handle the call correctly. [L1002.071]

Interview checks for the first hire:

- Use a five-minute interview test. A call that ends in five minutes or less shows no rapport, and the candidate is unlikely to build rapport with customers. [L1000.031]
- Reject candidates who are hard to understand on a call. They will be the voice of the business to customers every day, so poor spoken English or unclear speech is a reason to remove them from the list. [L1000.032]

Pay structure for new phone or chat staff (older, 2019):

## Measuring phone sales and ad attribution

### Measuring offline and phone sales

When half or more of sales happen over the phone, Google sees too few conversions and undervalues the campaigns. Either attribute offline sales back to the clicks that caused them, or lower the Google target ROAS to account for offline revenue. [L0454.046]

### Adjusting the target ROAS for offline sales

**Formula**

Google target ROAS = overall target ROAS × (1 − share of offline or phone sales) [L0454.048]

**Worked example**

- Share of sales made over the phone: 50%
- Overall target ROAS: 10x
- Google target ROAS: 10 × 0.5 = **5x** [L0454.048]

**Trade-off**

- This method avoids CRM work. [L0454.048]
- It requires a reasonable estimate of the offline share. [L0454.048]

### Diagnosing the funnel before blaming the creative

- Do not assume that a single poor result is caused only by the creative. Doing so leads to missed funnel problems. [L0361.010]
- Even with a weak creative, one add-to-cart from a large spend means something in the setup is also wrong. [L0361.010]
