4.19 Scaling the store: more suppliers, niches, content and delegation

Adding suppliers and products in stages

Supplier approval and catalogue build (older, 2020)

  1. Get approved with the first one or two brands. [L0468.072]
  2. Upload every product from those brands, using CSV files for speed. [L0468.072]
  3. Upload the next brands only after the first ones are complete. [L0468.072]
  4. Aim for at least 100 different products before running Google ads. [L0468.072]
  • A brand can start as a parked Shopify store with no products while the audience is being built. Products launch later, so audience reactions can guide what to sell. [L0487.025]
  • The store can go live and advertising can start before every product is uploaded. In markets with many products, upload a first set, launch, and add more after launch (older, Source B 2021-22). [L1166.030]
  • Keep adding quality supplier products while adjusting ads, even if the store is not yet profitable (older, 2022). [L0969.090]
  • Source the product and confirm the supplier can ship it before running any ad. Never start an ad for a product you cannot fulfil. [L1171.033]
  • Look for missing variants such as colours and sizes on competitor sites, for example light and dark wood finishes or new colours. Upload all available variants so shoppers who want a specific option choose your store. [L0894.011]
  • Oberlo and AliExpress are named as sources for product supply and price markup. [L0838.004]
  • Replace AliExpress or other Chinese products on an existing store with US suppliers only if the store's niche fits the niche criteria of modules one and two (2023). [L0964.094]
  • A native Shopify supplier integration is a win. External enterprise-level apps are not recommended for a new store (2024). [L0971.038]
  • Adding related products to an existing store is fine when analytics, Facebook pages, Google Merchant Center and Google Ads are already connected. Do not recreate them for a new store (older, 2021). [L0834.043]

Testing niches and products in one general store

Start small and expand on proof

  1. Start a new product test with one product and a product page. [L0388.011]
  2. Once sales show the audience buys, move to a collection page with several items to increase cross-sales and average order value. [L0388.011]
  • A general store can be used for testing with one product page. Building a community and a brand requires a niche store, so move to a niche store once a product is proven. [L0335.052]
  • Start with a general store under a brandable random domain rather than a single-product store, then test products one by one. Focus effort on landing pages with a strong offer and trust elements. [L0637.034]
  • During product testing, a simple store is enough to collect data. Invest in a polished design, stronger branding and a product page builder after one product shows clear winning numbers. [L1176.046]
  • A beginner is better off building one reusable general store personally than paying a freelancer to build a product page for every test product. Outsourcing each test becomes expensive, while skills carry over to every later test. [L1212.053]

One domain, one general store for many tests

  • Run one brand-style, generic domain name (under 10 characters, easy to say and remember, for example made-up names checked with Namelix). Test products by adding a new product page rather than a new store. [L1410.028]
  • The home page needs only 2-3 sections: a product banner, the product section, and trust badges such as priority shipping and a money-back guarantee. [L1410.028]
  • Keep one general store domain for all products tested in the same broad category, such as shoes. Buy a new domain and brand only when a proven product moves to its own store or a different market. [L1131.031]
  • Using one domain for all tests avoids the cost and friction of buying new stores and domains. A single-product focus with a strong product page still builds a brand identity. [L1217.039]
  • In a new store, test one product at a time instead of loading dozens of products. Each product gets its own focused page. [L1217.037]
  • Every new Shopify store requires its own home page, legal pages, notification settings and checkout setup. This is the main reason to keep testing products on one domain and only add a new product page. [L1410.046]
  • Keeping a single store and swapping products is the cheaper option for early testing. Rebuilding under a new domain costs money and means losing the free trial. [L0372.024]
  • To test many niches without paying for a new Shopify store each time, use one general store for all tests. When a winner appears, change the logo and name and keep the store setup, since opening a new Shopify store means paying again. [L0388.013]

Product testing flow under one domain

  1. Build one generic homepage. [L1194.008]
  2. Keep the About us, Contact, Track my order, FAQ and all policy pages the same across the store. [L1194.008]
  3. Create one product page template and duplicate it for each product, so every tested product has its own URL (five products, five URLs). [L1194.008]
  4. On the homepage, add a Shop now menu with a dropdown that lists every product. [L1194.008]
  5. On the product pages, remove or hide the top navigation so the page reads like a single landing page. [L1194.008]
  6. Launch one product in a single day. [L1410.029]
  7. Check performance after 3 to 4 days. [L1410.029]
  8. If it fails, create a new product page in about two hours and launch it within two hours. [L1410.029]
  9. When a product wins, either turn the store into a niche store or devote a store to the winner, and keep testing new products under a separate domain. [L1410.029]

Single-domain launch with unpublished backups

  1. Buy one brandable domain with no meaning, under 8-10 characters. Generate ideas with Namelix or ChatGPT, and combine two words into a new name if needed. [L1217.036]
  2. Build one high-converting product page for the first product using a page builder such as PageFly or GemPages. [L1217.036]
  3. Build the homepage with one banner that has the store name, a benefit-driven claim, and an image. [L1217.036]
  4. Under the banner, add a short section about the store and why the product helps, then show the product. [L1217.036]
  5. Prepare pages for the next products but keep them unpublished. [L1217.036, L1217.041]
  6. After about three days, if the product does not work, publish the next product page under the same domain. [L1217.036]
  • Keep unpublished product pages ready while the first product is tested. Judge a product after about three days of ad data before deciding whether to switch. [L1217.041]
  • Judge timing differs between sources: about three days (above) or three to four days (the flow above). Use three to four days for a full read when running the step-by-step flow. [L1217.041, L1410.029]

Winner-first store structure

  1. Start with a general store that carries several test products at once. [L0661.052]
  2. Once one product clearly wins, rename the store to the niche, for example from caricia to caricia shoes. [L0661.052]
  3. Keep the same domain name when renaming the store. [L0661.052]
  4. Remove the other products and leave only the winning product line. [L0661.052]

Store structure approach

  1. Start with a general store or a niche general store containing several products. [L1212.023]
  2. Send ads to a collection page and let customers choose. [L1212.023]
  3. Identify the best-selling product. [L1212.023]
  4. Add a new theme focused on that product, optionally with a new domain and logo. [L1212.023]
  5. Keep the existing Shopify store and pixel. [L1212.023]
  • A general store can hold several products tested together. If a product wins, either convert the store into a single-product store or add a new theme focused on the winner, rather than building a new Shopify store, so the existing pixel keeps its sales data. [L1212.022]
  • The usual setup is one product per store, or one general store used to test products and then move winners into their own store. Setting up a new store for a product can be quick, around three hours, if the design is reused. [L0372.020]
  • For a fast setup for product testing, use these steps: [L1171.030]
    1. Use the Shrine theme, which has an inbuilt quantity discount. [L1171.030]
    2. Name the store with a general female first name rather than a product name, so the same store can test many products. [L1171.030]
    3. After sourcing a product, add it to the store and make the product page live. [L1171.030]
  • When testing many products on TikTok, a store can be set up as a general deals-style store with a domain such as onlinedeals.com, instead of one store per product. Testing many products this way needs a lot of content. [L0561.016]
  • Use a store that can sell a product line well. Build it around the product's season or niche, and expand with 5 to 10 new products per month to raise the store's product range. [L1440.018]

Niche rules

  • Run each store around one niche, with multiple categories inside it. For example, a standing desk store can have categories for bamboo, whiteboard and L-shaped desks (older, 2021). [L0801.092]
  • Keep unrelated niches in separate stores. Single-niche stores tend to have higher conversion rates, since the buyer experience is focused on one product type (older, 2021). [L0801.094]
  • Use niche-specific stores rather than one broad store covering many categories. A niche store can be built even when competitors sell a wide range of categories (older, 2020). [L0664.040]
  • A single store should cover one niche only (older, 2022). [L1281.017]
  • Build a separate demo store for each new niche, even if you already run other stores. Mention your existing experience to suppliers and say the new store launches on a specific date (2023). [L0934.164]
  • When products do not fit the theme of the first store, open a second store for them instead of forcing them in. The second store reportedly performed well within about three months (older, 2020 is not stated; source undated). [L0832.028]
  • Do not create several identical clone stores with the same name, address and phone number to split a niche. This caused Google Merchant Center suspensions for one operator (older, 2021). Instead, after one sub-niche sells best in the main store, create a new store containing only those products. [L1090.047]
  • To turn an existing low-ticket store into a high-ticket store, keep the same Shopify account, payment setup and categories when the niche is related. Replace the main products with more expensive items, update the logo if needed, and link a new domain in the Shopify admin (older, 2019). [L1002.079]

Reusing a store when the niche is wrong

  1. Change the store name in the Shopify backend. [L1069.025]
  2. Replace the logo. [L1069.025]
  3. Replace all custom images that carry the old branding. [L1069.025]
  4. Check that the colour scheme still fits the new niche. [L1069.025]
  • The Shopify account can stay as it is, so this is easier than starting from scratch. [L1069.025]
  • If the niche of a Shopify store turns out to be wrong, the store can be reused by swapping products and connecting it to a different domain name in the Shopify settings. This avoids building a new store from scratch (older, 2020). [L1111.186]
  • An existing Shopify store can be completely rebuilt from scratch, including the domain name, theme, code and all products (older, 2022). [L1092.044]

Additional stores and domains

Costs of extra stores

  • Each separate Shopify store costs about $30. Run several stores inside one account with separate themes instead of opening new accounts. [L1131.032]
  • Keep the old theme as a saved version. Seasonal stores, such as a Christmas store, can be republished later with the same logo and domain setup. [L1131.033]
  • Old store versions remain stored as themes, so you can switch back and forth. [L1131.030, L1131.033]

Several stores on one account (procedure)

  1. Create each new store with the same email address already used for the account. [L1428.024]
  2. Go to shopify.com > Log in with email and click the green button "Create another store". [L1428.024]
  3. All stores owned by that email then appear in a list in the account, for example on the left side. [L1428.024]

Duplicating a store

  1. Create the new Shopify store with another email. [L1436.072]
  2. In the old store, go to Online Store > Themes, download the theme and export it. [L1436.072]
  3. Import the theme into the new store. [L1436.072]
  4. Export the product and customer data and import it into the new store. [L1436.072]
  5. Export the email and SMS lists (for example from Klaviyo or SMS Bump) and import them into the new store. [L1436.072]
  6. Rebuild any settings that do not transfer, such as logo and notification settings. [L1436.072]

Transfers

  • Transferring a Shopify store is complicated because the domain, the revenue source and account control all have to move cleanly. Many sellers use a broker or transfer service to handle it. [L1468.033]

Copying competitor structures

Finding and comparing competitors

  • Koala Inspector, a browser extension, shows which apps, themes, and upselling or cross-selling techniques competitor Shopify stores use. It also displays their best-selling products, revealing the sales tactics that work in your niche. [L0280.044]
  • CopyFile is a French-language store analysis tool. It shows a store's theme, lets you export its code and images, tracks daily revenue and a 30-day forecast, shows best sellers and recent orders, and looks up advertising networks. The lesson reports its revenue numbers matched the presenter's own store. It costs 77 euros per month. [L0387.031]
  • A collection URL with the best-selling sort order can reveal the best-selling products of any Shopify store. Append the path collections/all?sort_by=best-selling to the store domain. This works only on Shopify stores. [L0388.014]
  • To compare a product across stores, search for the supplier's SKU first. If it does not show up, search by the product name and check whether the product exists on the other site. [L0894.022]
  • To check whether two stores have the same owner: [L0656.023]
    1. Open the Contact Us page or the Terms of Service page of each store. [L0656.023]
    2. Note the email address shown on each page. [L0656.023]
    3. If the emails are identical, treat it as the final confirmation that the same people run both stores. [L0656.023]

Documenting differences (2023)

  • When analysing competitor stores, document specific differences in writing: product descriptions (length, detail level, technical specs), features or benefits highlighted, warranty or guarantee mentions, shipping or return policies, pricing structure, page layout, customer testimonials, and social proof elements. This documentation guides what improvements to make on your own store. [L0251.057]

Source D method: find, copy, adapt

  1. Find the product and list five to ten other stores that sell it. [L0600.003]
  2. Open each store and pick the parts that look proven: offer, photos, FAQ, reviews, testimonials and page sections. [L0600.003]
  3. Rebuild the offer on your store with the Coaching Bundles app from the App Store. [L0600.003]
  4. Copy the photos, FAQ block, reviews and testimonials that fit your product. [L0600.003]
  5. Copy the section layouts: video carousel, before-and-after block, timeline block and testimonial slider. [L0600.003]
  6. Adapt everything to your own theme and add your own brand name to copied images. [L0600.003]
  7. Launch the test store within about two days of finding the product. [L0600.003]
  8. Once the test produces sales, customise the landing page so it looks unique. [L0600.003]

Rules for copying

  • Copy only small parts from many competitor sites instead of large parts from one site. A store that mirrors a single competitor looks like a copy and loses credibility. [L0600.007]
  • Many competitor stores start with a basic design and add more polished elements once daily sales are high. One observed store doing over $10,000 per day had added these elements after its early phase. [L0600.014]
  • Copying the layout of a proven competitor store is a faster starting point than designing from scratch, and it lets you start testing sooner. A store selling the same product has already been shaped by what works in that niche. [L1249.001]
  • Building a store from scratch takes a few hours, or almost half a day, for an experienced builder. Budget that time per store when planning pre-launch work. [L1249.002]
  • Model your site on at least two to three competitors, borrowing their photos, text and layout structure. Then take the best elements from each and combine them into your own design, which is expected to outperform any single competitor. [L0897.005]
  • Beginners in dropshipping often do not know what a winning store looks like, so studying proven stores makes building far easier. Modelling your store on existing successful stores avoids starting from zero. [L0897.004]

Competitor research before the build (Source D 2026)

  1. Choose the product and confirm it through product research. [L0897.002]
  2. Find three to five competitor stores that sell the same product and list them in the product research sheet. [L0897.002]
  3. Take a screenshot of each competitor's homepage. [L0897.002]
  4. Submit the competitor list and screenshots before starting to build your own site. [L0897.002]
  5. If fewer than three competitors exist, tell the program team instead of stopping the process. [L0897.002]
  6. Build the site only after this research is complete and submitted. [L0897.002]
  • Competitor homepage screenshots must be made and shown before the store is built, not after launch. Some programs refuse to approve a site built without this prior competitor research. [L0897.003]
  • Building a store from scratch without studying competitors often produces weak quality. Fixing a poor site after launch can take about a week of revision and is harder than getting it right the first time. [L0897.006]
  • Building the site first and sending competitor research afterwards wastes time, because a poor build then needs a full week of revision. Competitor research should always come before the build starts. [L0897.007]
  • The source range for competitors differs: five to ten stores to browse for proven parts (above), three to five to submit in the research sheet (above). Use five to ten as your browsing pool and three to five as the list you submit. [L0600.003, L0897.002]

Store for a proven product (Source D 2026)

  1. Find a winning brand in your niche with long-running ads in the Meta Ad Library. [L1479.019]
  2. Study its product offer and page structure as a reference. [L1479.019]
  3. Use the ChatGPT prompt to rewrite its copy for your product and target avatar. [L1479.019]
  4. Paste the rewritten text into your store. [L1479.019]
  5. Add a few product images and short, clear texts, and keep the design clean. [L1479.019]
  6. Add testimonials, an FAQ section and custom reviews. [L1479.019]
  • A proven layout copied from another successful store is a far cheaper starting point than hiring a web developer for around $30,000 USD to build the whole store, which is not necessary for a store like this (older, source undated). [L1325.025]
  • A store-build service can include design, uploading demo products and content pages, and setting up Google Analytics and Google webmaster tools. It does not include getting approved with suppliers, which the owner must still do (older, 2020). [L0852.115]

Blog and content

What counts as content and when to start

  • Content in this framework means anything on a site that is not a product page or an admin page, such as blog posts, infographics, YouTube videos, images, category or collection pages, eBooks, checklists and user-generated content. Product pages alone cannot fill the gap in the customer journey (older, Source B 2021-22). [L0414.020]
  • Creating blog posts is optional and not required before supplier approval. [L0310.002]
  • Blog content is not needed from day one. Add it only after paid traffic is performing well and profitable (older, 2021). [L1279.042]
  • Blog content does not bring early sales. Do not copy other sites' articles. If you need content, have it written on freelance platforms such as Upwork or Fiverr, or buy PLR (private label rights) content (older, 2020). [L1277.057]
  • In the first stage, focus on the store itself, conversion optimisation and paid ads. Content and blog posts come later, and a test site does not need blog posts (older, 2020). [L1277.058]
  • Blog pages and blog posts should not be built or written during the initial site design. Blogging starts only after the business has launched, because it wastes time before launch (older, Source B 2021-22). [L1463.006]
  • The blog can be a stated preference on the same domain: run it on the same website as the store, for example on WordPress with WooCommerce, so readers move between articles and products without leaving the domain. This is a stated preference rather than a proven requirement. [L0382.043]
  • Use the native Shopify blog feature for content. SEO results depend on the theme, post optimisation and plugin speed, not on the platform (older, 2021). [L0962.034]

Blog structure and admin

  • Create one blog per store. Shopify allows multiple blogs, but the standard practice is to use a single blog per store. [L0310.003]

  • Blog post creation in Shopify:

    Navigation: Admin Panel > Online Store > Blog Posts

    To create a post: Click "Create Blog Post"

    Fields:

    • Title: the blog post headline (example: "Top Stand-Up Paddle Boards, Top Surfboards for 2024")
    • Document editor: area to insert text, images, and videos
    • Excerpt: summary text displayed on the homepage or main blog directory
    • Featured Image: image displayed at the top of the blog post
    • Author name: writer's name; can be ghostwritten
    • Blog: selection of which blog the post belongs to
    • Blog post template: use the default template (do not change)
    • Comments: toggle to enable or disable reader comments
    • Search Engine Preview: editable SEO metadata

    [L0310.004]

  • Shopify Magic is a built-in AI tool marked by a star icon in the blog editor. Use it to rephrase, extend, or simplify blog post content. [L0310.005]

  • Shopify automatically creates one default blog category named 'news' when the store is set up. All blog posts go to this category by default unless additional blog categories are created. [L0312.010]

  • To find and change a blog post's category, open the blog post in your admin panel, scroll to the Organization section, and select or change the blog category. Blog categories are created by clicking 'create a new blog' in the Organization section. [L0312.011]

  • To change the blog author name: go to Online Store > Blog posts, open the post, then in the right sidebar under Organization change the Author field (2023). [L0855.094]

  • Hidden blog posts do not appear in the blog listing of the theme. Each post must be set to visible before it shows up in the listing. [L1265.007]

  • Create an editorial in Shopify under Online Store > Blog posts by adding a new article in the blog, or under Online Store > Pages for a standalone page. Page builder apps such as PageFly or GemPages can be used to design the layout. [L0578.027]

  • Shopify supports both the blog and the pages function for topical authority articles, so either can be used for that content. Collection pages are created as separate category pages in the store. [L1389.023]

  • Shopify has a built-in blog function, so holiday blog posts can be created directly in the Shopify admin without extra apps. [L1224.035]

  • Blogs are built into Shopify. Create one from the Shopify admin by clicking create blog and then adding posts. This is the simplest place to publish SEO content for seasonal promotions (2025). [L1287.032]

Help and AI tools for content work

  • Use Shopify live chat for platform-specific setup questions instead of guessing. Ask exactly what you want to do so the answer is correct the first time (older, 2022). [L0963.047]
  • For Shopify setup steps, follow the official Shopify help documentation because it is kept up to date. The admin AI assistant is trained on Shopify's own help center and can troubleshoot a step you are stuck on (2024). [L1285.038]
  • Never let an AI tool write directly into the live Shopify store. Do the QA outside Shopify first, and only then apply the changes in bulk. [L1269.041]
  • When using custom rules with Shopify and a Claude MCP connection, set the rules for internal links explicitly, then have the connection apply only what those rules allow. Keep a manual review step before the rules are executed. [L1269.042]

Seasonal setup

Holiday and seasonal stores

  1. Have a store template and product catalogue ready before Q4 begins. [L0309.037]
  2. Add multiple product categories (Black Friday, Christmas, Valentine's Day, evergreen) to one store. [L0309.037]
  3. Run different ad campaigns and creative variations per product, but fulfil all orders through the same store to centralise inventory and customer data. [L0309.037]
  • Create a seasonal section and header message before a holiday, such as a Mother's Day gifts section with a note that gifts arrive in time for May 13. Make it visible early enough for buyers to order and receive the gift before the date. [L0355.009]
  • A holiday gift store should look built for the occasion. Use a brand name, logo, colours and wording tied to the holiday, and list only a few products. Shoppers who worry about choosing the wrong gift buy more confidently in a store that looks designed for that purpose. [L1214.016]
  • Keep old store versions such as a seasonal Christmas store as saved themes, and republish them later with the same logo and domain setup. [L1131.033]

Store build or outsourcing

Two paths and what each delivers

  • A store can be built in two ways: do it yourself by installing the provided theme and modifying it, or outsource the design to a specialised dropshipping web design team. Both paths lead to a store ready for supplier approval. [L1061.003]
  • You can build your demo store yourself by completing all Module 3 lessons, or outsource the entire build to the Source A Done-For-You team to receive a finished, supplier-ready store. Either way, you retain 100% ownership. [L0069.005]
  • Outsourcing store design is an acceptable choice, while the owner keeps control of supplier and traffic decisions. Many owners build their first store themselves and outsource design once the business earns money (older, 2019). [L0467.043]
  • Outsourcing the Shopify store build to a paid setup service is optional and costs extra on top of any training or programme fee. The seller can always build the store personally instead (older, Source B 2021-22). [L1466.001]
  • A done-for-you store build means a team builds a store from a proven design after checking the niche. Build quality is guaranteed, but revenue or a fixed return is not. An established profitable store sells at a multiple of its monthly net profit rather than at the build price. [L0941.103]
  • A custom dropshipping store built by an outsourced web design team is typically delivered in about five business days, so the builder does not have to go through the store build steps themselves. [L1061.004]

What a done-for-you design changes (2026)

  • A done-for-you design swaps the theme only. Existing products, brands, pages and customisations are kept, and policy pages are updated to meet Google compliance. [L0808.021]
  • Done-for-you store packages can differ. Some do not include Google account setup, so check what a package includes before buying. [L0808.022]
  • When a done-for-you design service rebuilds an existing Shopify store, the designer works through a collaborator code in draft mode. The current store is not deleted and no new Shopify account is created. Existing products, brands, pages and customisations carry over, and only the theme and policy pages change. [L0808.053]

Ordering a done-for-you build (2020, older)

  1. Enrol in the web design programme. [L0967.046]

  3. The design team builds the store and uploads demo products. [L0967.046]

  • Before ordering a web design build, completing the first two training modules is enough. The remaining modules do not need to be finished before the store is built. [L0967.103]
  • For the first store, do not build two stores at once. Start with one store, because mistakes made with the blueprint steps can only be fixed once. [L0967.051]
  • Learn Shopify while doing a task (older, 2020):
    1. Open the training video on one side of the screen and the Shopify admin, logged in, on the other side. [L0967.047]
    2. Watch about 30 to 60 seconds of the video, then pause it. [L0967.047]
    3. Repeat each click in Shopify while the video is paused. [L0967.047]
    4. Continue segment by segment until the task is done. [L0967.047]

Starting a done-for-you design after purchase (2022, older)

Queue and timing rules

  • Web design orders are queued first come, first served, based on when the client submits the store information form, not when they purchase. (2023) [L0804.041]
  • A store cannot be rebuilt for a different niche once the web design has been built from scratch. A new niche would require a second store, which is effectively a second job. Small tweaks are still possible (2023). [L0804.042]
  • Delay the store build until content shows some traction, such as a few thousand views, since a store built before any demand can waste about 10 hours. Build the store in parallel with content only if you have no web-building experience. [L1380.033]
  • A beginner's full build: learn first, then build one reusable store personally (procedure):
    1. Build the store yourself rather than paying a freelancer for each product. [L1212.037]
    2. Use the free course section about building a store with the Refresh team. [L1212.037]
    3. Watch the video on building a branded product page with PageFly and implement it at the same time. [L1212.037]
    4. Use a free website template from the course (Website Building channel, Paper Loop template). [L1212.037]
    5. Create a landing page with PageFly, which is free. [L1212.037]
    6. Run ads to the store. [L1212.037]

Build yourself first, then delegate

  • Learn to build a Shopify store yourself first, including theme, colour scheme, branding, copywriting and apps, so you know which design gets the best results. Only then delegate the build, giving the person your exact structure. [L1358.008, L1360.009]
  • Learning Shopify store building is a lasting skill, not only for your current dropshipping business. Owning it lets you judge a store build and decide what to change in the theme, branding and copy. [L1358.026]
  • A delegated store build can save a day or two of work. That time can be used to prepare creatives and set up Facebook or TikTok ad campaigns. [L1358.009, L1360.010]
  • A delegated store setup can include the theme, logos and branding, the copy, the apps you specify, and the payment providers, so the store is ready to run ads. [L1358.010]
  • The best case is a team member who already has a product they want to test. Give them your store structure, the apps to install and the branding requirements so they can set the store up for ads. [L1358.027, L1360.010]
  • Delegation steps:
    1. Build the store yourself at least once to learn which theme, design, branding and apps work for you. [L1360.011]
    2. Give the team member a product that you want to test. [L1360.011]
    3. Tell them to build the store following your structure. [L1360.011]
    4. Have them create the logos and branding, following your guidelines. [L1360.011]
    5. Tell them which apps to install and how to configure them, especially Klaviyo and SMS Bump. [L1360.011]
    6. Once trust is established, have them install the payment providers so the store is up and running. [L1360.011]

Freelancers and task services

  • Hiring developers or designers for store builds can be done through freelancers who specialise in Shopify. Expect the developer's skill to matter more than their theme knowledge, since HTML skills transfer across themes and platforms (older, 2021). [L0801.096]
  • The course's store design and VA sourcing is done via Upwork or Taskrabbit, with Source A support for small jobs. No internal VA list is provided (older, 2022). [L0969.093]
  • Every few months, have a freelancer audit the store for broken pages, page speed problems and oversized images that should be compressed. Use this as a project-based task rather than a permanent staff position. [L0921.011]
  • For most Shopify store tasks, spend about 30 minutes using Shopify support documentation and search before paying for help. Hire a Shopify developer only for custom coding beyond that (2024). [L1115.060]

B2B and trade pages

Why a trade page

  • For business-to-business buyers such as interior designers, a separate trade page with its own contact form and a list of trade buyers' logos builds trust and repeat orders. Logos can be added as the list of past trade customers grows (2024). [L0360.022]
  • High-ticket industrial products can be offered to business buyers on a separate page or collection named To The Trade. The page should describe business-to-business sales, list the industrial products, and include a contact form so buyers can request quotes for large orders before buying (2022). [L0963.156]

Adding a trade page (2022, older)

  1. Create a page in Shopify named to the trade. [L0429.036]
  2. State that bulk orders and trade discounts are available. [L0429.036]
  3. Add a contact form where buyers can request a custom quote. [L0429.036]
  • No other change to the marketing approach is needed. [L0429.036]

Adding a trade page (2025)

  1. Create a page that duplicates the contact page, including the contact form. [L0972.023]
  2. Replace the top heading with To the Trade and add trade copy describing bulk discounts, custom quotes and expedited shipping. [L0972.023]
  3. Link the page in the footer menu, and sometimes in the header menu. [L0972.023]
  4. Once business orders arrive, add a list of businesses you have sold to, to show credibility. [L0972.023]

Trade page text template (2025)

To the Trade

We work with [interior designers / businesses / contractors] nationwide for all of their [client / project / office] needs. We offer expedited shipping, bulk discounts and custom quotes.

For a business order, call us at [phone number], email us at [email address], or fill out the form below with your name, email address, phone number, the items you are interested in, the quantity, and the date you need them by. We will reach out to you right away.

Brands we have worked with: [logo list of business customers you have sold to]

[L0972.024]

Contact form and wholesale accounts (older, 2020)

  • On a "To the Trade" page, use a contact form instead of a separate order form, with these fields: name, email, phone number, business name, product of interest and number of units. Mention that bulk or quantity discounts are available on the page. [L0961.013]
  • Create B2B wholesale accounts with a Shopify app that assigns account-level pricing. Logged-in trade customers then see lower prices and can add discounted items to their cart. [L1088.030]

Learning resources and help sources

Free learning resources and reference tools

  • Free learning resources for web development include the YouTube channels Web Dev Simplified and Fireship, plus free online programming courses from Google. Books are also recommended for learners who prefer reading. [L0709.047]
  • Stack Overflow is useful for finding fixes to specific technical problems, though its answers are very technical for beginners. Reddit can be used to ask which developers people recommend, but beginners should expect blunt replies. [L0709.048]
  • The Shopify Help Center at help.shopify.com documents adding products, product details, variants, product media, collections and importing and exporting. Use it to check small questions about fields or variants. [L1166.002]
  • Build an operations resource list for each team member by collecting the Shopify Help Center guides for these tasks: creating collections, creating orders, and creating discounts. Add the links to the corporate board so staff can learn on their own (older, Source B 2021-22). [L1396.007]

Task-based services for small jobs

  • Task Husky is a marketplace for small, one-off site jobs such as installing a widget on a product page. Prices start from about US$39 per task, which is far cheaper than hiring an agency for a full site relaunch (older, Source B 2021-22). [L0719.031]
  • Freelance marketplaces such as Upwork often focus on full site relaunches, so they can be a poor fit for minor updates. Use task-based services for small refreshes and check the price before committing (older, Source B 2021-22). [L0719.032]
