4.1 Platform choice, Shopify plan, trial and account setup

Why Shopify

  • Shopify is the platform this handbook uses for every store, because it is built only for e-commerce and covers domain setup, storefront design, product management, inventory tracking, payment processing, checkout, analytics, email marketing, live chat, and built-in connections to Google and Facebook sales channels [L0069.004, L1062.005, L1304.001].
  • Shopify's all-in-one scope means separate tools are not needed for store design, product management, payments, inventory, email, order tracking, live chat and analytics [L1304.001].
  • Shopify is the recommended primary platform for dropshipping. It has superior integrations and technology built for e-commerce fulfillment, and it allows unlimited product uploads on day one [L0280.007].
  • Shopify is recommended for dropshipping stores because of its supplier-friendly integrations and app ecosystem. Other site builders are not recommended for stores that will run paid ads. (Older, 2022.) [L0665.132]
  • Shopify was chosen over newer builders for its maturity and feature depth. The newer alternatives were considered too young for a serious store at the time of the source. [L0941.038]
  • The course recommends Shopify as the single platform for every store. It is described as the market leader for about ten years, with the best platform and support. Using one account for all tools is preferred over combining several tools. [L1304.002]
  • Shopify is the managed platform. The merchant does not host, secure or maintain the software. [L1283.107]
  • Shopify has rarely been down for stores. Custom setups add hosting latency and maintenance work. A custom build would need separate APIs, such as PayPal and Stripe, to replicate the checkout, supplier connections and order back end that Shopify includes. [L0788.028]
  • Shopify handles the frontend, which is the storefront customers see, and the backend, which runs ordering, checkout, and product and order management. The merchant works in the backend every day, and customers only see the frontend. [L1314.004]
  • A hosted platform already includes ordering, checkout, order management and product management, so a beginner only adds design and content. This avoids a custom website, which typically costs tens of thousands of dollars. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1314.001]
  • A first business should not begin with a large upfront website development cost, because that money is hard to recover if the business fails. The priority is to launch quickly on a platform that is easy to use. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1314.002]
  • Shopify is recommended for beginners because it combines ease of use, easy daily management and cost-effectiveness. BigCommerce, Magento, WooCommerce and WordPress can also work, but they are harder for newcomers. An experienced builder can use another platform if they already know it well. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1314.003]
  • Shopify is used to build an online store in minutes or hours without coding or web development knowledge, using free themes and apps. [L0760.003]
  • The build is done step by step and does not require prior website experience. [L1060.006]
  • Shopify is for beginners with little or no coding experience. It is mobile responsive and handles security topics such as data encryption, fraud prevention and PCI compliance. [L1313.005]
  • Shopify can be used for both physical and digital products. [L1313.008]
  • Full-time support is available by chat, plus an active community. Answers to most common questions can be found through Google or the Shopify support pages. [L1313.007]
  • Large consumer brands such as Kylie Cosmetics, Tesla Motors and Gym Shark run their e-commerce on Shopify, which shows the platform works at very large scale. [L1313.009]
  • Many of the largest e-commerce stores still run on Shopify, and a store can grow on Shopify for a long time before a platform change is needed. (Older, 2019.) [L0960.074]
  • Shopify is suited to beginners starting a new business from scratch, and it is the platform used to build the high-ticket store in this course. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1481.018]
  • The coaching team supports only Shopify stores and does not work with WooCommerce. Feedback on a non-Shopify site will be limited. [L1090.146]
  • Build the store directly on Shopify rather than building elsewhere and importing it, because imported stores often need a lot of rework. (Older, 2021.) [L0671.136, L0671.271]
  • A Shopify store stays on the Shopify platform. Moving to another e-commerce platform later requires an app-based migration and substantial rework. (Older, 2021.) [L0671.137, L0671.272]
  • Moving a Shopify store to a self-hosted platform is not seamless. A developer can model the existing store on a new platform, since Shopify's structure is well established and widely understood. (Older, 2019.) [L0960.075]
  • Shopify takes a percentage of each sale, so the platform's cost depends on sales volume (see the plan section below). [L0694.024]
  • Shopify is a managed platform that is recommended over self-hosted alternatives, which require the merchant to handle hosting, security and customer data protection. [L1283.107]
  • Marketplaces such as eBay and Facebook Marketplace bring organic traffic because users visit with purchase intent. Google search traffic requires strong SEO or paid ads. Shopify has harder initial traffic acquisition, but it enables better long-term retargeting and scaling. [L0280.010]

Alternatives and when they fit

  • WooCommerce is self-hosted and needs separate hosting and plug-ins. Shopify keeps hosting, security and support in one platform. (Older, 2021.) [L0247.091]
  • WooCommerce plug-ins and add-ons are often paid. Compare the total add-on cost with Shopify before choosing WooCommerce. [L1283.107]
  • WooCommerce is not recommended for dropshipping. (Older, 2022.) [L0803.057]
  • Magento only makes sense for stores with around 50,000 to 100,000 or more products. (Older, 2022.) [L0803.057]
  • Do not mix WordPress and Shopify for new stores. Shopify's blogging system and SEO capabilities are excellent. Combine WordPress with Shopify only if you already own an established WordPress site with significant history and traffic, such as a blog subdomain. (Older, 2022.) [L0249.029]
  • Use WordPress for standalone blogs or review sites that earn affiliate commissions. Use Shopify for stores that sell products directly. (Older, 2021.) [L1279.041]
  • WordPress and BigCommerce are alternatives with similar ecosystems to Shopify. [L0788.028]
  • ClickFunnels creates single-page checkout funnels. Shopify product pages achieve the same conversion goals at lower cost, with better e-commerce integrations. Shopify is preferred for dropshipping because it was designed for e-commerce fulfillment, not generic funnel building. [L0280.045]
  • Shopify is the only store platform used for these stores. Funnel builders such as ClickFunnels are not used in place of Shopify. (Older, 2022.) [L0429.069]
  • Shopify gives the seller another sales channel and lets them send traffic from Google and Bing to their own products. A separate store also avoids losing buyers to other products on Amazon, because visitors who land on Amazon often buy something else. [L1153.059]
  • A Shopify store makes retargeting, upsells and email capture easier than Amazon does. It keeps the focus on your own products, which is hard to do with Amazon's search results. [L1153.060]
  • Compare the conversion rate of a digital product on Shopify with PayHip before choosing a platform. Test both with the same offer to see which converts better. [L1216.028]
  • PayHip allows PayPal and Stripe to be connected, the two most important payment options for digital products. Check the platform fee per purchase before choosing it, since the lesson does not state the fee. [L1216.029]

Moving an existing Wix store to Shopify

  • Move from Wix to Shopify for an e-commerce store, because Shopify is built only for e-commerce and has more apps and built-in tools. Keep existing logos, images and written content when migrating. [L0359.042]
  • Migration procedure (older, 2021):
    1. Look for a dedicated importer app. None was found for Wix at the time of the source. [L0962.035]
    2. Export the products from Wix as a CSV file. [L0962.035]
    3. Import the CSV into Shopify. [L0962.035]
    4. Recreate the collections, pages and text manually in Shopify. [L0962.035]

  • One source gives about 79 USD per month for Basic and almost 300 USD per month for Advanced. The 2021 table lists 79 USD for the middle Shopify tier and 29 USD for Basic. The two sources disagree; verify on the current plan page. [L0390.052, L0370.016]

Shopify Plus thresholds (conflicting figures; each applies to a different claim)

  • Upgrade to Plus only if processing fee savings exceed a minimum of 5,000 USD per month. (Older, 2020.) [L0268.017]
  • Custom Checkout is available only on the 2,300 USD per month Plus plan. Standard plans do not include custom checkout. (2024.) [L0276.017]
  • Plus is required for full control over checkout design and costs at least 2,000 USD per month, which makes it unsuitable for most new stores. Checkout apps that inject code are fragile and often stop working over time. [L0390.045]
  • Plus only makes sense for stores at nine-figure or mid eight-figure revenue. Below that level it is not worth the extra cost. (Older, 2022.) [L0963.044]
  • Verify the current Plus price and the revenue condition before deciding. The sources in this set do not agree on the minimum. [L0268.017, L0276.017, L0390.045, L0963.044]

Plan restrictions that change the choice

  • The Shopify 39 USD per month plan does not include staff accounts. Agencies or partners use a collaborator invitation instead, since partner access does not require a staff account. [L0667.039]
  • Basic plan changed on 24 January without notice, so it can only sell in free markets and cannot run multi-market setups. Check plan limits regularly, because a plan change can break an existing store configuration. [L0694.023]

Choosing and switching a plan

  • Start a new Shopify store on the Basic plan, not on Shopify or Advanced Shopify. Higher plans are worth it only once a store has more staff or a large volume of sales. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0370.011]
  • The switch point to a higher plan is approximately 30,000 to 40,000 USD monthly revenue, according to the 2021-22 lesson. Verify this against current pricing. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0370.011]
  • Higher plans charge a lower transaction percentage per sale, so a higher plan such as Advanced can pay off at high monthly sales, because the lower percentage adds up across many orders. [L0694.024]
  • On Shopify Advanced, the transaction percentage was about 1 percentage point lower than on the plan below it. Advanced was cheaper when billed yearly rather than monthly. [L0694.046]
  • Billing yearly was cheaper than monthly for Advanced. [L0694.024]
  • Shopify offers a discounted annual price when paying up front. Committing to a multi-year term is not advisable at the start. Monthly billing keeps the cost flexible while the store is unproven. The annual option can be considered once the store's sales and staffing needs are clear. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0370.018]
  • A plan upgrade can come with a temporary free period, such as three free months on Shopify Advanced. After it ends, the store may revert to Basic without notice. Note the expiry date, because a reversion can cancel multi-market settings that depend on the higher plan. [L0694.045]

Trial offers and how to verify them

Standard trial

  • Shopify offers a free 14-day trial that does not require a credit card at sign-up. Paid plans start from about USD 29 per month after the trial. [L1314.005]
  • The standard trial was 14 days in the 2020-2022 sources. (Older, 2020-22.) [L1277.052, L0771.033]
  • A credit card is still required to start a plan. [L0370.017]
  • The trial period must be paid for only after you have chosen a plan before the trial ends. [L1304.021]
  • During the free trial no payment is due, but the Select a plan button in the backend must be used to choose a paid plan before the trial ends. Nothing needs to be selected during the first days of the trial. [L1314.015]
  • Start the 14-day free trial before choosing a plan. In the lesson, selecting a plan first was said to lose the trial. Do not pay for the Shopify plan during the trial unless the product is already making sales. [L1444.040]
  • A new store can be built and worked on during the 3-day free trial without selecting a plan. The plan and the 1 USD first-month offer only need to be chosen before the trial ends, when Shopify prompts for a subscription. [L1304.021]

Verification rules

  • Confirm the trial terms before you rely on them. Promotions change, and the same offer may not be shown to everyone. Check the current price and offer on the Shopify checkout page before subscribing. [L1323.003]
  • Promotional offers reached through a referral or affiliate link can differ from the public offer. Compare the price on the official Shopify site before paying for a plan. [L1323.005]
  • The 3-month-for-1-USD offer was not shown to everyone and appeared to depend on the user's IP address. To claim it, open the link and create the account right away, because the offer can disappear once the IP is recognised. [L0372.022]
  • The trial shows at the top of the new Shopify admin. The store must be on a paid plan before launching. [L1292.027]
  • If a trial has ended, a trial extension can be requested from Shopify support. It gives 14 additional days. Support may refuse if the offer is only for qualifying participants. (Older, 2022 reference.) [L0372.023]
  • Each new account can ask Shopify support for an extra two weeks, extending a 14-day trial to 28 days. This can be done regardless of how far into the trial the account is. [L1380.034]
  • Reported in 2022: several stores under one account may each get a 14-day trial. Verify this in Shopify before relying on it. [L1444.041]

Cancelling during the trial

  • The plan selection screen allows a plan to be cancelled before the trial ends without any charge. Choose the plan and note the trial end date so that a cancellation can be made in time. Remaining free trial days are kept before the first payment. [L0370.017]
  • Forgetting to cancel before a free trial ends results in the full monthly plan price being charged. The checkout states the cancellation deadline, so note the date when you sign up. [L1323.004]

One account per store

  • Each separate store needs its own Shopify account and bill. A single Google account can manage several stores, but keep them separate to make a later sale of one store easier. [L0359.044]
  • Each Shopify store needs its own Shopify account. An unused second account should be cancelled to avoid paying for it. [L0471.025]
  • One Shopify account equals one store, regardless of the plan. A single account cannot host several separate niche stores. (Older, 2021.) [L0853.082]
  • Each separate store needs its own account, because one account supports one store. The name chosen at signup becomes the permanent myshopify.com address to which custom domains are attached. (Older, 2019.) [L1002.018]
  • Each new store requires a new account. The same email address can be used to switch between accounts after logging in. (Older, 2020.) [L1277.051]
  • Several accounts can share one login email using single sign-on, but one merchant account number cannot be used across several websites. [L0934.018]
  • Related stores can share a legal entity, but each unrelated group of stores should get its own entity. (Older, 2021.) [L1279.040]
  • Use a new email address for each new Shopify account to keep stores organised. (Older, 2022.) [L1444.041]
  • When creating a new account, use an email address that is the same as or closely similar to the TikTok account's email. This makes it easy to match each store to its accounts. [L1444.072]
  • The sources differ on whether one login can manage several stores. The Google login and single sign-on statements allow several accounts under one login, while the one-account-per-store statements keep each store separate. Keep one account per store, and verify trial counts in Shopify before relying on them. [L0359.044, L0934.018, L0853.082, L1002.018]

When to open the account

Two positions appear in the set. Use the one that fits your stage.

  • Do not open the Shopify account early. Open it when you start building the website, because early setup mistakes must otherwise be fixed later. The 14-day free trial is wasted if it runs before the build. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0458.033]
  • The 14-day free trial starts at signup, so do not open the account before the website build stage. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0771.033]
  • Opening the account before the website stage starts the trial too early, and early setup mistakes must then be fixed. Wait until the store build step, when you have a verified market and are ready to build. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0771.063]
  • Do not activate the Shopify plan before you build the store. [L0458.031]
  • Confirm Modules 1 and 2 are fully completed before using the Shopify extended trial link. [L0069.008]
  • A Shopify account is required whether you build the demo store yourself or the done-for-you team builds it, so it should be created first. [L1062.010]
  • Do not build a full store until the product is getting views and is near about 1,000 TikTok followers. A store is worth building once you see comments asking to buy, or once an Instagram profile with a link is driving buyers. [L1444.038]

Business email and signup details

Email address

  • Use a business email address for the Shopify account and all business communication, rather than a personal inbox. The verification email must be opened and confirmed within about 24 hours. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1314.014]
  • A personal email address can be used, including one from a personal account, because it can be changed later. [L1062.010]
  • A business email is not required. A regular personal email such as Gmail works. [L1313.001]
  • In the sign-up page, type the business email address into the Email address field. [L1314.008]

Verification

  • If the verification email from Shopify does not arrive, the Verify your email address card on the dashboard has a Resend verification email button. Clicking it sends the confirmation link again to the email address, so the account can be verified within the 24-hour window. [L1314.021]

Business details

  • The business entity name does not need to relate to your domain name or brand. Simple names such as ER Stores LLC or Online Stores LLC are acceptable. The entity name appears on bank accounts and tax documents; the domain is your public-facing store name. (Older, 2022.) [L0249.027]
  • A US address is not required to open a Shopify account. It is optional for business setup, and a virtual or physical US address can be added later if needed. (2024.) [L0360.021]
  • If you have no company yet, pick your home country as the business location (for example, the United States, or the country you live in). [L1313.001]
  • Onboarding Step 2 of 2 is titled Add an address so you can get paid. Its fields are First name, Last name, Address, Suburb, Country/Region, Phone, State/territory, Postcode and the optional Business or personal website. Below them is a checkbox labelled This store is a registered business, followed by the Enter my store button. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1314.009]
  • Onboarding Step 1 of 2 offers sales-channel choices. Under Online Store: Online Store, Social media and Online marketplaces. Under In person: At markets and fairs, At pop-ups and In brick and mortar retail stores. Selecting any in-person option leads to point-of-sale offers. The question Are you setting up a store for a client? is for agencies building stores for others. A solo online-only merchant should select only Online Store. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1314.018]

Store name and address

  • The name chosen at signup becomes the permanent myshopify.com address to which custom domains are attached. (Older, 2019.) [L1002.018]

Admin orientation

  • The Shopify admin is the back end where storefront design, product upload, product page design and inventory tracking are managed. The front end of the store is designed inside this admin. [L1304.017]
  • The left-hand navigation contains Home, Orders, Products, Customers, Analytics, Marketing, Discounts, Apps, Sales channels, Online Store and Settings at the bottom. Orders and products are managed daily here. Analytics and sales data start to fill in only after the store goes live. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1314.006]
  • Until the store is launched, the storefront is protected by a password, so the public cannot see it. A new store shows only a basic home page with no design or images until the theme is customised. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1314.007]
  • Practise Shopify edits on the free trial, which was 14 to 21 days at the time of recording, or on a copy of a store in progress. This prevents accidental deletions from damaging the live store. [L0921.012]
  • The dashboard card titled Your trial just started lists four first setup tasks: Add your first product, Customize theme, Add domain and Set up payments. Working through these four items in that order gives a new store its basic setup after account creation. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1314.020]
  • The Shopify admin home page shows a setup checklist for a new store with Add product, Customize theme, Add domain and Set up payments. These mark the main steps before launch. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1292.029]
  • Initial setup checklist on the Shopify admin Home page, in this order:
    1. Add store name. [L1323.002]
    2. Discover products to sell: install a recommended supplier app or add a product manually. [L1323.002]
    3. Set up Shopify Payments: click Activate. [L1323.002]
    4. Customize your online store: choose or generate a theme, then add logo, colors and images. [L1323.002]
    5. Review your shipping rates. [L1323.002]
    6. Customize domain: the store starts on a default myshopify.com address and a custom domain can be added. [L1323.002]
  • Settings > Plan shows the current plan, the trial terms (in the example, 3 months at $1 per month), the date monthly billing starts, and the payment method. Plan changes and switching to annual billing are made on this page. [L1305.007]
  • Settings > Billing shows the stored payment method, billing currency, active subscriptions including paid apps with their next bill, and previous charges. Use it to track what is paid to Shopify. [L1305.008]
  • Settings > Users shows who has access. The Pending tab shows requests from the agency. [L0472.037]
  • Shopify login problems can come from being logged into a different Shopify account. Use the admin URL (yourstore.com/admin) to log in to the correct store, or try an incognito window or a different browser. (2025.) [L0472.036]
  • To deactivate a Shopify store you created by mistake:
    1. Open the Shopify admin. [L1113.031]
    2. Click Settings. [L1113.031]
    3. Open Plan. [L1113.031]

Collaborator and staff accounts

Staff accounts

  • Shopify Staff Accounts let the store owner give team members limited access to the store instead of sharing the main login. (Older, 2021.) [L1089.003]
  • Create staff accounts for contractors and designers. Set permissions to restrict access to customer data, payment settings and financial information. Each staff member can have different permission levels. [L0276.030]
  • When bringing in a VA or helper, use staff permissions so they only get the access they need, such as editing store pages, and not financial or other sensitive areas. (Older, 2021.) [L1090.042]
  • Permissions to grant: Products, Catalogs, Online store and Store settings. Do not grant access to Orders, Draft orders or Customers. (2024.) [L1285.029]
  • Settings > Users and permissions > Add staff: invite a person (for example, a hired store builder) by name and email, and choose which sections of the store they can access. This gives them a separate login instead of sharing your username and password. [L1305.009]

Collaborator accounts

  • Shopify collaborator accounts give a staff member or outside partner access to a store without sharing your main login. Use them instead of sharing credentials. [L0675.002]
  • The collaborator request arrives by email once assets are ready. [L0472.037]
  • For agencies or partners on the 39 USD per month plan, use a collaborator invitation, since partner access does not require a staff account. [L0667.039]
  • Give a private supplier access by going to shopify.partners and inviting them as a partner with partnership access to your Shopify store. The supplier provides the step-by-step instructions, and the lesson does not name the exact menu labels. [L1156.010]

Cost of running the store

Fixed costs

  • Shopify is a fixed cost that must be paid every month while the store is running. Stopping payment can lead to the store being shut down. [L0759.011]
  • Shopify costs 29 USD per month when paid for a year in advance, or 39 USD per month when paid monthly. (2023.) [L0964.084]
  • The Shopify subscription at 29 USD per month is small compared with the cost of a physical retail store, which needs a lease, a rent deposit, build-out and fixtures. A store that makes only one 1,000 USD sale per month still covers the subscription cost. [L0759.033]
  • Shopify costs in the 2021 setting: a 14-day free trial, then 29 USD per month. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0771.033]

Transaction and add-on costs

  • Transaction fees per sale by plan are listed in the plans section: Basic 2.0%, Shopify 1.0%, Advanced 0.5% in the 2021 source. [L0370.012, L0370.016]
  • Add-on prices: POS Pro USD 89/month per location; Remove online store USD 9/month. (Older, 2021.) [L0370.016]
  • Paid apps appear on the Billing page with their next bill. [L1305.008]
  • For plans that cost more, Advanced was cheaper when billed yearly rather than monthly. [L0694.046]

Keeping the cost under control

  • Do not commit to a multi-year term at the start. Pay monthly while the store is unproven. [L0370.018]
  • Note the expiry date of any free period, because a reversion may cancel multi-market settings. [L0694.045]
  • Cancel any unused second account to avoid paying for it. [L0471.025]
  • Check plan limits regularly, since a plan change can break an existing store configuration. [L0694.023]

  • All stores in this model are built on the Shopify platform. Shopify is used to design the store, upload products, and manage orders. [L0759.008]
