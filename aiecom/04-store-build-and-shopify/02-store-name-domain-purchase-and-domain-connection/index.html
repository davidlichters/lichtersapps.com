4.2 Store name, domain purchase and domain connection

Naming the store and the domain

  • A domain is the web address for your online store and how customers reach it. Every store needs its own unique domain, for example the source's own site [L0079.001]
  • The domain is the root web address people type to reach the store. Check that the name is available and affordable before you commit to it. [L0336.003]
  • Choose the domain before you invest in the store build or the logo. Check availability first, because the name you want is often already taken. [L0520.001]
  • Check availability before you finalise brand or logo work. The.com version of a good name is often unavailable, so try variations of your product or brand name until you find one that is free and fits the brand. [L0520.008]
  • Business name and domain: choose the business name first, then check whether the matching domain is free before you commit. Brainstorm five to ten name ideas so a backup is ready if the favourite domain is taken. [L0522.001]
  • Keep the store name and the domain consistent. A business name does not have to match the domain, because the business name can stay broad enough for several businesses. Even so, keep the domain and the store as consistent as possible, ideally the same. [L0964.088, L0964.089]
  • A custom domain should match the Shopify store name so customers can find and trust it. [L1250.018, L1250.019]
  • The niche keyword is optional. Include it if it is available and inexpensive. A creative brand name that fits the theme is equally acceptable. [L0941.033, L0934.162]
  • A store name does not need to list every category. Buyers who search for a specific product will still buy from a store whose name does not mention it. [L0801.106]
  • The meaning of the domain matters little, as long as visitors can remember it. [L1325.026]
  • Choose a simple, memorable name. The domain can be kept while the brand name changes. One example reused a basic name after a niche rename. [L0661.053]
  • For testing, start with a broad, neutral brand name and only a few basic branding elements, because the offer and the creatives decide the first sales more than the logo does. For unrelated products, a general name such as a female first name works for testing. Add full branding once a product has its first sales. [L1428.026]

Practical name rules

  • Use.com, no dashes or hyphens, three words or fewer, and a name that is easy to remember and type. [L0941.033]
  • Do not use numbers or dashes, because they make the name harder to remember and to tell to others. [L0504.006]
  • Keep the name short and easy to remember, with no hyphens. [L1111.054]
  • Avoid unusual hyphens. Naming is free-form, but the name should be something customers remember. [L0964.086]
  • If the.com is unavailable, avoid a shortcut name with an odd ending. A clear form such as shop.brandname.com or brandname.com is better. [L0530.017]
  • Check that the name reads the same when spoken aloud. [L0468.058]
  • Use a generic company or LLC name rather than one tied to a single store, so several related stores in the same niche can share it. [L1090.046]
  • Google requires that an LLC's name is not used as a website. Build one website per sales store. The LLC itself needs no website. [L0934.163]

Naming procedure (Source B, 2021-22, older)

  1. Brainstorm 5 to 10 business name ideas that identify the market and stay within three words.
  2. Check each idea against Google searches for your product keywords to confirm it is unique in the market and not a close variant of a competitor's name.
  3. Pick the idea that resonates with you and whose matching domain is available.
  4. Confirm the domain is affordable, then buy it so the business name and domain match exactly. [L0336.014]

Domain-selection procedure (Source A, 2020, older)

  1. Choose a short, memorable domain with no hyphens that ends in.com.
  2. Check that the name reads the same when spoken aloud.
  3. Check the US trademark registry for conflicts before buying.
  4. Buy the domain through the Shopify admin, or through an outside registrar such as Namecheap at about $9 per year.
  5. Link the domain to the store before contacting suppliers. [L0468.058]

  • Google gives preference to domains that carry the local country designator. [L0522.002] (older, 2021-22)
  • A local extension is trusted more by local buyers than a.com. Example:.ch in Switzerland or.com.au in Australia. [L0549.038]
  • A country-specific extension can be chosen instead of.com when you target one specific country. [L0520.010] -.com is the first choice for a store domain because it is the most widely accepted..co is the second most common extension for e-commerce stores. [L0520.009]
  • A.com is recommended over.store or similar extensions, because customers type.com by default. [L0964.085, L0838.001]
  • For a US store, do not use.net,.store or.org. Customers who come back later will type.com out of habit and land on another site or none at all. [L0504.005]
  • For sales to US customers, do not use a.co.uk domain. Shoppers may hesitate to order from a store that appears not to ship to them. [L0967.101] (older, 2020)
  • In the US, only a.com is needed. In Australia,.com.au is preferred. Buying both.com and.com.au is possible but optional. [L0522.003] (older, 2021-22)
  • Use.com even when you sell in only one country, because shoppers remember and type.com by default. A.us domain is not recommended. [L0968.037] (older, 2021)
  • Meta Ad Library shows the domain to viewers. A.us or.store domain can make a pre-lander or advertorial look like a direct sales page, which can reduce trust. [L1327.045]
  • Example: a store that sources and sells only within one country used that country's extension,.com.au for Australia, as in an early home lighting store. [L1014.020] (older, 2021-22)

When the preferred name is taken

  • When the local extension of a name is taken, the search also suggests.net,.org,.online or.us. For a store aimed at one country, keep the local extension and change the name instead of switching to a generic extension. [L0522.018] (older, 2021-22)
  • When the.com is already taken, do not switch to a different-looking extension. Change the store name, or buy the.com if the owner is willing. A.shop address causes customers to mistrust email from a mismatched domain. [L0468.059] (older, 2020)

Checking availability, trademarks and history

Availability

  • Search the name in the store's domain search or at the registrar, with the correct local extension. If the result says available, add it to the cart. [L0522.004] (older, 2021-22)
  • In Shopify's Buy new domain search, the default results are suggestions. You can type a specific name you already chose straight into the search field. Delete any "http://" prefix and trailing slash before searching, then confirm the result says the domain is available. [L0521.018]
  • If a candidate domain's website cannot be reached, that usually means nobody is using it. Still confirm availability in the store's own domain search, because an unreachable site does not prove the name can be bought. [L0521.012]
  • A message that the domain is taken means someone already owns it, and it cannot be bought for that name. Do not force that name. Search the next backup name the same way and repeat until one shows as available with the correct extension. [L0522.005, L0522.006] (older, 2021-22)
  • AI chat tools cannot check real-time domain availability. Their suggestions may already be registered. Taken names often belong to someone else's site or are parked as premium domains for sale. [L0521.009]
  • Avoid buying domains that other people hold to sell at high prices. Choose a name that is actually available after brainstorming. [L0964.087]
  • Do not buy domain names for resale as a business idea. Many are held for thousands of dollars, and this is unnecessary. [L1111.055]

Trademark

  • Before buying, search the US trademark registry for conflicts. [L0468.058]
  • If a store name or domain may infringe a trademark, search the US trademark registry first. If it does, pick a new domain and link it to the existing Shopify store, which keeps all products and settings. [L0468.060] (older, 2020)

History checks (procedure)

  1. Pick the available domain you like best.
  2. Google "site:" followed by the domain name. Zero results are normal for a new name.
  3. Open the Google Transparency Report and enter the domain in the check site status field. "No data available" is normal.
  4. Open the Wayback Machine at archive.org and enter the domain to view its browse history.
  5. Only if all three checks show no problems, buy the domain. [L0504.014]
  • An old site: string search that returns old pages is acceptable if they are relevant to the niche. Spammy or scammy old pages are a red flag, and you should pick a different name. [L0504.015]
  • A domain can be available and still have a history of penalties, so the history checks are needed even when the name is free. A blacklisted domain, for example one that once carried a virus, is rare, roughly one in 500, but it can damage a store built on it. [L0504.016]
  • If the Wayback Machine has archived a domain, open its old snapshots to check for spam or scam content. Then search Google for the domain's past reviews. If there is no archive, the domain is effectively new, and that is fine. [L0504.017]

Price limits and premium domains

  • Do not pay more than $20 per year for a domain name. Premium domains are often offered for $500, $1,000, $5,000 or more, and buying one at that price is not worth it for a new store. [L0521.010]
  • Each store needs its own domain. Do not buy premium or parked domains at high prices. A similar domain can work just as well. [L0801.093] (older, 2021)
  • Avoid parked domains listed for resale, since they are often overpriced. Choose an open domain instead. [L0934.096] (2023 price: about $14 to $15 per year)
  • Keep the domain cost low: spend no more than about $14 per year on a domain name. Choose a generic name or one that contains your niche keyword. [L1090.046, L1090.045] (older, 2021)

Buying a domain in Shopify

Why Shopify

  • Shopify is the recommended place to buy a domain for a Shopify store. Common third-party registrars are GoDaddy, Namecheap and Google Domains. [L0520.002]
  • A domain bought through Shopify is live immediately, with no waiting for SSL and no DNS changes. This makes it the simplest option for a beginner. [L0520.003]
  • Buying in Shopify is the intended way to get a low-cost name for a new store. [L0521.011]
  • The Shopify purchase is quick and needs only a few clicks. [L0941.102]
  • Source A's 2019 advice was to buy the domain directly in Shopify for about $14 per year, rather than through an external registrar such as GoDaddy, which was advised against. Namecheap is another common registrar. (older, 2019) [L0356.027]
  • Buying in Shopify was used for ease of integration. [L0852.083] (older, 2020)
  • A domain bought in Shopify is added to the store's account and billed yearly at the price shown during purchase, for example $16 per year. The buyer owns the domain, so visitors who type that address reach the store. [L0521.017]
  • A domain bought through Shopify can be used in the same Shopify account and set as the primary domain. [L1382.060]

Limits of a Shopify-bought domain

  • A domain bought from Shopify cannot be moved to other platforms such as WooCommerce or ClickFunnels. The lesson's view is that this rarely matters if you stay on Shopify. [L0520.005]
  • A domain bought through Shopify can be transferred to another registrar such as Namecheap or GoDaddy. Every domain registrar allows transfers. [L1094.064] (older, 2023 source; conflicts with the platform limit above, which concerns platforms, not registrars)

Procedure A (Source A, undated)

  1. Log in to your Shopify admin.
  2. Click Settings.
  3. Click Domains.
  4. Ignore the default myshopify.com domain.
  5. Click Buy new domain.
  6. Enter your chosen domain name.
  7. Confirm availability and $16 per year pricing.
  8. Click Buy.
  9. The domain is added to your Shopify account.
  10. Shopify automatically assigns it as your primary domain. [L0079.009]

Procedure B (Source C, undated)

  1. In the Shopify admin, click Settings in the bottom left corner.
  2. Open Domains.
  3. Click Buy new domain.
  4. Type your desired name and check whether it is available.
  5. If the name is taken, adjust it and check again. Test several options.
  6. Select an extension, such as.com,.co, or a country-specific extension.
  7. Review the details and the price, then click Buy domain.
  8. Once purchased, the domain appears in the Domains section. You can set it as the primary domain so that visitors land on it. [L0520.007]

Procedure C (Source A, undated)

  1. Log in to the Shopify admin.
  2. Click Settings.
  3. Click Domains in the left navigation.
  4. Click "Buy new domain" at the top.
  5. Delete the "http://" prefix and the trailing slash from the entered name, then search for the domain.
  6. Check that the search result says the domain is available.
  7. Click "Buy" to add it to the account.

The domain is billed yearly, and it becomes the primary domain once purchased. [L0521.015]

Procedure D (Source A, 2024 path)

  • Open the Shopify admin, then go to Settings > Domains in the left navigation to view and buy domains for the store. [L0521.013]
  • Once the purchase is made, the domain shows in the list. Source A's 2024 lesson states that a new domain can be bought through Shopify (recommended for simplicity) or elsewhere, then linked under Settings > Domains, where it takes effect immediately. [L0971.026]
  • A new store starts with a default address made of random characters followed by.myshopify.com, which redirects to the domain you choose. [L0504.011]

Buying a domain at an external registrar

Cost and reasons to choose it

  • External registrars such as Namecheap and GoDaddy typically cost about $6 to $10 per year, which is considerably cheaper than buying through Shopify. [L0520.012]
  • External registrars are the better choice when budget matters, or when the domain may later be used on other platforms such as WooCommerce or Wix. The trade-off is that they do not include free email forwarding, so a separate paid email service is needed for a professional address. [L0520.013]
  • A domain held at an outside registrar is easier to move later and makes business email simpler to set up. [L0989.019] (Source E, 2025-26; this conflicts with the Shopify recommendation above. Both apply: Shopify for the simplest launch, an outside registrar for cheaper, portable and email-ready domains.)
  • Source A's 2023 lesson: domains can be purchased from any registrar and connected to a Shopify store. You do not have to buy them through Shopify. [L0934.095]
  • Buying a domain through GoDaddy and linking it to Shopify avoids TikTok warnings about links in the bio. A professional business email from the domain can be used for the store's social accounts. [L1381.058]

Procedure E (Source C, undated)

  1. Open the registrar's website.
  2. Search for the desired domain and confirm it is available.
  3. Compare the price and look for a discount code.
  4. Add the domain to the cart and complete the purchase. [L0520.015]

GoDaddy purchase (Source B, 2021-22, older)

  1. Search the domain on the domain search page with the correct local extension.
  2. If the result says available, click Add to Cart.
  3. Check out and pay by credit card. Checkout creates a GoDaddy login account for the buyer. [L0522.004, L0522.007]
  • Do not add any extra products or add-ons at GoDaddy checkout. Buy only the domain itself. [L0522.009]
  • GoDaddy's domain search lists.com.au results with a "Restrictions apply" note, which signals eligibility rules for the registrant. Check that the buyer qualifies for the local extension before adding it to the cart. [L0522.014]
  • GoDaddy may display prices in a currency that does not match the target market, such as euros for an Australian or US store. Confirm the currency and the final amount in the cart and at checkout before paying. [L0522.015]
  • The cheapest first-year prices at GoDaddy are often tied to a two-year registration. For example, about €0.99 for the first year applies only when registering for two years. Compare the renewal price for each period before choosing the term. [L0522.016]
  • GoDaddy's domain search may offer a broker service for a domain that is taken. Treat this as a last resort and move to a backup business name first, because restrictions apply. [L0522.013]
  • GoDaddy is a domain registrar used to search availability, register domains and manage the account. Other registrars can be used instead if an account already exists there. [L0522.012]

Namecheap purchase (Source E, 2025-26)

  1. Go to Namecheap, search for the domain, and add it to the cart.
  2. Check out with domain privacy kept on.
  3. Leave auto-renew off for now.
  4. Click Confirm Order.
  5. Create a username, enter your details and click Continue.
  6. Add a payment card and click Pay Now.
  7. Open the domain and click Manage. [L0989.021]

Connecting an external domain to Shopify (DNS, TXT and SSL)

Procedure F (Source C, undated)

  1. In the Shopify admin, click Settings in the bottom left corner.
  2. Open Domains.
  3. Click Connect existing domain instead of Buy new domain.
  4. Type the exact domain name you bought from the external provider.
  5. Click Next. If the domain was bought externally, Shopify shows the option to continue.
  6. Follow the DNS settings Shopify shows, and make the changes at your external registrar. Shopify's official guide describes the exact DNS settings to use.
  7. Expect a waiting period for the DNS changes and SSL before the domain works with the store. [L0520.016]
  • Use the connect existing domain option only for a domain already bought elsewhere, such as at Namecheap or GoDaddy. Shopify has documentation for the connection steps. [L0504.013]
  • A domain registered elsewhere can still be connected to Shopify, but it needs more setup than one bought in Shopify. [L0941.102]

Procedure G (Source E, 2025-26, Namecheap)

  1. In the Shopify admin, go to Settings > Domains.
  2. Click Connect Existing Domain and enter your domain.
  3. Shopify reports that the DNS records do not point to Shopify yet and shows the records to change.
  4. Log in to Namecheap and open the domain's Advanced DNS tab. [L0989.023]
Record Name / host Current value Change to

CNAME www parkingpage.namecheap.com shops.myshopify.com

[L0989.024]

  1. In Namecheap's Advanced DNS, update the existing A record from 162.255.119.46 to 23.227.38.65.
  2. If no A record is listed, use Add New Record and choose A Record with the value 23.227.38.65.
  3. Update the www CNAME record from parkingpage.namecheap.com to shops.myshopify.com.
  4. Click Save All Changes.
  5. Return to Shopify and click I updated it.
  6. Wait a while for DNS propagation and the TLS certificate to complete before assuming the connection failed. [L0989.025]
  • Right after DNS changes, Shopify shows DNS propagation at 0 and the TLS certificate as not provisioned. These statuses are normal during the waiting period, so do not change the records again straight away. [L0989.026]
  • After you connect a custom domain under Settings > Domains, the connected domain may show as redirecting to the store's default myshopify.com address until the DNS records are correct. Expect this status during setup, and do not treat it as a final result. [L0989.032]
  • Expecting the external domain to work immediately after connecting it is a mistake. DNS changes and SSL setup take time, so the store will not load on the new domain right away. [L0520.017]
  • Source A's 2024 lesson says a domain linked under Settings > Domains takes effect immediately. The DNS wait described above applies to external domains. Treat the wait as the rule when the domain was bought elsewhere. [L0971.026, L0520.016]

TXT records (domain verification)

  • For a domain bought through Shopify: go to Sales channels > Online store > Domains, click the main store domain and then Manage, then open DNS settings. Click Add custom record, choose TXT, keep the Name as @, paste the verification value into Text value and click Confirm. [L1025.015]
  • In Shopify's Domain settings > Add custom record, enter @ in the name or host field so the record applies to the whole domain. Paste the verification value from Search Console into the value field and confirm. [L0735.013]
  • For a domain bought from a registrar other than Shopify, add the TXT record in the DNS settings of the site where the domain is hosted. [L1025.016]
  • Always use the main domain, not the myshopify.com address, when editing DNS records. [L1025.016]

SSL

  • The SSL certificate on Shopify is free. Enable it through Shopify's documentation to show the HTTPS lock and secure connection badge to customers. (2022, older) [L0249.020]
  • An SSL certificate is included with Shopify, so buying one separately is unnecessary. [L0934.097, L0934.160] (2023)
  • Shopify's 2022 advice is to enable SSL through its documentation. The 2023 advice says it is included. Both describe the same certificate: it is free, and you do not buy it separately. [L0249.020, L0934.097]
  • A newly added custom domain can show the status Provisioning TLS while its SSL certificate is still being issued. Wait until this status clears before treating the domain as ready for visitors. [L1330.026]
  • A domain bought through Shopify does not wait for SSL. [L0520.003]

Primary domain, redirects and www

Setting the primary domain

  • After buying a domain, open Settings > Domains, find the new domain in the list and click Set as primary. From then on, anyone who types that domain lands on the storefront, the public side of the store. [L0504.018]
  • Connect the domain and set it as the primary domain before launch. [L1145.017]
  • The purchased domain must be set as the primary domain. Additional domains can be connected or transferred without creating a new Shopify account. [L1305.037]
  • Admin path for a primary change: Online Store > Domains > Change primary domain. Select the custom domain and click Save. Traffic from all domains then redirects to the primary domain. [L1292.018] (Source B, 2021-22, older)
  • Under Primary for Online Store you can switch the primary domain. [L1250.018] (Source D, 2026)
  • After a custom domain is bought, check the Domains list under Settings to confirm it is marked as the primary domain. The default.myshopify.com address should no longer be the one shown to customers. [L0521.019]
  • Leaving the default Shopify-assigned domain as your public address is a mistake. Use a professional domain that represents your brand and set it as the primary domain. [L0520.018]

Auto-assigned or manual primary (sources differ)

  • Shopify automatically assigns a newly bought domain as the primary domain. [L0079.009 step 10]
  • Once bought, the domain becomes the primary domain. [L0521.015]
  • Shopify's Settings say no additional configuration is required after purchase. [L0079.010]
  • Other sources say the purchased domain must be set as primary manually, from Settings > Domains. [L0504.018, L1305.037]
  • Check the Domains list after purchase, whichever way it was set. [L0521.019]

Redirects, www and non-www

  • If traffic from the other domains is not redirected to the primary domain, the page shows an "Enable redirection" link. After the primary domain is changed, the page shows "Traffic from all your domains redirects to this primary domain" with a Disable redirection option. [L1292.035]
  • When a custom domain is connected, Shopify also adds the www version as a third-party domain with its own SSL status. The www and non-www addresses redirect to each other, so visitors reach the store through either one once the primary domain is set. [L1292.036]
  • Use the non-www version of the custom domain as the primary domain, because www and non-www redirect to each other and the non-www form is simpler. [L1292.019] (2021-22, older)
  • Check that the www version is connected to the store and set to redirect to the root domain, so the store has one main address. [L1327.063] (Source D, 2026)
  • In Settings > Domains, the www variant of a custom domain can show "Redirects to the myshopify.com address" during setup. [L1330.027]
  • Two domains pointing to the same Shopify store show identical content, which splits brand authority. Choose one domain and build its authority. Redirects are possible but not recommended. [L1088.032] (2020, older)

The myshopify.com address

  • Every Shopify store keeps an automatically generated myshopify.com domain that cannot be deleted. It should remain as the backend, while the custom domain is the primary public domain. [L1292.017]
  • The admin default domain on Shopify cannot be removed, so the custom domain must be set as primary to avoid customers reaching the myshopify.com address. [L1292.026]
  • On the Domains page, the myshopify.com address is listed under Shopify-managed domains with the note "Never expires". It cannot be removed. [L1292.037]
  • The myshopify.com entry is labelled Primary for Online Store in the Domains list until a custom domain is set as primary. Use the myshopify.com entry for the Shopify URL field in the Shrine whitelist. [L1330.027]
  • A new Shopify account starts with a default domain made of random letters and numbers under.myshopify.com. Customers should not see this address, so replace it with a purchased custom domain as the primary domain. [L0521.014]
  • The default myshopify.com domain is only a technical placeholder. Choose any name for it, because the store is later connected to a purchased custom domain used for promotion. [L1313.004]
  • Using a store on a default myshopify.com address is a weak sign for a business and can appear with unprofessional branding. Use a custom domain name. [L0806.041] (2024)
  • A store on a myshopify.com URL looks unprofessional to customers and suppliers. Keep the myshopify.com URL only for demo stores. [L0852.082] (2020, older)
  • The myshopify.com subdomain is not visible to customers, so it does not matter much. The store name in the admin (name.myshopify.com) is not visible to customers either. [L0358.037, L0359.043]
  • Buying a domain in the admin and linking it hides the myshopify.com address from customers, with one click. [L0468.057] (2020, older)
  • Before a custom domain is connected, the store is reachable at a temporary address in the form store-name.myshopify.com, which is also the address shown in the admin header. The store is not visible to the public at that address while it is password-protected. [L1314.019] (2021-22, older)
  • A new Shopify store starts with a temporary myshopify.com address. The real domain is connected before the store goes live. The domain choice does not have to be made during signup, and there is no deadline for it. [L1114.026] (2023)
  • Register the business name and domain before creating the Shopify account, because the store is set up under that name. The domain is connected to the store later through the Add domain task on the dashboard. [L1314.017] (2021-22, older; this conflicts with the no-deadline point above. Both apply: the domain is not required at signup, but registering the name first is a sound order.)

Renewals and privacy

Auto-renew (sources differ)

  • To renew a Shopify domain, go to Settings > Domains in the Shopify admin. The domain is usually set to auto-renew, so check that setting before the renewal date. [L0962.032] (2021, older)
  • Turn on auto-renew every year. [L1327.044 step 5] (Source D, 2026)
  • At purchase, leave auto-renew off and keep domain privacy on. Once the business is working, go back and turn auto-renew on so the domain does not expire. [L0989.022, L0989.021 step 3] (Source E, 2025-26)
  • The two approaches differ at the start: the Source D and Source A sources keep auto-renew on from the start, while the Source E source turns it off at purchase and on later, once the business works. [L1327.044, L0962.032, L0989.022]

Registration term and renewal price (GoDaddy, 2021-22, older)

  • A domain can be registered for one or two years at checkout. It renews automatically each year (or each registration period) at the price originally paid. A one-year term saves money now, while a two-year term is equally valid. [L0522.010]
  • Introductory prices can be far below the renewal rate, so check the renewal price before buying. [L0522.008, L0522.016]

Privacy

  • Keep domain privacy on when registering the domain. [L0989.021 step 2, L0989.022]
  • GoDaddy states that domains include Basic Privacy Protection, which hides the owner's contact details in public registration records. Keep this default on when buying the domain for a business. [L0522.017]

Stability

  • Once a domain is bought and set up on the store, it should not be changed later. Treat the choice as long-term and do not rush it. [L0522.011]

Changing, transferring and moving domains

Whether to change at all

  • Changing a domain for SEO is usually unnecessary, since products, brands and SKU numbers are what rank, not the niche keyword in the domain. Keep an existing domain unless there is a strong reason to replace it. [L0941.034]
  • If the store is still new, changing its domain name is fine. Link the new domain to the existing Shopify account rather than building a new store. [L1088.031] (2020, older)
  • To move an existing store to a new domain: in the Shopify admin, remove the current domain and connect the new one. Products, categories and settings carry over, and no new Shopify account is needed. [L1002.019] (2019, older)

Safety rules for a change

  • Making changes to the primary domain, contact email and payment method in one session led Shopify to suspend the store, and support did not explain the reason at first. Make such changes one at a time and check the store after each one. [L1131.061]
  • Keep a saved copy of the working theme before editing, so the store can be restored quickly. [L1131.061]
  • Changing a store's domain name can break existing Google accounts (Workspace, Analytics, Merchant Center) that were set up on the old domain. If a domain change is needed, create new Google accounts for the new domain instead of reusing the old ones. [L0472.035] (2025)

Procedure H: change a store domain (Source A, undated)

  1. Buy the new domain and link it in the Shopify admin.
  2. Update your store emails to use the new domain.
  3. Update the domain in Google Merchant Center.
  4. Create a new product feed, since all URLs change.
  5. Relink the new URL to Google Analytics, Merchant Center, Facebook and Bing. [L0941.035]

Procedure I: rename a store domain without damaging ads (Source A, 2021, older)

  1. Turn off all Google Shopping ads.
  2. Delete the product feeds from Google Merchant Center, Microsoft Ads and Facebook shop feeds.
  3. Buy or link the new domain in Shopify so it loads the store.
  4. Resubmit the products from the new domain and expect a few days of downtime while they are approved again. [L0968.032]

Procedure J: switch to a new domain in the same Shopify store (Source C, undated)

  1. Buy a new domain in the same Shopify account.
  2. In the TikTok channel app in Shopify, disconnect and reconnect the channel, and select the same pixel so the historical data is kept.
  3. Set the new domain as primary in Shopify.
  4. Add the new URL to the new campaigns, because campaigns change every two to three days. [L1436.070]

Transfers

  • A domain bought through Shopify can be transferred to another registrar, and a domain bought elsewhere can be connected to Shopify. Every domain registrar allows transfers. [L1094.064] (2023)
  • Transferring a domain to Shopify is a separate procedure from building the store and needs to be handled as its own step. [L0851.005] (2025)
  • Shopify's Domains page offers Transfer domain alongside Connect existing domain and Buy new domain. Transfer domain moves a domain registered elsewhere into Shopify, while Buy new domain registers a new domain through Shopify. [L1292.034] (2021-22, older)
  • Shopify's Connect existing domain links a domain without moving its registration. Transfer domain moves the registration itself. [L1292.034, L0520.016]

Procedure K: move a domain bought through Shopify to a new store (Source A, 2025)

  1. Create the new Shopify account.
  2. Follow Shopify's knowledge base documentation to transfer the domain from the old account to the new one.
  3. Close the old account once the domain has moved.

Linking the domain through DNS settings is not enough; the domain itself must be transferred. [L0857.050]

Subdomains and multiple stores

  • Each store needs its own domain. A domain should not be bought for every niche before the first store proves itself. A new store should have its own URL rather than a subdomain of another store. [L0807.005] (2025)
  • Use a store on its own domain rather than a subdomain such as store.yourdomain.com. Separate memorable domains bring more returning customers and are better for a second store. A domain costs about $14 per year. [L0665.030] (2022, older)
  • A store can run on a subdomain, such as store.yourwebsite.com, linked from the header menu of the existing website. Alternatively, the existing website content can be moved to a new Shopify store, and the main URL can point to it. [L1068.044] (2019, older)
  • Shopify can host a blog on a subdomain: install the store on a subdomain such as store.yourdomain.com while the main domain keeps the blog. The store then has full Shopify functionality and the rest of the site remains unchanged. [L0357.052] (2019, older)
  • Build a separate.com store for each other market you sell into. [L0967.101] (2020, older)
  • Each extra domain costs about $14 per year, and each one has its own fee. [L1283.092] (2023)

Reusing a store for a new niche or domain

Reuse decisions

  • If the domain and company name are generic, reuse the existing store for a new niche by changing the tagline and branding in the backend. Starting from scratch is not necessary. [L0469.038] (2021, older)
  • An existing Shopify store can be reused for a new niche by editing the store and optionally buying a new domain (about $12 a year) and linking it. [L0834.042] (2021, older)
  • To add a new domain to an existing Shopify store, link it in the Shopify admin panel. The 2021 source says this creates a new store on the same account while the old one continues to exist. [L0469.039] (2021, older; later sources describe the same store with a new domain, see the next point)
  • Linking a new domain to the existing Shopify account, rather than building a new store, is the approach in later sources. [L1088.031] (2020, older), [L1305.037] (undated)
  • A new domain can be bought in the same Shopify account, then set as the primary domain. The downside of keeping one store is that other products cannot be tested in that same store without mixing their results. [L1382.060]
  • If one product is clearly working, keep it in the same store: buy a new domain, make it the primary domain, and keep the apps. The downside is that other products cannot be tested in the same store. [L1436.071]

Moving a winning product to its own brand (Source C, undated)

  1. Buy a new domain that fits the winning product. A reported cost is about $10 per year.
  2. Create a new logo for the product brand.
  3. Create a new Facebook page for the product's ads.
  4. Point the new domain to the Shopify store's base URL.
  5. Switch the store and the ads to the new logo, domain and Facebook page. [L1428.025]

Store name checks when reusing

  • The store name in the Shopify admin is not visible to customers, and the URL customers see can be changed as often as needed by changing the domain connected to the store. [L0359.043]
  • If the.com is already taken for a reused store, the domain may change while the store stays the same. Keep the domain and store consistent. [L0964.089]
  • Before buying a domain for a new niche, check whether the existing domain and company name are generic enough to reuse. [L0469.038]

Further details

  • Adding a new domain to Shopify is simple: buy the domain through the Shopify backend, then select which domain is primary for your store from the settings. [L0249.028]

  • Do not use.myshopify.com extensions for your store URL. Always use a custom domain name (e.g., chandelier.com, not yourstore.myshopify.com) for professional appearance. [L0253.034]

  • Shopify domain path: in the Shopify admin, open Settings (bottom left), then Domains, where the Buy new domain option is shown. The default Shopify-assigned domain shown on a new account is not a professional domain. [L0520.006]

  • Domain names can be bought from any registrar and linked to Shopify. Avoid expensive parked resale domains, and choose an open domain available for about $14 to $15 per year. [L0934.161]

  • Domain names can be bought directly through Shopify and are linked to the store, so the domain does not have to be bought from a separate registrar. [L1304.003]

  • Under Settings > Domains the Shopify domain and any purchased custom domain are listed. Both are visible there once the domain has been bought. [L1306.022]
