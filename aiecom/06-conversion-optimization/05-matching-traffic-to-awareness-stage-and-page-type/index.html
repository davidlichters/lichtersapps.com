6.5 Matching traffic to awareness stage and page type

Awareness stages and the customer journey

The five-stage model and the pyramid

  • Older (2021-22): Customers move through five awareness stages before buying: Unaware, Problem Aware, Solution Aware, Product Aware and Ninja Aware (also called Most Aware). Each stage is a mental step from first being in the market to deciding on a specific product. [L0904.001]
  • Older (2021-22): A four-stage version of the same model runs problem aware, solution aware, product aware and most aware, in that order. A product page converts visitors who arrive at an earlier stage only if it walks them through all the missing stages, because a price or buy button means little to someone who has not yet accepted the problem or the solution. [L0473.032]
  • Older (2021-22): The whole potential market fits into the awareness pyramid. The Unaware group is the largest segment at the base, and the segments get smaller toward the top, where Ninja Aware buyers are the fewest. [L0904.002]
  • Older (2021-22): The customer awareness pyramid is also called the customer journey, and marketers use the two terms interchangeably even though they are not exactly the same. The sales funnel describes the same stages turned upside down, with top of funnel at the pyramid's base and bottom of funnel at its tip. [L0481.019]
  • Older (2021-22): The awareness pyramid runs from completely unaware prospects at the bottom, through problem aware and product aware, to ninja aware buyers at the top, who are ready to purchase now. Traffic and landing pages should be matched to the stage each prospect is in. [L1392.015]

What each stage looks like

  • Unaware: Older (2021-22): Unaware people match the profile of a future buyer but do not yet know they have a problem or desire that the product solves. Reaching them requires messaging that first makes them see the problem. [L0904.003]
  • Problem Aware: Older (2021-22): At Problem Aware, people recognize a pain and start looking for a solution. Pain is caused by problems, and people either run away from pain or pursue pleasure, so the pain is the entry point for messaging. [L0904.004]
  • Solution Aware: Older (2021-22): At Solution Aware, people know a broad type of solution to their problem and need to learn which option fits them, possibly among several solution types. This is the largest group in the market at any time, but most of them are not ready to buy because they still need education to feel safe purchasing. [L0904.005]
  • Product Aware: Older (2021-22): At Product Aware, people know several specific products that solve their problem, including your product and competitor products, but have not yet chosen one. They are still comparing products before making a final buying decision. [L0904.006]
  • Ninja Aware: Older (2021-22): At Ninja Aware, people have learned their options, the desire for a solution has been built, and they know the exact product they need. They are ready to buy at this stage. [L0904.007]

Why the stage decides the destination

  • Older (2021-22): Roughly 10 percent of the total market is ready to buy at any given time, which is only the Ninja Aware segment. The earlier awareness stages hold much larger shares of the market. [L0904.008]
  • Older (2021-22): Product pages that only target Ninja Aware visitors reach a small pool of people. Visitors at earlier stages land on those pages, find nothing that speaks to their situation, and leave without buying. [L0904.009]
  • Older (2021-22): Product Aware and Ninja Aware visitors already know the solution, so for them the decision mostly comes down to price, the best offer and the best deal. Simple copy with a clear deal and a bit of urgency works only for this group. [L0904.010]
  • Older (2021-22): Serving people with good copy and the right product information at each awareness stage increases the likelihood of purchase. This raises the store's conversion rate. [L0904.019]
  • Older (2021-22): Buyers choose the type of solution before they compare specific products. For example, a beginner who needs a surfboard first decides between renting and buying, then compares products. Product pages and copy should answer the solution-level question before the product-level comparison. [L0904.021]
  • Older (2021-22): The landing page a visitor reaches must match the awareness stage that visitor is in at that moment. Sending problem aware or completely unaware traffic to a hard-sell product page ignores the buyer's need for education and usually yields few sales. [L0481.009]
  • Older (2021-22): Under the 40-40-20 principle, the first 40 percent is targeting, so the audience you target determines which awareness stage must be served on the landing page. [L0481.010]
  • Older (2021-22): Modern version of the rule: the first 40% depends on reaching the right audience in the right place, at the right time, with the right destination. The destination must match where the visitor sits in the customer journey, so cold social media traffic should not land on a high-ticket product page with a direct buy request. [L0408.005]
  • Older (2021-22): Sending cold, top-of-funnel social media visitors who are unaware of the solution straight to a product page selling a 3000 USD product is expected to yield an extremely low conversion rate. The traffic may match the market, but the destination does not match the buyer's awareness stage, so the visitor needs to be moved toward readiness first. [L0408.006]
  • Older (2021-22): A product page converts cold traffic only if it educates visitors from problem awareness through solution awareness and product awareness to readiness to buy. When visitors who are still problem-aware land on a product page without that education, the mismatch is usually a bigger cause of low conversion than the page design. [L0473.009]

Worked example: the awareness ladder for one product

  • Older (2021-22): Example for a home office worker with back pain from long sitting: the person is problem aware of the pain, then solution aware that a standing-capable desk could help, then product aware of the electric height-adjustable desk type. Ninja aware means they have chosen one exact model, and only then do they decide which retailer to buy it from. [L0481.018]

Matching the headline and page to the stage

  • Older (2021-22): Match the ad headline exactly to the primary promise on the destination landing page. If sending to a product page, use the primary promise from product copy; for blog or sales pages, match the page headline. Mismatches cause high bounce rates and wasted ad spend because users expect immediate visual confirmation of what they clicked. [L0241.010]
  • Message match between the ad and the landing page matters: if the ad shows one thing and the link goes to a clearly different product or topic, visitors leave immediately. [L0691.010]
  • Keep the product page visually close to the ad, so a GIF of the product in use in the ad is followed by the same GIF on the page. This one-to-one match reduces confusion for the visitor. [L1194.025]
  • Match your headline to the prospect's awareness of the product. A product-aware prospect should see the product named first, a desire-aware prospect should see the desire first, and a problem-aware prospect should see the problem stated specifically. Where a prospect stands on that scale decides how the copy begins. [L0523.056]
  • Relevance example: a golf-targeted ad for sports leggings should lead to a page that talks about golf performance, such as how the leggings support stance and swing, instead of generic sportswear benefits. A visitor who sees their own activity named on the page feels understood and is more likely to buy. Build this kind of audience-specific variant after the first landing page test shows a winner. [L0918.035]

Diagnosing a low-converting page

Older (2021-22): Steps to fix a product page with a low conversion rate:

  1. Identify the awareness stage of the traffic arriving on that page, which can be mixed; problem aware visitors can be recognized by search traffic about their problem.
  2. Check whether the page moves visitors from their current stage to the next one.
  3. If it does not, either segment marketing so problem aware visitors first reach a more suitable page, or add elements to the existing page that help visitors move through the stages.

[L0481.014]

Sequencing problem-aware and unaware visitors

Older (2021-22): For a problem aware or completely unaware audience, the sequence is:

  1. Send the visitor to a content page first, which explains the problem and why it exists and shows how to solve it and what solutions exist.
  2. Move them through email, retargeting or another page that introduces your products as solutions.
  3. Finally lead them to the product page.

[L0481.013]

  • Older (2021-22): Problem aware visitors typically arrive from Google searches about their problem and from social media posts that promote a product solving that problem. Because of this, traffic from these sources should usually land on an educational content page, not a product page. [L0481.017]

Traffic temperature and bridges

Hot, warm and cold traffic

  • Hot traffic consists of people who already know you, such as email subscribers, podcast listeners and blog readers. Speak to them personally and share stories, opinions and details of your life, because they already trust you. Such personal messages can generate large revenue from hot traffic. [L0523.053]
  • Warm traffic reaches you through someone else's relationship, such as affiliates and joint venture partners. A personal endorsement from a trusted source, such as a lift letter, raises response greatly. In classic direct mail, list owners and sellers often split profits 50/50. [L0523.054]
  • Cold traffic consists of people who do not know you, such as Facebook or pay-per-click visitors. These prospects need the longest bridge, and most sellers fail here because they speak in insider language. Plan a separate path for cold traffic instead of sending it straight to the offer. [L0523.055]
  • Bridge length scales with coldness. Hot traffic needs only a quick link or email, warm traffic needs an endorsement from a trusted person, and cold traffic needs the longest bridge that educates the visitor before the offer. Choose the bridge from the traffic temperature. [L0523.057]
  • Not all clicks are equal, because the context a visitor came from determines how they respond to your page. A visitor who has just read a glowing recommendation converts far better than one who has just read a hostile warning about you. Control the frame your traffic arrives through wherever possible. [L0523.050]
  • Visitors from cold traffic often need several steps before they buy, so a path through trust-building landing pages can come before the purchase page. Plan the journey so each page answers one question the visitor has. [L0524.076]
  • Traffic from different sources arrives at different awareness levels, so one landing page often cannot serve all of it. Plan up to three landing pages, one each for hot, warm and cold traffic, and send each traffic source to the page that matches its temperature. The lesson treats this setup as worth the extra effort. [L0523.135]

Pre-frames

  • A pre-frame is the mindset you establish before a prospect moves on to the following stage. Every stage of a funnel serves as the pre-frame for the next one, which is why each should be tuned for both selling and credibility. Visitors who arrive with a strong frame convert at a much higher rate than those who arrive with a weak one. [L0523.048]
  • The introduction a speaker receives strongly affects how much is sold at the back of the room. A bland introduction lowers energy and sales, while a strong pre-sell lifts them. Take control of the introduction with a short, prepared intro video that features endorsements and success stories, played just before going on stage. [L0523.052]
  • Sell testimonials as pre-frames. Pay customers or students to write blog posts about their results, or ask them to publish video reviews that include links and keywords, then drive traffic to those pages. Visitors arriving through someone else's endorsement convert much better. [L0523.059]

Cold bridge pattern

Pattern: cold bridge page that translates a visitor's problem into the vocabulary of your offer. Start with a broad question about something the visitor wants, then explain the jargon in plain words, and finally show how the offer solves the problem with the new term.

Why it works: visitors only convert when they understand the terms the offer uses, so the bridge closes that knowledge gap.

  • Example 1: "Want a free website that earns money?" followed by "To earn from it, people must visit, and visitors are called traffic. Here is how to bring them in."
  • Example 2: "If you wake up with stinging feet at night, the usual cause is nerve damage called neuropathy. Here is what to do about it."

[L0523.058]

Traffic sources and their fit with awareness stages

Traffic foundation

  • A single traffic source is not enough for a reliable e-commerce traffic base. A business should combine several sources to reach prospects across the whole awareness pyramid. [L1392.002]
  • Older (2021-22): A full e-commerce traffic foundation rests on five components: paid search ads billed per click, unpaid search engine visibility, retargeting campaigns, building an email subscriber list together with email marketing, and marketing through social media platforms. [L1392.003]
  • Older (2021-22): Traffic should reach prospects at every stage of the customer awareness pyramid, not only at the most aware stage. Each stage needs a page and message that matches where the prospect currently stands. [L1392.004]
  • Older (2021-22): Targeting only the most aware buyers is a mistake. That group is small, the most competitive, and often hard to convert, and if you are absent when they reach that stage, a competitor can take the sale. [L1392.005]
  • Older (2021-22): The goal is omnipresence: being available to prospects at any point in the awareness pyramid so the market's attention stays on your brand throughout their decision. [L1392.006]
  • Older (2021-22): The earlier a prospect is reached and given useful help, the longer they have to experience the business. That early exposure makes the brand more likely to be remembered when the prospect reaches the most aware stage. [L1392.011]
  • Older (2021-22): The industry average is about seven touchpoints, meaning interactions with a business, before a purchase in e-commerce. This is only an average, and the real number varies a lot between businesses, with some needing more and some fewer. [L1392.012]
  • Older (2021-22): Prospects who have already interacted with a brand several times are more likely to buy when they reach the most aware stage, because trust has been built. A brand that appears only at the last moment must rely on having a clearly better offer than the alternatives the prospect has already seen. [L1392.013]
  • Older (2021-22): Each traffic source must be matched with how the website engages visitors from that source. The content and landing experience should fit the awareness stage the traffic comes from. [L1392.014]
  • Older (2021-22): Some businesses can take a prospect from first visit to purchase in a single session, but many e-commerce businesses cannot. Plan for multiple visits, because the commonly cited industry average is about seven touchpoints before a purchase. [L1392.017]

Platform-specific matching

  • Google Shopping and paid search text ads point to product-level listings. Search text ads convert best when the landing page matches the search term closely, usually a single product page for a specific term or a narrow collection page for a broader one. A collection with hundreds of products makes the offer hard to find and lowers the chance of a sale. [L0880.006]
  • Broad or long-tail keyword traffic often converts poorly at first, so the visitor must be guided through several steps of value before buying. Expect a lower immediate conversion rate after a traffic boost ends. [L0949.059]
  • Smart links let traffic from specific ads or influencers land on a customized landing page built for that source. Matching the page to the visitor's entry point is meant to improve conversion from the first moment on the site. [L0593.004]
  • For influencer campaigns, keep the landing page simple. Use pictures or GIFs of the influencer using the product, add customer reviews, and offer a coupon code that the influencer shares with their followers. [L0789.029]
  • A website with only product pages engages only shoppers who are already ready to buy. Reaching people earlier in the customer journey, across the customer awareness pyramid, requires content. [L0500.010]

Display and retargeting audiences

  • Use website visitor audiences because they show prior intent; cold traffic on Display is nearly impossible to make profitable, so only run Display and Discovery to people who have already visited your store. [L0210.028]
  • Retargeting is where the most-aware visitor is served the offer directly; see the ad-type table above. [L1019.021]

Page types: product page, landing page, homepage and collections

Definitions and when each fits

  • A product page is a short page with a specific product picture, description, price, and add-to-cart button. A landing page has many sections such as header, banner, bonuses, icons, features, and reviews. A hybrid combines both and reduces a step before checkout, but it is not always better. [L0387.015]
  • Products that need explanation, such as health products (for example for back pain), often convert better with an advertorial landing page that explains how the product works before the product page, even though this adds a step. Impulse-buy products that sell on looks should have the shortest possible path to purchase. [L0387.016]
  • The product page must do the selling through its product description, product images, and product copy. The site's call to action in e-commerce is primarily to buy the product. The offer is delivered and presented on the website, not in the ad. [L1507.003]
  • A product page should receive visitors who are already solution aware or product aware, not problem aware or unaware ones. Visitors who do not yet know their solution should first be sent to content that educates them, which then links to the product page. [L0396.027]
  • Traffic must match the page it lands on: a product page is only right for visitors close to buying, so top-of-funnel search terms should go to content or collection pages instead. High-ticket buyers rarely make impulse purchases and need a longer decision process, so expect a longer gap between click and sale for generic traffic and plan for retargeting. [L1456.002]
  • A website converts only if the offer and page match what the buyer needs at that point; a product page should not be expected to educate buyers about the whole market. Where a generic search term has no good fit, the fix is often content that compares options and then links to the product page. [L1456.005]
  • For direct response dropshipping the main focus is the product or sales page at the purchase stage, because it has to ask for the sale quickly. Funnels and retention pages come later as the business grows. [L0919.003]
  • Lead capture pages offer something of value, such as an ebook, white paper or webinar seat, in exchange for contact details. Long form sales pages suit higher-priced single products that need more explanation before purchase. [L0919.005]
  • Roughly 95% of the paid traffic that lands on a product landing page decides there whether to buy or leave, while only about 5-10% look at other products. The landing page is therefore the most important part of the funnel after the ad click. [L0916.002]

Homepage versus product page

  • Do not send paid traffic to the store homepage. Visitors cannot see the product right away or add it to the cart, which makes the buying path harder. Send them to a collection page such as a bestseller collection or directly to the product page. [L0562.027]
  • A product page can be a better landing page than the home page when most traffic goes to the product page. Test whether direct-to-product traffic converts better before building a separate landing page for each product. [L0918.018]

Digital products and collections

  • Send traffic from a single digital product to a product page with bullet points, a screenshot or mockup, testimonials and instructions for use on tablet or phone. Send traffic for preset-type products to a collection page. [L1431.062]
  • Collection pages can serve as landing pages for new ad angles, such as a feature that customers care about, without building a separate landing page. The same pages can also be used for email segmentation and Google Ads campaigns. [L1389.020]

Variants and separate landing pages

  • Individual listings for each variant work well when the variants look clearly different, for example vibrant colors that are easy to see. Send traffic to a collection page that shows these separate listings. [L1217.049]
  • A dedicated landing page works better when the product has emotional marketing angles, such as love or mother and father gifts, or targets a specific demographic. Use GIFs and images to add emotion to the copy. [L1217.050]
  • When product variants confuse shoppers or do not match what they expect, separate them into their own landing pages. This clarifies what is available and tends to raise conversion. [L1315.018]
  • Case: A store that lacked currency settings and had confusing variants (one product page with mixed sizes or colors) had a conversion rate of 0.4% to 0.5%. After creating separate landing pages per variant group and setting up Shopify Payments with local currency checkout, the conversion rate rose to about 1.2%. A separate variant landing page helps when customers ask for options that are not available. [L1315.017]

Learning which page sends the most traffic

  • Check each landing page's share of a brand's ads to see where its funnel really sends traffic. In the example, one product page received 125 ads (81.2%) and a second page received 8 ads (5.2%). [L0374.022]
  • The Top Landing Pages list shows the share of a brand's ads linking to each page. In a drop shipping store the top landing page received about 3 percent of the traffic, while in established branded stores one landing page can receive around 30 percent. [L0892.020]
  • A landing page that receives a large share of a brand's traffic is likely its best-performing page. Checking which page a competitor sends most traffic to is a way to find a proven lander. [L0892.021]

Pre-sell pages: advertorials, listicles, editorials and presells

Gaps in competitor funnels

  • If a competitor sends its paid traffic straight to product pages with no listicle, advertorial or pre-sell page, that gap is an opportunity. Build dedicated landing pages, advertorials or listicles that warm up cold visitors before they reach the product page. [L0374.021]
  • When a competitor sends paid traffic straight to its product page, the gap can be an advertorial that agitates the problem further before presenting the product. Such a page can be a way to beat a direct-to-product competitor. [L0772.025]

Advertorials and editorial funnels

  • An editorial funnel is a pre-sell page placed before the product page that mixes article-style content with advertising to turn cold visitors into warm prospects who are ready to buy. The reader gets useful information first and the product pitch comes last. [L0578.001]
  • An advertorial is an ad styled as an editorial article that does not ask for the sale right away. A pre-sale or advertorial page warms up cold traffic by educating about the problem and the product and building trust, then captures data such as an email so the audience can be retargeted. [L0919.004]
  • An editorial widens the reachable market because it answers objections and builds trust before any sales pitch, which converts people who would otherwise leave. It also reduces dependence on luck in ad targeting, since the page itself raises awareness of the problem and explains why the product is the best solution. [L0578.007]
  • Use the 70/30 rule: about 70% of the page provides information and story, and only the final 30% introduces the product and the buying option. The reader must read the whole problem and solution before the pitch, so they are warm when they click. [L0578.016]
  • Build the editorial on the AIDA sequence of attention, interest, desire and action, because each stage moves a cold reader one step closer to buying. Skipping a stage leaves the reader unconvinced at the point of the pitch. [L0578.017]
  • For the desire stage, handle objections by explaining why existing solutions on the market fail to work well and why the new approach is different. The product can be presented as a new method without naming the brand early on. [L0578.020]
  • Social proof in the desire stage should include a customer's photo and review, a results timeline (for example after one week and after two weeks), graphs or data, and a count of other customers who had good results. These elements make the claimed benefit believable. [L0578.022]
  • In the action stage, present the product in the final 30% of the page and end with a clear call to action that leads to the product page, such as "check current availability" or "choose your package". The earlier stages must already have built agreement that the problem is real. [L0578.023]
  • Putting the product image, price and buy button at the top of a page meant for cold traffic makes the visitor feel pushed, and the page does not build the trust needed to convert them. Lead with the problem and story instead. [L0578.024]
  • Pitching the product before the reader agrees on the problem wastes the editorial, because the reader has no reason yet to care about the solution. Always begin with the shared problem. [L0578.025]
  • A salesy tone in the first part of the editorial makes the reader leave, because the page stops feeling like useful content. Keep the sales language for the final part. [L0578.026]
  • Editorial funnels are an advanced step, so add one only after the current funnel already converts well at a good level. The editorial is then used to win the prospect group that product pages miss, not to fix a weak product page. [L0578.034]

Case: A sleep-pain product store sent paid Facebook traffic to a story-style page instead of its product page. The headline promised a surprising solution to pain-filled sleep. The story then introduced a character who fits the ideal customer: someone who had a car accident years earlier and now has poor sleep and constant pain. The problem was agitated with photos, and the character then visited a doctor who recommended a foam pillow. The character tried it and her sleep improved over time, and the page showed other customers with similar results. The product was never pitched as the brand's own; the final button said "check current availability" and led to the product page. [L0578.035]

Presell pages and third-party pre-frames

  • Presell pages educate prospects before the sales page. A presell page is a longer article that provides background or explains a concept, such as what email marketing is and why it grows businesses. Affiliates use presell pages to warm up prospects before sending them to a partner's sales page. [L0523.076]
  • Articles placed on third-party sites serve as pre-frames for cold traffic from banner ads. Article types include a case study, a product explanation or a piece that frames the problem without mentioning you. Add the call to action as an inline link, a button at the bottom, a linked photo or a combination. [L0523.070]
  • News-style pre-frames borrow attention from current events. Linking your message to a timely story, such as an election or disaster, lifts attention while the story is hot, though the effect is short-lived. Designing a page to resemble a news page with a headline bar or sidebar can also boost credibility, but it must remain truthful. [L0523.072]
  • Blog posts are a pre-frame tool for almost any topic. A guest post on a high-traffic blog in your target market works better than your own blog, and the post is best published under the blog author's name. Pair it with a link to your offer and drive targeted traffic to the post. [L0523.073]
  • Pre-frame videos work well for testimonials and for educating viewers. Publish them on someone else's channel and place calls to action in the description or comments, as pre-roll or post-roll footage, or as annotations. The video should agitate a problem or teach a process that creates desire for your solution. [L0523.074]
  • Email pre-frames use solo ads or joint venture partners to endorse you to their list. The partner's endorsement borrows their credibility, and the call to action is a link in the email. In direct mail the same role is played by a lift letter from the list owner. [L0523.075]

Educational content and the one-page versus many-pages choice

  • Educational content such as guides and how-to articles helps customers choose the right product, and a site with strong guidance content turns visitors into buyers. Study content-rich stores that guide customers and adapt their approach to your niche. [L0487.057]
  • Older (2021-22): Education-based marketing walks a prospect through the whole awareness pyramid, and this can be done on one long page, but it demands strong copywriting. Using several linked pages is usually easier, because each page needs less text. [L0481.012]
  • A short quiz on the home page can ask a few questions about the visitor's situation and then recommend the right product or stage of the buying funnel. This helps undecided visitors who do not yet know which option fits them. [L1016.071]
  • A Learn section with short decision guides, covering how to choose, how to understand the product and how to avoid costly mistakes, answers pre-purchase questions before the buyer leaves. Each guide should link to the matching product or kit, and the section should be finished before ads point to the site. [L1016.072]

Quizzes and free entry points

  • Quizzes that give personalized results can serve as an entry point for a high-ticket or education-heavy product. In the example, the quiz ran on its own separate domain (neurotest.me), took about 20 to 25 minutes and ended with custom results and product recommendations. [L0334.014]
  • Step: Free digital course as an entry point: build the funnel in ClickFunnels, with a short course that promises one clear result (in the example, eliminating procrastination in three days), three modules and one bonus module from a partner. End the course with product offers, and link the offer to a Shopify checkout by using direct add-to-cart, so fulfillment stays on one system; for high-ticket items, enter the fulfillment details manually instead. [L0334.015]
  • Free trial entry points can be scaled only with your own fulfillment. Third-party logistics providers charge for each small task, which makes sample or trial packets expensive; a co-packer can assemble trial packets from bulk product, and the trial sign-up asks only for an email address. Test free-plus-shipping and free-only versions to see which converts better. [L0334.016]

Blog posts and content CTAs

  • Blog posts that educate and then end without a next step waste the traffic they already earn. Add an on-topic call-to-action block or email capture so existing readers can continue into the funnel. [L1269.029]

  • Choose a call-to-action based on the funnel stage of the article's reader, not because a product CTA seems expected. Readers of top-of-funnel articles have near-zero buying intent, so a high-priced product embed in an informational article will rarely convert. [L1269.030]

  • Procedure: Map existing article content to funnel stages and CTAs:

    1. Ask the AI to classify each article by awareness stage, for example problem aware, solution aware or product aware, or by top, middle and bottom of funnel.
    2. Create a CTA for each stage, matching the reader's intent at that point.
    3. Map each stage to an email segment, so readers receive messages suited to where they are.
    4. Map each stage to product embeds or offers, placing products only where the reader is ready to consider them.

    [L1269.031]

  • Example: A top-of-funnel article about a niche animal topic can carry an embedded signup for a topic newsletter, with the promise of regular practical tips on the subject. This captures interested readers instead of asking for a sale they are not ready to make. [L1269.033]

  • On top blog posts, pairing an on-topic product block with an email capture works better than a generic store banner. The product should match the subject of the article, for example apparel for an ocean-themed article. [L1269.034]

Funnel design and pre-frame structure

Funnel principles

  • Revenue levels from six to eight figures depend mostly on whether you understand each phase of your funnel and can monetize each point along it. Products, traffic sources, copy, website design and launch methods matter, but they are not what separates the levels. Focus on engineering the path each customer takes. [L0523.046]

  • Problem-based marketing beats product-based marketing. Don't sell product features ("take this pill for focus"). Instead: (1) Top of funnel: make audience conscious a problem exists and share content about it, (2) Middle of funnel: show how life looks without the problem and how to overcome it, (3) Bottom of funnel: present your offer as the solution. Find problems via Google Trends, not products. [L0307.011]

  • Each stage of a funnel serves as the pre-frame for the next one. Use the seven-phase procedure below to structure the path:

    1. Determine traffic temperature (hot, warm or cold) and the awareness level of the prospect.
    2. Set up a pre-frame bridge matched to that temperature.
    3. Qualify subscribers with an opt-in offer that delivers something of value.
    4. Qualify buyers immediately after opt-in with a low-cost or free-plus-shipping offer.
    5. Identify hyperactive buyers through upsells and downsells on the following pages.
    6. Age and ascend the relationship over time, moving customers up the value ladder.
    7. Change the selling environment for high-ticket offers by moving to phone, direct mail or live events.

    [L0523.047]

  • There are 23 interchangeable funnel components, sorted into four stages: the pre-frame bridge, screening subscribers, screening buyers, and spotting hyperactive buyers. You can combine them in any arrangement to fit a given offer. Assemble the setup, run a test, change a single element, then test again. [L0523.067]

  • Send all paid traffic to a squeeze page with one goal: the visitor either gives an email address or leaves. Remove menus, ads and any other links so nothing distracts from the opt-in. Every paid click should become owned traffic. [L0523.024]

  • Track traffic and offers on the basis of the stage they are at: where a squeeze page captures an email, a pre-sell or advertorial page warms cold traffic toward a sale, and a product page closes a visitor who is already ready. [L0523.024, L0919.004, L0919.003]

  • Ad-level funnels should match the path: a problem ad leads through educational content, a solution ad leads to a landing page, and retargeting leads straight to the offer. [L1019.019, L1019.020, L1019.021]

Pre-frames within the funnel

  • The seven-phase procedure above treats each prospect's temperature as the first decision. [L0523.047]
  • Stage-matched copy and page choices reinforce the pre-frame: a visitor who arrives with a strong frame converts at a much higher rate. [L0523.048]

Ad creative as a funnel stage

  • If a Kalodata rip campaign is saturated or not working, test bottom-of-funnel image ads instead of pausing entirely. You may capture sales from an audience that is already solution-aware but fatigued from watching videos. Bottom-of-funnel is cheaper to test (quick image creation) than new video production. [L0316.032]
  • Some stores build a separate product page per persona or angle, keeping the same layout and changing only the copy. Track each page separately to see which angle converts. [L1164.046]
  • When one product is sold to several audiences, keep the landing page structure identical and change only the copy and headline for each angle. Then split-test the variants to see which angle converts best. [L0387.035]
  • The landing page must match the marketing angle of the ad. With Page Fly, create one page theme per angle; with other themes, create separate product pages and see which converts best. [L1219.037]

Testing and scaling landing pages

Testing order

  • Landing pages should be tested against each other after a first test, with more relevant variants built for specific audiences. Relevance between ad, audience and page is a key driver of performance. [L0918.023]
  • Start with a standard original landing page. Build listicles, advertorials and whitelist pages only once daily revenue reaches roughly $500 to $1,000. [L1454.010]
  • Below roughly $200 in daily revenue, do not spend time on listicles, advertorials or whitelist pages. Focus on getting first sales with the original landing page. [L1454.011]
  • Do not build a pre-lander or pre-sales page until a single day has produced at least $500 in revenue. Before that threshold, pre-landers add work without enough sales data to justify them. [L1454.012]

Where the thresholds apply

The revenue thresholds from different units apply at different stages and do not conflict:

Daily revenue What to build
Below roughly $200 Only the standard original landing page; no listicles, advertorials or whitelist pages
Roughly $500 to $1,000 Listicles, advertorials and whitelist pages may be built
At least $500 in one day A pre-lander or pre-sales page may be built

[L1454.010, L1454.011, L1454.012]

  • Plan up to three landing pages, one each for hot, warm and cold traffic, when several sources of different temperature are already running. [L0523.135]
  • An editorial funnel is an advanced step that comes after the current funnel converts well. [L0578.034]

Test steps for a page

  • Create at least two variants of a landing page and split-test them. Use the same page structure and change only the parts that differ between angles. [L0387.035]
  • Test whether direct-to-product traffic converts better before building a separate landing page for each product. [L0918.018]
  • For each ad set, record its awareness level and the page it lands on, then compare conversion by page. [L1019.022]

Further details

  • Send cold traffic to the single simplified kit page rather than to a page with many options, because one clear problem and one clear solution convert best for people who have never heard of the brand. [L1016.044]
