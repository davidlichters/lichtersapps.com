6.21 Platform-specific conversion tactics

Amazon conversion benchmarks

  • Conversion rate is a number to track weekly, not once. Even small changes to images, copy or product features can move it. Once conversion is optimized, stop changing things to avoid regression. [L0227.023]
  • The sources set the "exceptional" line differently. L0227.023 puts exceptional at 18% and above and says to stop further changes once a listing reaches it. L0227.024 uses a fuller scale: 5% is poor, 10-18% is good, 20% and above is excellent, 30% and above is exceptional. Use the L0227.024 scale for grading and the 18% point from L0227.023 as the freeze point. [L0227.023, L0227.024]
  • A supplement product averaged 38% conversion on Amazon, with some days above 50%. Other platforms typically reach 2-3%. [L0227.024]
  • Amazon shoppers are in a buying mindset. Baseline conversion is 15-20%, rising to 33% on some days and above 50% in supplement categories. Dropshipping stores typically convert around 2%. Amazon ads are therefore the best traffic source for FBA products, despite high cost-per-click. [L0228.025]
  • Amazon conversion is much higher than on a website, but the sources set different working bars:
    • L1153.032 treats 3% as good on a store, and on Amazon treats a rate under 15% as a bad sign. It cites an 8% rate as a poor week.
    • L0227.024 counts 10-18% as good on Amazon.
    • Use L0227.024 as the general benchmark and L1153.032 as the stricter alarm level. [L1153.032, L0227.024]
  • Amazon shoppers already want to buy, which is why conversion is high compared with traffic from Google, Bing or Facebook. Items under $50 need little thought from the buyer and support fast purchases. [L1153.033]

Amazon listing optimization

  • The product page elements that influence Amazon conversion are the title, images, bullet points and description. Optimize each one to match the problems and benefits buyers find in competitor reviews. [L1153.035]
  • Research competitor reviews in this order:
    1. Open the product pages of competitors and read the positive and negative reviews. [L1153.034]
    2. Note the recurring problems buyers mention. [L1153.034]
    3. Use these problems in your title, bullet points and description to show how your product solves them. [L1153.034]
    4. Use the same insights to make small product improvements instead of building a new product. [L1153.034]

Amazon advertising sequence

Run the sequence in order. Do not run automatic and manual campaigns at the same time.

  1. Set up an automatic Amazon ad campaign for one week. Amazon selects keywords and displays the product. [L0228.026]
  2. Review which keywords produced conversions and which wasted spend. [L0228.026]
  3. Build a manual campaign that targets only the converting keywords. This gives you control over cost-per-click and bid amounts. [L0228.026]
  4. Do not run automatic and manual campaigns simultaneously. You will outbid yourself and waste budget competing against your own ads. [L0228.026]
  • In the short term, losing money or breaking even on Amazon ads is acceptable while you accumulate sales and conversions to improve organic ranking. Long-term profitability needs a margin high enough that each sale can reinvest in ads and still be profitable. Higher price points make this strategy possible. [L0228.027]
  • If Amazon ads are already profitable (for example, spending $10 to make $20), increase the budget. Keep scaling until conversion rates plateau. More sales improve organic ranking, which generates additional unpaid traffic. [L0228.028]

Dynamic product ads

  • Running dynamic product ads and optimizing them for CTR is the target for a 26% lift in conversion rate. [L1031.032]

Service-business websites

  • Use shorter text on a service website. Visitors rarely read long blocks, so services must be communicated quickly and clearly. [L0440.025]
  • On mobile layouts, page builders such as Gem Pages or PageFly can create side-scrolling carousels and drop-down (accordion) menus. Use them to compress feature lists and benefit copy while keeping detail accessible. [L0918.013]

High-ticket and bulky items

  • Older (2021-22): For high-ticket products, a phone call call to action can convert better than a buy button. End the copy with an instruction to call today on a visible phone number and offer to discuss purchase options. [L0461.013]
  • Older (2018): White glove delivery, meaning professional delivery and setup, can increase conversion rate for large or bulky items. [L1109.044]
  • Older (2019): Mobile hesitance is common for high-ticket products. Expect many mobile visitors to convert later on desktop, and measure performance with that in mind. [L1275.070]
  • Older (2018): There is no fixed rule that conversion falls as price rises. Traffic quality and how targeted it is matter more than the price point. [L1109.045]
  • Conversion rates for high-priced products can be higher, lower or similar to standard products. Measure conversion for each high-priced product after launch rather than assuming a figure. [L0970.099]
  • The social pages support high-ticket orders, with buyers placing orders of roughly $500, $1,000, $2,000 or $5,000. Credibility matters more than viral reach for purchases at this level. [L0654.021]
  • Older (2021-22): A search bar in the header is important for high-ticket dropshipping stores, because the catalogue grows over time and visitors need a fast way to find the product they want. Present it as a conversion measure that helps more people find the right product. [L1371.008]

Products that require assembly

  • Older (2022): For products that require assembly, request photos from the supplier showing what the product looks like when delivered in boxes or pallets. Display these alongside assembly time and instruction videos on the product page, so customers understand the unboxing and assembly reality. [L0249.098]
  • Older (2022): Example. Outdoor swing sets for backyards are sold for $5,000 with photos of children playing and climbing. Delivery arrives as three pallets of 1,000 pounds of lumber, while customers expect an assembled product. The fix is to show assembly time in videos, include delivery photos, and list assembly requirements prominently. [L0249.100]

Dropshipping-specific rules

  • Do not copy a competitor's landing page, video or ad creative exactly. If the original advertiser is banned, your account and store can be banned too. Change images, text, layout and creatives. [L1213.073]
  • Older (2021-22): Hide stock levels on a high-ticket dropshipping store, because showing stock levels gives no advantage in that model. [L1371.019]
  • Where stock is genuinely low on a standard store, the low-stock message can help conversion. See the inventory settings below. [L0976.033]
  • Hide the expiring coupon block until the store receives real traffic, because demo products do not need urgency messages. Re-enable it at any time with the same settings. [L0976.021]
  • Enable the out-of-stock alert for items that are backordered for a long time, so you can build a waiting list instead of taking orders. [L0976.010]
  • Test landing page themes before anything else. The procedure is in the page builder section below. [L0774.010]

Theme-native conversion features

  • Theme-native features aim to raise conversion rate and average order value. For most of these needs, the theme covers the function without a paid third-party app. [L0973.002]
  • Exit pop-up: enable it in Theme settings, optionally turn on an image, and offer a discount code. It appears to visitors who are leaving the site, displays text only, and captures no email. [L0973.007]
  • Social proof pop-up: enable it in Theme settings to show recent store orders in the bottom corner of the website. The format is "[Customer name] recently purchased [product name]", and a frequency setting controls how often it appears. [L0973.011]
  • Social proof pop-ups are optional. The built-in version avoids a monthly fee for a separate app. [L0973.012]
  • Sticky content on desktop: turn it on in Theme settings and save. The image stays in place while the text column scrolls. This helps on monitors where the page does not fill the screen, and the option is recommended on. [L0976.004]
  • Price colors: under Theme settings you can change the color of the list-price savings text (red by default) and the color scheme of the sales badge. Both can be set to any colors. [L0976.012]
  • Sticky add to cart: the button stays visible at the bottom while the visitor scrolls. It is optional and can be tested. Icon, colors and font size are set under customize add to cart button. [L0976.027]
  • Share button: keep it on. Visitors can copy the product link or share it on social media or by email. The link updates to the product page URL automatically, and the button text can be changed. [L0976.030]
  • Image block: add it at any position on the product page, and add several if needed. It suits a promotion banner, such as a sale graphic showing a discount and the checkout code. Prepare the image in advance in a design tool such as Canva. [L0976.045]
  • Countdown timer: it needs a tag on the individual product in the Shopify admin, not in the theme settings. The tag begins with the word sale and carries the offer's end date, which must lie in the future, because the timer counts down to that date. [L0976.047]

Inventory display

  • The low inventory threshold defaults to 10 units. When stock falls below that number, a low stock message appears. It can be dragged to sit directly below the buy button. [L0976.032]
  • With show inventory count turned on, the page displays the exact number left next to the low stock message, for example "low stock with 8 left". This can help conversion when real stock is actually low. [L0976.033]

Trust badges

  • The trust badges block sits across the bottom of the page by default, unlike the side placement of the shipping text block. Its title can be changed and aligned center, left or right. Each badge can have an image, a link and optional text under the icon. [L0976.037]
  • The block allows a maximum of seven badges. Enable display trust badge text to add a caption under each icon. The caption text comes from the badge boxes. [L0976.038]
  • The trust badges block lets the store show reasons to buy below or beside the product. It is an advanced option and can be turned off for a basic demo store. [L1189.023]
  • Switch a feature such as trust badges on temporarily in the theme editor to see how it looks on the product page before deciding to keep it. If it is not needed yet, switch it off again instead of building it out at the demo stage. [L1189.029]

Estimated delivery block

  • The block shows text such as "estimated arrival between", followed by the earliest and latest delivery days, counted from the day the order is placed, plus the shipping country. That country should be the country you ship to. Its text and color can be changed. The block is recommended once the store goes live. [L0976.023]
  • In the theme used in L1192, the block sits in the Product information section of the theme editor. Click its eye icon to make it visible. It then appears under the discount code with the label "estimated arrival" and a date range. [L1192.021]
  • The date range is built from the earliest and longest delivery days entered in the estimated delivery settings. Example: 4 days as the earliest and 7 days as the longest, shown as two calendar dates. [L1192.022]
  • The dates update automatically every night at midnight based on the day count, so the range stays current without manual edits. [L1192.023]

Setup procedure for coupon and delivery on the product template

  1. Create the discount code in the Shopify Discounts section first. [L1192.030]
  2. Turn on and configure the expiring coupon on the product template, matching the code name exactly. [L1192.030]
  3. Turn on and configure the estimated delivery section and set the delivery day range. [L1192.030]
  4. Save the template and check the product page in preview. [L1192.030]

Review widget and hero slide

  • Older (2021-22): Hero slide text in the Shopify theme editor:
    1. Open the slideshow section and click the first slide. [L1310.019]
    2. Select the image, upload it, and check how it looks. [L1310.019]
    3. If the image is too dark, remove the overlay so it looks brighter. [L1310.019]
    4. Enter the Heading and Subheading, and set Text alignment to Left. [L1310.019]
    5. Remove the button by clearing its Label field if no button is needed. [L1310.019]
    6. Click Save. [L1310.019]
  • To place the review widget, go to Online store > Themes in Shopify, click Edit theme, and switch from the homepage to a product page template. The review widget appears in the apps section of the sidebar and can be dragged to a different position. By default it sits directly under the product description. Save with the theme save button. [L1245.005]

Visually A/B tests (Shopify)

Running and reviewing experiments

  • The Pages setting "All Pages" covers the homepage, product pages, collection pages and the rest of the store. To test only one template, choose a specific page type such as Product Pages or Cart page, so the change does not affect unrelated pages. [L0389.027]
  • A running experience appears in a live list showing its start date, last edit date, pages, audience, device and allocation (for example 50%). From there you can Edit, Pause or Preview the experience without creating a new one. [L0389.028]

No-code A/B test setup

  1. Hover over the element on the live page and click Edit Visual. Change the text, font, size, spacing or color, then click Save. [L0389.001]
  2. Enter an Experience Name. [L0389.001]
  3. Set the Audience (for example All Visitors), Pages (for example All Pages or Product Pages), Devices (for example Mobile) and Trigger (PAGE LOAD). [L0389.001]
  4. Click Next and set the experience as an A/B test with a 50-50 allocation. [L0389.001]
  5. Select the goals: Conversion Rate as the primary goal, and Per Session Value and Profit as secondary goals. [L0389.001]
  6. Tick the box that sends the events to Google Analytics. [L0389.001]
  7. Publish. [L0389.001]

Social proof widget below Add to Cart

  1. Add the Social Proof element through Content Blocks, placed directly under Add to Cart. [L0389.003]
  2. Choose Bought instead of Viewed. [L0389.003]
  3. Set the General Customization to BOUGHT IN THE PAST X TIME, with Timeframe WEEK and Rounding (50's) for rounded numbers. [L0389.003]
  4. Set the threshold to a minimum of 150 purchases. [L0389.003]
  5. Style the text block: font Commons Sans-serif, 16px, bold, black to match the brand, with reduced padding. [L0389.003]
  6. Save. Then set Audience All Visitors, Pages Product Pages, Device Mobile, and name the experience (for example "mobile BDP social proof"). [L0389.003]
  7. Set the A/B test to 50-50 with the primary goal, send the events to Google Analytics, and publish. [L0389.003]
  • Choose Bought over Viewed, because bought counts show stronger proof. Timeframe options are day, week, month and year. Rounding (50's) shows a rounded count instead of the exact number. The Set threshold field hides the widget until the purchase count exceeds the chosen number. [L0389.005]

Trust elements (USPs) in the cart

  1. Copy the USP block from the product page (shipping, returns policy, lifetime warranty, active promotion). [L0389.008]
  2. Paste it into the cart page directly below the upsells and above the shipping line. [L0389.008]
  3. Set Audience All Visitors, Pages Cart page only, Device Mobile, and name the test (for example "mobile cart USPs"). [L0389.008]
  4. Set the A/B test to 50-50. [L0389.008]
  5. Add returns rate and profit as goals, because a visible return policy can raise the return rate. [L0389.008]
  6. Tick the Google Analytics box and publish. [L0389.008]

Opening a feature dropdown on page load (Fake Click)

  1. Add a Fake Click element from the element list. [L0389.015]
  2. Hover over the dropdown (for example Features) and click it, so the interaction is recorded. [L0389.015]
  3. Preview the page to confirm the dropdown opens on load. [L0389.015]
  4. Set Audience All Visitors, Pages Product Pages, Device Mobile. [L0389.015]
  5. Name the test, set it as an A/B test, set the goals, send the events to Google Analytics and publish. [L0389.015]

Cart, announcement and progress elements (Shopify)

Announcement bar

  • Header > Add > Announcement Bar: use it to promote sitewide sales or discount codes inside the cart. With several messages, they slide across the top of the bar. A single message stays static, without the sliding animation. [L1230.005]
  • An announcement bar can hold several announcements that scroll as a marquee, with a default display timing of roughly five seconds. The character limit was not stated, so keep messages short and watch for unwanted line breaks. [L1231.023]

Tiered progress bar (free shipping goal)

  • Goal Box > Add > Tiered Progress Bar shows customers their progress toward offers such as free shipping, free products, an order-wide discount, or a decorative tier with no reward. Up to four tiers can be configured. [L1230.007]
  • Tiered Progress Bar > Configure Tiers > Free Shipping: enter the unlock price as a dollar amount (the lesson uses $50). The free shipping options are Free Shipping on All Products with no minimum, Free Shipping on Subscription Product, and Enable Presentment Currencies. [L1230.008]
  • Example: with a $50 unlock price, a cart holding $29 in products shows that the customer is $21 away from free shipping. The bar updates as items are added or removed. [L1230.009]

Cart footer components

  • Footer > Cart Subtotal controls how the subtotal text appears in the cart. [L1230.019]
  • Footer > Checkout Area > Express Checkout Buttons: Shop Pay, Apple Pay and Google Pay can be shown in the cart through Accelerated Checkout. Pre-checkout options (Terms and Conditions, Pre-Purchase Pop-Up), standard buttons (Checkout Button, View Cart Button, Continue Shopping Button) and Installments are configured in the same area. [L1230.020]
  • Footer > Add > Discount Code Input lets customers enter a discount code inside the cart, so the saving is visible before the checkout page. The field can be dragged to any position in the footer. [L1230.021]

Section settings (Shopify themes)

  • The Instagram stories section displays images and testimonials as a stories-style feed. Add user post blocks inside the section. The section fills with stories automatically once content is added. [L1330.018]
  • Instagram stories section settings in the theme editor:
    • Display section ID: the ID can merge two sections in the Sections group, and can be placed in any button link so the button scrolls to this section.
    • Display on: All devices.
    • Heading.
    • Heading words highlight 1: words in bold get the highlight style (for example with Text color #6D388B).
    • Heading words highlight 2: words in italic get the highlight style. Choose None to keep the default italic style.
    • Heading size, Margin and Remove section. [L1330.019]
  • Older (2021-22): Highlights banner settings: Section background color, Desktop section spacing (set to 60px in the tested build), Mobile section spacing, and Banner text alignment set to Center. Colors: Banner headings, Banner text, Icon, Dividers and Background. [L1310.023]
  • Product information section in the Shrine theme: Display section ID, Enable sticky content on desktop, Color scheme (Background 1), Media settings, Display first variant image as featured image, Disable prepend of the new active media, Hide other variants' media after selecting a variant, and Active variant images filtering. Use the sticky option to keep the purchase block visible while the visitor scrolls. [L1331.021]
  • Product template block order in the Shrine theme: Title, Rating stars, Price, Variant picker, Quantity selector, Buy buttons (with Add to cart), Shipping checkpoints, Sticky Add To Cart, Description, Reviews, then Image with text sections and an Icon Bar. Remove blocks you do not need so the page does not get busy. [L1331.022]

Demo-store configuration

  • Hide the expiring coupon block until the store has real traffic. [L0976.021]
  • Turn off the trust badges block for a basic demo store. [L1189.023]
  • Keep the demo store simple: test features temporarily and remove them if they are not needed yet. [L1189.029]

Landing page builders and theme testing

Theme split test

Split-test landing page themes before anything else:

  1. If you do not use PageFly yet, build the landing page with PageFly and test it. [L0774.010]
  2. If you already use PageFly, switch to the Source F theme for the test. [L0774.010]
  3. Run ads for two days on one theme, then two days on the other, and compare results. [L0774.010]
  • Some products convert better on PageFly and others on Source F, so do not commit to one design. Both PageFly and Source F are free to use. [L0774.010]
  • Older (2024 source, no date mark needed): A store with a low conversion rate (for example 0.03%) on an older theme should be rebuilt on the current theme (Manhattan). A fresh build on a newer theme should show a much higher conversion rate. [L0856.038]

Page builders

  • Gem Pages is the page builder named in the lesson, and it has carousel and drop-down features. PageFly is a similar builder with duplicate-and-edit functionality for creating variant landing pages per audience. [L0918.021]
  • Instant is a page builder used for pre-landers, listicles and advertorials. GemPages has better templates, but Instant is easier to work with. PageDeck is another builder option. The choice of builder matters less than the flow: clean copy, then pre-lander, then funnel. [L1299.008]

Button styling in PageFly

  • To build a button, add a button element, enter the button text, set the background color and text color (for example white text on a dark background), and set the text to bold. Add inside padding so the button has space around its label. [L1462.029]
  • Set a button's border radius to 50 px to round its corners into a pill shape. Increase the value until the corners look fully round for the size of the button. [L1462.030]
  • A fixed-position button with bottom 30 px and left 30 px floats over the page while the visitor scrolls. This works as a sticky add-to-cart button on mobile product pages. [L1462.032]

Pinterest conversion setup

  • Pinterest conversion campaigns need a checkout event fired on a purchase-style trigger. Use this snippet, which has a sample value of 80 and a placeholder product. Replace the product name, ID, price and value with the real trigger product's details before use. [L1133.006]
pintrk('track', 'checkout', {
  value: 80,
  order_quantity: 1,
  currency: 'USD',
  line_items: [
    {
      product_name: 'Trigger Product',
      product_id: '6789',
      product_price: 80,
      product_quantity: 1
    }
  ]
});

[L1133.006]

Activating conversion events

  1. Open a product page on your store. [L1135.027]
  2. Open the browser developer tools: right-click the page and choose Inspect, or go to More tools > Developer tools if inspect is blocked. [L1135.027]
  3. Open the Console tab. [L1135.027]
  4. Paste the Pinterest conversion event code into the console and run it 10 to 20 times. [L1135.027]
  5. Wait five to 10 minutes. [L1135.027]
  6. Return to Pinterest. The conversion campaign option should now be active. [L1135.027]

Pinterest audience growth

  • Add a pop-up on the store that invites visitors to follow the store on Pinterest or check out new boards. This increases the number of people who follow the store and share its content automatically. [L1136.033]
  • Place a Pinterest follow button in the website header. Add links to boards and the profile in the sidebar or footer, so visitors who want to interact on Pinterest can find the profile easily. [L1136.034]

TikTok conversion setup

  • Set the TikTok pixel's Data Sharing to Maximum during the Shopify connection, so the pixel receives the most event data for optimization. [L1373.003]
  • Do not scale a TikTok campaign until the store conversion rate is above 1.5% to 2%. [L1375.005]
  • Set the call to action to standard Shop now, or to Dynamic to let TikTok choose the most suitable button. Both are valid. Test which converts better. [L1383.053]
  • If a video goes viral but produces no sales, the audience is probably wrong, even if the product is good. Switch the coins or Spark Ads objective to conversions to reach buyers. [L1429.012]
  • Views versus conversions in one example: $10 with a conversion objective produced 1 to 2 conversions and about 500 views. The same $10 on a views objective could double the views but produce no conversions. [L1429.015]
  • Conversion objectives cost more per result and give fewer total views, so choose between sales and reach based on your goal. If conversion targeting produces no sales, go back to views, because TikTok may still find the audience either way. [L1429.016]

Mixed coins strategy by stage

  1. Spend about $10 on coins for views in the first stage. [L1429.049]
  2. Once the account reaches about 1,000 followers, switch the remaining about $20 to a conversion objective. [L1429.049]
  3. If growth slows, use views and conversions again to re-balance. [L1429.049]

Common mistakes

  • Do not copy a competitor's landing page, video or ad creative exactly. Change images, text, layout and creatives to protect the account and store. [L1213.073]
