6.15 Email and phone capture: pop-ups, opt-ins and lead magnets

Why capture contact details first

  • A valuable free resource that matches what the visitor really needs turns cold and warm audiences into prospects by capturing name and email. [L0240.002]
  • Research what the target audience genuinely wants, then build a free resource (eBook or report) that addresses those needs. Include company information and relevant offers alongside the value-driven content to introduce the business. [L0240.003]
  • Older benchmark (2020): a new store with 100-150 qualified visitors per day performs acceptably. Five email subscribers from that traffic is below target and shows that email capture needs optimization. [L0245.041]
  • Collect customer emails for follow-up, including abandoned cart recovery and bounce-back campaigns. Email follow-up significantly affects conversions. [L0249.032] (older, 2022)
  • A sign-up pop-up that offers a small discount for an email address can raise conversion and grow the list at the same time. Run it from the start of the store. [L0476.003]
  • Ask every visitor for an email address in exchange for something of value, such as a free report or video. Visitors unwilling to give an email address are unlikely to pay you later. Example: 1,000 daily visitors at a 30% opt-in rate produce about 300 interested leads. [L0523.060]
  • Few shoppers want another newsletter, but many will hand over an email address for something free. An opt-in bribe makes the trade worth it. [L0757.011]
  • A new brand selling only on pre-order struggles to convert because it lacks trust and authenticity. Collecting sign-ups or notify-me requests is a softer way to capture demand. [L1016.053]
  • Capture visitors' contact details and set up exit-intent overlays so visitors who leave can be remarketed to. [L1002.033] (older, 2019)
  • An exit-intent overlay captures visitor emails at the moment they leave and is a core tool for building the list early on. [L0963.059] (older, 2022)
  • Even with a small ad budget, use an exit-intent overlay to collect emails from visitors who are leaving. Captured emails feed the abandoned cart and promotion emails. [L0468.088] (older, 2020)
  • Combine a notification bar, an exit-intent overlay and email capture with an automated follow-up to turn first-time visitors into buyers sooner. Also set up abandoned cart emails. [L1088.059] (older, 2020)
  • Blog posts are a good place to collect email leads. Adding an email signup section to the blog posts template helps build the list. [L1265.005]
  • For paid traffic, set the lead ad destination to a squeeze page that offers a free gift, a discount coupon or a popular product in exchange for an email opt-in. Sell offers to opted-in people over time by email. Enter the destination URL in the website URL field of the ad. [L0641.006]

Where opt-ins go on the site

  • Every content page should carry a content-based opt-in: blog posts, the blog index, the home page and collection pages. Use a sidebar opt-in, an inline opt-in below the content on blog posts and collection pages, and an exit-intent pop-up on all pages except product pages. [L0571.007] (older, 2021-22)
  • Offer opt-ins on every page of the site, not only in the footer or on the home page. Use different opt-ins for different parts of the site so visitors at different funnel stages get relevant offers and the message is not repetitive. [L0571.024] (older, 2021-22)
  • Footer-only opt-ins are seen by very few visitors, since only about 20 percent of visitors scroll to the bottom of a page. Scroll-mapping software can show this on most sites. [L0571.025] (older, 2021-22)
  • Running only one opt-in offer across the whole site is a common error. Visitors arrive at different stages of the buying journey, so one offer cannot match all of them. If an opt-in rarely gets sign-ups, it is usually the first thing to fix. [L0571.002] (older, 2021-22)
  • Blog layout: make the top third of each blog post a squeeze-style opt-in section where the only available action is entering an email address. Visitors from search, social or guest content see it first. This turns the blog into a conversion tool rather than only a content page. [L0523.026]
  • Place an inline opt-in in the middle of a blog post. An opt-in for a related downloadable guide on the same topic has a high opt-in rate, because the reader is already interested in that subject. [L0571.009] (older, 2021-22)
  • Place a related content opt-in below the author information at the end of the article. Readers who reach the bottom have just finished the article and are most interested in the topic. [L0571.041] (older, 2021-22)
  • In the Manhattan theme, the email signup section on blog posts can be given an accent color to make it stand out. The change is saved in the theme editor and is visible after refreshing the page. [L1265.006]
  • Place an opt-in bribe in the website footer, offering a free item in exchange for an email address. [L0757.012]
  • Replace the default Shopify newsletter opt-in in the footer or sidebar with a giveaway offer. Keep the same form, change the text to name a valuable prize, and state the entry rules. [L0923.007]
  • Add an exit-intent overlay to the website, which appears as a visitor is about to leave, to capture emails or promote offers. [L0757.013]
  • Place the consultation or bribe offer next to the opt-in form on the static site. [L0514.017]

Offer and opt-in copy

The bribe

  • A bribe is a free item that visitors already want, given in exchange for their email address. Email is low-friction to share, but visitors only give it when they trust the offer and expect it to be respected. [L0514.010]
  • Match the bribe to the stage of the visitor. Someone who has not chosen a niche yet responds to a list of niche ideas. Someone further along responds to a narrow piece of the paid product. [L0514.011]
  • Pattern for a converting bribe: offer one narrow, specific snippet of the paid product that gives an immediate, concrete benefit as a free download. It works because the visitor gets proof of value before paying anything. [L0514.012]
    • Formula: [specific free piece] + [what it solves] + [quick result]
    • Example 1: A free one-page checklist that shows how to fix your morning routine to cut afternoon fatigue within three days.
    • Example 2: A free 15-minute video on the three fastest ways to lower your monthly phone bill this week.
    • [L0514.012]
  • Pattern for a bribe headline: [number of tips] + [specific fast result] + [timeframe]. Specific numbers and a time limit make the free offer feel concrete and quick to use. [L0514.013]
    • Example 1: Seven budget meal prep ideas that help you cut 150 dollars from your grocery bill in four weeks.
    • Example 2: Five quick desk stretches that ease neck pain in ten days.
    • [L0514.013]
  • Physical bribes, such as a free product with a shipping charge, are more expensive but produce more valuable leads. People who pay for shipping give their full name, email and shipping address, which signals strong purchase intent. [L0514.014]
  • Case: a company marketed to preppers, people who expect a disaster and buy survival supplies. It offered a credit-card-sized folding knife as a free bribe, and customers paid shipping. The bribe was expensive, but the leads were very valuable because they came with shipping details and a paid order. [L0514.015]
  • A free consultation, such as a 10-minute video call on a specific topic, works as a bribe when you have expertise but do not want to create a digital or physical giveaway. It costs your time per lead, so it suits low volumes. [L0514.016]
  • The opt-in form collects a name and email address. Pair it with a catchy, specific headline that names the free bribe. [L0514.017]
  • Quizzes, surveys and giveaways are covered in their own section below.

Opt-in copy

  • Make every opt-in specific and valuable by naming the exact item the subscriber will receive. Vague promises such as "our newsletter and discounts" attract almost no one, because the visitor cannot judge the value. (Quote removed; vague promises such as our newsletter and discounts attract almost no one.) [L0571.003] (older, 2021-22)
  • Each opt-in should solve one specific problem and promise one quick win, with one call to action. State the problem solved, the benefit and the time frame of delivery. [L0571.026] (older, 2021-22)
  • A lead magnet performs best when it solves one specific problem with a specific solution for a specific segment of the market. Name the segment in the opt-in copy so the right visitors recognize the offer as meant for them. [L0571.031] (older, 2021-22)
  • Formula: [specific asset the visitor receives] + [the problem it solves] + [how and when it is delivered]. Clear copy works because the visitor sees exactly what they get and can judge its value before entering an email address. [L0571.034] (older, 2021-22)
    • Example 1: Enter your email and get our one-page checklist for recovering abandoned carts, sent to your inbox today.
    • Example 2: Join the list and receive our 12-page guide to choosing the right running shoe width, delivered instantly.
    • [L0571.034] (older, 2021-22)
  • Email signup headlines should match the brand voice, such as Join Shaka Nation or an equivalent branded call to action. Select a color scheme that matches your global theme to keep the design cohesive. [L0101.016]
  • Shorter content opt-ins often outperform long ones. A checklist, how-to guide or infographic that solves one problem is easier to accept than a 100-page ebook, because people's time is limited and large downloads put them off. [L0571.010] (older, 2021-22)
  • Content opt-in types include guides, PDF downloads of existing blog posts, infographics, ebooks, audio versions of content, checklists, cheat sheets, example collections, resource lists, inspiration files, recipe collections, calculators, spreadsheets, video courses, expert roundups and newsletters. Most can be reused from content already produced for promotion. [L0571.011] (older, 2021-22)
    • Formula: take a piece of content, identify the problem it solves, and offer it in a different format as a download behind an email field. [L0571.011] (older, 2021-22)
    • Example for an outdoor grill seller: Get our top 10 ways to grill fish, with the recipes using our grill accessories, sent to your inbox. [L0571.011] (older, 2021-22)
    • Example for a homeware seller: Download 50 living room designs that feature the products we sell. [L0571.011] (older, 2021-22)
  • Sign-up success message: remove any line that tells the visitor to use the code to claim an offer, and state the code directly, for example with a line like here is your discount code. Keep the confirmation line about subscribing. [L0607.016]

  • A squeeze page is a simple opt-in page that asks for an email address in return for a free report or video. It removes ads, navigation tabs and every other distraction, so the visitor either subscribes or leaves. Its single goal is to convert visitors into subscribers. [L0523.078]

Discount types and offer rules

Fixed amount versus percentage

  • A fixed-amount dollar discount tends to convert better than a percentage discount for sign-up offers, because shoppers can see the exact value they receive. This is the author's experience, not a measured result. [L0634.003]
  • A sign-up pop-up can use a fixed dollar amount instead of a percentage, such as save $5 with your first order, when a fixed amount feels like a clearer and bigger reward to the visitor. The matching discount code must be created in Shopify as a fixed amount with the same value, or the promise on the pop-up will not work at checkout. [L1297.046]
  • Example from the lesson: a 5-dollar discount for an email address, with the headline changed to a question such as Do you want 5 dollars off? and a button reading Sign up to get the deal. The coupon is then created in Shopify Discounts as an Amount off order fixed amount of 5 dollars. [L0634.004]
  • Percentage offers used in practice: 10% for an email sign-up and 15% for an SMS sign-up. [L0418.037] [L1138.001]
  • Sources give no measured comparison between fixed and percentage offers. Use the fixed amount where the reward is clearer that way, and make sure the Shopify code matches the promise. [L0634.003] [L1297.046]

Channel-specific and first-purchase offers

  • Use different discount offers by channel within one pop-up: 10% for email sign-ups and 15% for SMS sign-ups. Name the campaign so the offers are visible in the campaign name, which helps when comparing results later. [L1138.001]
  • Discount opt-ins should be used only on product pages and only for the first purchase. Keep them time-limited, with windows such as 48 hours, 36 hours or one week, and do not make the window too long. Offer a meaningful to exceptional discount and deliver it by pop-up or exit-intent pop-up. [L0571.013] (older, 2021-22)
  • Example (older, 2021-22): a discount opt-in on a product page shows a 15% off next-order offer with an email field, a Be the first to know about new products line and a No thanks option. [L0571.016]
  • Welcome offer only for new leads: filter for Amount spent equals zero dollars so the welcome offer goes only to new leads, not previous customers who already know the brand. [L0201.010]
  • Example: a merch store set the pop-up to display after 60 seconds, offered discount code save 10, and automated welcome emails to new subscribers while automatically excluding customers with abandoned cart tags and prior purchase history. [L0201.014]
  • An exit-intent overlay should offer something different from the header discount. Do not repeat the same percentage in the overlay, since visitors already see it and have no reason to enter their email. A vague wording such as enter your email for a special discount also works. [L1090.071] (older, 2021)
  • The Coupon Pop app for Shopify can show an expiring discount pop-up, such as 10 percent off, to collect email addresses. The same offer can stay in the store header, and the pop-up gives visitors a reason to leave their email instead of exiting. [L0923.006]
  • Older rule (2020): an exit-intent overlay that offers a fixed discount or coupon in exchange for an email address is standard on large e-commerce sites. Giving a $50 coupon for signing up to a mailing list is legal. [L0967.076] (older, 2020)
  • Offer must be stated clearly in the headline: a pop-up with a clear offer converts better than a prettier pop-up without one. [L0418.010]

Timing, triggers and frequency

  • Never show a pop-up or any overlay on page load. Do not show an email pop-up immediately on page load. [L1213.065] [L0607.011]
  • Older rule (2021-22): do not show intrusive pop-ups or interstitials, including discount or newsletter pop-ups, within the first 30 seconds of a visit. [L1099.005] (older, 2021-22)
  • Triggering immediately is penalized by Google, and discount pop-ups should be shown only to visitors who show real interest in the offer. [L0571.014] (older, 2021-22)
  • Google can see page code loaded by pop-ups and interstitials that cover a significant part of the screen when it crawls a page, and may deprioritize such a page. Such overlays also reduce the chance that visitors stay. [L1099.007] (older, 2021-22)
  • An interstitial is any overlay that covers part of the page, including discount or newsletter pop-ups and intrusive chat widgets that open as large bubbles. Keep both kinds off the screen during the first 30 seconds of a visit. [L1099.021] (older, 2021-22)
  • Avoid showing the pop-up at the very start of the visit. [L0363.037]
  • Showing the pop-up immediately when a visitor arrives annoys them and makes them leave. Let them scroll or stay on the page first. [L0605.041]
  • A scroll-depth rule works as an engagement filter. A visitor who has read about half the page is more likely to want a discount or need reassurance, so the offer appears when interest is already shown. Pair the scroll rule with a delay of about 10 seconds and show the pop-up only when all selected rules are met. [L0607.049]

Exit-intent overlays

  • Exit-intent overlay: triggered when visitors show exit behavior, to capture visitors who would otherwise leave without engagement or conversion. [L0040.004]
  • A bounced visit occurs when someone lands on the site but takes no action within a set time window. They may be distracted, on a call, or have the site open in a background tab. Interrupt them with a pop-up that exchanges a discount code for their email address, giving you another contact channel before they leave. [L0201.001]
  • Configure the email pop-up to display after 60 seconds of visitor inactivity on the same page (no clicks or navigation) to catch them before they close the tab. [L0201.002]
  • Page coverage conflicts between sources:
    • Course A-style setup: the email pop-up displays on all page types: homepage, blog posts, collection pages and product pages. Use this when the goal is maximum capture across the store. [L0201.003]
    • Older rule (2021-22): exit-intent on all pages except product pages. Use this when the product page should stay free of overlays. [L0571.007] (older, 2021-22)
  • Setup via the bounce visits module: use Module 5.7 (Bounce visits) to set up an exit-intent overlay that captures visitor email addresses before they leave the store. [L0208.008]
  • Setup procedure in Source E's pop-up tool: go to Trigger > Add new trigger and choose On exit-intent (When a visitor is about to leave your site). Set Device to Desktop or Both devices. Exit-intent catches visitors who leave without converting and works on both mobile and desktop. [L1138.002]
  • Use an exit-intent pop-up as the last chance to catch a visitor who is about to leave the site. Show it only when the visitor moves to leave. [L1213.066]
  • An exit pop is a last-chance pop-up that appears when a visitor tries to leave without subscribing. It can include a special offer for subscribing. Since departing visitors rarely return, it is acceptable to be a little intrusive here. [L0523.083]
  • Do not run two exit-intent pop-ups at once, such as one from the theme and another from Klaviyo. Keep one and turn off the other. [L0855.116] (2023)
  • Manual follow-up for opt-ins: if no exit-intent overlay is running, set one up. Manually email everyone who opts in (not automated messages) with the following text, which builds the relationship and recovers sales:
I'm the owner, noticed you on our site, happy to help with any questions.

[L0274.017] (2023)

Pop-up design

Visual and layout rules

  • Baseline case: a starting pop-up with a 0.4% conversion rate and 16,000 views produced only 71 email submissions. This baseline shows the value of a stronger visual pop-up design. [L0418.006]
  • Use a lighter pop-up tool than the Klaviyo embed (OptiMonk was chosen for this reason) because heavier pop-up scripts slow page load time, and page speed affects conversion. [L0418.007]
  • Make pop-up buttons and inputs very clickable: use display manual with a fixed width of about 400 px and a height of 50 to 70 px, and keep buttons larger than inputs on mobile. [L0418.008]
  • Match the pop-up button and input shape, color and shadow to the store's own product-page buttons so the pop-up feels like part of the site. [L0418.009]
  • Set the floating product image and text to be legible and eye-catching. A floating image breaking out of the pop-up frame attracts attention, and a text shadow improves readability on busy backgrounds. [L0418.011]
  • The image on a sign-up pop-up should relate to the niche or the product, or show a gift, because it sits beside the discount offer and supports it. Replace the default image in the Styles section before publishing the form. [L0607.054]
  • Check every pop-up on mobile view and adjust sizes separately for desktop and mobile, because the layout, button size and font size often need different values on each device. [L0418.012]
  • Check the sign-up form on a mobile preview before publishing. Most visitors in a mobile-heavy store arrive from phones, and a form that looks fine on desktop can be hard to use on a phone. [L0607.012]
  • Pure black on a pop-up background can look fake and digital next to a website. A dark charcoal tone that blends into the site looks more natural, so pick the shade by eye beside the store's own colors. [L0418.027]

OptiMonk settings

  • Layering: give the email input fields and the Reveal Code button a higher z-index (about 10) than the floating image (about 1), so the overlay image does not capture clicks. [L0418.013]
  • Padding: zero out any leftover column spacing first, then open Column > Advanced and set left and right padding to 0 and top and bottom padding to 50 px. If the result looks cramped or too loose, test 100 px on top and bottom and keep the value that looks more even. [L0418.023]
  • Text shadow: set text shadow under Style > Text shadows, using a medium shadow on all text blocks. A light shadow looked too weak on a sandy background, and applying the same shadow everywhere keeps readability consistent. [L0418.024]
  • Hero image: for a full-width hero background, OptiMonk's image fit options under Style > Image are cover, contain and original, with an alignment choice that can be adjusted. In the tested pop-up the original fit looked most natural, so compare fit modes visually before settling on one. [L0418.025]
  • Container shadow: give the whole pop-up container a soft shadow with a medium size and about 30% opacity so it lifts off the page. Keep the opacity low, because a heavy shadow makes the pop-up look dated. [L0418.026]
  • Mobile layout procedure:
    1. Switch the OptiMonk editor to mobile view and review the pop-up before changing anything.
    2. Select the floating image, go to Display > Position, and set it to Top.
    3. Select the column and set left and right padding to 0, then add back about 25 px if the content touches the edges.
    4. Select Main page and set its size to 95% of the screen.
    5. Set mobile inputs to a fixed width of about 400 px and a height of about 50 px, and set the button to about 60 to 70 px high.
    6. Raise any headline or button text that is too small on mobile; the tested pop-up used sizes around 125 to 150.
    7. Adjust the desktop and mobile layouts separately, because they often need different values.
    • [L0418.028]
  • Preview caveat: a pop-up preview may not show on some themes, and an OptiMonk pop-up does not appear until the app embed is enabled in the Shopify theme settings. Check that the embed is on before assuming the pop-up is broken. [L0418.014]

Pop-up tools and platform setup

Choosing a tool

  • Tools for pop-ups, exit-intent forms, inline forms on content pages and sidebar forms include OptinMonster and OptiMonk. Other options include Sumo and Just You Know. [L0608.006] (older, 2021-22)
  • OptinMonster is used for all pop-ups, including exit-intent, timed, inline forms and opt-ins, and it integrates with Shopify. [L0682.017] (older, 2021-22)
  • The key criterion when choosing a provider is its impact on page speed, because some scripts load slowly and hurt site performance. Privy is named as an example of a slow tool with problematic script loading, so avoid it or switch away from it. [L0608.007] (older, 2021-22)
  • Built-in forms from email platforms such as Klaviyo are weaker than dedicated form tools, because email software is not built around forms and pop-ups and those features feel like an afterthought. A separate specialized form tool is therefore preferred. [L0608.008] (older, 2021-22)
  • Conflict: the 2021-22 advice prefers a dedicated tool, while the Source C method builds the sign-up pop-up in Klaviyo. Use Klaviyo's sign-up form when it meets the page-speed test, and use OptiMonk when the Klaviyo embed is too heavy. [L0608.008] [L0607.005] [L0418.007] (older for the first)
  • Pop-ups can still collect email addresses if used carefully, although ad blockers and some ad networks reject ads that send traffic to pages with pop-ups. Newer pop-up formats are harder to block than older ones. Check the advertising platform's policy before relying on them. [L0523.077]

Klaviyo sign-up form

  • Choose Pop-up, not Full page or Flyout, because pop-up has the highest conversion. Choose Email only as the form's channel and select the Newsletter list so new addresses land in the newsletter list. [L0607.005]
  • Teaser: keep the teaser off by default, because a teaser that stays at the bottom of the screen annoys mobile visitors. Turn it on only when the sign-up form conversion rate drops. [L0607.006]
  • Targeting: under Show based on rules, show the pop-up after 10 seconds on the page, and add the rule that the visitor has scrolled 30 to 50 percent of the page. Set the condition to show only when all selected rules are met. [L0607.009]
  • Creation procedure:
    1. Click Create, choose the email-only option and select the Newsletter list.
    2. Choose the Pop-up form type and click Next.
    3. Set the teaser to off, unless the sign-up rate drops.
    4. Choose a static coupon for the incentive and enter the code created in Shopify (for example WELCOME10).
    5. Open Edit form and adjust the layout; check the mobile version, because most visitors come from mobile devices.
    6. Open Styles to change the image, text and styling options.
    7. Open Targeting and set the rules (see the targeting setting above).
    8. Edit the copy: headline, email field text, button text and decline link.
    9. Open the success step, remove the line about claiming the offer and add a line that gives the discount code directly.
    10. Change the status from Draft to Live, then click Exit.
    11. Preview the form on a mobile device before finishing.
    • [L0607.008]
  • Mistake: showing the sign-up pop-up the moment a page loads is too aggressive and annoys many visitors. A delay of about 10 seconds combined with a scroll condition gives better results. [L0607.011]
  • Coupon pop-up copy: use the Klaviyo success message and the Shopify code as described above. [L0607.016] [L0607.008]

Targeting, segments and reuse

  • Targeting: under Select who should see the pop-up, choose a predefined segment such as Non-subscribers so people already on the list do not see it. Other predefined options include Now visitors, Active shoppers, All visitors, Engaged visitors, New home page visitors, Just purchased and Custom targeting. Saved segments and Copy from previous campaign are also available. [L1138.005]
  • In the targeting step, Load segment opens your saved segments, and Save as segment stores the rule you built so it can be reused in other pop-up campaigns. Copy from previous campaign reuses targeting from an existing pop-up. Save a segment when the same audience rule is needed across several pop-ups. [L1138.017]
  • Welcome pop-ups can be limited to new visitors only, but this requires a custom code addition in the theme. [L1094.069] (2023)

Shopify automation after capture

  • Set up Shopify Email automation triggered by bounced-visit pop-up submissions. It automatically sends captured contacts a coupon code while simultaneously forwarding their contact information to your email for manual follow-up. [L0154.003]
  • Module 5.7 (Bounce visits) is the setup for the exit-intent overlay that feeds this automation. [L0208.008]
  • Captured emails feed abandoned cart and promotion emails. [L0468.088] (older, 2020)
  • Over time, feed contacts from exit-intent overlays into automated flows. [L1092.083] (older, 2022)
  • Remarketing: visitors who leave can be remarketed to once their contact details are captured. [L1002.033] (older, 2019)

Phone capture and multi-step forms

  • An extra incentive, such as an additional 5% off, can be offered in exchange for a phone number. This works because the visitor is already looking for a better deal when they try to leave. [L0363.038]
  • Trojan Horse Method for collecting email and phone number in one pop-up:
    1. Ask for the email address first.
    2. Once the email is captured, offer the visitor the option to upgrade by also adding a phone number.
    3. If the visitor adds a phone number, give them the upgraded offer, which is a discount double the basic one.
    4. If the visitor declines, keep only the email and send the basic offer they signed up for.
    • [L0494.003]
  • Asking for the email first guarantees the email capture, while the optional phone upgrade lets the visitor choose their own reward path. The basic sign-up is never blocked by the phone request. [L0494.004]
  • Two-step form: step one collects a name and email address, which qualifies the visitor as a subscriber even if they leave. Step two collects credit card details to cover shipping in a free-plus-shipping offer. [L0523.080]
  • Case: a Klaviyo pop-up on a store with about 16,000 views produced only 71 email sign-ups, a conversion rate of about 0.4%. The rebuild used an OptiMonk pop-up with a floating product image, a 10% email discount and a follow-up SMS upgrade to 15% with TCPA-style consent text under the button. The reported result was over 30,000 USD in under 10 days. This is a self-reported figure and not independently verified. [L0418.037]

Quizzes, surveys, contests and giveaways

Quizzes

  • Quizzes are a pre-frame tool that engages visitors with personal questions that point toward the offer. Use three to four numbered questions, such as Step 1 of 4, to show progress, although some successful quizzes run to 20 questions. Requiring an email address to see the results combines the pre-frame with subscriber qualification. [L0523.068]
  • A quiz can segment the audience by answers. Asking whether a visitor owns a dog or a cat splits the list, and a dog owner can then be asked how old the dog is and shown answers by age range. The next ad can then show a dog and recommend food suited to that dog's age. [L0523.069]
  • Quizzes and surveys can run on any page: home page, product pages, collection pages and blog posts, as pop-ups or as fixed items such as sidebar or inline forms. Home, collection and product placements suit pop-ups; collection, blog and blog post pages suit sidebar items. Each quiz or survey should have its own list. [L0571.017] (older, 2021-22)
  • Quizzes and surveys work because they are fun or engaging and carry no pressure to buy, so they can reach very high opt-in rates. The quiz does not have to be about the products; it only has to relate to the niche and interest the visitor. Example: a surfboard seller can use Which world surf pro are you? and later move these subscribers toward surfboard products. [L0571.018] (older, 2021-22)
  • Tools: Typeform was used to build quizzes and surveys in the course. Qzzr (Quizzer) is an alternative for quizzes. Google Forms is a simple alternative for surveys. Quizzes run on the page or in a lightbox pop-up, and the visitor enters an email address at the end to see the results. [L0571.019] (older, 2021-22)
  • Quiz formula: Which [recognizable figure or category from your niche] are you? with a short quiz that the visitor finishes to see the result. The question invites self-identification and fun, so visitors give their email to see the answer even when the quiz does not mention products. [L0571.036] (older, 2021-22)
    • Example 1: Which camping style matches your next trip?
    • Example 2: Which coffee brewing method fits your morning routine?
    • [L0571.036] (older, 2021-22)
  • Keep a quiz short, with only a few simple inputs before the email step, so visitors finish quickly. The email request should come after the quiz has already delivered some engagement. [L0571.039] (older, 2021-22)
  • A quiz works as a lead magnet when the results are revealed only after the visitor enters an email address. This turns the quiz into a fast way to grow the list with people who searched for related products. [L0922.018] (older, 2021-22)
  • A quiz built to capture emails can be linked from several places on the store, such as collection pages and the home page, not only from ads. For paid search, use a dedicated quiz page as the final URL so the ad headline and the landing page match. [L0922.026] (older, 2021-22)

Surveys

  • A survey asks visitors questions about their industry and preferences. Turn the results into a content piece such as We surveyed 100 people in this industry, which is likely to be shared. Send each respondent the results before publishing so they see how their views compare. [L0571.020] (older, 2021-22)

Contests and giveaways

  • Small contests and giveaways generate large numbers of opt-ins. The prize does not need to be the main product; cheap items that sell for around $100 can be bought and given away. Offer contests on any page through pop-ups, exit-intent pop-ups or sidebar forms, and promote them on social media to grow entries. [L0571.021] (older, 2021-22)
  • Giveaway prizes can be set so that one prize is awarded for every set number of opt-ins, such as one prize per 1,000 opt-ins. Even a 500 dollar prize can remain profitable under this rule, because collected emails are cheap to market to later. [L0923.008]
  • Giveaway opt-in text for the newsletter box. Replace the placeholders in square brackets:
Enter your email to win [PRIZE, for example a 200 dollar gift card to our store or our best-selling product] at [STORE NAME]. We will give away one [PRIZE] for every [NUMBER, for example 1,000] people who opt in. Full rules: [LINK OR TEXT WITH RULES].

[L0923.009]

  • Replace the default newsletter opt-in with a giveaway offer that names a valuable prize, such as a 200 dollar gift card or your best-selling product, and states the entry rules. [L0923.007]

Sales event and other capture formats

  • Sales event opt-ins offer presale or exclusive access to a big sales event such as Black Friday, Easter or Veterans Day. They can appear as pop-up or exit-intent on any page, or as sidebar items on collection, blog and blog post pages. Each sales event should have its own list. Show them only in the one or two weeks before the event. [L0571.022] (older, 2021-22)
  • Squeeze pop: a button placed in blog posts or articles that opens a squeeze-style pop-up when clicked. A visitor who enters an email address moves to the next page. Squeeze pops work anywhere a button can be placed, including places where normal opt-in forms would not fit. [L0523.079]
  • Free account: gives visitors access to a membership or a lite version of your software in exchange for signing up. Free accounts are similar to squeeze pages but collect more personal information, such as a physical address and phone number, while keeping conversions high. Use them for software and membership products. [L0523.082]
  • Webinar registration is a natural way to collect email addresses, because registrants expect follow-up emails with details. Automated webinars let people pick the day they want to watch, and after entering an email address they become subscribers. Registrants are also primed to look for your emails. [L0523.081]
  • Consultation as a bribe: see the bribe section above. [L0514.016]

Page speed and technical considerations

  • Choose the pop-up tool by its effect on page speed, not only its features. [L0608.007] (older, 2021-22)
  • Use a lighter pop-up script than the Klaviyo embed where page speed is a concern, because page speed affects conversion. [L0418.007]
  • Check that the pop-up embed is enabled before assuming a pop-up is broken. [L0418.014]
  • Overlays that cover a significant part of the screen can be seen by Google and reduce the chance that visitors stay. Keep overlays off the first 30 seconds of a visit. [L1099.007] [L1099.005] (older, 2021-22)
  • Pop-ups and overlays can be blocked by ad blockers and some ad networks. Check the ad platform's policy before sending paid traffic to pop-up pages. [L0523.077]
  • Measurement: use Lucky Orange, a screen-recording app, to watch visitor sessions and find the average time on site needed for pop-up timing. [L0363.039]
  • Do not add Google reCAPTCHA to an email sign-up form to block spam, because it can reduce opt-in rates. Monitor new sign-ups and delete obvious spam manually. [L0836.010] (2024)

Compliance and consent

  • Give a fixed-amount or percentage coupon only where the platform terms and local rules allow it. Giving a $50 coupon for signing up to a mailing list is legal. [L0967.076] (older, 2020)
  • SMS upgrades carry TCPA-style consent text under the button. [L0418.037]
  • Ads and pop-up policies: check the advertising platform's policy before relying on pop-ups in paid campaigns. [L0523.077]

Further details

  • Portugal candles brand: €1,000 total Black Friday ad budget for France market. Used list-building strategy with landing page opt-ins. Achieved €2.89 cost per lead. After €3,000 total investment, still acquiring leads at this low cost, demonstrating that list building remains profitable even when purchase CPCs spike to €20+. [L0307.008]

  • Set the pop-up targeting to Show based on rules with two triggers: exit intent (when the visitor is leaving the page) and a delay of 10 seconds after the page loads. The delay gives visitors time to scroll before the offer appears. [L0476.005]
