6.2 When to start: readiness and the pre-traffic launch checklist

Readiness gate: what must exist before paid traffic

  • Start conversion optimization only after at least five supplier approvals exist, with products from those suppliers live on the store, because without live products there is nothing to measure or improve. [L0135.001, L0583.001]
  • Conversion optimization comes after suppliers approve and real products are on the store, and the site must be optimized before any money is spent on paid traffic. [L0506.009]
  • Complete the store's conversion setup before launching paid traffic campaigns, because starting early wastes ad spend on visitors you cannot convert. [L0136.002]
  • Complete all store optimization before driving traffic to the store, so it converts from the first visitor onward. [L0158.001]
  • Finish conversion setup before sending paid traffic, so every paid click lands on a store with urgency, reviews, email capture and recovery flows already in place. Traffic setup comes after the store is ready for visitors. [L0393.010]
  • Before paying for traffic, confirm the store already converts visitors into buyers, because ads and other traffic sources only pay off when the site can close the sale. [L1064.001]
  • Conversion optimization should be done before paid or organic traffic is pushed to the store, because a store that converts poorly wastes the visitors that traffic work brings in. [L0583.005]
  • A store is never fully optimized, but the core conversion steps should be done before ads go live, since launching with the basics in place reduces the chance of wasted spend. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0664.068]
  • Before launch, the store should be optimized for conversions using a structured conversion-optimization process. This is the condition under which an early launch is recommended. [L1446.016]
  • Improve product pages and checkout before increasing ad spend, because traffic is wasted if product pages do not convert. (Older advice, 2020.) [L1111.086]

Money check before ads

  • Calculate total profitability before launching ads: supplier cost + shipping cost + allocated ad spend. This confirms margins can support scaling. [L0276.045]
  • A supplier margin of about 15 percent is too thin to fund ads, order processing and a future virtual assistant, so it does not justify paid traffic. Use organic traffic for low-margin products and spend paid traffic only on products with real margin. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0551.052]

The 11 action tasks before setting up traffic

Complete these 11 tasks before setting up traffic. [L0040.001]

  1. Enable expiring coupon codes. [L0040.001]
  2. Add and modify all provided content templates. [L0040.001]
  3. Install a review app and collect initial reviews from suppliers, family, or friends. [L0040.001]
  4. Set up social media accounts. [L0040.001]
  5. Install and configure Klaviyo for email marketing. [L0040.001]
  6. Set up an exit intent overlay to capture bouncing visitors. [L0040.001]
  7. Create an abandoned cart flow for automatic follow-up with visitors who do not complete checkout. [L0040.001]
  8. Brainstorm and plan the first bonus offer. [L0040.001]
  9. Identify competitor weaknesses and areas for improvement. [L0040.001]
  10. Apply for and set up a financing app so customers can pay in installments. [L0040.001]
  11. Install a live chat app for real-time customer communication. [L0040.001]
  • These 11 action tasks are the same process used for personal store launches before paid traffic. This confirms that the complete sequence is a validated best practice for conversion optimization. [L0040.016]

Reviews and trust: where the sources differ

  • The task list asks for initial reviews from suppliers, family, or friends. [L0040.001]
  • The Google Merchant Center note says store reviews are not required before approval, and stores without any customer reviews have been approved. (2023.) Trust should come from a clean, complete store rather than fake or added reviews. [L1114.041]
  • Apply both: treat the review app and initial reviews as part of the 11-task build, but do not treat reviews as an approval condition, and never substitute fake reviews for real trust-building. [L0040.001, L1114.041]

Store build for supplier approval (demo store)

Setup for supplier approval on a demo store: [L0976.034]

  1. Keep breadcrumbs on. [L0976.034]
  2. Turn on sticky content on desktop. [L0976.034]
  3. Set desktop media width to medium and keep constrain media to screen height on. [L0976.034]
  4. Set media fit to original or fill, whichever looks better. [L0976.034]
  5. Keep desktop layout set to thumbnail carousel. [L0976.034]
  6. Set image behavior to none, or to zoom only for high-resolution images. [L0976.034]
  7. Set mobile image behavior to none and mobile media to show thumbnails. [L0976.034]
  8. Keep the share button, SKU, vendor name, quantity selector, dynamic checkout buttons, and description on. [L0976.034]
  9. Hide the coupon block, estimated delivery, low stock message, inventory count, sticky add to cart, and countdown timer. [L0976.034]
  10. Save and reload the product page. [L0976.034]
  • Many conversion elements, such as low stock messages, coupon blocks, estimated delivery, countdown timers and extra trust badges, are not needed for supplier approval. Add them once the store has live products and traffic. Use the demo store to build the page structure first. [L0976.035]

Test purchase and checkout check

Current procedure (2023): [L0274.015]

  1. Put a $1 test product on the store. [L0274.015]
  2. Attempt a purchase on desktop. [L0274.015]
  3. Attempt a purchase on mobile. [L0274.015]
  4. Check whether checkout completes both ways. If there are zero conversions after clicks, checkout failure is the likely cause. [L0274.015]

Earlier procedure (2019, older): [L0663.065]

  1. Add a test product priced at $1 to the store. [L0663.065]
  2. Go through checkout as a customer and pay with a real credit card. [L0663.065]
  3. Confirm that every step works, including the order confirmation. [L0663.065]
  4. Install a session recording tool such as Lucky Orange and watch real visitor sessions to find where they drop off. [L0663.065]
  • Both versions test the same thing, a real purchase through checkout. The 2023 version adds the desktop and mobile comparison, and the 2019 version adds session recordings. Use both. [L0274.015, L0663.065]

Pre-traffic checklist in order

Before sending paid traffic to the store, check these items in order: [L0776.008]

  1. Payment processors [L0776.008]
  2. Markets served [L0776.008]
  3. Shipping prices [L0776.008]
  4. Legal pages [L0776.008]
  5. Checkout page [L0776.008]

Store pages to check before scaling (older advice, 2021):

  • Product pages should be fast on mobile. [L0801.119]
  • The contact page should offer multiple ways to contact you. [L0801.119]
  • The store should show a clear return and shipping policy. [L0801.119]

Do not run ads to a page that still has missing photos, broken navigation links or unfinished sections, because visitors will leave before the offer is understood. [L1016.047]

Store review gate with Elixir

Store review workflow before paid traffic: [L1452.004]

  1. Build the store with the program's tool (Elixir). [L1452.004]
  2. Do not go live directly. Submit the store for review. [L1452.004]
  3. The reviewer checks the product page, copy, offer, trust signals and checkout flow. [L1452.004]
  4. Fix the issues found. [L1452.004]
  5. Only then spend money on ads sending traffic to the store. [L1452.004]
  • A store that does not convert is a store problem, not a traffic problem, so an ad budget spent on an unreviewed store can be lost quickly. One example figure: $500 in ads sent to a store that never converted. [L1452.005]

Product page and homepage: what to finish first

  • Make the product page presentable and easy to buy from before focusing on the rest of the store. The home page can stay simple, since many visitors go straight to the product page. [L1444.035]
  • A product page in testing does not need to be perfect, but it needs the core sections. Get the structure right first, then decide how much more time to spend on it. [L1194.029]
  • Beginners often spend months polishing design while the site still has problems. A beautiful site with a scattered layout, weak trust signals, or a banner that does not show the product will not convert paid traffic. [L0709.025]

Design refinement: when to delay

  • Leave web design refinement and conversion rate optimization out of the initial store build. Schedule them after the store is live and receiving real visitors, because their decisions depend on actual traffic and behavior data. [L0555.010]
  • If the store looks fine to you, do not change the design before paid traffic arrives. Start paid ads, then compare bounce rate by paid traffic source to judge the store. (Older advice, 2022.) [L1281.045]
  • Apply both: during the initial build, defer refinement until live traffic exists. Once the store is acceptable to you, let paid-traffic bounce rates decide what to change. [L0555.010, L1281.045]

Pre-landers: when to build

  • Send paid traffic directly to the product page at the start. Add pre-landers only when scaling, and choose which pre-lander to use based on the market angle. [L1299.010]
  • Building many pre-landers before knowing which marketing angles work wastes time. First identify the winning angles through direct product page tests, then build pre-landers. [L1299.011]

Steps to build a new pre-lander test from an existing one: [L1299.009]

  1. Copy a basic pre-lander that already exists. [L1299.009]
  2. Swap in the new copy and new images. [L1299.009]
  3. Launch it to test a new marketing angle. [L1299.009]

Urgency elements: coupons, timers and announcement bars

  • An expiring coupon is a day-one requirement for all stores. It belongs to the 11 action tasks and is set up before traffic. [L0139.002, L0040.001]
  • Announcement bars create urgency and lift conversion rates, so they only make sense once real traffic and live products exist. Hide the bar until then. [L1309.040]
  • While the store is still a demo, hide the announcement bar with the eye icon in the customizer. Its text and colors can be set later. [L1309.041]
  • Countdown timers are hidden on the demo store and added once the store has live products and traffic. [L0976.034, L0976.035]
