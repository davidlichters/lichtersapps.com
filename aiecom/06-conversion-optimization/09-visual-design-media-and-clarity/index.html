6.9 Visual design, media and clarity

Clarity comes first

  • Clarity comes before everything else in how an offer and marketing message are presented, because a confused visitor does not buy. Before adding any element to a page, check whether it truly adds value for the customer and whether it can be made simpler. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0409.001]
  • Complex or expensive products may need more information on the page than simple ones, but every extra element must earn its place. Apply the same simplicity to the wider site content, not only to the offer. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0409.002]
  • Focus on the few most important pieces of information a customer needs to be persuaded to buy this product from this store, and present them as cleanly as possible. A clear page removes the need for conversion tricks such as fake scarcity or fake discounts. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0409.050]
  • An offer the visitor does not notice on the page effectively does not exist. Present the offer so it is easy to see and consume, and make sure it is not hidden behind other page elements. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0473.017]
  • Usability affects conversion more than visual design once trust factors and good products are in place. Overly decorative pages with heavy motion do not raise conversion if visitors struggle to find products and buy. [L1098.001]
  • Usable and fast beats heavily designed. Heavy design slows load time and hurts conversions, so a site should let people find products quickly. A beautiful design can still be kept if it loads fast. (Older advice, 2018.) [L1085.058]
  • A clean, simple and easy-to-use layout converts better than an aesthetically impressive design. You do not need to be an expert web designer to copy a proven layout that works. [L1325.021]
  • Keep the design minimal: avoid flashy effects, motion, excessive color, bells and whistles, because they distract customers and rarely improve conversions. Design does not drive conversion as much as often assumed. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1463.007]
  • Do not rely on large decorative images, video backgrounds or parallax effects to sell a product. These do not answer the buyer's questions and can hurt speed and usability. [L1098.023]
  • Avoid bright, flashy ad boxes on the homepage and product pages, because they can look tacky and reduce trust. Use a clean design that focuses on what your customers need, instead of copying competitors' layouts. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0358.053]
  • Common web design mistakes for a dropship store include making the site look busy (animations, motion, glitter-style effects, many bright colors), using very small text, using blurry or tiny product images, and uploading very large image files that slow the site down. Each one reduces trust, readability, or load speed. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1463.011]

Cognitive load and skimming

  • Shoppers avoid any work that makes them think, such as calculating a saving or decoding an offer. Use simple, direct wording on every element of a product page, so the value can be understood at a glance. Less cognitive load on the visitor makes the purchase decision easier. [L1207.048]
  • Design pages so the visitor does not have to think: every extra calculation or search for information adds resource load and reduces conversions. Keep the path to the decision simple. [L1209.008]
  • Shoppers avoid mental effort, so each element of a product page should be understood at a glance without calculation or interpretation. Work such as deriving a percentage discount or decoding a feature list lowers conversion, so state the result directly. [L1210.026]
  • Customers often skip long product descriptions and look at images, GIFs and reviews first, so visual proof and reviews matter more than text length. [L0773.022]
  • Most visitors look at product images and GIFs before reading long text, so the visuals carry the product description. Reviews should be visible and should answer typical buyer concerns, such as fit or size, in the review images. [L0774.024]
  • Readers who skim need evidence they can absorb at a glance, so present each proof point in a clear chart, simple graph, or short quote from a respected professional. Colorful, easy-to-read data convinces skimmers faster than dense tables, even though careful buyers will still check the underlying detail. [L0340.080]
  • Each section of a landing page should answer one question in a scannable way, since most visitors do not read long paragraphs. Use headlines, icons, and bold key words so people can scan the page. [L0387.024]
  • About 75 percent of visitors will not read three paragraphs of text on a page, so explain the product with visuals and short steps instead. [L0387.025]
  • Dense copy with small text and long blocks gets skipped by readers, so good wording has no effect if the layout discourages reading. This matters most on mobile, where small, bunched-up text is harder to read and less likely to be read than on desktop. Check the presentation of copy before judging its persuasive power. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0925.018]

White space, proximity and layout

  • White space is any empty area, not necessarily white. It groups elements, focuses attention on the centre and prevents clutter, which reduces cognitive overload and stops viewers leaving. [L0755.017]
  • Proximity: elements placed close together are perceived as belonging together, while space between them separates groups. On a product page, a title, subtitle, text, button and price box can be grouped or separated purely through spacing, even if they look different. [L0755.009]
  • White space is one of the strongest clarity tools: it makes text, images and buttons easier for the eye to process. Crowding elements into a small space makes visitors work harder and increases the chance they leave. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0409.008]
  • Generous white space between elements helps visitors read and interpret text and images, and reduces eye strain caused by busy layouts. White space matters especially on mobile screens, since most e-commerce traffic now comes from mobile devices rather than desktop. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1463.008]
  • More space around text and elements makes a page easier to read and understand. Increase section spacing rather than squeezing content together. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1310.025]
  • Format page content by breaking up long sections with spacing and shorter paragraphs. What you see in the Shopify editor is exactly how customers will see it. [L0090.010]
  • Keep the area around the add to cart button clean and free of competing colored elements, so the buy button cannot be missed. Visitors should never have to search for the main purchase action. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0409.023]
  • A cluttered area above the fold, with many colors, mixed font weights and colors, tiny text and tightly packed checkmarks, makes the page hard to read. Text too small to recognize and repeated lines, such as two identical 'best price guarantee' statements, waste space. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0409.034]

Color system

  • Use one main brand color for the design and one contrasting color reserved only for calls to action. Keep the palette as small as possible, because every extra color competes with the buttons. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0409.021]
  • The add to cart color does not need to be red, yellow, green or blue. What matters is that it contrasts with the other primary colors of the theme, and a color wheel helps find a contrasting hue for a given palette. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0409.022]
  • Use a limited color system: white, black and one brand color (or a brand color palette with lighter and darker shades). Never use more than four colors across the store, and make the call-to-action button a darker, bolder shade so it stands out. [L1259.016, L1260.028]
  • Keep the store colors to at most four: white, black, one brand color or palette, and a darker bold color for the call-to-action button so it stands out. [L1260.028]
  • Use a simple color scheme based on the brand's chosen colors, about three colors, and do not overuse them on the site. This is the older target of about three colors (2021-22). The Source C ceiling of four colors applies when a brand needs a fourth tone for the call-to-action. [L1463.007, L1259.016, L1260.028]
  • For a Black Friday store, use a theme color dedicated to the sale in a simple modern palette. Avoid very bright orange, yellow or red, which can look skeptical to customers. [L1381.041]
  • When the section background is changed from light to dark, switch the text, icon and divider colors from dark to light so they stay visible. Keep the colors taken from the brand palette. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1310.024]
  • Dark text on a dark tab background is hard to read, so use a light text color such as white on dark inactive tabs. Check the contrast in the live preview before you save the layout settings. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1300.038]

Typography and text length

  • Use one consistent high-quality font across the whole store and keep the same color and font usage on every page to build brand value. [L1259.017, L1260.028]
  • Use text large enough to read easily on mobile and desktop. Small text forces visitors to strain, causing them to skim and skip important information. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1463.009]
  • Long paragraphs on the website make visitors lose interest and leave. Bullets and short headlines work better. [L1436.040]
  • Keep each paragraph to no more than four lines and use bullet points and several short headlines instead. Landing page copy should never exceed about four lines per paragraph, because longer blocks make visitors leave. Long text blocks of eight or nine lines make visitors leave. [L1382.031, L1436.036]
  • Use one-line, benefit-driven bullet points on the landing page instead of large paragraphs, because first-time visitors leave pages that look like long text. [L0871.025]
  • Long walls of text on the product page are hard to read on mobile, so compress the product description into short sections that buyers can scan. [L1016.050]
  • Long product descriptions, especially AI-written ones, can overwhelm shoppers. Splitting them into collapsible H6 sections keeps all the text on the page while letting the visitor open only the parts they want to read. [L1189.025]
  • Long product descriptions are fine when the product needs them, but split them into short paragraphs and use several h2 and h3 subheadings. Scanning shoppers look for headings that match their question and read only that section. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1522.014]
  • Check that long text is split into chunks with subheadings, short paragraphs of two to three lines, and a readable font size of 18. [L1099.023]

Headlines and copy layout

  • The headline must be bigger and bolder than the body copy and the rest of the text on the page. Its size and weight determine whether it is seen as the entry point to the text. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0416.008]
  • Show product benefits within the product image gallery by adding short benefit text on the images. This lets visitors understand the offer and key benefits while scrolling through visuals. [L0918.019]
  • Keep star rating icons visibly smaller than the product price text in a product block. The price should stay the dominant element, and oversized icons pull attention away from it. [L1462.060]

Product images and visual proof

  • High-quality product images are critical. Images can be digitally created rather than photographed. Tools like Elance, Odesk, or 99designs can produce professional images that make products stand out from competitors. [L0227.019]
  • Image quality matters: product images can be edited with Canva or AI tools to look more professional. Testimonial images in raw, real-customer style also work well, but quoted written reviews are acceptable if you have no photos. [L0335.041]
  • Use as many product images as possible, covering every angle, in large, clean, high-resolution format. Strong product pages can show over 10 images, because buyers spending a lot of money want to inspect the product fully. [L1130.014]
  • Include product photos only if each one adds value to a purchase decision. Zoomed-in detail shots and a product in a room are valuable, while repeated lifestyle shots add little; 50 photos is excessive. [L1115.070]
  • Competitors with low-resolution photos or only a few small screenshots leave room to win. Upload high-resolution images from several angles and add more product shots than the competitor. [L0894.008]
  • Ask suppliers for additional product images, for example from their warehouse or from trade shows. Competitors often reuse the same two supplier images, so extra images help you stand out. [L0894.027]
  • Use good-quality lifestyle images on the product page, because they show real use and increase trust. [L1436.033]
  • Replace the demo hero image with supplier lifestyle photos that show products you actually sell, if you have them. Remove homepage and menu categories for product lines you have no approved supplier for, and add them back once you get one. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1145.031]
  • Use a short video or photo strip near the top of the page with three image placeholders, one showing the product itself, one showing the product in use, and one showing the result or a lifestyle scene. Real photos from the supplier or from customers beat stock images. [L1188.044]
  • When a product has no obvious size, show it being worn or held by a person in the product images so shoppers can judge its size. Without this reference, buyers cannot tell what they will receive. [L1225.091]
  • Show the size information inside the product image with clear indications. Buyers then do not need to scroll down to a separate size guide. Test this against the current layout. [L1194.027]
  • For products that need explanation, add a landscape video of customer testimonials or a longer case-study interview with a real user to show how the product is used and why it is valued. [L1016.038]
  • For bundle product images, use pack images with a transparent background. This makes it easy to display discounts and the add-to-cart button on the bundle. [L1299.007]
  • Enlarge product images on desktop and keep the thumbnails. Images are a major factor in conversion for products that need demonstration. [L1225.038]

Image quality, customization and compression

  • Product images must be high quality and correctly sized so the customer can see and identify the product, while keeping file sizes small enough that the site loads quickly. Blurry, tiny, or very large image files should be avoided; large image files slow site loading and make pages load poorly. Images from good suppliers are usually high quality, which is also a sign of a strong supplier. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1463.010]
  • Low-quality or poorly optimized product images directly reduce conversion rates, creating a trade-off between aggressive compression for page speed and maintaining visually compelling images that persuade purchases. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0301.020]
  • Before uploading product images to the store, resize them to the correct dimensions and compress them so file size is small while quality remains good. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1463.012]
  • Customize product images so they look different from competitors selling the same items. This matters most for resellers of other brands and can mean shooting your own photos or editing manufacturer images. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1522.013]
  • A static ad creative with text can also be used as a website image, since the same layout works on a product page or homepage. Check that the text stays readable on a phone before publishing it on the site, because mobile visitors make up most traffic. [L0341.040]
  • Add a stamp or badge to the main product image that states the offer, for example product A plus product B with the words for free below. An offer shown on the image itself works better than a pop-up. [L0549.028]
  • Product images with circular guarantee badges, benefit lines and a USP overlay get read more than plain paragraphs, because the information is already visible in the image. Use this on all main product images. [L0871.027]
  • High-quality infographics add information that visitors actually read, especially when placed on the product image itself. Early on, an infographic can be bought cheaply on Fiverr for about $5 to $10. [L0871.026]
  • Place visuals of the product prominently near the top of the landing page, using clean, high-quality images that show the product clearly. Strong product imagery supports the hero and reinforces trust. [L0918.006]
  • Once the product image is set, add a product image that shows the bundle, as above, with a transparent background. [L1299.007]

GIFs and video

  • End product pages with an image or video of someone using the product, smiling, to create a positive emotional vibe. Avoid empty statements or plain benefit lists; always pair benefits with visual social proof. [L0321.008]
  • Add a short GIF to the product description that shows how the product works. Visual demonstration helps customers understand the product before buying. [L0562.030]
  • Use high-quality short GIFs that show the product in action and the key steps, placed near the top of the product page. Avoid overusing GIFs, and check that they look right on the mobile layout. [L1380.041]
  • If there is no UGC, cut a 1 to 3 second clip from an existing video ad and export it as a GIF for the product page. Keep the GIF under 800 KB. [L1260.069]
  • Show product demonstration early, such as a GIF or autoplay video, but do not place video in the hero image. Put product videos below the add-to-cart button and lazy load them so they only load as the customer scrolls to them. [L1225.037]
  • The first 10 seconds of a video should state clearly the benefit of watching, so viewers who are in the right place stay on the video even if it starts slowly. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1511.010]
  • Livecom's vendor claims that bubble videos (short FAQ-style product videos placed on the page) can lift on-page conversion rates as high as 40 percent. This is a vendor figure, not a verified benchmark, so test it on your own store before planning around it. [L0951.001]
  • Livecom's vendor states that bubble videos reach an engagement rate of 20 to 30 percent. Treat this as a vendor-stated figure and measure your own engagement rate on the same videos. [L0951.002]
  • Shoppable video is meant to raise conversion by showing products in use, highlighting benefits, promotions and discounts, and adding UGC and reviews. It is also meant to increase time on site and buying confidence compared with a plain video player. [L0959.012]

Trust, badges and social proof

  • The halo effect means shoppers tend to trust a brand more when it looks polished and credible overall, so consistent visual and copy quality adds trust on its own. This is a separate idea from comparing your product against competitors. Keep the whole page professional, not only the headline or the price. [L1210.025]
  • Templates are a starting point only. Using a page-builder template without changing its copy, layout or design makes a store look generic and weakens conversion. [L0577.008]
  • Unique branding and a professional logo, with product photos and site colors that match, make a dropshipping store look like a real brand and set it apart from most competitors. [L0577.009]
  • A strong home page can use a moving announcement bar, short benefit statements, clear product imagery and a comparison section against competitors. These sections were presented as elements that helped visitors trust the store and convert. [L0709.044]
  • Add genuine customer reviews to product pages. [L1325.027]

Accessibility

  • Accessibility matters for the 5 to 10% of visitors who have visual or other limitations. Use good color contrast, readable font sizes and avoid small light gray text on white backgrounds, and consider voice search apps only if your niche has many such users. [L0390.044]
  • An overlay is a transparent layer placed over an image so that text stands out. Without it, text over a busy or bright image can be hard to read, so add one when placing text on an image. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1310.018]

Homepage design

  • A homepage headline that a visitor cannot understand, such as a vague phrase about returning to a theme, should be rewritten to state clearly what the store sells or removed entirely. [L1325.002]
  • A homepage that stacks blog posts, bestsellers, recently viewed items and recommendations at once looks random to a first-time visitor. Keep the homepage simple and focused on a few clear sections. [L1325.005]
  • Make the best products easy to find on the homepage and navigation. Visitors who have to search for 20 minutes before finding a product often leave without buying. [L0390.050]
  • In a niche store, most visitors arrive directly on a product page, so the home page only needs to look credible. Keep the home page simple with one clear image and a collection list that sends visitors to the products quickly. [L1331.035]
  • Remove the contact form from the home page and keep the layout basic, because the main goal is for visitors to click into the catalog or product page quickly. [L1331.038]
  • Check the homepage and the top promotion bar as the store is reviewed in the order below. [L1325.027]
  • Test the homepage on mobile: check that the homepage USP, the offer and the reviews appear on phone screens as clearly as on desktop. Do not let a key message exist only in the desktop header. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0409.009]
  • Check that the USP highlight bars on the homepage still match what you offer. Update the homepage sections that feature products and collections so they show live products rather than demo items. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1145.030]
  • Adding quick links navigation showing 5-6 visual category shortcuts styled like Instagram story cards, positioned above the fold on the homepage, generates +2.8% conversion rate lift. [L0197.020]
  • Mobile shoppers cannot discover categories when collection links appear below the fold. Using familiar visual design patterns like Instagram stories that shoppers already interact with daily reduces cognitive friction and naturally encourages browsing. [L0197.022]
  • Best sellers, new and trending sections on a store do not meaningfully change conversion rate, because most visitors arrive from targeted ads on product pages rather than from those sections. [L0966.071]
  • On the home page, a bold, clearly visible offer with simple visual icons and plenty of white space is easier for visitors to notice and understand. Offer presentation should follow this clean, prominent layout. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0399.008]
  • Homepage structure, in this order (older advice, 2021-22):
    1. Headline with the primary promise, visible as soon as the page loads. [L0409.006]
    2. Navigation to the main product types, with the most important ones first. [L0409.006]
    3. A large, simple USP block about the business, surrounded by white space. [L0409.006]
    4. A free shipping and free returns offer with one short line of detail, such as who pays for return shipping. [L0409.006]
    5. The review count and star rating, with a visible link to read reviews. [L0409.006]
    6. Best sellers. [L0409.006]
    7. Alternate business messages and product sections down the page instead of stacking product sections together. [L0409.006]
    8. Lower sections with ingredient or safety information and social media links. [L0409.006]
  • Set up the home page collection list:
    1. Drag the Collection list section up on the home page. [L1331.036]
    2. Select a collection such as Slippers. [L1331.036]
    3. Remove collections that do not match the niche, such as Jewelry or Clothing. [L1331.036]
    4. Add the products you want to feature. [L1331.036]
    5. Save and check the layout on mobile. [L1331.036]
  • Keep the homepage clear of decorative noise and make the first view answer what the store sells. [L1325.002, L1331.035]

Hero, banners and sliders

  • Place text on a hero image to tell the customer what the business does for them. Only add a promotional message when there is a real offer, and keep the wording clear enough to be changed later. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1310.020]
  • Use a hero image instead of a slideshow on the homepage, because slideshows rely on JavaScript and extra code that slows the site down. Turn off autoplay and keep one static image. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1371.041]
  • Reported research suggests only about 4% of visitors see or interact with the second and third slides of a slideshow. This is the reason for keeping only the first slide as a single hero image. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1371.042]
  • A slideshow as the main home page visual distracts from the product and is not necessary for a single-product or niche store. Use one clear image instead and remove the second slide. [L1331.037]
  • When a homepage slider has several slides, each slide should link to a different collection. Do not link several slides to the same collection. [L0667.032]
  • The Highlights banner section has four blocks as a starting point: Free shipping, Give us a call, Easy returns and Price match. Each block has a heading and text, and the section can hold up to four blocks by default. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1310.022]
  • Each Highlights banner block can use a preset icon or a custom one. Custom icons should be 50 x 50 px and in a similar style to the presets. Custom icons are optional. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1310.026]
  • Each Highlights banner block can link to a page on the store, such as shipping, contact us, returns or price match. Linking reinforces the benefit and gives the customer an easy path to details. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1310.027]
  • Use testimonial carousels and feature carousels that side-scroll, because they take less vertical space on mobile. Show a peek of the next slide so visitors see that the carousel scrolls. In PageFly the slideshow is set in the slideshow section of the element settings, under the General tab, where slides can be deleted or duplicated. [L1363.016]
  • A feature block can show an image with a title and explanation, or, when no images exist, an icon, title and explanation. [L1363.017]

Homepage sections and featured products

  • Repeat the same key call to action in several places on the homepage, such as the header, a mid-page section and the footer. Different customers notice different parts of the page, so repetition increases the chance they act. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1310.030]
  • A search bar placed lower on the homepage helps shoppers who scroll past the header to find products directly. Faster product discovery usually leads to more purchases than browsing through several collections. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1310.031]
  • Featured product section, as a pattern (older advice, 2021-22): a heading and a subheading, a collection chosen to show a small product grid, a button labelled Shop collection, and an optional light background color to separate it from the white sections above. Example: Featured Camping Gear with the subheading Our most-loved picks for the trail, a collection of four products, and a Shop collection button. [L1310.033]
  • Alternate business messages and product sections down the page instead of stacking product sections together. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0409.006]

Navigation, header and menus

  • UX (user experience) measures how easily visitors navigate your store. Improving navigation reduces friction, builds trust, and increases conversions. [L0146.001]
  • Mega menus display multiple columns with images and featured products, replacing simple dropdowns for main categories. They help customers find products faster and improve product discovery. [L0146.003]
  • Reorder the header menu from best-selling to worst-selling category. [L1325.027]
  • Turn the first menu item into a Shop dropdown with clear category names. [L1325.027]
  • The store logo should link to the full collection of all products, while a Shop Now button should go straight to one product. This keeps the two navigation paths clearly separate for visitors. [L1013.002]
  • Avoid adding extra header links such as Home or Shop Now above a product page, because they draw the visitor away from the purchase. Keep the visitor on one page so that more of the leads that arrive turn into sales. [L1013.012]
  • A navigation bar on the product page distracts visitors who should stay on one landing page. Remove it so the page stays focused. [L1194.028]
  • A sticky header keeps the navigation, search bar, and cart available as the customer scrolls by shrinking into a thin bar with a hamburger menu. This means customers do not need to scroll back to the top to navigate. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1371.035]
  • Quick links and category shortcuts on the homepage should follow the same navigation logic as the menu. [L0197.020, L1325.027]
  • Reduce the logo size and place it on the left side of the navigation. [L1325.027]
  • Check the top promotion bar and add free shipping plus a percentage discount with a code if it fits. [L1325.027]

Collection pages and filters

  • Collection filters improve user experience by enabling faster product discovery and create a more professional appearance, signaling to suppliers that the store is designed for growth and customer navigation. [L0103.010]
  • Collection filters let customers quickly narrow results when browsing large catalogs, reducing frustration and increasing purchase likelihood. [L0146.015]
  • Filters help visitors reach the product they want with fewer clicks, which is the purpose of building them into the collection page. Filters are most useful when the store has many products, so a store with few products only needs a short list of filters. [L0369.016]
  • Filters such as product type and color help shoppers find products faster within a collection, which improves user experience. Adding them is most useful once the store has several suppliers and enough products to filter through. [L0890.006]
  • Filters that match real product attributes, such as product type and color, reduce the search effort for shoppers and improve the storefront experience. Tags only have value when they are kept consistent across all products, so each attribute should use one agreed spelling. [L0890.014]
  • Collection filters work only on product tags: a product appears under a filter option only if it has the matching tag. Add the tags to each product page, then add the same tags to the tag lists in the collection sidebar. [L1010.015]
  • Filter options should be built from the attributes customers use to sort products, and tags should be added to products as each new supplier brand and product is uploaded. Expanding the tag lists over time keeps filtering useful as the catalog grows. [L1010.016]
  • Example: on a store with 500 products, a shopper who wants a blue longboard can narrow the grid by the Longboards type tag and the Blue color tag, so only matching products remain. Without tags on each product, this combination shows no results. [L1010.017]
  • Pagination that splits products across several category pages, such as 12 products per page, hurts usability, conversion and SEO. Customers miss the second page and bounce, and Google may fail to crawl pages behind JavaScript pagination, causing indexation problems and lost link value. Disable pagination and show all products on one category page; expect roughly 3-5% more organic traffic plus a small conversion lift, though this can take about two weeks to show. [L0575.021]

Product page layout

  • Keep the product page to one purchase path: remove navigation and extra header links so the visitor's attention stays on the product. [L1194.028, L1013.012]
  • Customers often skip long product descriptions and look at images first, so place the visuals above the text they support. [L0773.022, L0774.024]
  • Reviews should be visible and should answer typical buyer concerns, such as fit or size, in the review images. [L0774.024]

Reference stores and review procedures

  • When building or fixing a store, find an established store that sells in a related price range and product type, but not the same niche. Model its page structure and navigation, not its colors or exact design. [L1325.006]
  • Include the store's default currency check in the review: check that the default currency matches the main traffic source. [L1325.027]
  • Include the store's pricing display in the review: raise low prices, and show regular prices crossed out next to sale prices. [L1325.027]
  • Expand the footer with customer care links, an about section and an email opt-in. [L1325.027]
  • Add a brand story or about page. [L1325.027]
  • Create collections for sub-styles and group matching products in each. [L1325.027]
  • Review order for a store homepage:
    1. Check that the default currency matches the main traffic source. [L1325.027]
    2. Read the homepage headline and rewrite or remove it if a visitor cannot understand it. [L1325.027]
    3. Check the top promotion bar and add free shipping plus a percentage discount with a code if it fits. [L1325.027]

  • Review each key landing page in both views before deciding it is fine:
    1. Load the page on desktop and confirm the headline and the main product image or intro text are visible without scrolling. [L1099.023]
    2. Switch to the mobile view and confirm the image does not push the title or first paragraph far down the screen. [L1099.023]
    3. Check that no pop-up, interstitial or chat bubble covers a significant part of the screen within the first 30 seconds. [L1099.023]
    4. Check that long text is split into chunks with subheadings, short paragraphs of two to three lines, and a readable font size of 18. [L1099.023]
    5. Note any issue, fix it, and recheck both views. [L1099.023]
