18.16 Store policies and legal pages

Store Policies and Legal Pages

Which pages are required, by source

The required set differs by source and by platform. Each source's list is kept below, so you can see what it asks for.

  • Practical rule: build the five pages from the 5-page set (privacy, terms, contact, shipping, refund/return), which covers every list above, and add payment terms, which the cross-border list requires [L0377.001, L0377.003, L1167.031, L1233.001]. Add About Us if you will approach suppliers [L0509.002].
  • Launch with all required pages live: a missing privacy policy can lead to Google Merchant Center account suspension, and repeated violations can lead to a ban [L0014.006].
  • Merchant Center flags an account automatically when required pages are missing, and you must then request a manual human review, which slows the process [L0377.002].
  • Create the pages before opening any ad or merchant account; this helps avoid suspensions and closures [L0377.001].
  • Google Merchant Center requires the merchant to confirm they have read the policies and that the account complies with them [L0377.002].
  • Completing these pages does not make the store fully compliant; other compliance topics are handled in each platform's own setup [L0377.024].
  • Policies can be refined after supplier relationships are finalised. The launch goal is supplier-ready functionality, not perfection [L0090.012].
  • Policies must be published on the site and must match what is entered in Google Merchant Center [L0966.091].
  • Policies must be easy to find. Some traffic platforms require easy access before approving ads and may refuse to run ads otherwise [L1303.022].
  • Privacy policy and terms of service must match what Google Ads requires [L0965.090].

Work order in Shopify

Compliance setup order (complete sequence)

  1. Generate the privacy policy with Shopify's privacy policy generator. Answer the company questions, then request the document by email [L0377.004].
  2. Add the required extra privacy text to the generated policy, if it is missing [L0377.004]:
    • a remarketing sentence at the end of the introduction or closing;
    • a Google Analytics sentence as the last line of the third-party services section;
    • a Google "pref" cookie line as the last line of the cookies section.
  3. Generate the terms and conditions with Shopify's terms generator. Skip the trial, enter company and website information, and receive the document by email [L0377.004].
  4. Review the generated terms, modify anything needed, then publish them on the store [L0377.004].
  5. Create or confirm a contact page with at least two contact methods, for example phone number and business email, optionally Shopify's contact form [L0377.004].
  6. Create a shipping policy stating when orders ship, average transit time, shipping cost, and any free or upgraded shipping options [L0377.004].
  7. Create a refund and return policy stating the refund rules, how to request a refund, how returns work, and how a return is started [L0377.004].
  8. Link all five pages in the footer menu [L0377.004].
  9. Add two contact methods to the footer [L0377.004].
  10. Confirm accepted payment methods are displayed in the footer [L0377.004].

Publish order (Source D method)

  1. Go to Settings > Policies (the legal settings page) [L1167.030].
  2. For each policy (return and refund, privacy, terms of service, shipping, contact information), either turn on the automated policy option or click Insert template [L1167.030].
  3. Replace every placeholder with your real business details: trading name, business address, business phone number and contact information [L1167.030].
  4. Click Save, then Publish for each policy [L1167.030].

Generate with Shopify's free tools

  • Shopify has free privacy policy and terms generators. They ask for company information and send the generated document by email, so you can paste it onto the policy pages [L0377.022].
  • Shopify Policy Generator (shopify.com/tools/policy-generator): enter company name, email address, business address and website link. Shopify emails a ready-made privacy policy or terms text that can be pasted without changes. Other generators on the same page create refund and terms texts [L1233.017].
  • Privacy generator, skip-trial step: at the bottom of the page, click the link labelled "I'll skip the Shopify trial now, just create my policy". Enter the company information and click "send me my privacy policy" to receive the generated document by email [L0377.025].
  • Generated text changes over time. Regenerate privacy and terms pages from the current generator and replace older text when the generator updates [L0965.090].
  • Check the generated privacy policy regularly against the current store setup [L0855.130].
  • Older (2021): a privacy policy from Shopify's generator, used with only a few modifications, was roughly 95% standard Shopify text [L0247.109].

Privacy policy

Creating the page

  • Shopify admin steps (template route):
    1. Click Settings in the bottom left corner of the Shopify Admin.
    2. Scroll to the bottom of the Settings page and click Policies.
    3. In the Privacy policy section, click Create from template.
    4. Read the highlighted instructions at the top of the generated text.
    5. Delete the highlighted instructions after reading them.
    6. Enter today's date as the last updated date.
    7. Replace the generic example domain with your store's actual URL.
    8. Edit any other template parts that refer to your business [L0509.010].
  • Automated route: go to Settings > Policies, click Privacy policy, turn on the Use automated policy toggle at the top, then click Save [L1048.022]. The same toggle is described as Turn on the automated policy option [L0090.007].

Editing the content

  • Delete the "notes to merchant" section for now. It applies only once you run ads. Update the privacy policy with ad-specific details before ads go live [L0509.011].
  • In the contact section, delete any phone number or physical location you do not yet have. Keep an email address there: the default is the email used at Shopify sign-up, and it can be changed in the same place at any time [L0509.012].
  • Add the extra privacy text if missing: the remarketing sentence, the Google Analytics sentence and the Google "pref" cookie line described in the compliance order [L0377.004].
  • The privacy policy should cover data privacy and security as part of the full legal section [L1250.002].

Business name in the policy

  • The trade name (DBA) is used as the store name in terms and privacy policies. The speaker gives no legal advice on whether the LLC name must be included. Older (2023) [L0964.114, L0964.119].
  • Conflicting values, both kept:
    • Practice: use the DBA as the store name and do not add the LLC name. Applies where the speaker's practice is followed [L0964.119].
    • Caution: the privacy policy may need to name both the store name and the legal LLC name, since they often differ. Unverified. A lawyer should confirm the exact wording required in your jurisdiction. Older (2021) [L0853.121].

Missing pieces

  • The ready-made policy set (terms, shipping, returns, customer support and data protection) contains no privacy policy text. Shopify's written policies list still expects a Privacy policy page, so write and publish it separately [L1250.040].

Terms of service

Creating the page

  • Shopify admin steps (Insert template route):
    1. Go to Settings > Policies.
    2. Click Insert template. Shopify generates the full policy.
    3. Replace any bracketed placeholders with your business name and support email.
    4. Optionally leave address and phone number blank.
    5. Click Save [L0090.008].
  • Full template procedure:
    1. Go to Settings > Policies.
    2. In the Terms of service section, click Create from template.
    3. Replace the sentence that names the operator of the website with your store name.
    4. Read the whole text and replace every generic item with your own information.
    5. Remove references to policies you have not created yet, such as the refund policy.
    6. Replace the link to the privacy policy with a real link to the Privacy Policy you just created.
    7. Fill in the contact information section with your details, or remove lines that do not apply.
    8. Click Save [L0509.013].
  • Shorter variant:
    1. Go to Settings > Policies and click Terms of service.
    2. Click Insert template at the top to have Shopify generate the template.
    3. Replace the link to privacy policy placeholder with a link to the Privacy Policy page you created.
    4. Replace the trading name placeholder with the store name.
    5. Replace the contact email placeholder with the support email address.
    6. Leave the business address and phone number fields empty if only an email address is provided.
    7. Click Save [L1048.023].

Both variants above describe the same Shopify route. Their wording differs, and both are kept.

What the terms must cover

  • Terms set the rules for using the website and buying products. They should cover payment, shipping, returns and warranties [L1233.003].
  • The terms should state that the store may update them at any time and that changes are posted on the page. They should also cover correct use of the site, prohibited activities and age restrictions [L1250.027].
  • Standard template text (Source D). Use it as a starting point and edit it to fit your business:
Terms and Conditions

By visiting our website and buying products from us, you agree to follow these terms and conditions. This includes correct use of the site, activities that are not allowed, and age restrictions. We may change these terms at any time, and any changes will be posted on this page.

[L1250.034]

Publishing and reading

  • The generated terms are the store's real legal terms, not placeholder text. Read them, adjust anything that does not fit the business, and only then publish [L0377.007].
  • In one store the generated terms were published unchanged. The owner must still read them before going live [L0377.008].
  • Templates are a starting point. Edit each one to match how your business handles orders, refunds and customer data before publishing [L1124.020].
  • Remove any reference to a policy you have not built yet, and link the privacy policy to the real page [L0509.013, L1048.023].

Shipping policy

Creating the policy (Shopify route)

  1. In Shopify Admin, go to Settings and select Policies. Open Shipping Policy [L0090.005].
  2. Get the Shipping Policy AI Prompt that starts with "You are a professional e-commerce operations copywriter" and copy it completely. The full text of this prompt is not included in these source units, so take it verbatim from the course prompt file and do not rewrite it [L0090.005].
  3. Fill in the Student Inputs: store name, support email address (use support@yourdomain.com format), shipping region, handling time, transit time, delivery timeframe, and shipping method details [L0090.005].
  4. Use the standard timeframes below unless different circumstances apply [L0090.005].
  5. Create the policy using the prompt. Insert the generated policy into Shopify and save [L0090.005].

Total delivery 7-12 business days L0090.005

  • Which figure applies: the standard table applies for the usual case, and the dropshipping table applies to supplier-fulfilled stores, as the source describes them. Both values are given here so you can choose the one that matches your supply chain [L0090.005, L1233.014].
  • Example values from a 2026 course: orders leave in 2-3 working days and reach customers in 6-10 days [L1250.043].

What the shipping policy must contain

  • Shipping methods, estimated delivery times, shipping costs and international shipping details, and which customs duties or taxes the customer must pay [L1233.005].
  • Processing time, delivery times per country, shipping fees charged, and the domestic shipping time for the store's home country [L1233.015].
  • When orders ship, average transit time, shipping cost, and any free or upgraded shipping options [L0377.004].
  • Confirmation and tracking email, how shipping rates are calculated at checkout, which countries are served and how international shipping times can differ, and that customs or weather can delay delivery [L1250.004, L1250.043].
  • If the offer includes free shipping, state it clearly, and make the shipping cost terms transparent [L0167.011].
  • Google Shopping requires the customer to know the exact shipping cost before checkout begins. A free-shipping header must be matched by an explicit free-shipping statement in the shipping policy. Vague wording about shipping costs is a common reason for Merchant Center flags [L0724.027].
  • If products are built for one market, say so on the shipping page, for example that they need a 110V electrical system and are certified only to US standards. This protects the store from electrical-compatibility complaints from buyers elsewhere. It is a condition-specific notice, not a general legal disclaimer. Older (2021-22) [L1264.020].
  • Shipping and refund policies must be shown in checkout with the company name and at least an email address [L1260.051].
  • Text for the shipping policy prompt, with placeholders, from the 2026 course:
Write a shipping policy for my Shopify store called [Your Store Name]. Orders are shipped within [X-X] working days and usually arrive within [X-X] working days. Cover the processing time, the confirmation and tracking email, how shipping rates are calculated at checkout, which countries are served and how international shipping times can differ, and that delivery can be delayed by customs or weather. Use [placeholders] for any values I have not given you.

[L1250.004]

  • Second shipping prompt, for a store with known figures, from the 2026 course:
Draft a shipping policy for my Shopify store, [Your Store Name]. Orders leave our hands in 2-3 working days and reach customers in 6-10 days. The text must cover processing time, how checkout calculates shipping rates, international shipping, and the tracking email sent when an order ships. State that customs or weather can delay delivery. Put [placeholders] wherever I haven't supplied a figure.

[L1250.043]

Refund, return and exchange policy

Creating the policy (Shopify route)

  1. In Shopify Admin, go to Settings > Policies [L0090.006].
  2. Click Return and Refund Policy [L0090.006].
  3. Use the Return & Refund Policy AI Prompt. Its full text is not included in these source units, so take it verbatim from the course prompt file [L0090.006].
  4. Fill in the Student Inputs: store name, support email, return window (recommended: 30 days), condition requirements, restocking fee (recommended: none), return shipping responsibility, cancellation policy, damage reporting timeframe [L0090.006].
  5. Leave most other defaults as-is for now [L0090.006].
  6. Generate the policy [L0090.006].
  7. Paste into Shopify [L0090.006].
  8. Click Save [L0090.006].

Return window: values by context

Context Window Applies when Ids
Standard recommendation 30 days, no restocking fee Default for a new store L0090.006
Dropshipping, defect cases 14 or 30 days money-back guarantee Defective, damaged or not-as-described items. Chinese suppliers usually do not accept returns L1233.011, L1233.012
30-day guarantee in sales 30-day money-back guarantee Removes purchase risk; shown near the purchase decision L1209.025
EU shipping 14-day right to cancel or return for any reason Applies whenever the store ships into the EU, even if the normal window is shorter L1327.052
  • Make the 30-day guarantee visible near the purchase decision [L1209.025].
  • If the store ships into the EU, keep the 14-day right to cancel or return for any reason in the returns policy, even when the normal return window is shorter [L1327.052].
  • Chinese suppliers usually do not accept returns but do refund or replace items that arrive damaged, defective or not as described. The return policy should therefore offer a 14-day or 30-day money-back guarantee for these cases only [L1233.011].
  • Dropshipping clause to adapt. Replace the bracketed placeholder with your support email and choose 14 or 30 days:
We provide a 14-day (or 30-day) money-back guarantee if you receive a defective, damaged product or if the product is not as described. Please contact us at [support email] and we will do our best to help. Please send us a photo or video as proof so our team can investigate. If our team approves your request, we will issue a full refund or a replacement, as agreed.

[L1233.012]

Return address and return conditions

  • For a new store without a return address, the owner's own address or a virtual address can be used as the return address. Customers pay the return shipping label [L1233.013].
  • A returned item is accepted only if it is in good condition with intact, unopened packaging. Add these conditions to the return policy [L1233.013].
  • If you have no return address, remove or change that line in the generated policy [L1233.010].

Content of the refund policy

  • Rules for refunds, exchanges and returns, the conditions for returns, time limits and any restocking fees [L1233.004].
  • Eligibility, return window in days from purchase, proof of purchase, non-returnable item types, how refunds are processed and how long they take, when partial refunds apply, when exchanges are allowed, who pays return shipping and whether it is refundable, sale-item rules, and how customers contact you [L1250.003].
  • Refund rules, how to request a refund, how returns work, and how a return is started [L0377.004].
  • Return prompt from the 2026 course:
Write a return and refund policy for my Shopify store called [Your Store Name]. Cover eligibility, the return window in days from the date of purchase, the proof of purchase requirement, non-returnable item types, how refunds are processed and how long they take, when partial refunds apply, when exchanges are allowed, who pays return shipping and whether it is refundable, the rules for sale items, and how customers can contact us. Use [placeholders] for any values I have not given you, and keep the wording plain and customer-friendly.

[L1250.003]

Specific product risks

  • Fashion products have high return rates, so return and shipping policies must be clear before launch. Suppliers should also agree to refund defective or wrong-size items [L0335.039].
  • Do not copy another store's policy. Write the refund, return and shipping text from your actual return window, conditions and exceptions, and check it against consumer rules in each country you sell to [L1188.035].
  • Before writing your own return and shipping policies, review established dropshipping stores' policies as a reference. Adapt the structure to your own supplier terms rather than copying their wording [L1233.024].
  • The policy should protect the business while being fair to customers [L1124.020].

Dropshipping return procedure

  • Open Shopify Admin > Settings > Policies [L1233.010].
  • Click the policy you need (for example Return and refund policy) to generate the default template [L1233.010].
  • Replace the store's contact email with your support email [L1233.010].
  • Insert a return address if you have one; otherwise remove or change that line [L1233.010].
  • Adjust any other line to your needs [L1233.010].
  • Copy the finished text into a page, or save it in the policies section [L1233.010].

Contact page and contact details

Contact page

  • Shopify provides a default contact page for every store. Add its link to the footer [L1233.007].
  • The default page named Contact is already present under Online Store > Pages > Add page. If the store is password protected, remove the password before viewing it [L1233.018].
  • Create or confirm a contact page with at least two contact methods, for example a phone number and a business email, optionally Shopify's contact form [L0377.004].
  • Use a business email address, not a personal email, on the contact page and in the footer [L0377.005].
  • Use a support mailbox on the store's own domain, for example support@yourstorename.com. Use the same address in the return policy, shipping policy and contact page, and make sure someone reads it [L1233.022].

Filling the contact information section

  1. Open the policy in Shopify Settings > Policies and scroll to the contact information section [L0724.009].
  2. Click the template option, which prefills the store information [L0724.009].
  3. Confirm the email address, which can be a support or sales address, uses the real domain [L0724.009].
  4. Add a phone number labelled as a call option [L0724.009].
  5. Add hours of operation, for example 9am through 5pm EST [L0724.009].
  6. Confirm there is no placeholder text, then click publish [L0724.009].
  • A physical address is not needed in this section [L0724.009].
  • A valid hours entry for a US-based store can read 9am through 5pm EST, with the time zone stated. Only the contact section of the policies needs this. Customers rarely see it, but Google's crawler reads it [L0724.010].

Real, working details

  • Every email address and phone number in policies and contact pages must be real and current [L0724.007].
  • In every policy page, use the correct real email address and real phone number, remove any "555" placeholder phone number, and delete all bracketed or template filler text [L0167.009].

Footer, menus and visibility

Footer content

  • Create and link these pages in the footer menu before launching traffic: privacy policy (use a Privacy Policy Generator), terms and conditions (use a Terms and Conditions Generator), contact us, shipping policy, refunds and exchanges [L0162.001].
  • Add two contact methods to the footer [L0162.001, L0377.004].
  • Display accepted payment methods in the footer [L0162.001, L0377.004].
  • Link the refund, privacy, terms of service and shipping policy pages in the footer before submitting to Google [L0724.005].
  • Refund, shipping, privacy and terms pages should appear in the footer Quick Links menu, not hidden. Make them easy to find before running paid ads, since these pages are required storefront elements [L1319.013].
  • Put a footer link to the policy pages so customers can find them easily [L1326.015].
  • The footer phone number and address should match the Contact Us page details [L1145.012, older 2021-22].

Creating the footer menu (Shopify)

  • Create the menu under Online Store > Navigation > Add menu. Name it, for example, "Our policies". Add a menu item for each policy page (Privacy policy, Terms of service, Shipping policy, Return and refund policy, optionally Contact). Then Save menu [L1233.019].
  • In the theme footer:
    1. Go to Online Store > Themes > Customize.
    2. Open the footer section in the theme editor.
    3. Click Add block, choose Menu and select the policy menu.
    4. Rename the heading from "Quick links" to "Our policies".
    5. Check that all policy links appear in the footer and save [L1233.020].
  • Main menu dropdown (Source B, 2021-22, older):
    1. In the main menu, click Add menu item and name it Our Company.
    2. Link it to the About Us page.
    3. Drag the item under Our Company so it becomes a dropdown.
    4. Add the child items Contact Us, Shipping, Easy Returns, Price Match, Privacy and Terms of Service, linking each to its matching page.
    5. Click Save menu [L1303.021].

Easy to find

  • Make the refund and return policy clear and easy to find; the website footer is a recommended placement location [L0014.002].
  • Terms and privacy pages must be easy to find for customers, because some traffic platforms refuse to run ads without easy access [L1303.022].
  • Policies saved under Settings are also linked in the checkout footer. Pages and footer links are added afterwards so visitors can find them [L1233.021].

Checkout legal settings

How the checkout gets its policies

  • The Shopify checkout is hosted separately from the main site. Footer policy links do not appear there, so legal policies must be entered separately, even though the checkout appears to be on your domain. Older (2021-22) [L0677.032].
  • Policies entered in Shopify's policy settings are the texts linked at checkout, so fill them in there first to meet the checkout requirement [L1233.021].
  • Policies in the legal settings are linked in the checkout footer. They do not appear automatically in the storefront footer, so add them to your online store menus [L0677.051].
  • Legal pages must be visible in the checkout, because customers enter personal and card details there [L1233.008].
  • Publish each policy after saving. Each policy shows "No policy set" until it is added [L1167.031, L1167.030].

Placeholders, sample values and accuracy

Placeholders to remove

  • Do not publish a template with unfilled placeholders such as "(INSERT TRADING NAME)", "[INSERT BUSINESS ADDRESS]" or "[INSERT BUSINESS PHONE NUMBER]". Replace them with your real business details before publishing [L1167.032].
  • Templates contain bracketed placeholders, and each one must be replaced with real store information before publishing. Leaving a placeholder such as the trading name or the privacy policy link in the terms makes the page incomplete [L1048.024].
  • Shopify's generated policy texts contain bracketed placeholders and instruction notes. Replace every bracket with your store name, contact email and address, and delete the notes before publishing [L1233.023].
  • Sample values left in a prompt's input fields, such as a template's example brand name, carry into the generated policy or About Us text. Replace or delete every sample value before pasting the output into Shopify [L1048.027].
  • Placeholder text, bracketed template fields and fake phone numbers such as 555-555-5555 are flagged by Google as signs of an unprofessional or incomplete store [L0724.007].
  • Shopify's policy generators insert placeholder text, so each generated policy must be checked and edited [L0724.005, L0167.007].
  • Confirm there is no placeholder text before you publish [L0724.009].
  • Suppliers may click through your policies during review. A policy with placeholder links, missing sections or references to pages that do not exist makes the store look incomplete. Remove or fix every such item before submitting the store for approval [L0509.014].
  • Templates contain placeholders that must be replaced before submission. Review every generated policy under Shopify Admin > Settings > Policies [L0167.008].

Revising with real figures

  • After a generator (such as ChatGPT) writes a policy, reply in the same chat with your real figures and ask for a revised version. Include the exact processing days, delivery days, return window and contact email in each revision so the sample numbers are replaced [L1250.044].
  • In every policy page, the placeholders for the store name, the privacy policy link and the contact email must be replaced in turn [L1048.023].

Accuracy and reality

  • Policies must describe what the store actually does, not just exist as placeholders. In most US states, the core legal pages (privacy policy, terms of service, return and refund policy) are binding legal documents [L0421.002].
  • Every page has to describe what the shop really does in practice [L1250.002].
  • Promises the store cannot keep lead to customer complaints. Replace rough delivery estimates with actual figures before publishing [L1233.025].
  • Conditional statements, such as financing availability, exclusions, sales tax by state or a shipping restriction to certain states, must link to a page that explains the details. Without that, the visitor becomes confused and worried. Older (2021-22) [L0409.032].
  • Statements such as "Some exclusions apply" or "No shipping to" a state, with no explanation or link, make a store look unreliable. State the restriction plainly and explain it, even when there is a good reason for it. Older (2021-22) [L0409.033].
  • The price match guarantee can be stated clearly as applying to the listed price before coupon codes. This helps customers understand it before ordering. Older (2023) [L1283.057].

Google review requirements

  • Contact information read by Google's crawlers should list at least two contact methods, recommended as email plus phone, along with business hours. An address is not required for this business model, but the email and phone must be real and working [L0167.010].
  • Adding an at-a-glance summary, such as a table or a short summary section, to policy pages helps Google's crawlers parse the key details such as return and shipping terms [L0167.012].
  • Google Merchant Center flags accounts automatically when required pages are missing, and a manual review must be requested [L0377.002].
  • Policy pages must match the policies entered in Google Merchant Center [L0966.091].
  • Google Ads requires privacy policy and terms of service pages that match its requirements. Check them whenever the generator updates [L0965.090].
  • Missing a privacy policy can result in Google Merchant Center account suspension. Repeated violations can lead to account banning [L0014.006].

Country-specific content

  • For the four main markets (US, Canada, Australia and the UK), add the required privacy policy and terms text to the legal pages. Other countries, especially in Europe, have separate rules that must be added too [L1213.067].
  • If the store ships into the European Union, keep the 14-day right to cancel or return for any reason in the returns policy [L1327.052].
  • Check consumer rules in each country you sell to when writing refund, return and shipping text [L1188.035].
  • Customs duties and taxes that the customer must pay belong in the shipping policy [L1233.005].
  • In the US, the core legal pages are binding in most states. State-specific wording, such as for California, must be checked with a legal source rather than assumed. Older (2022) [L0421.002, L1092.022].
  • A product built for one market needs an explicit notice on the shipping page, for example for 110V electrical systems and US-only certification. Older (2021-22) [L1264.020].

Legal notice and disclaimers

  • Legal pages are not legal advice. Have a lawyer review the privacy policy, terms of service and refund policy, especially for stores with custom situations [L0421.003].
  • Shopify's legal page templates are not legal advice and must be customised for your store [L0677.049].
  • Shopify policy templates are not legal advice. You are responsible for their accuracy and for compliance with the laws that apply to your business [L1167.033].
  • Create the privacy policy and terms of service in Shopify admin under Settings > Policies, not as ordinary pages. This makes Shopify treat them as legal pages, and the links in Google Merchant Center should point to those pages. Older (2023) [L0470.052].
  • Legal ownership details: use the trade name in the policy text, and check the LLC question with a lawyer [L0964.114, L0853.121].

Payment and billing terms

  • The terms or a separate payment page should describe how payments are processed, which payment methods are accepted, and any recurring billing if subscriptions are offered [L1233.006].
  • Payment and billing belong in the terms of service, which should cover payment [L1233.003].
  • Display accepted payment methods in the footer [L0162.001, L0377.004].
  • Customers enter personal and card details at checkout, so the legal pages must be visible there [L1233.008].
  • Legal pages help resolve disputes with customers and payment providers [L1233.001].

  • Each legal page should include the company name. [L1345.072]
  • Each legal page should include at least an email address so customers can contact you. [L1345.072]
  • The Terms of Service and Privacy Policy templates use {Store name} and {Business email} for these details. [L1327.058, L1327.059]

Checkout and pre-traffic readiness

  • Refund policy and shipping policy appear in the checkout. [L1345.072]
  • Both must be in place before traffic starts. [L1345.072]

Visibility of shipping policy and guarantees

  • Publish the shipping policy and any guarantee, such as a 30-day guarantee, on the store where buyers can see it. [L1346.024]
  • Examples of placement: tabs on the product page. [L1346.024]
  • Visible policies signal that the business is legitimate. [L1346.024]

Footer links and Google ads compliance

  • Link the policy pages in the store footer. [L1446.009, L1331.033]
  • Do this before running any ads, for Google compliance. [L1446.009]
  • Do not start ads until the policy pages are linked in the footer. [L1446.009]

Country-specific content: legal notice (Impressum)

  • In Germany, an online store must show a legally required legal notice (Impressum) with the business information on the website. [L1428.029]
  • Missing information can lead to warning letters from lawyers and complaints from customers. [L1428.029]
  • The Impressum must be complete before the store goes live. [L1428.029]
  • Check the legal requirements of the country you sell from and the countries you sell to. [L1428.029]

Further details

  • TermsFeed is an all-in-one wizard tool recommended by Google for generating privacy policy agreements without legal expertise. [L0014.004]

  • Google Adwords provides sample privacy policy templates to use as a starting point. [L0014.005]

  • A store should have a privacy policy and terms of service live on the site before launch. These pages are part of the basic legal setup, separate from marketing copy. [L1264.010]
