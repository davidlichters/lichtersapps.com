18.21 Liability, insurance and risk protection

Insurance types for e-commerce

  • The main e-commerce insurance types are Product Liability Insurance and DNO (Direct Officer) Insurance [L0041.015].
  • Recall insurance exists but is very difficult to obtain [L0041.015].
  • Product liability insurance is optional. If you want it, compare quotes from several insurers [L0671.123].

Where lawsuits come from

  • As a successful business you will be sued, possibly unfairly, through an accident, competitor action or totally unfounded claims. Expect it and prepare by keeping legal reserves [L0041.045].
  • Most litigation risk in e-commerce comes from the business itself: terms of service, compliance, intellectual property and subscriptions. It does not mainly come from uncontrollable external events. Identify and fix the controllable risks [L0041.049].
  • Lawsuits against a small e-commerce business are rare, but the financial consequences can be severe. This is a low-probability, high-impact risk, and choosing the right business structure is a protective measure against it [L1405.040] (older advice, 2021-22).
  • Customers in the US can sue almost anyone, so general liability cover is a matter of your risk tolerance [L0961.021] (older advice, 2020).
  • If a product could cause injury, a lawsuit may name every party a lawyer can find, including the seller and the supplier [L0961.022] (older advice, 2020).
  • Plaintiffs often sue every party in the chain, including the store [L0247.105] (older advice, 2021).

Supplier protection

  • Suppliers do not protect you from product liability claims [L0247.105] (older advice, 2021).
  • If you white label through a manufacturer, it will often cover your product liability under its umbrella insurance policy [L1154.012] (older advice, 2021-22).
  • If the manufacturer does not cover you, you need your own product liability insurance, which is usually not very expensive [L1154.012] (older advice, 2021-22).

Product liability and product safety

  • Assess product liability by considering every way the product could injure someone, including misuse, and every person who might use it, not only the intended buyer [L1154.010] (older advice, 2021-22).
  • Address all required warnings on the product to protect your company [L1154.010] (older advice, 2021-22).
  • Products sold in plastic bag packaging need a suffocation hazard warning printed on the packaging [L1154.011] (older advice, 2021-22).
  • Confirm the exact legally required wording rather than relying on memory, and budget money to get all required warnings right [L1154.011] (older advice, 2021-22).
  • Avoid products that could be dangerous. If that risk concerns you, consider a general liability policy [L0961.022] (older advice, 2020).

Business structure and asset separation

  • Business liability insurance depends on the business entity, the expected liability and the owner's risk tolerance. Consult a professional before buying policies, because brokers are salespeople [L1111.135] (older advice, 2020).
  • Choosing a business entity is similar to choosing insurance: the choice is up to the owner [L0472.046].
  • Run the business through an LLC or S corporation and keep business expenses separate from personal ones [L0853.064] (older advice, 2021).
  • Consider general liability insurance once the business holds meaningful assets, and confirm the approach with a professional [L0853.064] (older advice, 2021).
  • An LLC or S-corp limits personal liability only when assets are kept separate. Consult an accountant once the business earns money [L0247.105] (older advice, 2021).

General liability insurance: is it required?

  • General liability insurance is optional for a US e-commerce business. Some suppliers require it before they will work with you [L0472.046].
  • It is optional and depends on supplier requirements [L0468.152] (older advice, 2020).
  • Most suppliers do not require business insurance [L0855.122].
  • If a gold supplier requires it, request general liability quotes from the insurer you already use for home or auto cover [L0855.122].
  • It is optional and depends on risk versus reward, meaning how much you have to lose [L0934.011].
  • It is a personal decision. The business can run without it, though it may be useful depending on the risk [L1112.060] (older advice, 2021).
  • It is optional and depends on where you operate [L1279.078] (older advice, 2021).
  • It is recommended for peace of mind [L0249.075] (older advice, 2022).
  • It is useful as the business grows [L0665.096] (older advice, 2022).
  • Business insurance is not required for dropshipping in the US. For the UK, the speaker did not know of a requirement, so a local answer should be checked [L0964.113].

  • Coverage size should be set according to revenue, which the insurer will ask about [L0468.153] (older advice, 2020).

Buying without brokers

Sources differ on which channel to use. They agree on avoiding small or unvetted brokers and on comparing several quotes [L0249.076, L1094.084, L1279.078, L0671.122, L0671.259, L0961.021].

  • Use an independent quote-comparison website and choose the lowest price for suitable coverage. A higher price does not mean better protection [L1088.081, L1088.095] (older advice, 2020).
  • Buy from a large direct insurer rather than through a broker, since commissions make broker policies expensive [L0468.152] (older advice, 2020).
  • Get general liability quotes through the same insurer that holds your personal insurance, such as auto or home. This usually gives better rates and less risk of being taken advantage of than online small business brokers [L1281.070] (older advice, 2022).
  • Ask the insurer you already use for home, auto or life policies for a business policy, and compare it with other quotes [L0934.011, L0934.142].
  • Start with a large insurer you already use for other insurance [L0804.098].
  • Shop through major agencies such as Geico for Business, or ask connections in insurance [L0249.075] (older advice, 2022).
  • Avoid small brokerages and use major agencies [L0249.076] (older advice, 2022).
  • Be careful with brokers who charge high premiums, and compare multiple quotes before buying [L0671.122, L0671.259] (older advice, 2021).
  • Brokers can overcharge heavily for weak coverage [L1279.078] (older advice, 2021).
  • Agents may overcharge for policies you do not need [L0961.021] (older advice, 2020).
  • Buy business insurance without overpaying [L1069.069] (older advice, 2019):
    1. Get a general, catch-all business policy first, since it covers the basics [L1069.069].
    2. Ask several insurance brokers for quotes, not only one [L1069.069].
    3. Be careful with agents who offer every policy they can sell, because many coverages may be unnecessary [L1069.069].
    4. Review the coverage again as revenue grows, since insurers may audit the business and premiums can rise [L1069.069].
  • Start with a small general liability policy. It is a cheaper starting point than a bundle of policies, and more coverage can be added later. Pick coverage based on actual risk [L1111.137] (older advice, 2020).

Premium audits and renewals

  • Insurance audits can happen retroactively based on revenue growth. They can result in bills above $10,000 for past premiums, even if no claims occurred [L0249.076] (older advice, 2022).
  • A revenue audit on a broker-sold policy can lead to tens of thousands in extra premiums [L1088.081] (older advice, 2020).
  • Do not buy general liability through a broker, because a revenue audit after the policy starts can produce large extra premiums [L1088.095] (older advice, 2020).
  • A carrier or broker audit after the first year can produce a large retroactive premium bill when sales grow. Check how audits and premiums are calculated before signing [L1111.136] (older advice, 2020).
  • Before buying, ask the provider how year-end premium audits adjust the price [L0804.098].
  • Check the premium once revenue grows. Low-price quotes can produce large bills at audit [L0665.096] (older advice, 2022).
  • A cheap first-year rate can increase sharply later [L1094.084].
  • Quotes can rise sharply at renewal, so compare quotes from several insurers [L0934.142, L0934.011].

Shipping insurance

  • Shipping insurance is important for expensive products, since it covers damage claims and lets you resolve them without a full return [L0970.145].
  • Carrier shipping insurance policies determine whether a damaged item in transit is refunded along with shipping costs. Check the carrier's policy before deciding who pays [L1094.088].

Licensing for food and special categories

  • Before selling food or beverage products, check the licenses and regulations that apply. They add steps beyond normal drop shipping [L0854.091] (older advice, 2022).
