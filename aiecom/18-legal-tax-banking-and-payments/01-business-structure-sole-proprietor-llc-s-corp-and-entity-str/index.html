18.1 Business structure: sole proprietor, LLC, s-Corp and entity strategy

Why a Legal Entity Matters

  • An LLC (Limited Liability Company) is a separate legal entity that protects personal assets from business liabilities and looks more professional to suppliers than a sole proprietorship. Form an LLC unless you genuinely have nothing to protect. [L0118.001]
  • A sole proprietorship has no legal separation from its owner, so personal assets such as bank accounts and property are exposed to business liabilities. A separate legal entity limits liability to the business assets only. [L0698.003]
  • Older advice (2021-22): A sole trader is personally liable for business debts. In a dispute, personal assets such as a house could be claimed to settle them. [L0702.003]
  • Older advice (2021-22): A proprietary limited company or LLC is separate from its owner. The owner is usually both director and shareholder. If the company cannot pay its debts, it can be wound up without the director generally being liable. [L0702.004]
  • Older advice (2021-22): Limited liability does not cover illegal conduct. A director can still be held liable if the company or the director acts illegally. [L0702.005]
  • Liability protection holds only if business and personal funds are not commingled. [L0429.009]
  • Commingling funds across unrelated LLCs makes separate businesses look like one entity. This weakens the LLC's liability protection and makes a later sale of one business much messier. [L1092.011]
  • Lawsuits against the business target the business and not the owner personally. [L1085.076, L1274.047]

Choosing a Structure

Structure Overview (US Terms)

Structure Separation from owner Setup and cost Suits Ids

Owners with a home, several vehicles, retirement savings or significant cash should avoid it. [L0703.001, L0703.002, L0698.005]
LLC Separate legal entity. A lawsuit reaches only assets the LLC owns, such as business bank funds and equipment. Inexpensive and easy to set up. Commonly about $100. The most common first structure for online sellers. [L0703.003, L0270.027, L1085.076]

C corp Separate legal entity. Used when bringing in shareholders, seeking investment or going public. Businesses seeking outside investment. Most small business owners use an LLC, the most popular structure. [L0969.034, L0272.042]

Sole Proprietorship

  • Trading under your own name is the fastest and cheapest way to start. It is a reasonable starting point but is usually replaced by a company structure once revenue grows. [L0698.005]
  • A sole proprietorship is not a separate legal entity, so all business profit is reported on the owner's personal tax return. Choose the structure with this in mind before you start selling. [L1344.001]
  • The owner's name is the business name, even if the website domain differs. [L0969.033]
  • A sole proprietorship has a higher tax burden. The owner cannot use as many write-offs and cannot split pay between salary and distributions. [L0698.004]
  • Some states require registration even for sole proprietors. Upfront cost is lower, but there is no liability protection. [L0270.065]
  • Older advice (2021): A sole proprietor operating under a business name may not need a DBA on day one. Many stores launched without one and later formed business entities as they grew. This is general experience, not legal advice. [L0968.024]
  • Older advice (2020): A new store can start as a sole proprietorship with an EIN and no state sales tax certificate. When sales grow, the owner can form an S corporation and obtain the state sales tax certificate at the same time. [L1111.180]
  • Older advice (2019): Starting as a sole proprietor using personal accounts is a valid option while you test the business. Once sales come in, a business structure can be set up with an accountant. [L1275.062]

LLC

  • Forming an LLC typically costs about $100. [L0270.027, L1085.076]
  • Older advice (2021): An LLC can be formed in a state other than where you live, through an accountant or yourself using an online filing service such as LegalZoom. [L0968.020]
  • Older advice (2021-22): In the US, the LLC is the most commonly chosen structure. It is quick to form, often possible entirely online, and separates the company's assets from the owner's personal assets, unless the owner breaks the law. [L1405.009]
  • Older advice (2022): Lean approach to entity setup in the US: form a cheap online LLC and optionally elect S-corp tax treatment. A sole proprietorship can be used first and an LLC formed later. Consult an accountant or tax professional for a personal situation. [L0969.005]
  • An LLC is optional at launch, and a sole proprietorship can work initially. Once the business is making money, consult an accountant about forming a separate entity. [L0247.103]
  • Form an LLC early and elect S-corporation tax treatment, which gives asset protection and tax benefits. Start as a sole proprietor only if the business is brand new, and form the LLC as soon as sales begin. [L0667.076]

S Corp and C Corp Elections

  • An LLC can be elected to be taxed as an S corporation. This decision affects tax obligations. [L0249.074, L0469.051]
  • Confirm the S-corp election with a tax professional before filing. [L0469.051]
  • An S corp is not a separate entity type but a tax election. An existing LLC is taxed as an S corporation by filing an election form with the IRS. The owner keeps the same liability protection while gaining the S-corp tax treatment. [L0703.004]
  • An LLC taxed as an S corporation offers tax advantages and separates personal liability from the business. This structure is commonly used by US-based dropshippers. [L0471.060]
  • Consider an S-corp election after formation to reduce self-employment taxes. [L0270.027]
  • Under an S corp or LLC, different pay structures are possible that can reduce taxes. [L0698.004]
  • In the US, the main difference between an LLC and an S corp is how business profits are taxed. An accountant should compare both for the owner's personal situation. [L1405.010]
  • Older advice (2019): An S corporation is the structure the presenter uses for protection and tax benefits. The right legal entity depends on your situation, so confirm it with an accountant or attorney. [L0663.060]
  • Older advice (2022): Form your business as an LLC elected to be taxed as an S corp. Use a C corp only if you plan to seek outside investment. [L0272.042]
  • Older advice (2022): An LLC can be elected to be taxed as an S corporation, which the lesson presented as a way to save on taxes. This is an example structure only and not tax or legal advice. [L1092.007]
  • Older advice (2022): A C corporation is generally used when bringing in shareholders, seeking investment or going public. Most small business owners use an LLC, which is the most popular structure. [L0969.034]

Structuring Multiple Stores

Related Versus Unrelated Stores

  • Related stores can sit under one LLC when they may later be sold as a network, especially when they cross-promote and share a remarketing pixel. Unrelated niches should be placed in a separate LLC. [L0471.059]
  • Stores are related if they target the same audience, share suppliers, or compete directly or indirectly. Unrelated niches should each sit in their own LLC. (Older, 2022.) [L1092.009]
  • Run separate niches under separate businesses. A closely related niche can share an LLC, while an unrelated niche should get its own LLC so finances stay separate. (Older, 2022.) [L0803.063]
  • Building a network of stores under one LLC works for stores selling related products. Keep stores in separate LLCs when they operate in unrelated product types, so funds are not commingled. (Older, 2021.) [L0801.146]
  • Put related stores that are cross-promoted or would be sold together under one LLC or S corp. (Older, 2022.) [L1113.046]
  • Test: ask whether you would compete with store A if you kept store B. If yes, keep them in one entity. Create a new LLC for unrelated businesses. (Older, 2022.) [L1113.046]
  • Run related stores in the same niche under one parent company, and open an unrelated niche under a separate business entity. Related stores can operate as DBAs under the parent company. (2023.) [L0835.113]
  • Businesses that would be sold together should share one LLC, one EIN, one bank account and one resale certificate. Unrelated businesses should be set up as separate entities. (Older, 2022.) [L0963.120]
  • An Amazon store and a Shopify store can share one LLC if they are related. Decide based on whether a future buyer would buy them as a bundle. (Older, 2022.) [L0963.122]
  • Related stores that are cross-promoted to the same customers can be sold together as one listing. (2024.) [L1285.073]
  • Group related stores under one LLC or S corp and form a separate entity for each unrelated category. This keeps stores easy to sell individually, since selling one store can compete with related ones. (Older, 2021.) [L1279.077]
  • Operate a dropship business under a separate entity from other businesses, such as Etsy or Amazon, if you may sell that business later. Commingled finances make the parts harder to sell separately. (Older, 2022.) [L0665.098]
  • Each store can be run under its own business entity so stores can later be sold separately. (Older, 2022.) [L0665.128]
  • Mixing funds and expenses means the businesses are effectively one business, so decide whether that is what you want. (Older, 2021.) [L1090.124]
  • Do not register an LLC under the name of a niche-specific website, because it ties the company to one niche and makes it hard to expand. Create a new entity for other stores and use DBAs for the store names. (2023.) [L0835.114]
  • One LLC can run several businesses under different trade names. A single LLC can be kept as a long-term vehicle for future projects. Each business does not need its own LLC. (Older, Source B 2021-22.) [L1415.019]

Conflict: One Umbrella Entity or One Entity per Store

  • Sources agree that related stores can share an entity. They differ on whether each store should have its own entity.
  • Shared entity: related stores under one LLC when they may be sold as a network or cross-promoted. [L0471.059, L1285.073]
  • Separate entity per store: each store under its own business entity so stores can be sold separately. (Older, 2022.) [L0665.128]
  • Apply the shared entity when you plan to sell a network as one asset. Apply separate entities when you plan to sell stores individually. [L0471.059, L0665.128, L1279.077]

Single Entity Mechanics

  • All stores can be run through one business entity with one EIN and one sales tax permit. Use one bank with sub-accounts for each store to see the money per store. (Older, 2020.) [L0852.094]
  • Keep the company that holds the bank accounts and credit card, and that appears on supplier forms, as the company that operates the websites. (Older, 2019.) [L0960.059]
  • An existing LLC can be used to run the stores as a business name, so a new company is not always needed. (Older, 2019.) [L0960.060]
  • An EIN is tied to the business entity, not to one store. One entity can run many stores, so changing niche or rebranding does not require a new entity. (Older, 2021.) [L0968.025]
  • Under one S corp, one entity can run several Shopify stores under different domain names, with each store linked to the corporation. A formal DBA filing for each domain was not needed in that setup. (Older, 2018.) [L1085.077]
  • One S-Corp can own all of your stores. (Older, 2018.) [L1274.048]
  • An existing registered company can sell online under a DBA. (Older, 2021.) [L1279.076]
  • Keep the entity if possible instead of forming a new one and closing the old one, even if the business name no longer matches the niche. Choose a neutral name where a rename is needed. (Older, 2022.) [L1281.072]
  • An older LLC can be reused for a new store only if it has a generic name. An LLC named after an unrelated former business should not be used. (2024.) [L1285.074]
  • To open a new store name, buy a domain and create a Shopify store under the new name while the operating company remains the S corp. Check with a lawyer or accountant whether a DBA or separate entity is needed. (Older, 2019. Not legal advice.) [L0467.072]

Naming the Entity

Generic Names

  • Do not name the LLC after the store brand. Use a generic business name such as "AK Stores LLC" or "AK Shopping LLC". This allows you to own multiple stores under one LLC, keeps accounting clean and makes future sales easier. [L0118.002]
  • Example: AK Stores LLC could own ShakaSurfboards.com initially, then add ShakaPaddleboards.com under the same LLC. [L0118.003]
  • Form the LLC with a broad, general name rather than a store-specific name, such as "DD Stores LLC". (Older, 2021.) [L0270.012]
  • Use a general, non-niche-specific LLC name so future related stores can be placed under the same entity. A narrow name makes it harder to add stores later. (Older, 2022.) [L1092.008]
  • Name legal entities with broad, non-store names, because one entity can later run several stores. (Older, 2020.) [L1088.078]
  • Name the parent company generically, for example with your initials plus "Stores, Inc.", rather than after one domain. This lets the same company own future stores. (Older, 2019.) [L1002.069]
  • Keep the company name generic and let each store name carry the niche. Stores built in the same niche can sit under one company. The store name, not the company name, is the part tied to the niche and domain. (2023.) [L1114.067]
  • The company name is mostly seen by banks and business contacts, while each store carries its own brand name. (Older, 2018.) [L1274.048]
  • Give the LLC a generic name that is not the store name, for example a pattern like "X Stores LLC" or "X Enterprises LLC", so it can hold several stores over time. The exact wording matters less than the name being general. [L0695.003]
  • Pick a vague LLC name such as a set of initials plus "Stores" or "Enterprises", so the same entity can own several stores. (2023.) [L0804.097]
  • Use a neutral business name, such as initials with the word "Stores" or "Inc", before the niche is decided, because it appears on bank accounts and supplier applications. (Older, 2022.) [L1281.071]
  • Do not name the company after the store. A separate company name, such as the owner's initials plus "Stores LLC", makes it easier to open additional stores later. (Older, 2021.) [L0834.080]
  • Entity name examples: "ER Stores LLC" and "Online Stores LLC". (Older, 2022.) [L0249.073]
  • Use the entity name for tax documents and bank accounts only. [L0249.073]
  • The business name does not need to relate to your domain or niche. [L0249.073, L0429.010]
  • Choose a different entity name rather than the store URL, so several related niche stores can be run under the same entity. (Older, 2022.) [L0429.010]
  • Example: a business entity "AK Stores Inc" owns the stores "Antons-Paddleboards.com" and "Antons-Kayaks.com". (Older, 2022.) [L0272.043]
  • The LLC name should differ from the store domain. This separates the legal entity from the brand. (Older, 2022.) [L0272.043]
  • Business names used for an LLC or EIN do not have to match the store names. The LLC or S corp owns the websites, so the legal entity name can differ from the brand. (Older, 2022.) [L0854.086]
  • The name on the LLC paperwork must match the name used for the tax ID and the submitted address. (2024.) [L0971.068]

DBAs (Doing Business As)

  • A DBA is usually needed when the LLC name differs from the store name, although this varies by state. Consult local requirements. (Older, 2022.) [L0272.044]
  • Register the DBA in the state where the LLC is registered. The existing sales tax certificate usually covers e-commerce sales in that state. (Older, 2020.) [L0468.160]
  • Use a DBA when the store name is close to the LLC name or is general. Form a new LLC when the new store is unrelated. (2023.) [L0804.097]
  • Check your original business setup with the person who helped create it before adding a DBA or reseller certificate to an existing LLC. Advice on legal or tax structures should come from an accountant or lawyer. (Older, 2018.) [L1085.079]
  • Choose an LLC over a DBA if you are fully committed to the business and plan to form an entity eventually. Form it early. Both DBA and LLC are fine for supplier approval. (2024.) [L1285.072]
  • Running an online store as a DBA under an existing LLC with the same EIN and bank account is possible if the businesses are related. (Older, 2021.) [L1090.124]

Registered Agent, State and Address

Choosing a State

  • Form the LLC in the state where you live. You need only one LLC per state. [L0118.004]
  • Alternative: Choose the US state for your LLC. Texas is business-friendly. A virtual address through a UPS store or PO box can serve as the official business address, so you do not have to use your home address. (Older, 2021.) [L0834.078]
  • Alternative: Choosing a state to form your company in is a personal decision. Texas is described as business friendly, and Delaware and Nevada are commonly used alternatives. (Older, 2018.) [L1274.049]
  • Wyoming's tax benefits are mainly relevant to non-US residents forming a US company. A US resident should generally form the company in their own state, since Wyoming or Delaware give no clear advantage there. (Source C.) [L1512.023]
  • An LLC can be formed in any state, but forming it in another state usually does not remove the obligation to pay tax in the state where you live. Check tax rules with a professional before relying on a different state. (Older, 2019.) [L0960.061]
  • An LLC formed in Wyoming while physically operating in Florida: assume a nexus in the state of operation and confirm with a tax professional. (Older, 2022.) [L0969.028]

Registered Agents and Legal Address

  • A registered agent is the person who receives legal paperwork for a business. A registered agent is needed when you set up a business in a state where you do not live. A legal service can provide one. (2023.) [L0835.115]
  • A registered agent must be located in the state where the LLC is registered. An LLC outside your state needs a local registered agent who forwards mail. (2024.) [L0971.068]

Before the First Sign-Up

  • Settle the business structure before any supplier, platform or ad account sign-up, because those accounts use the registered business details. Get an EIN and a sales tax certificate only when they are needed for your situation (US context). [L0393.003]
  • You do not need an LLC or business license before contacting suppliers. You can start as a sole proprietor, then hire an accountant to set up a corporation after revenue starts. (Older, 2020.) [L0268.045]
  • A business structure is not required to get approved by suppliers. Starting as a sole proprietor is valid, and an LLC or S corporation can be added later. (Older, 2018.) [L1085.075]
  • Entity formation is optional and is not needed before contacting suppliers. (Older, 2020.) [L1088.077]
  • Conflict: Many US suppliers prefer to deal with a company structure such as an LLC or S corp rather than a sole proprietor. This is an additional reason to form one. (Older, Source B 2021-22.) [L1405.011]
  • Australian suppliers generally do not care whether you trade as a sole trader or through a company, so the early choice does not limit supplier approval. (Older, Source B 2021-22.) [L1405.015]
  • An EIN can be used for more than one business in the view presented, but confirm this with an accountant before relying on it. (Older, 2021.) [L1090.123]
  • Get an EIN as part of the setup path. [L1277.104]
  • Running a store under your own name typically requires being at least 18 years old. A younger founder would normally need an adult to sign the accounts and agreements. Confirm the rules for your country or state before opening business accounts. (Older, 2022.) [L0803.100]

Structure by Country

United States

  • The usual US business forms are a sole proprietorship (trading as yourself), an LLC and an S corp. The last two are legal entities. [L0698.015]
  • Conflict: A sole proprietorship can be used at the start, and an LLC can be formed later. [L0698.006, L0962.122]
  • Conflict (older, 2021-22): A sole proprietorship gives no separation between the business and the owner. The owner is personally liable, and personal assets such as a home can be seized to pay business debts. Do not run a US store as a sole proprietor. [L1405.008]
  • Sole proprietorship, LLC and S corp differ mainly in liability and tax treatment. Use the LLC and S-corp options above for the relevant decision. [L0698.015, L0703.003, L0703.004]

Australia

  • Older advice (2021-22): In Australia the two structures are the sole trader and the Proprietary Limited company (Pty Ltd). [L1405.012]
  • Older advice (2021-22): A sole trader can be set up in minutes. A Pty Ltd costs about $800 to $2,000 to establish and requires an accountant. [L1405.012]
  • Older advice (2021-22): In Australia, a sole trader is personally liable for business claims. A company director's personal assets are protected if the business operates legally. Liability protection and tax treatment are the two main differences. [L1405.013]
  • Older advice (2021-22). Recommended path for most Australian beginners:
    1. Start as a sole trader to keep setup cheap and fast. [L1405.014]
    2. Once the business earns meaningful revenue and liability exposure grows, switch to a Pty Ltd company with an accountant's help. [L1405.014]
    3. If you already own a Pty Ltd, use it from the start. [L1405.014]

Sole Trader and Company Basics (Source B, Older)

  • Older advice (2021-22): A sole trader has no legal separation from the owner. [L0702.003]
  • Older advice (2021-22): The choice between sole trader and company has no universal right answer. It depends on liability exposure, tax situation, cash available and cost. Advice from an accountant is recommended when tax is complex. [L0702.008]
  • Older advice (2021-22): Starting as a sole trader is a valid low-cost choice, and it is easy to convert to a company later. Many owners do this when cash is limited and convert once the company's cost is financially justified. [L0702.011]

Other Countries

  • Most other countries use different names but the same two-tier choice between trading as an individual and forming a company. [L0698.015]
  • Older advice (2021-22): Outside the US and Australia, a local accountant or lawyer should confirm the structure choice. [L1405.007]

Company Path for Beginners (Source C)

  • Check whether Shopify Payments can be opened with personal documents. If so, a company may be unnecessary at first. [L1512.038]
  • If a company is needed, form a basic company in your home country, not abroad. [L1512.038]
  • After you have proof of concept, plan a better overseas setup for scaling. [L1512.038]
  • At about 100,000 USD monthly revenue, the advice was to consider Dubai for tax savings. Hong Kong was recommended for payment stability if the seller lives at home. Both need a home-country basic company for tax reporting. [L1512.039]
