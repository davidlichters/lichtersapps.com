18.4 Operating outside the US: foreign founders and cross-Border setup

Decide first whether you need a foreign company

  • A business does not need to reside in the country it targets to sell there. Stores have been run successfully by owners living abroad. [L0967.062] (2020, older)
  • A seller outside the United States does not need a US company to run a store. Both options have pros and cons, so ask people in forums who have done it in their own country before deciding. [L0551.064] (2020, older)
  • Sellers living outside the US can operate under their home-country business details or form a US company, for example through Stripe Atlas. Neither route is confirmed as required for every supplier, so check the choice with sellers who have done it in the same situation. [L0359.114]
  • Being a non-US citizen does not block you from selling in the US. Legal obstacles are mostly about setup, not citizenship. [L1277.106] (2020, older)
  • Non-residents who are US citizens can run US stores from abroad. One source ran stores from Southeast Asia and South America for years without problems, but check your own tax and legal situation. [L1090.125] (2021, older)
  • A business with no US entity can still sell to US suppliers in some cases. The team advises confirming this in the community first and not relying on a general statement. [L0665.091] (2022, older)
  • Sellers based outside the US without a US physical presence were reported to run US stores without a US business entity or sales permit. This is an informal view, not legal advice. [L1111.141] (2020, older)
  • Do not form a new US business entity if the current foreign entity already sells successfully to US suppliers and customers. Register in the US only when there is a clear benefit. [L0806.087]
  • A US entity is only worth forming when the current setup is not already winning supplier approvals and sales. [L0806.100] (2024)
  • Selling in other countries does not require registering a company there at the start. Registration becomes relevant when sales in a country approach the local tax threshold (about 80,000 as of 2020) and you must start collecting and reporting tax. [L0664.028] (2020, older)
  • Expanding to a country where the company is not registered may require a local business partner or relocating the company. Check payment provider eligibility before planning the expansion. [L0492.030]
  • Opening a US-based business is comparatively simple even for people who are not US citizens. It still has challenges that need separate research before you commit. [L0781.010] (2021-22, older)

Routes to a US company for a non-US owner

Route A: keep your home-country business details

  • Sellers living outside the US can operate under their home-country details. [L0359.114]
  • A Canadian business selling to US customers can keep operating under its Canadian details. Both approaches are used in practice, so the choice depends on whether the owner wants a US entity. [L0429.084] (2022, older)

Route B: form a US company through a formation service

  • Stripe Atlas (stripe.com/atlas) sets up a US company, a US bank account, a Stripe account and a legal entity together. It is one option and is not required. [L0359.092]
  • Stripe Atlas enables non-US residents to establish US business formation and operations without being physically present in the United States. [L0044.001]
  • Stripe Atlas lets non-U.S. residents set up a U.S. company and U.S. bank account without traveling to the United States. This was described for 2018, so check current pricing on the website before you commit. [L1274.050] (2018, older)
  • For a store owner who is not based in the US, Stripe Atlas can set up the US company and related business bank setup. It is the recommended route for that situation. [L0360.064] (2024)
  • A non-US seller can set up a US company and US bank account through Stripe Atlas. This gives a US business presence without living in the US. [L0852.092] (2020, older)
  • Stripe Atlas can set up a US business entity for a founder living abroad and handles the setup steps. Use it as one of the options when you need a US business for suppliers and banking. [L0968.019] (2021, older)
  • Stripe Atlas is a paid service that incorporates a company, commonly a Delaware entity, and helps set up its tax ID and banking. It suits founders who want a US company without handling the paperwork alone. [L0675.005] (2025)
  • Stripe Atlas incorporates a business in Delaware, opens a bank account and obtains an LLC for a founder, including for those outside the US. It charges a fee for these services. [L0667.082] (2025)
  • Stripe Atlas helps non-US residents form a US LLC and set up a Stripe payment account. It is a common route for international students or founders who cannot otherwise form a US company. [L0467.071] (2019, older)
  • Stripe Atlas is a company-formation service for setting up a US company, which can then be used to open a Stripe payment account. It is useful for founders who need a US entity to accept card payments. [L0843.001] (2024)
  • Stripe Atlas is a service for incorporating a US company and setting up its banking and Stripe payments account. Founders use it when they want a US entity without handling the paperwork alone. [L1095.003] (2024)
  • Stripe Atlas can help with EIN, sales tax certificate and nexus setup. Confirm its included services directly with Stripe support. [L0969.032] (2022, older)
  • Stripe Atlas was cited as an option for forming a company and opening a banking setup as a non-US founder, but requirements such as a social security number should be checked. [L0671.119, L0671.256] (2021, older)
  • Stripe Atlas is an optional incorporation service that some companies offer during setup. You do not need it to form an LLC, get an EIN, open a bank account or connect Stripe. [L0808.059] (2026)
  • Comparable company formation services exist for non-US residents. Several providers offer similar packages, so compare offers before paying if the flat fee is a concern, and check community forums for cheaper, trusted options. [L0698.002]
  • Other countries may have comparable services, which should be checked before choosing. [L0469.046] (2021, older)
  • Stripe Atlas for non-US residents costs a one-time payment of 500 dollars (2022 figure) and includes a Stripe account, a Delaware LLC, a US tax ID number and a business bank account. [L0665.092] (2022, older)
  • The current flat fee is $500, per the time of recording. [L0698.001]

Procedure: flat-fee US LLC through Stripe Atlas

  1. Go to stripe.com/atlas.
  2. Pay the one-time flat fee of $500 (current pricing at time of recording). [L0698.001]
  3. The service sets up a Delaware LLC as a legal US business entity. [L0698.001]
  4. The service connects the entity to Stripe for accepting customer payments, which integrates with Shopify. [L0698.001]
  5. The service also opens a US bank account for the business. [L0698.001]
  6. Once set up, the business is ready to operate without further formation steps. [L0698.001]

Entity type and state

  • Stripe Atlas is described as forming a Delaware corporation (2019 source) and as forming a Delaware LLC (2022 and later sources). Confirm which entity type you receive before paying. [L0357.049] (2019, older), [L0698.001, L0665.092]
  • Wyoming and Delaware are the two most popular states for forming an LLC. Stripe Atlas can form a Delaware company. [L0806.086]
  • Delaware was named as the default state in Stripe Atlas. [L0969.030] (2022, older)
  • For non-US nationals forming a US LLC, Stripe Atlas is one option that sets up the entity. [L0969.030] (2022, older)
  • A non-US founder can set up a US company (S corp or C corp), obtain an EIN, and get a US business address and phone number through a formation service. Choose the provider by checking reviews and experiences of people who have used it, not a single recommendation. Confirm that the entity type and state fit your plan before paying. [L1086.066] (2019, older)

Choosing a provider and checking advice

  • Stripe is a trusted payment company that has been used for a decade. For an unanswered question, try the Stripe phone line and support forum. Stripe Atlas-specific advice should come from community members who have used it, not from a coach. [L0805.054] (2024)
  • A Stripe Atlas or formation service may ask for a social security number. This is a common hurdle for sellers abroad who need a US bank account. [L1285.078] (2024)
  • For sellers abroad needing a US bank account, Mercury bank and Stripe Atlas (which provides a US bank account, with a Delaware entity mentioned) are options. [L1285.078] (2024)

Choosing the company location

Step-by-step framework for any country

  1. Look up the business types your country offers. There is usually a choice between operating as yourself and operating as a legal entity. [L0698.011]
  2. Search community forums and Facebook groups for your country name to see which entity others have used. [L0698.011]
  3. Post a specific question in the community if no answer exists. [L0698.011]
  4. Consider a free consultation with a local accountant about which structure fits your business and whether they can handle the books. [L0698.011]
  5. Register the chosen entity directly through the official government website. [L0698.011]

Principles

  • Business entity types differ by country and there is no universal recommendation. The same framework usually offers doing business as yourself or as a legal entity. The legal entity is generally preferred for tax and liability reasons. [L0698.013]
  • International tax law is complex and region-dependent with no universal playbook. Hire a lawyer in each target region to provide written advice. This creates their professional liability and binding guidance. [L0041.035]
  • Advice on tax for sellers located in another country, selling into the US, should come from an accountant with that situation in mind, not from general store rules. [L0855.126] (2023)
  • Check current account approval rules and tax treatment with a local accountant before choosing a location. [L1512.049]

Hong Kong and UK as first steps

  • A UK company was described as cheaper to start than a Hong Kong business, so a UK company is the suggested first step. A Hong Kong company with a PayPal account that accepts dropshipping is the fallback if UK restrictions continue. [L1347.007]
  • A UK LTD was used as a cheap start, but the cheap option later caused account problems. Compare the total cost of the setup you will need before choosing. [L1127.003]
  • Using a Singapore or Hong Kong company for PayPal gave stronger payment processing with few holds, at a dispute rate of about 4 to 5%. [L1440.044]

Canada

Options, by source date

  • Registering in Canada is often simpler for a Canadian seller who wants to sell into the US, since a US entity does not automatically unlock more supplier approvals. Shopify can show prices in the customer's currency. [L0671.124, L0671.260] (2021, older)
  • A Canadian business selling to US customers can keep operating under its Canadian details or form a US entity. Both are used in practice. [L0429.084] (2022, older)
  • Canadian sellers in the US often form a US LLC. A Canadian sole proprietor can register a DBA to show a business name to suppliers. Confirm the best Canadian entity setup with an accountant. [L0471.061] (2024)
  • A Canadian founder can register the business in Canada, and Canadian banks may open accounts for it. The EIN is optional at the start, but the business still needs a US address and entity setup if it sells into the US. [L0667.083] (2025)
  • You can operate a US-based dropshipping business from Canada selling to US customers if the business is registered in the US and suppliers ship from US warehouses. As of 2025, this should not be affected by current regulations, though tariff and trade laws may change. Confirm with legal or tax advice if uncertain. [L0255.075] (2025)
  • A Canadian resident can form a US business, such as in Delaware, and operate it in the US. Whether the reverse arrangement works (operating a US company from Canada) should be checked with an accountant. [L1090.126] (2021, older)
  • A Canadian sole proprietor selling only to US customers may be accepted by suppliers with an EIN. Start as a sole proprietor with a business name and an EIN, and check with an accountant before converting to a corporation. [L0667.084] (2025)
  • Shopify can run Canada and US sales on one store, with currency conversion through Shopify Payments. A US entity is optional until the US business grows. [L0468.161] (2020, older)
  • A business bank account and address for Canadians selling to US customers can be discussed in the Discord North America channel. [L0964.122] (2023)

Selling in the US from outside it

Payment and tax-ID requirements

  • For a foreign owner operating in the US, a US EIN (also called FEIN) is needed. Resale certificates are needed in some cases. Ask a US-based accountant or the Discord channel about setup rather than relying on general advice. [L0472.045] (2025)
  • Non-US residents who are unsure about US paperwork, such as resale certificates, should get specialist advice. Forming a US LLC was suggested as a route that may simplify that paperwork. [L0466.068] (2018, older)
  • A business can be run from Europe with a US LLC and Shopify, using a credit card or debit card linked to a European bank or a US bank account. A US credit card is not strictly required. [L1092.019] (2022, older)
  • For a non-US founder who wants a US bank account, a service such as Stripe Atlas can set up the US company and bank account. This is 2020 information, so check current requirements. [L0468.162] (2020, older)
  • A US PayPal account for a non-resident business can face long holds of 21 to 60 days, sometimes 50,000 to 60,000 USD. Obtaining an ITIN or SSN to remove these holds took about two to three months, so plan for that delay before scaling payments. [L1440.060]
  • A traditional US bank account requires in-person opening at a US branch. That was impractical in 2021 because of travel restrictions. Mercury Bank was then the one option offering a virtual US bank account for non-residents. [L1405.024] (2021-22, older)
  • A US company can get a bank account as a non-resident through banks or fintechs such as Mercury or Brex, which helps with payment processing. [L1440.043]

US address and the card rate

  • The card rate can be lowered by running the store as a US LLC on the Shopify Basic plan, which the lesson gives as 2.9% per transaction. To get that rate you need a US address (a residential address of a contact or a US 3PL fulfillment center address) and a connection willing to lend it. [L1327.011] (2026)
  • To establish a US LLC for banking, you need LLC registration, a physical US address, and potentially a representative (a real person) to appear in person at the bank if you are not physically present in the US. [L0324.030]

Registration address and proof of address

  • The Shopify account country and the business address must match the country where the business operates, not the country where the owner lives. For example, an owner living in Japan who runs a US store must enter a US business address and select United States as the country. [L1314.010] (2021-22, older)
  • Mixing up the owner's residence country and the business country can cause problems with Shopify Payments, checkout functionality and payouts. Set the account country correctly from the start. [L1314.011] (2021-22, older)
  • A formation service can provide a US business address and phone number for a non-US founder. [L1086.066] (2019, older)
  • Canadian founders need a US address and entity setup if they sell into the US, even when the business is registered in Canada. [L0667.083] (2025)

Banking, payment accounts and the owner's identity

Multi-currency accounts

  • For payment setup, use multi-currency accounts such as Wise or Airwallex to get virtual cards and USD or EUR accounts. Personal bank cards may reveal your location to the ad platform. [L0363.061]
  • Airwallex is an online business account used to pay advertising, send international transfers in about 1 day and convert currency at real-time rates. It can be used alongside a Hong Kong company. Its currency conversion was reported as more accurate than Wise. [L1512.033]
  • Airwallex transfers from a Hong Kong-linked account were reported to reach a US account in about one day and an Indian account in about 2 to 3 hours. Conversion rates were reported as real-time with low transfer fees. These figures are seller-reported and should be verified. [L1512.051]

Owner identity on supplier and platform accounts

  • A non-US citizen running a business as a sole proprietor gives suppliers personal details. A US LLC with a US bank account is generally easier for suppliers to approve. [L0671.116, L0671.253] (2021, older)
  • A supplier's request for a Social Security number is a common hurdle. [L1285.078] (2024)

Funding ads from a non-US card

Procedure: funding Facebook ads from a non-US card with a USD account [L0690.024]

  1. Open a US LLC. [L0690.024]
  2. Open a Mercury account, which works with Facebook. [L0690.024]
  3. Transfer money from the bank to Wise. [L0690.024]
  4. Convert there before paying Facebook. [L0690.024]

Payment processors and payout chain

Merchant account

  • A merchant account is the payment-processing account a business needs to accept customer card payments. Stripe provides these accounts and connects to Shopify. A foreign owner can use it to take payments from US customers once a US entity is set up. [L0698.014]

Payout chain for a non-US seller

Procedure: receiving Stripe payouts as a non-US seller [L1127.001]

  1. Open a Stripe account linked to a US LLC. [L1127.001]
  2. Move the Stripe balance to a US business bank account such as Mercury or RelayFi, which requires a US LLC. [L1127.001]
  3. Transfer the funds from that account to a WISE account. [L1127.001]
  4. Convert USD to the local currency in WISE with minimal fees. [L1127.001]

PayPal

  • Opening PayPal in one country while the company is registered in another, then switching to a different account type, led to repeated permanent bans in one reported case. Pick a single consistent company and country setup before opening PayPal. [L1347.006] (undated, Source C)
  • PayPal from India can be held for 21 to 60 days, sometimes with holds of 50,000 to 60,000 USD. [L1440.044]
  • A Hong Kong business account for PayPal requires paying taxes, and the first tax payment is due after 18 months. [L1347.008]

Compliance: Corporate Transparency Act and annual filings

  • Check whether Corporate Transparency Act beneficial ownership reporting applies to a US LLC before assuming any deadline. A reporting window of 30 to 90 days was mentioned for LLCs formed in 2024, but it was not confirmed. Verify the current rule with an accountant or an official source. [L0806.101] (2024)
  • Each foreign company usually needs an annual filing, which an accountant or a CPA can handle. Plan for filing deadlines, which the coach noted fall in March in his case. [L0363.064]
  • If a second company is set up in another country, confirm its tax obligations first. Check whether the local advisors understand the dropshipping model, since some do not. A US LLC is usually formed quickly. [L0693.002]
  • Expanding into a country with a local company may need a local business partner or relocation. [L0492.030]
  • The US resale-certificate and sales-tax-related setup can be checked with a US-based accountant. [L0472.045, L0969.032]

Structure and tax cautions

  • A coach's company setup presented dropshipping as an export of marketing services to avoid VAT. This applies only to the coach's own India and US setup. Confirm any such structure with a local tax advisor before use, especially in Germany, where invoices must show the company address. [L0363.063]
  • In India, an 18% withholding applies to foreign advertising payments, so 1,000 USD of ads costs 180 USD extra. This made the setup unsuitable long-term, and a US company was set up instead. [L1440.042]

Selling into a country without registering there

  • Selling in other countries does not require registering a company there at the start. Registration becomes relevant at the local tax threshold (about 80,000 as of 2020). [L0664.028] (2020, older)
  • A business does not need to live in the country it targets to sell there. [L0967.062] (2020, older)
  • For selling into the US without a US entity, the source notes conflicting positions: some sellers operate that way (2020, informal), while others say it is possible only in some cases and should be confirmed in the community (2022). Consider both, and check the current position with a local advisor. [L1111.141, L0665.091] (2020 and 2022, older)

Living abroad while running the store

Procedure: planning before moving abroad for long-term travel [L1441.035]

  1. Find a tenant to rent your home. [L1441.035]
  2. Research the visa requirements for each country. [L1441.035]
  3. Clarify your tax situation with your home government, which may not be set up for long-term travelers. [L1441.035]
  4. Book flights only after the above steps are complete. [L1441.035]
  • Non-residents who are US citizens can run US stores from abroad, but check your own tax and legal situation. [L1090.125] (2021, older)

  • A cheap UK company later caused account problems, so compare total setup cost before choosing. [L1127.003]
