18.6 Sales tax, nexus and indirect taxes (US, VAT, GST)

Nexus: Which States Require Sales Tax

  • Sales tax nexus determines which states you must collect and remit sales tax in, based on your business operations and customer locations [L0006.005].
  • Nexus is a connection to another state that obliges you to collect sales tax there. It is triggered by meeting that state's threshold, not by simply having a customer in that state [L1344.019].
  • Nexus is created by too many local employees, office locations, or other significant business activities. Consult your 3PL and a local tax specialist to avoid unintended tax liability [L0041.034].
  • A business with a physical presence in a state (office, employees, or ownership there) can have nexus in that state, even below sales thresholds [L0665.129].
  • Sales tax obligations follow where your business entity has a physical presence. Orders shipped into that state then require tax collection. Competitors in no-sales-tax states may look cheaper to buyers in that state, but this alone is not a reason to stop advertising there [L0968.107] (older, 2021).
  • Sales tax is based on the shipping address, not the customer's billing address. Collect the tax for the state where the order is delivered [L0972.047].
  • Collect sales tax only where you are legally required to collect it, but pay it wherever it applies. Services that claim to offer free sales tax either pay it themselves or operate in states where collection is not required [L1274.051] (older, 2018).
  • Collect sales tax only in states where you have crossed the nexus threshold. Sales tax you collect belongs to the state, so you cannot keep tax you were never required to collect [L0358.044] (older, 2020).
  • Most states with no nexus require no collection, so competitors not collecting tax may simply be below the threshold [L1281.063] (older, 2022).
  • Not charging sales tax in states where the store has no nexus is normal for small online stores. Customers in those states do not expect to pay it [L1114.046].
  • Sales tax obligations start only when a store has nexus in a state [L1114.046].
  • Sales tax is charged on orders in the state where the business is registered if that state has sales tax, for orders shipped to that state [L0970.139].
  • Do not charge tax in a state where the business has no registration or nexus. If you hold a sales tax certificate for one state, only that state's orders need tax collection [L1281.066] (older, 2022).
  • Do not assume your home state has no sales tax. Verify its current rules before concluding you owe nothing [L0358.041] (older, 2020).
  • Check your business's tax obligations early, before the first sales. Sales tax or VAT rules depend on where the business is registered and where its customers are located [L1007.006].
  • Operating in many states creates sales tax nexus obligations. With nexus in 16 states, a business may need to track sales tax across all of them. Review rules regularly, because they change [L0960.063] (older, 2019).
  • A business in one state generally only needs to register for sales tax in another state if it has nexus there. Check nexus with an official resource before registering. This is general information, not tax advice [L0666.008] (2024).
  • Sales tax is usually charged only in the state where the business is located, because thresholds in other states are high. Some states have no sales tax, and sellers below a state's threshold do not need to report or pay there [L0664.025] (older, 2020).
  • Federal income tax is based on overall net profit and does not require state-by-state registration. Register for sales tax only in states where you have nexus [L0971.014].
  • A non-US seller with third-party suppliers in other countries usually does not have US nexus and is unlikely to exceed the sales thresholds in the first years. Confirm this with a tax provider for your situation [L0852.093] (older, 2020).
  • Whether a non-US company must charge sales tax depends on how the business is structured. Confirm the setup with an accountant before deciding [L0803.071] (older, 2022).
  • Each state sets its own nexus rules, so check them before charging tax on orders shipped to that state. Advice from a tax professional is needed before acting [L0470.099] (2023).

Economic Nexus Thresholds

Core rule

  • Once sales into a state cross that state's economic nexus threshold, you must collect and remit sales tax there [L0961.019] (older, 2020).
  • Thresholds are set per state and are usually based on revenue. Check the current figure for each state where you ship before acting [L0852.091] (older, 2020), [L0358.042] (older, 2020).
  • Track revenue by state each year [L0972.049] (2025).
  • Most early-stage stores do not reach nexus thresholds, but check the nexus levels as sales grow [L0970.141] (2023).
  • In most states, a business under $100,000 in annual revenue has no nexus outside its home state, so it needs a sales tax certificate only in its home state [L0855.119] (2023).
  • Thresholds are typically high, so most new sellers need a certificate only in their home state. They register elsewhere when sales there reach that state's thresholds, which usually happens after several years of growth [L1344.021].
  • Thresholds differ by state. Only collect and report tax where you are obligated to do so [L0968.021] (older, 2021).

Nexus notification procedure

  1. Wait for the sales tax liability notice that Shopify sends for that state [L0972.050] (2025).
  2. Obtain a sales tax certificate or permit for that state [L0972.050].
  3. Configure the tax settings in Shopify so orders shipping to that state are charged sales tax [L0972.050].
  4. Repeat these steps for each state where you reach the threshold [L0972.050].

Home-State Collection

  • Collect sales tax in the state where the business is located [L0247.100] (older, 2021).
  • In the US, sales tax generally applies when a product is shipped to a customer in the state where your business is based. You collect this tax from the customer and pass it on to your state [L1344.015].
  • The state where your business is registered is where sales tax registration is first needed [L0836.065] (2024).
  • Charge sales tax only on orders shipping to a state where the business holds a sales tax certificate, and only to addresses in that state [L0836.003] (2024).
  • Charge sales tax on orders shipped to the state where the business is based, and check the rules for each state where orders are delivered. Do not absorb the tax to compete with out-of-state stores [L0667.011] (2025).
  • Below nexus thresholds in other states, sales tax is charged on orders shipped to the state where the business is registered [L0967.059] (older, 2020).
  • If the home state has sales tax, sales tax applies to orders shipped there. If the home state has no sales tax, this obligation does not apply [L1092.001] (older, 2022).
  • A business may owe sales tax in its home state for orders shipped there even if the customer was not charged. Do not skip it to offer discounts. This is general guidance, not tax advice [L0965.066] (2024).
  • Collect sales tax only in the state where the business has a physical presence or nexus [L1281.063] (older, 2022).
  • Sales tax is shown at checkout, not on the product page, because it depends on where the business is registered and where the customer is. It must not be hidden from the customer [L1281.064] (older, 2022).
  • Sales tax charged at checkout is collected for the government and does not reduce your margin. Your wholesale cost stays the same and competitors pay the same tax [L0469.049] (older, 2021).
  • Advertising "no sales tax" is allowed only if you are not collecting sales tax on that order. If you collect sales tax in some states, add a note such as an asterisk that explains the exception [L0835.109] (2023).

Specific states

  • Texas: Texas is a state with no income tax, and that point alone is used as an example in [L0270.043] (2021). However, Texas has a state sales tax and requires a sales tax permit. A Texas-based store must collect Texas sales tax on Texas shipments [L0972.051] (2025). The two sources answer different questions: no income tax does not remove the Texas sales tax duty.
  • Nevada: Nevada levies a statewide sales tax. A Nevada store should check with the Nevada Department of Taxation for a sales tax permit when a supplier asks for a resale ID [L1086.056] (older, 2019).
  • Delaware: Delaware was reported to have no state sales tax, but this should be verified [L0469.048] (older, 2021). An LLC based in Delaware that sells online usually has no sales tax certificate to obtain there, though a form stating the exemption may exist [L0666.055] (2024). Ask your company formation provider about filing requirements, including any franchise tax [L0469.048] (older, 2021). Sales from Delaware into other states are taxed only where nexus exists there [L0360.073] (2024).
  • Some US states have no sales tax, so check the rules of the registration state before applying for a certificate [L1116.007] (2025). In a state without sales tax, search the state name with "sales tax exemption" to see whether a form is needed. In most cases no certificate is required [L0963.127] (older, 2022).
  • Maryland: Maryland requires a sales tax permit under its own rules. Check your state's rules before you register [L0468.163] (older, 2020).
  • Illinois: Illinois sales tax registration requires a sales tax and use certificate, which you can request from the state revenue department. It is different from your individual 1099 tax form [L0804.094] (2023).
  • Income tax versus sales tax: Federal income tax is based on overall net profit and does not require state-by-state registration [L0971.014].
  • Multi-state sellers from Delaware or no-sales-tax states: The sales tax obligations that apply are those tied to nexus in each destination state [L0360.073] (2024), [L0666.055] (2024).

Sales Tax Certificates, Permits and Resale Certificates

Certificate basics

  • In the US, each state issues its own seller's license or sales tax certificate. Search your state's name with "seller's license" or "sales tax certificate" [L1275.063] (older, 2019).
  • Sales tax permits are issued by each state, not nationally. Some states, such as California, add extra sales tax. Check whether your state requires a permit by searching your state name with "sales tax permit" on your state government's website [L1405.028] (older, 2021-22).
  • A sales tax certificate is also called a reseller permit in some states, such as California. The name differs by state, so use a resource that lists the local term for each state [L1344.018].
  • A sales tax certificate is required in the state where the business is located. It is advisable to obtain it as the business grows, and it can usually be obtained online as a PDF [L1111.138] (older, 2020).
  • Sources differ on timing for the home-state certificate. L0836.065 (2024) says register at the start. L1111.138 (2020) says obtain it as the business grows. Both agree that you should not charge tax before you are registered [L1114.044] (2023).
  • A sales tax certificate is required in the state where the business operates. Once you have economic nexus in another state, you need a certificate there as well [L0665.093] (older, 2022).
  • The EIN is a federal identifier. You usually do not need a sales tax certificate for every state until your sales volume becomes very large. Check the tax training before registering for each state [L1090.122] (older, 2021).
  • When starting a US store, register the business in the owner's home state and obtain that state's sales tax certificate [L0836.065] (2024).
  • Do not set up nexus tax collection from day one. Wait until you actually reach nexus before enabling it [L1283.050] (2023).
  • Sales tax should be collected only in a state once the store is registered there. This normally happens after nexus is created. Charging tax before registration can create legal risk [L1114.044] (2023).

Process to check whether you need a certificate

  1. Go to taxjar.com/states [L1344.017].
  2. Click the state where your business is located [L1344.017].
  3. Read the information on the page about sales tax in that state [L1344.017].
  4. Click the link that explains how to register for a sales tax permit, which is provided on every state page [L1344.017].
  5. Follow the FAQs there to see whether you need a certificate and how to get one [L1344.017].

Resale certificates

  • You do not need a resale certificate in every state you ship to. Nexus forms only when you exceed your state's yearly revenue threshold, which varies by state [L0276.010] (2024).
  • Most states require a resale certificate once you generate approximately $100,000 in yearly revenue in that state. Check your specific state and any state where you ship substantially [L0276.011] (2024).
  • A resale certificate is needed for a state outside the home state once sales into that state exceed about $100,000 per year. Example: a business registered in New York needs a New York certificate from the start and a New Jersey certificate once it sells over $100,000 per year into New Jersey [L1116.006] (2025).
  • If a supplier charges you sales tax but you have not reached the sales threshold (nexus) in that state, provide your resale certificate. You should not owe sales tax [L0276.031] (2024).
  • If a supplier charges sales tax on a purchase, identify the state charging it, look up that state's minimum nexus threshold, and send the supplier documentation showing the threshold and your current sales in that state [L0358.045] (older, 2020).

Procedure for supplier-charged sales tax

  1. If a supplier charges you sales tax on a purchase, identify the state that is charging it [L0358.045].
  2. Look up that state's minimum nexus threshold [L0358.045].
  3. Send the supplier documentation showing the threshold and your current sales in that state [L0358.045].

Shopify Tax Settings

Where settings are

  • To set up taxes in Shopify, go to Settings in the bottom-left corner of the admin and open the Taxes section. Shopify's own documentation covers the full steps [L0965.064] (2024).
  • In Shopify admin, go to Settings > Taxes and select only the regions where you must collect tax. Tax will not be added for other regions unless you enable it there [L1283.049] (2023).
  • Shopify's tax settings let you choose the regions where tax is charged. If orders from a state carry no tax, check these regional tax settings, since the store may not be collecting tax there [L0968.023] (2021).
  • Shopify charges sales tax based on two settings working together: the Charge taxes option on each product and the tax rules set up in the Shopify admin taxes settings. The product option alone does not decide when and how much tax is charged [L1114.045] (2023).
  • Shopify's built-in sales tax does not usually get charged automatically. The operator must determine liability and register for tax [L0969.031] (older, 2022).
  • Shopify collects sales tax automatically once you configure the tax settings, but it does not file the tax returns for you. Use a separate service if you want collection and filing handled [L1274.052] (older, 2018).
  • Sources differ on whether Shopify collects automatically. L1274.052 (2018) says it collects once configured. L0969.031 (2022) says it usually does not charge automatically and the operator must determine liability. Work from the liability analysis first, then configure the settings.
  • Tax collection can be set up directly in Shopify or with an app such as TaxJar, which can also file the taxes. Compare both options before choosing one [L0356.015] (older, 2019).
  • With "Include or exclude tax based on your customer's country" enabled in Tax calculations, checkout uses the local tax rate of the buyer's country rather than the store's own rate [L0677.038] (older, 2021-22).

Default tax values on products

  • Disable the default tax value on new products (such as an included VAT of 20% or 21%) if you do not charge taxes. A shown tax can cause visitors to leave checkout [L1260.044] (Source C, undated), [L1345.059] (Source C, undated).
  • When creating a product in Shopify, check the tax setting and disable the default VAT or tax-included option if no tax is charged. Otherwise a default tax line such as 20% or 21% appears at checkout [L1260.044], [L1345.059].

Stage-by-stage defaults

  • At the foundation stage no tax entity needs to be configured. Leave Settings > Taxes and duties at default, which uses Shopify tax services with the default taxes for the countries you sell to [L1327.028] (Source D 2026).
  • Taxes and duties for shipping are not configured at the start and are handled in a later stage of the build [L1306.018].
  • Duties and import taxes, and taxes for each market, are initially set to "Not collecting." Shopify recommends starting collection in every place you sell. Make sure taxes and duties are accounted for in all markets where you sell [L1167.029] (Source D 2026).
  • Sources differ on defaults. L1327.028 says to leave the foundation stage at default. L1167.029 says Shopify recommends starting collection in every market. Apply the default at foundation, then configure each market before selling into it.
  • On the Shopify setup page, ticking "This store is a registered business" is relevant for tax purposes. The address entered during setup can be changed later [L1314.013] (older, 2021-22).

Regions and shipping profiles

  • Settings > Taxes lists its tax regions from your shipping profiles. A destination only appears there once it is part of a shipping zone. To add a missing region, add the destination in shipping settings first [L0677.036] (older, 2021-22).
  • If you do not sell to other countries, you do not need to set up tax for the "Rest of world" region in Taxes. Only regions where you have a tax obligation need to be collected [L0677.031] (older, 2021-22).
  • Shopify calculates "Charge tax on shipping rates" automatically for Canada, the European Union, and the United States. The option includes shipping in the tax calculation [L0677.030] (older, 2021-22).
  • On a tax region page in Settings > Taxes, the GST box reads "You're not collecting GST" until you select Collect GST. Below it, Tax overrides lets you set custom tax rates per product collection (Add product override) or per shipping destination (Add shipping override) [L0677.035] (older, 2021-22).

Checking collected tax

  • Shopify's tax report shows the tax your store has actually collected. Use it to check collected amounts instead of estimating them [L0677.039] (older, 2021-22).

Remitting Collected Tax

  • Sales tax that a store collects from customers is not the store's money. It must be remitted to the state where it was charged [L0965.065] (2024).
  • Customers cannot be asked to pay a supplier's tax. The built-in system does not work that way [L0965.065] (2024).
  • Sales tax must be collected only where you are legally required to collect it, but it must be paid wherever it applies [L1274.051] (older, 2018).
  • Shopify does not file returns for you. A separate service handles filing and remittance if you want that done for you [L1274.052] (older, 2018).
  • TaxJar calculates tax, can file it, and remits it to the state on your behalf. It tracks tax and provides a resource library on nexus [L1281.065] (older, 2022).
  • TaxJar can automate quarterly sales tax reporting when it is set up through the account. It is treated as an industry standard for this [L0803.060] (older, 2022).
  • As of January 2022, TaxJar calculated sales tax for the US, Canada, and VAT, but did not file or report those taxes for Canada or VAT. Check the current scope before relying on it for filing [L0803.059] (older, 2022).
  • Once you have a nexus notification in a new state, apply the steps in the nexus notification procedure above [L0972.050] (2025).

Australia: BAS and remittance

  1. Register for GST [L1405.031] (older, 2021-22).
  2. Lodge a BAS (Business Activity Statement) regularly [L1405.031].
  3. Remit to the ATO the difference between GST collected from customers and GST paid to suppliers [L1405.031].

Registration Timing and Build Sequence

  • Most early-stage stores do not reach nexus thresholds. Check nexus levels as sales grow [L0970.141] (2023).
  • Register for sales tax only in states where you have nexus [L0971.014] (2024).
  • Register for sales tax permits only in states where sales exceed the economic threshold or where the business has employees. Registering in all 50 states is not necessary [L0247.101] (older, 2021).
  • For a US store, in the first stage: register in the home state, obtain its certificate, and collect tax on home-state orders only [L0836.065] (2024), [L0967.059] (older, 2020), [L1281.066] (older, 2022).
  • Multi-state tools become relevant as order volume spreads across states [L1092.004] (older, 2022), [L1092.005] (older, 2022).
  • Competitors in no-sales-tax states may look cheaper. Tax obligations still follow the rules above, and that is not a reason to stop advertising in those states [L0968.107] (older, 2021).
  • Sales tax obligations depend on the state where your business is based and on economic nexus in other states. Verify current rules before concluding that you owe nothing [L0358.041] (older, 2020).
  • Sales-tax registration is tied to nexus. Register in the state where the business is based and in states where you have nexus. For orders shipped to states without nexus, no registration was needed in the lesson's experience. Verify nexus rules with an accountant before relying on this [L0934.007], [L0934.141] (2023).

Canada

  • For a Canadian seller, sales tax rates differ by province. Use the ecommerce business's own taxing rules, and check TaxJar's Canadian coverage rather than relying on general statements [L0665.095] (older, 2022).
  • Canadian sellers must check sales tax per province, because rates differ. For example, Alberta's sales tax is 5 percent. Confirm province-level rules with TaxJar's Canadian coverage instead of assuming one rate applies across Canada [L0665.130] (older, 2022).
  • A Canadian business shipping to US customers does not charge US sales tax [L0964.121] (2023).
  • Canadian sellers who are not charging sales tax to US customers should set their tax rates to zero in Shopify and Merchant Center to match competitor prices in Google Shopping [L0964.118] (2023).

Australia: GST

Registration threshold

  • In Australia, a new business does not have to register for GST until its revenue reaches $75,000 or more in a single financial year. Before that it does not have to charge GST on sales [L0677.006] (older, 2021-22).
  • GST sales tax registration is not required for a new business until revenue reaches 75,000 Australian dollars in a single financial year. A business already GST-registered under an existing structure may continue as it is [L0702.023] (older, 2021-22).
  • GST registration only becomes mandatory once revenue in a single financial year reaches $75,000. Revenue from several businesses under the same legal entity is added together toward this threshold [L1405.030] (older, 2021-22).
  • A new business that has not made any sales does not need to register for GST yet. Registration can be done later, either directly or with an accountant, once it becomes relevant [L0702.024] (older, 2021-22).
  • In Australia you need at least an ABN from the start. GST registration is not required straight away for a sole trader. Registering early makes you collect and remit GST on every sale [L1405.029] (older, 2021-22).

Obligations once registered

  • In Australia, once a business is registered for GST it must collect GST on sales. Not collecting it when registered is illegal [L0677.027] (older, 2021-22).
  • In Australia, retail prices must include tax. Shopify's setting "All prices include tax" must be ticked for this [L0677.029] (older, 2021-22).

ABN and Shopify billing

  • A business registered for GST should add its Australian Business Number under General settings (Add ABN) so Shopify does not charge GST on its own subscription invoices. In the US the equivalent federal number is the EIN [L0677.005] (older, 2021-22).
  • In Australia, Shopify charges 10% GST on invoices. Adding an Australian Business Number (ABN) under Settings > General exempts invoices if you are GST registered [L1145.020] (older, 2021-22).
  • For a store based in Australia, Shopify may show a missing-tax notice. A GST-registered merchant can enter an ABN so that Shopify bills are exempt from GST [L1166.032] (older, 2021-22).
  • For a store based in Australia, Shopify bills are exempt from GST if the business is GST registered and enters its ABN in the tax settings. Without the ABN, the store will see a notice that tax information is missing [L1300.030] (older, 2021-22).

Shopify GST setup procedure

  1. Go to Settings > Taxes and open the Australia tax region [L0677.028] (older, 2021-22).
  2. Click Set up [L0677.028].
  3. Click Collect GST [L0677.028].
  4. Enter your ABN [L0677.028].
  5. Click GST; Shopify then collects 10% GST on each order [L0677.028].
  6. Under Tax calculations, tick "All prices include tax" [L0677.028].
  7. Click Save [L0677.028].

Australia: Process once threshold is reached

  1. Register for GST [L1405.031] (older, 2021-22).
  2. Lodge a BAS (Business Activity Statement) regularly [L1405.031].
  3. Remit to the ATO the difference between GST collected from customers and GST paid to suppliers [L1405.031].

International expansion

  • International expansion requires VAT numbers (EU/UK) and EORI numbers for importing products. The source writes "EORE", which refers to EORI. Consult a local tax specialist familiar with e-commerce in each target market [L0041.033] (Source E 2025-26).
  • VAT and tax for selling in Europe depend on the country where the seller lives and operates. Get advice from a local accountant before selling in Europe [L0773.051] (Source C, undated).
  • VAT and tax rules depend on the country you live in and the countries you sell to. Check with a local accountant before selling in Europe or any other region [L0774.049] (Source C, undated).
  • Tax is based on the country you sell to, not always the country where you live. Use common sense and check locally when unsure [L0677.008] (older, 2021-22).
  • Sales tax rules depend on the country and, in some countries, the state where you sell. The US charges sales tax state by state only where the business is obligated. Australia uses one federal GST, and the EU uses VAT. Check local rules with an accountant or a local tax expert [L0677.007] (older, 2021-22).

Shopify VAT settings

  • Settings > Taxes > Tax calculations: the "Charge VAT on digital goods" option creates a collection. Digital products added to that collection have VAT applied at checkout for European customers [L0677.037] (older, 2021-22).
  • Ads taxes in the UK and EU were flagged as a risk in the Source C lesson. Consult a tax accountant before acting [L0552.042] (Source C, undated).

Tax on Ad Spend

Singapore GST on ad spend

  • Singapore GST on ad spend equals 9% of the spend converted into SGD. The speaker's example of $100k USD gave S$9,000 in GST. At about S$1.30 per USD, $100k is about S$130k, and the GST would be about S$11,700. Verify the conversion before budgeting [L0351.028] (Source D 2026).
  • When estimating Singapore GST on USD ad spend, convert the spend to SGD at the current exchange rate first, then apply 9%. Treating USD 100k as S$100k understates the taxable base [L0351.050] (Source D 2026).

Other countries

  • Ad spend in some other countries carries VAT rates higher than Singapore's 9% GST. The total tax on scaling spend must be included in the budget [L0351.029] (Source D 2026).
  • Ads taxes in the UK and EU were flagged as a risk in the Source C lesson [L0552.042] (Source C, undated).

Sales tax on ad spend in the US

  • Sales tax obligations for ad spend are not separate from nexus. Ad spend in a state can be a factor, so check the nexus rules for your situation [L0357.046] (older, 2019).

Tariffs and Duties

  • Shopify has no native tariff option. A third-party app such as Magical Fees and Tariffs can add tariffs at checkout, or shipping profiles can carry tariff charges for a specific brand [L1116.034] (2025).
  • Duties and import taxes are initially set to "Not collecting" in Shopify. Shopify recommends starting collection in every place you sell [L1167.029] (Source D 2026).
  • Importing products into the EU or UK requires EORI numbers [L0041.033] (Source E 2025-26).

Checklist for Operators

  • Identify your home state, check its sales tax rules, and register there before charging tax [L0836.065] (2024), [L1092.001] (older, 2022).
  • Track revenue by state each year and check each state's nexus threshold as sales grow [L0972.049] (2025), [L0970.141] (2023).
  • Do not charge tax in states without registration or nexus [L1281.066] (older, 2022), [L1114.044] (2023).
  • Enable tax collection only in the regions where you must collect it, in the Shopify Taxes settings [L1283.049] (2023).
  • Check the default tax value on every new product [L1260.044], [L1345.059] (Source C, undated).
  • Verify all thresholds, VAT rules, and GST rules with an official source or a local tax professional before acting [L0664.027] (older, 2020), [L0962.118] (older, 2021), [L0666.008] (2024).

Further details

  • Sales tax nexus is typically triggered at around $100,000 in sales per year per state. Collect sales tax only where you have nexus, and avoid collecting or paying it in states where you have none. [L0469.047]

  • Sales tax applies in the state where the business is registered if that state has sales tax, for orders shipped to addresses in that state. Not every US state has sales tax. [L0806.084]

  • For most states, economic nexus (a tax obligation in another state) starts when sales in that state exceed 100,000 dollars in a year. Check each state's rules with an accountant. [L0806.085]

  • Before any state reaches its economic nexus threshold, a store charges sales tax only on orders shipping to the state where it is registered and holds a sales tax certificate. Each other state is added only when the store reaches that state's threshold. [L0972.098]
