18.22 Contracts, partners, contractors and agencies

Written contracts and agency agreements

  • Use a written contract even when the relationship feels casual or informal. It protects both parties legally, clarifies expectations, and gives you recourse if problems arise. [L0217.019]
  • Every contract should specify:
    • payment structure
    • timeline of work
    • cancellation terms
    • performance expectations
    • communication protocols
    • penalties for unlawful behavior, such as deleting accounts without consent [L0217.020]
  • Get a written contract even if the agency offers performance guarantees, refund guarantees, or payment-only-if-successful terms. Legal protection matters regardless of how confident either party feels. [L0217.035]
  • Written contracts with offshore freelancers and staff are often seen as unnecessary for simple engagements, because cross-border legal issues are complicated. The main value of a contract is making each party's position and obligations clear. [L1359.039]

Contractors, freelancers and employee classification

United States: 1099 contractor or employee

  • In the US, pay independent contractors, including family members who work as freelancers, as 1099 contractors. Agree on this and on the expectations in writing before the work starts. [L0832.053]
  • The team in the Source A example was hired as 1099 independent contractors, not employees. If the owner sets fixed hours and full-time control over the work, the contractor may need to be converted to an employee. That brings insurance and benefits obligations. Check local labor and tax laws before deciding. [L0337.026]
  • Where the two rules meet: a 1099 setup assumes the contractor controls their own working pattern. If you set fixed hours and control the work full-time, the arrangement may need to become employment, as described above. [L0337.026, L0832.053, L1000.038, L1473.021]

Paying hourly contractors

Pay hourly contractors every two weeks:

  1. Each contractor logs their hours on their personal board in Trello.
  2. Every other Thursday the owner checks the logged hours.
  3. Pay US contractors through QuickBooks.
  4. Pay Philippines-based contractors through Wise with direct deposit.

[L0337.027]

Virtual assistants

  • Sign a VA contract before training starts. Include:
    • pay
    • weekly work hours (for example Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:00 Eastern Standard Time)
    • the holidays observed (here US holidays)
    • vacation time and how much notice is required for it
    • how much notice is required to quit
    • the training obligations [L1473.021]
  • Sign a short agreement letter with the assistant that repeats the job post terms in writing. Include an exclusivity clause stating the assistant will not work for other businesses. [L1000.037]
  • Classify the virtual assistant as an independent contractor rather than an employee in the signed agreement. Confirm the tax reporting rules for your country with a qualified adviser before paying. [L1000.038]

Non-disclosure agreements

  • For an NDA template, search Google for an NDA template in Google Docs, then adapt it to your needs before sharing it with a contractor. [L1285.077]

Data and account ownership

  • Clarify data ownership in the contract. Verify that ad accounts, creative files, and account configurations remain your property, and that the agency cannot delete or withhold access if the relationship ends. [L0217.024]
  • Store all supplier, shipping and app logins in one restricted accounts sheet, so the assistant has what they need without searching. Keep the owner's social media logins there too when the assistant manages those accounts. [L1000.055]
  • Do not give Fiverr freelancers access to Google Ads or Google Merchant Center accounts. If you need help unsuspending an account, use a reputable PPC agency with a public reputation, since logins from unfamiliar IP addresses can flag an account. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0803.049]

Payment providers and bank access

  • Give a team member access to your payment providers only after you have built enough trust with them, and handle this step with great care. They can install the payment providers in the store so it goes live. [L1358.013, L1360.014]
  • If an assistant pays for orders, give them a dedicated payment method, such as a PayPal account or a credit card, rather than the owner's main account. [L1173.035]
  • While delegating everything else, the owner kept control of the bank accounts and of supplier payments that needed transfers. This kept financial control in one place during a seven-week absence. [L1437.034]

Business partnerships

  • Two partners can own a store through a new corporation split 50/50 or 51/49, or through their existing LLCs owning the corporation. Whichever structure is chosen, a clear written partnership agreement is required. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0834.123]
  • Any business partnership needs a written agreement covering:
    • the responsibilities of each partner
    • the money split
    • every foreseeable event
  • Typical clauses cover one partner wanting to buy out the other, the death of a partner, and a partner failing to meet their obligations. [L1097.007]
  • AI tools can produce a useful first draft, but a lawyer should review the final agreement before anyone signs. [L1097.007]
  • Prompt for drafting a first partnership agreement:
I run a dropshipping store called [STORE NAME] on Shopify with my business partner [PARTNER NAME]. We have no written partnership agreement yet. Please help me draft a one-page partnership agreement. First, list everything a standard business partnership agreement should contain. Then include clauses for: what happens if one partner wants to buy the other out; what happens if one partner dies; what happens if one partner does not meet their obligations. Add a responsibilities section that splits tasks between us, for example: [PARTNER A] handles website design and maintenance, product uploads and supplier relationships; [PARTNER B] handles customer service and marketing. Also add a section on how profits, costs and finances are split and paid out. Ask me for any details you need before drafting, and mark which clauses should be reviewed by a lawyer before signing.

[L1097.008]

Influencers and ambassadors

  • A simple one-page, three-paragraph agreement with each influencer formalizes the arrangement and confirms the posting obligation. It acts as a digital handshake and does not create liability, but it helps set expectations. [L0790.072]
  • A simple written influencer agreement with the creator's name and key terms creates a record, even if it is not a strict legal contract. Enforceability across countries varies, so get local legal advice before relying on agreements abroad. [L1213.054]
  • Keep ambassadors unpaid and in the ambassador role. According to general counsel's advice, unpaid ambassadors who sell or refer products but are not paid can be treated as non-employees, which helps avoid employment liability. [L0790.073]

Community rules

  • Members must not post or offer paid services to other members of the program, and must not use the course community or direct messages to sell anything to other members. Anyone who wants to sell a service must build a separate business rather than use the program's audience. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1498.014]

  • Members may not copy or share program content, such as lesson videos, attachments, and spreadsheets, with anyone who is not a member, unless the program owners give express permission. The content is copyrighted and owned by the company behind the program, and sharing it outside the membership can lead to legal action. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1498.015]

  • Permitted sharing with a non-member, such as a virtual assistant helping build the business:

    1. Message the program owners before sharing.
    2. Name the specific videos or materials you want to share and explain why.
    3. Share only after receiving written approval.

    Sharing without approval is not allowed. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1498.016]

Scam avoidance

Unsolicited offers and messages

  • Shopify and Google never send unsolicited emails to a store asking for money to optimize the store. Emails from agencies offering store optimization are usually cold outreach and should be ignored. [L0472.048]
  • Legitimate mentors, coaches, and staff of an online community never send unsolicited direct messages first. Any direct message that arrives right after joining, claiming to be from the community's team, is a scam until proven otherwise. [L1442.001]
  • Anyone asking for money in a direct message, including for store setup help, course access, or a service fee, is a scammer. Real staff do not request payment through private messages. [L1442.002]
  • Requests for payment in cryptocurrency made by someone posing as a community coach or support person are a clear fraud signal. Refuse and do not send any funds. [L1442.003]
  • New members often trust the first private message they receive after joining a server, which is exactly what impersonators count on. Treat every unsolicited message from a community member as untrusted until it is verified in an official channel. [L1442.005]

Spotting impersonators

  • Impersonation scams copy a real person's display name, and sometimes profile picture, then add a small change such as an extra word, a letter, or trailing punctuation. Check the exact spelling of every name against the official member list before trusting a message. Example pattern: a real handle Alex_Store becomes Alex_Store vs or Alex_Store. in a fake account. [L1442.004]
  • Official announcements, pinned messages, and public channels are the reliable source for who the real staff and coaches are. Compare any suspicious account against these before replying. If a fake account is confirmed, block it and do not engage further. [L1442.006]
