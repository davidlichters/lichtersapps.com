18.11 Funding, cash flow and financing the business

Debt principles

  • Avoid debt where possible and prefer funding through margins. Shopify Capital and similar financing take a share of sales and can be expensive. (older advice, 2021) [L0247.097]
  • Credit can be a useful tool when used carefully, but a business should not be run on borrowed money. If a loan's purpose is unclear and the business does not need it, treat the repayment as a risk to address. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1046.026]
  • You do not need large upfront capital to pay for inventory. Paying suppliers with a credit card and paying the card off quickly needs only about the wholesale cost of one order in credit. (older advice, 2019) [L1275.064]

Limits on the Shopify balance

  • Do not rely on the Shopify Payments balance for working capital. Withdrawals are limited to 5,000 dollars per payout, which can block funds needed for orders. [L1469.041]
  • Shopify Balance can be used as a short-term source of funds for scaling ad spend, but only for stores on Shopify Payments and only for US residents. Treat it as a stopgap and focus on getting a business credit card as the longer-term funding source. [L1124.019]

Matching supplier and ad payments to cash inflows

  • Scaling requires cash flow to cover the MOQ purchase up front, because Shopify payouts may take longer than supplier and ad payments. Suppliers with net terms, such as about 14 to 20 days, and credit cards with a 30-day billing window can help match outflows. (older advice, 2020) [L0351.026]
  • When stock must be paid before sales come in, some established sellers cover the gap with business lending or other financing rather than relying only on cash. Plan for month-end inventory bills so that a slow month does not force a sudden sale of assets. [L0690.043]
  • When a store sells, the customer's money goes into your own account, and you pay the supplier separately for each order using the payment method you agree on with them. (older advice, 2024) [L0965.068]
  • Plan cash flow before peak sales: ad spend on credit cards and fast budget increases quickly exhaust cash, so arrange extra funding in advance. [L1363.042]

Funding stock with credit cards (routine)

Use this routine when the business credit card limit is too low to fund orders. Sources: L0934.014 and L0934.140 are the same routine, merged here. (older advice, 2023)

  1. Max out the business credit card every day to pay supplier orders as they come in.
  2. Pay the card off in full every night.
  3. Call the card issuer every day and request a higher limit, repeating until the limit rises gradually.
  4. Apply early for an American Express charge card as a second card.
  5. Optionally open a second credit card from another issuer and rotate between both cards while continuing the nightly payoff and limit requests.
  6. When no credit is left, wait for Shopify payouts (about 48 hours) before paying the supplier, or use a debit card as a backup even though it earns no points.
  7. Spend the first one to two months building credit limits and cash reserves this way.

[L0934.014, L0934.140]

  • PayPal offers business loans to eligible accounts. (older advice, 2021) [L0247.099]
  • Short-term business loans such as Amex short-term loans with 30, 60, or 90 day terms can be cheap if repaid within the window, but interest begins to accrue once the period ends. Compare the fees of each lender and confirm you can repay within the allotted period before borrowing. (older advice, 2020) [L0358.040]
  • A line of credit is not needed at the start. It only makes sense once ad spend is proven to turn clicks into customers within two to three days and is profitable. (older advice, 2023) [L0804.100]

Checkout financing

  • When a customer pays through a financing provider at checkout, the store receives the full order amount right away, so the merchant does not finance the purchase. If cash is short before the first payout arrives, a credit card can cover the supplier order and be paid off later, while Shopify Payments payouts typically arrive within about 48 hours. (older advice, 2023) [L1283.110]

Peak season and reserves

  • Around the end of the year, banks and suppliers can slow down because many people are on holiday, while order volume peaks. Keep reserve cash or a backup payment source to cover fulfillment during the peak period so that payout delays do not stall orders. [L1469.046]
  • Treat cash as profit only after the money is in the bank, all expenses are paid, and the return window has closed. Keep a cash reserve for returns and unexpected costs. (older advice, 2022) [L0803.067]

Currency conversion

  • Revenue earned in US dollars and converted into another currency is valued at the exchange rate on the day the money is transferred. A stronger US dollar adds value only if the transfer happens at a favorable rate, so compare the transfer-day rate with the sale-day rate before counting any gain. [L0667.092]
