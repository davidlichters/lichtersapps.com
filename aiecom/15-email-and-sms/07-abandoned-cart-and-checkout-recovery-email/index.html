15.7 Abandoned cart and checkout recovery (Email)

Purpose and Priority

  • An abandoned checkout occurs when a customer adds products, starts checkout, and does not complete the purchase [L0282.001]. An abandoned cart flow is triggered for shoppers who started checkout but did not complete the order, and it brings them back to the cart they already built [L0606.004].
  • Visitors who add to cart and leave without buying waste paid traffic unless you can follow up with them. Automated reminder emails or SMS let you re-contact them hours later at no extra ad cost [L0476.001].
  • Roughly 7 to 8 out of 10 shoppers who add products to their cart leave without buying, so an abandoned-cart sequence reaches a large share of site visitors [L0572.006].
  • Recovering about 25% of abandoned carts through an email sequence is cited as a realistic result to aim for once the flow is optimized [L0572.007].
  • Abandoned cart recovery is among the cheapest ways to win back shoppers who added items to their cart [L1251.039].
  • Improving the abandoned-cart flow is one of the fastest ways to see results in an online store, because it recovers sales that already had strong intent. Gains can show as lower cost per lead, better traffic quality and a higher conversion rate [L1208.020].
  • A recovered buyer can become a repeat customer who leaves reviews, refers others and eventually becomes a brand advocate. This long-term value justifies the extra effort in recovery sequences [L1208.013].
  • Set up the abandoned cart email sequence before any other optimization. Use it to learn why visitors leave, and avoid discounts as the first lever; discounts should be strategic and paired with something of higher value [L0575.027].
  • The abandoned cart sequence should be set up before traffic is scaled [L0430.013].
  • Older advice (2018): set up the abandoned cart email sequence in Klaviyo from day one, before any traffic campaigns run [L1085.004].
  • Older advice (2021): once the store is established, run abandoned cart follow-up as an automated email flow, for example one built in Klaviyo [L0671.020].
  • Older advice (2021): Klaviyo is used to set up abandoned cart emails with customized messages and urgency. Abandoned carts can turn into sales in the days after a visit, so configure these early [L1279.047].
  • Older advice (2021-22): an abandoned cart email sequence is an automated message sent through the email autoresponder when a shopper leaves a cart without buying [L1298.031].
  • Klaviyo can run abandoned cart recovery, which sends a notification to visitors who left items in their cart without completing the purchase [L1399.005].
  • Shopify's email or Klaviyo can both send abandoned cart messages and both work; the speaker's team uses Klaviyo [L0964.124].
  • Older advice (2021-22): a person who added items and started checkout is the warmest prospect you will get, so extra reminder emails are acceptable here [L1449.001].
  • Abandoned-cart messages are reminders about items left in the basket, not welcome emails. Keep the copy focused on the cart and its contents. Trust comes from the logo, the product block and clear next steps, not from a long introduction [L0567.043].
  • A shopper who abandoned a cart has already moved through the ad, the site, the product page and add-to-cart, so purchase intent is high. The messages should help them close the sale, not reintroduce the brand [L1208.001].
  • Abandoned-cart messages are bottom-of-funnel. Do not use top-of-funnel content such as broad brand introductions; focus on trust, objection handling and social proof [L1208.017].
  • At the abandoned-cart stage the buyer has already decided. Messages should validate that decision, build trust and handle objections instead of pushing for a sale. Pressure or a hard sell at the first contact tends to backfire [L1208.003].

Channel Choice

  • Source E first message: 20 minutes after abandon, sent as SMS to subscribers and as email to non-SMS subscribers [L0261.001].
  • SMS can only reach customers who agreed to receive SMS. SMS Bump only sends to opted-in customers, so SMS cannot recover carts for customers who never opted in. Set up Clavio and SMS Bump in parallel [L1347.030].
  • Source E SMS: within 24 hours, send one SMS that reassures the buyer, points out the saved order and lists three value propositions. If the cart is over $100, mention free shipping instead of one of them [L1208.019].

Spacing Rules

  • Use 20-minute, 45-minute and 23-hour intervals rather than random timings. The 23-hour wait ensures the second follow-up hits the customer at the same time of day as when they originally abandoned their cart, when they are most likely to be free and receptive [L0261.005].
  • Always space abandoned cart emails using multiples of 24 and 12 hours (20 min + 45 min ≈ 1 hour, then 23 hours, 24 hours, 12 hours). Avoid arbitrary intervals like 4 hours, which hit customers at inconvenient times (for example 2 a.m.) and get lost in inbox clutter by morning [L0261.006].
  • Conflict: the Source A recommendation uses a 4-hour first delay [L0149.005, L0266.004], and the Source E path 2 pain email also waits 4 hours [L0567.005]. The Source E rule says to avoid 4-hour intervals [L0261.006]. The Source E test data show 8.1 orders per 100 abandons with a 20-minute delay and 3.7 with a 4-hour delay [L0261.001]. Use the Source E evidence where the 20-minute start is used; the 4-hour delay remains the Source A recommendation for the first email [L0149.005].
  • Scheduled send time: in Klaviyo, use Time Delay with Wait until specific time to send at a consistent time in the customer's timezone (for example 7 a.m.). This prevents emails sent at inconvenient hours [L0261.010].
  • Example from the same source: wait 13 hours to reach an 8 p.m. send time, which creates midnight expiration urgency [L0261.010].
  • Discount offer email at 7:00 am in the sequence [L0567.007].
  • Source C: the 48-hour email is optional [L0607.030].

Expiry Windows

  • Source E: the abandoned checkout flow has a 72-hour window. After that window, the product is returned to the shelf and the flow ends [L0494.015].
  • Source C: the cart discount should expire only after the final cart email. Send email 4 a few hours before that expiry. Derive the gaps between emails 1 to 3 from the expiry you set, because the cart copy does not fix them [L0606.038].
  • Source B: time-limited discounts should be labelled clearly as limited, here for 3 days, and reminders should show the remaining time, such as 24 hours and 4 hours [L1449.007].

Day-Based Countdown (Source A)

  • Abandoned-cart email countdown over seven days:
    1. Day 5: send an email saying there are 72 hours left [L1243.034].
    2. Day 6: send an email saying there are 48 hours left [L1243.034].
    3. Day 7: send three emails within the day, at 12 hours, 6 hours and 1 hour left [L1243.034].
    4. Make clear in the final message that this is the last and best offer [L1243.034].

  • A cart-abandonment flow is a series of emails, not a single reminder. Start with a short first reminder built from a fill-in-the-blank template, then plan the second and third emails using the same principles [L0572.022].
  • Source E: each of the first three abandoned-cart emails should have exactly one objective, which is returning the buyer to checkout to complete the order. Remove additional calls to action such as social media icons, app download prompts or other links that distract from the purchase [L1208.014].
  • Source C: each email in the abandoned checkout sequence should answer one of the four reasons customers abandon (offer, price, lack of trust, uncertainty): product value first, social proof second, and a discount last [L0604.004].
  • Use the same sequence logic for every abandoned-cart follow-up [L1208.017].
  • Keep non-purchase prompts such as social media follow requests, app download invitations and other promotions out of the abandoned-cart sequence. Put those messages into separate flows or manual campaigns so the buyer has only one reason to return to checkout [L1208.022].

Full Sequence Procedures

Source C four-email abandoned checkout flow (in this order):

  1. Email 1, about 10 to 15 minutes after checkout is abandoned (at least 10 minutes, since entering card details takes 5-7 minutes; 20 minutes can also be tested). Use a benefit-led headline, not a salesy one. Describe the customer's pain point and how they will feel after getting the product, add 3-4 bullet points on why they need it, and end with a benefit-driven call to action such as "get yours today" or "make your partner happy by ordering now" [L0605.020].
  2. Email 2, about 2 hours after email 1. Use a subject line that says the order is not yet complete. Add 3-4 five-star reviews with images, a link back to the order, and urgency such as limited stock (for example only 50 pieces remaining), a price increase after 4-5 days, or "while stock lasts" [L0605.020].
  3. Email 3, about 6 hours after email 2. Use a new subject line that announces an exclusive deal with an extra 10% off. Repeat the benefit points, reviews, headline and bullet points, and restate the same offer [L0605.020].
  4. Final email: say the deal is about to end, give a final confirmation and state that no further emails will follow [L0605.020].

Source C three-email abandoned checkout sequence:

  1. First email: explain the benefits of the product, address the customer's pain point or current situation, and explain why this product fits them. Show the product with 3-4 bold headline points and a call to action [L0604.003].
  2. Second email: show image reviews and testimonials [L0604.003].
  3. Third email: make a better offer with an extra 10-20% off [L0604.003, L0604.005].

Source B abandoned-cart sequence (older advice, 2021-22):

  1. Reminder email immediately after abandonment, containing the product and a link back to checkout [L1449.002].
  2. Second reminder email 6 hours after the first, same type (product plus checkout link) [L1449.002].
  3. Personal welcome-style email from the business owner 18 hours after the second reminder, asking whether you can help and inviting a reply, without pushing the product [L1449.002].
  4. Discount offer 24 hours later, limited to 3 days, with the product, a checkout link and the discount code [L1449.002].
  5. After 2 days without purchase, send either a helpful content email (such as a buyer's guide, if the product range is broad) or a discount reminder if the range is narrow, stating 24 hours remain to finish the purchase [L1449.002].
  6. 24 hours later, send a final discount reminder stating about 4 hours remain before the discount expires [L1449.002].
  7. Hand the contact over to the 30-day master flow for prospects so they keep receiving emails [L1449.002].

Source A Shopify template sequence (overview):

  • The pre-built template automatically includes the customer's abandoned products in the first email and uses the color scheme configured in the Messaging app settings [L0282.008].
  • The pre-built template is effective because Shopify designed it using performance data. Keeping it unchanged is a safe choice; customize only if you have specific improvements beyond the default version [L0282.015].

Desire vs Pain Messaging

  • Design the abandoned cart flow using desire messaging (what the customer wants to gain or feel) versus pain messaging (the biggest problem the product solves). Test both with a 50/50 split to find which resonates with your audience, because effectiveness varies by product category and niche [L0261.011].
  • Desire messaging outperformed pain messaging for the initial abandoned cart email in the same Source E test. Desire path A reached 83.8% open, 16.2% click and 8.1% order; pain path B reached 82.4% open, 5.9% click and 2.9% order [L0261.016].
  • Find the pain point by asking what goes wrong during the product's hardest real use, such as long sessions in heat or a full working day. Name that exact situation in the email so the reader recognises their own experience [L0567.045].
  • Pain-based example for bike gear: the subject line "Saddle Sores? Say No More!" targets the specific objection (uncomfortable clothing causing soreness) rather than generic benefits. It overcomes the biggest barrier to purchase in that product category [L0261.014].
  • Objections are usually offer, price, lack of trust or uncertainty. Address each in its own email [L0604.004].
  • Trust-based messaging: focus on trust, objection handling and social proof, not on introducing the brand [L1208.017, L1208.003].
  • Source E offer email: tease what waits in the cart without naming the product. This creates curiosity and gets opens [L0567.010].
  • Short versus story-driven pain copy after the 23-hour wait: see the benchmark results below [L0261.017].
  • Reminders must not suggest the customer forgot something, because that implies the item was not valuable to them. Use assumptive wording such as saying something went wrong at checkout instead [L1208.004].

Discount Rules

  • Holding the discount back until the second email protects margin from shoppers who would have bought anyway. Only the people who still hesitate receive the offer [L0606.018].
  • The most common abandoned-cart mistake is offering a discount immediately. Start with a plain reminder and a checkout link, because many people abandon carts for non-price reasons such as distraction, connection loss or interruptions [L1449.003].
  • Lead with value and the product before any discount, so the offer does not come first [L0567.001].
  • Discounts should be strategic and paired with something of higher value [L0575.027].
  • Use only the offer that is cheapest for your margin: free shipping first, then 10% off if free shipping costs more [L1243.046].
  • For carts over $100, mention free shipping instead of one of the SMS value propositions [L1208.019].
  • Source C discount progression: none in email 1, then $5, then $10 across the sequence. The discount increases across the sequence [L0476.016].
  • Time-limited offers should be labelled clearly as limited, here for 3 days, and reminders should show the remaining time, such as 24 hours and 4 hours [L1449.007].
  • Source C: the first email can carry no discount, the second a small code, and the third the largest code, with its deadline stated [L0634.010].

Conditions, Splits and Filters

Send Conditions

  • Second email conditions (Source A): the customer must not have placed an order since abandonment, and they must not have started a new checkout [L0149.007].
  • First email conditions (Shopify template): the first email sends only if both are true, the customer has not placed an order since the workflow started, and at least one product from the abandoned cart is in stock [L0282.007].
  • Second email conditions (Shopify template): the second email sends only if the customer has no order since abandonment and more than 24 hours have passed since their last abandoned checkout, preventing duplicate emails from multiple abandoned carts [L0282.011].
  • Purchase exclusion: if a customer completes their purchase before a delay timer expires, they are automatically excluded from all remaining email steps [L0282.009].
  • New checkout during a running automation: if a customer initiates a new checkout before the first abandoned checkout automation completes, that automation cancels without sending emails. If the new checkout is also abandoned, the customer enters a fresh abandoned checkout automation for the new session, enabling re-entry without duplicate emails [L0266.016].
  • Second email spend filter: send the second abandoned checkout email only to customers who have previously spent over $0.00, filtering out brand new customers who may not be ready for follow-up offers [L0156.005].
  • Conflict on the spend filter: the Shopify workflow sets the Amount Spent condition to "is equal to $0" (Customer > Amount Spent) in its condition step and again in its review step [L0266.015]. The spend filter described in L0156.005 sends to customers who spent over $0.00. The sources do not explain which applies in which case, so confirm the condition in Shopify before relying on either.
  • Branch by cart size (Source E): if the cart is over $100, mention free shipping in the SMS; if under the threshold, add free shipping or add-on suggestions in email four [L1208.019].
  • Branch by product range (Source B): after 2 days without purchase, send a helpful content email (such as a buyer's guide) if the product range is broad, or a discount reminder if the range is narrow [L1449.002].

Splits

  • Source E 50/50 split: send the first email with a 50/50 A/B test. Path A uses desire messaging; path B uses pain messaging [L0261.002].
  • Klaviyo setup for the split: use a Conditional Split with the condition 'Is part of a 50% random sample' to divide shoppers into two paths for the first email test. The live split was about 51% and 49% [L0567.003].
  • Path 1 (desire/objection email #1A): sent 20 minutes after checkout starts [L0567.004].
  • Path 2 (pain-point email #1B): waits 4 hours after checkout starts before it is sent [L0567.005].
  • Conditional split for reminders: after the first email, use a conditional split to check 'Has Opened Email zero times since starting this flow.' If unopened, send a 45-minute reminder with the same subject line. If opened, let the customer continue to the next step without a redundant email [L0261.018].
  • Source E test step: after the 23-hour wait, test short direct pain copy against story-driven pain copy [L0261.002, L0261.017].

Copy and Subject Formulas

Subject-Line Formulas

  • Formula for a cart-recovery subject line: suggest that something may have gone wrong at checkout, then promise a direct link back to the cart. The question-style opener lowers pressure and reduces the sense of being chased [L1243.026].
    • Example 1: "Did something go wrong at checkout? Here is your cart: {link}" [L1243.026]
    • Example 2: "Quick question about your order, {first name}. Your cart is saved here: {link}" [L1243.026]
  • Pattern for the last-chance subject line: put the exact hours left first, then the offer and its expiry, for example a plain countdown plus discount. Examples of the author's own: "2 hours left: your 10% code ends tonight" and "Last call: your saved bag closes in 3 hours" [L0567.009].
  • Pattern for the offer email subject: tease what waits in the cart without naming the product, which creates curiosity and gets opens. Examples of the author's own: "Something good is still sitting in your basket" and "Your favourite pick is still on hold" [L0567.010].
  • Formula for the Source C email 1 subject line in the Clavio setup: a friendly reminder, with the first email written as a reminder that the cart is waiting [L0476.011]. Example of the same tone: "Still thinking it over? Your cart is saved for a few more hours." [L0476.015]
  • Example for the second email: "Stock on your picks is running low. Here is 10 dollars off for the next hour." [L0476.015]
  • Subject-line examples for a benefit-led first email: none specified beyond the template; set the subject to include the product name [L1297.020].
  • Discount offer example subject (Source E test): "You Won't Believe What's Waiting in Your Cart" with a 15% discount [L0261.008].
  • Discount reminder example subject (Source E scarcity test): "4 Hours Left: 15% Off Expires Soon" [L0261.015, L0567.038].
  • Default subject line for the first abandoned checkout reminder email: "You left items at checkout" [L0266.010].
  • The Source E sender name uses the brand and a monitored support address, and the subject "RE: About your order" makes the email look like a reply in the inbox [L0567.006].
  • Source C third email subject: "your cart is still waiting for you and so is our discount" [L1297.020].
  • Day 3 to 4 email subject (Source A): "Your {product name} is still waiting for you" [L1243.046].
  • Older advice (2019): "Did you forget something?" was used as the first abandoned-cart subject line [L1086.045]. Source E advises against wording reminders as if the customer forgot something [L1208.004], so use the newer wording.
  • Source C second-email subject: say the order is not yet complete [L0605.020].
  • Source C third-email subject: announce an exclusive deal with an extra 10% off [L0605.020].

Copy Rules

  • One strong headline is enough in the first abandoned cart email. A sub-headline can be dropped. Testing in this store showed the extra line added nothing, so remove it unless a split test proves otherwise [L0567.044].
  • Repeat the real deadline in the P.S. line of an offer email, because many readers scroll to the end. Keep it consistent with the countdown and the code expiry time [L0567.046].
  • Sign the offer and last-chance emails with the founders' first names to add a personal touch. The sign-off should match the brand voice used across the sequence [L0567.047].
  • Each abandoned-cart email should contain a direct button to the checkout link, so the customer returns to the exact cart with one click. Copy the same link into each email in the sequence [L0634.039].
  • Text-based abandoned-cart emails with some images and plenty of personal text tend to convert better, because they feel personal and land in the primary inbox more often [L0607.032, L0573.023].
  • The personal welcome email in the cart sequence should ask a direct question and invite a reply, rather than repeating the product. Its goal is to uncover objections such as an unresolved question or hesitation about a competitor, and a reply can lead to a personal conversation that moves the sale forward [L1449.005].
  • Personal message from the store owner: frame the second follow-up email as a personal message from the store owner offering direct help with questions. Invite the customer to reply, positioning it as one-on-one conversation rather than automated broadcast, to increase engagement and encourage direct response [L0149.008].
  • Insert the abandoned checkout products block into the second reminder email so recipients see exactly which items they left behind, rather than generic product suggestions or inspirational content [L0266.013].
  • Last-chance email build order (Source E):
    1. Copy the layout of the offer email [L0567.038].
    2. Change the subject to a countdown such as "4 Hours Left: 15% Off Expires Soon" [L0567.038].
    3. Update the headline to say time is almost up and the discount ends [L0567.038].
    4. Insert the product block of the items left behind between the headline and the CTA [L0567.038].
    5. Add the final warning and the CTA to complete the order with the discount applied [L0567.038].
  • Source C: the first email's copy as a reminder, with a product benefit or guarantee and a link back to the store; keep no discount in this first message [L0476.011].
  • Source E: abandoned-cart copy should be benefit-led and describe the customer's pain point and how they will feel after getting the product [L0605.020].
  • Use the Shopify AI tool to refine the first email template and improve its messaging [L0156.006].
  • For the first abandoned checkout email, Shopify's Generate Text tool can be used with a description (for example "You left items at checkout"), a tone (for example playful) and a choice of suggested subject line [L0266.015].

Tracking and Mistakes

Tracking and Go-Live Checks

  • Source C: for each email, set Smart sending to off, UTM tracking to on, and switch from Draft to Live [L0476.011, L0634.010, L1297.020].
  • Source C: set every email in the sequence to Live after cloning [L0573.022, L0607.028].
  • Shopify Admin: click Apply Changes and confirm to make the changes effective immediately [L0266.015, L0282.013].
  • Shopify status check: confirm the button shows 'Turn off workflow' to verify the automation is active [L0149.009, L0282.016].
  • Shopify Messaging: the automation now runs indefinitely once active [L0149.009].
  • Check each timer before going live [L0476.027].

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving the default timers in place for welcome and abandoned cart flows means messages arrive too late or too late for the offer. Check each timer before going live [L0476.027].
  • Customizing the Shopify template without a specific improvement adds risk, because the pre-built template is built on performance data [L0282.015].
  • Offering a discount in the first message is the most common mistake; start with a plain reminder and a checkout link [L1449.003].
  • Stopping all emails after the abandoned-cart flow ends is a common error. Contacts who did not buy should move into the 30-day prospect master flow rather than being dropped, because they may still be in the market [L1449.004].
  • Do not word reminders as if the customer forgot something, because that implies the item was not valuable to them [L1208.004].
  • Do not restart the shopping journey in the early abandoned-cart messages by suggesting other products or new options. Doing so confuses a buyer who was ready to complete the purchase [L1208.005].
  • Do not add extra calls to action (social media icons, app download prompts or other links) to the first three abandoned-cart emails [L1208.014, L1208.022].
  • Do not add a second headline or sub-headline to the first email unless a split test proves it helps [L0567.044].
  • Do not send a last-chance email with a deadline that conflicts with the countdown or the code expiry time; keep the P.S. line consistent with both [L0567.046].
  • Do not keep a 4-hour first interval as a fixed rule for all emails; the Source E guidance avoids arbitrary 4-hour intervals that hit customers at inconvenient times [L0261.006].
  • Do not treat the abandoned cart flow as a one-off reminder; it is a series [L0572.022].
  • Do not leave an automation running with an expired discount; the discount should expire only after the final email, with email 4 sent a few hours before that expiry [L0606.038].
  • Do not send SMS to customers who did not opt in; SMS cannot recover their carts [L1347.030].
  • Do not use the abandoned-cart sequence to deliver top-of-funnel content or broad brand introductions [L1208.017].

Scope and Standard

  • Use an abandoned checkout flow with more than one email, not a single reminder. A typical flow runs five to seven emails spread across five to seven days [L1522.027].
  • If a store already runs an abandoned-cart flow, review it against the standard sequence: reminders, a personal owner email, a time-limited discount and a final reminder. Add only the missing emails rather than rebuilding the whole flow [L1449.013].
  • All advice in this section is older advice from Source B's 2021-22 course [L1449.008, L1449.009, L1449.010, L1449.011, L1449.012, L1449.013, L1449.014, L1449.015, L1522.027].

Why Emails Are Spaced

  • Abandoned-cart emails are spaced with waiting periods because many people do not check email right away [L1449.011].
  • Each step gives the shopper time to act before the next message is sent [L1449.011].

Sequence Timeline

The following schedule is derived from the moment of abandonment [L1449.012]:

  1. 0 hours: first reminder with product and checkout link [L1449.012].
  2. 6 hours: second reminder of the same type [L1449.012, L1449.015].
  3. 24 hours: personal owner email asking whether you can help and inviting a reply [L1449.012, L1449.008].
  4. 48 hours: time-limited discount offer for 3 days [L1449.012, L1449.014].
  5. 96 hours: either a helpful content email (broad product range) or a discount reminder with 24 hours left (narrow product range) [L1449.012, L1449.010].
  6. 120 hours: final discount reminder with about 4 hours left [L1449.012, L1449.009].
  7. After the final reminder, move the contact to the 30-day prospect master flow [L1449.012].

Discount Timing

  • The discount is offered at 48 hours and runs for 3 days [L1449.012, L1449.014].
  • The first two reminders contain no discount [L1449.015, L1449.012].
  • The narrow-range reminder is sent at 96 hours, when 24 hours are left [L1449.012, L1449.010].
  • The final reminder is sent at 120 hours, with about 4 hours left [L1449.012, L1449.009].
  • The source values do not fully align, so check them before building:
    • The 96-hour send matches the "24 hours left" wording, since the discount ends at 120 hours [L1449.012, L1449.010].
    • The wording "sent 24 hours after the discount offer" does not match the 96-hour timing. The offer is at 48 hours, so 24 hours after it would be 72 hours [L1449.010, L1449.012, L1449.014].
    • The 3-day discount from 48 hours ends at 120 hours. The final reminder at 120 hours is described as "about 4 hours left", which implies expiry near 124 hours [L1449.012, L1449.009, L1449.014].

Conditions and Splits

  • Product range decides the 96-hour email. A broad range receives a helpful content email. A narrow range receives a discount reminder with 24 hours left [L1449.012].
  • After the final reminder, the contact moves to the 30-day prospect master flow [L1449.012].

Personalisation Fields

  • Templates use these fields: {first_name}, {product_name}, {checkout_link}, {discount_code}, {expiry_date} and {business_name} [L1449.014, L1449.015].
