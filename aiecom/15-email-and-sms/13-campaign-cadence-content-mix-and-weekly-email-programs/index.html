15.13 Campaign cadence, content mix and weekly email programs

Sending Frequency by Lifecycle Phase

Automated sequences for new subscribers (Source B, 2021-22, older advice)

  • There is no fixed industry rule for email frequency. Use a hybrid approach: send many emails when a subscriber is new and very interested, then reduce the volume as their interest cools. [L0603.001]
  • A new subscriber's interest peaks right after they join or make a first purchase. That window is the best time to sell or sell again. Interest then declines, so the attention and offers needed to keep the person engaged shrink. [L0603.002]
  • On joining, the email goals are to build trust, establish authority in the niche, and drive a first sale, an upsell, or a repeat purchase. Once trust and authority are built, far less effort is needed to maintain them. [L0603.003]
  • Run each phase as its own automated sequence. [L0603.004]
Phase Timing Automated emails per week Total in phase
Hot phase Days 0-30 4 16
Cooling phase Days 30-60 2-3 -
Long-term phase Day 60 onward 1 -

[L0603.004]

  • Hot phase (days 0-30): Send 4 automated emails per week, 16 in total. Engagement typically drops after day 30, so the chance of a sale falls for a while if nothing is sent or bought. [L0603.005]
  • Cooling phase (days 30-60): Reduce to 2 or 3 automated emails per week, using a separate sequence for this phase. [L0603.006]
  • Long-term phase (day 60 onward): Send 1 automated email per week. [L0603.006]
  • In the first 30 days, a subscriber receives 5 emails per week in total: 4 automated emails plus 1 blast email. [L0603.008]
  • While a subscriber is in the hot phase, more offers can be placed in front of them because they are most receptive. As interest cools, offer frequency should drop along with overall email volume. [L0603.024]

Blast emails

  • Blast emails (one-off sends for a new blog post, news, a new product or collection, or a sale such as Black Friday) are separate from automated emails. [L0603.007]
  • On average, send about 1 blast email per week. Send more during big sale events and fewer in quiet periods. [L0603.007]
  • Blasts usually go to the whole list regardless of lifecycle stage, unless they are aimed at a specific segment. [L0603.008]

Touch points and persistence (Source B, 2021-22, older advice)

  • Plan for about 7 touch points before a typical person buys, so more emails create more chances to sell. Not every email has to be a sales email, but each should be a chance to sell or to deliver value that leads toward a sale. [L0603.009]
  • Most sales happen after the 5th, 6th, or 7th follow-up rather than the first few contacts, so people often need to see a brand several times before buying. If the emails provide relevant value, err on the side of sending more rather than fewer. [L0603.010]
  • Brands that consistently have genuinely useful content can email subscribers every day without harm, as long as unsubscribe and open-rate figures stay stable. Judge frequency by list health numbers rather than a fixed rule of thumb. [L0603.023]
  • Content created for the website, organic social, and retargeting can be reused in emails, which keeps frequency sustainable. Each email can deliver a useful piece from a growing content library. [L0603.011]

Long nurture series

  • Long nurture series can be set to send daily for two months or a full year. A daily cadence is optional, not required for the flow to work. Choose the frequency based on how much the subscriber gains from each message. [L0572.023]

  • Daily sending and revenue: In one case, moving from one email per month to two per month more than doubled income. Daily emails were later found to be most profitable. A business that does not email its list daily can lose money every day. [L0523.127]

Content and Promotion Mix

Split between brand and selling content (Source E 2025-26)

  • Split email content roughly 30/70. About 30% is brand-building, educational, and informative content that also entertains (infotainment), to nurture the audience and add value. About 70% is direct selling content. [L0475.002]
  • Send the 30% brand-building content to every email segment, including recent buyers, because it builds the relationship without asking for a sale. [L0475.003]
  • Do not send the 70% direct selling content to recent buyers, because they are not ready to buy again yet. [L0475.004]
  • Quality comes from following the weekly mix. Brand-building, educating, entertaining, and nurturing keep building brand value and keep content fresh enough to open and click. The Problem-Solve format can always be used, because customers always have problems to solve. [L0475.015]

Example content mix (Source A 2020, older advice)

  • Each email can combine four elements:
    • A link to a new blog post or content.
    • Niche-relevant news.
    • A product promotion, for example "buy this week save 2%".
    • A customer engagement request, for example "send us a photo of your product in your home". [L0245.040]
  • Target email frequency is two emails per week, mixing value content (blog links, niche news) with promotional offers. [L0245.039]

Offers and content per week

  • Release one promotional offer per week throughout the year via email to your lead list, to maintain engagement and convert prospects into customers. [L0240.004]
  • Plan at least one relevant offer or piece of valuable content per week for every lead list, and ideally two. Waiting until the next holiday to email a new subscriber lets them forget the brand. [L1076.017]
  • Broadcast offers to the list keep working for as long as subscribers remain on the list, but response rates are lower than the first follow-up emails after a purchase. Send them when a relevant new offer appears or during big sales seasons. [L0923.035]
  • The job of email is to sell more of what already sells to people who do not yet own it. Top-selling products should therefore be the core of regular campaigns, including upsell pushes. [L0494.010]

Content as a library

  • Every new piece of content should be added to the email program. A library of useful content gives the list something to read besides offers and promotions. Without content, email has little to say beyond product pitches, while content keeps subscribers engaged and warm between sales. [L0385.026]
  • Email marketing is very hard to make effective without content. A subscriber list needs more than product offers to stay engaged. [L0500.008]
  • Send interview or video content to the email list to add backstory, which can convince buyers who are still undecided. Email is for warming people already on the list. [L0941.078]
  • A free weekly design sent by email keeps customers engaged while they wait for a physical product to ship. The emails also help the seller reach an engaged audience with high open rates and promote future offers. [L1153.065]

Building and maintaining the list

  • Build and maintain the list in this order:
    1. Offer incentives (coupons, free accessories, giveaways) to collect emails.
    2. Send 2+ promotional emails per week, separate from email funnels.
    3. Include new blog posts, product announcements, promotions, and industry news.
    4. Monitor open rates and reply engagement.
    5. Periodically test affiliate partnership offers to monetize the list further. [L0226.031]
  • Email marketing sends promotions to a broadcast list of subscribers to bring them back to buy again. Klaviyo can be used to build and manage this list. [L0597.012]

Weekly Email Templates (Source E 2025-26)

What's Hot Weekly (WHW)

What's Hot Weekly (WHW) email layout, in this order:
1. Large hero image at the top.
2. Six-product block headed by the trending products of the last 7 days, showing 6 products.
3. Three customer testimonials.
4. Shop bestsellers section at the bottom.
Keep this layout as one reusable template and change only the products each week.

[L0475.007]

  • Send the What's Hot Weekly email every Friday. Build it once as a template and resend it each Friday by swapping the products, which makes it easy to automate. [L0475.008]
  • The six-product featured block can hold any of these:
    • The top six products by Shopify sales over the last 7 days.
    • The top six most-searched products.
    • Six products chosen manually.
    • A mix of these.
    • It can also feature a bundle or a deal of the week or month. [L0475.009]
  • For a What's Hot Weekly campaign, place the selected products in the featured section at the top of the store, then move the other sections further down the page. The featured block should match the products in the email, so visitors who click through see the same offer. [L0475.016]

Hot Tips (brand-building, part of the 30% share)

Hot Tips email, a brand-building email (part of the 30% share): a headline announcing three tips, then three short numbered tips on a topic your customers care about, each with a brief explanation of how to apply it. Keep it free of a sales push.

[L0475.011]

Problem-Solve (direct selling, part of the 70% share)

  1. Name one specific problem your target customer has.
  2. Agitate it by showing the cost or frustration of leaving the problem unsolved.
  3. Present your product as the solution to that problem. [L0475.012]

Weekly schedule for 3 emails

  1. Monday or Tuesday: Problem-Solve email.
  2. Wednesday or Thursday: Hot Tips email.
  3. Friday: What's Hot Weekly email.
  4. Optional: resend an email on Saturday or Sunday, but only with changed content. [L0475.013]

Repeats and spam risk

  • Never resend the same content unchanged. When content matches what was recently sent, Google can route the email to spam. Change the content each time an email is repeated. [L0475.014]

Campaign planning workflow (Source E 2025-26)

  • The campaign calendar is built in Google Sheets with Claude Cowork and the /email-strat-plan skill, using brand-kit.json and the audience segments as inputs. The output is a 30-day calendar with 4 campaigns, which are then fed into Klaviyo. [L1361.026]

Newsletters and Content-Promotion Emails

Publish first, then email

  • Publish the article first, then send the email to the list, so readers land on a live page. Repeat this cycle with every new keyword and keep a steady weekly rhythm. [L0380.029]

Article email formula (plain text, 4 to 6 sentences)

  • Structure: open with a question or teaser, link to the article, repeat the same link once more, and end with a call to action such as checking out a new blog post.
  • It works because a plain text message reads like a personal note, and short sentences move the reader quickly to the link. [L0380.030]
  • Examples:
    • "Still unsure which hosting service fits a new podcast? I compared seven of them this week on price and ease of use. Read the full comparison here: [link]. Check the ranking and tell me which one you would pick. [link]"
    • "Still not sure a budget blender can handle frozen fruit? I tested four models for a month and noted what held up and what broke. See the results here: [link]. Let me know which one you would try first. [link]" [L0380.030]

P.S. line

  • End the article email with a P.S. line that links to a second, related page, such as a product review. The main call to action stays the same. Readers who want more on the topic get one extra path toward a purchase, without a second competing message.
  • Keep the P.S. to one sentence and one link. [L0380.034]

Share requests

  1. When a new piece of content is published, send it to your entire email list.
  2. Include past buyers, people who opted in through promotions on your site, exit-intent overlay sign-ups, and newsletter subscribers.
  3. Ask every recipient to share the content with someone who would benefit from it. [L0899.003]
  • Promotion emails should ask readers to share the content on Facebook, on X or Twitter, by forwarding it by email, or by embedding it on their own sites. The goal is for readers to consume the content and then pass it on, not just leave the site. [L0899.005]
  • Sending content only once and not asking readers to share wastes most of the promotion potential. Content that is not worth sharing will not keep visitors on the page, so make it share-worthy first. [L0899.006]

Reactive and Daily Story Emails

Reactive campaigns

  • Campaigns are regular emails that are reactive, seasonal, trending, and relevant. Reactive marketing means turning news or trends in your target market into brand content quickly, sometimes the next day. [L0494.011]
  • Reactive campaign formula: [relevant event] + [your product or angle] + [same-day or next-day send].
  • When a news event, trend, or season hits your target market, send a campaign that ties it to your product within about a day. It works because the audience already pays attention to the event, so the email feels timely rather than like a generic promotion. [L0494.018]
  • Examples:
    • Running shoes: a record marathon time makes headlines, so the next morning's email recommends a beginner shoe for people inspired to start running, with a 48-hour offer.
    • Coffee brand: a cold snap is forecast for the weekend, so the email promotes a hot-drink bundle sent Friday morning. [L0494.018]

Daily story emails (Seinfeld-style)

  • After the soap sequence, Seinfeld-style emails are sent daily as broadcasts. They are usually entertaining stories from everyday life rather than pure content. Subscribers who finished the soap sequence move to a broadcast list where they receive that day's email. These emails are not part of a fixed auto-responder sequence. [L0523.125]
  • Soap sequence emails run as an auto-responder with a fixed order. Daily Seinfeld emails are broadcasts that can be scheduled ahead but are usually tied to real events in the character's life. Keep the two systems separate in your email provider. [L0523.132]
  • Shift the emails to roughly 90% entertainment and 10% content. In one case this increased opens, clicks, and sales significantly compared with a content-heavy approach. Entertainment is what keeps subscribers reading after they have bonded with the character. [L0523.126]
  • Brainstorm topics by asking:
    1. What is happening in the character's life right now?
    2. What recently happened that was embarrassing?
    3. How are you getting through the holiday season?
    4. Where are you planning your next vacation?
    5. What did you recently buy that you regret?
    6. What did you recently buy that you adore?
    7. What made you furious yesterday that you laugh about today?
    8. What did a child or pet get into yesterday?
    9. What funny past event taught a lesson? [L0523.128]
  • Every story must lead back to an offer, which can be your core product, another product of yours, or a partner's product. Entertaining emails that never connect to an offer will not earn money, however well they are written. The tie-in is what turns storytelling into sales. [L0523.129]
  • Pattern for Seinfeld emails: a short everyday story, a turn that exposes a lesson or mistake, then a natural bridge to an offer. The subject line creates curiosity or a clear hook. The story keeps subscribers entertained every day, and the link makes every send commercially relevant. [L0523.130]
  • Examples:
Example 1, subject '[True Story] I nearly lost a client over one spreadsheet'. Body: 'Yesterday a client called to say my report was wrong. I checked, and it was. The mistake came from a template I had trusted for years. That is why I now use the checklist in this guide: [link].'

Example 2, subject 'My dog ate the invoice (and what it taught me)'. Body: 'The dog chewed through the paper invoice this morning, which made me laugh. It also reminded me that I still send mine by post. The digital invoice tool I switched to last month is here: [link].'

[L0523.130]

  • Copy the daily entertaining email onto your blog to create consistent content that leads readers toward a sale. The same story serves two purposes without extra writing. This is a quick way to keep the blog active. [L0523.131]

Product Launch, Giveaway and Event Campaigns

Campaign definition and ideas

  • Campaigns are one-time email blasts sent to a list or segment for events, launches, and seasonal offers, such as Valentine's Day, Father's Day, Mother's Day, Christmas, a new product launch, or early access for VIP customers. Unlike flows, they are not triggered by a visitor action. They are sent to an existing audience on a set date. [L0573.026, L0607.044]
  • Campaign ideas for a store with a consistent seller: launches, offer launches, event launches (occasions such as Mother's Day or Valentine's Day), new product launches, specific collection promotions, bundle offers, giveaways and referral campaigns, and a weekly deal. Each should target a defined segment such as buyers, non-buyers, or VIPs. [L0605.049]
  • Run 3-4 email campaigns a month from this list: offer launch, event launch, new product launch, specific collection promotion, giveaways, and deal of the week. [L0604.012]

New product and category announcements

  • New product categories can be announced by email and SMS to existing customers, including the launch date and key features. Repeat purchases from these customers need no ad spend, so the sale is close to pure profit because the acquisition cost was already paid. Good customer service is a precondition for this to work. [L0434.007, L0435.006]
  • Once you have a good customer email list, you can announce new products through an email campaign instead of paying for ads. This makes an existing customer base a low-cost channel for new product launches. [L1172.052]
  • Email your customer list when you launch new products or promotions, such as a new private-label brand, because email drives traffic to products that Google Shopping rarely reaches. (Source A 2021, older advice) [L0968.068]

New product launch sequence

  1. One week before launch, send a VIP sign-up email announcing the upcoming product with its features and images.
  2. Offer VIP sign-ups an extra discount on launch, for example 30% off an $80 product.
  3. Send three to four emails over the one-week period.
  4. Count the sign-ups to estimate how many purchases to expect (for example 200 sign-ups out of 1,000 non-buyers).
  5. Send the next email to the people who signed up, and a separate email to those who did not, asking why and offering an extra benefit point. [L0605.024]
  • Campaigns should warm up the audience before asking for the purchase: educate first, then announce the launch, then ask for the sign-up. A direct buy request in the first email is too aggressive. [L0605.026]

Weekly deal

  • Weekly deal campaigns have a comparatively low conversion rate, but they keep existing customers warm and prevent them from forgetting the brand after the first purchase. Combine a benefit of a new product with a small extra discount each week. [L0605.028]
  • A weekly 10% off deal is part of a monthly structure of about four campaigns, plus one new product launch campaign per month. [L1213.056]

Referral giveaway

  1. Run the giveaway on social media, asking followers to tag three friends and post to their stories.
  2. In the email, ask the customer to refer three people using a referral or affiliate link.
  3. Reward the referrer when a referred contact signs up with an email or phone number.
  4. Add a larger reward when a referred contact buys. [L0605.050]

Event teaser

  • Add an event teaser at the bottom of a regular content email:
PS: [Event name] is coming. [Link to signup page]
  • The linked page should ask only for an email address to qualify interested buyers. [L0334.040]

  1. Open Campaigns and click Create campaign. [L0607.045]
  2. Enter a campaign name, for example the event and year, and set the start date (for example 15 January for a Valentine's Day push). Click Save and continue. [L0573.027, L0607.045]
  3. Select the recipients: the Newsletter list or a segment such as VIP customers. Choose whether to exclude any groups. Choose whether to use tracking, and turn tracking on. [L0573.027, L0607.045]
  4. Click Continue to content. [L0573.027, L0607.045]
  5. Enter the subject line and preview text. [L0573.027, L0607.045]
  6. Pick a template under Popular or from the template library. For an event, choose the event template from the Popular library. If there is no event template, change a similar template and edit the event name. [L0573.027, L0607.045]
  7. Change the background color to white or your brand color, and add text, images, or a dynamic product section. [L0607.045]
  8. Click Next. [L0573.027, L0607.045]
  9. Turn Smart sending off. [L0573.027, L0573.028]
  10. Choose Schedule or Send, then select Gradual send over several hours. [L0573.027, L0607.045]
  11. Set the start time (for example 11 am Eastern) and the share of recipients per hour (for example 20 percent per hour). [L0573.027, L0607.045]
  12. Click Schedule. [L0573.027, L0607.045]
  • Gradual campaign sending: send to a set share of recipients per hour, for example 20 percent per hour, instead of pushing all emails at one time. Turn Smart sending off for campaigns too. [L0573.028]

Sponsored Blasts

  • Sponsored email blasts: you pay to use a partner's email list to send your message. The price usually rises with the number of emails you send. (Source B, 2021-22, older advice) [L0525.013]
  • A sponsorship does not have to be a single email. You can plan a series of emails with the partner that promote your products, drive visitors to your site, or point to your content. (Source B, 2021-22, older advice) [L0525.014]
