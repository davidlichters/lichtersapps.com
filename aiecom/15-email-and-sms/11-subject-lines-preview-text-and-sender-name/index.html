15.11 Subject lines, preview text and sender name

Competitor research

  • Competitor email research procedure:
    1. Sign up to many email lists from competing and non-competing businesses. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0569.005]
    2. Collect screenshots of their inbox subject lines, sender names, and send times. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0569.005]
    3. Note which subject lines catch your attention and why. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0569.005]
    4. Use those observations to shape your own subject lines. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0569.005]
  • Subscribing to the lists of entries in the matrix shows what content they send and how they talk to their audience. Use a separate Gmail account for this and check it about once a month for 20 to 30 minutes. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0478.023]

Purpose of the Subject Line

  • The subject line has one job: get the reader to notice the email and open it. It does not need to sell or convince, because the body copy handles persuasion and the call to action. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.001, L0569.003]
  • Open rates decide whether inbox providers flag your domain as spam. Low engagement can affect the deliverability of your entire list, not only of the people who did not open. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.001]
  • Subject lines work by combining attention, which makes the email stand out in crowded inboxes, and curiosity, which motivates the reader to open it. Both are essential. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.002]
  • A subject line must stand out in a crowded inbox and must be compelling, urgent, or curious. It can combine more than one of these qualities. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0569.004]

Writing Order

  • Write the subject line first, before the body copy. It is the first element a reader sees, so it determines whether the rest of the email gets read. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0569.023]

Subject Line Formulas

  • Question (older, Source B 2021-22):
    • Question lines trigger curiosity and fear of missing out. Common formats: 'Did you know this?', 'Do you know how to [benefit]?', 'Have you ever made this mistake?', 'Can you afford to miss out on this?' [L0198.003]
    • Keep them short and cryptic. The email body must deliver genuine value on what the subject line promises, or future open rates will fall. [L0198.003]
  • How-to (older, Source B 2021-22):
    • How-to lines appeal to the desire to gain skills and self-improve. Examples: 'How to pick the perfect beginner surfboard', 'How to improve your complexion', 'How to bake a cake in five easy steps.' [L0198.004]
    • They naturally boost engagement because everyone wants the proven method. They are particularly effective as top-of-funnel content that introduces prospects to your business. [L0198.004]
  • Scarcity and urgency (older, Source B 2021-22):
    • These lines pressure warm audiences who already want your products by creating fear of missing out on limited deals. Examples: 'Ends Midnight - 2 for 1 Wine Glasses', '50% Off Storewide till tomorrow 9pm', 'Free shipping on all orders (if you buy today).' [L0198.005]
    • Always include a precise deadline. [L0198.005]
    • Use only for genuine inventory limits, legitimately rare promotions, or real time constraints. [L0198.005]
  • Offer and urgency formula:
    • Formula: benefit (free shipping, a percentage off, a bonus) plus a time limit (ends tomorrow, last day, this weekend). [L0569.008]
    • Why it works: a benefit makes the email relevant to a buyer, and a deadline removes the option to postpone. [L0569.008]
    • Example one: 'Ends tonight: free shipping on every order', with preview text 'Your cart is waiting.' [L0569.008]
    • Example two: 'Last chance: 15 percent off this weekend only', with preview text 'Use it before Sunday midnight.' [L0569.008]
  • Declaration (older, Source B 2021-22):
    • Declaration lines spotlight new products or breaking announcements to capitalize on novelty. Examples: 'Introducing Luxo premium dog kennels', 'NEW: primo chandeliers (just $549 today)', 'Available now: Thrustmaster T-LCM racing pedals (free shipping).' [L0198.006]
    • Apply them to product launches, revived items with upgrades, refreshed versions, new content, videos, or downloadables. [L0198.006]
  • Number:
    • Numbers in subject lines significantly increase open rates compared with lines without them. Numbers add clarity, precision, and intrigue. Examples: '3 easy steps to choose a surfboard', 'The top 10 induction cooktops 2020', '5 ways that an e-bike will change you.' (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.007]
    • Saying 'the top 10' outperforms just 'the top.' (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.007]
    • Number formula: state a number and the keyword benefit, for example 'Five budget blenders for smoothies' or 'Three apps that schedule Instagram captions.' It works because a number signals a finite, scannable list and the keyword matches what the reader searched for. [L0380.031]
    • Keep the subject specific to the article it sends readers to. [L0380.031]
  • Curiosity (older, Source B 2021-22):
    • Curiosity lines withhold information to spark interest. They are structured like questions but are sometimes statements. Examples: 'The little known trick to paddling', 'The big mistake 99% of surfers make', 'You need to see this...' [L0198.008]
    • They succeed because readers worry they are missing knowledge or repeating a common error. [L0198.008]
    • Essential: deliver exactly what the line hints at, or trust and open rates are permanently damaged. [L0198.008]
    • Curiosity formula: a short quoted claim or attributed statement, followed by a secondary line that raises a question or promises an insider detail. [L0569.006]
    • Why it works: an unanswered question creates tension the reader wants to resolve. [L0569.006]
    • Example one: 'The one thing our returns team never tells customers', with preview text 'Ask us this before you buy.' [L0569.006]
    • Example two: 'Why we stopped stocking our bestseller', with preview text 'It wasn't what you think.' [L0569.006]
  • Personalization:
    • Personalized lines insert the recipient's first name alongside a question or curiosity angle. Examples: 'Jon, did you see this?', 'We've got a big surprise Jon', 'Jon, do you want to save 50%?' (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.009]
    • Names stand out because personalization is rare in bulk email. Klaviyo, Drip, and Mailchimp support this with shortcodes that auto-populate names. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.009]
    • Use sparingly to maintain impact. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.009]
    • Personalization formula: open with a first-name token, followed by an offer that reads naturally with the name. It works because a personal opening makes the message feel directed at the reader. [L0547.007]
    • Check that the tool's fallback works for subscribers without a stored name, so no empty gap or raw placeholder appears. [L0547.007]
    • Example in the same pattern: 'Jordan, your spring picks are now on sale'. [L0547.007]
  • Emoji:
    • Formula: a clear message plus one emoji matched to its meaning, not a string of emojis. [L0569.007]
    • Why it works: the emoji breaks up the visual pattern of plain text lines and signals the type of message before the reader opens it. [L0569.007]
    • Use an alarm clock for time pressure, a lightning bolt for speed, or a flash deal symbol. [L0569.007]
    • Example one: 'Flash sale ends at midnight ⏰', with preview text 'Up to 30 percent off our full range.' [L0569.007]
    • Example two: 'Your restock is here ⚡', with preview text 'Limited quantities, once they are gone they are gone.' [L0569.007]
  • Welcome (older, Source B 2021-22 is not the source; this is Source C):
    • Keep it short, name the gift or the welcome offer, and avoid heavy sales words so the email reads like a personal message. Examples: 'Your welcome discount is inside' and 'A thank-you gift for joining us.' [L0607.024]
  • Follow-up after a full presentation:
    • Formula: [specific payoff with an amount] that could [pay or deliver the benefit] [specific next timing]. [L1365.053]
    • Use it for a follow-up email sent after a reader has watched the full presentation. A concrete amount and timing signal a specific offer rather than a generic message. [L1365.053]
    • Why it works: specific numbers and a near-term timeframe create curiosity and make the benefit easy to picture. [L1365.053]
    • Example one: 'The Refund Check That Could Return $480 by Friday'. [L1365.053]
    • Example two: 'The Restock Alert That Could Save You $62 Before Tuesday'. [L1365.053]

Length

  • Keep subject lines as short as possible. They are truncated after a set number of characters on mobile devices, depending on the device and email client. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.010]
  • A cut-off subject line shows an incomplete message, so take the time to craft short, punchy subject lines. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.010]

Tone

  • Write subject lines conversationally, as if writing to a friend rather than a crowd. Use informal language and address a single person, not a group, even though many receive the email. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.011]
  • At any moment, one person is reading the email. Email is a personal communication tool, so keep that tone. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.011]

Spam Trigger Words

  • Avoid known spam trigger words that inbox providers (Gmail, Hotmail, Yahoo) maintain on spam lists as indicators of promotional or spam emails. Common triggers: money, make money, free, free inside. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.012]
  • Some autoresponders check for these words and warn you; others do not. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.012]
  • If you use spam words, emails land in spam folders undetected, causing mysteriously low open rates. Manually check your subject lines against spam word lists before sending. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.012]
  • Trigger words can send emails to the spam folder or the Gmail promotions tab. Check a current published list of known spam trigger words and keep it in mind while writing each subject line. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0569.010]
  • Trigger words matter less for e-commerce than for other business types, but they still need to be checked. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0569.010]

Capitalization and Emoji Tests

  • Test different capitalization styles: all lowercase, title case (first letter of each word capitalized), and variations. Testing reveals what stands out most in your audience's inbox. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.013]
  • Test emojis in subject lines selectively. They appear in color in inbox lists, which makes them visually stand out and increases the likelihood that recipients notice and read the line. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.014]
  • Do not use an emoji on every email. Varying your approach preserves the tactic's effectiveness. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.014]
  • Use emojis only when they are genuinely relevant to the email content. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.014]

Over-Use and Fake Urgency Pitfalls

  • Overusing any single subject line tactic, such as questions, scarcity, personalization, or emojis, diminishes its effectiveness. Recipients grow accustomed to it and open rates decline. Rotate among different tactics to maintain impact. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.015]
  • Constant daily sends or fake scarcity claims train subscribers to ignore these emails entirely. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.005]
  • Faking scarcity destroys audience trust. If you claim limited supply or deadlines that are not genuine, or if these recur constantly, audiences quickly recognize the false pattern and permanently ignore these emails. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.016]
  • Overpromising in curiosity or question subject lines erodes trust. If a line says 'The big mistake 99% of surfers make', the email must deliver a significant, widespread error, not a beginner-level or rare mistake. Overhyping without delivery damages credibility for future campaigns. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0198.017]

Preview Text

  • Email inboxes display a short second line next to the subject line, often called preview text or preheader. Use it as a companion to the subject line, adding a detail, a question, or a hint that supports the subject without repeating it. (older, Source B 2021-22) [L0569.024]
  • The subject line and pre-header work as a pair. The subject names the message (a gift, a reminder, a last chance), and the pre-header repeats the offer and the deadline in short form, so the inbox preview shows both the reason to open and the value. [L0606.016]
    • Example 1 for a 10% welcome offer: subject 'Your first order is waiting', pre-header '10% off your first order [valid 48 hours]'. [L0606.016]
    • Example 2 for a saved cart: subject 'Your cart is almost gone', pre-header 'Your saved items plus 10% off [today only]'. [L0606.016]
  • Reminder without a discount: playful, self-aware humor about the shopper hesitating, followed by the saved item and one plain reason to return, lowers the pressure of a reminder without a discount. [L0606.043]
    • Example 1: subject 'Your cart is still warm', pre-header 'Your picks are waiting, no pressure.' [L0606.043]
    • Example 2: subject 'Hard to say goodbye to that jacket', pre-header 'The jacket is still in your cart.' [L0606.043]
