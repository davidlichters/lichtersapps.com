15.6 Welcome series and new subscriber nurture

Welcome flow structure

  • The welcome series is the first automated sequence a new subscriber receives. It is triggered by a sign-up through the pop-up or footer newsletter form, for example a pop-up app such as Wheelio. [L0606.003, L0605.019]
  • In Clavio, the series is triggered when a visitor subscribes to the chosen list, such as Newsletter, through the sign-up pop-up. The first email goes out immediately, and later emails follow on set delays. [L0634.034]
  • In Klaviyo, the series starts automatically for each person who joins through a sign-up form, so the first email goes out right after subscription. [L0573.033]
  • The three-day structure of Source C:
    1. Day 1, welcome email, sent immediately after subscription. Keep it short and simple: thank the subscriber, explain that the newsletter keeps them updated with promotions and offers, and include the brand's social media links. Also send an SMS if budget allows. [L0307.003]
    2. Day 2, brand introduction email, sent 24 hours later. Introduce the brand and go deep on product benefits, technology, design, craftsmanship, and what differentiates you from competitors. For electronics, explain technology benefits. For jewelry, highlight design, designer experience, and handmade quality. For any product, explain why it is worth buying. [L0307.003]
    3. Day 3, user-generated content email. Include a natural product photo (not a professional one) and a text testimonial in this form: "We got an awesome comment on our Facebook/product page: [Customer name] faced [problem], tried our product, [their positive feedback]." This adds social proof without video. [L0307.003]
  • The welcome flow order for a community-style brand:
    1. Tell new subscribers they are joining a community, family or club.
    2. Offer a welcoming gift.
    3. If you have a collection, add a collection block after the welcome content.
    4. Add your brand story in the footer. [L0604.002]
  • A pop-up welcome flow with a 5-10% welcome kit discount:
    1. Trigger the flow when someone signs up through the pop-up or the footer newsletter form.
    2. Send the first email straight away: welcome them to the community, family or club, give a welcome kit discount of 5-10%, add a link to a collection, and close with a short brand story in the footer.
    3. If the email is not opened after one day, send the same email again with the same offer.
    4. Keep sending emails every 2-3 days, not several times a day, until the subscriber opens an email or buys.
    5. Make the content educational: address the customer's use case and pain points, and avoid hard sales pitches such as "buy now" repeated in every email.
    6. End each email with at most a one-line pitch, such as checking out the collection. [L0605.019]
  • The welcome flow has three emails with a 10% storewide discount. The deadline gets shorter in each one: last 3 days, then 48 hours, then 24 hours. The same discount code is used in all three emails. [L0606.010]
  • Some sellers run longer introduction sequences of 40 to 50 emails. Choose the length you can realistically write and maintain, since a shorter sequence that gets finished is more useful than a longer one that is abandoned. [L0523.136]

  • The first emails must bond the subscriber with your Attractive Character. A boring first email usually means the subscriber stops opening your messages. [L0523.112]
  • Place your most emotional story early in the welcome sequence to build a bond with new subscribers before pitching them. Readers who connect with the story tend to open and buy more. [L0378.092]
  • The second email of a store welcome series should show bestsellers, active deals or promotions, and customer reviews or testimonials so new subscribers can judge product quality. It can also explain the store's mission and values and how the products match them. Keep it focused on buying decisions rather than on follow-up actions. [L1297.042]
  • The third email should build the relationship, not push a sale. Invite subscribers to follow the social accounts or join a loyalty program for rewards, and link useful help such as product guides, size charts, or a customer service phone line so they can buy with confidence. Put the social follow request last, and only include it if the store actually has social accounts. [L1297.043]
  • The last welcome email shifts from brand talk to product. A best-sellers block shows what the discount buys. Use this switch when the message moves to the final deadline, so the subscriber sees products instead of another plea. Only show items that are in stock. [L0606.039]
  • Sample flow with timed steps: a Time Delay step sets both the gap and the send time between emails. Email one goes out on Day 0, a Wait 3 days step puts email two on Day 3 at 12:00 PM, and a Wait 4 days step puts email three on Day 7 at 12:00 PM. A noon send is a weak fit for welcome emails, so set the delay steps to send around 8 PM instead. [L1470.027]

Timing and spacing

  • Trigger and first send:
    • Shopify: insert a 1-minute wait after the email subscription trigger so processing finishes before conditional checks run. [L0201.011, L0201.005]
    • General, older (Source A, 2021): the first email after someone subscribes or asks about a product usually goes out after a delay of about 5 to 10 minutes. [L1090.114]
    • Use the 1-minute wait in Shopify automations and the 5-10 minute delay as the general reference. [L0201.011, L1090.114]
  • Send time: schedule welcome emails for around 8 PM rather than noon. Lunchtime sends were judged a poor fit for this series, so avoid a 12 PM send time after a multi-day wait. [L1470.022]
  • Gap between welcome emails: several sources differ, so choose by offer deadline:
    • Second email moves from a 3-day delay to a 1-day delay, because a longer gap lets the buyer lose interest. [L0573.019, L0607.021]
    • Email spacing of about 24 hours: email 1 on signup, email 2 about 24 hours later, email 3 about 48 hours later. For a three-day offer this keeps each email's deadline true, since 48 hours remain after one day and 24 hours after two. [L0606.036]
    • Lead-magnet flow: wait 24 hours between each further email. [L1076.011]
    • Soap-opera sequence: five emails sent once per day after signup. [L0523.113]
    • Ongoing frequency: every 2-3 days, not several times a day, until the subscriber opens an email or buys. [L0605.019]
  • Bestsellers and social follow-up in Klaviyo: the bestsellers, offers and reviews email is sent 3 days after signup. The follow-us-on-social-media email is then sent 4 days after that, so the social ask is the last message. [L0477.037, L1297.019]
  • Source ambiguity: one Klaviyo procedure labels the 3-day wait as "Email 1 is sent after 3 days' wait" and then lists the bestsellers email as Email 2 after a 4-day wait, which conflicts with the 3-day and 4-day timing above. Read the 3-day wait as the delay before the bestsellers email and the 4-day wait as the delay before the social email. [L0477.018, L0477.037, L1297.019]
  • The 30-day master flow spreads early emails in the first days, for example Day 0, Day 2, Day 5 for prospects, and Day 1, Day 7, Day 21 for first-time buyers. [L0493.018]
  • Offer deadlines and timing are covered in the discount section below. [L0606.010, L1470.020]

  • Deadline logic:
    • The welcome series deadline is 72 hours, or the expiry date of the coupon or discount code if that is the shorter limit. [L0494.016]
    • Three-email flow: last 3 days, then 48 hours, then 24 hours. [L0606.010]
    • Clavio: email 1 states a three-day deadline; the reminder (email 2) says the offer is available for 48 hours only. [L0634.007, L0634.009]
    • Offer clock in the Source E structure: welcome email with the discount code at 0 hours, bestseller showcase at 48 hours to go, social proof story at 24 hours to go, urgency email at 12 hours to go, and last-chance email when the offer ends in 4 hours. [L1470.020]
    • Only state a deadline you can keep. A true deadline turns interest into action, while a fake one destroys trust. [L0523.119]
  • Discount code formula for the welcome email: the subject line names the offer and adds a deadline; the body thanks the new subscriber, shows the discount code in bold on a colored background, states where to use it, and states when it expires. The code is the reason the person subscribed, so it must be easy to find and clearly time-limited. [L0476.014]
  • Examples from the same formula: subject "Your $5 welcome code ends Friday", with the code and a Shop now button; and subject "Your 48 hours to claim $5 off start now", with a short thank-you line, the code, and a link to the best-selling collection. [L0476.014]
  • Separate codes per channel: give each welcome channel its own discount code, such as a 10% sitewide code for email and a 15% VIP code for SMS. Separate codes show which channel brings redemptions and make the offers easy to tell apart at checkout and in reporting. [L1470.032]
  • Automatic application: before saving a discount link, confirm that the discount is set to apply automatically at checkout, so the customer never has to type the code. Use the link itself as the test, since the preview in Klaviyo does not make discount links clickable. [L1470.034]
  • Use the same discount code in all emails of one flow. [L0606.010]
  • Offer and reward messaging in the Clavio reminder: the discount is valid until the stated time, and the subscriber can still load the cart after that. [L0634.009]
  • Newer and older discount advice: the 5% first coupon in the lead-magnet flow is from Source A, undated, and the 5-10% welcome kit is from Source C, undated; both apply to different flow types. [L1076.011, L0605.019]

Filters: buyers, abandoners and channels

  • Audience for the discount: limit the audience to new subscribers who have never placed an order, so buyers do not receive the new-customer discount again. [L0573.033]
  • Klaviyo audience setup: select the audience of new sign-ups who have never purchased, then choose a static coupon. [L0573.014]
  • Klaviyo prebuilt flow: the prebuilt welcome flow branches between subscribers who have never ordered and customers who have ordered at least once. Customers with at least one order see a separate flow path, so the template covers both groups. [L0607.019, L0573.015]
  • Beginners should start with the prebuilt Klaviyo welcome flow. [L0573.015]
  • Source E flow filter: add the filter "Placed Order zero times since starting this flow". Anyone who buys is removed from the welcome flow and stops receiving its emails, so the flow only targets new customers. [L1470.004]
  • SMS split: add a Conditional Split with the condition "Is not consented to receive SMS". Put the SMS message on the No branch, which means subscribers who did consent to SMS, and let the Yes branch carry only the email path. [L1470.005]
  • Shopify abandoned-cart exclusion: exclude customers tagged "abandoned cart" from the welcome automation because they enter a separate abandoned cart automation. Sending both sequences creates duplicate emails and wastes effort. [L0201.009]
  • Shopify rules used in the automation: the first rule is Customer > Tags > does not include > abandoned cart, and the second rule is Customer > Amount spent > equals > 0. [L0201.005]
  • Pop-up trigger: the welcome flow is triggered for people who enter their email in the pop-up installed on the store. It is the first automated sequence a new subscriber receives. [L0606.003]
  • Klaviyo newer flow experience: the pre-built welcome flow can be used with the newer experience. Beginners should start with this pre-built flow. [L0573.015]

Welcome email structure from Source C, for the welcome email formula, see the discount section. [L0476.014]

Email 1 structure (soap opening, set the stage):

  • Pattern: a warm welcome from the character that sets expectations about frequency and promises a valuable gift or lesson soon. The formula is: introduce yourself, share one personal milestone that shows your credibility, state the send schedule, and tease tomorrow's email in a P.S. with its subject line. Subscribers know what to expect and have a reason to open the next message. [L0523.115]
  • Example 1, subject "Welcome: Day 1 of 5". Body: "Hi, I am Sam. I started my first online shop from my kitchen table. Tomorrow I will share the checklist I used to get the first 100 orders. P.S. Look for the subject line Day 2 of 5: The checklist." [L0523.115]
  • Example 2, subject "Day 1 of 5: A quick hello from me". Body: "Welcome to the group. I will send one short email each day for the next five days. Tomorrow I will show you the one mistake that cost me my first six months." [L0523.115]

Email 2 structure (high drama and backstory):

  • Pattern: open at the emotional peak of a story, then rewind to the events that led there, stop at the wall you hit, and leave the answer for the next email. The reader's situation should mirror the backstory. Starting in the middle of a dramatic moment hooks attention faster than starting at the beginning. [L0523.116]
  • Example 1, subject "Day 2 of 5: The night my account got frozen". Body: "I stared at the locked screen at midnight, knowing rent was due in four days. Two years earlier I had been sure this business was simple. Here is how I got stuck, and why the answer surprised me. Tomorrow I will show you exactly what I did." [L0523.116]
  • Example 2, subject "Day 2 of 5: The month I almost quit". Body: "My order count had been flat for ninety days, and my savings were nearly gone. I had tried every tutorial I could find. I will tell you what finally broke the pattern tomorrow." [L0523.116]

Email 3 structure (epiphany):

  • Pattern: describe the moment of realization, which often occurs when a simple observation exposes a flaw in the old approach, and connect the new insight directly to the product you sell. End with a link to the offer. The subscriber now sees the offer as the answer to the drama you built. [L0523.117]
  • Example 1, subject "Day 3 of 5: The math that changed everything". Body: "I worked out how much I earned per hour compared with a single product that sold hundreds of copies a day. Selling a system once beat selling my time forever. The method behind it is what I want to show you. Here is the link: [link]." [L0523.117]
  • Example 2, subject "Day 3 of 5: The tiny detail I had missed". Body: "One day I noticed that my best customers all came from the same place. That observation became my whole strategy. The short training that teaches it is here: [link]." [L0523.117]

Email 4 structure (hidden benefits):

  • Pattern: list two or three benefits of your solution that the reader has not thought about, phrase each as "Sure, you will get X, but you will also get Y", and back them with a short story. Then present the offer with a try-before-you-pay option. Non-obvious benefits create a new reason to act and deepen the bond with the character. [L0523.118]
  • Example 1, subject "Day 4 of 5: The benefit nobody mentions". Body: "Sure, you will earn more, but you will also get the freedom to take a Tuesday off. Last month I took three days away, and my store kept selling. Normally this course costs 300 USD, but you can take it free and pay only if you find it useful. Sign up here: [link]." [L0523.118]
  • Example 2, subject "Day 4 of 5: More than money". Body: "Sure, you will grow your income, but you will also spend more evenings with your family. I did not expect that. The full program is free to try, and you only pay if it is worth it to you." [L0523.118]

Email 5 structure (urgency and call to action):

  • Pattern: give a real reason the offer will change or disappear, such as a webinar starting tomorrow or a discount ending. State the price the reader will pay if they wait, then give one clear link. A true deadline turns interest into action, while a fake one destroys trust. [L0523.119]
  • Example 1, subject "Day 5 of 5: Last call". Body: "The free-access window closes tonight at midnight. After that the course returns to its full price of 3,000 USD. If you want to try the complete training before paying, grab your place here: [link]." [L0523.119]
  • Example 2, subject "Day 5 of 5: Seats are almost gone". Body: "Only ten seats remain for Thursday's live session. After it starts, late entries pay the full fee. Reserve your seat now: [link]." [L0523.119]

Welcome email principle: keep the content educational and avoid hard sales pitches. [L0605.019]

Soap-opera and story sequences

  • Purpose: the first emails after someone joins your list must bond the subscriber with your Attractive Character. A boring first email usually means the subscriber stops opening your messages. Use the first sequence to build an emotional connection. [L0523.112]
  • Soap Opera Sequence, five emails sent once per day after signup:
    1. Email 1, set the stage: thank the subscriber, introduce yourself as the character, explain how often emails arrive, and promise a valuable gift or lesson in the next email. [L0523.113]
    2. Email 2, open with high drama: start at the most intense moment, then tell the backstory that led there, stop at the wall you hit, and open a loop for email 3 without giving the answer. [L0523.113]
    3. Email 3, epiphany: reveal the discovery that changed things for you and tie it directly to the offer, with a link. [L0523.113]
    4. Email 4, hidden benefits: list two or three non-obvious benefits of the solution, tell a parable that shows one, and present the offer with a risk-reducing try-before-you-pay offer. [L0523.113]
    5. Email 5, urgency and call to action: give a real reason to act now and end with one clear call to action. [L0523.113]
  • Open a loop in each email and close it in the next one, so each email ends with a reason to open the following one. Tease a result or secret without revealing the details, then give the answer later. Repeat this pattern from email to email. [L0523.114]
  • The five-email soap sequence is a short version of a longer introduction sequence; see the length table in the structure section. [L0523.136]
  • Trust series before a higher-priced ask: send a five-part email series that tells the brand's story, the problems it solved, and how to solve them in the niche. Then make the larger offer once trust is established. [L0552.039]
  • Emotional story placement: see the structure section. [L0378.092]

30-day master flow

  • The first 30 days after a subscriber joins or a customer buys are the most important engagement period, since interest is highest then. Use this period to build the relationship and drive a first purchase (for prospects) or a repeat purchase (for customers). (Older, Source B, 2021-22.) [L0493.017]
  • A 30-day master flow is a single automated email series that covers both pre-purchase and post-purchase contacts, regardless of how the person entered the business. It is built so that the early emails carry the most engagement weight. (Older, Source B, 2021-22.) [L0493.018]
    • Example one: a 30-day flow for prospects who opted in via a guide: welcome email on day 0, product story on day 2, first offer on day 5. [L0493.018]
    • Example two: a 30-day flow for first-time buyers: order follow-up on day 1, usage tip on day 7, repeat-purchase offer on day 21. [L0493.018]
  • Build the first 30-day master flow this way: start building it right away, add one email every day or every other day, and accept that the complete flow will take a few weeks rather than a single day. (Older, Source B, 2021-22.) [L0493.019]
  • Replicate an existing master flow for each new entry point to the email list (older, Source B, 2021-22):
    1. Copy the complete flow. [L0493.020]
    2. Update only the specific emails that need to be more relevant to the new entry point. [L0493.020]
    3. Keep shared early emails, such as the welcome email, unchanged. [L0493.020]
  • Integrate introductory emails for the loyalty program, referral program, and customer feedback survey into your existing 30-day new customer welcome sequence, so all new customers receive them automatically. (Older, Source B, 2021-22.) [L0203.012]

Content-based nurture sequences

  • Send automated sequences to subscribers grouped by the content piece or product page they opted in on, with emails leading down the purchase path linked to that content. The first few emails should follow the call to action in that content, such as buying a recommended product. (Older, Source B, 2021-22.) [L0603.020]
    • Example pattern: an opt-in from a guide comparing two products leads to emails that explain the guide's pick and then push that product. [L0603.020]
  • Quiz and buyer's guide follow-up flow:
    1. The visitor completes a product quiz. [L1204.021]
    2. A follow-up email sequence sends product recommendations based on the quiz answers. [L1204.021]
    3. The sequence points the visitor to the buyer's guide. [L1204.021]
    4. If the visitor downloads the buyer's guide first, the sequence points them to the quiz instead. [L1204.021]
  • Giveaway leads: leads captured by the giveaway funnel go into email nurture sequences that aim to convert them over time. These email flows also generate part of the measured revenue. [L0797.006]
  • To launch quickly, duplicate your existing welcome flows or welcome series and run a similar funnel alongside them. [L0797.020]

Lead-magnet follow-up flows

  • Collecting opt-ins without a follow-up email sequence produces leads who never buy. The money from a lead magnet comes from the follow-up, so every opt-in must enter a flow. [L1076.012]
  • Build a separate flow for every lead magnet, so each requested item receives its own follow-up sequence. A single flow is enough at the start, and you add more as you create more free items. [L1076.024]
  • Lead-magnet email flow in Klaviyo:
    1. Immediately send the requested free item with the download link. [L1076.011]
    2. Wait 24 hours. [L1076.011]
    3. Send a welcome email introducing the company, with a first coupon such as 5 percent off. [L1076.011]
    4. Wait 24 hours between each further email and keep making offers. [L1076.011]
  • A new lead should first go through a welcome flow of about four to five emails, after which they move into the main broadcast list. Beginners can start with about one broadcast email a week and increase the frequency as the list grows. [L1076.076]
  • Download-delivery emails: in one published benchmark (older, Source B, 2021-22), welcome and thank-you emails that contained a download link for a lead magnet had an 88.7% open rate and a 35.07% click-through rate. Use these figures as a reference point when measuring your own download-delivery emails. [L0571.029]
  • The email that delivers a lead magnet is opened by most subscribers, so it can carry a second offer. Use it as a second conversion point, for example to promote a membership discount or a related product. (Older, Source B, 2021-22.) [L0571.030]
  • Influencer digital products:
    1. Send a five-email sequence that provides value first. [L0789.038]
    2. Then send the new subscribers a 24-hour promotion with a coupon to convert them into buyers. [L0789.038]

Ongoing welcome-series handling

  • Tag-based and purchase-based exits: keep the welcome flow limited to new subscribers, using the buyer and abandoner filters above, so the flow does not mail people who already bought or who sit in a separate abandoned cart sequence. [L0201.009, L1470.004, L0573.033]
  • Use separate codes per channel and check redemption by channel in reporting. [L1470.032]
  • Smart sending and tracking: set Smart sending off and UTM tracking on for every welcome email, so the flow sends on the set schedule and shows its traffic in analytics. [L0476.009, L0607.022, L1297.018]
  • Saving a reusable store template: save the first email as a template so later emails reuse the logo, images and layout. [L0476.009, L1297.018, L0634.007]
  • Reviewing the flow after launch: check each email's delay, the discount link, and the send time, then review opened and clicked results. [L1470.022, L1470.034]
  • Duplicate flows: confirm that the old welcome flows are off before switching the new flow on, and notify the client that the flow is live. [L1470.023, L1470.024]
  • Discount link test: test the link itself, because the preview in Klaviyo does not make discount links clickable. [L1470.034]
  • Ongoing frequency after the welcome series: continue with one message every 2-3 days until the subscriber opens an email or buys. [L0605.019]
  • Ongoing sequence after the welcome series: move new leads from the welcome flow into the main broadcast list. [L1076.076]

Further details
