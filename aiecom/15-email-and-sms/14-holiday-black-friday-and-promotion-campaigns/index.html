15.14 Holiday, black Friday and promotion campaigns

Q4 and Seasonal Planning

Timing

  • Start preparing Q4 at least two months before the peak, and warm the email and SMS audience from about September or October. Interaction-based emails and surveys build the list before the sales period. [L1377.051]
  • Use plain-text emails with one link or one form instead of heavy designs and images, because designed emails are more likely to land in the promotions tab. Surveys can be built with Jotform and sent to subscribers. [L1377.052]
  • Plan the holiday content early. Steps:
    1. Create blog posts for major holidays (Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Christmas) 1-2 months before the event. [L0226.032]
    2. Write articles about upcoming sales, new inventory, and limited-time promotions. [L0226.032]
    3. Send an email to the list 2-4 weeks before the event with the article and promotional details. [L0226.032]
    4. Aim to rank in Google search when customers search "Black Friday [niche]" or "Cyber Monday deals." [L0226.032]
  • Run a campaign for every major event or occasion, such as Christmas or Black Friday, because customers expect a discount at these times. This adds to the regular weekly and monthly campaigns. [L1213.057]
  • Use seasonal events such as Valentine's Day for promotions and extra emails. Plan promotions and emails around holidays to create reasons to send more messages. [L0934.060]
  • Plan a monthly promotion to the email list and build it into the regular calendar. Monthly promotions lift sales and help the store scale into other platforms later. [L0941.079]
  • Follow up holiday promotions in a later month. A promotion for a holiday can close sales that a first visit did not convert. [L1325.024]
  • Match email promotions with the same offer on Meta at the same time. Past customers who bought months ago, such as six months back, should receive both the email and the Facebook promotion. [L0649.024]

Event campaigns

  • If Klaviyo's template library has no template for a seasonal event, pick a template for a similar occasion and adapt it, for example a New Year layout for Valentine's Day. Replace the headline, offer text, and background color so the email matches the event. [L0573.035]
  • A seasonal gift promotion, such as a Father's Day offer, fits stores whose products appeal to dads. Promote it with a short series of emails to the subscriber list. [L0857.071]
  • An event-based promotion sent to the email list, such as 5% or 10% off on May 5 for Cinco de Mayo, is a quick way to earn extra sales. (Older, 2019.) [L1002.038]
  • Event campaign for occasions such as Mother's Day or Valentine's Day, in this order:
    1. Choose a specific marketing angle for the event. [L0605.027]
    2. Send the first email to educate the audience and announce the upcoming offer, without a hard pitch. [L0605.027]
    3. Send the next email with a benefit of the product and a sign-up request. [L0605.027]
    4. Count the sign-ups to estimate the sales. [L0605.027]
    5. Send the follow-up to sign-ups, and a separate email to non-sign-ups asking why they did not sign up, with an extra benefit point. [L0605.027]

Monthly promotion procedure (older, 2022)

  1. Choose a high-ticket product from a supplier with good margins. [L0963.137]
  2. Send an email offering a discount on that supplier's products, for example 10 percent off. [L0963.137]
  3. Link directly to the product collection. [L0963.137]

Black Friday and Cyber Monday announcements (older, 2019)

  • Send an email to your list, post on social media, and use every channel you have to announce a Black Friday sale and a Cyber Monday sale. Even a small email list or a small social following can produce extra sales from these promotions. [L1086.046]
  • In 2019, Black Friday fell on November 29 and Cyber Monday on December 2. Plan holiday promotions around these dates in the year they apply. [L1086.047]

Abandoned cart contacts (2023)

  • Abandoned cart contacts who are not subscribed to the main list can receive about one promotional email per month, tied to a holiday or niche event. [L1094.103]

List Building Before the Peak

  • Build an email and SMS list before the Q4 peak, because acquiring customers gets expensive then. Start retargeting ads after roughly two to three weeks of traffic, once you have stats to target from. [L0692.040]
  • List building avoids high CPMs. During the holiday season, CPM and CPC spike and CTR drops on all ad platforms (Facebook, Google, Pinterest, TikTok). Instead of direct sales ads, send traffic to a landing page that collects email addresses, which builds a qualified audience for engagement and future sales. [L0307.001]
  • Email marketing is a free or low-cost channel during BFCM and delivers strong ROI. Prioritize email list building and VIP segmentation before peak season. [L0298.051]
  • Build the email list from organic and paid visitors, since subscribers can be promoted to during monthly promotions. Sync the monthly promotions with your ad accounts so buyers see them on social too. [L0941.077]
  • Holiday promotion emails should go to the existing list regardless of its size, whether it has around 50 or around 50,000 contacts. Each email should include the discount code and aim to bring back visitors who have not decided to buy. [L0623.005]
  • A small email list is still worth using for Q4 promotions. The remarketing budget for a small audience is about $5 a day, and the sales are close to pure profit. [L1285.097]
  • A small email list still makes the holiday sends worth doing, because the remarketing spend with a small audience costs about $5 per day, and the sales it brings are close to pure profit. A few responses out of 100 to 300 contacts across five promotions justify the work. [L1223.020]
  • Set up abandoned cart emails, a content opt-in capture, and promotional follow-ups before a high-traffic sales period. This lets you contact visitors who did not buy on their first visit. (Older, 2018.) [L1274.010]
  • Black Friday and Cyber Monday results depend heavily on remarketing to the audience built up during the year, not only on first-time visitors. Give that audience a reason to return with a promotion. (Older, 2018.) [L1274.011]
  • Expect slow sales from influencer digital products. Collect emails for one to two months while keeping the audience engaged with the influencer, then blast the promotion to the list and on stories to create strong sales days. [L0789.039]
  • Case: A store spent about $50,000 on Facebook ads in Q4 but got most of its sales through its email and SMS marketing. It warmed its list from October with surveys, plain-text emails and a launch of one or two offers per month, then sent VIP bundle offers before the sales period. Retargeting campaigns with Black Friday and Cyber Monday offers reached roughly 8x ROAS in some campaigns. [L1377.054]

Dated Promotion Calendars

Black Friday and Cyber Monday sequence (Source C)

  1. November 24 (48 hours before launch): send "Going live in 48 hours. Last year everything sold out in 4 hours. You're among our first 100 subscribers, so take advantage." Include SMS. [L0307.005]
  2. November 25 (24 hours before): send "Going live in 24 hours." Tease a product image with a blurred price (e.g., show "9.99" to create curiosity). Include SMS. [L0307.005]
  3. November 26 (Black Friday, launch day): send the launch email and SMS with a link to the hidden category page. [L0307.005]
  4. November 27 (day after Black Friday): send "We had huge success, received many requests, some items sold out. We just restocked limited pieces. Shop now." [L0307.005]
  5. November 28 (Sunday): send "This is your last day. Only a few items left. Shop now." Send SMS. [L0307.005]
  6. November 29 (day after): send "It's finished. Thank you for your trust and business." Send SMS. [L0307.005]
  7. Monday (Cyber Monday, one week later): launch a new offer with a different discount structure (example: change from 50% off to 40% off plus a 50% cashback voucher for the next purchase). Send the new offer email and SMS, then a 48-hour countdown, then a last-chance email, then a closure email. [L0307.005]
  8. Optional Black Week extension (November 29-30, only 2 days): send an email plus SMS, then a UGC email, then a closure email "It's over." [L0307.005]

Black Friday sequence for a store

  1. Before the sale, send an email announcing Black Friday week and asking customers to decide now what they want. [L1363.057]
  2. On the day, send the main announcement with the bigger discount. [L1363.057]
  3. Retarget visitors who did not buy with a message saying they missed the offer. [L1363.057]
  4. After the sale, send an email saying the sale has been extended for people who missed it. [L1363.057]

Hidden Black Friday category page

  • Create a product category page that is not linked in website navigation or menus. It is only accessible via direct links from email, SMS, or retargeting ads. Include all Black Friday-discounted products, a countdown timer showing the promotion end time, and low-stock quantity labels on each product to create urgency. [L0307.006]

Welcome series and standard flows during BFCM

  • Standard email flows (abandoned cart sequences, welcome series, post-purchase) remain active and run in parallel during Black Friday promotional campaigns. Do not pause them. They operate on their own triggers and segments, separate from the VIP Black Friday list. [L0308.035]
  • For ongoing welcome series, if new leads enter after Day 3 emails have started, pause emails 2 and 3 for those new subscribers to prevent clashes with Black Friday email blasts, which have priority. Resume the welcome series after the promotion period ends. [L0307.004]
  • The welcome series runs automatically on fixed dates (e.g., Nov 1-3) regardless of when individual subscribers join. For subscribers entering late (e.g., Nov 22), pause emails 2 and 3 to avoid overlap with high-priority Black Friday blasts. Resume normal sequences after promotions end. [L0307.019]
  • Reconciling the two: standard flows stay active [L0308.035], and the welcome-series pause applies only to late joiners' emails 2 and 3 during the promotion period [L0307.004, L0307.019].

Campaigns versus flows

  • When time is short, use campaigns instead of flows for Black Friday emails. Campaigns are simpler: send an initial email to the list, analyze results, then send a new campaign to the list minus unsubscribes. There is no need for conditional flows. [L0308.007]

Christmas promotion (older, 2020)

  1. Launch the holiday promotion email as early as possible, ideally right away. [L0468.134]
  2. Include the message that products arrive before Christmas if ordered now. [L0468.134]
  3. Send one or two reminder emails as the holiday gets closer. [L0468.134]
  4. Do not send the main holiday promotion on the holiday itself, because most customers have already bought. [L0468.134]

Five-day holiday promotion

  1. Send an email at the start of the promotion announcing the sale and including the discount code. [L0623.006]
  2. Send an email in the middle of the window, or on a following day, saying the sale was extended to the final day. [L0623.006]
  3. Send a last email stating that the code expires at midnight on the final day. [L0623.006]
  4. Every email should include the code. [L0623.006]
  • For a five-day promotion, spread a three-email sequence across the window: the first email on the launch day, the second about two days later, and the third on the final day. Each email should match the stage of the promotion it is sent in. [L0620.004]

One-week December promotion

  • For a one-week December promotion with three emails, send the gain email on the first day, the logic email two days later, and the fear email on the last day. For example, December 8, December 10, and December 12. [L1224.019]

High-ticket promotion calendar

  • Close offers on a weekday. For more expensive, high-ticket products, the offer should close on a Friday. [L1205.022]

Timers and extensions

  • Klaviyo email campaigns can include countdown timers that are reset in each send. When a sale is extended, update the timer and the matching offer text in every campaign so the messages stay consistent. [L1225.087]

Repeat buyers

  • Email customers who bought a single item with a targeted discount to buy a gift or a second item for a partner or family member. This extends the value of first-time buyers in the holiday season. [L1225.010]

Promotion Segments and Exclusions

Standard promotion segment (2024-2025 rule)

  • For a promotion email, send to all contacts in the list, except people who purchased in the last 30 days and contacts currently in an evergreen automation, such as the abandoned cart flow. This works with either Shopify Email or Klaviyo. [L1221.005, L1222.007, L1223.018, L1285.064, L1285.105, L1287.045]
  • Segment settings:
    • Include: all contacts.
    • Exclude: contacts who purchased in the past 30 days.
    • Exclude: contacts currently in the abandoned cart flow (or any evergreen automation).
    • Segment size does not matter; a list of 500 or 10,000 contacts uses the same rules. [L1223.018]
  • Promotional emails go to all contacts except two groups: contacts who bought in the last 30 days, and contacts currently inside an evergreen automation. [L1221.005]
  • Recent buyers and automated flows should not receive the promotion sequence. [L1222.007]

Body: Explain that subscribers get 24-hour first access to upcoming BFCM offers; this creates urgency and FOMO without requiring truly different pricing-simply send the email 24 hours before the public campaign launches. Landing: Direct to a dedicated BFCM collection or campaign page. Effect: Drives eager subscribers to site before the main sale begins.

VIP positioning for lead capture

  • For Black Friday lead capture in Facebook Creator Studio, use a VIP positioning: "Enter details to get exclusive discounts up to 60% off with early access." This entices email submission without over-promising. [L0308.005]

VIP access sequence (Source E 2025-26)

  1. Start VIP access at least 72 hours before the sale begins. [L1225.007]
  2. Give VIP access first to ambassadors. [L1225.007]
  3. Then open it to organic followers. [L1225.007]

Trade-off of early access

  • Replacing a welcome discount pop-up with an early-access sign-up usually softens sales in the short term. The trade-off is a larger list of interested buyers that converts strongly once the sale starts, so the fair value exchange must be clear to the subscriber. [L1225.008]

Gain, Logic, Fear Sequences

Order and purpose

  • Each promotion's email sequence follows the order Gain, Logic, Fear: first the benefit, then the rational reason to act, then the fear of missing out. The series is meant to move the reader through different emotions that lead to a purchase. [L1222.005]
  • The three-email sequence can follow the gain, logic, and fear structure: the first email shows the benefit, the second gives the reason it works, and the third shows the cost of not acting. The structure is an older copywriting formula, and AI tools can help adapt it to an e-commerce store. [L0857.072]

Gain-logic-fear sequence (2024 pattern)

  • Email 1 states what the buyer gains (the benefit plus the offer). Email 2 gives a rational reason the offer is the smart choice. Email 3 says the offer is ending now. The sequence moves the reader from desire to justification to urgency, which is why it converts consistently across promotion types. [L1224.018]
    • Example A (kitchen knives, Black Friday): "Get 20% off the premium knife set, delivered before the holiday" / "Why one quality knife set replaces five cheap ones" / "Final hours: the 20% code ends at midnight". [L1224.018]
    • Example B (pet beds, Cyber Monday): "Cyber Monday: 15% off orthopedic pet beds with fast shipping" / "Why a supportive bed reduces night-time restlessness for older dogs" / "Last chance: the 15% code expires tonight". [L1224.018]

Formula with examples (2025)

  • A three-email promotion sequence follows the gain, logic, fear pattern. Email one states what the buyer gains, email two explains why this offer is a sensible choice, and email three says the offer is ending, so the timing creates urgency and the sequence works across most promotion types. [L1287.047]
    • Example A: Gain, 15 percent off plus delivery before the holiday weekend. Logic, a gift that arrives on time saves a last-minute store trip. Fear, the 15 percent code expires Sunday at midnight. [L1287.047]
    • Example B: Gain, a free carry strap with every yoga mat ordered this week. Logic, the buyer avoids a shopping trip to a sports store. Fear, the top color is limited and the strap offer ends Friday. [L1287.047]

Closing-email patterns

  • Closing-reminder email: the subject line says the discount ends at a specific time, and the body gives the code and a reason to act today. Reuse the same subject line as the headline of the remarketing ads. Example new subject lines: "Last Call: 20% Off Ends Tonight" and "Final Hours to Lock In Your Holiday Price". [L1223.021]
  • Buy-by-date email: the message states the last order date that still guarantees delivery before a holiday. The reason is the delivery window, not the discount. Example new subject lines: "Order by Dec 13 to Get It Before Christmas" and "Your Last Chance to Ship Before the Holidays". [L1223.022]

Remarketing during an open window (high-ticket)

  1. Send one pre-promotion email before a high-ticket offer starts. [L1205.025]
  2. Send one email per day during the promotion window. [L1205.025]
  3. On the closing day, send at least two reminder emails. [L1205.025]
  4. Run Facebook and Google remarketing ads to the same people who receive the emails, for maximum reach. [L1205.025]

Remarketing timing

  • Start retargeting ads after roughly two to three weeks of traffic, once you have stats to target from. [L0692.040]
  • During an open promotion window, run remarketing alongside the emails, as in the procedure above. [L1205.025]

Subject Lines by Holiday

Formula and structure

  • Holiday subject line formula: event name or seasonal cue, then a concrete savings promise, then an optional emoji or short action word. The event cue shows why the email matters now, and the savings promise gives a reason to open it. Examples in the same pattern: "Spring Clearance Starts Today: Save on Your Favorites" and "Summer Sale Is Live, Up to 30% Off Our Picks". [L0547.004]
  • Write a separate set of subject lines for each seasonal moment (Halloween, Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Christmas, New Year) instead of reusing one generic sale line. Each moment has its own vocabulary and should be matched to its own campaign. [L0548.001]
  • Across a holiday campaign, mix three types of subject lines: direct deal statements, urgency lines, and mood or holiday lines. Varying the type keeps recipients from tuning out a repeated message format. [L0548.011]
  • Use one emoji per subject line as an accent, placed at the start or end, and choose it to match the season (ghost, cart, gift, fire, laptop). Skip emojis when the brand or audience does not suit them. [L0548.010]

Subject line patterns by holiday

  • Halloween: combine a spooky or playful theme (ghosts, treats versus tricks, monster-style wordplay) with a savings promise, often with one ghost or pumpkin emoji. The theme makes the offer feel fun and fits the season, so the discount reads as a treat. Examples: "Spooky Season Steals: Up to 30% Off Everything 🎃" and "Witch Better Believe These Deals Are Here 🧙". [L0548.002]
  • Black Friday: use the words "Black Friday" with a savings or deal promise and a sense of now, such as early access, the biggest sale, or a last chance. Exclusivity ("just for you", "early access") and time-bound language make the email feel important. Examples: "Black Friday Early Access: 25% Off Starts Right Now" and "Last Chance: Black Friday Prices End Tonight 🛍️". [L0548.003]
  • Cyber Monday: name the day and pair it with either a countdown of hours left or a sitewide percentage off, with "Extended" used when the sale runs longer. The online focus (laptop or phone emoji, "sitewide") signals that the whole store is in the sale. Examples: "Cyber Monday: 12 Hours Left for 40% Off Sitewide 💻" and "Cyber Monday Extended: Our Deals Stay Live Through Tonight". [L0548.004]
  • Christmas: blend holiday warmth (gifts, Santa, decorations, festive phrasing) with a savings message and a deadline-style phrase such as "before they're gone" or "countdown". The festive tone builds gift-buying urgency. Examples: "Santa's Gift List Is Ready: Save 20% on Top Picks 🎁" and "Christmas Countdown: Grab Your Favorites Before Stock Runs Out 🎄". [L0548.005]
  • New Year: frame the offer as a fresh start, using resolutions, "new year, new" phrasing, or "start the year" wording plus a savings promise. This ties the purchase to a personal goal, which makes the offer feel purposeful. Examples: "New Year, Better Savings: Start 2027 With 15% Off 🎉" and "Your Resolution Starts Here: Save Big on Your Favorite Brands". [L0548.006]

Placeholders and dates

  • Fill every placeholder before sending: X%, XX% and similar must become the real discount, NAME must become the merge field of your email tool, and any year must be the current year. A leftover placeholder in a live email is a visible error that damages trust. [L0547.005]
  • Use placeholders in subject lines for the discount percentage (X%) and the year (20XX), and for personalization use a NAME field that is filled per recipient. Replace every placeholder with the real figure before the email is sent. [L0548.008]
  • Do not leave an old year in a seasonal subject line, such as an outdated "Welcome 20XX" phrase from a previous year. Update the year in every subject line for the current season before sending. [L0548.009]

Reuse and extensions

  • Do not reuse the same subject line across different events or repeat an identical line in the next campaign. Tie wording to the specific offer of each event so recipients do not see the same message twice. [L0547.008]
  • When a sale is extended, send a follow-up with "Extended" wording instead of repeating the original subject line. The change signals a new reason to open the email and keeps the offer credible. [L0548.012]

Ready-made list

  • When AI-written email headlines are weak, use a proven list of holiday subject lines grouped by holiday (about 97 lines covering Halloween, Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Christmas, and New Year's). Use them as email subject lines or ad headlines. [L1224.020, L1287.054]

Scarcity, Urgency and Teasers

Urgency must be real

  • Urgency must be real. Valid reasons include a webinar starting tomorrow, a limited number of seats, a limited stock of a physical product, or a video being taken offline. For evergreen products, use a special sale that ends soon or a coupon that expires in 24 hours. [L0523.120]
  • Fake urgency backfires and destroys credibility once subscribers notice. Invented deadlines teach readers to ignore future emails. Only use deadlines you can honor. [L0523.121]
  • Do not claim urgency that is not real. Lines such as "only 4 hours to go", "last day", or "extended" must match the actual sale timer or end date. Use "before stock runs out" only when inventory is truly limited, because subscribers who find a fake deadline stop trusting later messages. [L0547.006, L0548.007]
  • Urgency phrases such as "hours left", "ends tonight", or "last day" should only be used when the deadline is real. False countdowns damage trust and make later emails less effective. [L0548.007, L0547.006]

Deadlines and expiry

  • Within each flow, every later email shortens the deadline: 3 days, then 48 hours, then 24 hours or a few hours. The repeated shrinking window creates escalating urgency without changing the offer itself. Examples: "Offer ends in 72 hours" followed by "Offer ends in 24 hours"; "Your code expires this weekend" followed by "Last chance: expires at midnight tonight". [L0606.017]
  • Set the send delays so that each email arrives while its deadline statement is still true. The lesson does not give fixed delays, so the timing must be derived from the deadlines in the copy. [L0606.015]
  • If an email says the offer ends at midnight, name the time zone and make the coupon expire at exactly that time. A midnight claim that differs from the real expiry damages trust the same way a false stock claim does. [L0606.041]

Stock claims

  • Scarcity claims such as "selling out like crazy", "inventory is running low", or "we might not be able to fulfill your order" must be true for the store at the time of sending. False stock claims damage trust and can create refund and complaint problems. [L0606.019]
  • Use scarcity messaging in Black Friday campaigns, such as: "This exclusive discount releases to the public tomorrow. Act now to lock in the early-bird price before the public gets access." This creates FOMO without over-exaggerating the offer. [L0308.031]

Teasers

  • Teasing a larger promotion:
    1. Send a teaser email to the broadcast list a few days before the offer starts, for example on the Friday before a Monday start. [L1204.046]
    2. Hint at the offer without giving the full details, so people start thinking about the purchase. [L1204.046]
    3. Send the full offer at the start of the window. [L1204.046]
  • Show a product image with a blurred price a day before launch to create curiosity (e.g., "9.99"). [L0307.005]
  • For high-ticket offers, send a single pre-promotion email before the offer starts, saying a big offer is coming and asking the audience to check their inbox first thing on the start day at a specific time. Example: for leather sofas averaging $2,500, send the email the Friday before a Monday-to-Friday promotion. This prepares buyers for a larger purchase. [L1205.021, L1205.030]

AI Prompts for Sequences

Building the prompt first (procedure)

  1. Write one message that includes the store domain, promotion dates, discount code, percentage off, delivery guarantee, and the send date of each email. [L1224.036]
  2. Ask the AI to first write the best prompt for this task instead of asking for the emails directly. [L1224.036]
  3. Review and edit that generated prompt for your own store. [L1224.036]
  4. Send the edited prompt, then ask for the sequence using the gain-logic-fear structure. [L1224.036]
  5. Use a fresh, customized prompt for each promotion rather than one template for all of them. [L1224.036]

Remarketing and sequence build (procedure)

  1. Set the promotion's start date, end date, discount, and discount code. [L1286.005]
  2. Add a delivery guarantee if the promotion has a deadline such as Christmas. [L1286.005]
  3. Write a 3-email sequence and send one email on the first promotion day, one in the middle, and one on the last promotion day (for a Monday to Friday window: Monday, Wednesday, Friday). [L1286.005]
  4. Write remarketing ad copy for Meta and Google Display based on the same email copy and offer details. [L1286.005]
