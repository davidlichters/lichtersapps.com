15.12 Email copywriting, design and templates

Copy Structure and Purpose

  • Email copy has two parts: the subject line and the body copy. Each is written and optimised separately. [L0568.007]
  • Marketing emails that aim to get the reader to visit the site need copywriting, because their purpose is persuasion toward a specific action. [L0568.006]
  • Direct selling emails start from one core problem of the target market, which the product is positioned to solve. Named frameworks for structuring them include Problem-Agitate-Solution, the Bencivenga persuasion equation and AIDA. [L0475.005]
  • The body copy of a marketing email has one job: generate clicks to a destination, not sell inside the inbox (older advice, 2021-22). Persuasion, full proof and purchase decisions belong on the website, where there is room for images, videos and complete copy. [L0602.001]
  • If an email tries to make the full purchase case, readers who are not convinced do not click, and the store loses the chance to convert them on the site (older advice, 2021-22). Send traffic straight to the website where the full picture can be shown. [L0602.002]
  • Do not answer the question or problem raised in the email (older advice, 2021-22). Tell the reader they will learn the answer through the click, because otherwise there is no reason to take the action. [L0602.023]
  • Email shares many similarities with content. Knowing customers' experiences, problems and pain points lets email address them more effectively (older advice, 2021-22). Email marketing built on this research communicates more precisely with the customer. [L0872.008]
  • The strategies that work in email are rooted in direct mail marketing and have stayed consistent for decades because human psychology has not changed (older advice, 2021-22). Tested direct mail principles can therefore be applied to email copy and sequencing. [L0609.003]
  • Treat every email as a potential reusable template (older advice, 2021-22). Once an email is written, reuse it across other flows instead of writing from scratch, which saves significant time. [L0493.021]
  • Message matters more than format. Postage cost and fancy stationery do not increase response; the offer and the opening sentences do. [L1261.157]

Persuasion Principles

The six power reasons customers buy:

  1. Ownership: use language and lifestyle images to make them feel they already own it (car salesman technique: give control and choice). [L0261.012]
  2. Logic: identify the right customer, answer why it is relevant, and confirm it solves their specific problem. [L0261.012]
  3. Social Proof: include testimonials, reviews, ratings and credible endorsements. [L0261.012]
  4. Scarcity and Urgency: use a limited-time offer, limited stock language, show others buying, and highlight the cost of inaction. [L0261.012]
  5. Reciprocity: frame the offer as a gift (free shipping, discount), create obligation, and provide value first. [L0261.012]
  6. Status: identify the status the customer wants (luxury, eco-friendly, athletic) and position the product to align with and elevate that status. [L0261.012]

Body Copy Formula and CTA Rules

Order of Work

  • Decide the call to action before writing the body copy. Typical CTAs in a copy email are learning more about a product, reading a blog post or downloading a lead magnet (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.004]
  • Write the body copy after the subject line, and make it follow on from the subject line's promise. Together they should create curiosity, so the body reveals only enough to make the reader want more (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.003]

Body Copy Formula

Email body formula for e-commerce copy emails, in order (older advice, 2021-22):

  1. Greet the reader and confirm the problem that the CTA will help them solve.
  2. Tease the solution, which is the product's primary promise or the answer the content provides.
  3. Explain at least one benefit of taking the CTA.
  4. Offer the CTA.
  5. Sign off.

[L0602.010]

  • After the subject line, briefly name the primary value or benefit the reader gets by taking the CTA. Do not explain the whole content in the email (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.007]
  • For a content CTA, one primary benefit is usually enough. If there are several, list up to three primary benefits and keep each one short, because the content itself delivers the detail (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.008]
  • For a product CTA, focus the email on the product's primary promise. If the product copy already has a clear primary promise, reuse that promise in the email (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.009]
  • Open the email body with a short greeting that also introduces the sender, so the reader knows who is writing before the problem is named (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.025]
  • Confirm the problem by pointing to how often customers ask about it, for example by saying the store receives many emails or questions on the topic. This makes the problem feel recognised and real before the solution is teased (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.027]
  • Name the destination in the email, such as a blog post, guide, product page or download. This avoids surprising readers when the page asks for something other than a simple read, especially when the link leads to an opt-in download (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.026]

Length

  • A copy email for e-commerce should be a few hundred words in four or five short paragraphs (older advice, 2021-22). Longer formats are used by digital product, consulting and coaching businesses, not typical e-commerce stores that send traffic to a full site. [L0602.011]
  • A complete copy email that follows the problem, solution, benefit and CTA formula, with two benefits and one guide link, can run about 145 words (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.029]

Body Copy Checklist

Body copy format checklist, in order (older advice, 2021-22):

  1. Use a minimum font size of 14 point or larger.
  2. Use short, simple sentences in easy-to-read language.
  3. Use short paragraphs of one to two lines, and single-line paragraphs are common.
  4. Make the CTA or links stand out.
  5. Use only one CTA per email.
  6. Use a personalised signature.

[L0602.012]

  • Sign emails with a real person's name, either the owner or a team member, rather than a generic business name or the word Team. Emails that feel personal from a person to a person tend to get better response rates (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.015]

One CTA, Placed in Several Spots

  • Focus most emails on exactly one call to action. Mixing several offers or links in one email reduces the response to each of them. Several different CTAs overwhelm readers, lower interaction and often lead them to ignore the email and not return to it (older advice, 2021-22). [L0569.014, L0602.005]
  • Adding many calls to action to one email reduces the number of clicks for each one. Avoid daily-deal style emails stuffed with many offers unless that is actually your business model (older advice, 2021-22). [L0569.015]
  • Several clickable elements in one email are acceptable if they all point to the same CTA and the same content. Multiple different CTAs to different content should be avoided (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.022]
  • Place the one CTA in several spots that all lead to the same page: a link within the sentence that names the topic, a clear click instruction in the middle, and a link or button near the end. These are multiple clickable areas for a single action, not several different offers (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.028]

Button or Text Link

  • Use a button rather than a plain text link for the main call to action. Research cited in the lesson indicates that buttons improve click-through rates and action (older advice, 2021-22). [L0569.012]
  • A CTA can be a clickable button or a standard underlined text link in a different colour, such as "click here to view". Test both styles, since neither is proven to be better in general (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.014]
  • The two positions differ. The button is the recommended default, and the text link is an accepted alternative. When you cannot tell which works better for your list, run both as a test. [L0569.012, L0602.014]
  • The call-to-action button must use a unique colour with strong contrast against the rest of the email, such as bright red, bright green or bright blue. Any colour works as long as the button clearly stands out (older advice, 2021-22). [L0569.013]

Pattern: Problem, Guide, Two Benefits, One CTA

Pattern (older advice, 2021-22):

  • A curiosity-driven subject line.
  • A greeting.
  • One sentence that names a common buying problem.
  • A sentence saying there is confusing information online.
  • A sentence promising a guide or resource that settles the question.
  • Two benefits the reader gets from clicking.
  • One repeated CTA with a clear link or button.

Why it works: the reader sees a problem they recognise, a promise of an answer and a single clear next step, so the click is the only logical action. [L0602.024]

  • Example A (running shoes, subject: "Flat feet, which shoe?"): "Hi Sam, choosing running shoes with flat feet is a big decision. Advice online contradicts itself on stability versus cushioning, so we wrote a short guide. Click here to see which features matter most and which model our fitters recommend for your stride." [L0602.024]
  • Example B (mattresses, subject: "Firm or soft, which is right for your back?"): "Hi Lee, picking a mattress for back pain is not something to rush. There are many mixed opinions about firm and soft, so we put together a simple comparison. Click here to see the pros and cons of each and which one our sleep team suggests for back sleepers." [L0602.024]

Pairing a Discount With a Product

  • Pair the discount with one specific product suggestion in a single sentence that links the saving to a second purchase. This turns the coupon into a reason to add to the basket rather than just a price cut. [L0606.044]
    • Example 1: "Your 15% covers a second pair of socks, so add the matching cap while you are here." [L0606.044]
    • Example 2: "Use your 15% on the refill pack and the saving pays for a travel size of the same cream." [L0606.044]

Writing Process and Scannable Style

  • Keep emails easy to scan with one or two sentences per line, lots of white space and no long paragraphs. Readers scan first and read only what hooks them. Short lines keep attention on the story and offer. [L0523.122]
  • Use short, choppy sentences and short paragraphs of one to two lines (older advice, 2021-22). [L0569.011]
  • Writing process for emails: [L0523.123]
    1. Write the basic structure of the email first, including the story beats and the offer link.
    2. Add the juicy details, emotional hooks and personality afterwards.
    3. Dictate ideas into a phone recorder and send the recording to someone for transcription if writing is slow.
  • Email body text for flows can be drafted with ChatGPT, but the draft still needs checking. Before going live, confirm the links point to the correct checkout and product pages and that the wording matches the offer. [L0476.040]
  • Small grammar errors are acceptable in emails and can make the character more human and relatable. Do not delay sending because of fear of mistakes, since consistent sending matters more than perfect text. The flaw is part of the bond. [L0523.124]
  • Keep a separate swipe file of emails collected from competitors and from marketers in other niches. Use it as a source of ideas when you lack inspiration for your own email content, without copying it directly (older advice, 2021-22). [L0609.018]

Font Size and Readability

  • Set the email body font to at least 14 point. Automatic sizes of 12 or 10 point that many email tools produce are too small and hard to read for some people (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.013]
  • Body copy should use a minimum font size of 18 pt, with up to 22 pt acceptable, because bigger text is generally easier to read (older advice, 2021-22). [L0569.011]
  • The two figures conflict. Use 18-22 pt for body copy where you can, and treat 14 pt as the floor for copy emails. [L0569.011, L0602.013]

Personalisation and Dynamic Content

  • Email content can use personalisation variables such as {{firstName}} with a fallback value like 'Friend' for customers without a stored first name. [L0282.012]
  • Personalise the greeting and subject line with the first name. Adding the subscriber's first name makes the email feel more personal. [L0607.025]
  • Add a default such as 'there' to every personalised greeting and subject line. Otherwise an empty first name in the profile leaves an empty greeting. [L0606.022]
  • Personalisation in Klaviyo (Liquid) for the greeting and the subject line:
Greeting: Hey {{ first_name|default:'there' }},
Cart subject line: Still thinking it over, {{ first_name|default:'there'|title }}?

Use the fallback in both places, so an empty first name in the profile does not show up as a blank greeting.

[L0606.008]

  • The two fallback values differ. 'Friend' is the general personalisation fallback for variables (L0282.012). 'there' is the greeting and subject fallback used in the Klaviyo flow templates (L0606.008, L0606.022). Use 'Friend' for general content variables and 'there' where the greeting reads "Hey there,". [L0282.012, L0606.008, L0606.022]
  • The placeholder syntax also differs between sources. Klaviyo uses {{ first_name }} (L0606.025 shows it without a fallback, although L0606.022 requires one). Generic text uses {first_name} and {discount_code}, which you replace yourself (L0607.023). [L0606.025, L0606.022, L0607.023]
  • Placeholder mapping in the flow templates: [L0606.007]
    • {{ first_name }} and [NAME TAG] take the subscriber's first name from the Klaviyo profile.
    • [DISCOUNT CODE] takes the discount code of that flow.
    • [BUTTON] is a call-to-action button linking to the store.
    • [CART] is the block listing the products left in the abandoned cart.
    • [BEST SELLERS] is a product block of top sellers.
    • [REVIEWS] is a block of customer reviews.
    • [TIMER] is a countdown.
    • [SIGNATURE] is the sender sign-off.
  • Dynamic content may not appear fully in email previews, so check the preview of each campaign before sending. [L0864.020]

Flow Setup and Review Block

Setting up the flows in Klaviyo: [L0606.006]

  1. Create or open the Klaviyo account.
  2. Create the abandoned cart flow, the welcome flow and the post-purchase flow.
  3. Load the ready-made team template for each flow into the matching flow, one email per step, in the order given below.
  4. Replace every blank (______) and every bracketed placeholder with real store content, your real discount code and your real links.
  5. Check the offer, the deadline wording and the review block in each email before turning the flow on.
  • Place the review block at the end of each email, after the call to action. Reviews act as social proof and reinforce the decision without competing with the discount code. [L0606.024]
  • Keep the stock line only if it is accurate, and keep the 100% satisfaction line only if the store offers that guarantee. [L0606.028, L0606.033]
  • Every flow email should show the brand logo and footer, which Klaviyo takes from the brand assets once they are set up. A footer can also hold social media icons, which are added as their own section in the email builder. [L0607.053]

Claims, Accuracy and Launch Checks

  • Vague superlatives and invented numbers such as "150% more awesome" or a "100% satisfaction guarantee" must not stay in the copy unless they are real. Replace them with a specific, verifiable benefit, and only mention a guarantee the store actually offers in its return policy. [L0606.020]
  • Leaving blanks (______), [NAME TAG], [BUTTON], [REVIEWS] or [DISCOUNT CODE] unreplaced before launch sends visible placeholder text to customers. Check a test send of every email in the flow before switching it on. [L0606.021]
  • An email inside a flow that is still in draft status is not sent, so an unwanted email can be kept safe by leaving it in draft. [L1297.044]
  • Before using a bought email template, check that it works on mobile and across all email clients. Look-alike designs can fail in real inboxes even when they look good on a marketplace page. [L1263.006]

Plain Text vs HTML

Positions in the Sources

Position Source When it applies
HTML is preferred over plain text, though the gap matters less than it used to. HTML allows clickable buttons and a logo, so use it, but keep the layout simple (older advice, 2021-22). L0569.001 When you need buttons and a logo.
Simple text-based emails are preferred for small and medium e-commerce stores. HTML remains useful when images must be shown (older advice, 2021-22). L0602.016 Small and medium stores; HTML when images must be shown.
Write emails mostly as plain text with few or no images. The wording sells, and many email clients do not display images. L0572.018 Default writing mode.
Plain text emails that look like a message from a friend can perform well, even compared with heavily designed templates. L0923.037 Any list used for many offers.
For a new store, start with text-led emails that use a few images, since they are more likely to reach the primary inbox. Compare open and click results in a test rather than relying on taste. L0607.052 New stores; test to decide.
  • Heavily designed HTML emails are typically associated with promotional mailings and are not how people usually write to each other (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.016]
  • Text-based emails with a few images are more likely to land in the primary inbox. Image-heavy emails from the start risk the promotions tab, where they are opened less. [L0607.025]
  • Heavily formatted HTML emails may not display well across all inboxes, devices and email clients. Some evidence suggests they are more likely to be classed as promotional and land in the Promotions tab in Gmail or in spam (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.017]
  • Large big-box e-commerce stores often send three or four heavily formatted HTML emails per day with many offers. The author does not consider this the best approach for small to medium e-commerce businesses (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.018]
  • Elaborate HTML design is unnecessary for a simple email with one CTA. There is no evidence that heavily formatted emails achieve higher click-through rates, and some evidence points the other way (older advice, 2021-22). [L0602.019]
  • Overloading HTML emails with decorative elements, many images and many colours is a mistake (older advice, 2021-22). Email providers may strip out part of the code, and the layout can break on some devices, so the email may look broken or make less sense. [L0569.002]
  • Build email layouts with HTML and CSS instead of image banners. Images can be blocked, so the layout should work without them. [L0572.019]
  • Include links to a few website categories in most emails. Recipients who are not interested in the email's topic may still click through to the shop. [L0572.020]
  • Email HTML is not web HTML. It is still largely stuck in 1990s-era techniques and renders differently in each email host, for example Gmail, Yahoo and Hotmail render the same design differently. [L1263.007]
  • Design and code emails for the lowest common denominator across all email clients. Learn what each client supports and does not support before building. [L1263.008]

Images, Colour and Visual Layout

  • Use images sparingly and choose only the best product images, such as the product itself or the product in use. Many images raise the risk of spam-folder placement and of formatting being stripped, and heavy HTML can look like scam emails. Product emails benefit from one product image, while many email types need no images at all (older advice, 2021-22). [L0569.018]
  • There is no reliable evidence that emails containing images perform better than text-only emails. Add images only when they serve the message, such as a product photo, and choose your best product images rather than decorative graphics (older advice, 2021-22). [L0569.025]
  • A combined image of a picture and short text, such as a collage of customer testimonial screenshots, can be made in Canva and placed as an image block in a Klaviyo email. Used in abandoned cart emails, this adds social proof without making the whole email an image. [L0573.037]
  • Include pinnable product images in emails so subscribers can save products to Pinterest when they are not ready to buy. This keeps the product visible and gives the customer a place to return to later. [L1136.032]
  • Put the text on a white background so it is easy to read. A column of text with a different coloured background on either side is acceptable as long as the area behind the text stays white (older advice, 2021-22). [L0569.017]
  • In a campaign email, white is a clean base for product-focused emails. The background colour is set in the editor's styling options, and offers, text blocks and product feeds are added as extra components on top of this background. [L0573.036]
  • Limit email colours to your brand colours, usually one or two primary colours already used on the website. Use these colours when you need to add colour to an email (older advice, 2021-22). [L0569.019]
  • Apply one consistent email template to all emails so that subscribers recognise every message as coming from your brand immediately (older advice, 2021-22). [L0569.020]
  • Consistent design builds on trust that already exists with subscribers, while changing styles and layouts from email to email creates doubt about who is sending. Keep the design recognisable across all emails (older advice, 2021-22). [L0569.021]
  • Email design should stay simple and not be overthought. Most results come from strong copy, a clear message and a call to action that is easy to identify (older advice, 2021-22). [L0569.022]

Klaviyo Editor Structure

  • The Klaviyo email editor builds content from sections and blocks, like Shopify theme editing. A button block cannot sit between two blocks of text directly. Add two text blocks and insert the button between them. [L0573.020, L0607.026]
  • In a campaign, add a product feed: click Product > Create product feed, then show a collection, best-selling products or most viewed products over a set period such as 90 days. This promotes the main products during an event. [L0573.029, L0607.046]
  • The dynamic product section works the same way. Under Products, click Create product feed, then choose to show best-selling or most viewed products over a set time range, for example 90 days. [L0607.046, L0573.029]

Modular Templates and Content Blocks

Building Blocks

  • Email modules work like building blocks. Each one can be reused and stacked in any order inside an email. The same modular approach applies to both campaigns and flows. [L0610.001]
  • Content blocks and modules are the individual parts of an email, while a template is the complete email assembled from them. Using one shared set of content blocks and templates keeps campaigns and flows consistent. [L0610.002]
  • Build a system of reusable modular content blocks rather than one individually designed email. The same blocks can then be used for every campaign and every flow you send in the future. [L1263.016]
  • Some modules, such as M19, are universal and nearly every brand can use them. Those are the first modules to build in a library. [L0610.008]
  • With a ready module library, done-for-you clients can build an email in three to five minutes instead of designing each email from scratch. [L0610.009]
  • All modules are tested on mobile and in dark mode, so emails built from them render correctly without extra checking. Building from tested blocks lets you produce emails quickly with consistent results. [L0610.010]
  • The always-on programme covers both campaigns and automated flows and should remain editable in the email platform. Sliced-image designs look attractive but cannot scale into flows and break in real inboxes. [L1263.019]

Design Method Comparison

  • Email design methods are compared on two axes: programme fit (editability, render safety and scalability, higher is better) and design wow factor (higher to the right). Every method involves trade-offs, so no single option wins on all axes. [L1263.001]
  • The same method comparison applies to other email platforms such as Omnisend. The tool name changes, but the trade-offs between editability, render safety and design polish stay the same. [L1263.014]
Method Strength Weakness
Self-design in Canva or Figma Full control if you have real graphic and email design skill Easily looks amateur, can damage the brand, often abandoned after weeks or months
Bought template (Envato, ThemeForest, similar marketplaces) Quick start, can look good on paper Often made by graphic designers, may be image-based, mobile and cross-client rendering often unverified
Third-party tool (BeFree, later renamed LGE Studio) Polished, modular templates Two-tool workflow, pushed to Klaviyo as one large HTML block, every edit needs a re-push
Professional designer file (Figma or Canva) Pleasing, professional look Delivered as locked images, text can be changed but layout and module order cannot, does not scale into flows
Klaviyo native editor Editable, render-safe, repeatable Needs patience and skill to produce polished design
Klaviyo Composer (alpha at time of lesson) Expected to allow better native, editable Klaviyo designs Still in alpha, full extent unknown
Modular system with reusable content blocks On-brand, editable, scalable for campaigns and flows Less polished than a bespoke designer look

[L1263.002]

  • Only self-design emails in Canva or Figma if you have professional graphic design and email design skills. If you lack them, avoid this route entirely. [L1263.003]
  • Graphic design and email design are different skills. Email design depends on specific psychology and on the placement of modules, which a graphic designer may not know. [L1263.004]
  • Founders who design their own emails without the right skills often spend weeks or months on it and then give up for another route. Self-designed emails can also do real damage to the brand. [L1263.005]
  • BeFree, later rebranded as LGE Studio, is a third-party tool for building modular email templates and pushing them to Klaviyo. The pushed result is one large HTML block, so every change requires returning to the tool and pushing again. [L1263.010]
  • Professional designer files in Figma or Canva are usually locked images. Buyers often can change text but cannot change the layout, and the building blocks do not stack in any order. [L1263.011]
  • The Klaviyo native editor produces editable, render-safe emails. It requires patience and design skill to make the result look polished. [L1263.012]
  • Klaviyo Composer was in alpha and expected to be released publicly within about one month of the lesson. It is meant to help design better emails natively in Klaviyo while staying editable and scalable. [L1263.013]
  • The modular system trades some visual polish for on-brand consistency, editability and scalability. It suits operators who send many emails and value speed over a bespoke look. [L1263.015]
  • The modular email system described in this method is also a two-tool workflow, similar to a third-party tool setup. Its main advantage is reusable content blocks and campaign-level scaling, not a single-tool process. [L1263.021]
  • The modular email system is positioned as the most widely rendered option among the methods compared, and it should work on mobile and in dark mode. It is chosen for scalability first, with polish as a secondary consideration. [L1263.022]
  • On the programme-fit scale (editability, render safety, scalability), a modular system built with an AI approach and Klaviyo's native editor score roughly equal. The sliced-image designer route may look more impressive, but it breaks in real inboxes and cannot scale into flows. [L1263.020]
  • Choose a professional designer if the priority is a fully bespoke look and you accept locked, non-editable files. Choose the modular system if the priority is editability, consistent branding and scaling across campaigns and flows. [L1263.018]

Launch Procedure for Flows

  1. Create the flow and load the matching template for each email, in the order given. [L0606.006]

  5. Turn the flow on only when all emails pass. [L0606.006, L0606.021]

Email body copy and calls to action

  • Older advice (2021-22): an email has one job, which is to get the reader to visit the website. Trying to close the sale inside the message misses that job, because the purchase decision belongs to the site. [L1460.005]

  • Length matters less than whether each paragraph keeps the reader's interest. Long copy can outperform short copy when the reader is already interested, because he will read several pages to learn more. [L1369.097]

  • A short promotional email that produced a single tracked result used this structure [L1356.072]:

    1. The headline is used as the subject line.
    2. Two or three one-line payoff sentences follow.
    3. One big promise.
    4. One quote from an authority figure.
    5. One link, as the only call to action.

    The email revealed no product details, so readers had to click to learn more. In that tracked campaign, roughly 11 out of every 100 recipients clicked. This is a single result to watch, not a guaranteed benchmark. [L1356.072]

  • Place social proof directly beside each unique value proposition, such as a review, a bestseller or a customer story. Claims alone persuade less than claims shown with evidence. [L1470.017]

  • Remove text that repeats information already in the email header, such as a brand name next to a logo. This keeps the email shorter and easier to scan. [L1470.015]

  • Compose.ai is a tool for writing marketing emails, useful when a store needs many email drafts without hiring a copywriter. [L1416.009]

Brand colors in the email

  • Keep the email text and background colors black and white, and use only the brand color for the button fill, so the emails match the store's look without being distracting. [L1317.006]
  • The Chrome extension "ColorPick Eyedropper" (named "color picker" in the lesson) copies the six-digit hex code of any color on a page to the clipboard, so the exact brand color can be pasted into Shopify's color fields. [L1317.007]

Brand voice and project files

  • Keep the brand kit, tone-of-voice files, templates and campaign work in one permanent project that the AI can always access. Files in a one-off chat are lost when the chat closes, and then the AI guesses at the brand instead of using the real files. [L1506.001]
  • Build the work in a fixed order: brand kit and tone of voice first, then the email templates and universal content blocks built from them, then campaigns built from both. Each step reads the output of the previous one, so every file must sit in the same place for this hand-off to work. [L1506.002]
  • A tidy project folder for this email workflow uses numbered folders for each stage, such as workspace setup, brand context, template from scratch and building campaigns, plus a stack setup folder and an archive folder for older files. [L1506.030]

Claude Cowork workspace setup

Workspace setup for Claude Cowork and Klaviyo [L1506.006]:

  1. Download the Claude desktop app from claude.com and install it like any other program.
  2. Open the app, which starts on the Chat tab, and switch to Cowork.
  3. Go to Projects and click New project, then choose to start from scratch.
  4. Name the project, for example Email Template Project, and optionally add a short description such as building email templates to push to Klaviyo. No detailed instructions are needed.
  5. Choose the project location folder and click Create.
  6. Confirm that Memory is switched on for the project.
  7. Select the model (Opus is used in this setup) and choose the approval mode (see the approval setting).
  8. Create a dedicated folder inside the project location and put all downloaded course files into it.
  9. Always start new chats from inside the project, never from a standalone chat or a new Cowork task.
  • Creating a project: in Cowork, go to Projects, click New project and choose to start from scratch. Give it a name and a short description, then choose the project location folder and click Create. The Memory toggle is shown in the same dialog and must be on. [L1506.007]
  • The project instructions field is optional. A short line of context describing the goal, such as building email templates to push to Klaviyo, is enough to start. [L1506.008]
  • Project memory must be switched on so Claude keeps the conversation history across all chats in the same project. Without it, the brand and background must be re-explained in every new chat. [L1506.009]
  • In a project, the page shows an Instructions section, a Context section where files are added with the plus button, and a Memory section where you can ask Claude to remember something. All chats started in the project appear there with their history. [L1506.010]
  • The workspace setup for AI-written Klaviyo email templates takes about five minutes and is done once. If it is skipped, the AI loses access to the brand kit and image files and starts guessing at the brand. [L1506.026]

  2. Click the plus button, choose Create skill, then Upload skill.
  3. Drag the skill files from the course modules into the upload window.

Modular content blocks and master template

  • Universal content blocks are reusable email building blocks. A set of them with a master template lets the same modules power welcome flows, campaigns and one-off sends, and templates built this way stay consistent across all sends. [L1361.011]
  • Universal content blocks are created with the email template builder skill from two reference images, an anatomy image and a module library image, together with brand-kit.json. The output is a set of blocks and a master template pushed into Klaviyo. [L1361.021]
  • Once universal content blocks exist in Klaviyo, new emails are built quickly by swapping images and reordering a few modules, which keeps every send consistent in look. [L1361.015]
  • After the foundation stage (universal content blocks and master template), a brand can already stop and use working templates for sales postcards, product showcases and editorial emails, because later stages only add flows, audits and segmentation. [L1361.014]
  • Do not push an AI-written email into Klaviyo as one large raw HTML block, because the result cannot be edited in Klaviyo and must be re-exported to change it. Push templates and universal content blocks as structures that stay editable in Klaviyo. [L1506.017]

AI-assisted build sequence

Planned sequence of the AI email build [L1361.013]:

  1. Set up the tech stack and install all tools (Claude Cowork, Klaviyo MCP, Brandfetch MCP, optional image tools).
  2. Create a brand context document from the brand kit (brand-kit.json with voice, colors and fonts).
  3. Build the universal content blocks and master template with the email template builder skill, then push them into Klaviyo.

Rendering and testing

  • Email templates must render well in major email clients such as Gmail, Yahoo and Hotmail, on mobile devices and in dark mode. Test each template across these before using it in production. [L1361.012]

Hero image, layout and links in Klaviyo

  • Use a welcome email hero image of 1250 x 2335 px. Check the file quality after downloading and before uploading, since blurry images reduce trust. [L1470.013]
  • Check a hero image's dimensions and file size in the file properties before uploading it. The example welcome hero measured 1250 x 2335 px at 309 KB and looked sharp at that size. [L1470.030]
  • Set the top padding of the email content block to 30 px so the first element does not sit against the top edge. A text block that is not full width on mobile is acceptable for this layout. [L1470.014]
  • In Klaviyo's email builder, select the hero image block, choose Upload image, and drag the file in to replace the old one. The Image block settings include Link address, Image size with Width set to auto, Fill column on mobile, Alignment, Background color, Border, and Padding. Preview the email before saving to confirm the image and padding render correctly on desktop and mobile. [L1470.029]
  • To link an email or SMS button to a bestseller page on most Shopify stores, open storedomain.com/collections to see the store's pre-built collection list. Open the bestseller collection there and copy its URL into the link field. [L1470.028]
  • In the greeting, insert the first_name profile property with a default fallback so subscribers without a stored name still get a natural opening line. Open Preview profile info to check how the greeting renders with a real subscriber profile before going live. [L1470.031]

Further details

  • To prevent image distortion in Shopify email templates on mobile: 1. Click the Mobile Preview tab while editing the email. 2. Check how images render on mobile. 3. Select each image and add padding, typically 10 pixels on each side. 4. This provides space between images and the email borders. [L0278.034]
