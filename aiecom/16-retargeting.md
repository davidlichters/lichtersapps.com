# 16. Retargeting

## Why Retargeting Matters and What to Expect

### Why Most Visitors Leave Without Buying

- A conversion rate is never 100 percent, so most visitors leave without buying, and remarketing ads bring those visitors back. [L0586.010]
- Plan on most visitors not buying on their first visit, even if the store is well designed, converts well, and has strong products. The purpose of remarketing is to get them to take the first purchase step. [L0676.016]
- Most first-time visitors leave a store without buying. Retargeting ads bring them back to the products they already viewed, so the brand stays in front of the people who are closest to ordering. [L0733.001]
- About 97.7 percent of visitors leave a store without buying on their first visit. Retargeting and Facebook dynamic product ads (DPA) are used to bring these visitors back. [L1031.010]
- Typical e-commerce conversion rates are around 1 percent, 2 percent or 0.5 percent, so most visitors leave without buying on that visit. Retargeting aims to keep these non-buying visitors engaged so they can return and complete a purchase. [L1240.002]
- In an example store with about 500,000 dollars of monthly sales, the conversion rate was below 1 percent. Since no site reaches a 100 percent conversion rate, most visitors leave without buying, and retargeting them has real value. [L1203.016]
- Visitors who buy on their first visit are a minority, so retargeting covers the rest. [L0771.040]
- Shoppers forget a store quickly, even with a strong domain name, so remarketing is needed to bring them back. *(Older: Source A, 2021.)* [L0671.017, L0671.293]
- Skipping retargeting means leaving sales on the table, because many visitors leave without buying and need repeated exposure to convert. Treat retargeting as a standard part of the setup, not an optional extra. [L0920.015]

### The Case for Retargeting

#### Reaching People Who Already Showed Interest

- Retargeting is effective because it reaches people who have already shown interest in the product. It works best running alongside cold prospecting ads rather than replacing them. [L0561.060]
- Remarketing targets people who already showed interest, and it brings previous website visitors back to the store to complete their orders. [L0597.011]
- Remarketing recaptures sales from first-visit non-converters by re-engaging visitors who demonstrated interest in specific products. [L0163.010]
- Remarketing works by showing ads for the exact products a visitor viewed to people who left the site without buying. Clicking the ad returns them to those product pages so they can complete the purchase. [L1029.002]
- Retargeting, also called remarketing, shows ads to people who have already visited your website. [L0720.016]
- Retargeting ads show specific ads to people who have already been on the store and viewed products, to bring them back to finish a purchase. *(Older: Source B, 2021-22.)* [L0771.040]
- Retargeting shows ads to people who already visited the website and took an action such as adding to cart, viewing a product page, viewing a collection page or viewing a content page. Its purpose is to bring those visitors back to complete a purchase. *(Older: Source B, 2021-22.)* [L1239.001]
- Retargeting means showing specific ads to people who already interacted with your store, for example by adding a product to cart, viewing a product page, a collection page or a content page. The audience is built from these on-site actions. *(Older: Source B, 2021-22.)* [L1240.001]

#### Higher Conversion Than Cold Traffic

- Retargeting conversion rates are 2 to 10 times higher than cold traffic conversion rates. [L0257.006]
- People who have already visited the store are expected to convert at a higher rate than cold traffic, which is why retargeting is usually the stronger bottom-of-funnel layer. The lesson claims this holds for about 99 percent of e-commerce stores, but this is a rule of thumb and not a verified statistic. Check it against your own account data. [L0867.005]
- Retargeting lists convert better than cold traffic, so you can bid more aggressively for these users or offer them a coupon. [L0720.016]
- Retargeting ads lifted conversion rate by about 26 percent on average. For example, a 1 percent conversion rate becomes 1.26 percent after adding Facebook dynamic retargeting ads. [L0510.041]
- A cited figure claims retargeting raises the chance that a visitor converts by 70 percent. This only holds when the original traffic was well targeted, so do not apply the figure to poorly targeted campaigns. [L1031.011]

#### Lower Cost of Reach

- Converting existing buyers or visitors is said to cost about seven times less than acquiring new customers, so retargeting should carry real budget and attention. This comparison is cited as a general claim, not as a verified benchmark. [L1243.003]
- Retargeting ads show to people who already visited the store, which allows a small budget to bring back visitors who are likely to buy. This is the main way to spend little on paid ads early. *(Older: Source A, 2022.)* [L1281.117]

#### Several Touchpoints Before Purchase

- On average across e-commerce, a customer needs about seven touchpoints with a brand before feeling ready to buy. This is an average, so some customers buy after fewer and others after more contacts. *(Older: Source B, 2021-22.)* [L1239.005]
- Across the e-commerce industry, a customer needs on average about seven touchpoints with a business before buying. Some customers need fewer and some more, so seven is a benchmark, not a fixed rule. *(Older: Source B, 2021-22.)* [L1240.005]
- Because customers need several brand interactions before buying, the goal is to reach them as quickly as possible and keep the brand within their attention until they are ready to purchase. *(Older: Source B, 2021-22.)* [L1239.006]
- Because customers need several brand interactions before buying, the goal is to fit those touchpoints into as short a time as possible. Staying within the customer's attention until they are ready to buy is the purpose of retargeting. *(Older: Source B, 2021-22.)* [L1240.006]
- Retargeting keeps the brand top of mind during the period before purchase, so that when the customer is ready to buy they think of this store rather than competitors who are also targeting them. *(Older: Source B, 2021-22.)* [L1239.008]
- Retargeting is also meant to keep your brand top of mind, so that when a visitor is ready to buy, they choose your store and not a competitor. Direct conversions from these ads are a welcome bonus, not the only goal. *(Older: Source B, 2021-22.)* [L1240.008]

#### Competition and Brand Choice

- The main remarketing goal is to make sure no lead or customer is lost, because many competitors sell similar products, and the customer should choose the brand when ready to buy. Abandoned cart emails and dynamic product ads are the basic must-have tactics. [L1076.072]

#### Platform Advantage

- A Shopify store lets the seller run retargeting for visitors who did not buy, which is hard to do on Amazon. Retargeting works best when the traffic is sent to a store focused on the seller's own products. [L1153.070]

### Retargeting Setup and Process

#### Audiences and Ad Creative

- Remarketing targets website visitors and past buyers who did not complete a purchase or made a one-time purchase. It uses abandoned cart emails, custom audiences on Facebook and Google, and dynamic or static ad creative to re-engage them. [L0240.020]
- Facebook dynamic product ads (DPA) are used to bring back visitors who left without buying. [L1031.010]
- The ideal return point is the product page the visitor was already viewing, because that is where the first purchase action is most likely. [L0676.014]
- Missing follow-up such as retargeting or email lets visitors who leave without buying disappear, which pulls the conversion rate down. Retargeting must be planned as part of the traffic review, because it runs on the same traffic platforms. *(Older: Source B, 2021-22.)* [L0473.010]

#### Audience Window and Budget

- Facebook retargeting ROAS depends on the audience window. A 4-day audience usually performs much better than a 30- or 60-day audience. With small budgets and a good traffic source, a 30 to 50 times return on ad spend is realistic. [L0971.018]

#### Example Sequence

*(Older: Source B, 2021-22.)*

1. Send visitors to the store from Google Shopping ads and search traffic.
2. Track the visitors who did not buy on their first visit.
3. Retarget those visitors with email marketing, Facebook ads and Google ads.
4. Keep showing products until the visitor is ready to buy, then move them to purchase.
5. Repeat the cycle with new first-time search traffic.

[L0458.051]

#### Example From Source C

- Retargeting drove most sales for this store. Visitors often leave on their first visit, then return after retargeting and are pushed to buy by a compelling offer. Plan retargeting ads for visitors who did not purchase. [L1077.041]

### Do Not Judge Retargeting Only by Direct Conversions

#### Direct Conversion Varies by Ticket Size

- Retargeting should be seen as more than a direct conversion tool, because many visitors do not convert immediately but still benefit from repeated exposure to the brand. *(Older: Source B, 2021-22.)* [L1239.003]
- Retargeting ads should not be judged only by direct conversions. Low-ticket products often show high conversion rates from retargeting, while high-ticket products often show no directly attributable conversions even though the ads still influence buyers. *(Older: Source B, 2021-22.)* [L1240.003]
- On low-ticket products, retargeting ads often show a high, directly measurable conversion rate. On high-ticket products, retargeting often has no clearly attributable conversion rate, yet it still serves a purpose and should not be judged only by direct sales. *(Older: Source B, 2021-22.)* [L1239.004]

#### Check the Role Before Cutting

- Retargeting campaigns that generate clicks and cost money but no attributed sales are not automatically failing, especially for higher-ticket products. Before cutting them, check their role in keeping the brand in front of the customer. *(Older: Source B, 2021-22.)* [L1240.004]

#### Further details

- At any time only about 10% of the total available market is ready to buy, which is the bottom-of-funnel segment that needs little extra effort to convert; growing traffic increases the share of audience that needs education and support. [L0414.037]

## When to Start and How Much to Spend

### Timing: Launch Day or After Traffic and Sales

Sources differ on when paid retargeting should start. Launch-day setup is recommended in most sources. Several sources also require store traffic and sales before paid retargeting is funded.

**Set up at launch**

- Set up Facebook remarketing from the first day, alongside Google Ads, because most first-time visitors from search do not buy on arrival (figure cited: about 98%), so they need to be brought back (older advice, 2019). [L0663.017]
- Facebook ads can be set up from day one, but only for remarketing to people who already visited the store, not for cold prospecting (older advice, 2019). [L0356.054]
- Set up remarketing campaigns on Google, Bing and Facebook at launch, so visitors who leave without buying can be shown the products again. [L0393.017]
- Start remarketing campaigns on Google and Facebook early in the launch, so visitors who did not buy can be reached again. [L0532.021]
- Set up Facebook remarketing campaigns to start as soon as the first store traffic arrives, not later, so retargeting can convert from the launch day (older advice, 2021). [L0671.015, L0671.291]
- Even one extra remarketing sale every few days can be very profitable early on. This is why remarketing should be live at launch (older advice, 2021). [L0671.016, L0671.292]
- Run small remarketing campaigns on both Google and Meta from the start, alongside Shopping (2025). [L0667.003]
- On Facebook, start with remarketing only. Do not spend on cold audiences or brand awareness initially (older advice, 2020). [L0245.037]

**Wait for traffic, sales and performance**

- Retargeting requires store traffic and sales first, because the audiences are built from visitors and buyers. Without early sales there is nothing to retarget. [L0661.062]
- Abandoned cart emails and exit-intent capture are the first retargeting layer. Paid remarketing (Google and Facebook) comes after the store has traffic (older advice, 2020). [L0468.136]
- Start retargeting after about two weeks of consistent performance from the product. Before that, the audiences are too small and the data too thin. [L0648.052]

**How to read the conflict**

- The launch-day sources describe setting up campaigns and capturing the first visitors. [L0663.017, L0393.017, L0532.021, L0671.015, L0671.291]
- The traffic-first sources describe when paid budget should be funded and grown. [L0661.062, L0468.136, L0648.052]
- The only dividing line given in the sources is the two-week performance rule. [L0648.052]

- Putting most of the budget into remarketing is a warning sign (older advice, 2020). It means the store is not finding new prospects. [L0961.065]
- Response: shift the budget back toward Google Ads for new visitors. [L0961.065]
- Keep the remarketing share closer to 10% in the early phase. [L0961.063]
- The warning sign applies while the store is still finding its first buyers. Source B's growing share (2021-22, older) describes a later stage, once returning visitors and buyers exist. No threshold is given in the sources. [L0961.065, L0563.036]

### When to Scale

**Conditions before scaling**

- Retargeting starts after about two weeks of consistent performance from the product. [L0648.052]
- Scale only once traffic and sales are growing. [L0733.015]
- Increase the budget only once it brings sales. [L0468.137]

**Scaling steps**

1. Confirm about two weeks of consistent product performance. [L0648.052]
2. Confirm that traffic and sales are growing. [L0733.015]
3. Duplicate the campaign to target only 7-day website visitors. This audience usually converts best because those visitors were on the store recently. [L0733.015]
4. Increase the budget only where it brings sales. [L0468.137]
5. Add longer audiences, such as the 180-day audience, later for promotions. [L0733.015]

## Tracking, Pixels and Data Prerequisites

### Prerequisites for Tracking and Data

#### Setting Up Pixels and Tags

- Retargeting pixels and tracking codes from Google Ads, Facebook and Google Analytics must be installed on all content pages of the site. [L0493.002]
- Retargeting only works after tracking is set up on each ad network you plan to use. Cookies placed on visitors who view pages or take actions on your site become the basis for the audiences you target. [L1390.001]
- The Google Ads retargeting pixel must be installed on the Shopify store before a website visitor audience can fill with visitors. Without it the audience stays empty. [L1242.009]
- Add the remarketing tag through the Feeds for Google Shopping app before launch so that visitors are tracked from their first visit. The remarketing campaigns themselves are set up after the store goes live. [L1145.048]
- Every visitor should be pixeled on every store you own, so you can retarget them on Facebook and Google later. Stores you own can carry the pixel because you control their code, but you cannot track visitors through affiliate links. [L0923.011]

#### Avoiding Duplicate Installs

- Install each tag or pixel only once, because duplicate installs of the same tag distort tracking. [L0493.003]
- Installing the Google Shopping feed app's tracking events twice creates duplicate events with the same names in the tag list. Choose the one labeled 'purchase in category' and keep only one copy of each event in the account. [L0420.011]

### Verifying Firing

- Pixels and tracking codes often exist in an existing store but are set up incorrectly or not firing, so verify that they register site visits. [L0493.003]

### Data Sharing Settings

- Since 2023, GA4 remarketing audiences require data sharing settings to be enabled, or audiences show a 'not eligible' status. [L0274.026]
- Enable the following settings:
  1. Granular location and device data
  2. Google insights
  3. Ads personalization
  4. Acknowledgement of user data collection [L0274.026]

### Privacy and Ad-Blocker Losses

- A remarketing audience can show zero impressions even with daily clicks, because ad blockers and browser privacy prevent tracking of part of the visitors. Estimate the trackable share first; for example, about 40 of 50 daily clicks may be trackable. [L0470.010]
- Visitors who block tracking in their browser are excluded from Google and Meta remarketing audiences, and repeat visits from the same person count only once. [L1283.109]
- Remarketing audiences built on advanced privacy blocking still work using platform-owned data such as link clicks, comments and shares. Platforms with their own data, such as Google-owned YouTube, can still target viewers. [L1112.027]

### Traffic Minimums

- Each advertising platform requires a minimum amount of traffic, usually within a 30-day window, before a retargeting audience can be built and activated. [L0493.004]
- A low-traffic store can keep its audiences below the 100-person minimum for weeks, so check audience size before changing bids or budgets. [L1283.109]
- A new store with low traffic can still create audiences in Google Ads and Facebook ahead of time. Such an audience stays inactive until enough visitors fill the pool, then it can be used straight away and ads can run on it automatically. [L0493.005]

### Filtering Low-Quality Visits

- Add a minimum session duration rule to each audience, or delay pixel firing with a trigger rule in Google Tag Manager. Set the delay to 30 seconds so bouncing visitors stay out of the cookie pool. [L1390.018]

## Planning Audiences: Funnel Stages, Segmentation and Windows

### Audience Foundations

- An audience is any group of people who took a specific action on your website or social media accounts, such as visiting a page or engaging with a post. Audiences are the basis for every later ad and email campaign. [L0757.005]
- Build audiences so you can promote current and future offers to them, aiming for the highest possible ROAS. The same audiences are used for search ads and audience ads. [L0757.006]
- A remarketing audience is a group of people who visited your website, identified through tracking cookies. Showing Google or Facebook ads to those visitors is remarketing, and it builds the audience for later promotions. [L0801.082] *(older, 2021)*
- Remarketing audiences grow from paid ads, organic search and direct visits. Each visitor who lands on the store adds to the audience for future campaigns. [L0801.083] *(older, 2021)*
- Retargeting needs no prior sales. People who viewed products on the site without buying are enough to fill the remarketing audience. [L1086.029] *(older, 2019)*
- Do not target people who have never visited the store or do not know the brand with Meta remarketing ads. Remarketing is only for visitors who already showed interest by viewing products on the store. [L1020.007]
- Audiences are what make retargeting work, because they turn the cookie pool into groups that receive more personal and relevant ads. Without segmenting them, most of the benefit of retargeting is lost. [L1390.014] *(older, 2021-22)*
- Retention is the number of days a person stays in an audience after the triggering action. Visitor and purchase audiences are dynamic and refresh automatically. A person who visited within the last 7 days is in the 7-day audience and drops out on day 8 if they do not return. [L1023.007]
- Build dynamic audiences in Meta and Google before launch, such as 180-day website visitor audiences. They start empty but fill automatically as people visit, and each person drops out after the window ends. [L0934.061]
- The three main audience types are:
  - retargeting and customer match (people who already interacted with the brand or are on your customer list);
  - search intent (people actively searching for relevant terms);
  - lookalikes (people who resemble existing customers).

  Lookalike and search-intent audiences are central to cold traffic campaigns. The lesson gives the first two the most attention. [L1386.016]

### Growing the Audience Pool

- Fill your email list and your invisible audiences using both free (organic) and paid traffic. The aim is to grow every list in the audience table, not only the email list. [L0757.009]

- Start small and expand as traffic grows. Build retargeting audiences for the products and content that matter to the business. More audience opportunities appear as traffic grows over time. [L0493.006] *(older, 2021-22)*

### Audience Types by Funnel Stage

- The units define middle-of-funnel (MOF) and bottom-of-funnel (BOF) audiences. They do not define top-of-funnel retargeting audiences. For cold traffic, the units point to search-intent and lookalike audiences. [L1386.016]
- Facebook remarketing can follow visitors who viewed low-margin product pages, even when those products are excluded from cold Google traffic. This builds brand association without buying cold traffic. [L1068.067] *(older, 2019)*

**Middle of funnel (MOF)**

- Facebook page engagement and Instagram engagement from the last 7, 14 or 30 days, and video viewers who watched 50-95 percent of a video. Create them in the custom audiences section of Business Manager. [L0648.053]
- Sources differ on engagement and video windows:

| Audience | Source value | Context |
|---|---|---|
| Page and Instagram engagers | 7, 14 or 30 days | Source C's narrow MOF pool [L0648.053] |
| Page and Instagram engagers | 90 days | Source D' look-back for this audience [L0315.003] |
| Facebook and Instagram engagement | 180 days | Ecom list-building for the dynamic audience set [L0757.010] |
| Video viewers | 50-95% watched | Source C's MOF definition [L0648.053] |
| Video viewers | 25%, 50% or 75% watched | Source D' BOF table [L0315.003] |

  The units do not say which value is correct. Use the 7-30 day MOF pool when you want a narrow, recent audience, and the longer values when a bigger pool matters more than recency.

**Bottom of funnel (BOF)**

- BOF audiences are people who added to cart, started checkout, or purchased. [L0648.054]
- Always exclude purchasers from retargeting campaigns, unless you are promoting another product to them. [L0648.054]
- Past buyers and non-buyers should have separate remarketing audiences, with date ranges that vary across campaigns. The lists of people who bought and people who did not buy are the basis for this. [L0247.072] *(older, 2021)*

### Segmentation by Pages and Actions

- Segment retargeting audiences by how each visitor interacted with the store, such as which pages they viewed or whether they added to cart. Each segment should receive ads matched to that behaviour. [L1240.013] *(older, 2021-22)*
- Putting all visitors into one or very few retargeting segments gives generic ads that convert poorly. Split the cookie pool into several audiences with different intent levels. [L1390.003] *(older, 2021-22)*
- Using one broad audience, such as everyone who viewed a product in the last 30 days, misses opportunities. Segment audiences by funnel stage so each group receives more relevant content. [L0563.020] *(older, 2021-22)*
- Most people start by retargeting everyone who viewed a product page. That can be enough at very low traffic. As traffic grows, segment the cookie pool further so ads match each group's stage and interest. [L1390.013] *(older, 2021-22)*

**Procedure: building segmented audiences and ads (Source B)**

1. Build a range of retargeting audiences tailored to specific pages and actions on the website. [L0414.038] *(older, 2021-22)*
2. Run a suite of retargeting ads that remind, build trust, educate, recommend and show why to buy from this business. [L0414.038] *(older, 2021-22)*
3. Vary the angle to reduce ad fatigue and match each prospect's stage. [L0414.038] *(older, 2021-22)*

**Procedure: starting from high-value visits (Source B)**

1. Start by retargeting the highest-value visits, such as longer sessions and product or content engagement. [L0563.021] *(older, 2021-22)*
2. Use Analytics Audiences to build highly segmented retargeting audiences. [L0563.021] *(older, 2021-22)*
3. Deliver a different message to each segment based on its stage in the funnel. [L0563.021] *(older, 2021-22)*

### Quality Filters: Time on Site

- A standard retargeting pixel adds every visitor to the audience, including people who stay only 10 seconds on a content or product page. These visitors are very unlikely to buy, yet they are shown ads for 30 to 60 days. This wastes spend and lowers click-through rate. [L0563.018] *(older, 2021-22)*
- Focus retargeting on your most valuable potential customers: visitors who spend more time on the site and engage seriously with products and content. This tightens ad spend and improves conversion rate. [L0563.019] *(older, 2021-22)*
- A narrower product page audience can be limited to visitors who stayed on the product page for longer than 30 seconds. These visitors are further down the funnel than people who only glanced at the page. [L1169.003] *(older, 2021-22)*
- Visitors who stay about 10 seconds are rarely serious buyers. People who spend minutes on the site or on one page are considering a purchase. Filtering by duration focuses the budget on that group and raises click-through rate. [L1390.019] *(older, 2021-22)*
- Time on site is one of the audience types in the segmentation list ("Users by time on site"). [L1390.015] *(older, 2021-22)*

### Lookback Windows by Price and Traffic

**Standard windows for website visitors**

- Website-visitor audiences are commonly built at 7, 30, 60, 90 and 180 days. The right window depends on traffic volume. A store with heavy traffic can use a short 7-day audience as its main pool. A smaller store may need a 30- or 60-day window. [L0623.026]
- One lesson sets the remarketing audience window at 30 days: target only website visitors from the last 30 days. [L0189.003]
- For each audience, create time-based versions covering the last 7, 14 and 30 days. [L1390.023] *(older, 2021-22)*

**Recency groups**

- Create separate audiences for people who visited in the last 7 days, 7-30 days, 30-60 days, and 60+ days. This lets you adjust messaging frequency and offer intensity for each recency group. [L0240.024]
- Three segment names to set up for remarketing: Website Visitors exc. Customers, 7 Days, Website Visitors exc. Customers, 30 Days, and Website Visitors exc. Customers, 180 Days. [L0165.002]

**Price-based duration**

- Retargeting audience duration by product price (lesson guideline):

| Product price | Retargeting audience duration |
|---|---|
| Under $100 | 7 days |
| $100-$500 | 14 days |
| $500-$1,000 | 30 days |
| Over $1,000 | 60-90 days |

  [L1031.013]
- Higher-priced products get a longer audience window, because buyers usually take longer to decide on a larger purchase. Lower-priced products get a shorter window. [L1031.014]
- Shoppers typically make their purchase decision within two to five days of first visiting the store. Plan remarketing windows around that period. [L0972.069]

**Look-back periods by BOF audience (Source D)**

| BOF Audience | Look-back Period |
|---|---|
| Website visitors | 7 days, 14 days, or 30 days |
| Video viewers | 25% watched, 50% watched, or 75% watched |
| Add-to-cart without purchase | 30 days |
| Checkout initiated without purchase | 30 days |
| Instagram/Facebook page engagers | 90 days |
| Email list (uploaded custom audience) | No expiration |

[L0315.003]

**Customer lists**

- Customer lists can use longer windows, such as three to four months. [L1390.023] *(older, 2021-22)*
- The uploaded email list has no expiration. [L0315.003] Use the three-to-four-month window for time-based customer audiences and the no-expiration setting for an uploaded custom audience.

**Shorter or longer windows**

- Shorter remarketing windows usually have a better return on ad spend. A 60-day window is still better than none if the 30-day audience is too small. [L1094.053]

### Narrow-Window Audiences

- Narrow window audience = longer audience minus shorter audience. Example: 14-day product viewers minus 7-day product viewers equals people who entered the audience between day 7 and day 14. Ad networks often cannot set this day range directly, so exclusion rules create it. [L1390.020] *(older, 2021-22)*
- A retargeting ad can be limited to visitors who viewed a product 7 to 14 days ago by targeting the 14-day product viewer audience and excluding the 7-day product viewer audience. This keeps the ads away from people who just visited and stops them once the visit is older than 14 days. [L1342.004] *(older, 2021-22)*
- A 7 to 14 day window reserves the ads for people who are still considering the product. Reaching them too early risks ads that feel pushy. This is a starting setup for the Google and Facebook examples and can be changed based on results. [L1342.038] *(older, 2021-22)*

**Procedure: building the remarketing window structure (Source A)**

1. Create website visitor audiences for 180, 90, 60, 30, 14, 7 and 2 days, if each audience is big enough. [L1094.052]
2. Create one remarketing campaign per audience. [L1094.052]
3. In each campaign, target recent website visitors for that window and exclude visitors from the next shorter window: 180 excludes 90, 90 excludes 60, 60 excludes 30, 30 excludes 14, 14 excludes 7, 7 excludes 2, and 2 excludes nobody. [L1094.052]
4. Exclude recent customers in every campaign. [L1094.052]
5. Set the budget for each campaign separately. [L1094.052]

### Stacking

- Retargeting audience stacking layers a second targeting method on top of the website visitor audience to reach people actively researching. Example: YouTube content in the same category as the business, such as furniture for an office furniture store. [L1510.029]
- Audience size rule for stacking targeting filters:
  - under 5,000 users: do not add an extra topic or placement filter;
  - over 5,000 users: add a filter to target people actively searching the topic.

  [L1510.030, L1510.029]

### Retargeting Only Valuable Visitors

- Two sources give different defaults for the audience pool:
  - Older (2018): retarget every visitor who leaves the store without buying, using Google and Facebook remarketing from the start. This applies to all visitors, not only those who added items to the cart. [L1085.005]
  - Newer (2021-22): focus on the most valuable visitors, meaning those who spend more time and engage seriously. This tightens spend and raises conversion rate. [L0563.019] *(older, 2021-22)*
- Use the focused approach as the default. Reach every product viewer only when traffic is too low to segment, as the 2021-22 guidance allows. [L1390.013] *(older, 2021-22)*
- Filter by duration to keep the budget on visitors who are considering a purchase. [L1390.019] *(older, 2021-22)*
- Do not target people who have never visited the store. [L1020.007]

## Building Google Audiences (Google Ads and GA4)

### Account structure and setup order

- Google Ads and GA4 must be linked before remarketing audiences can be built from website data. Setup order: (1) link Google Ads to Google Analytics, (2) set up data settings in Google Analytics, (3) update data retention in Google Analytics, (4) create remarketing audiences. [L0165.001]
- Create remarketing audiences early, even before any remarketing campaign is running, because they keep filling with users over time. An audience created later starts empty. [L1520.008]
- Google Ads Audience manager builds remarketing audiences from site visitors, app users, YouTube users and customer lists, and shows where each audience can be applied. Google also auto-creates some audiences there. [L1242.008]

### Google Ads: data segments in Audience manager

#### Concepts

- A data segment is a group of people who completed an action on the website, such as visiting a page or buying. Saving them as segments lets you target each group separately later. [L0420.006]
- A newly created audience does not show its size immediately. It can take several hours to populate. Only then does Google Ads show how many people it contains and can be targeted with remarketing ads. [L0420.005]
- The segment name is only for the account owner to recognise the audience later. It does not need to be perfect; choose a clear name that shows what the segment contains. [L0420.012]

#### Naming

- Name the website visitor segment so it identifies the store, the audience and the time window, for example "Store name, visitors 30 days". [L0420.003, L0420.013]
- Name the buyer segment in the same pattern, for example "Store name, purchase 30 days". [L0420.013]
- Use names that show the lookback window and the purpose, for example "540 day retargeting list for search". [L1242.005]

5. Choose **Website visitors**. [L0420.002, L0420.013]

8. Keep the pre-fill option switched on, which adds people who visited in the past 30 days, so the segment starts with existing visitors. [L0420.004, L0420.013]
9. Click **Create segment** and allow several hours for the audience to fill. [L0420.004, L0420.005, L0420.013]
10. Press the plus icon again and select **Website visitors**. [L0420.013]
11. Name the buyer audience, for example "Store name, purchase 30 days". [L0420.013]

## Building Meta, YouTube and Other Platform Audiences

### Audience Tiers by Engagement Depth

Source C sorts retargeting audiences into low-level and high-level engagement. Low-level audiences are easy to capture on fresh pixels and ad accounts. High-level audiences need Shopify or Klaviyo data and more historic data. [L0375.030, L0376.030, L0376.031]

| Engagement level | Audience | Data needed |
|---|---|---|
| Low level | Video views 50% to 95% | Easy to capture on fresh pixels and ad accounts |
| Low level | All website visitors, 30 days | Easy to capture on fresh pixels and ad accounts |
| Low level | FB/IG engagement | Easy to capture on fresh pixels and ad accounts |
| High level | People who added to cart | Shopify/Klaviyo data, needs more historic data |
| High level | All email subscribers | Shopify/Klaviyo data, needs more historic data |
| High level | Buyers (great for LLAs) | Shopify/Klaviyo data, needs more historic data |

[L0375.030, L0376.030, L0376.031]

### Meta Website Visitor Audiences

- Meta website-visitor audiences can be built for 7, 30 or 180 days. A new store with little traffic should use the largest available window, which is the 180-day audience. [L1029.013]
- Facebook website-visitor custom audiences should be built for the last 7, 30, 60 and 180 days. Each window can carry different ads, and the audience gets less warm as the window grows. [L1076.061]
- The sources differ on window sets. L1029.013 lists 7, 30 and 180 days. L1076.061 adds 60 days. Use the 7/30/180 set for a new store with little traffic, and the 7/30/60/180 set when each window needs its own ads. [L1029.013, L1076.061]
- Source C lists "All website visitors, 30 days" as a fresh-pixel audience. Source A recommends 180 days for a new store with little traffic. The sources do not state a traffic threshold between the two. [L0375.030, L1029.013]
- A newly created Meta custom audience can take up to 3 days to finish matching profiles. Ads can run during this time, but the audience grows as it populates. [L1029.014]

### Engagement Audiences on Meta, YouTube and Facebook Pages

- Many ad platforms let you target people who have interacted with a website, YouTube channel or Facebook page. Knowing where your buyers already spend time gives you a shortcut to placing ads and offers in front of them. [L0478.024] *(older advice, 2021-22)*
- Subscribers of a niche YouTube channel or a Facebook page are a pre-qualified audience for targeting. Because they chose to follow a niche source, they are more likely to engage with your offer. [L0478.025] *(older advice, 2021-22)*
- Facebook can also build segmented audiences from user behavior on your site and on the Facebook network, in addition to Google segmentation. [L0563.022] *(older advice, 2021-22)*
- People who engage with a boosted post, through likes, comments or shares, can be saved as remarketing audiences. These audiences can be used later for remarketing campaigns and to show future page posts to some of them. [L0666.065]
- A brand that had been posting content built a Facebook engagement audience of about 280,000 people and an Instagram custom audience of about 140,000 people who engaged in the past 365 days. Pools of this size can be shown new content at very low cost per reach, so check their size regularly as the audience grows. [L0487.071]

### Cold Video Ads and the BOF Warm Pool

- Cold video ads automatically feed the BOF warm pool. Viewers who complete 50% or more of a cold video are automatically added to a custom audience for BOF retargeting, with no manual setup required. [L0315.010]
- Source C lists "Video views 50% to 95%" as a retargeting audience. Source D uses 50% or more as the entry point to the warm pool. The sources do not explain the 95% upper bound. [L0315.010, L0375.030]

Procedure:

1. Deploy cold video ads to broad audiences. Some viewers convert immediately, but most do not. [L0315.011]
2. Automatically, viewers who complete 50% or more of the video are added to a custom warm audience pool. [L0315.011]
3. BOF ads immediately retarget this pool with all five concept angles at lower CPMs. [L0315.011]
4. As cold video spending continues, the warm pool grows daily, and BOF ROAS increases automatically without BOF campaign changes. [L0315.011]

### Audience Growth Cycle: Content, Engagement and Lookalikes

- Retarget the seed audience and the engagement audiences with new content each time a post is published. Then build lookalike audiences from those groups and repeat the cycle. [L0487.046]

Procedure:

1. Publish content that is funny or educational and promote it to the seed audience. [L0487.047]
2. Build custom audiences from people who liked, commented on, or shared the content. [L0487.047]
3. Create lookalike audiences from the seed and engagement audiences. [L0487.047]
4. Show new content to the seed, engagement, and lookalike audiences. [L0487.047]
5. Repeat until the target market knows the brand. [L0487.047]

### Lookalike and Similar Audiences from Retargeting Pools

- Any retargeting audience can be turned into a similar audience in Google or a lookalike audience in Facebook. The platform then finds new users who resemble that group in behavior and demographics, which gives more targeted cold traffic than broad prospecting. [L1390.016] *(older advice, 2021-22)*
- Buyers are the strongest seed for lookalike audiences. Source C marks them as "great for LLAs" and needs Shopify or Klaviyo data to build them. [L0375.030, L0376.031]

### Google Remarketing for Cart and Product-Page Visitors

- Set up Google remarketing for cart and product-page visitors using lesson 6.1.8, so you reach the same audience you are targeting on Facebook. [L0276.007]

## Audience Size, Eligibility and Troubleshooting

### Minimum Audience Sizes per Platform

Ad platforms won't serve ads to an audience until it reaches a minimum size. The thresholds differ by platform, ad type and source, so check the figure that applies to your setup.

#### Google Ads and the Display Network

| Threshold | Source | Applies to | Ids |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 unique visitors within the time window | Source A | Below this, ads won't show even if the audience is set up correctly | [L0733.013] |
| 100 active users in the last 30 days | Source A, 2024 | Display remarketing; ads can run only at this level | [L1285.058] |
| 100 people | Source B, 2021-22 (older) | Custom audiences on the Google Display Network | [L0563.026] |
| 1,000 active members in a network | Source A, 2023 | Remarketing audience; the source specifies the network | [L0804.065] |
| 1,000 unique visitors | Source A, 2019 (older) | Audience error means the list is too small | [L1068.065] |

- The roughly 100-person minimum for a 7-day audience applies only to some Google Ads ad types, not to every Google option. Check the audience size for each ad type before you choose step-by-step sequencing. [L1241.013]

#### Facebook (Meta)

- Facebook remarketing audiences need at least about 100 unique people before ads can serve. Facebook counts fewer visitors than Google Analytics, so ads usually start reliably at around 150 to 200 visitors. (Source A, 2021, older) [L1279.069]
- The roughly 1,000-person minimum for a 7-day Facebook audience is a general guideline, not an exact threshold. Newer stores often reach it only with the all-product-viewers audience, while single-product audiences stay below it. (Source B, 2021-22, older) [L1241.014]
- Facebook's minimum is considerably higher than Google's 100-person minimum for custom audiences. (Source B, 2021-22, older) [L0563.026]

#### General principles for small audiences

- Audiences have minimum size thresholds on most networks. If a segment is too small, keep it set up anyway, since it will fill as traffic grows. Start with the largest segment that is still big enough, such as 14 days upward. (Source B, 2021-22, older) [L0719.037]
- A retargeting audience of around 50 people who visited over two weeks is too small to run alone. Combine several related audiences, such as video viewers, engagers and page visitors, into one larger audience. (Source C) [L0640.055]

### Lookback Windows

#### Lengthening a small audience

| Starting window | Extended window | Source | Ids |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 days | 60 days | Source A, 2023 | [L0251.047] |
| 7 or 30 days | 45 or 60 days | Source A, 2023 | [L1094.050] |
| 14 days | Up to 180 days | Source A, 2021 (older) | [L0853.076] |

- If a remarketing audience is too small, lengthen its window so more people fall into it. A larger audience makes remarketing more effective. (Source A, 2021, older) [L0853.076]
- If a remarketing audience is too small, lengthen its window, for example from 7 or 30 days to 45 or 60 days, so more people fall into the audience. (Source A, 2023) [L1094.050]
- If a remarketing audience shows as Not Eligible because it is too small, expand the lookback window from 30 days to 60 days to reach sufficient audience size for ad serving. (Source A, 2023) [L0251.047]
- Check both the audience size and the membership time frame in days for each remarketing audience. If the audience is too small or the window is too short, it will not fill up with enough people to show ads. (Source A, 2023) [L0470.011]

#### Choosing the starting window

- Create the audience with the longest lookback window, 540 days, and pre-fill it with the past 30 days of visitors. Google remarketing needs at least 1,000 active members in a network. (Source A, 2023) [L0804.065]
- Use a 30-day audience for display remarketing, because a 7-day audience usually has too few people to serve ads. A larger audience is needed for impressions to start. (Source A, 2025) [L0972.066]
- A new store's 7-day website visitor audience usually contains about 30 to 90 people, which is often below the 100 unique visitor minimum.
  1. Use a 30-day audience to start. A new store can usually build more than 100 unique visitors within 30 days.
  2. Create the 7-day audience anyway so it builds in the background.
  (Source A) [L0733.014]

### Checking Audience Size in Google Ads

#### Where to look

- To check whether remarketing audiences are big enough in Google Ads, go to Tools, then Shared Library, then Audience Manager (the Shared Audience Manager). Audiences that are too small show the message "too small to advertise to". Wait until they grow. (Source A, 2022) [L0429.051]
- Navigation labels differ between sources. The 2019 path was Tools, then Audiences. (Source A, 2019, older) [L1068.066]

#### Checking audience sizes

1. In Google Ads, open Shared library.
2. Open Audiences.
3. Click an audience to see the total number of people and how many are eligible to be remarketed to on each Google platform.

(Source A, 2023) [L1094.049]

- Audiences built from GA4 and linked to Google Ads can show a smaller eligible count than the total audience, because Google can only match some visitors. This gap is normal and cannot be fixed. (Source A, 2023) [L1094.048]

#### Estimated sizes in the audience form

- The estimated audience size shown in the audience form is based on past data. It is not always accurate and changes over time. It becomes more reliable as tracking history builds up and data retention is extended. (Source A) [L1520.014]
- Newly created audiences often show fewer than 10 users at first, which is normal. They grow as site traffic increases and the audience has time to fill with data. (Source A) [L1520.015]

### Not Eligible Status

- A remarketing audience that shows as not eligible because it is too small is normal early in a store's life. Wait for more traffic and check the audience size before expecting impressions. (Source A, 2025) [L0807.027]
- If remarketing ads do not serve and there are no errors, the audience is most likely too small. Create a larger audience, such as 60-day website visitors, and add it to the remarketing campaign. (Source A, 2023) [L1094.051]
- Google can flag an audience as too small to target on the Display Network, for example a 30-day product viewer list. Check the audience status before launching. A flagged audience cannot be used for display targeting until it is large enough. (Source B, 2021-22, older) [L1342.007]

### Audience Counts That Differ from Analytics

- Drop-off between GA4 or Universal Analytics counts and the audience counts in Google and Meta is normal, and iOS opt-out settings add to it. Keep all tracking and data-sharing settings maximized to get the largest audience possible. (Source A, 2024) [L0965.048]
- A retargeting audience includes all visitors to the website, not only traffic from Google Ads. Organic visitors therefore count toward audience size. (Source B, 2021-22, older) [L0719.038]

### No Impressions and Identical Counts

#### First checks

- If the remarketing campaign shows no impressions, check that the audience size is enough for each platform under the audience tools, then recreate the campaign. If it gets a few hundred impressions but no clicks over a month, raise the bids. (Source A, 2023) [L0804.066]
- If Google remarketing campaigns serve no impressions and audience counts look identical, check the audiences' creation dates and settings against the process before recreating them. Recreating audiences or campaigns alone may not fix the issue. (Source A, 2025) [L0667.086]

#### When display remarketing still shows zero impressions

1. Confirm the audience is a 30-day audience.
2. Confirm there are enough visitors in the audience, such as the thousands of clicks seen over several months.
3. If it still shows zero impressions, delete the remarketing campaign and recreate it from scratch.

(Source A, 2025) [L0972.067]

### Policy Flags

- Google remarketing can run despite a policy-violation warning, but reach is limited until the flagged content is fixed. (Source A, 2023) [L0855.156]

### Shrinking Audiences After Pauses

- Remarketing audiences shrink when ads are paused, so restarting ads can produce very few impressions. Recovery can take two to four weeks before remarketing returns to profitability. (Source A, 2025) [L0972.068]

### Fixing a Failing Pixel Setup

#### Checking for an empty audience

- If a remarketing audience is empty or shows zero, the tracking pixel or code is not firing and must be reinstalled. (Source A, 2019, older) [L1068.066]

#### Google Ads pixel procedure

1. Repeat the setup steps in the lesson exactly, one more time.
2. Note the exact error message each time it appears.
3. If the same error keeps appearing, contact Google Ads live chat and share the error message.

## Exclusions and Targeting Controls

### Core rule: exclude recent buyers from retargeting

- Exclude customers who completed a purchase in the last 30 days, so no budget goes to showing ads to recent buyers. [L0189.004]
- Exclude people who purchased in the last 30 days from the remarketing audience. Once they buy, showing ads for the products they already bought wastes spend. [L0734.031]
- Build a 30-day purchase audience and exclude it from remarketing ads. This keeps remarketing focused on people who have not yet bought from you. [L0934.063]
- Always exclude purchasers from retargeting audiences, and check that the exclusion is set up before the campaign starts. Retargeting people who have already bought annoys them and wastes ad spend. [L0643.030]

### Purchaser windows by platform

The sources give two windows: 30 days for Google Display remarketing and 180 days for Meta and TikTok. Use the window listed for the platform you are running.

| Platform or context | Purchaser window to exclude | Ids |
|---|---|---|
| General remarketing and Google Display remarketing | 30 days | L0189.004, L0734.030, L0734.031, L0934.063 |
| Meta remarketing ad set | 180 days | L1029.015 |
| TikTok prospecting | 180 days (Complete Purchase pixel event) | L1374.022, L1374.023 |

### Meta: excluding 180-day purchasers

- Always exclude the purchaser audience of the last 180 days from the remarketing ad set, because showing ads to recent buyers wastes budget. [L1029.015]
- Add this audience under Ad exclusions and leave the expansion checkbox unchecked. [L1029.015]

### TikTok: excluding past buyers from prospecting

1. Go to Assets, then Audiences, and click Create new audience. [L1374.022]
2. Build the audience from the pixel for Complete Purchase over the last 180 days. [L1374.022]
3. After two to three weeks of successful running, and before scaling, add this audience as an exclusion to the current prospecting ad sets. [L1374.022]
- TikTok tends to repeatedly show ads to people who already bought, which hurts performance. Buyers from the last 180 days must therefore be excluded manually from prospecting campaigns. [L1374.023]

### BOF and buyer exclusions during MOF retargeting tests

- When testing MOF retargeting audiences, exclude the BOF audiences. [L0375.029, L0376.028]
- Remove buyers from every retargeting ad set. Keep buyers in retargeting only when promoting a new product or a special offer to them. [L0375.029, L0376.028]
- Leaving buyers inside retargeting ad sets wastes budget on people who already purchased, unless the ad promotes a new product or special offer. [L0375.032]

### Time-based remarketing audiences in Google Ads

1. Create separate remarketing audiences by time period: 7-day, 14-day, 30-day, and so on. [L0253.041]
2. In each audience, exclude users from the shorter time periods to prevent overlap. [L0253.041]
3. Example: the 14-day audience excludes users from the 7-day audience. The 30-day audience excludes users from both the 14-day and the 7-day audiences. [L0253.041]
4. This prevents the same user from seeing ads in multiple time-based remarketing audiences. [L0253.041]

### Turning off Optimized Targeting

- Disable Optimized Targeting in the audience segment configuration. Leaving it enabled is a critical error because it prevents proper audience precision. [L0189.007]
- Always turn off optimized targeting in remarketing campaigns. When it is left on, Google may show the ad to audiences outside the remarketing list. [L0734.020]

### Turning off targeting expansion

- Targeting expansion must be switched off for retargeting ad groups. Otherwise the platform can reach cold users who never visited the site, so only the chosen retargeting audience should see the ads. [L1342.005] (Older advice, 2021-2022.)
- Leaving the option that lets Meta reach people beyond the custom audience turned on broadens the ads to anyone Meta predicts will buy. This defeats a remarketing campaign, which should only reach people who already visited the site. [L1029.016]

### Adding audience exclusions in Google Ads

1. Go to Display campaigns and open the remarketing campaign. [L0734.030]
2. Open Audiences, keywords, and content, then click Audiences. [L0734.030]
3. Click show table to see the audience segments. A new audience shows as not eligible while it is under review. [L0734.030]
4. Scroll to the bottom to the Exclusions section and click edit exclusions. [L0734.030]
5. Search for the purchasers audience (people who bought in the last 30 days) and select it. [L0734.030]
6. Click save and check that the exclusion appears in the list below the targeted audience. [L0734.030]
- An audience exclusion cannot be added while the campaign is being created. Add it after the campaign exists, under the campaign's Audiences section and its Exclusions tab. [L1342.006] (Older advice, 2021-2022.)

### Negative keywords

- Remarketing campaigns should remain wide open with minimal negative keywords. Only exclude audiences of recent purchasers. Do not limit remarketing by keywords or other filters, because everyone who visits your site is a potential repeat customer. [L0251.019] (2023.)
- Remarketing does not use negative keywords. Its targeting is based on audiences, so the setup only needs recent website visitors and the exclusion of recent customers. [L1094.054] (2023.)
- The two 2023 sources differ on whether any negative keywords are used: minimal ones according to L0251.019, none according to L1094.054. Both agree that keyword or other filters should not narrow remarketing. [L0251.019, L1094.054]

### Location, demographic and placement settings

- Retargeting usually does not need narrowing by location beyond the target country, or by demographics, because the people in the audience have already visited the store. [L1342.009] (Older advice, 2021-2022.)
- Automatic placements are the default choice for these campaigns. [L1342.009] (Older advice, 2021-2022.)

### Audiences for visitors who did not convert

1. Open Audience Manager and create a website visitors audience: any page visited, matching the URL. [L1510.028]
2. Exclude visitors of a page with the thank you text, which is the Shopify order confirmation page. [L1510.028]
3. Combine the two into a single audience. An optional time window of up to 60 days can be set. [L1510.028]

Formula:

Combined audience = all website visitors (any page, matching the URL) minus visitors to a page containing the thank you text

- The thank you page is the Shopify order confirmation page, which usually includes the thank you text unless it has been changed. [L1510.027]
- The time frame can be extended to 60 days. [L1510.027]
- Remarketing audiences should exclude customers, so people who just bought are not shown ads asking them to complete an order. Exclusions for buyers are applied automatically when the audience uses the non-purchaser setting. [L1520.010]

## Dynamic Product Retargeting on Google

### Concept and Scope

- Dynamic remarketing on Google targets people who viewed products on your store but did not buy. The ads show those exact products and link to the same product pages [L0163.009, L0168.004, L0586.011].
- Dynamic ads are the most important form of Google retargeting because they perform best. Standard display and YouTube remarketing are also possible, but test them for profitability first [L1357.015].
- Dynamic ads are the fifth element of the Power Five and drive dynamic remarketing [L0649.014].
- Dynamic ads are the form of Google remarketing to use with Shopping ads so customers see the products they viewed [L0964.128].
- Dynamic ads pull images and links from the product feed, so each viewer sees their own products. Each image links to its own product page. A static display ad shows the same image and destination to everyone [L0733.002].
- Dynamic ads pull products from the Merchant Center feed based on each visitor's views. Clicking an ad opens the exact product, so no product-level setup is needed in the ad itself [L0734.022].
- Dynamic display retargeting follows visitors across the Google Display Network, including when they browse other websites [L0558.001].
- Dynamic ads automatically show products the visitor already looked at, including items added to cart [L0276.008].
- Google remarketing ads also appear on any website monetised by Google AdSense, including eBay, without special configuration. *(Older advice, 2021. The unit heading names Facebook, but its rule describes Google remarketing.)* [L0270.032]

### Feed and Product Requirements

- Dynamic ads show products from your product feed. A new store with an empty feed shows no products, so the feed must contain your products before the ads are useful [L0558.010].
- Google Display remarketing campaigns update automatically when the product feed changes in Merchant Center [L0189.014].
- Products used in dynamic display ads must pass Shopping ads policy review. Until they pass, they may show as disapproved or invalid for that destination. *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* [L1170.044]

### Visitor Tagging and Tracking

- Dynamic retargeting requires each visitor to be tagged with the products they viewed. This is what makes product-specific ads possible [L0558.002].
- Custom code for this tagging often breaks after Shopify platform updates. For non-developers, a maintained app is the lower-risk option [L0558.002].

### ROI Hunter Setup

Use ROI Hunter as the standard route for non-developers. Installation and sync take a couple of minutes, and no custom code is required [L0558.013].

1. Search the Shopify App Store for ROI Hunter and open the app by ROI Hunter [L0558.004].
2. Click Get to install the app [L0558.004].
3. Run the quick sync. It asks for your starting budget and other initial settings [L0558.004].
4. Optionally preview the ads and upload your logo [L0558.004].
5. Confirm that the three display campaigns and their audiences were created automatically [L0558.004].
6. Manage all further campaign changes in Google Ads, not inside Shopify [L0558.004].

### ROI Hunter Campaigns and Audiences

- ROI Hunter creates three separate display campaigns [L0558.006]:

| Campaign | Audience it targets |
|---|---|
| Dynamic Remarketing for Product Viewers | Product Viewers |
| Dynamic Remarketing for Category Viewers | Category Viewers |
| Dynamic Remarketing for Cart Abandoners | Cart Abandoners |

- ROI Hunter creates these smart audiences automatically in Google Ads. The default lookback window is 7 days [L0558.007]:
  - Cart Abandoners (past 7 days) [L0558.007]
  - Category Viewers (past 7 days) [L0558.007]
  - Product Viewers (past 7 days) [L0558.007]
- At first launch, keep the default audience conditions instead of editing them [L0558.008].
- Editing conditions is optional and only worth doing once you have data to test against [L0558.008].
- To edit conditions, open the audience in Google Ads Audience Manager and use the edit list option. The audiences appear under their names, for example Cart Abandoners, Category Viewers and Product Viewers [L0558.009].

### Management and Optimisation

- The only ongoing adjustment is increasing the daily budget as the store scales [L0189.014].
- You do not optimise dynamic ads directly. Improvement comes from increasing the volume of qualified visitors to your store [L0276.008].

### Expected Clicks, Conversions and Cost

- Dynamic retargeting on the Display Network usually produces cheaper clicks and higher conversion rates than standard retargeting, because the ads show the products the visitor already looked at [L0558.011].

## Dynamic Product Ads on Meta

### Retargeting Window by Product Price

- Dynamic product ad retargeting window depends on product price. Rule of thumb: [L0510.040]

| Product price | Retargeting window |
|---|---|
| Under $100 | 7 days |
| $100 to $500 | 14 days |
| $500 to $1,000 | 30 days |
| Over $1,000 | Longer window |

- For most advertisers, 30 days is a suitable starting point. [L0510.040, L0510.042]
- Test the windows. The longer the sales cycle, the longer the retargeting window should be. [L0510.040]

### Setup Requirements

- Dynamic retargeting shows people the exact products they viewed on the site. [L1203.025]
- It requires the Facebook pixel on the site and an uploaded product feed. [L1203.025]
- It is the fourth campaign type to launch. [L1203.025]

### Format and Creative

- The usual dynamic retargeting format is a carousel that shows the viewed product and related accessories. The product and price are visible, so people recognize what they looked at, which makes them more likely to click. [L0510.043]
- Dynamic product ads are the main Meta remarketing format. They show the products a visitor actually viewed on the store, with product images, descriptions and text filled in automatically per visitor. They run across Facebook, Instagram and Messenger. [L1020.008]
- Example: a jewelry store visitor views three specific rings or necklaces on the site and then leaves. Later, on Instagram, the visitor sees those three exact products in an ad with a Shop now button that leads back to them. [L1020.009]

### Improving Dynamic Product Ads

1. Review the dynamic product ads every two weeks and ask what can be improved. [L0510.042]
2. Set the audience duration by product price and sales cycle, usually starting at 30 days. [L0510.042, L0510.040]
3. Use dynamic text in the primary text that shows the product name and price. [L0510.042]
4. Add a price overlay to the image to make the price visible. [L0510.042]
5. If the audience is small, use link clicks as the optimization event instead of website conversions. [L0510.042]

### Landing Pages

- Mistake: sending paid traffic to the homepage loses buyers. Each product in a multi-product ad should link to its own product sales page where the customer can enter payment, not to the store homepage. [L0643.033]
- Dynamic multi-product ads were a fairly new format, about six to twelve months old at the time. Facebook's automatic ordering of products by best sales was being phased out, so do not rely on it to show your top seller first. [L0643.065]

### Cross-Device Behavior

- Facebook can recognize the same shopper across devices. A visit on a work desktop can lead to a retargeting ad on a personal phone later. [L0510.067]
- Results are less predictable for B2B products with longer sales cycles, because the ad may be seen at a different time and on a different device than the original visit. [L0510.067]

### Remarketing Workflow

1. A qualified visitor arrives at the store through a targeted search such as Google or Bing and views a product page. [L1020.006]
2. The visitor leaves the store without buying. [L1020.006]
3. Meta Ads Manager shows the visitor an ad for the product or products they looked at. [L1020.006]
4. The visitor clicks the ad and is sent back to the exact page they were viewing. [L1020.006]

### Combining with Other Ad Types

- Older advice (2019, so outdated): use Facebook remarketing with a simple dynamic product ad group that retargets visitors who came from Google. Keep the daily budget small, such as $5 per day. [L1002.052]

### Step-by-Step Setup on Google Display

1. Log in to Google Ads, click Campaigns in the left navigation, click New Campaign, then select "Create a campaign without guidance." [L0189.001]
2. Select Display as the campaign type and click Continue. [L0189.001]
3. Enter your store's homepage URL as the Website. Create a Campaign name in the format [YourStore].com Remarketing. Choose the Country where you sell and set Language to English (or your store's primary language). [L0189.001]

6. Set Daily budget to approximately 10% of your total Shopping Ads budget (example: $10/day Shopping = $2/day Remarketing). Change Bidding focus to "Viewable impressions" and set Viewable CPM to $10. Click Next. [L0189.001]
7. Click "Edit Audience segments." Select your [YourStore].com Visitors, 30 Days segment. Disable "Optimized Targeting" (critical; do not skip). Click Next. [L0189.001]

9. Review for errors and click "Publish campaign." [L0189.001]
10. Navigate to the new campaign, go to Audiences, keywords, and content → Audiences. Scroll to Exclusions. Add the [YourStore].com Purchase, 30 Days exclusion. Save changes. [L0189.001]

### Static vs. Dynamic Setup

- Static and dynamic remarketing differ only at campaign creation. For dynamic remarketing, link the product feed at the first setup step. For static remarketing, skip the option to link the product feed. [L0836.064] (2024)
- A static Google Display Network banner for a specific offer can be set up as a Repeat audience campaign aimed at product viewers. Use a Shop Now call to action, and show the same deal stated in the banner as in the matching Facebook ad. [L0799.023] (Source B, 2021-22, older)

### Bidding Choice

- Google Display, as set up in the procedure, uses Bidding focus "Viewable impressions" with a Viewable CPM of $10. [L0189.001]
- In the early stage, the goal of remarketing is reach, not conversions. Do not optimize remarketing campaigns for add-to-cart events. Focus on getting ads in front of people, especially when starting out. [L0965.047] (2024)
- Conversion bidding often underperforms for retargeting, especially at low volume, because retargeting frequently aims to bring visitors back rather than force an immediate sale. Click-based or traffic-based bidding is the more practical default for these campaigns. [L1342.002] (Source B, 2021-22, older)
- A higher-ticket store may not reach the number of conversions a Facebook conversion campaign needs within its optimization window. In that case, a traffic campaign is the practical choice. Test conversion campaigns once the store has shown sales and steady volume. [L1342.003] (Source B, 2021-22, older)
- How the sources fit together: the Viewable CPM setup applies to Google Display. The click/traffic default and the conversion-later rule (Source B) describe Facebook campaigns, where the store's volume decides between traffic and conversion bidding. [L0189.001, L1342.002, L1342.003]

### Budgets per Ad Set and Campaign

- A retargeting ad set can run on a low budget of $10 to $15 per day each. [L0375.028, L0376.029]
- Adjust the remarketing ad budget per campaign rather than with one shared budget. Each window should still reach an acceptable return on ad spend. [L1094.056] (2023)
- On Google, set the Daily budget at about 10% of the Shopping Ads budget (example: $10/day Shopping = $2/day Remarketing). [L0189.001]

### Meta Learning Phase and Changes

Procedure (Source A, 2020, older):

1. When the campaign launches, Facebook shows a random sample of the audience to set costs. [L0268.038]
2. Once costs stabilize at the desired level, don't pause the campaign, because that resets the learning phase. [L0268.038]
3. If you modify a campaign, pause the ad set (not the campaign), unpause it after 5 days, let it run 1 day, and pause again if needed. [L0268.038]
4. Budget changes over 20% also reset the learning phase. Make large budget changes in small increments. [L0268.038]
5. If the campaign is paused for 5-7 days and restarted, the learning phase resets and costs will likely increase. [L0268.038]

Pausing strategy (merged with step 3 and step 5 above; both ids kept):

- When an established campaign needs modification, pause the ad set, not the campaign. Unpause within 5-7 days if needed, which preserves learning phase costs. If the campaign itself is paused for 5-7 days and restarted, the learning phase resets and costs likely increase. [L0268.080, L0268.038] (2020, older)

### Performance Max Caveats

- Performance Max is not recommended for remarketing. It can work for a few months, but return on ad spend drops once remarketing audiences saturate. [L0837.053] (2025)
- For remarketing and monthly promotions to existing audiences, use standard display campaigns instead. [L0964.127] (2023)

## Search Retargeting (RLSA) and Branded Search

### Search Retargeting (RLSA) Overview

- Retargeting can be combined with search or Shopping campaigns, so text ads or Shopping ads run only for people who previously visited your website. This allows different ads or offers for returning visitors than for cold traffic. [L0720.019]
- Google retargeting through a Performance Max campaign plus a branded search campaign caught people who saw a Meta ad and then searched for the product name. [L0646.056]
- This Performance Max and branded search combination was described as the main retargeting channel once Facebook retargeting stopped working well for the account. [L0646.056]

### Why Returning Searchers Convert Better

- Google search retargeting is very high converting because returning visitors who search again are comparison shopping and close to a purchase. Serving them special ads at this point captures them when they are most ready to buy. [L1203.015]
- People who visited the site earlier and are now searching again have higher relevance than cold searchers, even if the earlier visit was months ago. Their earlier research or saved budget for a high-ticket item makes them worth a stronger bid. [L1242.006]
- The audience should therefore not be split by how recent the visit was. [L1242.006]
- Multi-session website visitors were reported to convert 2.17 times more often than other visitors. This is the basis for giving this audience higher bids and a wider reach. [L1242.007]

### Results Compared with Product Listing Ads

| Campaign (same account) | ROAS |
|---|---|
| Search retargeting | 9.4x |
| Product listing ads | 3.5x |

[L1203.019]

- In that account, search retargeting was the highest-converting campaign, which is why it is treated as a priority. [L1203.019]

### Audience Settings

- Set the membership duration of the website visitor audience used for search retargeting to the maximum of 540 days. This is the longest period Google keeps the cookie. [L1242.001]
- Shorter windows discard earlier visitors who are still searching. [L1242.001]

### Setup Steps

1. Find the AdWords retargeting code in the back end of the Google Ads account. [L1203.020]
2. Place that code on every page of the website. [L1203.020]
3. Create a retargeting list of site visitors. [L1203.020]
4. In Google Ads Editor, copy the existing search campaign. [L1203.020]
5. Add the retargeting audience to the copied campaign and upload the change. [L1203.020]
6. Expand the keyword list, including broad match, because visitors are already in market. Coupon keywords are only worth targeting here if the store actually offers coupons. [L1203.020]
7. Rewrite the ad copy for returning visitors, for example with a returning-visitor offer. [L1203.020]
8. Apply the same retargeting audience to the product listing ads. [L1203.020]

## Retargeting Ad Types and Creative

### Retargeting ad types and networks

- Every retargeting ad fits one of four basic types: static image ads (a fixed image, optionally with text on it, and no separate ad copy), responsive ads (fixed images plus separate ad copy variants that the network mixes and tests automatically), static image and text ads (fixed image plus separate ad copy, from a headline and call to action to long body copy), and video ads (a video alone, as on YouTube with optional overlays, or a video with separate ad copy, mainly on Facebook). The type determines which assets you must prepare. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1238.001]
- Each type runs mainly on specific networks: static image ads mostly on the Google Display Network and smaller display networks; responsive ads mostly on the Google Display Network; static image and text ads mainly on Facebook, Taboola and Twitter; video ads on YouTube (video alone, with optional overlays) or Facebook (video with separate ad copy). (older advice, 2021-22) [L1238.014]
- Choose the ad type first, then prepare the assets that type needs. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1238.014]
- Static image ads are the simpler format to set up for retargeting on the Google Display Network and Facebook. The same setup logic carries over to most other ad networks. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1342.001]

### Image sizes and asset preparation

- Image-based retargeting ads need good-quality images. Dynamic product ads are excluded from this work because they reuse existing product ads from the store. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1238.002]
- When running across several networks, the same ad must be recreated quickly in many sizes, so a fast resizing tool is needed. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1238.002]
- The Google Display Network offers about 20 different image and ad sizes for static retargeting ads. Produce only the most-used sizes with the highest inventory and performance first. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1238.015]
- Facebook retargeting ads of every type use a single image size of 1200 x 628 pixels, so one resized image set covers all Facebook placements in retargeting. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1238.016]

### Dynamic and site-wide static ads

- Use dynamic remarketing for product-specific ads on Google display and Meta, because it shows the actual products the visitor viewed. [L0470.065]
- Use static site-wide ads only for promotions that cover the whole store. [L0470.065]
- Keep dynamic product ads in the retargeting plan, but pair them with other ad types that approach the visitor from different angles. Use these combinations to address the specific reason each visitor has not bought. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0563.017]

### Product reminder ads

- A product reminder ad targets people who already know a specific product and reminds them of it. It is meant for audiences that have already seen the product, not for cold traffic. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1169.001]
- Build the product reminder audience from visitors who viewed a specific product page, and show the ad 1 to 7 days after that view. The goal is to keep the product top of mind while the shopper decides. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1169.002]
- Use product reminder ads only for high-volume products such as best sellers, because the audience has to be big enough to fill.
  - If the store has hundreds or thousands of products, do not build this for every product.
  - If the store has only a handful of products, it can be used for all of them. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1169.004]

### Trusted advisor ads

- Trusted advisor ads show social proof to people in retargeting audiences who visited the site without buying. They address doubt about whether the product lives up to its promises or whether the business can be trusted. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1398.001]
- Audiences and windows for the trusted advisor ad:

| Audience | Window |
|---|---|
| Viewers of specific product pages (generally high-volume product pages) | 7 to 14 days |
| Viewers of specific content pages | 7 to 14 days |
| Viewers of any product page | 7 to 14 days |

  (older advice, 2021-22) [L1398.014]
- Run several variations of the trusted advisor ad with different reviews, so the audience does not see the same ad repeatedly. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1398.016]

### Introduction ads

- An introduction ad is a brand ad that shows the business owner's face and invites contact. It works best in retargeting at roughly 14 to 30 days after the first contact with the visitor, and not as the first retargeting ad a person sees. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0488.001]
- It can be shown to anyone who interacted with the business, for example people who viewed product or all-visitor sequences. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0488.001]

### Informant ads

- The content of an informant ad must be genuinely valuable. It may link to products inside the content or mention them without a hard sell. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0415.011]
- Products should come up naturally in the content rather than dominate it. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0415.011]

### Promotion ads

- Retargeting image offers can push a viewer who already saw a video to buy. Examples: 50% off with an end-today deadline, a free gift, or a testimonial. [L1476.049]
- Run these offers in separate image campaigns in the same ad account. [L1476.049]
- Video ads bring in colder customers, similar to a sales letter. Image ads in a separate campaign for the same product can act as retargeting for people who watched a video but did not buy. [L1476.048]
- Pattern: a caption that leads with a discount and a coupon code for a specific percentage off, then two real customer review images (for example, the product in hand or worn). The offer addresses price doubts and the reviews address trust doubts in one ad. [L0648.056]
  - Example: Still thinking about it? Use code SAVE15 for 15 percent off today, plus two photos of customers with the product.
  - Example: Your cart is waiting. Take an extra 10 percent off with CART10, with two real reviews from buyers in your region.

### Testimonial and proof videos

- Testimonial and proof videos perform especially well in retargeting, because warm visitors who saw the brand for the first time need trust, not just an offer. [L0398.018]
- Trust is the bigger barrier for people who do not know the store, so show real reviews and people using the product. [L0398.018]
- Classic dropshipping-style videos also perform well in retargeting, even though they convert less in cold traffic. Use them as retargeting creative alongside UGC and testimonial videos. [L0398.019]
- Static trust ads matter because visitors leave for reasons other than lack of interest, such as needing to check their card, asking someone, or not knowing the brand. Showing videos, testimonials and product photos helps them come back. [L1076.065]

### Creative that works

- Retargeting should be subtle. Use each ad to deliver value, state product benefits and offer the next step in the sales funnel without mentioning past browsing. Visitors connect the dots themselves, and the ads stay effective. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0563.013]
- Using one retargeting message for all audiences fails, because visitors have different pain points: trust, not understanding the product's value, not understanding why the business is better than competitors, and doubting that the product fits their problem. One ad cannot address all of these, so the same person should see different ads across the retargeting window. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0563.033]
- Procedure for building the message set (older advice, 2021-22) [L0563.034]:
  1. Create several ad creatives, messages, styles and angles for retargeting. [L0563.034]
  2. Address each likely pain point with its own ad, such as trust, product value, competitor comparison or product fit. [L0563.034]
  3. Match each ad as closely as possible to the pages the visitor previously viewed on your site. [L0563.034]
  4. Test different combinations and keep the ones that perform best. [L0563.034]
- Customer research makes retargeting ads more personal and relevant. Retargeting ads should hit the points customers need to hear before they buy. [L0872.009]
- A first ad must contain the full case, instead of depending on a later reminder. Readers who see an ad once may never see it again, and a repeat exposure does not replace a complete message. [L1261.120]

### Copy rules

- The goal of retargeting ad copy is to get the viewer to click through to the website, not to make the sale. The conversion happens on the website, where the selling takes place. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1238.008]
- On some placements, such as many Google Display image-only ads, no ad copy is shown, so the product image alone must do the persuading. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0563.015]
- On Facebook, ad copy always appears, so weak copy hurts dynamic product ads there. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0563.015]

### Recycling best offers

- Identify your best-performing promotional offers and ad creatives from past campaigns. Then systematically recycle and repurpose them across different remarketing audience segments: abandoned carts, one-time buyers, inactive visitors and email subscribers. This maximizes ROI while reducing the time and cost of creating new creative assets. [L0240.025]

## Ad Sequencing, Timing and Frequency

### Choosing a Sequencing Approach

- Ad sequencing decides which retargeting ad type a visitor sees and when. It has a large effect on results, so map it out before building retargeting campaigns. There are two approaches: step-by-step sequencing, where one ad is shown at a time, and all-in-one sequencing, where several ad types run at once. [L1241.001]
- (Older, 2021-22) Step-by-step sequencing works like a retargeting funnel. Each step addresses a different sticking point, problem or angle to re-engage the visitor and keep the brand top of mind until they are ready to buy. The order can and should be tested. [L1241.002]
- (Older, 2021-22) Step-by-step sequencing needs large audience volumes, because audiences are built in 7-day blocks. A 7-day Google Ads retargeting audience needs about 100 people, while a 7-day Facebook retargeting audience needs about 1,000 people. Step-by-step is therefore best for best-selling, high-traffic products. New stores may only reach the thresholds with the all-product-viewers audience. [L1241.003]
- (Older, 2021-22) Plan retargeting sequences for specific products and content before building ads, so that each ad has a clear purpose. Planning first also makes it faster to hand the creation of ads or emails to outsourced workers. [L0493.007]
- (Older, 2021-22) Focus retargeting sequences on what the customer might need to know or be convinced of before completing a sale. Practise building different retargeting ads to find which work best. [L0493.008]

### Step-by-Step Sequencing

**Product viewer schedule**

- (Older, 2021-22) Each ad links back to the site. [L1241.004]

| Days since product view | Ad type |
|---|---|
| 1-7 | Dynamic Retargeting Ad |
| 8-14 | Trusted Advisor Ad |
| 14-21 | Introduction Ad |
| 21-30 | Irresistible Deal Ad (special offer to re-engage; can be dynamic or static) |
| 30+ | The Informant Ad (valuable content piece with broad appeal to product viewers) |

[L1241.004]

- If the visitor buys at any point, the sequence stops. Continuing the sequence after the Informant Ad can restart the cycle through the content viewer sequence once the visitor clicks that content. [L1241.004]
- (Older, 2021-22) Sequence timeframes are flexible and can be changed or shortened. Examples given are 1-14 days, 14-30 days and 30-44 days. The timeframes shown are a proven example, not a fixed rule, so they should be tested. [L1241.007]

**Technical setup with audience exclusions**

- (Older, 2021-22) Use this setup for step-by-step sequencing:

1) Create audiences of all product viewers for the windows 1-7 days, 1-14 days, 1-21 days, 1-30 days and 30-90 days (or up to 90 days).
2) When setting up the ad for a window, exclude the audiences of all earlier windows. Example: to reach people in the 14-21 day range, exclude the 14-day audience and the 7-day audience.
3) Result: the ad only reaches people who fall inside that window, not the ones who already entered an earlier stage.

[L1241.006]

### All-in-One Sequencing

**When to use it**

- (Older, 2021-22) All-in-one sequencing runs different retargeting ad types at the same time, each in its own campaign or ad group, so the visitor sees them in no fixed order. It works best for smaller volumes, such as low-traffic products that cannot fill 7-day audiences above the threshold, which is often most of a catalogue. In that case the audience is typically the old product viewers bucket, excluding people who viewed high-volume products. [L1241.008]

**Windows**

- (Older, 2021-22) All ad types run concurrently in these windows:

| Ad type | Window |
|---|---|
| Dynamic Retargeting Ad | 1-30 days |
| Trusted Advisor Ad | 1-30 days |
| Introduction Ad | 1-30 days |
| The Informant Ad | 1-30 days |
| Irresistible Deal Ad | 30+ days |

[L1241.009]

- (Older, 2021-22) End the all-in-one sequence with either an Informant Ad or an Irresistible Deal Ad to get people across the line or back into the sequence. Do not run all-in-one sequencing for more than 30 days. [L1241.009]
- (Older, 2021-22) In all-in-one sequencing the order of ads is random, so a visitor may see the Introduction Ad before the Dynamic Retargeting Ad. This is less effective than a set order, but still far better than showing one identical ad for 30 days or more, because every ad type has value for someone who has already visited the site. [L1241.010]

**Frequency under all-in-one**

- (Older, 2021-22) When running several retargeting ad types at the same time, set the frequency very low in each campaign or ad set to avoid ad overload. Each individual ad type should show no more than once per day per user, and possibly only a couple of times per week. Because the ads may run on two, three or even four networks at the same time, the cap must hold across all networks. [L1241.011]
- This stricter limit applies to all-in-one only. For a single retargeting campaign the general cap is 3 or fewer per person per day. [L1241.011, L0563.003]

### Schedules by Days Since View

**Product viewers**

- (Older, 2021-22) Product-viewer retargeting, such as dynamic product ads on Google or Facebook, is useful for about seven days after the view. Beyond that the ad has passed its usefulness and should stop running. [L0719.033]
- (Older, 2021-22) For specific product viewers, show a product reminder ad and social proof first, then show relevant content in the 14 to 21 day window, or up to 30 days, in case they need more information before buying. [L0415.015]
- (Older, 2021-22) Use the following windows, audiences and content for each group. [L0415.020]

| Audience | Timing window | Ad to show | Content to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specific content viewers | 1 to 7 days after viewing another piece of content | Informant ad | Follow-up content on a more specific problem that leads to products |
| Product viewers who did not buy | 30+ days after viewing a product, after all other ads have run | Informant ad for re-engagement | Content that approaches the topic from a different angle than what they saw before |
| Specific product viewers | 14 to 21 days, or up to 30 days | Product reminder ad and social proof first, then informant ad | Content relevant to the exact product the viewer looked at |

[L0415.020]

- (Older, 2021-22) For specific content viewers, show the informant ad to people 1 to 7 days after they viewed another piece of content. [L0415.013]
- (Older, 2021-22) Show trusted advisor ads to people who saw a product reminder ad and still have not bought. Start them after the reminder ad, running for 7 to 14 days. [L1398.015]
- (Older, 2021-22) Use this 30-day stop rule for most audiences: stop retargeting after the first 30 days, because the chance of a sale after that is very low. Customer lists are the exception. [L1390.024]
- Note: the table above shows an Informant ad from 30+ days for product viewers who did not buy, after all other ads have run. This applies only once the 30-day stop rule is set aside for that re-engagement step. Test this before running it for long periods. [L0415.020, L1390.024]

**Product-page review sequence**

- (Older, 2021-22) For a large catalog without product-specific audiences, show dynamic product ads for the first 7 days so the shopper sees the products they viewed. After that, switch to a business-wide trusted advisor ad that features reviews of the store. [L1398.017]
- (Older, 2021-22) Do not run dynamic product ads for 30 days in a retargeting audience. Limit them to about the first 7 days, then move the audience to a different ad. [L1398.018]
- (Older, 2021-22) Use this sequence for product-page retargeting with review ads:

1) Show a product reminder ad with the product image to viewers of high-volume product pages who have not bought.
2) If they still have not bought after the reminder ad, show trusted advisor ads with reviews for 7 to 14 days, rotating several review variations.
3) For a large catalog without product-specific audiences, show dynamic product ads for about the first 7 days so the shopper sees the products they viewed, then switch to a business-wide trusted advisor ad that features reviews of the store.

[L1398.019]

- (Older, 2021-22) Split product viewers into groups by time since the view, assign a different ad to each group, and refresh the messages so repeated viewers do not see the same ad. Use the following procedure:

1) Split product viewers into groups by time since the view: 7, 14, 21 and 30 days.
2) Assign a different ad to each group so visitors see a series of ads from different angles over a month.
3) Use static ads for trust and review messages, and dynamic product ads for the shortest window.
4) Refresh the messages in each group so repeated viewers do not see the same ad.

[L0719.036]

### Offers and Messages by Timing

- Front-load the budget and the strongest offers into the first 7 days after a visit, when interest is highest. Spend less and change the message for the older windows so that the audience stays engaged without being overexposed. [L1076.062]
- During the first 7 days after a visit, show dynamic product ads that bring the visitor back to the product they viewed, plus static ads with videos, testimonials and real product photos to build trust. In later windows, show promotions and content and run the same campaigns that are sent by email. [L1076.064]
- Retarget visitors with different entry offers based on how they behave on the site. Keep testing and improving these offers, because the optimisation never really ends. [L0334.017]
- For people who already know the brand, restart the story with fresh angles, social proof and an offer such as a bundle discount. These viewers may be on the fence and need a new reason to buy. [L0524.053]
- Retargeting needs new creatives, because the audience has already seen the top-of-funnel ads and will get irritated by repetition. Use offers such as an extra discount compared with the new visitor and real customer reviews to remove doubts. [L0648.055]

### Matching the Ad to the Objection

- Match the ad type to the visitor's position and objection in the funnel. Visitors near a purchase can receive dynamic product ads, visitors who distrust the business need reviews and the unique selling proposition, and undecided visitors need helpful related content or alternative product suggestions that fit their problem. [L0563.016]
- (Older, 2021-22) Match the retargeting ad to the reason a visitor did not buy. A trust barrier calls for a static ad with reviews, a bestseller can get an ad built on its own reviews, and an undecided visitor can be sent to a content piece such as a buying guide. [L0719.035]
- (Older, 2021-22) Rotating different ads lets you address different objections and pain points, because each person may be held back by a different reason. It also prevents the same ad from being pushed at one person continually. [L0563.007]
- (Older, 2021-22) Do not show the same retargeting ad to everyone. Split retargeting by audience stage and by the page or action the visitor took, with different messages for each group. Showing every visitor the same dynamic product ads for 30 days, with a discount at the end, ignores prospects who do not yet trust the brand or still have a problem they have not defined. [L0414.050]

### Frequency Caps

- (Older, 2021-22) Frequency means how many times one person in an audience sees your ad within a set period, usually per day, though it can also be measured per week or per month. [L0563.001]
- (Older, 2021-22) Almost every retargeting platform lets you set a frequency cap at the campaign or ad group level. [L0563.002]
- (Older, 2021-22) Set the frequency cap for retargeting campaigns to 3 or fewer impressions per person per day. Campaigns running on a single network can sometimes be set to 2 or 1 per day, depending on how many ads run. [L0563.003]
- (Older, 2021-22) Control ad frequency so the ads do not annoy the viewer. Repetition should build curiosity, not irritation. [L0415.010]
- (Older, 2021-22) Use this procedure to set frequency and rotation:

1. Set the campaign or ad group frequency cap to 3 or fewer impressions per person per day.
2. Change ad types, images and copy regularly; this does not have to be done manually.
3. Split a long retargeting window into blocks. Example: for a 30-day window, show ad A for days 1 to 10, ad B for days 11 to 20, and ad C for days 21 to 30.

[L0563.006]

- Google allows audiences to retarget the same person for up to 540 days. That setting is extreme, and showing the same ad to a non-buyer for a long period has a negative effect. [L0563.005]

### Cross-Network Impression Math

- (Older, 2021-22) When retargeting runs on several networks at once, the frequency cap must be lowered because the impressions add up across all networks. Lower the cap further if many retargeting campaigns run in parallel. [L0563.008]
- (Older, 2021-22) Use this formula for the maximum daily impressions per person:

  Maximum daily impressions per person = number of retargeting networks x frequency cap per network

  Example: 4 networks x 3 = 12 impressions per person per day.

  [L0563.009]

- (Older, 2021-22) The more often someone sees an ad, the less likely they are to click it, which lowers click-through rate. On Google and Facebook a low click-through rate reduces relevance and quality scores, which weakens auction performance and can raise costs. [L0563.010]

### Creative Rotation

- (Older, 2021-22) Use the same image across all placements so the viewer repeatedly sees the same ad in different places. Repeated exposure raises curiosity, so people who did not click at first often click later. [L0415.009]
- Within a window, change the images and copy across the sequence, as the procedure above describes: rotate ad blocks across the window and change ad types, images and copy regularly. The same image is used across placements for one ad, while different ads carry different images from one block to the next. [L0563.006, L0415.009]
- Rotate at least three different remarketing ads during a promotion so the same audience does not see one message repeatedly. [L1115.090]
- (Older, 2021-22) Refresh the messages in each group so repeated viewers do not see the same ad. [L0719.036]

### Avoiding Repeated Ads and Creepy Copy

- (Older, 2021-22) Showing retargeting ads too many times per day, or running the same ad to the same person for too long, lowers effectiveness and annoys people. Repeated identical messages feel spammy and creepy. [L0563.004]
- (Older, 2021-22) Showing the same dynamic product ad every day for 30 days annoys people and produces little return. This is why many retargeting campaigns report no results. [L0719.034]
- (Older, 2021-22) Showing the same retargeting ad for 30 days or longer wastes the sequence and loses the chance to re-engage visitors with new angles. Running several ad types at once without lowering the frequency causes ad overload, for example seeing three of each ad per day. [L1241.012]
- (Older, 2021-22) Showing the same dynamic product ads to a visitor for 30 days is a weak retargeting method. Rotate a series of different ads with different messages across the 30-day window. [L0473.011]
- (Older, 2021-22) Ad copy that tells a visitor they were on your site today or yesterday puts people off. Avoid any wording that reveals you know what they did on the site. [L0563.011]
- (Older, 2021-22) Many people do not know that advertisers see only anonymous cookie data, so they may worry that tracking reveals their identity, location or other personal information. Explicit tracking references increase that worry, especially after recent privacy scandals. [L0563.012]

## Content-Based Retargeting

### Why content matters for retargeting

- A store with only product pages has little useful material to show visitors who have already left, so meaningful retargeting is very difficult without content. [L0500.009]
- *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* Visitors who have not bought yet often lack enough information to feel confident. Useful content can help them move past that hesitation and back into the sales funnel. [L0415.001]
- *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* Content pieces published on the store's website can also serve as the basis for retargeting ads that re-engage people who already visited the site. Plan the content so it can be reused in those ads, not only in organic posts. [L0381.017]

### Building the content library

- Develop content continuously and promote it to cold traffic. Use the same content to fill out remarketing plans, so one content library serves both acquisition and retargeting. *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* [L0415.018]
- Not all content must promote products directly. *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* Top-of-funnel content can hook a viewer, and a follow-up piece on a more specific problem can then lead them to products. [L0415.012]
- Base content on customer feedback, such as questions asked by customers or virtual assistants and hesitations they mention. Turn these into emails and Facebook ads sent to the full list, so that every piece of content works toward making buyers feel safer and trust the brand. [L1076.070]

### Matching content to pain points

- Choose the content piece for a retargeting ad by matching its main problem to a pain point the visitor already linked to the products or pages they viewed. *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* [L0415.019]
- The content should answer that problem directly and link naturally to relevant products without a hard sell. *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* [L0415.019]
- Do not show generic content to specific product viewers. The content must be relevant to the exact product audience the viewer belongs to, otherwise the ad does not answer their reason for not buying. *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* [L0415.017]

### Sequencing content before offers

- Do not move people straight from a meme or engagement post to an expensive product offer, because they do not know or trust the brand yet. Show them several pieces of content first, such as recipes, reviews, and videos, before presenting any offer. [L0487.045]

### Content audiences

- Create a separate retargeting audience for each content piece used in these ads. Viewers who click that content enter the new audience and move through a product sales funnel built around the products in that content. *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* [L0415.016]
- A second audience for a product reminder ad is people who read a content page that recommends the product, such as a buyer's guide or a best-of article. Retarget these readers with the ad whether or not they clicked through to the product page. *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* [L1169.005]
- Content-based reminders work because the reader has already received a recommendation. The reminder moves them to the next funnel step, which is looking at the product itself. The ad is also simple to set up and keeps the product in front of the reader. *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* [L1169.006]
- The content-page audience for product reminder ads uses the same 1 to 7 day window after the visit as the product-page audience. Ads are shown only within that window, so the product stays in mind while the reader decides. *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* [L1169.027]
- Visitors who arrive through a content piece can be added to a retargeting audience, so they can later be served retargeting ads that move them into the sales funnel. *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* [L1421.028]

### Social engagement audiences

- People who engage with a Facebook post about a piece of content, such as liking, commenting or clicking, can be added to a retargeting audience. *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* [L0385.029]
- Those people can be shown retargeting ads later in the funnel, so a small content campaign can feed the retargeting pool at low cost. *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* [L0385.029]

### Content viewer sequence

*(Older advice, 2021-22.)*

1. Build the audience only from people who stayed on the content page for a minimum of about 30 seconds, ideally 1 to 2 minutes, since they are the most worth retargeting. Include people who arrive at the content from a retargeting ad, as well as cold visitors. [L1241.005]
2. Days 1 to 7: show a Product Reminder Ad for the product that the content recommends. This is needed because the visitor may have read the content without clicking through to the product page. If they did visit the product page and did not buy, the reminder is still relevant. [L1241.005]
3. After that, run the same sequence as the product viewer sequence for that specific product: Trusted Advisor Ad, Introduction Ad and Irresistible Deal Ad. [L1241.005]
4. After 30 or more days, show an Informant Ad that points to a different piece of content or a different angle on the problem that started the sequence. [L1241.005]

### Timing and re-engagement

- Product viewers who have not bought after all other ads have run for 30 days can be re-engaged with content. *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* [L0415.014]
- Use content that approaches the topic differently from what they saw before. *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* [L0415.014]

### Advertorial retargeting logic

1. Identify visitors who reached the advertorial but did not click through to the offer page. [L1131.025]
2. Show them an ad that educates them further about the product and explains the benefits. [L1131.025]
3. Include a discount such as 40 percent off or 10 dollars off. [L1131.025]
4. Send the ad click directly to the offer page instead of the advertorial. [L1131.025]

### Site-wide and dynamic retargeting with content

- Use a site-wide retargeting pixel to remind visitors of your store, and use article or content retargeting to drive readers of your content back to your website. [L0643.032]
- Dynamic multi-product ads show several products from your store, with the best-converting products at the front. [L0643.032]

### Email capture from readers

- A content visit does not need to produce a sale, but an opt-in form should be present on the content page to collect leads. *(Older advice, 2021-22.)* [L1421.028]

## Promotions, Holidays and Q4 Retargeting

### Offer-based retargeting

#### Two types of deal

- Irresistible deal ads come in two types: offers aimed at one person based on their stage in the customer journey, and offers tied to a holiday or seasonal event such as Black Friday, Christmas, Thanksgiving, Veterans Day or Easter. Event deals can also be niche-specific or global. (older: Source B, 2021-22) [L0799.002]
- For a holiday deal, retarget everyone, including previous customers, with a campaign that runs only for the deal period. Audience position in the funnel does not matter here. (older: Source B, 2021-22) [L0799.016]
- For a specific offer, target product viewers who are near the point of no return. This is generally around 30 days after the product view, so a 14 to 30 day viewer window is the usual target. (older: Source B, 2021-22) [L0799.017]
- If your funnel moves fast, shorten the specific-offer window, for example to 7 days after the product view. High-ticket products usually have longer buyer journeys, so their offers should come later. (older: Source B, 2021-22) [L0799.018]
- Do not offer a discount just because someone viewed a product. A buyer who needs more time may not need a discount to convert, so do not rush the deal and protect profit by timing it to the customer journey. (older: Source B, 2021-22) [L0799.019]

### Deal timing

- Schedule the Christmas promotion in early December. Running it on Christmas Day misses buyers, who have spent their budgets and are not shopping online. [L1224.011]
- Publish holiday content weeks ahead of the event. A holiday blog post published just before the sale is wasted effort, because it will not have time to rank and few people will see it. [L1224.034]
- Run promotion-specific Google and Meta ads only during that promotion's dates, so each holiday has its own creative and a short exposure window. [L1224.024]
- Run retargeting ads from November 24 through the end of Cyber Monday. [L0307.007]
- High-CPM periods are ideal for retargeting, because the warm audience has high conversion intent despite CPM increases. [L0298.050]
- Build the visitor audience early, then spend on remarketing when ad costs rise in Q4. (older: Source A, 2019) [L1110.035]

### Monthly promotions

- Retargeting ads work alongside email to capture additional revenue during holidays and promotions. They are run as a standard part of the promotion plan. [L1097.040]

- Promotion ads are not product-specific like dynamic ads. They push one store-wide deal, bundle or bonus to lapsed contacts. The goal is to re-ignite the reason the shopper searched for the product in the first place. [L1204.005]

## Retargeting Existing Customers and Repeat Purchases

### Why Existing Customers Are Worth Targeting

- Not retargeting existing customers is a major missed opportunity, because selling to an existing customer is much easier than acquiring a new one (older, 2021-22). Relevant upsells and resells should be promoted through ads to past buyers (older, 2021-22). [L0563.027]
- After a first sale, retargeting and repeat email campaigns bring the same customer back for second and third purchases (older, 2021-22). Repeat sales are cheaper to win than new ones, so their profit margins are usually higher (older, 2021-22). [L0771.041]
- Second and third purchases from existing customers usually generate more profit than the first sale, which makes existing-customer retargeting a key driver of customer lifetime value (older, 2021-22). Combine it with email marketing for the best effect (older, 2021-22). [L0563.030]
- Do not assume existing customers will never buy again. Not all of them will, but some will, so test repeat offers rather than dropping them from campaigns (older, 2021-22). [L0563.032]
- People who already use the product have already decided and rarely read acquisition ads, so repeated acquisition ads for current customers waste budget. [L1261.119]

### Building Customer Audiences

- Leaving existing customers out of retargeting means missing repeat sales. Build a separate customer audience and give it its own campaigns (older, 2021-22). [L1390.004]

- Wait until the list reaches the network's minimum size before using it. Google Display needs 100 people (older, 2021-22). [L0563.028]
- Show ads to this audience only when you have a relevant offer or content for these customers (older, 2021-22). [L0563.028]

- Repeat buyers were cited as converting at 60% to 70%, compared with an industry average of 2% to 3% for new customers. Targeting past buyers is therefore presented as the main source of repeat sales. [L1243.036]

**Repeat-purchase funnel procedure:**

1. **Step 1, after delivery:** Have the owner send a short thank-you video through Facebook, then follow up with an email containing a coupon code for the next purchase. Target only first-time buyers by excluding customers with a purchase frequency above one. [L1243.037]
2. **Step 2, 7 to 14 days after delivery:** Run a cross-sell campaign with dynamic product ads. Set up pairings in the product catalog beforehand, such as when a customer buys product X, show product Y. Then send an email that recalls the coupon code from step 1. [L1243.038]

### Cross-Sell and Upgrade Ads

- Set up cross-sell pairings in the product catalog before launch, so that a purchase of product X triggers product Y (older, 2021-22 pairing logic is the same as the current funnel). [L1243.038]
- Example: customers who bought a product a year ago are shown an ad for a newer model, presented as an upgrade to the product they already like (older, 2021-22). Some of them will buy again, so the offer is worth testing (older, 2021-22). [L0563.031]

### Channel Split: Acquisition vs. Retention

- Use paid ads to acquire new customers, and use SMS and email to retarget existing buyers with new products or better offers. [L1431.051]
- Retargeting existing customers avoids paying again to reach them on Facebook. [L1431.051]
- Use retention and reactivation messages separately from the acquisition campaign. [L1261.119]

## Retargeting for Long Buying Cycles and Competitive Niches

### Retargeting for High-Ticket Products with Long Buying Cycles
- High-ticket products usually have longer buying journeys, so follow-up must cover a longer period. Without follow-up for first-time visitors, many will leave and buy elsewhere before they are ready. [L0473.012]
- High-ticket buyers, such as people furnishing a home, may research products up to six months before they buy. Keep paid traffic running in slow periods so these early planners enter your retargeting list and recall your store when they are ready to purchase. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0949.069]
- For some high-ticket products, customers shop months before they intend to buy, so retargeting must keep your brand present through that whole consideration window until they are ready to pay. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1239.010, L1240.010]

### Keeping Paid Traffic Running in Slow Periods
- For high-ticket products with long buying cycles, keep paid traffic running during slow periods. Customers may plan purchases months ahead, and retargeting lists keep your store in front of them until they buy. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0949.047]
- The right level of paid traffic depends on your risk tolerance and long-term view. Some owners cut traffic in slow periods to save money, while others keep it running to build audiences for later. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0949.048]
- Remarketing audiences built during slow economic periods compound in value. Maintaining consistent remarketing metrics during downturns means a higher audience size and message frequency when the economy recovers, which multiplies campaign effectiveness. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0245.038]

### Building Audiences in Slow Periods, Then Activating Them
(Older procedure, 2020.)

1. Collect customer emails through opt-in forms. [L0245.036]
2. Create Google audiences of all website visitors (website visits). [L0245.036]
3. Create Facebook audiences of website visitors and page engagement. [L0245.036]
4. During periods of low purchase intent (economic downturns), build these remarketing lists without aggressive selling. [L0245.036]
5. Once consumer confidence recovers, execute heavy remarketing campaigns to these built audiences via email, Google, and Facebook. [L0245.036]

### Competitor-Aware Retargeting for Resellers
- Resellers who sell other brands' products face competitors selling the same items. Prospects who research in advance see several sellers, and in long consideration periods the seller a prospect saw most during research often wins the sale, not only the seller with the best offer. Retargeting therefore gives a competitive advantage. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1239.009, L1240.009]
- Retargeting brings back visitors who did not buy yet. It is one of the reasons a store wins repeat sales when competitors offer the same MAP price. [L0966.098]

### Retargeting in Ultra-Competitive Niches
- In an ultra-competitive niche, retarget visitors on Google, Facebook, Instagram, Bing and Yahoo with higher bids. Buyers often return days or weeks later, forget which store they researched, and buy from a competitor, so repeated exposure keeps your store in front of them. [L1357.016]

### Private-Label Products
- Remarketing to your own visitors is a main traffic source for newly private-labeled products that Google Shopping does not reach, because shoppers do not search for them. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0968.066]

## Channel Selection and Priorities

### Start with search, then retarget
- Search-based campaigns come before retargeting. Google Search, Google Shopping and Bing are the preferred sources of store visitors because people actively look for the product there. [L1020.005, L1243.001]
- Facebook retargeting starts only after Google Search, Google Shopping and Bing are maxed out. Facebook is a discovery platform, not a search platform, so it is the next channel to add, not the first. [L1243.001]
- Cold Google campaigns target people searching for the product, while remarketing runs on both Google and Meta. This does not change based on whether your audience is more active on Google or Facebook. [L0471.015]
- Retargeting should run across several networks with the same core messages. Using only one channel loses customers whose attention is split across platforms. (2021-22, older) [L0563.023]
- Aim for a subtle, omnipresent presence across relevant networks so visitors meet your ads wherever they go without feeling followed. Reach cold traffic this way, and retarget warm and hot audiences across the same networks so competitors do not dominate attention. (2021-22, older) [L0563.025]
- Scale retargeting to your traffic volume. [L1251.044]

### Channel order
1. Start with Google retargeting, which has the highest priority. Set up dynamic remarketing first, then standard remarketing, then video/YouTube remarketing. Build these audiences before any other retargeting channel. [L0920.013]
2. Add Facebook, Instagram and Bing next, using the same audience logic on each platform rather than separate strategies. [L0920.014]
3. Add the next bottom-of-funnel campaigns: website retargeting on the Google Display Network and retargeting on Shopping. Both target people who have already visited the store. [L0867.004]
4. Keep YouTube low priority until other channels are fully used. (2021, older) [L0247.071, L0962.158]
5. Test TikTok last, and only after the Meta and Google remarketing budgets are fully used. It is not a main revenue source. (2025) [L0837.055]

## Measuring and Optimizing Retargeting Performance

### Core KPIs

- Retargeting ROAS is revenue from the ads divided by ad spend:

```
ROAS = revenue from the ads / ad spend
```

  [L0360.057]
- Facebook Dynamic Remarketing and Google Remarketing should keep a conversion rate of at least 2.5% as a baseline. These are the warmest audiences and should outperform cold traffic. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0272.041]
- Define business measurables for each horizon: customer count, average order value, cost per acquisition or cost per sale, repeat purchase rate, and number of products sold. Choose the measurables that directly drive revenue and profit figures. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1519.004]

### Sample size and attribution caveats

- With fewer than 300-400 qualified people in the audience, results appear random. Larger audiences show clearer conversion patterns. [L0276.057]
- Retargeting audiences from Google and from Facebook or Instagram can be built from site visitors. Include them when calculating the conversion rate for paid traffic. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0963.138]
- Traffic from days earlier can convert later through remarketing and abandoned cart emails. Do not judge a campaign on the last day's orders alone. (Older advice, 2021.) [L1279.070]
- Mobile shoppers often add products to the cart on a phone and finish the purchase later on desktop. Retargeting brings them back, so mobile traffic should not be judged only by immediate sales. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1275.068]
- If retargeting converts worse than cold-traffic campaigns in an account, the result is unusual and should be investigated: check audience size, lookback windows, and tracking errors. [L0867.006]

### Testing retargeting against cold audiences

- Retargeting is often the cheapest audience, but this does not always hold. One advertiser found that a retargeting audience cost 10 to 20 percent more than a cold audience of similar businesses in a similar niche that competitors had not yet targeted. Test cold audiences against retargeting audiences rather than assuming. [L0643.031]
- Both results apply at once. Warm audiences should outperform cold traffic on conversion rate [L0272.041], but cost per result must be tested, because retargeting can cost more than a cold audience [L0643.031].

### Diagnosing weak results

| Signal | First check | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Retargeting underperforms | How qualified the cold traffic is, and what the copy in the retargeting ads says (older advice, 2020) | [L1277.092] |
| High click-through rate, zero conversions | Traffic quality is usually fine. The bottleneck is the store, typically at product page level. Fix the conversion side before changing the audience. | [L1083.011] |
| Retargeting converts worse than cold traffic | Audience size, lookback windows, tracking errors | [L0867.006] |

- The quality of the remarketing audience depends entirely on the quality of the traffic that creates it. The best remarketers are people who arrived through well-optimized cold traffic campaigns. [L0276.009]
- Better cold traffic, such as traffic from Google Shopping, improves warm and hot retargeting results. Retargeting only works with the quality of the first visits that feed it. [L1083.013]
- Keep Meta ads running for remarketing at a small budget even when they have not yet produced conversions. Remarketing only performs well when the initial traffic was qualified, so weak traffic from Google also weakens remarketing. [L0837.054]

#### Learning from non-buyers

1. Create a custom audience of visitors who did not buy.
2. Retarget them with a short video in which you ask why they did not buy and what would change their mind.
3. Ask them to comment with the reason.
4. Use the answers to improve the product, price or page, so more visitors convert.

[L0643.034]

### Reviewing and improving ads on a schedule

- Track remarketing ROAS and units sold as the retargeting KPIs, because more organic visitors grow the remarketing list. Compare them with the earlier month to see whether the growth is working. [L0941.080]
- Review results across accumulated days rather than the last day's orders, since delayed conversions from earlier traffic also count. [L1279.070, L0941.080]

### Scaling budgets

1. Check that the audience is large enough for the result to mean something. Below 300-400 qualified people, results appear random. [L0276.057]
2. Check the return. A retargeting campaign should reach a ROAS of at least 10 (10x) before its budget is increased. [L0360.057]
3. Keep the Meta remarketing budget small until conversions appear, and only increase it once the campaign has passed the ROAS threshold. [L0837.054, L0360.057]

## Common Mistakes and Operating Rules

### Strategy Principles

Three principles drive remarketing success:

1. Stay consistently visible so customers do not forget you. [L0240.021]
2. Directly address the specific objections or concerns that prevented their initial purchase. [L0240.021]
3. Deeply understand what your target customer wants and why. [L0240.021]

### Product Ad Mix

- Running only dynamic product ads is a common mistake. They fit some visitors but not all, and repeatedly showing a product someone already rejected for 14 to 30 days rarely changes their decision. [L0563.014]

### Ad Angles and Segmentation

- Retargeting works best when ad angles are varied and audiences are segmented by the pages visited and the actions taken on the site. Each ad type can expose the customer to different aspects of the business and its products. [L1239.014]
- Retargeting ads work best when they use different angles and communicate different things about the business and its products. They are a chance to educate customers, not only to bring them back to the site. [L1240.015]

### Repetition and Message Fatigue

- Showing the same retargeting ad again and again over weeks, for example a full month, is one of the biggest mistakes and a common error. Once a customer has seen an ad a few times, repeating it adds little value, so change the angle or message instead. [L1239.013, L1240.014]

### Exposure Limits

- Retargeting visitors for too long and too often wastes budget and annoys people. Limit how long and how frequently each audience sees ads. [L1390.002]

### Operating Rules

1. Do not run only dynamic product ads; combine them with other ad types, because each type fits different visitors. [L0563.014, L1239.014]
2. Segment audiences by the pages visited and the actions taken on the site. [L1239.014]
3. Vary the angle, and after a customer has seen an ad a few times, change the message instead of repeating it. [L1239.013, L1240.014]
4. Use each ad to educate the customer, not only to bring them back to the site. [L1240.015]
5. Limit how long and how frequently each audience sees ads. [L1390.002]
6. Stay visible, address the specific objection that blocked the first purchase, and understand what the customer wants and why. [L0240.021]

### Traffic Sources and Affiliate Offers
