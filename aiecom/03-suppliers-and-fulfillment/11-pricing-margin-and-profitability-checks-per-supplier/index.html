3.11 Pricing, margin and profitability checks per supplier

Cost inputs for the margin calculation

  • Cost of goods sold (COGS) is only accurate when it is entered per product in Shopify. COGS equals the cost to deliver the product to your 3PL plus the average shipping cost per unit to the customer. [L0222.006]
  • Packaging and supplies cover only the materials used to fulfil an order: the box, tape, polymailer and the cost of the shipping label itself. Postage paid to the carrier is entered separately as shipping cost. [L0433.013]
  • Shipping cost is the average postage paid per order for a typical order size, for example $9.17. Take it from actual carrier costs, not from packaging. [L0433.014]
  • If a 3PL charges per pick, an order with several items incurs one pick fee per item, so the 3PL cost per order must be scaled to the average number of picks. Typical 3PL pick and pack fees run about $1.50 to $2.50 per pick. If no 3PL is used, this input is $0. [L0433.015]
  • Keep COGS as low as possible, but never at the expense of the quality customers notice. Cutting costs that buyers feel will hurt repeat sales. [L0378.016]
  • Customisation and rush production options from the supplier carry their own costs. Add a markup to them, and ask suppliers which custom or rush options they offer before building them into your pricing. [L0920.023]
  • A supplier's percentage margin must be checked for its basis. Ask whether it is a net margin left after the product cost or a markup on that cost. The two give very different selling prices, so confirm which one applies before committing. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0551.076]

Worked cost examples

  • Ceramic flower pot: sold at $30 (about $29.90) with an $11 COGS including fulfilment costs. [L0642.044]
  • Zendrop: one unit sourced at $20 with $10 shipping to the customer gives a total of $30 entered as COGS + SHIPPING per unit. [L0987.023]
  • CJ Dropshipping: a cost of $17 per item on CJ was entered into profit calculators to check margin. [L0440.018]

CJ Dropshipping price basis

  • On CJ Dropshipping, after you choose the variant and the target country, the product page shows a field called total dropshipping price. It already includes the product cost and shipping to the customer, so use it as the base cost for pricing instead of the item price alone. [L0436.024]

Confirming cost before pricing

  1. Estimate the product cost from AliExpress listicles by checking average prices.
  2. Open a sourcing request at CJ Dropshipping to get accurate supplier prices.
  3. Use the sourcing result as the real cost basis for pricing.

[L0693.019]

  • AliExpress prices are only estimates, because sellers without fulfilment can set any price. Use a sourcing request to confirm the cost. [L0693.021]

Shipping outside the MAP

  • Shipping costs are typically not included in a supplier's MAP price, because shipping varies by destination. If a supplier does not explicitly state that shipping is included in its pricing, assume it is not. [L0276.012]

  • Estimating shipping and margin (older advice, 2020):

    1. Find the supplier's location and zip code on its website.
    2. Note the product's size and weight.
    3. Get a freight estimate from freightbroker.com between the supplier's zip code and two customer zip codes in the states you plan to sell in, for example one in Florida and one in California.
    4. Average the two estimates to get a shipping cost.
    5. Subtract wholesale cost and shipping from the selling price to see the margin left for ads and fees.

    [L0961.036]

  • Some suppliers based in Japan charge an average of about $15 shipping per product, which can remove most of the margin on lower-priced items. Add this shipping cost into the spread calculation before listing their products. [L0965.086]

  • If a supplier adds tax to its wholesale prices, compare competitor prices with that tax included and recalculate the MAP-to-wholesale spread. Keep the products only if the spread still works. [L0965.027]

Retail price basis

  • Google Shopping is used for competitor price research: search your product names as a customer, then compare sellers' prices to your wholesale cost. Use store averages, not the lowest prices. [L1051.004]
  • Extremely low competitor prices are often a warning sign rather than a bargain. Stores selling with almost no margin often have poor customer service, late or missing shipments and short business lifespans. Check company names, websites, reviews and ratings before treating their price as market price. [L1051.005]

Net profit and ad-spend thresholds

Worked profit example at 2x (older advice, 2020)

  • Wholesale $800, sale price $1,000: gross profit $200. Minus 2.9% fees ($29) gives $171. Minus 10% customer acquisition cost ($100) gives $71 profit. Subtracting shipping and operational overhead leaves minimal or negative margin.
  • Solution: price at $1,600 instead, and drive only organic and remarketing traffic, not paid ads.

[L0268.010, L0268.011]

  • Transaction fees (2.9%) and customer acquisition (up to 10% of sale) are the costs that a 2x MAP has to cover. [L0268.010]

Dollar profit matters as much as percentage

  • A wholesale price of $161 against a MAP of $230 leaves too little dollar profit per sale to justify advertising. Accepting a lower spread is only reasonable for very high-ticket products worth thousands of dollars, where gross profit per unit is still large. [L0835.020]
  • A lower spread is acceptable only when the dollar profit per sale still makes the sale worthwhile. [L0835.021]
  • Established industry suppliers offered about $125 profit on a $1,000 product and about $200 on a $4,000 product, which can be too thin for paid ads. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0671.002]
  • Do not sell products at very low margins, such as 5% or a flat $50 per sale. This is especially true for suppliers without a price policy, where other retailers race to the bottom. These deals create more problems than sales. [L0835.023]
  • Products sold below 25% margin including shipping should not be added to the store. Keep the supplier on file for later. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1046.031]

Product-level margin checks

  • Product-only margin in the example was roughly 20% once outbound shipping was removed, showing that shipping was the main reason the margin was low. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1046.030]
  • Outbound shipping in the example cost about $98,000 to $100,000 per year, roughly 6.3% of total income. It is one of the largest levers for improving margin. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1046.028]
  • Check each product's margin including shipping before listing it. Add only products with at least 25% margin including shipping. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1046.031, L1046.032]
  • Small, lightweight products have low fulfilment costs because they ship in small packages. This leaves room for a good selling price and margin, so use it when choosing test products. [L0562.018]
  • Outbound shipping cost in one case was about $98,000 per year; the owner planned to bring it to about $75,000, a saving of about $23,000 per year, while keeping the rest of the cost structure. This was the third of three supplier priorities. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1046.036]

Margin review steps (older advice, 2021-22)

  1. Contact the supplier and build the relationship before asking for terms.
  2. Request the supplier's current pricing sheet.
  3. Calculate the margin for each product, including shipping.
  4. Add only products with at least 25 percent margin including shipping.
  5. Keep low-margin suppliers on file and note any individual high-margin products they offer.
  6. Keep searching for higher-margin suppliers as the first priority.

[L1046.032]

  • Products from suppliers with weak sales can stay listed in free Google Shopping listings if they still show margin, so they earn traffic without ad spend. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0967.041]

Evaluating a supplier before listing

Supplier margin is set per supplier

  • Margins are supplier-specific, not niche-specific. No actual margin is known before approval, because pricing tiers and credit terms differ by supplier. One supplier may offer 50% margin while another in the same niche offers only 25-30%. [L0274.003]
  • The profit margin is set by each supplier, not by the niche. Two suppliers in the same niche can offer very different margins, so judge every supplier on its own price list before deciding what to sell. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0671.001, L0671.157]
  • Supplier margins are negotiated per supplier, not per niche. A low 20-30% margin from one supplier does not mean the niche is wrong. Keep adding approved suppliers in the niche. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0429.040]
  • Margin between MAP and wholesale varies by supplier, not by niche. Calculate each supplier's margin after approval rather than assuming an industry margin. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0854.022]
  • Supplier-specific prices, MAP policies and margins are unknown until approval. Use category averages for planning and confirm the real margins after approval. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0469.031]
  • Wholesale cost is not known until you are approved, but you can still plan around it. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0854.022]
  • A supplier's price list is the fastest way to confirm true profit per supplier. Margins can often only be confirmed after approval, but they can be calculated as soon as the price list is received. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0967.024]
  • Approved suppliers typically send an Excel sheet with three columns, or three separate price lists: MSRP, MAP and wholesale. Use these to estimate margins before listing products. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0663.053]

Checking a supplier with no MAP policy

  1. Get the supplier's price list.
  2. Search Google Shopping for several of its products.
  3. Check whether anyone already advertises those products and at what markup.
  4. If competitors mark them up only 10% or 15%, do not list them; this is a no-go.
  5. If there is little competition, become the main seller for those products; if competitors sell them at healthy margins, list them too.

Judging a supplier's position and terms

  • Suppliers that sell direct at MSRP and let retailers sell at MAP are good partners. Avoid brands that sell at MAP themselves or below MAP, because this removes your price advantage. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0961.030]
  • If a brand sells direct at MAP, look for a unique value proposition (UVP), such as better presentation, trust or customer experience, that sets your store apart. Skip the brand if its own site already sells well and it expects you to match or beat its price. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0961.031]
  • Retail margins are usually the same for every reseller, so negotiate only if the numbers do not work. (Older advice; undated.) [L0359.029]
  • Supplier prices are usually fixed once you are approved, so negotiation is rarely possible. Focus on the research and the approval process instead. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0961.027]
  • Some manufacturers sell direct at MSRP but allow retailers to sell at MAP, so a retailer can price below MSRP. Get approved, request the price list and confirm the margin before deciding. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0467.031]
  • When a brand is only available through a wholesaler, check the margin after the wholesaler's price and whether you have pricing control. Working through the wholesaler is acceptable when margin and pricing are right. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0801.028]
  • A supplier that sells the same product direct at the same price with free shipping leaves no room for a value proposition. List such products only if the store offers a bonus or better service; otherwise skip the supplier. (Older advice? Undated, 2023.) [L0964.030]
  • If a supplier sells direct to consumers and runs its own paid ads, listing its products on your store can still help build credibility when approaching other brands. Do not include them in paid ads, since they are unlikely to become a major earner. (2025.) [L0837.040]
  • If the supplier also sells directly to consumers at MAP, check its own retail website before deciding: whether it runs ads and whether the site is built to convert visitors. A supplier that does not invest in its retail channel can still be a profitable partner. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0854.117]
  • Judge a supplier's offer on its own numbers before listing. Approval does not mean every product is worth selling. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0663.033]

Supplier with thin margin after shipping

  • If a supplier's margin is too thin after shipping, drop that supplier's products even after approval. Approval does not mean every product is worth selling. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0663.033]
  • Supplier margins of 10-20% before payment fees and ad costs signal an affiliate-style relationship. Negotiate with these suppliers or skip them in favour of suppliers that meet the 50% wholesale rule. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0967.015]

Negotiating better terms

Timing: when negotiation works

  • In the early stage, with minimal sales history, you have little negotiating power with suppliers for better rates or terms. Focus optimisation on conversions and product selection. Negotiate more heavily once you have proven sales volume. [L0208.014]
  • Wholesale cost is usually not negotiable for a brand-new retailer, even one with a track record in other businesses. Suppliers typically want proof of sales first. Wholesale cost becomes negotiable as your sales volume with that supplier grows. [L0939.008]
  • Once a relationship is established, a supplier may give about 10% off wholesale cost. Some suppliers also offer special holiday pricing so the retailer can sell more of their products. [L0939.009]
  • Demand comes before cost. Build consistent demand first, for example 20 sales per day consistently and later 50 per day. Inconsistent demand is a bigger problem than supplier cost at an early stage. [L0644.041, L0644.042]
  • Validate first, then negotiate once you know the product will scale. Keep a second backup supplier so you can justify the price. [L0780.021]

Basic sourcing sequence (Source C)

  1. Start with CJ Dropshipping for convenience, since it is easy to set up and offers acceptable prices and shipping.
  2. Build consistent demand first, for example 20 sales per day consistently and later 50 per day.
  3. Once demand is steady, contact other suppliers, state your sales volume and ask whether they can beat your current price.
  4. Move the business only if the new supplier matches or beats the price and service.

[L0644.041]

Negotiation procedures

  1. Ask your current supplier, such as CJ, whether they can lower the price.
  2. Request quotes from several other suppliers for the same product.
  3. Compare the quotes and choose the supplier that gives the best margin and delivery terms.

[L0661.019]

  • In the shoe example, fulfilment cost per pair was about $13 to $15 after negotiation. That cost still left a healthy margin on a selling price of $40. [L0661.020]
  • A jewelry product with a fulfilment cost of about $7 per item allowed a selling price of $30 with a comfortable margin. The margin came from low cost of goods rather than from a high selling price. [L0661.021]
  • Check your supplier's best price rather than accepting the first offer, and ask whether the price can go lower once you have scale. [L0780.021]

Negotiation procedure with a volume threshold (older advice, 2020)

  1. Obtain the supplier's price list showing wholesale and MAP.
  2. If MAP is not about double the wholesale price, state that the price must be at that level to sell and that otherwise the brand will not be sold.
  3. Ask immediately whether the supplier offers volume-based pricing incentives, for example a discount once a monthly dollar or unit threshold is reached.
  4. Track monthly volume and follow up for the discount once the threshold is reached.

[L0967.023]

Incentive pricing after approval (Source A)

  1. Once you are approved, get their price list and upload their products right away.
  2. Ask whether they offer incentive pricing for selling a set number of units per month or for reaching revenue targets.
  3. Ask whether they give discounts off wholesale prices for your account.
  4. Before the busy season (Black Friday, Cyber Monday through Christmas), contact them again to negotiate special seasonal pricing.

Requesting better margins

  • Ask suppliers for a tiered retailer discount structure early in the process. Standard options include a percentage off wholesale or discounts at set sales volumes. [L0934.153]
  • Ask whether the supplier offers tiered retailer pricing, such as 20% off wholesale. Ask for discounts at set sales volumes, such as a number of units or sales. Decide to list or advertise the products only after you see the final margins. [L0934.030]
  • A supplier can be asked for better margins with a specific framing: say you want to promote their products and spend more on ads, and ask about incentive pricing. Start with friendly questions rather than an immediate request for discounts. (Older advice, 2023.) [L0835.046]
  • Before running ads on a supplier offering a 15-20% discount off retail, ask for better margins first. Some stores have negotiated better margins with suppliers, so a low spread can sometimes be improved. (Older advice, 2024.) [L0666.066]
  • When a supplier adds a drop-ship fee, shipping and taxes that erode your margin, negotiate first. If prices cannot change, exclude that supplier from paid ads, because organic sales still earn margin. Remove the supplier if the margin is still too small. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0469.026]
  • Ask whether a supplier offers a value-for-volume deal. Ask for better wholesale prices, incentive or volume programmes, and whether a retailer hitting a set number of units or sales unlocks lower wholesale prices. (Older advice, 2023.) [L0970.035]
  • Add the question to your early calls: ask suppliers for their tiered retailer discount structure, including volume discounts. Ask for 5 or 10 percent off at set sales levels. (Older advice; undated.) [L0359.029]

Excess stock and special offers

  • Suppliers often hold excess or discontinued stock they want to clear quickly, and offer discounts of up to 40% for sellers who promote it. A store that already has traffic can start moving that stock almost immediately by featuring the products or running new ads on them. A brick-and-mortar retailer must place a bulk order, wait for shelf space and then handle the stock before it can sell. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0929.034]
  • When a service provider such as a finisher or waterproofing shop has to market its services itself, you can ask for a discount if you can line up a steady group of customers who need the same service. The provider accepts a lower price because you reduce its acquisition effort and time. [L0701.038]

Using the list of suppliers as leverage

  • When competitors sell the same product for less than your supplier price list allows, they most likely have better supplier pricing or better shipping rates. They are not usually selling at a loss. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1068.032]
  • Ask suppliers about the product's price when a competitor is undercutting you. Possible answers include a cheaper carrier or a lower wholesale tier. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1002.062]
  • Email the supplier with your wholesale price, the competitor's price with free shipping and your shipping cost. Ask whether they can offer better pricing or cheaper shipping. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1002.060]

Low-margin handling

Options when the spread is too thin

  1. Contact the supplier to negotiate better wholesale pricing.
  2. If the margin is still acceptable but ad spend is unaffordable, keep the product and rely on organic traffic only.
  3. Otherwise drop the product.

[L0665.011] (Older advice, 2022.)

  • Low-margin products can still be listed without being run in paid campaigns. Keep them in free Google product listings for organic traffic, and keep paid campaigns focused on products with the largest spread. (Older advice, 2024.) [L0666.022]
  • If a supplier's margin is too thin, either do not list its products or list them without including them in any paid ads, so only free traffic reaches them. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0671.003]
  • Products with too small a margin to advertise can still be listed on the store and in the free listings section of Google Shopping, without any ad budget. (Older advice, 2024.) [L0836.041]
  • Spend paid traffic only on products from suppliers with healthy margins. Send the full catalogue to free Google product listings, and add suppliers to paid campaigns only after their margins are proven. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0968.012]
  • Non-MAP suppliers: do not run Google Shopping ads for products where competitors undercut your price on every listing. Invest in free traffic sources instead, and do not expose the products to price-match guarantees. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0969.042]
  • When margins are too thin, ask suppliers whether they have incentive or volume programmes that lower wholesale prices once a retailer hits a set number of units or sales volume. If the answer is no, decide whether to list the brand with no ad spend or skip it entirely. (Older advice, 2023.) [L0970.035]
  • Do not match prices of non-MAP competitors when no margin is available. If the supplier enforces MAP, ask it to contact the competitor. Otherwise keep your price where you can make money and do not run Google Shopping ads on those products. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0834.025]
  • Do not sell a product at a price below MAP through advertising. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0963.093]
  • If a supplier that does not enforce MAP sells the same product elsewhere, check it has no price-match policy before approaching. If margins are too thin, exclude those products from paid ads and rely on free Google Shopping listings. (Older advice; undated.) [L0941.020]

Thin margin: a shipping example (older advice, 2019)

  1. Calculate the margin: retail price minus wholesale cost minus shipping. Example: $699 - $530 = $169, but $200 shipping would leave a loss.
  2. Email the supplier with your wholesale price, the competitor's price with free shipping and your shipping cost, and ask whether they can offer better pricing or cheaper shipping.
  3. If the supplier refuses, still list the product if people search for it, but exclude it from paid traffic.
  4. Price the product with your own margin, and exclude it from your price-match guarantee.

Checks before listing a product

Set up each supplier separately

  • Pricing rules, margins and MAP requirements belong to each supplier, not to the niche. A niche is not disqualified by one low-margin supplier if others offer the 50%-of-MAP structure. [L1090.014]
  • Margins are set per supplier and can differ within one store. Some suppliers offer 15%, others 20% or 30%, so track margin per supplier, not as one store-wide figure. (older, 2021-22) [L1480.015]
  • Individual products within one supplier can also have different margins. [L1090.014]
  • MAP requirements differ from brand to brand. Run the 2x wholesale check for each supplier, not once across the catalog. If a brand's MAP is too close to its wholesale cost, negotiate better pricing before listing its products. (older, 2019) [L1086.069]

Check the pricing rules before discounting

  • Ask the supplier for its MAP policy before you discount. Many suppliers allow discounts, but some forbid coupon codes. An automatic coupon that pushes the advertised price below MAP can break the policy. (older, 2020) [L1088.020]
  • Extra discounts or coupon codes on top of MAP are allowed unless the supplier's retailer approval agreement forbids them. This restriction is rare. If a supplier has it, exclude that brand from promotions. (older, 2019) [L1086.032]
  • To find a brand's MAP without asking the supplier, search the brand name on Google Shopping and compare the prices sellers list for its products. Ask the supplier directly only if this does not reveal the price. (older, 2022) [L1113.085]
  • If a supplier's listed online price is below its MAP, ask the supplier about it immediately. Do not assume MAP is being enforced. (older, 2020) [L1111.027]

The 50% rule (wholesale against MAP)

  • Wholesale cost should be no more than 50% of MAP. Example: a MAP of 5,000 USD requires a wholesale cost of 2,500 USD or less. (older, 2020-21) [L1090.012, L1111.021, L1112.044, L1277.029]
  • The spread must cover shipping, payment fees, ad spend and profit. A wider spread leaves more room for these costs. (older, 2020-21) [L1111.021, L1112.044, L1277.029]
  • Exceptions are rare and only considered for very expensive items that sell consistently and easily. (older, 2021) [L1090.012]
  • MAP is often around 2x the wholesale cost. (older, 2018) [L1274.031]

Profit after product cost

Formula:

Profit after product cost = MAP − wholesale cost − average shipping cost − payment fees (2.9% for Shopify Payments) − other fees [L1111.022]

Shipping in the margin

Judge shipping against price

  • Shipping cost must be judged against the product's sale price and average order value, not against weight alone. A $800 shipping cost on a $5,000 product can still leave about $1,500 net profit, while the same $800 on a $1,200 product destroys the margin. (older, 2018) [L1085.006]
  • Example: two dining tables of the same size and weight cost $300 on average to ship. At $500 retail the $300 shipping makes the product unworkable, while at $1,500 retail the same shipping cost is absorbable. Shipping is therefore judged relative to the product price, even within one industry. (older, 2018) [L1085.007]
  • Example: a $1,000 product with about $500 gross margin and shipping of up to $300 leaves only about $200 for ads. Bringing shipping down to about $200 frees up enough money for ad costs and profit. Targeting customers in states close to the supplier is one way to lower shipping. (older, 2018) [L1109.007]
  • Shipping costs vary widely by destination. Remote states such as Montana or Idaho can be far more expensive to ship to. For a large item, a shipping cost of $450 instead of $250 can push a product into the danger zone. (older, 2021-22) [L1148.021]

  • Shipping costs depend on origin, destination, package size, weight and the carrier used, so they do not need precise calculation for every product at the start. If the shipping cost makes low-price items unprofitable, sell only the higher-priced items in that range. (older, 2018) [L1085.008]
  • Shipping can be quoted or estimated from the average shipping cost for each niche when those averages are known. Quotes are only needed when shipping costs are unknown. (older, 2020) [L1111.047]
  • Estimate shipping as follows (older, 2021-22) [L1148.023]:
    1. Ask your supplier what it generally costs to ship an item from its location to your target regions. [L1148.023]
    2. Do not aim for exact figures, because the cost varies per order unless the supplier offers flat-rate shipping. [L1148.023]
    3. Estimate a typical shipping cost for each product, and compare item A with item B. [L1148.023]
    4. Enter the estimate in the supplier shipping cost field and adjust it once real orders come in. [L1148.023]

  1. Apply for approval with each supplier. [L1146.003]
  2. After approval, receive the supplier's price list. [L1146.003]
  3. Build your own price list by adding a third column for your sale price next to the wholesale cost and MSRP columns. [L1146.003]
  4. Set the sale price using the 2x wholesale minimum, and go higher if competitors sell above that level. [L1146.003]
  5. Review your paid advertising KPIs every week. [L1146.003]

Structure of your own price list (supplier lists usually contain only the first two columns) [L1146.004]:

Column Content
Wholesale cost What you pay the supplier per unit (your cost of goods)
MSRP Manufacturer's Suggested Retail Price, the selling price the manufacturer recommends
Sale price Your own selling price, added by you because no MAP sets it outside MAP markets

Check the starting price against competitors

  • For non-MAP suppliers, set a starting price of roughly 2.1 times the wholesale cost, then compare with competitor prices on Google Shopping. Competitors may be selling at 3 times MAP, which would make a higher price the right choice. (older, 2020) [L1111.025]
  • Check the supplier's listed MAP-free wholesale prices and the prices two to three stores advertise them for (older, 2021) [L1279.024]:
    1. Check the supplier's listed MAP-free wholesale prices and the prices two to three stores advertise them for. [L1279.024]
    2. If those stores sell at at least 2x wholesale, list the product at that price without undercutting them. [L1279.024]
    3. Exclude these products from your price match guarantee to avoid losses. [L1279.024]
  • Products of the same type can be offered by different companies at very different price levels, for example about $700 from one company and about $3,500 from another. A store that combines both price levels for a high-end supplier did not face pushback from that supplier. (older, 2020) [L1111.179]

Raise margin without negotiating

  • Dropshipping an accessory from the same manufacturer you already use solves the logistics side, which makes upsells and downsells of existing brand accessories one of the easiest ways to raise margins in a store. (older, 2021-22) [L1152.006]
  • Case: a dog toy was sourced for 6 USD from suppliers while competitors sold it for 9.99 USD. Competitors likely either bought in bulk or used slow AliExpress fulfillment, and they accepted lower margins to win volume. A beginner should aim for higher margins to reinvest profits, so the choice was between raising the price or adding a shipping fee. [L1176.029]

Negotiating better terms

Ask early and track volume

  • Ask suppliers early about volume-based pricing tiers and incentive pricing. Revisit negotiation when a product shows no margin or when the store brings a large volume of sales to that supplier. (older, 2019) [L1069.016]
  • Once you have proven sales volume with a supplier, you can ask for better pricing terms. If margins do not work from day one, ask the supplier for better pricing at the start. (older, 2020) [L1277.034]
  • A processing fee of about $6 to $25 per order is uncommon. It is acceptable when the order value is high (for example $200 to $300), and it can be negotiated later. (older, 2019) [L1069.005]

Thank you,
[Your name]

We have worked together for [time period] and we want the partnership to be profitable for both sides. Right now our profit margin on your products is only about [X]% after shipping. We have seen competitors [Competitor A, B, C] selling the same products at [price] with free shipping.

Low-margin handling

Decide what to do with a thin product

Procedure when a supplier's margin is too low to fund ads (2023) [L1283.008]:

  1. Ask the supplier if it has pricing incentives or different pricing tiers. [L1283.008]
  2. If nothing changes, list the products on the store without running ads for them and rely on organic traffic. [L1283.008]
  3. Only keep them if the margin is enough for the products to be worth the time. [L1283.008]

Procedure when a product has negative net after shipping (2024) [L1285.006]:

  1. Ask the supplier for a better wholesale price. [L1285.006]
  2. If the supplier says this is their best price, exclude that product from paid ads. [L1285.006]
  3. Add other suppliers who can offer better margins so the budget goes to winning products. [L1285.006]

Procedure when a supplier's product is priced below your margin needs (2021) [L1112.054]:

  1. Ask for better wholesale pricing first. [L1112.054]
  2. If they refuse and the margin is too thin for ads, keep the product at MAP and rely on organic traffic without including it in Shopping, or drop it. [L1112.054]
  • If a supplier gives a pricing incentive, the question is whether margins support ad spend. If a supplier refuses any change in price structure and competitors list at a 10 percent markup over the supplier price, it is often not worth listing. (2023) [L1283.018]
  • For a very poor supplier where little margin remains, either skip the products entirely or list them above MAP with no paid ads. Paid ads should be reserved for products with enough margin to cover the ad cost. (older, 2020) [L1111.024]
  • Case: a seller had large, heavy products whose shipping cost wiped out the margin at MAP. The case and fix are in the shipping section above. [L1085.010, L1085.011]

Ads or free traffic

  • If the remaining margin covers a sale at MAP but not paid ads, keep the products listed and exclude them from Google Ads. They can still earn free Google product listing and organic traffic. (older, 2020) [L1111.023]
  • Reserve paid ads for products with enough margin to cover the ad cost. [L1111.024]
  • Before you run ads on a product, confirm that the target price covers the average cost per purchase. For TikTok ads, the source sets 3x landed cost as the minimum sale price. [L1183.008]
  • Target product margin before running ads, so that sales are profitable and scalable. [L1259.055]
  • When the supplier's margin is too low, the order of action is: ask the supplier for incentives, then list without ads for organic traffic, then keep only if the margin is worth the time. [L1283.008]
  • If MAP enforcement fails after escalating up the supplier's chain of command, stop paid ads for that brand. Keep the products on the store for organic traffic only if the MAP policy allows it. (older, 2021) [L1112.056]
  • If a product has negative net after shipping and the supplier cannot improve the price, exclude it from paid ads and move the budget to suppliers with better margins. [L1285.006]
  • Before pausing ads on a product whose competitor is violating MAP, search the competitor's name online and read the reviews. If reviews show poor customer service, late shipping or payment problems, the violator is likely to lose customers and it can be worth keeping ads running. (2023) [L1094.019]

Direct-to-consumer undercutting and competitor prices

  • Supplier sales and temporary discounts can be listed on the store, and there is no need to worry about undercutting other suppliers. Contact brands around the holidays to ask if they will offer deals, and the store can promote those discounts with graphics and emails. (2023) [L1114.024]
  • Clearance items are typically exempt from MAP compliance. A competitor selling clearance stock below MAP is therefore not automatically a violation. (older, 2019) [L1275.019]
  • If a supplier starts selling its best-selling products below your dealer cost or discounts them heavily on its own site, pause those products and do not spend ad money on them until the supplier explains and corrects the pricing. (older, 2021) [L1090.022]
  • Some suppliers sell at a price so close to cost that a MAP violator cannot sustain it. Such retailers tend to get bad reputations, so their reviews and customer complaints should be checked before reacting to their price. (2023) [L1094.020]
  • A retailer that sells below wholesale price is very likely a scam or burner store that never ships orders. Do not match such prices, and build trust with your own store through reviews instead. (2024) [L1115.015]

MAP violation monitoring and reporting

Find the violation

  • Testing whether a competitor violates MAP on Google Shopping (2023) [L1094.018]:
    1. Search the product on Google Shopping. [L1094.018]
    2. Read the prices shown by the listings, including the free listings from three stores. [L1094.018]
    3. If most listings show one price and one or two show a much lower price, the most common price is probably MAP and the lower one is a violation. [L1094.018]
  • Search the brand name and SKU in Google to find competitors who are selling below the minimum advertised price. Copy the links to the competitor listings. (undated) [L1472.036]
  • Expect manufacturers to use software to monitor MAP compliance, and note that large retailers are less often penalized than smaller ones. (undated) [L1472.036]
  • A distribution center that warns you about not updating prices on time is a good sign, because it means they care about you maximizing profit. Keep product prices current in your store to avoid these warnings. (undated) [L1472.035]

Report the violation

  • When a supplier's MAP is known and a competitor sells below it, report the violation to the supplier right away with a screenshot. Ask whether the competitor has a special deal or whether the supplier will contact them. Good suppliers enforce MAP and may remove an unauthorized retailer as an authorized dealer. (2024) [L1115.014]
  • Handling a MAP violation by a competitor (2021) [L1090.018]:
    1. Screenshot the competitor's product page showing the low price. [L1090.018]
    2. Search the product in Google Shopping and screenshot the listing showing the same lower price. [L1090.018]
    3. Email the supplier contact with the competitor URL, both screenshots and a request to correct the pricing. [L1090.018]
    4. If the price is not updated within a few days, reply to the same thread. Most suppliers fix pricing quickly. Amazon and Walmart listings below MAP can be reported to the supplier the same way. [L1090.018]
  • Reporting procedure (older, 2019) [L1275.018]:
    1. Report every MAP violator to the supplier. [L1275.018]
    2. Follow up with the supplier regularly. [L1275.018]
    3. If nothing changes after about one to two months, stop selling that supplier's products and stop spending ad budget on them. [L1275.018]
    4. Take screenshots of competitors selling below your cost, send them to the supplier, and ask whether a better pricing tier or incentive is available. [L1275.018]
  • Send the supplier screenshots of the Google Shopping listing and the competitor's website. A good supplier enforces MAP and responds within the number of days in the MAP policy. If nothing changes, stop paid ads for that brand, keep listings at MAP for organic traffic, or remove the supplier. (older, 2021) [L1112.055]
  • Send the supplier links to the competitor listings. The supplier can then contact the violating retailer through its representatives. (undated) [L1472.036]

Escalation and when to stop

  • If MAP enforcement fails after escalating up the supplier's chain of command, stop paid ads for that brand. Keep the products on the store for organic traffic only if the MAP policy allows it. (older, 2021) [L1112.056]
  • Sources differ on timing. The 2019 procedure stops selling after about one to two months without change [L1275.018]. The 2021 procedure responds to the number of days in the MAP policy and then stops paid ads [L1112.055]. Use the timing in the supplier's MAP policy where it exists, and the one to two month limit where it does not. [L1275.018, L1112.055]

Supplier invoices and cash reserve

  • Not tracking exactly what is owed to suppliers for each order leads to unexpected supplier bills. Allocating a COGS percentage of every sale into a dedicated account gives a reserve that covers these invoices. (older, 2021-22) [L1200.025]
  • Case: a high-ticket dropshipper had just started out and was beginning to see sales and profit rise, believing the business was close to breaking even. After several months, a supplier sent an unexpected bill of 10,000 to 15,000 dollars. The dropshipper had believed all supplier orders were paid, but they were not, so the owner had to borrow money from friends and family to cover the bill. (older, 2021-22) [L1200.022]
  • Allocate the COGS percentage from [L1200.010] into a dedicated account from each sale, so that supplier invoices are covered. [L1200.010, L1200.025]
