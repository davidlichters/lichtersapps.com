3.14 Freight, heavy items and white glove delivery

Why heavy items change the margin

  • Large and heavy products cost more to ship, which reduces the profit left per sale. When you compare a cheap product with an expensive one, include the shipping cost in both. [L0474.003]
  • Large and heavy items need a separate shipping calculation, because their weight- or dimension-based cost can change a product's margin far more than it does for small items. [L0430.011]
  • Large or bulky products such as home decor can be expensive to ship. Calculate the shipping cost against the ad price and margin before testing, because shipping can make a product that looks profitable in the ads unprofitable in practice. [L0638.058]
  • Large and heavy products usually ship at freight rates rather than standard parcel rates. Confirm weight and dimension thresholds with carriers before you set the selling price. [L0675.011]
  • Heavy and bulky products stay profitable only if the margin can absorb freight costs. Compare quotes from several companies and check the supplier's preferred carrier first. [L0967.039]
  • Heavy items can cost more to ship than the margin allows. Example: a 60 lb item may cost 160 USD to ship. Calculate the average freight cost before you list and advertise the product. [L0836.040]
  • Example of shipping-driven pricing: a 430-dollar shipping cost on an order that earns 90 dollars of margin makes the item unprofitable. [L0964.044]

Check shipping cost before listing or advertising

Set a price floor by category

  • Older (2019): For heavy or bulky items, ask suppliers for average shipping costs to the markets you plan to serve before choosing a product. Set a minimum price threshold so the margin covers shipping, and do not list cheaper items in that category. [L0357.019]
  • Older (2019) example: If a heavy item such as a gun safe costs about $250 to ship, a $200 product cannot be sold profitably. If the lowest profitable price is $600, every item below $600 is excluded, and customers who want cheaper items are left to competitors. [L0357.020]
  • Older (2020): If a brand's terms require a freight shipment on your account and the product margin works without MAP, freight is worth doing. Ask the supplier which freight company it recommends and work with that company. [L0358.047]
  • A 30% markup on heavy products may not cover shipping costs. Shop freight quotes from multiple carriers instead of accepting the supplier's shipping quote. [L0276.025]
  • Older (2022): For a large or heavy product with nationwide shipping of 100 to 150 dollars (up to 200 to 250 dollars for huge items), selling at a low price is not profitable with free shipping. [L0665.067]
  • Older (2019): Carriers such as freight companies can cost around $150 per shipment, which makes items priced at $250 to $300 unprofitable. Ask each supplier which carrier it ships with. [L0663.044]

Estimate a whole range of products

Procedure: estimating shipping costs for many supplier products without quoting each one (2024)

  1. For each product, calculate the spread as retail (MAP) minus wholesale cost in a spreadsheet, and sort from highest to lowest spread.
  2. Look at the products with the smallest spread at the bottom of the sheet.
  3. Ask the supplier for package size and weight for those 20 to 30 low-spread products.
  4. Get free shipping quotes for those products from freightcenter.com.
  5. Products where the shipping cost would consume the margin are the ones not to list.

[L0666.024, L0666.025]

Procedure: estimating shipping cost for heavy items before pricing (2024)

  • Enter the supplier warehouse address and several made-up customer addresses across the country into freightcenter.com.
  • Read the average shipping cost per item from the results.
  • Set the selling price so the product stays profitable.

[L0836.039]

Procedure: estimating whether a large, heavy product is worth selling before you have supplier prices (older, 2022)

  1. Assume a wholesale cost, for example half of the expected selling price.
  2. Ask the supplier candidates for the box size and weight of the product.
  3. Get shipping quotes using these dimensions and weight (no account login needed).
  4. Check whether the spread after shipping is big enough to leave a solid margin.

[L0854.023]

Procedure: get estimated freight rates before orders come in (2024)

  1. Use a free instant freight quote site such as freightcenter.com.
  2. Enter the supplier's zip code as origin.
  3. Enter several customer zip codes in different regions, for example one near the supplier, one on the East Coast and one in the West or South.
  4. Compare the quotes to estimate shipping costs.

Exact costs need both addresses and the package size. [L0806.024]

Procedure: average freight cost for free-shipping pricing (older, 2021)

  1. Get the supplier's typical product size and weight.
  2. Use a freight quote tool such as freightcenter.com for freight shipments.
  3. Set the ship-from address to the supplier's warehouse, for example New York.
  4. Get quotes to ship-to addresses in several regions, for example New York, Florida, Texas and California.
  5. Average the quotes and build that cost into the price of free shipping.

[L0801.039]

Get freight quotes and compare them

Why quotes differ

  • Freight brokers are approximately 50% cheaper than UPS or FedEx for large and heavy items. [L0251.010]
  • Using only one freight carrier can lead to overpaying by about $100 to $200 per large shipment. Comparing quotes from several carriers can recover that difference. [L0854.006]
  • Different quotes for the same shipment often differ by $100 to $300. [L0852.043]
  • Older (2019): Freight prices can differ by hundreds of dollars between providers. Use several freight brokers for large freight. [L1086.037]
  • Older (2019): Accepting the first freight quote is costly, because overpaying on freight can cost hundreds of dollars per shipment. Compare several brokers before shipping large goods. [L1086.039]
  • Older (2021): The same carriers are quoted with different markups on different platforms. Compare the quotes from providers such as Freight Club and Freight Center on every order, because one can be cheaper by about $100. [L0834.018]
  • Older (2021): Large, heavy products shipped from a single distribution point can vary by $150 or more between regions. Use separate shipping calculators for the supplier's location and average costs from several destinations, for example California, New York, Texas and Florida. [L0834.017]
  • Freight quotes are free, and prices differ for the same shipment. Get quotes from several freight brokers. Freight Center is a common starting point, and Blue Freight Forwarder is another option members often use. [L0941.030]
  • Freight quotes from brokers such as freightcenter.com and Red Hawk can differ. Collect quotes for the same shipment from several broker websites. [L0966.036]

Procedure: building an average rate from supplier products (older, 2020)

  1. Ask the supplier for the top three best-selling products, with weight and packaging dimensions.
  2. Open a freight rate calculator and set the supplier's warehouse as the origin.
  3. Enter the dimensions and weight of each product as the shipment.
  4. Enter several destination addresses across different regions, for example one in the origin state, one in Florida, one in Texas and one in California.
  5. Average the results to get a ballpark rate.
  6. Repeat with more suppliers until the rates are known from experience.

Procedure: getting a freight quote through freightcenter.com for an item shipped from a supplier warehouse (older, 2021)

  • Step 3: Accept the quote to receive a bill of lading (BOL), which states the shipment route.
  • Step 4: Send the BOL to the supplier, who attaches it to the package.

  • Suppliers usually have the best shipping rates because they move high volume. To test a freight quote, send the supplier's shipping quote, with supplier details removed, to one or more freight brokers and ask them to beat it. [L0805.025]
  • Older (2023): If a supplier will not ship on its own account, compare its freight carrier quote with a freight aggregator quote. Differences of $100 to $200 per shipment are possible and add up over time. [L0970.037]
  • Freight aggregators collect quotes from many carriers for heavy items when you enter origin, destination, package size and weight. A quote that seems extremely high, such as over $1,000 for a 250-pound item crossing from Canada to the US west coast, should be checked before you accept it. [L0970.040]

Procedure: checking supplier freight quotes (2023)

  1. For each new order, ask the supplier to send a quote from its freight carrier, giving the order number, product and customer shipping address.

  3. Compare the aggregator quotes from different carriers with the supplier's quote.

Choose who books the freight: supplier account or your own

Supplier account

  • Some suppliers allow you to ship on their account. They charge you for product and shipping together. Others require you to ship on your own account. [L0255.011]
  • The ideal setup for freight: the supplier quotes the order, creates the shipment and the Bill of Lading (BOL), and charges shipping on its own carrier account. The store does not set up freight shipping in Shopify. [L0854.003] (older, 2022)
  • For large and heavy items such as furniture, it is normal for the supplier to ask the retailer to ship on the retailer's own shipping account. Expect this request when a supplier names a shipping account. [L0470.018]
  • When suppliers ship on their own account, they typically get volume discounts on freight, so the shipping cost is better than you can negotiate alone. [L0255.012]

Procedure: freight shipping when the supplier ships on its own account (2024)

  1. The supplier books the freight carrier and sends a tracking number when the shipment is ready.
  2. You forward the tracking number to the customer.
  3. The freight carrier contacts the customer to schedule delivery, for example on a specific day between 12 and 3 pm, since large heavy items are not dropped at the door.

[L0805.022]

Procedure: freight shipping with a supplier that ships only to you (2024)

  1. Get quotes from a freight broker for the shipment.
  2. Choose the price and carrier you want.
  3. The broker issues a bill of lading (BOL).
  4. Send the BOL to the supplier.
  5. The supplier arranges with the trucking company to pick up the goods at the warehouse and deliver them to the customer.

[L0806.022]

Your own account

  • Shipping on your own account makes you responsible for anything that happens in transit. Accept it only for large or heavy items when no other option exists, and do not write the supplier off immediately. [L0466.036] (older, 2018)
  • Some suppliers ship on their own account, and some require you to ship on yours. When you do, arrange the shipment as described below. [L0255.011, L0255.013]
  • If a supplier says it will not ship on its own account, ask which of its top retailers use which carrier. Those retailers usually have the best rates. Contact that carrier to arrange pickup at the supplier's warehouse. [L0854.008] (older, 2022)
  • Ask the supplier to send a quote and to check whether its carrier is competitive. Use the supplier's carrier if it is in line with the market. [L0970.038]

Procedure: freight shipping when you ship on your own account (not UPS or FedEx) (2024)

  1. Create the shipment through a freight broker such as freightcenter.com.
  2. The broker generates a BOL (Bill of Lading), a document describing the shipment from the supplier warehouse to the customer address.
  3. Send the BOL to the supplier.
  4. The supplier arranges pickup at its warehouse with the freight shipper, who delivers to the customer.

[L0805.023]

Procedure: arranging freight shipping on your own account (2025)

  1. Shop online for freight quotes from multiple carriers.
  2. Compare pricing across different quotes.
  3. Select the quote you prefer.
  4. Pay for the shipment.
  5. Receive a BOL (Bill of Lading), which shows pickup location (supplier warehouse) and delivery location.
  6. Email the BOL to the supplier.
  7. The freight company contacts the supplier to arrange pickup date and time.
  8. The freight company picks up the order from the supplier warehouse.
  9. The shipment gets a tracking number and delivers to the customer.

Pick the carrier type: parcel or freight

Parcel carriers for small items

  • For small parcels, UPS and FedEx usually have the best rates. Their freight rates for large items are often well above what freight brokers quote, so for freight use freight brokers and ask them about discounts. [L0835.033]
  • For normal-size parcels, UPS and FedEx are reasonable. [L0972.099]
  • Older (2019): Small parcels with UPS, FedEx or USPS usually cost about the same. The freight-broker advice in this section applies only to freight. [L1086.037]
  • Ask each supplier which carrier it ships with. UPS, FedEx or DHL keep shipping affordable for compact products. [L0663.044] (older, 2019)

Freight for large and heavy items

  • Large and heavy items are shipped by LTL (less than truckload) freight from the supplier's warehouse to the customer, not with UPS or FedEx. [L0855.061]
  • Large and heavy items are far too expensive to ship with UPS, FedEx or DHL as parcels, so they move through freight brokers instead. Parcel carriers are only used for smaller products. [L0854.002] (older, 2022)
  • For products above a certain size and weight, shipping with UPS or FedEx becomes too expensive, so you ship with a freight carrier. Freight is the standard option for large and heavy items. [L0961.032] (older, 2020)
  • For large and heavy items such as 9 x 57 x 36 inches and about 70 pounds, use LTL freight shipping instead of parcel carriers like FedEx and UPS. [L1085.009] (older, 2018)
  • Heavy items of 300 to 400 pounds usually ship as LTL freight, which is expensive. Get freight quotes from a site such as freightcenter.com by entering the supplier warehouse, package size, weight and destination. Negotiate rates, since there is often room. [L0804.035]
  • Freight-shipped items for a product of about 100 to 350 pounds can be quoted up to $600 per shipment as LTL. Compare quotes from several freight companies before setting shipping prices. [L0667.017]
  • Less-than-truckload freight quotes for heavy or long-distance orders can be very high, for example 500 to 600 USD per shipment. Check whether a product can still be sold profitably before advertising it. [L0966.037]
  • For large and heavy furniture and similar bulky products, freight shipping is usually handled as LTL freight. Aggregator sites that handle heavy shipping cover this category. [L0970.041]
  • Use the 'UPS or FedEx for normal parcels, freight for large' split: for large and heavy items, use traditional freight shippers, since the freight rates of UPS and FedEx are far higher. [L0972.099]

UPS and FedEx freight

  • Never use UPS Freight or FedEx Freight for freight items. Their rates are far too expensive for heavy or large freight orders. [L0806.023] (2024)
  • UPS and FedEx are not used for freight shipments because they are too expensive. For freight, the shipment is created through a freight broker and a BOL is generated. [L0805.024]
  • UPS and FedEx freight for large or heavy items costs about four times more than other freight companies. Use them only for very small items. [L0855.062]
  • For large and heavy items, UPS and FedEx freight rates are usually far above traditional freight shipping rates. Get quotes from traditional freight shippers before you set shipping prices. [L0972.010]
  • Parcel carriers such as UPS, FedEx and USPS are usually expensive for heavy or bulky items. Use a freight broker or freight company for those shipments and compare quotes. [L0468.041] (older, 2020)

  • Ask the supplier which carriers its biggest retailers use. Get quotes from those carriers directly. Compare with freight quotes from FreightCenter.com, which is commonly used for LTL and white-glove freight. [L0803.021] (older, 2022)

Procedure: finding a freight shipper for a product (2025)

  2. Ask for the names of those shipping companies.
  3. Request quotes from those companies, since they usually offer the best rate for that product size and weight.

Book the shipment and the bill of lading

What the BOL is

  • A bill of lading (BOL) is the freight equivalent of a parcel shipping label. The freight company provides it, and you send it to the supplier so the freight company can pick up the order. [L0835.031]
  • BOL stands for Bill of Lading. It shows the pickup location (supplier warehouse) and the delivery location (customer address) for freight shipments. [L0255.014]
  • Freight transit estimates, such as four to six business days, are not a fixed promise until a delivery window is confirmed, so delays can happen before that point. [L0470.097]

Geo-targeting heavy products in ads

The principle

  • For heavy items that are expensive to ship over long distances, sell nationally but run paid ads only in the area near the supplier's warehouse. Geo-targeting keeps shipping costs low for paid orders. [L0853.059] (older, 2021)
  • Products from a heavy, large supplier may be sold with a geo-targeted campaign around the warehouse, so that orders ship only from a short distance and shipping cost stays low. For most campaigns, geo-targeting should be off. [L0964.045]
  • To keep expensive heavy items profitable in ads, split suppliers into separate campaigns with geo-targeting. Put the heavy, low-margin supplier products into their own campaigns targeted within a 50-mile radius of the warehouse, so shipping costs stay low. [L0941.026]

Freight Basics for Large and Heavy Items

  • Large and heavy items that do not fit normal parcel shipping are quoted as freight. Request a freight quote before setting the price of a bulky item. [L1105.011]
  • A freight calculator estimates shipping costs when you compare supplier or shipping options. [L1096.004]
  • Less-than-truckload (LTL) freight has no fixed price. In a 2018 source, quotes for the same shipment differed by around $200 between companies. A 2020 source (older) says the difference can be a hundred dollars or more. The gap varies per shipment, so compare quotes for each one, even when you offer free shipping to customers. [L1109.008, L1111.037]
  • Overpaying for freight is a costly early mistake (2020, older). Always compare quotes before booking a shipment. [L1111.038]
  • For large and heavy items, freight brokers quote the shipping. Brokers are intermediaries, not the carriers themselves. Early on, quotes from different brokers tend to be similar. A quote of about $400 for a sub-$200 item shows that the selling price must be high. Competing quotes will not fix the margin on low-priced heavy goods. [L1114.068]

Shipping Terms and Carrier Accounts

  • Confirm each supplier's shipping terms before you build LTL costs into product prices. Estimate costs for the suppliers that do not cover them, because who pays changes the cost. [L1097.045]

  • Some suppliers require the retailer to have its own freight account on file. They ship on that account and add the freight cost to the invoice. These charges can get expensive, so compare them before committing. [L1094.001]

  • Shipping on your own account lets you track shipments, follow up with the carrier and file claims for the customer. This is easier when the supplier is not the point of contact for the carrier. [L1094.003]

    3. Send the account number to the supplier, who enters it when creating shipping labels.
    4. Pay the supplier for the product, and receive the shipping bill directly from the carrier.

    2. On the home page, request a fast quote by entering the package dimensions and weight, the pickup suburb (supplier) and the delivery suburb (customer).
    3. From the price list, choose the cheapest option whose name ends in 'ATL'. If no option ends in 'ATL', choose the cheapest option overall. Click 'Book Now'.
    4. Enter the collection address (supplier) and the delivery address (customer), then click next.
    5. Pick the pickup date. It must be the following business day, so a booking made on Wednesday is set for Thursday. You do not need to set a time. Click next.
    6. Once the pickup date and time are confirmed, click next again.

    8. In the Payment section, select the 'Stored Debit/Credit Card' button. This bills the company account automatically.
    9. Leave the card dropdown on its default setting, which is the company card saved to the account. Tick the box, then click 'Pay Now'.
    10. On the confirmation screen, download the Shipping Labels and the Address Labels and save them to your computer.
    11. Send both labels to the supplier along with the payment confirmation for the order. Give the supplier the pickup time and the carrier.
    12. If you need to track the order for the customer, use the saved documents.

    The method works for any niche and fits margin-sensitive products. [L1281.005]

Regional Shipping Cost and Geo-Targeting

  • Shipping cost varies with origin and destination. Shipping within the same state is cheaper than shipping across the country. Cost estimates for heavy items should use realistic average values, not a single fixed number. [L1180.006]
  • For bulky products sold with free shipping, prefer a supplier located near the main customer region. In the example, shipping within one state cost $150 versus $300 across the country. That difference alone changed net profit from $300 to $450 per order. [L1180.016]

Bulky Products: Packaging and Shipping Method

  • Volumetric weight starts to matter above about 1 kg. Bulky items are charged for the space they occupy, so large or heavy products such as furniture can become very expensive and may need special shipping lines. [L1217.013]
  • Large high-ticket products such as outdoor structures usually ship disassembled in boxes, not fully assembled. Before listing a bulky product, confirm the packed form and shipping method with the supplier (2021-22, older). [L1481.027]

Further details

Freight cost check for a new supplier:

  1. Ask the supplier for a freight quote for the first order from the carrier they use.
  2. Get a second quote on freightcenter.com using the product dimensions, the supplier's pickup location and the customer's delivery location.
  3. Compare both quotes and choose the cheaper option, or negotiate with the supplier using the lower quote. [L1094.002]
