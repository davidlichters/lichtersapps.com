3.10 Approval, agreements and account setup

Approval, Forms and First Contact

  • Expect the supplier's account manager to reply within a few days after you submit the application forms, with approval, an account number and contact details, or a request for more information. [L0133.012]
  • Delaying the return of supplier forms significantly lowers your approval rate, so complete and submit them as soon as they arrive. [L0133.013]
  • (older, 2022) Fill out supplier authorized-retailer forms as soon as they arrive and return them right away, so products can go live without downtime. [L1092.038]
  • Letting an application or product information sit unanswered slows the whole sourcing process. Return applications and upload product data as fast as possible so you do not lose time between suppliers. [L0495.004]
  • A dealer or retailer application on a supplier's website is the same process as the formal approval paperwork, so fill it out the same way. [L1111.178]
  • (older, 2021-22) A brand's dealer application typically asks for first and last name, email, phone number and a business street address, and a sales representative then calls back. Use the same details and phone number you plan to use when you call the supplier. [L0932.018]
  • (older, 2020) To find the right person:
    1. Call the supplier's main contact numbers and ask who handles retailer approvals.
    2. Submit the approval paperwork.
    3. Ask whether they ship on your account or their own. [L1111.028]
  • Approval reveals the supplier's actual price list, which shows your real margin and what competitors earn per sale, so you can skip any product with weak margins. Getting approved costs nothing and does not oblige you to list any product. [L1146.002]
  • Some suppliers do not approve a new store immediately. Gold suppliers commonly reject or ask to re-review once a store has more products. Application fields asking for Facebook or Instagram accounts can be left blank until those accounts exist. [L0969.007]
  • (older, 2020) You can contact suppliers and get approved while your business bank account or DBA is still pending. You can request price lists and CSVs and prepare the site, and simply hold orders until the account is ready. [L0961.028]
  • (older, 2020) If a supplier that owns several brands approves the account, get approved with all of its brands rather than just the first one. [L0967.026]
  • (older, 2019) If you have not been approved by a brand yet:
    1. Contact the brand and go through its normal approval process.
    2. Once approved, request its price lists.
    3. Compare the wholesale price, the retail price and the shipping cost for each item.
    4. Approval does not require listing every product the brand sells, so list only the items whose margin works. [L0357.066]
  • (older, 2018) Some approved suppliers do not require a contract and only send specifications, prices and photos. This is enough to start, and products should be uploaded and traffic set up as soon as possible. [L0466.023]
  • (older, 2021-22) Secure supplier approval before listing their products on your store, because suppliers must agree to let you sell their products through your website. [L0912.008]
  • (older, 2019) Approval steps once a supplier moves toward approval:
    1. Stop other tasks and prioritize the supplier while the conversation is moving forward.
    2. Send the documentation the supplier requests and sign the MAP agreement.
    3. Get approved, then receive the product CSV and pricing sheets.
    4. Check that your selling price can be competitive and still profitable against the MSRP and MAP.
    5. Upload the supplier's products to the store. [L0467.077]
  • Approval order (2025):
    1. After approval, the supplier sends a simple agreement to sign and return.
    2. After they acknowledge the signed agreement, they send product information.
    3. If product information does not arrive, follow up with every approved supplier and state your launch date. [L0972.007]
  • Once authorized as a retailer, repeat this cycle for as long as you keep adding brands:
    1. When a supplier agrees to work with you, immediately send the authorized retailer application form it requests.
    2. Once authorized, upload that supplier's products to your store.
    3. Return to your master supplier list and contact the next brand.
    4. Repeat getting approved, uploading products and moving to the next supplier. [L0938.006]

Product Data and First Uploads After Approval

  • (older, 2018) After approval, ask the supplier for its top three selling products and how many units of each it sells per month. The monthly unit count gives you a baseline for how much of that demand you can capture. [L1274.037]
  • (older, 2022) When a supplier approves you:
    1. Ask for the top three bestselling products and write them down, since these are the priority for early work and conversion optimization.
    2. Ask how many units of each sell per month.
    3. Upload the approved products, then move to the next supplier. [L0803.024]
  • If a supplier agreement requires you to upload the whole catalog, ask the supplier by email to confirm that you only plan to list selected products before you rely on that. [L1113.023]
  • Upload only the products you want. You do not need to upload everything. [L0253.024]
  • (older, 2021) If a supplier requires a product purchase or deposit for a product list, a common workaround is to ask them to upload the catalog first, then pre-purchase only the proven bestsellers. For custom products, you can offer to forward each order to the supplier once it arrives. [L0801.027]

Agreements and Terms to Check

  • Supplier agreements are usually short and cover payment, business information, shipping method and the price policy (MAP). Review them carefully. (older, 2020) A lawyer review was not needed for typical supplier agreements in the lesson's experience. [L0468.051]
  • Supplier agreements usually cover the MAP policy, brand representation terms, the contact details for both sides, payment terms and the return policy. (older, 2021) Use a signed agreement from a supplier you already work with as a template when a new supplier asks what terms you propose. [L1112.045]
  • The authorized retailer agreement you sign with a brand contains its MAP policy and return policy. Read these terms before selling, because they decide whether discounts and returns are allowed. [L0667.058]
  • (older, 2021) A supplier can require approval before you offer discount codes. When signing a MAP agreement, check whether the supplier prohibits discount codes. This is rare, around 5% of cases, and if it applies, do not offer discounts on that brand. [L1090.033]
  • (older, 2022) Supplier agreements can require a supplier to contain the order process. Read each agreement and document how orders are processed, by email or through a dealer portal, so the process can later be handed to a team. [L0856.055]
  • Supplier agreements typically describe how orders are processed, by email or through a dealer portal. Read each supplier's agreement and document its order process, so the process can later be handed to a team. [L0856.055]
  • (older, 2021) If a supplier's application or contract contradicts itself, for example about who arranges freight, call them before submitting and ask which term applies. A professional supplier should be able to clarify the wording and send a corrected version. [L0853.115]
  • If a supplier has no standard terms for online retailers, define them yourself:
    1. Expect that the supplier has no ready dropship terms and will need to be guided.
    2. Take the terms from your existing supplier agreements. These cover the MAP pricing formula (for example 2x or 2.5x wholesale), who ships and bills the order, and the length of the return window.
    3. Copy these terms into a Word or Google document and edit them to fit the new supplier.
    4. Save the document as a PDF and send it to the supplier, stating that these are the terms you are looking for. [L0626.006]
  • (older, 2022) If a supplier has no drop shipping agreement, take the clearest existing supplier agreement from another brand and adapt it into your own template. [L1113.024]
  • (older, 2021) When a supplier asks you for a contract, it often means they have little experience with online retailers. A contract for this purpose can fit on one page and list the basic terms as bullet points. [L0968.005]
  • If a supplier has no dropship agreement, create a simple one-page agreement in Google Docs. Include how to place orders, the return policy, who to contact and any warranties. Base it on an agreement from another supplier, then save it as a PDF. Suppliers rarely lack such policies, so this should be uncommon. [L0666.030]

  • (older, 2022) Supplier agreements typically forbid selling their products on marketplaces such as eBay and Amazon. Check the agreement before listing a supplier product there. [L0803.030]

Payment on Card File and Prepay

  • Most supplier relationships run on a credit card kept on file: the supplier charges it when an order is sent, so the item ships right away. A debit card on file can work too. [L0970.044]
  • Many suppliers keep the operator's card on file so an order can be charged and shipped right away when it is submitted. The card is used for orders the operator places with that supplier and is part of the supplier's online vendor account setup. [L1114.016]
  • When a supplier approves an online vendor account, it may ask for a card on file, a preferred courier and the store's account number so orders can be charged and shipped quickly. It may also send a spec sheet with dimensions. Use the supplier's own data file where it is usable, and fill in the missing details yourself before uploading. [L1114.069]
  • (older, 2022) Standard supplier approval paperwork often includes a credit card authorization form with the card number and a signature, so the supplier can process orders quickly. Some sellers also send a card photo for verification. [L1113.021]
  • Providing credit card details to approved suppliers is standard practice for pre-pay arrangements. Suppliers charge the card to fulfill orders after customer purchases. [L0270.017]
  • (older, 2021) To pay suppliers:
    1. Keep your company credit card on file with the supplier.
    2. Send the order. The supplier charges the card and ships the product.
    3. You receive the tracking number and invoice by email. Some suppliers instead require payment through a dealer portal on their website. [L0247.027]
  • (older, 2020) Keep a credit card on file with suppliers so they can charge it immediately when you submit an order, rather than asking for payment each time. Pay the card off the same or next day, and ask the card company to raise the limit as sales grow. [L0852.030]
  • (older, 2020) A supplier can be paid per order by debit card or PayPal if no card is kept on file. A stored card lets the supplier charge immediately and dispatch sooner, so it is the faster option. [L0852.120]
  • (older, 2020) Products from a supplier can be paid with a debit card, as suppliers generally accept it. [L1111.052]
  • (older, 2021) Do not ask suppliers for credit terms. Instead, leave a credit or debit card on file that they charge when each order is submitted. [L0834.015]
  • (2023) Keep all purchases on your card-on-file with a supplier as prepaid orders. When describing this to a supplier, use the term "prepay for orders", because you pay before they ship. [L0835.013]
  • (2023) Prepay wording for a supplier application that asks for credit terms: write "prepay terms" over the request. Because the store pays the supplier before it ships, credit references are not needed. [L0964.037]
  • (2021) Credit-application forms from suppliers often ask for credit and trade references. You can cross those sections out, mark the application as wanting prepaid terms, and send it back. [L1279.031]
  • (undated) Suppliers may offer net payment terms such as 30, 60 or 90 days, which means they extend credit and ship inventory before being paid. Asking for prepay instead means you pay by card for each order before the supplier ships it. This takes the credit risk away from them and speeds up approval. [L0933.022]
  • (undated) Prepay wording to use with a supplier:
We are not looking for any credit terms. We would like to prepay: we will keep our credit card on file, and when we send you an order, you can charge our card and ship the order to our customer. [If you have no credit yet: We will start with a debit card on file and switch to a credit card once our credit has built up.]

[L0933.023]

  • (undated) With prepay terms, the supplier does not ship any item until you have confirmed a specific customer order and told them where to send it. The supplier never carries the cost of inventory you have not sold, which is why prepay makes approval easier. [L0933.033]
  • (2023) Some suppliers accept bank transfers, but many prefer payment by credit or debit card for orders. A business card is therefore the more practical way to pay suppliers, even though transfers work with some of them. [L1405.038]
  • (older, 2019) Avoid credit terms. If an application asks for business credit and bank references, prepay orders instead, so that you can pay with a credit card and collect card rewards. [L0960.031]
  • (2023) A supplier that asks for bank information or references is usually checking your credit for credit terms. An alternative is to prepay each order by keeping a card on file that is charged before the order ships. [L0855.057]
  • (2023) Payment timing differs per supplier. Most suppliers charge the card when the order is placed, but confirm this with each supplier before placing orders. [L0664.022]
  • (2023) Most suppliers charge the card when an order is sent, so the item ships right away. Confirm the terms before the first order with each supplier. [L0970.044, L0664.022]

Credit Limits and Building Credit

  • (older, 2020) Minimum credit card limit: $1,000 covers a typical first order (COGS $500 + shipping $100). A debit card is an acceptable alternative. [L0268.007]
  • (older, 2020) To grow the limit:
    1. Pay off the credit card balance daily after orders go through.
    2. Call the card issuer after the first few weeks and request a limit increase based on the demonstrated daily payoff history.
    3. Repeat monthly or bimonthly. Limit increases are granted based on usage pattern, not waiting period. [L0268.008]
  • (older, 2023) Build credit limit by paying off the supplier card every day. This keeps it near its limit while showing the bank consistent use. Then ask the bank for limit increases, since the payment history supports larger limits over time. [L0970.046]
  • (older, 2020) A new seller does not need a high credit card limit at the start. Limits can grow over time as you repay the card each day and request increases from the bank. [L0852.031]
  • (older, 2021) Build credit in stages: low limit, then increased limit over time, then a secured credit card backed by a $5,000+ deposit. [L0270.064]

Deposits, Stock and Pre-Purchase

  • (older, 2020) Never pay a deposit to a supplier before a customer order exists, so the cash outlay is zero until sales come in. Refuse requests for large credit card deposits, such as $4,000, because they are uncommon and risky before you have a relationship with the supplier. [L0852.029]
  • (older, 2021) Only pay a supplier deposit or opening order from profits your store has already earned with other suppliers. Do not pay a deposit or stock inventory before you know the best sellers. Commit to larger inventory only when the opportunity is clearly massive. [L0801.026]
  • (older, 2020) Never pay a supplier upfront or buy inventory in advance to start a relationship. Propose a 30-day trial run of listing their products instead. [L1277.031]
  • (older, 2018) Do not send stock to a fulfillment center to meet a supplier minimum or to handle a first order. It is not necessary in this situation. [L1109.004]
  • (older, 2021) If a supplier requires a product purchase or deposit for a product list, ask them to upload the catalog first, then pre-purchase only the proven bestsellers. [L0801.027]

Minimum Orders

Principles

  • (2023) Minimum purchase requirements from suppliers are not recommended for most people. Only consider them if you have proven niche knowledge, existing cash flow and high confidence in sales velocity. New sellers should avoid them. [L0270.035]
  • (older, 2021) Handle a supplier minimum purchase requirement by:
    1. Requesting that the supplier split the order into multiple charges if your credit card limit is low.
    2. Providing a debit card associated with your business checking account.
    3. Avoiding the supplier if you are new to business. [L0270.064]
  • (older, 2019) If a supplier's minimum order roughly equals the MAP of one product, list the products and place the first order only after a customer buys. If the minimum is larger, ask the supplier to let you test the products on your site for one month without the minimum commitment. [L0961.026]
  • (older, 2021) When a new account is held pending a first order of a set amount, check the terms and conditions for how the requirement is worded. If the supplier has already sent product information and images, upload the products and place the order. [L0853.031]

Email template for a new account held pending a first order:

Hi [Supplier contact name],

I saw your email about the [amount] minimum first order. We are happy to do business with you and send you many orders, but we are online-only and do not yet know which products will sell best. Could you send us your product information, product images, price sheet and descriptions? We will upload them, drive traffic to them, and expect orders within our first month.

Thank you,
[Your name]
[Store name]

Exclusivity and Territory

  • (undated) Ask a supplier for exclusivity for a new product in one region for a limited time, such as six months, before testing it. This is typically only possible with a close relationship with the supplier. [L0334.043]
  • (older, 2020) Exclusive distribution offers are rare. Before accepting, ask why the supplier wants exclusivity and whether they expect your store to sell only their products. Then weigh the upsides against the downsides, such as obligations that limit how you promote other brands. [L0358.048]
  • (older, 2021) An exclusive retailer arrangement is rare and is usually not needed. It becomes realistic only when you already generate most of a supplier's revenue. A partnership with equity or payouts over time may then let you acquire part or all of the company. [L0469.114]
  • (older, 2019) Exclusive selling rights for a product in a niche are usually granted only after a very strong relationship and a high sales volume with the supplier. They are rarely possible from day one. [L1068.036]
  • (older, 2021-22) Confirm that you hold an exclusive distribution deal for a product in your market before promoting it. Exclusivity means you are the only retailer selling it locally, which lets you own its brand search traffic. [L0949.007]
  • (older, 2022) For an exclusive supplier, maximize sales while the exclusivity lasts. Forecast unit sales and stay in contact so the supplier keeps stock full. [L0963.091]
  • (2023) Suppliers rarely require you to list all their products or specific ones. A rare exception is a multi-brand supplier that reserves some brands for physical stores. [L0934.027]
  • (2024) Some suppliers will not revoke approval or ask you to remove products due to low sales volume, even if the products do not convert. This is standard in dropshipping relationships. [L0253.022, L0253.055]

Conditions on Trade Accounts

  • (2025) Some supplier trade accounts require verified dealer status before you can buy, and some brands set conditions on your sales. One brand allowed sales only within 100 miles of your address, and another required a guaranteed 50,000 dollars in sales per year. Check these conditions before you plan your sales territory, budget and outreach. [L0857.067]
  • (2024) Trade program links on supplier websites usually mean the same as becoming an authorized dealer. Open the trade program page to confirm the terms. [L0856.049]

Tax Documents and Sales Tax

Forms and Requests

  • (2025) The W9 is a standard supplier requirement. It is an IRS form that shows your business entity information (LLC, sole proprietor, S corporation, etc.). [L0255.005]
  • (2025) W9 steps:
    1. The supplier sends a W9 form request.
    2. Complete the W9 form with your business entity details.
    3. Send the completed W9 and contact person information to the supplier.
    4. Provide an email address and phone number as the contact person.
    5. The supplier responds with a price sheet containing product information, wholesale pricing, MAP and MSRP. [L0255.006]
  • (2023) When a supplier asks for a state resale certificate, send the certificate for your home state and explain that you do not have economic nexus in the state the supplier is asking about. Some suppliers may also request a W-9 to confirm you are a business reselling the products. [L0835.043]

Email template for a resale certificate request:

Hi [Supplier contact name], our business is based in [Home state], and we have attached our [Home state] resale certificate. We do not currently have an economic nexus in [State the supplier asked about], so please let us know how you would handle orders shipping to that state.
[Your name]
[Company name]

[L0835.044]

  • (2023) For a supplier that needs a resale or sales tax certificate:
    1. Search Google for the state name plus "reseller certificate" (or "sales tax certificate").
    2. Use the official.gov site and apply online.
    3. Send the resulting document to the supplier. [L0964.033]
  • (2023) Expect a supplier to ask for a sales certificate, and be ready to provide buyer details when they request it. [L0964.034]
  • (2024/2025) If a supplier that requires a reseller permit or tax ID is for a Canadian or non-US seller, search the community for members who have done this rather than relying on general advice. An EIN is not always necessary but should be checked. [L0665.023]

Sales Tax Charged by Suppliers

Two positions apply to different cases:

  • (2025) Supplier agreements can require the reseller to pay sales tax. Accept this as a cost of credibility when a supplier requests it. [L0472.028]
  • (2023) If a supplier charges sales tax because it has no resale certificate on file, provide the resale certificate or W-9 it asks for. Do not accept the charge until the supplier has confirmed it has the documents it needs. [L0835.045]

Insurance

  • (older, 2022) If a supplier contract requires insurance, obtain it. A general liability policy from a large company with fixed rates is the recommended type. [L0963.085]
  • (older, 2022) Case: a policy sold through a broker with a low premium led to an insurance audit after revenue grew. The business owed more than 10,000 USD extra. Check that the rates are fixed before buying a policy. [L0963.086]

EDI, FTP, Portals and Order Transmission

  • (2025) Suppliers that use EDI or FTP run enterprise-style order integrations, and the Shopify apps for them are expensive. If a supplier asks for EDI, ask for email or an online ordering portal to submit orders instead. [L0667.055]
  • (2023) EDI is a system for connecting directly with a supplier's inventory and order systems. Shopify apps that do this are expensive because developers must configure them for each supplier system. The answer is usually no. Ask for an alternative method of ordering and inventory checks. [L0835.040]
  • (older, 2021) If a supplier requires EDI integration over SPS and your business does not use EDI, say plainly that you are not set up for it. Then ask whether they will still work with you, since EDI may be a preference rather than a hard requirement. [L1112.080]
  • (older, 2020) Most suppliers do not need EDI. Around 95 percent of orders are sent to suppliers as automatic purchase orders by email, which is enough for most brands. [L1277.038]
  • (older, 2019) Automated electronic order transmission (EDI) to suppliers was not used in 2019. Some suppliers offered it, but for a small operation it was judged not worth building on Shopify. [L1002.077]
  • (2023) Most suppliers do not accept orders through a portal. Typically the supplier agreement, usually a one- to two-page document, describes how to place orders, most often by email to an orders address. [L1283.004]
  • (undated) When a supplier asks for a physical document or signature, you can handle it remotely:
    1. Forward their mail to a mailbox in your home country.
    2. Have it scanned to a PDF and emailed to you.
    3. Sign the PDF digitally and upload it to the mailbox service, which prints it and posts it to the supplier. [L1441.015]

Marketplace and Channel Restrictions

Marketplaces

  • (undated) Some suppliers forbid selling their products on third-party marketplaces such as Amazon, eBay or Walmart. This is common and can be ignored for the store itself, since no marketplace seller account is needed to follow the store setup. [L0360.010]
  • (2024) Do not sell supplier products on third-party marketplaces such as Amazon or eBay, because most suppliers only authorize sales on the retailer's own store. [L1115.016]
  • (2025) Most suppliers do not allow resale on third-party marketplaces such as Amazon or Walmart and expect sales only on your own website. The permitted sales channels are defined in the supplier agreement. [L0966.025]
  • (undated) Most authorized-retailer supplier contracts forbid selling on third-party marketplaces such as eBay, Amazon and Walmart. True competitors are therefore online-only stores that sell the same brands, not marketplaces. [L0941.025]

Channel Permission and Ads

  • (undated) Before listing a supplier's products on another sales channel, get the supplier's permission, because some suppliers may not be comfortable with it. Confirm this in writing or in the account agreement before you expand. [L1441.011]
  • (older, 2020) Some suppliers forbid paid ads using their brand name, including Google Ads. In that case, do not run paid campaigns on the brand. Rely on organic search and Bing traffic. [L1277.044]

Accessories and Flat-Rate Shipping

  • (2023) Supplier distributor and channel rules apply. If a supplier prohibits selling accessories, or offers a flat-rate shipping cost that causes losses on accessories, sell accessories only as add-ons to a main product. [L1283.019]

Trade Accounts, Affiliate Programs and Private-Supplier Setups

  • (2023) Do not affiliate with a supplier's affiliate program. If a supplier offers an affiliate sign-up, ask instead whether you can become a retailer. [L0964.026]
  • (older, 2019) Working as an authorized retailer means the supplier ships without a supplier invoice, and the customer may see the shipping origin. This is normal for brand retailers and does not cause customer issues. [L0467.032]
  • (older, 2019) Buying products from third-party resellers, such as eBay sellers, and shipping them with their invoice causes problems. Authorized retailer status avoids this, because the supplier never includes an invoice. [L0467.033]
  • (2024) Get approved with both distributors if a supplier is in stock on only one distributor and both are offered. Pricing and stock levels may differ, and approval costs nothing. [L0665.019]
  • (2025) The authorized retailer agreement and its MAP and return terms apply to every sale of that brand. [L0667.058]
  • (older, 2023) Some suppliers: the affiliate, trade program and dealer routes all lead to the same outcome, so confirm which one you are on before you start. [L0856.049]
  • (2024) Trade program links on supplier websites usually mean the same as becoming an authorized dealer. [L0856.049]
  • (2024) Gold and private-supplier setups: Gold suppliers may reject a new store or ask to re-review it. [L0969.007]
  • (2020, sole proprietor to LLC) Use this procedure when a sole proprietorship converts to an LLC:
    1. Fill out new vendor forms with the new business information and EIN.
    2. Email the same contact who received the original forms.
    3. Ask the supplier to update the business information on file to the new company name. [L0967.035]
  • (2024) Resources such as the LLC setup document are shared in the supplier community channel and may require an LLC or UEN number to access. If you cannot get one, ask the community for help, and the supplier can arrange it. [L1167.023]

Business Entity, Address and Account Setup

  • (2024) To list a supplier with your business address, use the address from your Shopify settings:
    1. Contact the supplier as normal using your email and phone scripts.
    2. In Shopify settings, provide your official business address (for example, a Canadian address for a Canadian business).
    3. In the supplier approval forms, list your business address as it appears in your Shopify settings.
    4. The supplier will send its full product catalog regardless of how many products you mentioned.
    5. Upload only the products you want.
    6. This approach is standard and fully accepted by suppliers. [L0253.024]
  • (2020) Ask in the initial paperwork whether the supplier ships on your account or theirs. If it is not stated, ask after approval and before orders start. [L1111.029]
  • (undated) Some supplier approvals and documents can be handled remotely through a mailbox service, as described under EDI and portals. [L1441.015]
  • (2025) Tax documents follow the entity type on the W9, so match the entity you register with the entity on the form. [L0255.005]

Red Lines: What to Refuse

  • (undated) If a supplier asks you to co-brand a website, to run their official site with your store content, or to take over advertising for their brand in exchange for an arrangement that sounds unusual, decline politely. Keep the current arrangement and do not agree to unclear terms. [L0941.023]
  • (older, 2021) Reject any brand that asks for an extra dropshipping fee on top of its wholesale price. Argue that the store brings new traffic, market reach and sales, so the brand gains without paying more. [L0962.152]
  • (older, 2021) If a new supplier asks the store to handle foreign certification, product liability or export logistics, decline unless the relationship is long-term and the extra work is worth the effort. Build such services only for established partners who rely on the store for most of their sales. [L0962.153]
  • (older, 2020) Never pay a supplier upfront to start a relationship. Refuse large credit card deposits, such as $4,000. [L0852.029, L1277.031]
  • (2025) Avoid accepting a large initial minimum order, such as 2,500 USD, just to get listed. [L0966.023]
  • (2021) Do not pay for a deposit or stock before a customer order. [L0270.035]
  • (undated) Never guarantee a minimum sales volume. [L0933.013]

Further details

  1. When the supplier responds with interest, they will email application forms.
  2. These forms request standard business information: business name, address, phone number, and billing information.
  3. Complete all sections of the forms completely and accurately immediately upon receipt.
  4. Submit the completed forms back as quickly as possible-do not delay.
  5. Maintaining momentum is critical; delayed submissions result in significantly lower approval rates.
  6. An account manager will respond within a few days with approval, account number and contact details, or a request for additional information.
  7. If additional information is requested, respond immediately with what's needed. [L0133.017]
  • Before sending ID to a supplier for a credit card verification, confirm the company is legitimate by finding its products on other retailers' websites. A real brand's products normally appear in several places online. [L0468.189]
