3.8 Approaching suppliers: scripts, calls and positioning

Why Suppliers Approve Retailers

  • Suppliers evaluate every potential retailer on three traits: a professional, functioning website already operating; a clear, defined strategy for generating sales; and demonstrated exceptional customer service. [L0133.004]
  • Show the three capabilities: a quality website that represents their brand well, a clear strategy for generating additional sales through your channel, and exceptional customer service that protects their reputation. [L0121.004]
  • Approval is not guaranteed, regardless of website quality, traffic level or business experience. Not every supplier will approve you even with professional preparation. Success depends on the right communication strategy, not external credentials alone. [L0133.001]
  • Suppliers accept a new retailer only when they believe it is a real, qualified business that will bring extra sales to their brand. Your job in the first contact is to make that belief clear. [L0927.002]
  • Suppliers want retailers who prove themselves before approval, because a retailer represents their brand. They expect the retailer to honor policies such as MAP and to support customers after the sale. [L1094.008]
  • Suppliers approve retailers they expect to care about customer service, run a lasting business and bring traffic that adds value to their brand. Show that intention in the approach call and in the store. [L0806.017]
  • Suppliers generally want to work with new stores that are excited about their products. Clear enthusiasm and specific requests are usually well received. [L0804.037]
  • Suppliers sell business to business, to physical and online retailers, so an online store is simply another retail customer for them. Approval is usually easy because you offer a sales channel that fits what they already do. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0458.039]
  • High-ticket suppliers usually do not sell to the public. They earn from selling to retailers, so they wait for serious businesses that will manage sales to customers. The approval call is a request for a partnership, not a favour. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0771.029]
  • The products already exist on the market and demand already exists. The store does not need to create demand; it taps into existing demand for these products. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0771.030]
  • Suppliers evaluate outreach from their side and accept a retailer only when they believe it is a real, qualified business. [L0927.002]
  • Suppliers often ask the same questions when reviewing an approval request. Prepare answers in advance so the first conversation is not stalled. [L0590.006]
  • Dropshipping brands usually require approval before a store may sell their products. Copying a product list from the web and listing it directly is not a valid route. Plan a contact and approval step for every supplier. [L0590.001]
  • Suppliers are approached directly, and the retailer becomes an authorized retailer through approval, not by paying for supplier access or joining supplier directories. [L0506.007]
  • Ready-made phone and email scripts are used to ask suppliers for approval as a retailer, so that approval is obtained without spending money. [L0506.008]
  • Contacting suppliers and getting authorized to sell their products costs nothing. The first real spending starts with ads, so supplier approval should be requested before any budget is committed to traffic. [L0360.004]

Positioning: How You Come Across

  • Establish yourself as an attractive opportunity they would regret losing, not as a retailer who needs them to succeed. Make them wish they had accepted you sooner. [L0133.002]
  • Sounding desperate, or signaling that you need the supplier to succeed, damages your position. They should believe they would be making a mistake by passing on you, not that you depend on them. [L0133.014]
  • Position the relationship as the start of a long-term partnership, not a transaction. Suppliers want retailers who grow with them and generate reliable repeat revenue over time, not one-off buyers. [L0133.006]
  • The quality and content of each supplier conversation matter far more than call frequency. Make each call count rather than contacting suppliers repeatedly. [L0133.003]
  • Suppliers receive many calls about the same products, so they ask what sets you apart. Show that your business is long term, that customer service is your priority, and that you understand their brand and why it fits your store, rather than only asking to list their products. [L0933.026]
  • Approach suppliers owner to owner, because suppliers need sellers who promote their products. Frame your outreach as giving them an opportunity to be sold through your store. [L0832.031]
  • Describing your launch plan works best when you have actually prepared it. Position yourself as someone about to take a market share, and show that you understand the subject well enough to be believed. [L0927.008]
  • Do not call a supplier with a generic request, such as asking whether you can drop ship their products. Research their brand first and explain specifically why your store is a good fit for them. [L0933.028]
  • Suppliers judge retail partners by whether they will last. Show that you treat the relationship as lasting, not as a quick resale opportunity. [L0933.026]
  • Some suppliers want to see your marketing plan before they approve you as a retailer. Before calling, prepare a short outline of how you will sell their products, such as through Google Shopping ads and retargeting. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0771.065]
  • Nervousness about calling is normal, since most suppliers are professional and welcome the contact. The discomfort decreases with practice, so use it as energy rather than trying to eliminate it before calling. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0235.012]
  • If you feel nervous, start with smaller, less visible suppliers to build confidence and practice the pitch. Approach the larger brands after you are comfortable with the conversation and have a few approvals behind you. [L0941.106]

Phone First

  • Always call suppliers first rather than emailing. Phone contact is the most efficient way to reach the right decision maker (the eCommerce account person) on the first attempt. [L0133.007]
  • Call every supplier with a phone number first. Use email or an online form as a follow-up or fallback. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0771.027]
  • Contact suppliers by phone first rather than email, because phone calls achieve higher success rates and lead to stronger business relationships. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0235.001]
  • Email is unreliable for initial supplier contact. Many recipients never read emails: inboxes get cluttered, unfamiliar senders are often ignored, and mailboxes reach capacity limits, so your opportunity depends on chance. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0235.003]
  • Contact suppliers by phone when a phone number is listed, because phone contact gets better results than email. Use email only when no phone number is available. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0962.014]
  • Calling suppliers gets better responses than emails. Emails are easily ignored, and a phone call creates a direct connection and a sense of urgency. (2023.) [L0970.030]
  • Phone calls are the recommended way to reach suppliers. Once you reach the right person and describe what you want, the application usually takes 5 to 10 minutes to complete, and approval often follows right after it is returned. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1086.031]
  • Call suppliers instead of sending a long letter, because letters are easy to set aside or miss. A phone call reaches the right person quickly and gives a faster answer on approval. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0960.026]
  • Reseller approval requests to brand suppliers can be sent by phone, by email, or through the supplier's online application form. The first contact states that you want to sell their products through your business and asks for their approval. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1060.009]
  • Call suppliers rather than submitting only the trade or dealer intake forms. A supplier listing a trade or designer program does not stop you from getting approved, since those programs mainly serve larger corporate or B2B buyers. [L0941.019]
  • Phone supplier contacts early in the morning, around 8:00 to 8:30 AM in the supplier's time zone, before their workday fills up. Choose the suppliers you call by time zone so the morning call makes sense for each. (2023.) [L0835.017]
  • Calling suppliers early in the morning, around 8:30 to 9:00 AM, can get you through to decision makers before their workload builds up. If responses are poor at other times, test this window for a few days. (2023.) [L0970.031]
  • Before calling, check the business or retail contact hours on the supplier's contact page, which may be listed in a specific time zone, and schedule calls within that window. Some manufacturers run a separate business line from their consumer support number. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0937.032]
  • Whether a supplier needs a personal meeting is rare, under 1% of supplier interactions. When a supplier wants to fly in, politely decline and suggest meeting at a trade show or local event instead, unless they are already nearby. (2023.) [L0964.019]

Preparing Before You Call

Research the supplier

  • Research each supplier's website and company positioning before calling to identify specific connection points. Reference these findings in the pitch to show genuine interest in the brand, not generic outreach to every supplier in the category. [L0133.005]
  • Research each supplier before calling, as you would prepare for a job interview. Suppliers approve retailers who show they understand and value the supplier's brand, and this improves approval rates. [L0928.007]
  • Before a supplier call, learn its story: read its About and home pages, then open the conversation by saying what you like about the company and why you want to sell its products. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0962.015]
  • Pitch with something specific from their website, such as better materials or a virtual showroom, and explain how it fits your customers' expectations. Generic praise weakens the approval chance. [L0928.009]
  • Evaluate the supplier before calling: its company background, history, products and how it sells. [L0927.004]

Supplier research method (2024 version, as written):

  1. Google the brand or product category. [L0806.015]
  2. Check that the brand sells through online stores and is not a dropshipper. [L0806.015]
  3. Open the brand's About us page. [L0806.015]
  4. Spend about three to five minutes reading what the brand is proud of and wants customers to know. [L0806.015]
  5. Use those points in the supplier conversation, for example customer service focus, long history of the brand, or shared location. [L0806.015]

Supplier research method (undated version, 5 to 10 minutes per supplier):

  1. Open the supplier's website from your research worksheet. [L0928.008]
  2. Read the About Us section and homepage to find what the brand says about itself, such as its philosophy, materials, quality or differentiators. [L0928.008]
  3. Check for extra content such as a virtual showroom or 360-degree tour, and browse it. [L0928.008]
  4. Note what stands out and what the brand wants customers to know. [L0928.008]
  5. Plan to reference one or two of these points on the call and explain why this brand is a better fit than other suppliers. Spend about 5 to 10 minutes per supplier. [L0928.008]

Both research methods are current. Use the 3-5 minute reading of the About page (2024) for a quick first pass, and the full 5-10 minute method when you prepare a supplier you plan to call. [L0806.015, L0928.008]

Know what you bring

  • Know what you bring to the table: your store, your audience, your marketing channels and your sales plan. [L0927.004]
  • Prepare answers to likely questions so you do not depend on improvising under pressure. [L0927.004]

Prepare the list

  • Open your completed master supplier list. Make sure each supplier has a URL, phone number and email. [L0928.001]
  • Have at least 20 suppliers researched and filled in before starting supplier calls. Work in batches, for example researching 20 suppliers one day and calling them the next. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1068.027]
  • Batch order: 1. Research the contact information for the first 20 suppliers. 2. On the next day, call those 20 suppliers. 3. On the following day, research the next 20 suppliers. 4. Repeat the cycle. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1068.028]
  • Calling one supplier and then researching the next one slows the process down. Expect voicemails and callbacks, so a prepared list should be ready before calling begins. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1068.029]
  • The scripts, emails and day-by-day timing in the supplier contact plan are starting points you can edit to fit your store and wording. Keep the overall order of calls and follow-ups intact, because the sequence is built to keep outreach going until a supplier gives a definite answer. [L0936.020]
  • Prepare for the most likely supplier questions in advance. (see L0590.006 above)

Order and Pacing of Outreach

  • Contact suppliers you expect to approve you first. Reach out to top-tier suppliers only after your business information is complete. Keep adding suppliers over time rather than waiting for every approval before uploading products. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0968.004]
  • Start at the top of the supplier list. Once approved, sign the agreement, upload the products, then move on to the next supplier. Have products from the first two approved brands live with traffic before you proceed. (2023.) [L0804.026]
  • Silver suppliers come first: they are the majority of the list, and gold suppliers are left for later. Call each supplier, even when its website has a dealer form. Introduce yourself and your store, and say you are about to submit the form. When a dealer application arrives, fill it out and return it immediately. Stop contacting new suppliers while several applications are pending, so each is completed and approved before you move on. Once all silver suppliers are handled, contact the gold suppliers. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0801.020]
  • Order in a niche with many suppliers (older advice, 2021):
    1. On competitor category pages, note which suppliers' products appear first, since these pages sort by best-selling products. [L1090.143]
    2. Do not contact the top-ranked suppliers first. [L1090.143]
    3. Contact about 10 suppliers that rank lower but still carry products in the niche. [L1090.143]
    4. Get the first 5 to 10 approved and build comfort with the approval process. [L1090.143]
    5. Return to the top-ranked suppliers and seek approval from them. [L1090.143]
    6. Contact all remaining suppliers. [L1090.143]
  • Credibility approach when a brand accepts a new reseller (2025): become an authorized seller, upload the supplier's products to the store, price them above the supplier's minimum price if margins are thin, and exclude them from paid campaigns. They can still be found through organic search and free Google Shopping listings, while paid ads go to proven sellers. Then reach out to more suppliers and mention the existing brand. [L0472.024]

Call Sequence and the First Conversation

Goal of the first call

  • The goal of the first contact is only to reach the decision maker, not to close the deal. Keep the early conversation short and focused on introduction, your planned launch and your reasons for choosing them. [L0928.004]
  • Keep calls short, maintain professionalism and focus on clarity and confidence. [L0126.004]

Full call procedure (17 steps)

  1. Research the supplier's website and company positioning to identify why their products fit your niche. [L0133.015]
  2. Find the supplier's phone number via Google search or directly on their website, looking for a toll-free number. [L0133.015]
  3. Call and ask for the person in charge of eCommerce accounts. If unavailable, request their email and/or direct phone number for a callback. [L0133.015]
  4. When you reach the eCommerce decision maker, begin by briefly explaining that you own an eCommerce store focused on their niche and want to add quality suppliers to your catalog. [L0133.015]
  5. Give a brief overview of your company without referring to any other websites you operate. [L0133.015]
  6. Explain that your team is finishing a major project in their niche that you are about to launch. [L0133.015]
  7. Share your excitement, noting that your team has extensively researched the niche and is confident you can dominate it with your marketing approach. [L0133.015]
  8. State that you are building out your catalog before launching a major marketing push on [specific date: 1-2 months from today]. [L0133.015]
  9. Reference specific findings from their website that show why their products are a perfect fit for your customers. [L0133.015]
  10. Express confidence that your company will become one of their top retailers within weeks of launching. [L0133.015]
  11. Emphasize building a long-term relationship, similar to partnerships you have developed with other suppliers in other niches. [L0133.015]
  12. Clearly state you are not looking for special payment terms, only a solid business relationship. [L0133.015]
  13. Ask directly how to apply for an eCommerce account. [L0133.015]
  14. If they say no or that they are not taking new retailers, explain how it is a shame they will miss out on the significant launch you are planning. [L0133.015]
  15. Ask who specifically needs to be involved to make the partnership happen. [L0133.015]
  16. Even if declined, understand they will remember this call and who you are, which positions you for future contact. [L0133.015]
  17. Document the conversation completely: the person's name, the date and all details discussed. [L0133.015]

Eight-step workflow (decision maker focus)

  1. Open the master supplier list and make sure each supplier has a URL, phone number and email. [L0928.001]
  2. Call first whenever a phone number exists. Use email only when no phone is available. [L0928.001]
  3. On the call, introduce yourself and your store, state the planned launch and why you like their products, and ask how to apply for a supplier account. [L0928.001]
  4. Ask to be connected to the decision maker, who is the person who sends the approval form or grants access. [L0928.001]
  5. Speak with the decision maker, reference something specific you learned from their website, and ask for the dealer application form to be emailed. [L0928.001]
  6. As soon as they promise the form, give your email address, thank them and confirm you will return it right away. [L0928.001]
  7. Fill out and return the form the same day you receive it. [L0928.001]
  8. Log every contact in the tracking columns, then follow up on the agreed date. [L0928.001]
  • When a supplier agrees to send an approval or dealer application form by email, give them your email address during the call, thank them, and confirm you will send it back right away. Then check your inbox, fill out the form and return it immediately. [L0928.015]
  • Call the supplier and ask to reach the right decision maker during that same call. Ask them to email the approval form right after the call. If a promised callback or email does not arrive within a few days, follow up. Return the completed form as soon as possible. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0853.012]
  • Do not ask a supplier many detailed questions before they send their approval form, because many answers are in that form. Ask follow-up questions after the form is returned. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0853.013]
  • Trade account contact (2022): 1) Call and ask for the person in charge of opening new accounts. 2) If it is a small company, that person may answer directly; if larger, ask who handles it. 3) For email outreach, a short intro works: state you are interested in selling their products and ask who opens new accounts for internet retailers. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0969.012]
  • Getting a supplier approved by phone (older advice, 2021):
    1. Call the supplier rather than relying on email, since phone gives a faster answer. [L1090.023]
    2. Say you want to open an account as an online retailer and ask who handles new retailer accounts. [L1090.023]
    3. If nobody answers, ask how to email or whom to contact for a new account. [L1090.023]
    4. Follow up by email if no one calls back, because follow-up is part of the process. [L1090.023]
    5. If no phone number is listed, use supplier research tools to find other contact numbers. [L1090.023]
  • Calling a supplier to ask about dropshipping stores (undated):
    1. Call the supplier and ask whether they work with dropshipping stores. [L0495.002]
    2. If they agree, ask them to send you the supplier application, and give them your email address so they can send it. [L0495.002]
    3. Fill in the application and return it to the supplier as soon as possible. [L0495.002]
    4. When the supplier sends you their product information, upload their products to your store right away. [L0495.002]
    5. Repeat these steps with more suppliers until at least five suppliers are approved and all their products are live on your store. [L0495.002]
  • Trade account procedure (2020):
    1. Contact the brand by phone or email and ask for an account. [L0468.033]
    2. Ask which format they use for inventory updates and whether they can send a regular report. [L0468.033]
    3. Ask for the top three best-selling products with their weight and packaging size. [L0468.033]
    4. Record every answer, the reason for any rejection, and the date to follow up. [L0468.033]
    5. If the brand does not respond, follow up every few days. If it rejected you, follow up when the stated condition is met. [L0468.033]
  • Approval workflow (2023):
    1. Call the supplier first and ask for the name and direct email of the person responsible for retailer relationships, even if they say to email. [L1094.012]
    2. Send a short, direct email to that contact that mentions the phone call, the store link and a request for the next steps. [L1094.012]
    3. If there is no reply after a few days, reply to your own first email with a short follow-up. [L1094.012]
    4. Keep applying to more suppliers until you have at least two approvals. [L1094.012]
  • Outreach for approval (2023), in short form: call the supplier and ask for the name and direct email of the person who handles retailer relationships, even if they first say to email. Send a very short email to that contact that names the person you spoke with, includes the store link and asks for the next steps. If there is no reply after a few days, answer your own first email with a brief follow-up. Keep contacting more suppliers until at least two are approved before going live. [L1094.117]

Call scripts and rules

  • Use phone scripts as conversation frameworks rather than reading them verbatim. Before calling, review the script and prepare to speak naturally. [L0126.004]
  • Do not present your business as established. Keep to what is true, and never claim anything you do not have. [L0365.006] (See the honesty section below.)
  • Send Day 1 emails only if the supplier requests them. [L0126.005]
  • Do not bring up dropshipping, and do not say that you only want to resell their products (the opening pitch). [L0928.002]
  • Note the conflict: L0928.002 says not to bring up dropshipping in the pitch, while L0495.002 has you ask whether the supplier works with dropshipping stores. Use the wording of the first pitch from L0928.002. Ask the dropshipping question in the step of L0495.002 only if the supplier raises the subject or the step requires it. [L0928.002, L0495.002]

Opening script, phone (2023 version)

Hi, [name of person who answers], this is [your name] calling from [your company name]. We run a group of boutique e-commerce shops across several niches, and recently we spotted an opening in the [supplier's product niche] niche. We have just launched [store name, URL] and are looking for dependable suppliers who want to work with our shop. Based on our early research, my team and I believe our marketing will make this new venture our strongest yet. We are not asking for credit terms or anything similar; we simply want a sound working relationship. Could you tell me how we would go about applying for an account with [supplier name]? Once you have said this, stop and listen to their reply. Do they offer to help directly, or do they refer you to someone else? Write down the name of anyone they mention. Do not bring up dropshipping, and do not say that you only want to resell their products.

Presenting Your Business Honestly

  • Stay honest when contacting suppliers. There is no need to lie about who you are or what you have done to get a good supplier relationship. [L0365.006]
  • Supplier sourcing templates and Q&A forms are available in the sourcing section to structure supplier outreach. Use them to present your business accurately. [L0365.007]
  • Never claim a longer history than you have. If your store is not live yet, point to any earlier online or offline business experience you can honestly name. [L0933.008]
  • When a supplier asks how long you have been in business, the question is about whether they can trust you to stay around and treat their brand with respect. [L0933.008]
  • Established suppliers judge whether a new store will last mainly by its track record. A store that is not live yet should say so and point only to real earlier online or offline business experience, with no inflated history. [L0933.034]
  • Our store is a new venture, and we are launching it now as part of our launch strategy. [If you have prior experience: Before this, we ran [previous business, for example an eBay store or an offline business] for [number of years].] [L0933.009]

Using "I" and "we"

  • In outreach emails from a one-person store, writing "I" instead of "we" is acceptable, and a small mismatch like this is unlikely to end a supplier deal. Using the word "niche" in supplier communication is also fine. (2023.) [L0470.101]
  • When speaking to a supplier, using "we" to refer to your business is acceptable as long as you do not claim anything untrue. The "we" refers to your company, not to a false claim about size or history. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0960.027]

Expertise in a technical niche (older advice, 2020)

  1. Do not claim expertise you do not have. [L0967.100]
  2. Study the niche and its product types at a surface level, for example through a few hours of video covering the basics. [L0967.100]
  3. Tell the supplier honestly that you have a working knowledge and aim to become an expert. [L0967.100]
  4. State that you want a good working relationship so you can educate customers and build a resource section on the store. [L0967.100]

Demo Store, Launch Date and Existing Stores

Demo store

  • When contacting suppliers, always have a demo store ready and mention it during approval conversations. Show it as a living example of how their products will appear on your store. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0249.014]
  • Always inform a supplier in advance that your store is in demo mode. This frame prevents suppliers from misinterpreting a limited product catalog or demo placeholder items as evidence of an unfinished or fraudulent operation. [L0294.005]
  • Sample wording for demo mode: "My name is [Your Name], I've been building e-commerce stores for [your years of experience], please visit this site to see how your products would look. The store is launching on [specific launch date]." [L0294.005]
  • During supplier review you can share your store template with a stated launch date, so the supplier can see how their products would look on it. Do not present the unfinished store as an active shop with products for sale. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0834.120]
  • Do not start with cold calls to request authorization, even when a demo store already exists. First determine why a supplier would choose you, and set up legitimacy signals beforehand. [L1063.006]
  • Note the sources: L0249.014 says always have the demo store ready and mention it during approval conversations. L1063.006 says a demo store does not justify a cold call that asks for authorization. Use the demo store in the approval conversation, after you have established why the supplier should choose you. [L0249.014, L1063.006]

Existing stores

  • If you already have successful online stores (dropshipping or otherwise), mention this when contacting suppliers. It helps with approval regardless of whether the stores are in the same niche. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0249.015]
  • Never claim to have existing stores or sales if you do not have them. Describe only the store you are actually building. [L0928.003]

Launch date

  • When calling suppliers, specify a launch date 1-2 months in the future. Use an actual date you have set for your marketing launch, not a hypothetical timeline, so you can reference real results in follow-up calls and strengthen credibility. [L0133.010]
  • State a real planned launch date when speaking to a supplier, and keep it no more than two months in the future. A date further out makes the supplier treat you as not relevant now and ask you to come back later. [L0928.005]
  • When giving a supplier a launch date, a window of about three to four weeks is typical, though a fixed date such as January 1 can also be given. [L1094.015]
  • Describing your launch plan to a supplier works best when you have actually prepared it. [L0927.008]
  • Use the launch strategy, not previous store history, to build excitement. Reference the launch date to set expectations. Include scripts from module 4.6 or Drop Ship Weekly training. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0268.016]

Launch date: sources differ

Source Rule Ids
Default 1-2 months ahead, an actual date you have set L0133.010

Typical window About three to four weeks, or a fixed date such as January 1 L1094.015

Use the 1-2 month window as the default. Keep the date within two months, and use the 3-4 week window when you need a short, realistic timeline.

  • Use the launch date in the supplier pitch to ground the conversation. [L0133.010, L0928.005]

Traffic and Sales Questions

  • During a launch, if you have no traffic yet, tell suppliers you plan to send hundreds of qualified leads to your website every day. Do not present current traffic numbers you do not have. [L0933.010]
  • Suppliers want partners who add value, mainly through orders. Projected sales are essentially a guess, so do not invent a sales forecast. State a baseline instead: a brand must be able to reach at least 10,000 USD per month in sales through your store, otherwise it is not a good fit. [L0933.011]

Template: traffic and baseline sales answer

Right now we do not have traffic yet, but we are planning to send hundreds of qualified leads to our website every single day. I do not want to make up a sales number. What I can tell you is that if I cannot get you at least 10,000 USD a month in sales, I do not think it would be a good fit for us. So our baseline goal is at least 10,000 USD a month in sales for your brand on [your store domain].

[L0933.012]

  • For traffic, use the statement from L0933.010 and avoid any figure you cannot support. [L0933.010, L0933.012]

Template: sales revenue and credit references (2023)

I'm not comfortable sharing specific revenue numbers. What I can tell you is we're getting X unique visitors each month and seeing profitable conversions with our existing supplier partners X, Y, and Z. I'm confident we can do the same with your products.

[L0274.063]

Warehouse and Stock Questions

  • Suppliers ask about your warehouse to learn whether you are an online-only retailer who needs their products stocked and shipped by them. Your answer should explain your focus, not present the lack of a warehouse as a gap. [L0933.001]
  • Do not answer the warehouse question by saying you are new and may have a warehouse one day. That sounds like a weakness and invites doubt, so state it as a deliberate business focus. [L0933.003]

Template: warehouse answer

That is not something we do. Our focus is on marketing, customer service and retail. We are an online retailer and would be a non-stocking dealer, which means you keep the products in stock and ship orders for us. Our focus is on being the best online retailer, bringing awareness to brands like yours, fulfilling orders and providing exceptional customer service. So we do not handle warehousing, and that is by design.

[L0933.002]

Template: how the model works (2022)

Here is how we operate. Each week our suppliers send us an inventory report listing what they hold in their warehouse, and we add their products to our store based on what is available right now. We pay for ads that promote these products. When a customer places an order, we forward it to the supplier, usually by email, and the supplier ships it through its own shipping account. If you cannot ship through your account, we can provide shipping labels for every future order.

[L0854.009] (Older advice, 2022.)

Competing Brands and Other Suppliers

  • Suppliers ask which other companies you work with because they want to know whether others have already approved you and whether competitors are already selling on your site. They do not want to lose sales to competing brands, so they want to be on your site too. Answer with the right information for that reason. [L0933.005]
  • If you have not been approved by any supplier yet, name the two or three biggest players in your niche that you are reaching out to. If you are already approved with one or two suppliers, name them and say you will work with them for your launch. If you are in discussions with other companies, say you are in discussions. [L0933.006]

Template: competing brands answer

Right now, as you know, we have not launched yet, but we are reaching out to [top brand 1], [top brand 2] and [top brand 3], which we see as major players in this industry. [If approved with other suppliers: We have already spoken with [approved supplier A] and [approved supplier B], and we will be working with them for our launch.] [If in discussions with other companies: We are currently in discussions with other companies in this space.]

[L0933.007]

Template: why you, not just a listing

We are not just looking to list products. We are focused on the long term, and customer service is our priority. When we researched the market, we saw [number or description of competing brands], and what we love about your [specific product attribute, for example design, quality or uniqueness] is [why it stands out]. We will treat your customers with care and bring people to your store who want what you sell because of [specific benefits of their products].

[L0933.027]

  • A broad approval email avoids over-explaining (older advice, 2021): When a supplier asks which companies you compete with or what your business does, keep the initial approval email broad and vague. Do not over-explain your business model, customer base or marketing strategy in early communications. Let the supplier focus on whether they can work with online retailers, not on your specific market positioning. [L0270.066] (Older advice, 2021.)

Credit References, Prepay and Bank Details

Credit terms

  • Make clear you are not asking for credit terms. Suppliers usually give credit terms of 30 or 60 days to physical retailers that buy in bulk, and a dropshipping business should not request them. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0929.007]
  • State that you pay upfront for each order, before the product leaves the supplier's warehouse. Because the supplier is paid before shipping, it carries no financial risk, which makes approval easier. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0929.008]
  • Use prepay terms with suppliers, meaning you pay for orders before they ship. (2018.) [L1085.016]
  • If a supplier asks for trade references, you may use references from another niche. Tell the supplier you are not asking for credit terms, since references are usually requested to check payment history for credit. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0853.062]

Credit references on applications (2018 and 2023)

  • If a supplier application asks for three credit references, you can leave that section empty when you use prepay terms, because it only matters for credit terms. (Older advice, 2018.) [L1085.016]
  • When a supplier asks for bank details or a location, this is usually a credit check. If you are prepaying orders, you do not need to provide bank information. On the form, cross out the bank section and write prepay terms. (2023.) [L0970.047]

Template: revenue and credit references (2023)

Contact plan procedure

  1. Open your completed master supplier list and generate a contact plan for the first supplier. [L0126.008]
  2. Initiate Day 1 outreach with a phone call asking about their application process and identifying the decision maker (use the email template only if they request it). [L0126.008]
  3. Record the date, contact person's name and conversation notes. [L0126.008]
  4. Follow the 7-day contact schedule with the mix of phone calls and emails provided. [L0126.008]
  5. Stop contacting when you receive approval, rejection or "not right now." [L0126.008]
  6. Upon your first approval, proceed to the next lesson and upload that supplier's live products. [L0126.008]
  7. Return to this lesson and generate a new contact plan for your second supplier. [L0126.008]
  8. Repeat steps 1-7 until you have approval from five suppliers. [L0126.008]
  9. Continue this process with additional suppliers from your list to build silver and gold tier approvals over time. [L0126.008]

Follow-up timing (older advice, 2020, and sources)

  • If there is no response, follow up every 2-3 days. If they say "we'll follow up," follow up with them after 1 week if they have not responded. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0268.016]
  • Follow up with a promised callback or email after a few days. [L0853.012, L1094.012]
  • Follow up on the agreed date and log the result. [L0928.001]
  • Trade account follow-up procedure: if the brand does not respond, follow up every few days; if it rejected you, follow up when the stated condition is met. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0468.033]
  • Note the sources: the 2-3 day cycle (L0268.016) and "every few days" (L0468.033) give the same rhythm. The contact plan tool (L0126.008) sets the schedule within seven days.

Onboarding after approval

  1. Call the supplier and ask to be approved. [L0671.032, L0671.171]
  2. Once approved, request their product CSV or price list. [L0671.032, L0671.171]
  3. Upload their products to the store the same day or the next day. [L0671.032, L0671.171]
  4. Keep calling new suppliers every day. [L0671.032, L0671.171]

Trade Shows, Meetings and Territory

Trade show approach (2025)

  1. Remove the password from the store before the event so suppliers can see it live. [L1097.015]
  2. Print about 200 business cards with your logo, name, store email and cell number (Vistaprint is one option). [L1097.015]
  3. At each booth, introduce yourself and your company, and say you want to sell the brand's products online. [L1097.015]
  4. Ask whether they work with online retailers and who to speak to if they do not. [L1097.015]
  5. Collect each contact's business card. [L1097.015]
  6. That evening, email each contact a short note asking them to get in touch once they are back home. [L1097.015]
  7. If there is no reply after about a week, send one more follow-up. [L1097.015]

Territory (older advice, 2019)

  • When suppliers ask "What is your territory?", they are routing you to the correct regional sales representative. Simply state your location (city and state or geographic region). They will assign you to the territory rep responsible for your area. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0243.046]

Documentation

  • Document every conversation: the person's name, the date and all details discussed. Even if declined, the supplier will remember the call, which positions you for future contact. [L0133.015]
  • Record each answer, the reason for any rejection, and the date to follow up. [L0468.033]

Phone-first outreach sequence

  • Build a supplier list first, then contact each supplier by phone and by email. Once a supplier approves, upload its products to your Shopify store so customers can see them. In theory this is simple; the difficulty is carrying out the calls and emails. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L1248.012]
  • Follow this order for each product in the target country:
    1. Find the suppliers of the product in the target country.
    2. Call each supplier and ask for approval to retail their products through your website.
    3. After approval, list the products on the store. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L1481.016]
  • If calling feels uncomfortable, start with brands you care less about. This builds confidence before you call the brands that matter most. (Older advice, 2022.) [L1113.015]

Your position with suppliers

  • Approach suppliers as a mutually beneficial business opportunity, not as someone asking for a favor or begging for approval to sell their products. Both sides can make money from the arrangement. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L1248.003]
  • Suppliers benefit because you bring them sales and improve their cash flow. This win-win basis is what makes the relationship work; it is not a favor you ask for. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L1480.013]
  • Suppliers do not have to pay to have their products listed on your website. You handle marketing, customer service and payment, and you pay them only for the products you order. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L1248.004]
  • Suppliers already prepare and ship orders for their existing retail clients, so dropshipping orders add little new work for them. Their risk is minimal. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L1248.005]
  • In the worst case you sell nothing for a supplier, which leaves their net result at zero. They cannot lose money from the relationship, but they can gain. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L1248.006]
  • Suppliers compete with other manufacturers and distributors for the most prominent placement on retailer websites. This is why they want their products sold through a new store. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L1248.007]
  • Suppliers judge a seller by professionalism and business seriousness, not by where the seller lives. A non-US seller who holds a US reseller permit and an EIN can approach US suppliers. Follow the call script closely and stay calm. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1110.002]

Preparing before the call

  • Fix your website launch date before you contact any supplier, so you can give it without hesitation. (2024.) [L1115.005]
  • Do not reveal your exact location in early conversations. In traditional industries this can reduce trust or cause resentment. Set privacy settings so suppliers who connect with you on Facebook cannot see personal photos, such as work from the beach. (2024.) [L1441.006]
  • Prepare your own traffic ideas and a basic business plan. Suppliers need to believe you will add sales that require little work from them. (2024.) [L1422.016]
  • Prepare a short explanation of how you will add sales to the brand and how you will provide excellent customer service. Brands approve a reseller when it can show both. (Older advice, 2021.) [L1279.019]

Presenting your business

  • Suppliers unfamiliar with dropshipping need a pitch that explains the benefits and how the model works for them. Covering that education is a key part of getting them on board. (Older advice, 2018.) [L1274.038]

  • Present your business in this order (older advice, 2019):

    1. Show the supplier the value you will bring to their brand.
    2. Explain how you will market their products.
    3. Describe your business.
    4. Explain why you want to work with this specific supplier.

    [L1275.020]

  • Explain that you run a long-term business with customer support as your top priority, and that you are not a short-term or fly-by-night operation. (2024.) [L1422.013]

  • Approval depends on the supplier believing you will benefit its brand. Suppliers mostly want to know that you will look after customers and run a real business. (2023.) [L1283.005]

First message to a supplier. Keep it short, say you want a long-term partnership, and ask concrete questions. Questions on dropshipping, delivery time to each target market, shipping fee and method, and custom packaging or logo options let you qualify a supplier quickly before deeper negotiation. (Older advice, 2024 source.) [L1353.002]

Hi, I'm [YOUR NAME], a seasoned dropshipper. I'm already selling this product and want to find a supplier for a lasting partnership. If that interests you, could you help me with a few questions?

1. Do you provide dropshipping?
2. What is the typical delivery time to the USA, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and Europe?
3. What would shipping cost, and which carriers or methods would you use?
4. Can I add custom packaging and my own logo to the product?

Thanks in advance. I look forward to hearing from you.

Application forms and terms

  • When a supplier application asks what collaboration type you want, choose retailer, because you sell as a retailer on your own website. If asked about other sales platforms or physical stores, answer no when you only sell on your own website. (2024.) [L1115.097]
  • You usually do not need to send your own terms. The supplier reviews the agreements it provides. (2024.) [L1115.097]

What suppliers weigh

  • Suppliers weigh approval by downside risk. A retailer that brings no sales costs them little time. A retailer that damages their brand is their worst outcome, so your pitch should show you will protect their reputation. (2022 source; the same point appears in two units.) [L1422.019, L1422.022]
  • Suppliers are approving a future, not current results. Approvals are about the store's future potential and the niche, not current store results. (2020.) [L1111.050]

Honest positioning

  • State clearly during approval talks that you will never list a supplier's products below the MAP or price range it specifies. Suppliers need to believe this before they approve you. (2024 source.) [L1422.010]
  • Present the plan honestly and do not overstate the store's performance. (2020.) [L1111.050]
  • If the store is still in development and has only a few sales, describe it honestly as a store that is launching and plans to scale traffic and bring in the supplier's brand. Avoid sharing specific sales figures that would not impress a supplier. (2023.) [L1283.016]
