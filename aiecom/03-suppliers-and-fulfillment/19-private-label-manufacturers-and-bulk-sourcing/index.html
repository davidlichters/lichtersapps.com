3.19 Private label, manufacturers and bulk sourcing

Choosing a sourcing model

  • Pick the model before you pick a supplier. Dropshipping means the supplier ships each order directly to the customer. A manufacturer model means you buy orders upfront in minimum order quantities. [L0552.014]
  • Start with an existing store in the chosen niche before you move into private label or bulk importing. Bulk importing from Alibaba or China, for example one 40-foot HQ container at 30,000 to 50,000 USD, is more capital-intensive and time-consuming than dropshipping. Do it only with more money and time available. [L0836.042]
  • Use a two-step path: launch the dropshipping store first, then research the niche. [L0836.043]
  • Enter private label only when you can use the store's sales data to make the decision. [L0836.043]
  • Use off-the-shelf white-label products as an entry tier. Sell an existing product under your own brand to prove demand, then move to custom fragrance, molds and packaging in a later version. Margins are lower at this stage, but you learn how manufacturing works. [L1016.008]
  • Do not invest in inventory, custom molds or fragrance development before demand is proven. That locks up time and money before anyone has shown they will buy. [L1016.009]
  • Avoid private labeling technically complex products with specialized manufacturing know-how at the start. Customers often prefer to buy from a specific established manufacturer. Enter that category later, once expertise and demand are clear. [L0487.056]
  • Use the just-in-time model first for private label. An existing supplier makes your branded product only when an order comes in, with no upfront inventory cost. Choose this before any loan-funded stock purchase, and consider in-stock inventory only later. (Older advice, 2021.) [L1090.030]
  • Test a private-label product in this order:
    1. Find one or two products in the niche that could be private-labeled.
    2. Test the model with a few units.
    3. If it works, create a version 2.0 with the supplier and seek exclusivity on it. [L0334.044]

Finding and vetting suppliers

Where to search

  • Alibaba and AliExpress are the usual sourcing platforms for white-label commodity accessories. Electronics such as remote thermometers are harder to source and need a more careful supplier check. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0511.014]
  • Alibaba.com is the platform for finding manufacturers, mainly useful for sourcing from China. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1155.007]
  • Thomasnet.com is used to find American manufacturers. Prefer an American manufacturer when the store sells primarily in the United States. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1155.008]
  • Thomas Net and Google are used to find American suppliers. Search for the product plus terms such as manufacturer, private label or white label, for example "grilling utensils manufacturer" or "grilling utensil white label". (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1152.021]
  • Alibaba and Aliexpress are used to source products internationally. Prepare your questions before contacting suppliers. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1152.020]
  • Alibaba is worth searching when you want to sell under your own brand or source directly, not only for dropshipping. Its catalog can hold items useful for building a private-label store. [L0706.063]
  • If you cannot find an exact match of a product on AliExpress, it may be a private-label item made by a factory with custom features. Such products need a different sourcing route than standard AliExpress items. [L0372.026]
  • Search Google for suppliers in your product category. [L0227.012]
  • Search Google for Chinese box companies if the dropshipping supplier cannot produce custom packaging. [L0403.014]

Finding the manufacturer behind a brand

(Older advice, 2021-22.)

  1. Identify the companies in your market that already sell the product. They may white-label for you if you build a relationship with them. [L1152.018]
  2. Check whether one of the brands you carry is actually a house brand of a manufacturer. Compare the company address on the product listing with the address of a known manufacturer. [L1152.018]
  3. If the addresses match, the brand is produced by that manufacturer, which can usually white-label the product for you. [L1152.018, L1152.019]
  4. Contact the manufacturer directly to request white-label production. [L1152.018]
  • If a brand's address matches a manufacturer's address, that manufacturer makes the product and can be expected to offer white-label production. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1152.019]
  • Once a product becomes popular in China, many factories will manufacture it. Manufacturers will often contact a store that sells a hot product to white-label it. Expect this outreach and do not treat it as a differentiator. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1155.006]
  • Not every product category suits American manufacturing. A store targeting the US market should check for a US manufacturer first but may need to accept a Chinese one for some products. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1155.010]

Agents and intermediaries

  • Chinese agents cannot easily provide customization or branding services. These require connections to separate factories and English-speaking representatives, which adds complexity and time. [L0298.022]
  • Unified Dropshipping, CJ Dropshipping, Honest Fulfillment and Ecom Ops all offer branding services and fulfillment center integration. Use them for private labeling and quality control. [L0324.008]
  • Most dropshipping suppliers, including CJ Dropshipping, Hyper Rescue USA Drop and Zendrop, offer customization such as printed thank-you cards and custom boxes. The store owner only needs to supply the design files. [L0403.008]
  • For logo product customization, contact the manufacturer directly, for example via Alibaba, rather than a dropshipping middleman. The manufacturer can offer the best price for 500 to 1,000 units of custom printing. [L0403.022]
  • Avoid unknown private suppliers reached through random messages or unlisted websites. One operator paid such a person, received silver jewelry instead of the ordered gold jewelry, and had to refund most customers. [L1017.032]

Supplier checks before ordering

  • Confirm the supplier allows private label before negotiating price. There is no point negotiating costs if you cannot put your brand on the product. [L0227.016]
  • Ask for a copy of the supplier's business license as proof of a legitimate company. Refusal is a red flag. [L0228.017]
  • Before you order from a new supplier, ask for a photo or video of the actual product as it will be made in the factory. If a supplier refuses or delays this, treat it as a serious warning sign. The request is simple and gives you a basis for quality control. [L0694.015]
  • Visiting the factory, or having a representative visit for due diligence, is strongly recommended. [L1225.072]
  • Finding a supplier for a unique product made to spec means messaging and emailing many suppliers before one delivers the right quality. Chinese suppliers are often easy to work with, but local suppliers can also be a fit. [L1225.071]

Private label, white label and dropshipping

Definitions

  • White label means buying a supplier's already-developed product and adding your own label. It needs far less product development than building a new item from scratch. Commodity accessories with many existing suppliers are the easiest starting point. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0511.028]
  • Private label products are made by a contract or third-party manufacturer and sold under the buyer's own brand name. They can be sold through wholesalers, your own website, or marketplaces such as Amazon, eBay, Walmart or Etsy. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1151.003]
  • Creating a private-label product requires the manufacturer to produce a special batch for the buyer. This custom run is why private label costs more and is harder to start than white label. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1151.006]
  • White label manufacturers can produce thousands of units of a single product per day, which is why they can supply many brands at once. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1151.007]
  • A supplier that relabels its stock product under your brand name is called private label. The product is unchanged and still dropshipped from the supplier. [L0855.068]
  • Private label usually requires a higher minimum order quantity than white label. The manufacturer must run a special batch built to the buyer's own ingredient, packaging or label specifications. White label products made in large standard runs typically allow a much lower entry order. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1151.013]
  • Importing a generic product to sell under your own brand requires a warehouse or fulfillment center relationship. The brand name you create must differ from your company name, and competitors can sell the same product through their stores. (Older advice, 2021.) [L1090.031]

Which products fit

  • Commodity accessories with no differentiating factor are strong candidates for white labeling. Examples include utensils, cedar planks, thermometers, smoking racks, grill covers and slicing knives. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0511.013]
  • Pellet or consumable refills can be sold as a white-label product, creating recurring revenue, because manufacturers often approach brands to put their label on existing products. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0511.015]
  • For electronic accessories such as remote thermometers, a simpler manual or non-electronic product that serves the same purpose can be easier to source and carries less quality risk. Check whether a manual option exists before committing to an electronic supplier. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0511.027]
  • For average-priced products around 50 to 100 dollars, order samples first and check quality. If quality is poor, create a private-label version and fulfill it through a fulfillment center. This keeps the flow similar to dropshipping. [L0487.053]
  • Give private-label products their own brand name, separate from the main store. Sell them alongside established brands on the main site and through other online retailers. This gives customers a familiar buying environment while building a separate asset. [L0487.055]
  • Owning the supply chain removes stock-out surprises, which otherwise make forecasting hard and lead to refunds when a supplier suddenly reports no stock. Private labeling shifts this control to the brand, with its own set of problems to solve. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0511.016]
  • For gift products, ask the supplier to include personalized note cards in the shipping packaging. If the supplier does not do this by default, contact them and request it. [L0355.013]

Testing a private-label idea

  • Test a private-label product with a supplier in this order:
    1. Find one or two products in the niche that could be private-labeled.
    2. Test the model with a few units.
    3. If it works, create a version 2.0 with the supplier and seek exclusivity on it. [L0334.044]
  • Transparency costs money. Third-party testing and full sourcing disclosure make manufacturing more expensive. The brand estimated that some of its products could be made for roughly half the cost without these practices, but it keeps them for authenticity. [L0334.046]

Transition from a multi-brand store (older advice, 2018)

Private label transition from a multi-brand store:

  1. Build the store with third-party brands and keep it running for six months to a year, pushing as much traffic and as many sales as possible.
  2. Survey customers after purchase about the buying experience, what they like about products and what help they want.
  3. After that period, analyze your sales data to find the top three best-selling products and the reasons they sell, such as price, durability or color options.
  4. Build a separate brand website, for example on WordPress, that presents your brand instead of selling products as a store.
  5. Find three product variations from manufacturers that match the winning criteria, such as a good price, durability and multiple colors.
  6. Add your new brand to your existing store alongside the other brands and promote it in the product page sidebar, without claiming ownership on the store itself.
  7. Once you have shipped the products, gathered reviews and proven demand, add them to your brand website and sell your products to competing retailers as a supplier.

[L1274.042]

Research before private labeling

Research before private labeling:

  1. Launch a dropshipping store in the chosen niche first.
  2. List products from around 20 brands in that niche.
  3. Track which brands and products sell most, their top price points, and their features and benefits.
  4. Introduce your own brand at a price range and with features that proven products already sell at.
  5. Sell that brand on your own store and on competitor stores.

[L0836.043]

  • Once a product sells consistently, sourcing a refined or better-quality version from a manufacturer can improve the product and support a higher price. Only do this if the base product is already selling well. [L0871.017]
  • Manufacturers offer products as dropshipping or bulk stock. Choose the model by the stage of the business, not by the supplier's pitch. [L0552.014]

Negotiation

  • Always negotiate minimum quantities. Suppliers initially quote high minimums. Emphasize that this is your first private-label attempt to push them lower, for example 500 → 200 → 100. [L0324.011]
  • Propose a minimum order quantity, negotiating down from the supplier's initial quote. [L0324.010]
  • Request a minimum order quantity (MOQ) waiver. Explain that you are new and will order larger quantities once satisfied. [L0228.038]
  • Raise MOQ questions early, during sampling. MOQs are usually negotiable, but suppliers often open with very high numbers, so do not be discouraged by the first quote. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1154.003]
  • Do not ask "What is your MOQ (minimum order quantity)?" as the first question. This signals you are small and unprofessional. Request a tiered pricing sheet instead; the MOQ will be provided in the response. [L0227.013]
  • Ask every question you have with a supplier, leave nothing unknown, and treat all terms as negotiable. [L1225.072]
  • When a supplier's minimum order quantity is too high, ask for a sample or tester order of around 10 pieces before committing to the full MOQ. Alibaba-type platforms are commonly used for this request. [L1017.030]
  • Ordering fewer than 100 units will not get a cheaper price from manufacturers such as those on Alibaba. [L0788.048]
  • Ask suppliers for a single sample unit at wholesale price to inspect quality before committing, and mention it during the phone call. Sample units are not required for every business, but they can be requested. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0960.030]

Costs and pricing effects

  • Product cost with private labeling increases by double or by a quarter, depending on product type. Upfront stock investment is typically $500-2,000, depending on product price and minimum quantity. [L0324.013]
  • Example: a product costing $4.99 including shipping may double in cost, or rise by a quarter. Pricing varies by supplier and customization level, such as logo placement and packaging. [L0324.014]
  • Putting a logo on a product costs about 30 cents extra per unit. It requires buying a minimum order quantity in bulk and holding inventory. There is no way around the bulk purchase when branding products. [L0694.021]
  • A $49.95 product example: supplier cost was around $6 to $7 for a single unit and about $2 to $3 or less when ordered in bulk. Bulk orders lower unit cost significantly and only pay off once sales volume is consistent. [L0871.014]
  • A product costing $10 at 500 to 1,000 units can cost $12 to $13 at lower quantities. [L0788.048]
  • Bulk orders in the source reduced unit costs by about 50-60%, which raises profit margin. Inventory is a risk, so bulk buying only makes sense with consistent sales projections. [L0637.054]
  • Bulk example: buying 200 units at about 2 USD per unit instead of 7 USD per unit lowers unit cost substantially. Stock is kept at the supplier's location and orders are fulfilled from there, with only shipping costs paid per order. [L1108.034]
  • Do not buy stock in bulk at the start, because it is not known yet whether the product will sell. Consider bulk buying only when scaling. [L1108.029]
  • Buy stock in bulk only once a product is generating consistent orders, such as around 50 to 100 orders per day for 10 consecutive days. Start with a small stock and expand as the product proves itself, because stock investment can be as high as 50,000 dollars for private label. [L0780.029]
  • Bulk buying should start only after sales have been consistent for at least 3-4 weeks, ideally one to two months. Minimum order quantities tie up capital, and sea freight adds delay and risk. [L1217.006]
  • Do not buy in bulk before product quality has been verified. Test with a small first order, because a bulk order from a supplier with quality problems multiplies the losses. [L0694.020]

First contact, communication and channels

Outreach procedure

Procedure for contacting suppliers:

  1. Search Google for suppliers in your product category.
  2. Send an initial email requesting information.
  3. If there is no response within 2 weeks, follow up with a phone call. Suppliers often ignore initial emails.
  4. Request a tiered pricing sheet showing unit costs for volumes such as 1-500, 500-1,000, 1,000-2,500, 2,500-5,000 and 5,000-10,000 units.
  5. Confirm the supplier allows private label.
  6. Ask for lead time, meaning the time from order to shipment ready.
  7. Build the relationship by maintaining communication and calling when needed.

[L0227.012]

  • Use phone calls when initial emails go unanswered. Suppliers often prefer phone contact to assess whether the buyer is serious. A phone call can convert a non-responsive email into an engaged supplier. [L0227.014]
  • Overseas suppliers come with time zone differences and a language barrier. Reputable suppliers usually have someone who speaks decent English, but the English will not be perfect. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1153.051]
  • Private suppliers provide dedicated support via channels such as Skype or WhatsApp, including daily updates and phone calls. This helps build a long-term partnership. [L0685.021]
  • Private-label suppliers such as Unify may prefer different contact channels, for example Skype or WhatsApp. Ask which channel they use before sending product links and requests. [L0577.074]

Samples, tests and quality control

Ordering a sample

  • Use a fast but economical shipping method when ordering a sample, not an expensive express courier. A sample only needs to arrive quickly enough to test the product. [L0552.053]
  • Ask the agent or supplier to order one sample, have them film the item and take close-up shots of stitching and details, then decide if quality is good enough by your own standards. If yes, start selling and see whether demand exists. Ask the warehouse to take photos when the product arrives. [L0552.008]
  • Expect to pay for samples, although a small discount may be offered. Some suppliers may never send them, so do not rely on a promised free sample. [L0694.016]

Sample check procedure

Procedure for checking a supplier sample before committing:

  1. Ask for a photo or video of the sample, including close-up shots of the material and a stretch test for stretchable fabric.
  2. If the product needs a logo, have the logo applied to the sample so you can check its placement and quality.
  3. Have the sample shipped to yourself so you can inspect the product in person.
  4. Place a test order through your own store, so you can see the real packaging, shipping and how the parcel arrives at the customer.
  5. Expect to pay for samples, although a small discount may be offered; some suppliers may never send them, so do not rely on a promised free sample.

[L0694.016]

Inspecting your own first batch

Procedure for inspecting a first batch:

  1. Ship your first smaller batch directly to yourself, via Amazon Partnered Carrier, typically about $50.
  2. Inspect the product quality, packaging and branding.
  3. If satisfactory, reship to Amazon, another about $50.

Alternatively, use a third-party inspection service in China or the United States to inspect before forwarding to Amazon. This shifts quality control risk to the service provider.

[L0228.039]

Substitutions and mismatches

  • A substitute product, such as a printed item shipped instead of the woven product that was ordered, causes quality complaints and refunds. Confirm the exact material and construction before ordering, and check what arrives instead of trusting a video call alone. [L0694.017]
  • A mismatch in product, color, material or country can lead to refunds and closing the store. Make sure the supplier actually ships the product you sell, and check quality before going live. [L0780.023]

Ongoing quality control

  • Start with tech packs and spec sheets, then run quality checks continuously. Get photos or videos of production regularly. [L1225.073]

  • Procedure for production runs:

    1. Write a contract with clauses that hold the supplier accountable for the approved sample.
    2. Have the first lot of every production run sent for inspection.
    3. Request weekly photos or videos of the product during production.
    4. Check that each batch matches the approved sample.

    [L1225.074]

  • Suppliers can change quality over repeat runs, for example by using less plastic in molds, substituting ingredients or prioritizing other customers. Without checks on every run, a brand may only notice after the damage is done. [L1225.075]

  • Holding stock at a supplier warehouse in China means you can see the product before shipping it out. Pure dropshipping only pushes the product to another supplier. This visibility matters for quality control. [L0780.025]

Tech packs

A tech pack is the key document for suppliers and manufacturers. It can be downloaded as a template, hired out to be made from scratch, or made yourself in Illustrator or Photoshop. It must include:

  1. Digital sketch of the garment
  2. Construction details and trims
  3. Measurements
  4. Fabrics
  5. Size
  6. Look inspiration
  7. Colours
  8. Artwork scales

[L0779.012]

Essentials needed to run a clothing brand from the start:

  1. A label maker for product labels.
  2. Poly sender bags for shipping.
  3. Home storage or a warehouse space to keep stock.

[L0779.013]

Payment terms and order risk

  • Negotiate payment terms with factories as early as possible. Without them, the business has to fund every order upfront, which quickly strains cash flow when money is scarce. [L0790.012]

  • Payment terms procedure:

    1. Use bank wire transfer, not Western Union, for secure payment.
    2. Offer a 30% deposit upfront and 70% on completion.
    3. Request photographic proof of your manufactured product with branding before releasing the final 70% payment.
    4. Verify the supplier's legitimacy through its verified badges.

    Check that the verified badges are Trade Assurance, Gold Supplier or Assessed Supplier. These indicate third-party inspections and legitimacy. [L0228.017]

  • Negotiate a smaller initial order, for example 200-250 units, for a new supplier as a test batch to verify quality and reliability. [L0228.038]

  • Once Amazon pays you after two weeks of sales, use that revenue to fund the 70% balance on a reorder while the supplier is still building the first batch. Maintain short lead times of 7-14 days so reorders arrive before inventory depletes. [L0228.038]

  • Lead time is the duration from payment to product completion. Longer lead times delay your first inventory arrival and make it difficult to reorder quickly when inventory runs low, especially during peak seasons. [L0228.015]
  • Long production lead times must be included in inventory and promotion planning. [L0790.011]
  • Supplier lead times can be around one month, so reorder early enough that stock never runs out. Lead times differ depending on whether goods come from the US or China and on the freight method. Measure your own lead times. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1154.016]
  • Longer supplier lead times of 6-8+ weeks reduce competition, because most sellers avoid the wait. Accepting longer lead times is a competitive advantage that makes ranking and dominating the listing easier. [L0227.044]
  • Bulk stock should be ordered when there is still enough time to cover the production and transit. Plan the next order so the stock does not run out. [L1153.049]

Inventory in transit

  • Inventory is in the pipeline at several stages at once: at the manufacturer, at the freight origin ready for shipping, on the vessel, at customs, and in your warehouse. Even at a steady sales rate, you must have several shipments ordered ahead to avoid stockouts. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1154.017]

Sea and air freight

Choosing the mode

  • Air freight is faster but more expensive. Sea freight is cheaper but takes 30-45 days. Use air freight for initial orders and smaller shipments with short lead times. [L0228.016]
  • Switch to sea freight only when ordering large quantities of 5,000-10,000+ units lasting 1-6 weeks. At that volume the total shipping cost becomes economical despite the longer transit time. [L0228.016]
  • Shipping time from China by sea freight is about 14 days to California, plus about one more week to reach Amazon's warehouses. Air freight from China can arrive within three to five days. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1153.046]
  • Compare the total landed cost, including shipping and time, not only the unit price. A US supplier can reduce shipping cost and lead time, and products can be labeled Made in the USA, which helps on Amazon. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1153.048]
  • Amazon's paid carrier service can collect goods from a US supplier or printer and deliver them to Amazon warehouses. The seller reported this is cheap enough to beat sea freight costs for US-made products. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1153.047]

Landed cost by mode (case)

  • The landed cost of one blanket was about 20 to 25 USD by air freight. It fell to about 7 to 10 USD with sea freight at large order volumes. [L0492.018]
  • Order the next season's stock early and ship it by sea to lower the per-unit cost. For a winter season, order in April and ship by sea. [L0492.020]
  • Supplier production paused over Chinese New Year. After the pause, plan for 7 to 15 business days of shipping from the supplier to the customer. [L0492.022]

Consolidated shipping to a warehouse

  • Shipping from a supplier to a warehouse in China costs about $0.50 per piece for a single order. It drops to about $0.10 per product when local domestic shipping is consolidated across around 10 orders. Stock shipped in one consolidated batch therefore has a much lower per-unit logistics cost. [L0780.022, L0780.041]

Sea freight and suppliers in China

  • Sea freight from a supplier with a direct address takes about 20-30 days. For a fast-moving product, order in advance and keep stock on hand. [L1217.004]

  • Freight on board (FOB) terms for goods from China: map the full route and cost every stage before setting prices.

    1. Freight on board at the origin port, where the goods are loaded onto the vessel.
    2. Ocean transport to the destination.
    3. Customs clearance.
    4. Transport from the port to your warehouse.
    5. Preparation for shipping to customers.

    Add the costs of all these stages, plus the outbound shipping cost to the customer, into your product price. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1154.004]

Custom packaging, branding and inserts

When to invest

  • Do not invest in custom packaging at launch. Wait until a product has produced good results for one to two weeks, then order a small run of about 100-200 pieces. [L0692.043]
  • Supplier packaging is often low quality. Research the right box type for each product and invest in a sturdier box that protects the product during shipping and improves the unboxing experience. [L0403.019]
  • Many fulfillment companies offer personalized packaging, inserts and notes such as thank-you cards. These improve the customer experience and support a transition from a generic dropshipping store to a recognized brand. [L0760.022]

Custom boxes

  • Custom boxes from local or Chinese box makers can cost from about $0.50 per box. Ordering at least 50 to 100 units is needed to get bulk pricing. [L0403.013]

  • Order at least 50 to 100 units of custom packaging to get good bulk prices. [L0403.015]

  • Use supplier-provided or local box makers if the dropshipping supplier cannot produce custom packaging. [L0403.014]

  • Commissioning a custom box design:

    1. First ask the supplier for the exact box dimensions.
    2. Give the same box size to the freelance designer on Fiverr or Upwork, together with the logo, the website link and the brand colors.
    3. The designer produces the box design based on this brief.

    [L0403.010]

  • Fiverr and Upwork are used to commission design work for thank-you cards, tags and custom box designs. [L0403.009]

Thank-you cards and tags

  • Thank-you cards and tags usually come from the dropshipping supplier, so the store owner needs no design skills to add them. If the supplier does not offer them, they can be outsourced to a freelancer on Fiverr for under $5 to about $20. [L0403.024]

Custom designs and product changes

  • Custom branding and logos on hoodies typically require a minimum order of 500 pieces. [L1212.025]
  • Customization of a product design can usually be done by a supplier if the customer provides their own design. Changes in cut or direction, such as a hoodie that opens differently, may require a different supplier. [L1212.026]
  • Custom designs require a minimum order quantity of 500 pieces from the supplier. Plan for that volume before offering custom designs. [L0492.017]
  • Logo product customization usually requires a minimum order quantity of about 500 to 1,000 units, though the supplier may be able to reduce it to around 200 units. [L0403.021]

Branding before a full packaging order

Procedure for branding a product before a full packaging order:

  1. Ask the supplier for a sample with the packaging and a small logo, if possible.
  2. Have the sample shipped to you.
  3. Create product photos with it in a clean setting, such as a rented space or a simple background.
  4. Edit the photos lightly in Canva to create a branded look.

[L0692.044]

Branding supplier

  • Best Dealer (bestdealer.com) was the main supplier for the blanket stock. It also produced custom packaging and bags with branding from China. Its owner recommended it for both dropshipping and buying stock. [L0492.015]
  • Best Dealer is a Chinese supplier with strong service, fast shipping and good packaging. It is well suited for building a brand with bulk orders. A shipment of 25 large packages was delivered from China to Switzerland within 10 days. [L1184.026]

Inventory pipeline and stock levels

Staging stock

  • Stock up a little before the holiday, because the warehouse is closed only for about three days but has limited capacity. [L0780.030]
  • Start with bulk stock from the supplier while testing. Switch to a 3PL warehouse once consistent orders have run for two to three weeks or more. A 3PL is an investment and carries risk, so it fits best with predictable sales. [L0871.015]
  • Consider holding stock in a local market only once a product brings consistent orders, for example at least 50 to 100 orders per day for a consecutive period of 10 days. Before that, buying a small stock locally or in China, around 10 pieces, keeps risk low and shortens delivery to about three days. [L0780.028]
  • Stock levels should be planned around the time it takes for inventory to move through each stage. Stock can be ordered ahead to cover the next period. [L1154.017]

Phased approach to moving stock

Phased approach to moving stock:

  1. When entering a new region, fulfill directly from China. Buy customized goods, store them in China and ship from there.
  2. When a product sells strongly in one country, move at least the best sellers to a warehouse close to the customers, for example the UK for UK customers, Germany for German customers.
  3. For widespread demand across Europe, keep some stock in China, because shipping from China to a country such as Sweden can be cheaper than shipping from a neighboring country.
  4. Scale later once the first country is serviced well.

[L0780.026]

  • Fulfil from China when margins are thin, because shipping a product to a local warehouse can eat up profit. Move fulfillment closer to customers when the product is high-ticket and margins are large enough to afford local fulfillment. [L0780.027]
  • Shorter delivery times improve the customer experience, so placing stock in a warehouse close to the buyers is worth doing once a product sells consistently. Wait until demand is proven before moving stock overseas. [L0780.043]
  • Moving stock to a US warehouse could cut shipping times by three to four days or more. It means shipping large containers that can be lost with the money inside. Only move stock once the store is stable and the extra speed justifies the container risk. [L0694.022]
  • For faster shipping in the US, products should be stored in US warehouses. The products themselves do not need to be manufactured in the US. (Older advice, 2023.) [L0804.034]
  • Bulk stock held in China can help avoid delays around Chinese holidays. Keep the stock ready there so orders can still ship. [L1108.035]

Chinese New Year planning

Timing

  • Chinese New Year typically closes factories around 1 February. Warehouses keep running but also close for a few days. Stock is needed before the closure, and suppliers should be contacted early because they stop at different times. [L0349.025]
  • Chinese New Year in the year of the lesson started around 20 January. Suppliers were barely available for about two weeks around it, while the warehouse itself was closed only for about three days. Plan stock levels and ad pauses around that roughly two-week window. [L0780.042]
  • During Chinese New Year, factories in China close for roughly 20 days to one month, and the exact end date differs by factory. Ask your supplier for it. For the case discussed, the holiday ended around the 17th or 18th of the month, and factories needed one to two weeks after that to resume normal operations. [L0694.011]

Before the holiday

  • Communicate with your supplier about Chinese New Year closure dates. If you plan to restock after Q4, arrange shipment to a US-based warehouse instead of waiting for inventory to arrive after New Year. Confirm whether their warehouse will operate during the holiday period. [L0309.013]
  • Before Chinese New Year, stock a winning product in advance, because stocked orders still ship during the holiday. Without stock, orders take about a month, and the advice is to wait until the holiday ends. [L1161.015]
  • Plan bulk orders ahead of predictable slow-supply periods such as Chinese New Year. [L1017.020]
  • Stock up a little before the holiday, because the warehouse is closed only for about three days but has limited capacity. [L0780.030]

During the holiday

  • Stop running ads for products you cannot ship, because during the factory closure you cannot fulfil orders. If you have no stock, a pause of at least one week is advised around the holiday. [L0694.012]
  • Slow down or turn off ads during the roughly two-week period around New Year when suppliers are barely available. [L0780.030]
  • Reduce product variants and keep only the two best-sellers, marking others as sold out without removing them from the site. [L0780.030]
  • During the factory closure, suppliers reply slowly, so ask questions early. Use the time to prepare creatives, optimise the website, improve existing products and research more products. [L0694.013]
  • Use the quiet period to catch up on orders and negotiate a better price, because quantities per item are higher. [L0780.030]
  • Watch for suppliers who offer bulk orders as a fix for Chinese New Year without checking whether stock will be fulfilled during the holiday. The bulk order can become a money trap if workers are away. [L0780.031]

Warehousing, fulfillment and 3PL transition

Stages of the transition

  • Start with bulk stock from the supplier while testing. Switch to a 3PL warehouse once consistent orders have run for two to three weeks or more. [L0871.015]
  • At $100k-500k per month, if a fulfillment agent is still used, the store should build its brand and move products into a US 3PL. This protects customer relationships, brand loyalty and LTV (lifetime value). [L1129.015]
  • For single-country sales, a fulfillment service can keep stock in a warehouse in that country. The owner can also store and ship stock locally if they live in the target country. [L1108.036]
  • For stock kept in a warehouse rather than pure dropshipping, Daily Fulfill was named as a supplier another seller used and rated as solid. The seller pointed to a supplier that requires bulk orders. [L1127.019]
  • Honest Fulfillment is a 3PL cited for the best per-order shipping rates in the industry. It requires at least 1,000 units per month for each SKU. Verify the exact company name before contacting it. [L1217.067]

Warehouse selection and storage

  • Physical inventory needs a storage solution: either your own warehouse or a third-party logistics (3PL) provider, which are easy to find. Where possible, choose a storage location in the US Midwest, or one facility on each coast. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1154.006]
  • With a white-label product, ask the manufacturer whether it will store the inventory and dropship orders directly to customers. Many manufacturers offer this. Raising it early can remove the need for your own warehouse. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1154.007]
  • Fulfillment center storage can be arranged as a retainer fee, a per-unit fee or free, negotiable. [L0324.010]
  • Running your own warehouse gave one brand control over fulfillment at scale. The team included about 20 warehouse staff alongside about 22 office staff, including customer service. [L0790.013]
  • When selling on local marketplaces such as Amazon or eBay, buy stock in larger bulk and hold a baseline of goods in a local warehouse, because the marketplace needs fast local delivery. Dropshipping from China alone usually cannot meet that delivery standard. [L0780.040]

Packaging when leaving Amazon

  • Amazon branded shipping boxes were used for orders shipped from Amazon in the past. Amazon later offered blank-box shipping as an alternative. When selling outside Amazon, move away from Amazon-branded packaging as soon as possible. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1154.009]

Tariffs and country of manufacture

  • Manufacturing in the US can avoid import taxes that change often. This matters for brands whose 3PL and customers are in the US. Compare the total landed cost, including tariffs, before choosing a country. [L1225.078]
  • Not every product category suits American manufacturing. Check for a US manufacturer first, but accept a Chinese one for some products. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1155.010]

Unsold MOQ stock

  • For products bought under a supplier minimum order quantity, ship orders quickly. Slow shipping drives chargebacks and RDR refunds. This applies especially to products sold on a custom brand. [L0351.017]

  • Options for an MOQ product that is not selling well:

    1. White-hand method: refund all customers who ordered the product.
    2. Black-hand method: ask the supplier to send a similar product with a different book cover, and keep selling the same product under the new cover.

    [L0351.018]

  • The black-hand method has downsides. Customers may complain, the total gross revenue and net margin are still kept, and chargebacks can still occur because customers say the product was not as described. It is worth doing when the order value is low, since profit is still profit. [L0351.019]

  • Use the black-hand method only when the product suddenly stops selling and the proof of concept no longer holds, and not frequently. It is mostly a testing tool. [L0351.020]

The US supplier offered about $2 per set at higher order volumes and cut shipping cost and time. The seller planned to place orders slightly above $2 per set and expected prices to fall as order quantities grow. (Undated source, Source A.) [L1153.045]

Wearable blanket business (case)

  • Best Dealer was the main supplier for the blanket stock. [L0492.015]
  • Custom designs require a minimum order quantity of 500 pieces from the supplier. [L0492.017]
  • The landed cost of one blanket was about 20 to 25 USD by air freight. It fell to about 7 to 10 USD with sea freight at large order volumes. [L0492.018]
  • The first batch of 500 blankets cost about $10,000 in product. Later packaging for a second batch cost about $500, bringing the total to about $20,000. [L0492.019]

Australian fashion seller expanding to Switzerland (case)

Validate Demand Before Buying Stock

  • Placing a large upfront stock order before a product is validated can lock a store into one supplier and raise costs. Start with small test orders, compare the price with private agents, and only buy bulk after consistent sales. [L1315.061]
  • Large bulk stock purchases before a sale create cash-flow and stock risk. Unsold stock locks up capital; in the lesson, about 10,000 units were still unsold. Sales are hard to forecast, so scale stock in step with proven demand. [L1363.038]
  • Buying stock in advance carries risk: if one variant of a multi-variant product does not sell, the money tied up in that variant is lost. [L1440.017]
  • Build inventory through a repeated loop: order a small batch, sell it out, reinvest the revenue into a slightly larger reorder, and repeat. This keeps risk low while you learn what sells. [L1438.013]
  • Before buying a full range of designs from a supplier, order a single sample to judge quality, and use it for product photography. Only designs that look good in real life should be ordered in stock. [L1439.004]
  • Do not commit to selling products you cannot fulfil. Forecast demand and account for shipment times and production times, so orders can be delivered as promised. [L1438.014]

Minimum Order Quantities and Test Variations

  • Most suppliers set a minimum order quantity of around 50 pieces per item. Look for suppliers who accept a lower minimum and offer more variations, such as three or four colours, so you can test which version sells best before reordering. [L1438.012]

Finding and Comparing Manufacturers

  • Alibaba and AliExpress list factories with ratings, reviews and contact information, which helps you compare manufacturers. Many suppliers also list their email addresses and phone numbers on their own websites. [L1438.016]
  • When choosing a manufacturer, look for proof that they have made similar products before, good communication, and an established network of fabric suppliers, pattern cutters and pattern makers who can produce your design. [L1438.015]
  • Choose the manufacturing country by whether its factories have the fabric network and pattern-making skills your specific product needs, not by price alone. For this clothing brand, production was split between China and Pakistan, two regions used for apparel manufacturing. [L1438.058]
  • Switching manufacturers between repeat orders of the same garment can change its cut and shape, so returning customers receive an item that looks and fits differently and feel let down. Keep the same supplier for repeat designs so the product stays consistent. [L1438.059]

Apparel Development Workflow

Clothing development workflow:

  1. Gather initial ideas from your own inspiration, other brands, street fashion and luxury runway shows.
  2. Draw the designs (the founder used Photoshop) and make initial drawings.
  3. Create a detailed tech pack covering measurements, fabrics, washes, colours and material weights.
  4. Choose the fabrics together with the supplier.
  5. Order a sample and wait for it to come back.
  6. Note every change you want before the bulk order.
  7. Place the bulk order.

[L1438.019]

Tech Packs and Sampling

  • A detailed tech pack reduces production errors and misunderstandings, especially across language barriers. The more precise the specification, the smoother manufacturing runs for both you and the supplier. [L1438.020]
  • Plan for two or three sample rounds before the final product when starting with a new design or supplier. With an established supplier and the same design, you may only need to pay for one sample, with further adjustments made free of charge. [L1438.021]

Rush Production

  • For products with long lead times, ask the supplier whether you can pay an overtime or labour fee to shorten production (rush production). Then charge that fee to the customer as a markup. [L1357.026]

Private Label, Custom Labelling and Trademarks

  • Custom labelling and private label products are not realistic for a beginner with a contract, because a single Chinese factory cannot be bound by contract to stay loyal unless it is a very large brand. A cheaper, more practical route is custom labelling, such as a logo on the product, custom packaging and boxing, combined with a better customer experience. [L1440.015]
  • A trademark or patent for a private-label product applies only in the country where it is registered, so a brand sold in several markets needs protection in each one. Factor the cost and time of these registrations into any private-label plan, along with sampling and contracts. [L1440.061]

US Forwarding Addresses and Warehousing

  • Older advice (Source A, 2021): A US forwarding address with warehouse storage adds investment and operational complexity. Use it only if you are fully convinced the products will sell exceptionally well. [L1279.030]
