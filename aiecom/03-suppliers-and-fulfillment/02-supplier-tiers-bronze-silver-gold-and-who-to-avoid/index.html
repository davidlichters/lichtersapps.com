3.2 Supplier tiers: bronze, silver, gold and who to avoid

Supplier Tiers at a Glance

Supplier tiers are an informal ranking, not an official industry standard, so searching for "gold, silver, bronze supplier" will not return a definition [L0711.001]. Each tier leads to different results, so check the tier before deciding which suppliers to pursue [L0590.002]. Supplier type determines your profitability, how much price competition you face, how much control you have over prices, and whether your store can scale [L0110.008].

Tier Access cost Approval MAP enforced Share of your suppliers Service Sources
Bronze Fee or payment required Anyone who pays; no real screening Usually not Avoid Poor L0110.001, L0486.001, L0711.002, L0711.007
Silver No fee Each applicant reviewed Yes Default: 100% of first five, about 95% long term Good to great L0110.002, L0486.011, L0711.009
Gold No fee Strict; requires business history Yes, acted on immediately About 5 to 10% of suppliers Excellent L0110.006, L0711.012, L0711.014

Bronze Suppliers: Why to Avoid Them

What bronze means

  • Bronze suppliers accept anyone who applies and usually charge a fee for access to their catalog [L0486.001].
  • They accept any applicant without screening, and the only requirement is payment. Treat this as a major red flag [L0711.002].
  • Bronze suppliers typically ask for approval fees of $50, $100, $1,000 or more before authorizing a retailer [L0711.004].
  • A supplier that asks you to pay before you can sell its products is classified as bronze and skipped [L0486.002, L1111.026].
  • Bronze suppliers are the ones most likely to cause MAP violations [L1279.020].
  • Bronze suppliers charge a fee to become an authorized retailer, while suppliers whose relationship benefits both sides through product sales are preferred [L0934.033].
  • Bronze suppliers often make money from fees or from poor sales terms [L0806.018].

Why they hurt profit

  • The same products are listed by countless other sellers, and you cannot stay profitable under that price pressure [L0486.001].
  • Without MAP enforcement, retailers undercut each other until margins disappear, even when orders come in [L0486.003, L0110.001].
  • Example: a chandelier with a MAP of $1,000 that competitors sell for $600 to $800, with no action from the supplier [L0711.006].
  • Competitors selling at thin margins (for example around 5%) undercut you, so listings and paid traffic sent to those products are largely wasted [L0711.008].
  • Products from bronze suppliers show inconsistent quality, with high rates of shipping damage and defects [L0711.008].
  • Service is poor: slow replies, delayed shipping and missing tracking numbers. A retailer may have to follow up a week after submitting an order to learn whether it shipped [L0711.007].
  • Bronze suppliers offer no real advantages to a retailer. Their drawbacks are unenforced MAP, inconsistent quality and poor support [L0711.017].
  • A store built on bronze suppliers may produce early sales, but the business tends to fall apart quickly and become a constant problem [L0711.021].
  • Their products often bring more headaches than profit, so do not run ads for them or sell them [L0806.018].

Rule for building your network

  • Bronze suppliers must be avoided entirely when researching suppliers and building your network [L0112.002].
  • Never sell for bronze suppliers, even when their catalog looks attractive [L0486.016].
  • Avoid bronze suppliers that require you to buy their product or pre-purchase inventory before you can sell [L0801.025].

Exception for existing listings (conflicts with the rules above, see "Conflicting Guidance")

  • Handling a bronze supplier with no MAP policy and an MSRP below 2X wholesale:
    1. Check whether its products still sell at a healthy margin without ad costs [L0934.034].
    2. If they do, keep them on the site and optionally in the product feed for free listings [L0934.034].
    3. Set their Google Ads cost per click to one cent so no ad money goes to them [L0934.034].
    4. Remove them if nothing sells [L0934.034].

Silver Suppliers: The Default Tier

Identifying a silver supplier

  • Silver suppliers review and approve each retailer individually based on credibility [L0110.002].
  • Identify silver suppliers by three characteristics: they charge no fees to sell their products, they require approval before onboarding, and they enforce MAP pricing [L0110.004].
  • A silver supplier does not charge for access and reviews its applicants. If a supplier meets both criteria, classify it as silver [L0486.011].
  • A supplier you found through niche product research, whose website has no page offering paid access to sell its products, is generally silver. Confirm the other silver traits, such as MAP enforcement and retailer review, before applying [L0486.019].
  • Silver suppliers do not show a "where to buy" section [L0486.014, L0801.034].

What silver suppliers offer

  • They enforce MAP quickly once a retailer reports a competitor selling below it. They contact the offending retailer to correct the price or remove it as an authorized seller [L0711.010].
  • Example: a MAP of $1,000 with a competitor listing at $900 leads to immediate action [L0711.010].
  • They provide good to great customer service, with fast shipping, quick responses to inquiries and care for their retailers [L0110.002, L0486.010].
  • They usually assign a dedicated sales representative, reachable by email or phone, for orders, tracking, new variations and questions [L0711.011].
  • They ship quickly and respond promptly because they earn from retailer sales rather than from fees, so their interests align with yours [L0711.011].
  • They protect your margins through MAP enforcement and provide quality control and fast shipping. They are plentiful, with thousands available [L0711.018].
  • Their drawback is competition: any legitimate store can sell their products, so traffic and paid ads must be well optimized [L0711.018].
  • Silver suppliers tend to offer good to great customer service and are preferred partners [L0486.010].

What silver suppliers require

  • Silver suppliers review every retailer that applies and approve only selected sellers [L0486.006].
  • Expect a refusal if you apply without a finished demo store and a complete business setup, so prepare both before contacting them [L0486.006].
  • They review every applicant, including the live store, so the store must be built before applying [L0711.009].
  • Trust is the foundation of the relationship. Silver suppliers use your business setup and presentation as signals of legitimacy and professionalism before granting access [L0110.010].
  • Approval is the supplier's decision that it trusts you with its brand. Silver and gold suppliers expect retailers to earn that trust before approval [L1422.003].

Silver suppliers in practice

  • Silver suppliers approve most sellers and will make up nearly all of your suppliers until you have a sales track record [L1274.029].
  • A store can be profitable with only silver-tier suppliers. Gold-tier suppliers add strength but are not required [L1281.120].
  • Silver suppliers have similar margins to gold but are less selective [L1283.007].
  • Silver suppliers are the core of the business and make up most brands sold over time [L0969.006].
  • Silver suppliers because they approve new retailers without needing a reputation [L0466.033].
  • Start outreach with silver suppliers first to build momentum [L0251.049, L0466.033].
  • Select Silver as the supplier tier in the contact plan, because over 95 percent of the brands you will sell for are silver tier [L0936.004].

Gold Suppliers: The Top Tier and a Later Goal

Identifying a gold supplier

  • Gold suppliers are extremely selective, enforce MAP, do not sell directly to the public, and actively refer customers to authorized retailers through a "Where to Buy" page [L0110.006].
  • Identify a gold supplier by a "where to buy" section on its website that links to other online stores, typically 5 to 10, in your niche [L0486.014, L0801.034].
  • Seeing that the supplier already refers buyers to other stores shows it is gold without prior approval [L0711.016].
  • A "where to buy" section listing retailer logos is a good sign that a supplier is gold. Other suppliers usually need to be contacted to confirm their level [L1068.034].
  • Gold suppliers usually list their approved retailers on their site [L1283.007].

Scale and selectivity

  • Gold suppliers authorize only a small number of retailers, often around 5 to 10 companies in total [L0486.012, L0110.006].
  • Gold suppliers are the highest-quality tier but a small minority, roughly 5 to 10 percent of a retailer's suppliers [L0711.012].
  • They build relationships closely and work with only a very small number of selected retailers [L0711.012].

Requirements and approval timing

  • Gold suppliers require proof of established business history, existing traffic and a sales track record before approval [L0110.007, L0486.012].
  • They are a long-term goal after your business has proven sales success rather than a starting point [L0110.007].
  • Gold suppliers often reject new stores until they show traffic, sales or time in business [L0853.016].
  • Gold suppliers do not approve new retailers on day one because their screening is stricter. Approval typically comes after the store has traffic, sales and a track record with silver suppliers, often three to twelve months later [L0711.013].
  • Gold suppliers that send traffic by linking to your store can take 3 months, 6 months, or more than a year to authorize [L0938.004].
  • Some brands approve a new retailer right away, while gold-level suppliers can take a year or two because they want to see a long sales history [L0857.033].
  • Gold approvals are initially difficult but gradually become easier as relationships develop and trust grows [L0112.004].
  • Gold suppliers are usually the hardest to get approved with. Still contact every supplier in your niche, because outreach is quick, and circle back on rejections [L0934.036].
  • Gold suppliers are normal to lag in approval [L0969.006].

Service and referrals

  • Gold suppliers enforce MAP and act right away against sellers who list below it [L0711.014].
  • They give excellent customer service, with a point of contact who reaches out proactively to help increase sales [L0711.014, L0486.013].
  • They do not sell to the public themselves, so they refer customers to their authorized retailers [L0486.013, L0486.021].
  • Approved gold-tier retailers receive referrals: when a customer wants to buy directly from the supplier, the supplier passes the lead and contact details to an authorized retailer [L0711.015].
  • The referral flow is the main reason gold suppliers are so limited in number [L0711.015].
  • Gold suppliers are most likely to link back to your store. Not all suppliers will do this, and tier placement determines this benefit [L0359.030].
  • Expect referrals only after you have been approved and have proven traffic and sales [L0486.021].
  • Gold suppliers combine MAP protection, customer support and quality control with referrals, and they limit competition because they work with only a handful of online retailers. Their main drawback is that approval is hard without a sales history [L0711.020].

Using gold for credibility

  • Once suppliers begin approving you, use the approved brands to build credibility before approaching your top targets [L0667.059].
  • Over time, get approved with as many brands in the niche as possible, as long as they are silver or gold, because a low-selling brand with strong margins can fund monthly promotions [L1113.018].
  • Once you have revenue and traffic, circle back to gold suppliers, whose approval odds are much higher [L0251.049].
  • Reapply to gold once the store is proven, after building with silver suppliers first [L0853.016].

Identifying Tiers Before You Apply

Check in this order:

  1. Check whether the supplier charges for access to its products. If yes, classify it as bronze and drop it [L0486.017].
  2. Check whether it enforces MAP and reviews applicants. If yes, it is a silver supplier [L0486.017].
  3. Look for a "where to buy" section linking to 5 to 10 online stores. If yes, it is a gold supplier, and you should plan to approach it after building traffic and sales [L0486.017].

Supporting checks:

  • A supplier without a MAP policy is classed as a lower tier than suppliers that meet all criteria, including MAP. It can still be workable if margins hold after shipping [L1285.017].
  • A supplier that enforces MAP belongs to the higher-quality group. Prioritize enforcing suppliers when choosing whom to approach first [L1275.017].
  • Prioritize slow-responding, frequently out-of-stock or slow-shipping suppliers lower than reliable ones, because these can signal financial instability and may not survive a cash-flow shock [L0967.019].
  • A supplier with an outdated website, low-resolution images or thin product information can still be worth carrying, because your job is to give customers a reason to buy. Clearer product content and professional presentation on your own store can make that difference [L1086.067].

Sequencing:

  • Start by contacting silver-tier suppliers, and work up to gold-tier brands once you have experience [L0667.059].
  • Submit only your top two or three niche ideas for verification [L0667.059].
  • Do not wait for the best supplier before you begin. Open an account with any supplier who will work with you, build demand, and premium suppliers may approach you later [L1441.044].
  • Premium or top-tier suppliers are not needed at the start [L1441.044].

Red Flags: Paid Access and Fees

  • A legitimate supplier never charges a retailer for access to its products, including approval fees. If a supplier asks for money to get approved or to sell, decline and move to the next supplier on your list [L0711.003].
  • Do not pay an opening fee or buy a minimum starter quantity just to open a supplier account. Counter a minimum order request by explaining when you will reach that volume [L0664.012].
  • Never pay for access to any supplier. Use the course login on the dropship platform for supplier access, which is included in the membership [L0964.024].
  • Distributors that require a paid membership fee to access products should be avoided. Distributors without a fee can be worth working with if they enforce pricing controls [L0429.060].
  • Do not accept offers that charge per order. Example: $25 per order plus a $100 fee per invoice on small invoices is a warning sign. Ask whether there are sales tiers without fees. If there are none, do not sell the products [L0806.019].
  • Do not pay a one-time pre-purchase stock fee, such as $10,000. Propose a one-month test listing with ads instead. If the supplier refuses, launch with other brands and return later with a stronger store [L1283.011].
  • Some suppliers outside China charge yearly license fees, reported at up to $300 to $500 [L1214.005].
  • Big Buy requires a paid membership, reported at about €300 per year [L1214.005].

Red Flags: Paid Directories and Middlemen

Paid directories

  • Paid supplier directories that charge a monthly fee (for example $150 per month) for access to products are middlemen and should not be used. Their products are not worth the cost [L0359.026].
  • Do not pay monthly or yearly fees to paid supplier directories, even when they claim access to over a million products, because they typically offer no direct relationship with the brands [L0466.019].
  • Paid directories sit several layers away from the brand through distributors, overstock sellers and resellers, so pricing control, MAP compliance and current product data are lost. Products are often outdated and already marked up at least twice, and every paying retailer competes with the same products [L0466.020].
  • Avoid supplier directories. They mostly connect you to middlemen, and the margins through them are usually too thin [L0962.008].
  • Avoid supplier directories that sell access to brand catalogs for a percentage. They skip the direct relationship with the brand, which is what makes the business work long term [L1277.032].
  • Supplier directories and paid supplier lists were judged not worth the money. Finding suppliers through your own direct research gives much better brand quality [L0551.012].
  • Supplier directories and paid apps that promise instant access to thousands of products are bronze-level sources [L0486.002].

Middlemen

  • Do not pay middlemen for supplier lists or catalog access. A paid monthly CSV of supplier products is a red flag and can harm your account standing [L0247.030].
  • Bronze-tier suppliers that route you through a marketplace or middleman such as Alibaba are generally not worth working with. Look for suppliers elsewhere [L1113.019].
  • Overseas contacts may be middlemen rather than factories, so verify before investing. When an overseas contact offers an affiliate-style commission, ask for their U.S. contact or U.S. warehouse and for the reseller program [L1274.033].
  • Source A suppliers charge no fee for approval or access. They are the manufacturers or brand owners themselves, not middlemen or distributors [L1422.002].
  • Using a distributor is only acceptable without a fee and with pricing controls [L0429.060].

Red Flags: Preloaded Stores and Store-Building Offers

  • Some bronze suppliers offer to build a preloaded store with all their products for $2,000, $5,000 or $10,000. Such an offer is another red flag for a bronze supplier [L0711.005].

Red Flags: Affiliate-Only Deals and Low Margins

Affiliate-only offers

  • Affiliate-only offers from a dealer that refuses drop shipping, such as an 8% commission, are not worth it. Promoting as an affiliate only makes sense for a related niche you would never sell yourself [L0664.014].
  • Do not accept offers that pay a commission of about 8% to 12% on sales. That is an affiliate arrangement, not an e-commerce or dropshipping deal, and does not leave enough margin [L1274.032].
  • Suppliers that sell to physical stores often suggest becoming their affiliate instead. Affiliate promotion only fits when customers may want the product but it does not make sense for the store to sell it directly, for example promoting a drum school to buyers of beginner drum sets [L0666.017].
  • Affiliate offers are not suitable for your own core products, because the store cannot show add-to-cart buttons or run the usual ads for products you do not sell. Affiliate links fit indirectly related products, such as courses or services for the same audience, and the margins are very small [L1283.013].
  • Affiliate-level margins of 10 to 15% leave too little room for ad spend. Such products should be listed only if the sale costs nothing and should not receive paid traffic [L1111.048].
  • Suppliers that offer only an affiliate program should be deprioritized. Follow up with other suppliers and return to affiliate-only suppliers once the store has traffic and sales [L0855.072].

Low margins and no MAP

  • A supplier requiring a minimum annual sales volume (for example $10,000 per year) or tiny margins (for example 12%) is a red flag. Starting with a supplier that has a credible brand can help get approved by more suppliers later [L0472.023].
  • When a supplier has no MAP policy and offers only a thin percentage margin (for example 20 to 35 percent with volume tiers), treat it as bronze and avoid it, because advertising costs, merchant fees and shipping leave no margin [L0665.014].
  • Margins of 15 to 20 percent place a supplier in the bronze category, and bronze suppliers should not be used [L1283.007].

Red Flags: Trial Terms and Unusual Setups

  • Do not accept terms requiring you to sell products at no profit as a trial period before negotiating a profitable margin. This indicates a low-tier supplier and is not worthwhile. Avoid suppliers that require this as a mandatory trial phase before allowing margin negotiation [L0276.029, L0276.041].
  • Negotiate a test where products go live on your store first, so the best sellers show up before any inventory is bought [L0801.025].
  • Be cautious with a wholesale company that asks retailers to send customers to its own website without a cart and to take orders by email or phone. This setup is unusual and a poor fit for a dropshipping store, so question its terms before accepting [L0967.097].
  • Do not sell Black Friday orders through supplier stores based in China. The risk of bad customer experiences is not worth the sales [L1097.019].
  • Prefer silver or gold suppliers for reliability and work with as many suppliers as possible. Holiday peaks such as Black Friday, Cyber Monday and Christmas can cause stock shortages with disorganized suppliers [L0357.024].
  • Avoid sourcing from marketplaces like AliExpress. Reasons: long shipping times, unbranded products and little to no quality control [L1277.033].

Conflicting Guidance

Bronze: never sell versus sell on existing listings

  • Never sell for bronze suppliers and do not run ads for them [L0486.016, L0806.018].
  • Exception for products already listed, where the margin works without ad costs and MSRP is below 2X wholesale: keep them with a one-cent CPC and remove them if nothing sells [L0934.034].
  • Applies when: you are building a store (rule above) or you already have listings that earn a healthy margin without ad spend (exception) [L0486.016, L0934.034].

Tier versus judgment

  • A supplier's tier does not decide whether to work with it. Judge the supplier by the cost of goods, which should be about 50% of the selling price, by whether it enforces MAP, and by the discount it offers [L0664.089].
  • Tier rules still apply to silver and gold screening, approval and MAP expectations. Use the cost-of-goods test to judge any supplier you can actually access [L0664.089, L0110.002].
  • Applies when: you are comparing offers from suppliers you can access without paying [L0664.089].

Bronze without MAP

  • A supplier without a MAP policy is lower tier, but it can still be workable if margins hold after shipping [L1285.017].
  • Thin percentage margins with no MAP are treated as bronze and avoided [L0665.014].
  • Applies when: margins are high enough after shipping and ad costs (workable), or when margins are thin with volume tiers (avoid) [L1285.017, L0665.014].

Gold approval timing

  • 3 to 12 months after the store has traffic, sales and silver track record [L0711.013].
  • 3 months, 6 months, or more than a year, for suppliers that link to your store [L0938.004].
  • A year or two for brands that want a long sales history [L0857.033].
  • Applies when: the supplier's own site has a referral section or wants to see sales history [L0938.004, L0857.033].

Silver share of suppliers

  • 100% of your first five suppliers, and about 95% of all suppliers long-term [L0110.003, L0486.005].
  • 90 to 95 percent of a retailer's suppliers [L0711.009].
  • Majority of both total suppliers and sales revenue [L0112.003].
  • Nearly all suppliers until you have a sales track record [L1274.029].
  • Applies when: the first five suppliers and the long-term base are being planned (first five and about 95% long term) [L0110.003], or when the store is still building its track record (nearly all) [L1274.029].

Gold share of suppliers

  • Around 5 to 10 retailers in total, which counts how many retailers a gold supplier authorizes [L0110.006, L0486.012].
  • Roughly 5 to 10 percent of a retailer's suppliers, which counts how much of your supplier base gold makes up [L0711.012].
  • Applies when: you are judging how many retailers a gold brand works with versus how many gold brands you should hold [L0110.006, L0711.012].

Target Mix and Sequencing

Target mix

  • Silver: 100% of your first five suppliers and about 95% of all suppliers long-term [L0110.003, L0486.005].
  • Gold: about 5 to 10% of your suppliers [L0711.012].
  • Bronze: none; avoid entirely [L0112.002].
  • A store can be profitable with silver only, and gold adds strength but is not required [L1281.120].

Sequence

  1. Build a store with silver suppliers, which should make up most of your supplier base [L0853.016].
  2. Build sales, traffic and brand reputation with silver suppliers [L0711.021].
  3. Once you have sales and traffic, apply to gold suppliers [L0251.049, L0853.016].
  4. Keep contacting other suppliers while waiting for gold approval [L0938.004].
