3.13 Shipping setup, policies and delivery expectations

Who Ships the Order: Supplier Account First

  • The preferred arrangement is for the supplier to ship on its own account, so you receive one invoice, and the supplier handles returns, loss and breakage (older, 2019) [L0357.015].
  • Ask suppliers to do this first, before you arrange any other shipping option (older, 2019) [L0357.015].
  • Always have suppliers ship on their own accounts when possible. They negotiate better rates through volume and can handle tracking issues. Provide your UPS or FedEx account only if the supplier explicitly cannot ship from its own (older, 2021) [L0270.046].
  • Ship through the supplier's own shipping account whenever possible, because suppliers ship high volumes and often receive bulk discounts that lower the cost (2025) [L0966.035].
  • When possible, have the supplier ship from its own carrier account, because suppliers ship more volume and get better discounts. If a supplier will not ship on its account, the store must send bills of lading for freight shipments (2025) [L0667.019].
  • Use the supplier's shipping account for shipping. Do not plan for freight carriers unless the supplier refuses to ship from its account (older, 2019) [L1069.015].
  • Ask suppliers to ship on their own shipping account whenever possible, because it reduces liability and simplifies operations. If a supplier refuses, using your own freight account through a freight broker is still a workable option (older, 2019) [L1069.004].
  • Prefer suppliers who ship on their own account, since they bill shipping on the invoice and handle damage claims and replacements. Take this option whenever it is available (older, 2018) [L0466.034].
  • Prefer suppliers who ship from their own accounts because it keeps operations simpler. Bill of lading shipments are still acceptable when a supplier requires them (older, 2021) [L0469.022].
  • Set shipments to go out on the supplier's account by default. If the supplier will not, get a freight quote and bill of lading, and have the shipment billed to the supplier (older, 2021) [L0834.019].
  • Ship orders on the supplier's shipping account whenever possible. The supplier then handles damage insurance claims and can send a replacement right away (older, 2019) [L0663.038].
  • Ship on the supplier's carrier account whenever possible, because suppliers ship high volume and usually get discounted rates. Use your own UPS, FedEx or freight accounts only when the supplier refuses (older, 2022) [L0665.015].
  • If a supplier ships on its own account, use that setup because it means less work. If it will not, ship on your own account, which is also common (older, 2022) [L0803.016].
  • Ship on the supplier's own account, with the supplier billing the store, whenever possible. Use this approach to keep operations simple (older, 2020) [L1088.011].
  • Ship on the supplier's own account when it offers published or discounted UPS rates, because that makes dropshipping easier and often cheaper. Ask the supplier whether it has a discounted rate sheet for its carrier account (older, 2021) [L1090.028].
  • Prefer suppliers with short shipping times, and ship on the supplier's own account whenever possible, since this is smoother for every order. Your shipping page can list all carriers you may use, and orders from different carriers are not a problem (older, 2021) [L0968.006].
  • Ship on the supplier's account by default and count the shipping fee as a cost per order. Offering free shipping to customers means the store pays this fee to the supplier, so it is not actually free for the business. In the example it was 100 USD per order [L0759.017].
  • Suppliers either ship on their own account and charge you for shipping, or they ship on your account. Your preferred option is for the supplier to ship on its account and bill you for shipping. If it does not allow that, you ship on your own account [L0933.018].
  • Shipping on the supplier's account makes it faster for the supplier to print labels and ship the same day. It also makes claims simpler if an item is damaged or lost in transit, because the supplier can file the claim and send a replacement quickly [L0933.020].
  • Template for asking a supplier to ship on its account (no year):
We would prefer to ship on your account and simply pay you for shipping. If that is not something you do, we can arrange shipping on our account, and we will give you our UPS or FedEx account number when we process orders.

[L0933.021]

  • Suppliers that refuse to ship on your account are not a deal breaker, but work out who pays the carrier charge before accepting the order arrangement (older, 2022) [L0969.097].
  • Ask suppliers which carriers their largest retailers use. Shipping on a supplier's business account usually gives bulk pricing (older, 2020) [L0468.038].
  • Suppliers should ship directly to customers, not drop-ship on the store's behalf for their own products. For a US store, the supplier should be in the US. A foreign manufacturer is acceptable if its products are warehoused in the US (2023) [L0964.023].
  • Ship on the supplier's own account, and do not send products to your own address first and then to the customer. Ask the supplier to ship directly to the customer's address (older, 2020) [L1111.039].
  • Some suppliers, roughly 5 to 10 percent, require the seller to ship on their own account. In that case, ask for a UPS or FedEx account number to use for labels, or for a freight carrier the supplier prefers for freight orders (older, 2020) [L1088.012].
  • Most suppliers do not pay shipping. When the store offers free shipping, the store pays that cost itself (older, 2020) [L1088.015].

  • Shipping on the supplier's account keeps damage and replacement claims with the supplier, and large freight is not shipped via UPS or FedEx. Insure shipments yourself only if damage is rare with that supplier (2024) [L0971.046].
  • Seller accounts with UPS or FedEx are free to open (older, 2022) [L0969.008].
  • Shipping on your own UPS or FedEx account makes you liable for overage claims, for example when declared measurements are wrong. In-transit damage must be claimed from the carrier rather than replaced free by the supplier (2024) [L0971.085].

Using Your Own Carrier Account

Opening the account

  • Do not open a shipping carrier account before contacting suppliers. Ask which carrier the supplier recommends, because suppliers know which carriers already collect at their warehouse at the best rates (older, 2021) [L1090.025].
  • For standard UPS or FedEx shipments, create your own account with each carrier and give the supplier your account numbers, so it ships on your account rather than its own (older, 2022) [L0272.002].
  • Create a free UPS or FedEx account online and share your account number with suppliers. They will use their own access to create shipping labels and charge the cost to your account. This requires almost no effort from you once set up (older, 2022) [L0249.091].

Setup for small parcels when a supplier will not ship on its own account (UPS or FedEx) (older, 2022) [L0854.007]:

  1. Open a free account with ups.com or fedex.com.
  2. Send your carrier account number to the supplier.
  3. The supplier creates the shipping labels on your account for each order.

Shipping options when a supplier does not ship on its own account (older, 2019) [L0357.016]:

  1. Give the supplier a FedEx or UPS account number so it ships on your account (both can be opened free online).
  2. If that is not possible, ask the supplier which freight carrier or freight broker it recommends.
  3. Partner with the recommended carrier or broker for those shipments.
  4. For each customer order, place the order with the supplier after the sale, then use the chosen shipping method.

When a supplier will not ship on its own account (older, 2019) [L0357.070]:

  1. Ask the supplier which shipping company or freight broker it recommends, since it already works with other retailers and knows which carrier handles most of its orders.
  2. Contact the recommended carrier or broker.
  3. Set up the account or partnership with that company for your shipments.

Choosing a method with a supplier that does not ship on its account (2023) [L0251.022]:

  1. Contact the supplier and ask about its preferred shipping method.
  2. If items are large and heavy, use a freight broker via freightcenter.com. The supplier may provide a freight account, or you create your own.
  3. If items are standard parcel size, ask the supplier for its UPS or FedEx account number, or create accounts under your business name.
  4. Communicate the chosen method clearly so both parties execute correctly.
  • Ask suppliers who they recommend as a shipping company rather than asking whether they ship on their own account. Suppliers that do not ship themselves still know which carriers their other retailers use (older, 2019) [L0357.017].
  • Ask which carriers the supplier recommends and which large retailers it uses. Choose carriers from that list (older, 2021) [L0247.019].
  • Ask suppliers which carrier their largest retailers use, and open a business account in your own name (older, 2020) [L0468.038, L0468.039].

Opening a business account for a better rate (older, 2020) [L0468.039]:

  1. Ask the supplier which carrier its top retailers use for shipping.
  2. Contact that carrier and open a business account in your own name.
  3. Tell the carrier where you ship from and that current pricing is not competitive.
  4. Ask for a discount on the account.
  5. Expect discounts to grow with shipping volume.
  • Template for asking a carrier for a discount (older, 2020) [L0468.190]:
Hi [carrier contact name],

This is [your name] with [business name]. We have opened a business account with [carrier name] and we ship our products from [ship-from city, state].

The current shipping pricing is not competitive for us. Is there any discount you can apply to our account? We expect our shipping volume to grow, so we would also like to know how discounts increase as we ship more.

Thank you,
[your name]
[business name]
[phone number]

[L0468.190]

  • Shipping discounts from carriers are usually based on volume. Higher volumes through a carrier, or shipping through a supplier who ships a lot on its own account, generally produce the best rates (2023) [L0835.032].
  • Shipping services that charge a fee on top of carrier rates are only useful for stores with their own warehouse and high shipping volume. For drop-ship stores they are generally not worth the cost (older, 2020) [L0468.047].
  • Before accepting a third-party group shipping discount, compare its rates against your actual shipping lanes. Some lanes may get better rates while others get worse. Do not assume a group deal is a good deal for everyone [L0942.027].

Choosing Carriers and Comparing Prices

  • For items that are not large, heavy or freight, use the carrier the supplier prefers, usually UPS or FedEx. Large, heavy items need a different carrier (2025) [L0857.030].
  • Carrier choice for small parcels in Australia: Couriers Please, Sendle and eParcel were recommended. Compare their rates and delivery times before choosing one for a store (older, 2020) [L1087.001].
  • In Australia, many brands rely on Australia Post because it is government-mandated to deliver nationwide. A hybrid setup with StarTrack, which Australia Post owns, can offer a branded tracking page on top of the carrier (Source E, 2025-26) [L0955.030].
  • Pirate Ship is mentioned as a good option for small parcels. The lesson presenter recommended it from experience but had not used it personally (2025) [L0667.020].
  • Pirate Ship is reported to be cheaper than UPS for small parcels, with insurance provided through a third party. Compare its rates against your own UPS quote before switching (2023) [L0835.140].
  • Check shipping costs with a supplier's shipping calculator before committing, since international UPS shipping is typically very expensive. If the cost looks unreasonable to you, customers will likely see it the same way (older, 2021) [L0968.007].
  • Sales reps at supplier brands are the best source for shipping advice. Ask which carrier they prefer, what pricing they get, and how their largest online retailers ship (older, 2020) [L0967.025].
  • Before you use the same carrier as a supplier's other retailers, check its label status. If a supplier has a label pending status with FedEx for more than about 24 hours, call the carrier to check whether the delay is on the supplier or the carrier side (older, 2020) [L0967.032].
  • If a supplier marks up shipping above its real cost, ship on your own account and do not pay the markup [L0359.027].
  • If a supplier offers a better price when you handle shipping yourself, ask whether it means you buy the stock in advance or pay for shipping. If you ship with your own UPS or FedEx account, or through a freight broker's bill of lading, this can save money (older, 2020) [L0551.018].
  • Ask the supplier whether it can use the same shipping method you select. If the price still does not match, shipping on your own account is the next step (2023) [L0835.024].

Matching the carrier price with your own UPS account (2023) [L0835.025]:

  1. Ask the supplier to ship with the same shipping method you select, so the price matches your quote.
  2. If it cannot match it, open a free UPS account for your business.
  3. Give the supplier your UPS account number.
  4. The supplier creates each shipment in UPS and enters your account number in the billing section.
  5. UPS bills you for the shipping, and you pay the supplier only for the cost of the goods.
  • Template for querying a supplier's higher shipping price (2023) [L0835.026]:
Hi [Supplier contact name], we were reviewing the quotes for our shipments and noticed yours are substantially higher than the ones we get from UPS. Just wanted to make sure you are choosing the same shipping method, or are we missing something here? Could you check whether you can use the shipping method we select so the price matches? If not, we would like to ship on our own UPS account, so please use our account number [UPS account number] when you create shipments.
[Your name]
[Company name]

[L0835.026]

  • Supplier surcharges for shipping on their own account (for example a 5 dollar surcharge on regular items and 25 dollars on large items) are a reason to compare with self-arranged carrier quotes before accepting (older, 2022) [L0969.010].
  • Shipping on the supplier's account versus on the seller's account: when the supplier ships on its own account, it charges the product plus shipping in one invoice. When the seller ships on its own UPS or FedEx account, the supplier charges only the product, and the carrier bills the shipping separately (older, 2022) [L0969.008].

Negotiating shipping costs with a supplier (older, 2020) [L0852.042]:

  1. Bring real numbers to the supplier, including the number of sales, where orders shipped, shipping costs and the margin you are left with.
  2. Ask whether the supplier can ship through its preferred carriers on its own account, which often gives better rates.
  3. Ask for incentivized pricing or a wholesale discount so the product becomes profitable for both sides.
  4. If the supplier ships through freight, get separate freight quotes from freight brokers and compare them with the supplier's rate.
  • Template for asking a supplier to cover shipping on its account and cut costs (older, 2021-22) [L1046.034]:
Hi [Supplier name], last year we spent about [98,000] with you on shipping and fulfillment. Your shipping volume is likely higher than ours, so could you check whether your freight accounts or carrier rates can cover our orders at a lower cost? Our target for next year is about [75,000], and I would like to agree on a plan to reach it. Could we set up a call this week to review the rates? Thank you, [Your name].

[L1046.034]

  • Some suppliers do not coordinate shipping, or their shipping is too expensive, so the store may use a separate freight provider such as UPS. In that case product cost and shipping cost must be tracked separately, and both need to be compared against the supplier's pricing (older, 2021-22) [L1046.029].
  • Record each supplier's shipping and fulfilment details so an assistant can arrange pickups, including supplier pickup times, warehouse hours, the contact person, and which carriers collect from the supplier's area [L1000.051].

Insurance, Damage and Claims

  • Insure every shipment. Insurance is the baseline protection, regardless of which carrier is used, and it stops occasional damage from costing the store money (older, 2021) [L0853.002].
  • Insure every shipment, whether the supplier ships it or you ship from your own shipping account, so damaged or lost goods can be replaced without an out-of-pocket loss. Insurance is selected as an option when the shipment is created (2025) [L0966.030].
  • Shipments sent to customers should be insured against loss and transit damage. Suppliers usually insure shipments made on their own accounts (older, 2020) [L1088.013].
  • Insure shipments yourself only if damage is rare with that supplier. If the supplier ships on its own account, it handles claims (2024) [L0971.046].
  • Before paying extra for specialized shipping on fragile goods, check each supplier's products for complaints (older, 2021) [L0853.001]:
    1. Search Google for the supplier's name and product names together with "reviews".
    2. Look for repeated mentions of returns, refunds or damage from other stores' customers.
    3. If no clear pattern appears, ship with a recommended carrier and insure every shipment.
    4. Only use costlier specialized shippers if damage remains a recurring problem.
  • Check the carrier's insurance terms before relying on insurance. Some policies exclude parcels shipped in manufacturer boxes printed with product names or SKUs, which can lead to denied damage claims (older, 2021) [L0247.020].
  • Insurance is covered by the supplier when it ships on its account, so the store can focus on tracking the claim and the replacement [L0663.038].

Tracking and Multi-Supplier Shipments

  • Require suppliers to send a tracking number when an order ships. A supplier that offers no tracking at all is a risk, so ask it to at least reply to purchase orders with tracking numbers (older, 2021) [L0247.022].

  • In-stock orders should have tracking information sent within 24 hours of the order leaving the warehouse. If the customer has none after four business days, they should follow up by email (older, 2021-22) [L0556.024].

  • Customers accept multiple packages if they receive tracking numbers. When customers order products that ship from multiple suppliers, send tracking information for each shipment as it ships separately (2024) [L0276.022].

  • Handling a multi-item order shipped from different suppliers (older, 2020) [L0852.038]:

    1. Expect the items to ship separately from different warehouses, and tell the customer this is normal.
    2. When part of an order ships, enter the tracking number in the Shopify order backend for only the products that shipped.
    3. Keep the customer updated so each separate shipment is expected and understood.

  • When a store's products come from several suppliers, supplier-specific shipping costs can make some products unprofitable. Check shipping cost and the supplier's location before deciding where to advertise (older, 2020) [L0967.044].

  • Free tracked shipping instead of standard shipping can increase trust and reduce checkout drop-off (Source C) [L0640.038].

  • Weekly shipments for bulk ordering (Source C) [L0324.012]:

    1. Collect all orders placed Monday to Friday.
    2. On Friday, batch-ship all week's orders to DHL in one consolidated shipment rather than individual drops.

  • Regional assignment of suppliers reduces shipping times. Instead of one centralized warehouse, assign suppliers by region: Northeast suppliers handle Northeast orders, Southeast suppliers handle Southeast orders. This speeds delivery without increasing costs (2025) [L0278.040].

Free Shipping and Pricing It In

Deciding whether to build shipping into the price

  • Offer free shipping on all products as the base option. Carrier costs are paid by the business, and rates are checked after orders come in rather than calculated at checkout (older, 2021) [L0247.034].
  • Free shipping is paid out of the seller's own margin, so sell a product under a free-shipping threshold (for example under 3,500) only if the margin covers the shipping cost. Shipping fees vary by supplier (2025) [L0472.022].
  • Because the store offers free shipping on everything, shipping cost must be built into product prices so each order still leaves a minimum of 25 percent net profit (older, 2018) [L0466.008].
  • If a supplier can't be priced to cover shipping, restrict its products in advertising to nearby regions (older, 2018) [L0466.009].
  • If you offer shipping charged to customers, decide whether to build it into the price or charge at checkout by checking what the top 2 or 3 competitors do for the same brand (2023) [L0804.036].
  • Free shipping is the usual standard in these niches, so it is not a differentiator. Check what the top two to five competitors charge for shipping and price your products to match, since those stores are your direct rivals on Google Shopping (2024) [L0965.038].
  • Free shipping from suppliers is a plus when launching a store with a few suppliers (2024) [L0836.044].
  • When a supplier says shipping is free, confirm whether the shipping cost is built into the product price, because that changes the margin calculation [L0359.028].
  • Passing international shipping costs to the buyer is possible only if the final price still works for the product. Shipping should not cost more than the product itself (older, 2020) [L0852.048].
  • For heavy items, avoid real-time shipping calculation in the store. Build the shipping cost into the product price and offer free shipping, provided the product is still profitable (older, 2019) [L1068.030].
  • Shipping that a supplier charges directly to the customer can be handled by adding the cost to the product price or by creating a separate Shopify shipping profile for that supplier's products (2024) [L0971.049].
  • Some suppliers require the seller to create and send a shipping label for pickup, so the store pays that shipping cost directly. For heavy items, check this cost before listing, because it can exceed the margin and make the product unprofitable (2024) [L0836.067].
  • When a competitor advertises free international shipping for a bulky or heavy product, the cost is either built into an expensive price or the seller stocks the same product in warehouses in both countries. Cross-border shipping of large or heavy goods is very costly, so a claim of truly free shipping only works for very high-priced, small items. Check which explanation applies before copying the model (older, 2021) [L1090.144].
  • Free shipping is excluded for Hawaii and Alaska because it is too expensive to offer for free (older, 2021) [L0834.031].

Calculating an average shipping cost (2018 method)

  1. Note each approved supplier's shipping origin and where your customers are located, for example the share of sales per state.
  2. Ask each supplier for shipping quotes for its most popular product to those regions, ideally while applying for approval.
  3. Calculate an average shipping cost per product, weighted by the share of your business in each region (for example a 50 percent split between two regions).
  4. Add that average into the product price instead of marking the price up more for shipping.
  5. Check that the resulting net margin still meets the 25 percent minimum while free shipping is offered.
  6. If the margin still does not cover shipping for a supplier, restrict that supplier's products in advertising to nearby regions (see geo-targeting).

(older, 2018) [L0466.009]

  • Example: a stand-up paddle board costs about 50 dollars to ship within the same state and about 150 dollars from a supplier in a distant state. If half of the customers are in each zone, the average of 100 dollars is the figure to build into the price (older, 2018) [L0466.010].
  • If a product's shipping cost can consume nearly all of its net profit per order, for example 150 dollars of profit against 150 dollars of shipping, do not sell it to the expensive regions. This situation should be rare (older, 2018) [L0466.011].
  • If a supplier cannot quote shipping for individual items, ask for package specifications (dimensions and weight of each item and box) in a spreadsheet or CSV and estimate costs from those numbers (older, 2018) [L0466.013].
  • Template for requesting shipping quotes from a supplier (older, 2018) [L0466.069]:
Hi [Supplier name], we have been approved and are preparing our store. Most of our customers are in [State A], about [X]% of our business, and we also sell to [State B]. What would it cost to ship your most popular product, [product name], to a [State A] address and to a [State B] address? Please include the package weight and dimensions with each quote.

[L0466.069]

  • Estimating a typical shipping cost from a supplier's warehouse (2025) [L0966.034]:
    1. Get the zip code of the supplier's warehouse.
    2. Request sample shipping quotes from that warehouse to several random US zip codes, for example New Jersey to New York, Florida, Texas and California.
    3. Average the quotes to estimate the typical shipping cost per order.
    4. Use this average, not a single quote, when calculating margins.
  • Track the shipping cost against the margin before you cut prices. Shipping costs can quickly erase margin on heavy items, so apply the calculated average, not a single quote, when setting prices [L0966.034].
  • Shipping costs can jump sharply when a product crosses into a higher shipping size class, so even a one-inch increase in length can matter. Ask suppliers for the dimensions that define each shipping class and the packing materials they use before deciding how to box or group products (older, 2022) [L0963.155].
  • Enter accurate product shipping dimensions for every item, especially large ones. Wrong sizes can make shipping costs higher than needed [L1000.062].
  • Enter package sizes such as box dimensions in Shopify so that carrier-calculated shipping rates are accurate (Source D, 2026) [L0798.016].
  • Branded mailer boxes can be sourced from a friend's business in exchange for free packaging, with the parcel carrying that business's branding as part of the arrangement. This lowers packaging cost in the early phase of a small brand (Source C) [L0492.065].

Shopify Setup: Locations, Rates, Zones and Profiles

Locations and origin address

  • Settings > Locations: keep the default location unchanged and do not add supplier addresses. The location represents the business's billing location, not the suppliers [L0096.014].
  • Shipping origin address in Shopify: enter your business address or main fulfillment location. With free shipping offers (no shipping cost charged to the customer), this address does not affect shipping rate calculations and is primarily for documentation purposes (older, 2019) [L0243.001].

Shipping rates and free shipping

  • Settings > Shipping and delivery: create one domestic shipping rate with the country United States and the rate Free shipping. Customers then see Free Shipping at checkout. This setup applies when the store offers free shipping [L0096.012].
  • In the shipping rate editor, the Offer free shipping option makes a rate free, and checkout then shows the rate name with FREE instead of a price. A paid rate uses rate type Flat with a Price field (Source D, 2026) [L0798.020].
  • Settings > Shipping and delivery has a Shipping labels section for buying discounted shipping labels and managing preferred carriers. The Carrier accounts section lets you connect an existing carrier account so its own rates are used at checkout (Source D, 2026) [L0798.017].
  • Settings > Shipping and delivery > Additional delivery methods offers Local delivery and Pickup in store as options alongside regular shipping. The Sender name on shipping labels field is in the same section (Source D, 2026) [L0798.018].

Shipping profiles and product templates

  • Shopify shipping profiles segment which products ship from which regions or suppliers. Multiple profiles allow assigning Supplier A products to specific states and Supplier B products to others (2025) [L0278.006].
  • The Shipping profiles section has an Add custom profile button for creating an extra profile for specific products, which can have its own zones and rates. Products not assigned to a custom profile stay in the General profile, which is labeled as covering all products not in other profiles (Source D, 2026) [L0798.019].
  • Under Settings > Shipping and delivery > General profile > Packages, enter package sizes so that carrier-calculated shipping rates are accurate. The Packages field sits in the same profile view as the shipping zones and estimated delivery dates (Source D, 2026) [L0798.016].
  • Different suppliers have different shipping speeds and require different delivery windows. Create separate product templates for each supplier group, assign the correct template to products, and set estimated delivery to match that supplier's actual timeline [L0139.008].
  • Set a shipping profile for each supplier that lists its made-to-order lead time (see the made-to-order section below) [L0856.053].

Customs information in Shopify

  • The Customs information section in a Shopify product takes a Country/Region of origin, which is usually where the product is manufactured, and an HS (Harmonized System) code that can be searched by keyword. Border officers use these fields to calculate import duties on international orders, and they appear on the customs forms printed during fulfillment. Demo products can skip this section (older, 2021-22) [L0452.027].

Alaska, Hawaii and the continental states

Regional supplier assignment and multi-state profiles

  • See regional supplier assignment and shipping profiles above [L0278.040, L0278.006].

Warehouses in different countries

  • When a supplier stocks both a US warehouse and a China warehouse, US stock usually ships faster to US customers. Route orders to US stock where the product is available, and compare delivery times on both warehouses before choosing (Source C) [L0524.071].

Unsupported countries

  • For a country your supplier will not ship to, check the real shipping cost by entering the item's dimensions, weight and destination address before selling it (older, 2019) [L1002.063].
  • Shipping surcharge test for a country your supplier will not ship to (older, 2019) [L1002.064]:
    1. Track the actual extra cost per order, for example from reshipping through a relay address, and calculate the average.
    2. Add a surcharge for that country at about the average. Example: average cost $100, surcharge set to $120.
    3. Watch whether orders continue. If they do, the surcharge covers the cost. If they stop, you avoided losing money on shipping.

Country-specific notes

  • Fast delivery sells more in markets like Spain. Delivering within about 48 hours helps conversion, and cheap prices matter most for buyers there (Source C) [L0361.039].
  • Cross-border export: before shipping products from suppliers in other countries to your customers, ask each supplier how it handles export and cross-border shipping, because the process varies by supplier (older, 2019) [L0663.058].
  • For products made abroad with long delivery times, state on the product page where the product is made, what happens after the order, and the estimated delivery time, for example four weeks. Clear disclosure prevents most complaints (older, 2019) [L1086.034].

Handling Time, Transit Time and Delivery Estimates

Definitions

  • Handling time is the number of days between the customer placing an order and the item being processed and ready to ship, not the shipping duration. Dropshippers do not control this, so a single-day handling time is risky and a window should be used instead (older, 2021-22) [L0731.011].
  • Delivery expectations should separate average handling time, average transit time and typical total delivery time, each stated in business days. Giving all three figures sets accurate expectations for large purchases and reduces refund requests caused by surprise delays [L0541.004].

Claims about speed

  • Claims of same-day or one-day shipping on dropshipping stores are usually unrealistic, and many stores show three to five days shipping. Plan delivery times and customer messaging around realistic supplier shipping times instead of copying competitor claims (Source C) [L0372.025].

  • Lead times of 3 to 6 weeks are acceptable only if your top 2 to 3 competitors have the same lead time.   • Long shipping times are a problem only when they are not standard for the niche. In custom-made product niches, customers accept longer waits, but the custom order process should be clear to buyers before they purchase (older, 2021) [L0968.109].
  • Prefer suppliers with short shipping times. (See the supplier-selection rules above) [L0968.006].

Shipping Model: Supplier Account or Own Account

  • Ship through the supplier first, since suppliers usually offer the best shipping rates. If the supplier marks up shipping, ask for better rates or another service, then compare quotes from several shipping companies using the same shipment details. (Source A, 2020, older.) [L1277.035]
  • Choose products that suppliers can ship on their own account when they allow it. If an item is damaged or lost, the supplier sends the replacement and handles the insurance claim. (Source A, 2018.) [L1274.035]

Carriers and Who Books the Shipment

  • Ask a supplier which carrier they use for their top retailers, such as FedEx, UPS, DHL or a freight broker, and send shipping details accordingly. Ship to the customer's address from the supplier's account. (Source A, 2020, older.) [L1111.040]

Free Shipping and Pricing It In

  • Free shipping is the default offer in this dropshipping model because competitors offer it and customers expect it. The seller does not get shipping for free; the seller pays the supplier and absorbs that cost. [L1180.004]
  • Free shipping is offered to customers, and the supplier is paid the actual shipping cost. The store does not calculate shipping through Shopify in this model. (Source A, 2020, older.) [L1111.049]
  • Free shipping is offered only to the continental United States, not to Alaska, Hawaii or international destinations, because shipping there is very expensive. In other countries, international shipping is usually too costly to offer for free or to make available at all. [L1180.005]
  • Free shipping is an offer many stores overlook. Shoppers who want to save money will choose a free-shipping store over a similar store that charges delivery, so the store can absorb shipping costs to win more customers and stand out from competitors. [L1400.009]
  • Shipping to Canada from US-based suppliers is usually fine with free shipping, but at the start put most ad budget into US sales, since those produce the highest profit. (Source A, 2020, older.) [L1277.043]
  • Offering free shipping worldwide is only sustainable if the shipping cost is covered by the product margin or the supplier. Add the free shipping claim only after checking the cost per country. [L1250.024]
  • Shipping cost limits, two sources, both apply to the same goal:
    • Keep shipping costs at a maximum of 10 dollars, or make shipping free by building it into the product price, especially for lower-margin products under 50 dollars. [L1260.050]
    • For products under $50, keep shipping at a maximum of about 10% of the product price, or offer free shipping by including the shipping cost in the product price.   • Expedited shipping should cost no more than 15-20% of the product price, and high expedited fees should be avoided. [L1260.050, L1345.069]
  • Expedited shipping can be marked up for profit, but only offer it with suppliers who respond very quickly. If your supplier usually takes a day or two to respond, a second-day delivery promise puts you in a bad position. (Source A, undated.) [L1357.023]
  • Free shipping and white glove can be communicated on the product page, at the product price and near the add-to-cart button. Showing the estimated arrival date next to free shipping builds trust at the point of purchase. (Source A, 2022, older.) [L1281.006]
  • Ship trial or free-examination products from the warehouse or branch nearest the buyer, with shipping paid in advance. A shorter delivery distance shortens the time before the trial starts and keeps the buyer from losing interest. Prepaid shipping also removes a reason to delay. [L1369.124]

  • To give customers a local tracking number instead of a China-origin number, follow this procedure:

    1. Ask the supplier or agent to ship with a local carrier such as USPS, Royal Mail or Australia Post, and to hide the origin information.
    2. Ask them to send the local number to Shopify automatically once the courier issues it.
    3. If no local number appears quickly, send the original tracking number and ask them to hide the origin.

    The local number often appears the same day or in a few hours for destinations like UK, Germany, Australia and New Zealand, but may only appear on arrival in the destination country for others. [L1315.004]

  • A courier tracking number that is only the first leg (for example a China number) may not show delivery progress or days left. The local carrier number is needed for that, and tracking with a local carrier like FedEx or DHL shows the remaining days. [L1315.005]

  • Once an order ships, the customer receives a tracking number by email so they can follow the package. [L1250.025]

  • Procedure for Google Merchant Center shipping: (Source A, 2020, older.)

    1. Check the Google Merchant Center shipping settings.
    2. Locate the free shipping entry.
    3. Enter the average delivery timeframe for the whole catalog.

    The delivery time applies catalog-wide, not per product. [L1111.051]

Multi-Supplier and Add-On Shipments

  • Products sold as add-ons or upsells should come from the same supplier as the main product, so the order ships together without an extra shipping fee. Items from another source ship separately and need their own handling. (Source A, 2020, older.) [L1277.041]
  • Use a separate delivery template for each supplier whose window differs from the default. [L1192.024]
  • Each supplier's shipping arrangement must be recorded separately in the table above, because each one may book its own carrier. [L1291.001, L1291.002]

Shipping Insurance and Damage

  • Shipping insurance should always be included on every shipment. When the supplier ships from its own account, insurance is typically the default and the seller should confirm it in advance. (Source A, 2022, older.) [L1281.009]
  • Insurance and replacement responsibilities follow the account used: a supplier shipping on its own account handles replacements and claims, while a store shipping on its own account must file and wait for repayment. [L1274.035, L1274.036]

Customs, Duties and Cross-Border Shipments

  • When a product is shipped from stock in the same country, there are no extra import fees for the customer. Customs clearance only matters for cross-border shipments. (Source A, 2022, older.) [L1281.007]
  • For cross-border shipments, carriers such as FedEx, UPS and DHL usually handle customs clearance and contact the customer when duties must be paid. In practice the seller typically pays those fees. Some carriers were not verified for this. (Source A, 2022, older.) [L1281.008]
  • Delivery times can be affected by customs, weather and other factors outside the store's control. Naming these in the policy sets expectations and reduces complaints about delays. [L1250.026]
  • For international orders where product dimensions vary widely, the store can give shipping prices on request rather than publishing one rate. The customer confirms the quote before the order and payment are taken through a secure online payment gateway. This approach suits low-volume stores that cannot yet predict shipping cost per destination. (Source B, 2021-22, older.) [L1264.021]
  • Tell customers that international delivery can take longer than the stated estimate and that you only ship to selected countries. Customers should also receive a tracking number by email once the order ships. [L1250.025]
  • Customers pay customs fees, taxes and import duties on international orders, and the policy should state this. [L1327.060]

Shipping Policy and Customer-Facing Text

  • Shipping rates are calculated at checkout based on the customer's location and the shipping option chosen. The policy should say this instead of listing fixed prices that may not match the checkout. [L1250.023]
