17.3 Hiring, briefing and managing support staff and virtual assistants

When to outsource

  • Outsource customer service first, because a constant flow of negative messages can drain the owner, and even major brands receive negative feedback. [L0780.012]
  • Outsource customer support early. Answering customer questions can take a lot of time, especially with high order volume, and some days may bring hundreds of emails that overwhelm the owner. [L1360.019]
  • Answering customer emails can take three to four hours per day and blocks growth. A virtual assistant (VA) at $3 to $5 per hour can take over this work and return those hours to the owner. [L1127.034]
  • Typical daily tasks in an online store are answering live chat, phone and email questions, handling follow-up questions after a purchase, and processing incoming orders with suppliers until they ship. These tasks require human interaction and are usually the first candidates for delegation once they are understood. [L1447.004]
  • Outsource customer service only after you have seen call volumes, call times and how many calls turn into sales. A new hire in another time zone should be fully briefed before taking over. [L1277.093]
  • Do not outsource phone support until business profit can pay for it. Until then, use a voicemail with a clear message and return calls and emails the next day. [L0941.082]
  • Per-call phone answering services are not recommended at the start. The vendor example is only a starting point to see what is possible. [L0941.083]
  • Early on, answer live chat from the owner's phone when possible. Consider a paid 24/7 answering service only once the business is profitable and reinvesting profits. [L0967.091]
  • Hire your own employees for customer service and other tasks that need care and attention toward customers. Third-party agents have less interest in your customers and often cost about the same as a direct hire. [L1359.014]
  • Where sources differ on structure, use direct hires for work that needs care toward customers, and a virtual team for hours and time-zone coverage. [L1359.014, L0962.129, L0852.110]
  • Scope of support hours depends on three factors: product price (higher price means fewer sales and fewer support contacts), customer demographic (older customers generate more phone calls), and budget. [L0721.031]
  • Product knowledge questions can be handled by general support staff. Pre-purchase questions call for live chat and email coverage, which may give a competitive edge if others avoid it. (Older, 2019.) [L0663.003]
  • Prepare customer service before a major sales peak such as Black Friday. In the lesson's case, a team of three VAs was not enough when orders surged, and tickets fell four to five days behind. Train extra helpers in advance, even family or a VA at around 3 USD per hour. [L1363.043]

Where to find virtual assistants

  • onlinejobs.ph is the go-to platform for hiring virtual staff to answer customer queries in real time when the owner is in a different time zone. Job posting, qualifying, hiring and training guidance is covered in the outsourcing module of the course. (2024.) [L0666.006]
  • Staff customer support with VAs in regions with reverse working hours, such as the Philippines, to cover different time zones. Hire them through job platforms such as onlinejobs.ph. (2021.) [L0962.129]
  • Customer support can be outsourced to a virtual team in the Philippines, which is a major growth step. Support covers live chat, phone and email. (Older, 2020.) [L0852.110]

Hiring and vetting

  • When hiring customer service staff, look for people with similar past experience who can provide a reference. Treat the job as a real position even though it is virtual. (2023.) [L0970.135]
  • For a store selling to US customers, the person answering calls should be a native speaker with an American or Canadian accent. Customers then assume the business is a normal domestic company. Accents from some outsourcing regions can reduce trust for this audience. [L0910.060]

Onboarding a new hire

  • Set up the assistant in this order:
    1. Give access to the support mailbox, sales and orders. [L1173.032]
    2. Write standard operating procedures and reply templates. [L1173.032]
    3. Hold a weekly call to review work. [L1173.032]
    4. Update the instructions in one batch rather than sending small changes continuously. [L1173.032]
  • When hiring fulfillment or design help, give clear, complete instructions, since vague briefs lead to unwanted results. Schedule calls regularly and do not wait for the assistant to start the conversation. [L1173.033]
  • Onboarding a customer service or fulfillment hire (older, 2020):
    1. Document every customer service and fulfillment step for each supplier and each scenario. [L1277.094]
    2. Set up an open communication channel with the new person, such as Slack or Facebook Messenger. [L1277.094]
    3. Expect questions for the first few weeks, possibly up to a month, because the new hire will not know every answer yet. [L1277.094]
    4. Keep answering questions until the process runs smoothly. [L1277.094]

Scope of the role

  • A virtual assistant for customer service handles three areas: visitor questions before purchase, customer updates after purchase, and order processing. Order processing means contacting the supplier to place the order after a customer order comes in. It does not mean taking orders by email from customers. [L1430.002]
  • VAs can handle order processing, tracking and routine customer questions without expert skills, since many customers mainly want to hear a real person. Phone support is often the first role to hand off. [L1515.031]

Phone coverage and time zones

  • Responding to customer queries instantly raises conversion. An owner in a distant time zone should outsource real-time responses rather than rely on a night shift schedule, since working through the night is unhealthy over the long term. [L0666.007]
  • When operating from a distant time zone, staff phone support first, then email support, with people who work during your customers' business hours (US business hours in the example). Do not answer calls yourself at night. [L1515.032]
  • Answering support calls personally at 3 or 4 a.m. because of a time-zone gap is not sustainable. Plan staffing before sales volume grows. [L1515.033]
  • Split phone support and chat or email support into separate people only when volume grows beyond what one person can handle within the response targets. [L1111.126]

Setting up a first phone helper (small business):

  1. Forward the business phone line to the helper's personal mobile. [L0337.005]
  2. Pay per answered call instead of a fixed salary. [L0337.005]
  3. Greet callers with "Hi, can I help you?" [L0337.005]
  4. Take a message and ask the caller's questions. [L0337.005]
  5. Tell the caller that a product specialist will call back. [L0337.005]

Access rights and permissions

  • Give VAs access only to the support mailbox and the sales and order data they need, not to the entire personal inbox. Keep the owner's personal inbox separate from customer complaints. [L1173.031]
  • A VA can independently approve discounts up to 10%. Any discount above 10%, or any other customer credit, requires owner approval. [L0230.021]

Inbox labels and email triage

  • Colour-code Gmail labels by priority so the assistant knows which emails to handle first. Customer issues get a red label, since they are the most urgent. [L1000.063]
  • An overnight inbox may hold around 20 emails, and an urgent $20,000 order could be buried among them. Priority labels make sure the assistant handles that order before routine messages. [L1000.064]
  • Keep customer issues and sales inquiries in dedicated Gmail labels, not mixed into the general inbox, so they can be found later. Customer issues get a red label so they are actioned first. [L1000.068]

Order boards

Order Processing board (card per order):

  1. Order Sent: record the product name and shipping address, then send the order to the manufacturer. [L1396.016]
  2. Once the manufacturer confirms the item is in stock, move the card forward. [L1396.016]
  3. If the item is on backorder, notify the customer first and only then move the card to Backorder with a due date. [L1396.016]
  4. Move the card to Shipped once fulfilled, and record the tracking number. [L1396.016]
  5. Move the card to Claims and Damages when a claim or damage notice arrives, and add notes about its progress. [L1396.016]
  • Keep cancelled orders on the board instead of deleting them, because the same customer may call back and the quote information may still be needed. Put a due date on backorders so they are not forgotten. Move cancelled orders to a cancelled list, not deleted. [L1396.017]
  • If a replacement item is out of stock, notify the customer that a new item is coming but is temporarily unavailable. Keep the card in the Claims and Damages list with a due date, so the claim stays visible until the replacement ships. Add status notes to the card with each update. [L1396.018]
  • Shopify order notes alone are hard to track once there are many backorders. Staff must scroll through each order and cannot see due dates. Track order status on a visual board with due dates on each card. This matters once volume reaches about 30 to 50 orders per month. Keeping important customer details only in individual Shopify orders makes information hard to find as volume grows, and the owner has to scroll through orders and cannot see all pending items together. A separate board for order tracking solves this. [L1396.019, L1396.063]
  • Without a shared board, backorders and customer updates get lost. One customer waited since July, and the follow-up depended on memory. Lost notes from calls and chats also cause missed quotes. Record every customer request on a board that everyone can see. [L1396.020]

Asana order board (automated order flow):

  1. A new order arrives in the store and is automatically added to the first column of the Asana order board, and the help desk sends a notification. [L1473.010]
  2. The VA waits for the outside fraud-screening service ClearSale to approve the order. [L1473.010]
  3. After approval, the VA forwards the order to the appropriate supplier or places it on the supplier's website and waits for confirmation. [L1473.010]
  4. When the supplier confirms stock, the VA charges the customer's card in Shopify, since the card was only authorized at checkout. [L1473.010]
  5. The VA moves the card to the confirmed column in Asana. [L1473.010]
  6. When the order ships, the VA adds the tracking number in Shopify and updates the status in Asana. [L1473.010]
  7. The VA marks the order complete in Asana with a check mark. [L1473.010]
  8. If a shipped order later has a problem, the check mark is removed and the card is moved back to the column that matches the new issue. [L1473.010]

Backorders on the Asana board:

  1. When a supplier reports a backorder, the VA gets the backorder date from the supplier. [L1473.011]
  2. The VA records that date in Asana. [L1473.011]
  3. The VA moves the order card to the backorder column. [L1473.011]
  4. The VA informs the customer about the backorder using a ready reply template in the help desk. [L1473.011]

Damaged items and returns on the Asana board:

  1. For a damaged item or a return, the VA asks the supplier for their damage or return procedure. [L1473.012]
  2. The VA explains that procedure to the customer. [L1473.012]
  3. The VA updates the order status in Asana. [L1473.012]

Standard operating procedures and templates

Support workflow with a VA:

  1. Create reply templates for the most common messages, such as where is my package, when will it arrive and where is my tracking number. [L0519.040]
  2. Translate messages with DeepL (free online tool) when the assistant does not speak the customer's language. [L0519.040]
  3. Review the assistant's drafts before sending them, especially for general messages. [L0519.040]
  4. Keep separate templates for each target market. [L0519.040]
  • Routine questions get a direct reply from the assistant. For unusual cases, the assistant sends the email screenshot to the founder, who dictates the response. [L1432.037]
  • Staff have first responsibility for monitoring and answering emails to the business's customer email addresses. The owner monitors these accounts in the staff member's absence. Emails addressed to the owner by name must be forwarded to the owner's personal email. [L0404.015]

Escalation when the answer is unknown

  • When you cannot answer a customer's question by phone, chat or email, do one or more of the following, as relevant, in this order:
    1. Contact the business owner through the agreed contact methods. If the owner is available, they will either answer the question or ask you to forward the customer to them. [L0404.009]
    2. If the owner is not immediately available, tell the customer you will get back to them shortly, take their contact details, and reply as soon as you can. [L0404.009]
    3. If the owner is not available and you need product information, first check the supplier's webpage. If the information is not there, call the supplier and ask. [L0404.009]
    4. Do not rush. Reply to the customer once you have the correct information, since a short delay of around 10 minutes is acceptable. [L0404.009]
  • To find product information for a customer, the VA follows this order (Source A, undated):
    1. Search Google for the answer. [L1430.004]
    2. If the answer is not found, take the customer's question and contact details, and pass them to a sales rep contact number or email. [L1430.004]
    3. The sales rep then calls the customer back once the needed information is available. [L1430.004]

Agent training and knowledge base

  • Customer service agents are usually the customer's first contact with the company, so improving agent training is typically the first and most powerful step to raise overall satisfaction. [L0401.009]
  • Build an internal knowledge base or wiki for agents containing a guide to the brand voice, canned responses for common scenarios such as angry customers and refund requests, and upselling scripts. [L0401.010]
  • Support agents hear the most direct customer complaints, so ask the support team for their view on what causes low satisfaction. Their input on customer needs is one of the most useful sources for changing how support is delivered and for improving the wider purchase experience. [L0401.018]
  • Check customer questions and contact requests by reviewing chat and phone activity during the first months of a new hire's training, and send corrections privately as needed. Watching the chat app is the simplest way to see the answers given to customers. This helps catch misspellings and rude replies before customers notice. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1396.064]

Quality rubric

  • A quality rubric for support replies defines the key elements a high-quality response must have and gives an objective framework for scoring response quality. Typical criteria are response time, alignment with company policy, brand tone and voice, plus any other elements the business considers essential. [L0401.011]
  • Judge the rubric against the actual customer outcome too. A response can score well on tone (for example, playful humor) yet still fail to produce a clear, satisfying interaction. [L0401.011]
