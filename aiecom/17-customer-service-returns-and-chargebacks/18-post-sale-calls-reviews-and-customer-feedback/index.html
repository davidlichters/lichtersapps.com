17.18 Post-sale calls, reviews and customer feedback

Why post-sale contact matters

  • Most e-commerce stores see only about 1%, 5% or 10% of customers leave a review. A structured post-sale call program can raise the review rate to roughly 50% or more. [L1011.001]
  • Post-sale calls serve two goals at once. They make the customer feel looked after by checking that their needs are met and their questions answered, and they bring in reviews and feedback for the business. [L1011.002]
  • Post-sale calls reveal what customers expect, want, or would like improved. This can be information the business did not have before. [L1011.003]
  • A customer who receives an unexpected, helpful follow-up call is more likely to talk about the business and to buy again. Sending upsell messages before or just after the call strengthens this effect. [L1011.004]
  • Reviews will not come on their own. The business has to ask for them actively, most effectively by phone. Relying on passive hope is not an effective approach. [L1012.009]
  • Asking customers for feedback on the business reveals improvement opportunities and is often a core part of conversion rate optimization. It also makes customers feel they have input, which increases their mental investment and the chance they buy again. [L1052.001]
  • Customer feedback will not arrive on its own. Ask for it proactively, every time you want to learn something about the business. [L1052.002]
  • Feedback can point to website usability problems, content gaps customers want filled, weak customer service, or issues with how products are delivered. Targeted customer answers show where the business can grow. [L1052.003]
  • Track customer feedback and queries to find weak points that are not visible in analytics. Be proactive about shipping delays or site issues. [L1259.053]
  • After-sale service (refunds, returns, damages and defects) shapes the feedback score and loyal customers. Be proactive about shipping delays and monitor customer feedback for product or page issues. [L1260.054]
  • Request reviews from happy customers consistently, and track the number of reviews relative to sales volume. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0962.130]

Timing the contact

Who makes the calls and how the routine runs

  • Assign the calls to yourself, using calendar reminders, or to a customer service virtual assistant (VA) who performs them during gaps between inbound support calls. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0203.007]
  • Making the calls yourself gives you a much better understanding of your market and customers. The insights can improve other marketing, content ideas, and emails that answer common questions. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1011.020]
  • Run the post-sale review call as a routine for every new customer, rather than picking only customers who seem likely to review. The consistent routine raises the review rate; a selected sample does not. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1011.022]
  • Do not place the review call to customers known to be unhappy. About 95% or more of customers are usually not disgruntled, so the call is safe to make to most of the customer base. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1011.017]

Setting up the review multiplier call system (older advice, 2021-22) [L0399.013]:

  1. Decide whether you or your customer service team makes the calls.
  2. Share the call script with the team.
  3. Have the team schedule calls to customers at the appropriate time after purchase, to collect reviews.

Review call process

Review collection process, in order (older advice, 2021-22) [L1011.007]:

  1. Develop a call script first, based on a template and adapted to your own circumstances.
  2. Offer the customer several ways to leave a review.
  3. Ask customers who are very positive for a video review.
  4. Have your team manage the process.
  5. Track which reviews were gained, and follow up with customers who committed to a review but have not left one.

Delivered-order review follow-up, Trello workflow [L0286.025]:

  1. Identify delivered orders not yet reviewed in Trello's "Delivered/Pending Review" list.
  2. Call the customer and ask about product experience and satisfaction.
  3. Request specific content: text review, product photo, or video.
  4. If the customer hesitates, offer an incentive (discount code, gift card, or item).
  5. Collect the content and confirm the incentive via follow-up email.
  6. Move the card to "Reviewed" and document in a Trello comment the content type and date.

Scheduled follow-up call, review and testimonial [L0286.035]:

  1. Schedule a follow-up call 3-5 days after delivery confirmation.
  2. Call the customer and ask about product experience, quality, and satisfaction.
  3. Request specific content: text review, product photo, video, or testimonial.
  4. If the customer declines, offer an incentive (discount code, gift card, or physical item).
  5. Collect the content via email or directly document the response.
  6. Send an incentive confirmation and thank-you message.
  7. Add a Trello comment with the completion date and content type received.

Review call script

Use the script as a guide to content only, and speak in your own natural language. Fill the [brackets] before the call. (Older advice, 2021-22.)

1. Greeting: Hi [customer first name], this is [agent name] from [store name]. I am calling to thank you for your order of [product name].
2. Check-in: I just wanted to make sure it arrived in good condition and that it is working the way you expected. Are you happy with it?
3a. If the customer is happy: That is great to hear. Feedback from customers really helps us serve you and others better. Would you be willing to leave us a quick two-minute review? Then send the text review link [review link]. If you like, you can also leave a short video or send a photo with the product. Our video review tool [VideoPeel link] makes that a few clicks on your phone.
3b. If the customer is not happy: Thank you for telling me, I am sorry about [issue]. Let me fix that now: [resolution options]. Once it is sorted, would you be open to sharing your experience in a review?
4. Close: Thank you for your time and for being a [store name] customer. You will get a short follow-up email with the review link [and reward details, if any].

Follow-up email: send it within [number of hours] of the call. Restate the thank-you, include the text review link and the video or photo link, and state any reward terms [e.g. 10% off a future order or store credit for a text review; a larger reward for a video or photo review].

[L1012.025]

  • Use the call to mention other retention programs, such as loyalty and referral programs, so customers learn about them while they are already positive about the business. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1011.021]

Review options and rewards

  • Offer four review options during the call: dictating the review over the phone, emailing the review text to the business, following a review link from the review app to post it online, or recording a video review. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1011.008]
  • Ask customers who are very positive for a video review. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1011.007]
  • For a post-purchase customer testimonial, send an email within 24 hours of purchase offering a refund in exchange for a video testimonial submitted through a form. The form must collect first and last name, the video upload, and a checkbox giving permission to use the content in ads. [L0789.034]
  • Offer an incentive when a customer hesitates or declines, as in the procedures above. Do not offer incentives in a way that makes the review inauthentic. See the integrity rules below. [L0286.025, L0286.035, L0243.044]

Review request emails and messages

Review request, short delivery check (Source A, 2024). The note in this template says to keep the message to one or two sentences, to include no link to a review app, and not to ask for a photo or video [L0965.072]:

Subject: [store name] order #[order number]

Hi [customer name],

I see your order arrived on [delivery date]. I would really appreciate any feedback on the product and your experience ordering from [store name].

Thank you,
[your name]

Notes: Keep this message to one or two sentences. Do not include a link to a review app, and do not ask for a photo or video.

[L0965.072]

Review request, follow-up after delivery (Source A, 2024) [L1115.107]:

Hi [customer first name], I saw that your order was delivered, and I wanted to make sure it arrived in good condition and matches what you expected. Please reply and let me know how your experience was, since I am the owner of [store name] and your feedback reaches me directly. If you are happy with it, would you mind leaving a review here: [review link]? If you know anyone who might like our products, we would be glad to take care of them as well. Thank you, [your name]

[L1115.107]

Review request, delivery check with photo request (Source A, 2020, older) [L1111.130]:

Hi [Customer name], I just saw that your order was delivered, and I wanted to check in. I hope you are loving it. Is everything okay with your order? If you are happy with it, would you be willing to leave a short review, and send photos or a video if you have them?

[L1111.130]

Review request, after-delivery conversation (Source A, 2024) [L0292.009]:

Hi [customer first name], I wanted to follow up on the [product name] you received a few days ago. I'm curious-how are you enjoying it? How was your experience with us? [Listen to response] That's wonderful to hear! Since we're building our reputation, I'd really appreciate if you could leave us a review on our site. Would you also be willing to send us a photo with the product? Thank you so much for your business.

[L0292.009]

Personal review request email procedure (Source A, 2024) [L1115.071]:

  1. Send a personal email to each customer after delivery, not an automated one.
  2. Ask whether the product matches what they expected and whether their experience was good.
  3. Ask for a review by linking to the review page.
  4. If the customer replies with a positive experience but leaves no review after a set period, you may use that email as a review on the store, with permission.
  • The sources give two different approaches to the review link in the request message. The 2024 Source A template [L0965.072] says to include no link, and the 2024 Source A template [L1115.107] includes the review link. Both are recorded here; the sources do not state which applies in which situation. [L0965.072, L1115.107]

Review request channels and volume

  • Review requests can reach customers through post-purchase email automation, phone calls, text messages, or photo submission requests. Phone calls generate the highest engagement and personal connection, which is especially valuable in the early stage when building social proof. Email automation handles volume; calls handle relationship-building. [L0292.011]

Review counts and content

  • Minimum review count to launch: 10 reviews in total, with 6 to 7 of them image-based. Image reviews convert significantly better than text reviews. [L0321.032]
  • Only use reviews that belong to the store, such as existing reviews from the supplier with permission. Never copy reviews from competitor sites. [L1088.075]

Increasing review count with CSV import (not recommended) [L0321.033]:

  1. Import initial reviews from AliExpress or Amazon via Riview or Loox app.
  2. Download the CSV file of imported reviews.
  3. Copy individual reviews and duplicate them 10x within the CSV.
  4. For example, take 10 reviews and duplicate each 10 times to create a list of 100 reviews.
  5. Re-import the expanded CSV into the app.
  6. Customers see the higher review count and trust the product, even though they rarely check beyond the first 10-20 reviews.
  • This procedure is documented here as the source gives it, but it produces copied, fabricated reviews. It conflicts with the honest-review rules and must not be used. [L0321.033, L0243.044, L1088.075, L1188.058]

Authentic reviews and review integrity

  • Build product reviews honestly, without fabrication. When a customer makes a purchase, follow up relentlessly to request a review. Authentic customer reviews build market credibility faster than hiding "no reviews yet" or creating fake reviews. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0243.044]
  • Changing reviewer names, genders, or avatars on borrowed or template reviews to make them look real turns them into made-up testimonials. Use only real reviews with the names or initials the customer actually gave. [L1188.058]
  • Look at the reviews on the product page for negative or suspicious entries. Identify reviews from people who did not buy the product, which are often posted by competitors or haters. Remove or do not approve those reviews, and keep genuine feedback visible. [L0657.043]

Review dates

  • Review dates can be omitted if you are concerned that older reviews make the product look outdated. Alternatively, keep dates visible, because they show product longevity and an established sales history. The sources give both options; choose by the product's situation. [L0253.005]

Ratings and platform risk

  • Meta may ask buyers about their experience about a week after purchase and collect a rating. If the store's ratings fall below a threshold, the ad account can be banned. This risk is mostly seen with very high-volume stores that have many poor ratings. The source does not give the threshold. [L0649.053]

Post-sale feedback surveys

  • A post-purchase survey should contain about five questions. One to three questions also work, but five balances getting people to engage against making the survey too long to finish. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1052.004]
  • Survey questions must be relevant to customers, answerable by them, and produce meaningful feedback on what the business actually wants to learn. Rotate the questions over time as your open questions change. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1052.005]
  • Ask only a few questions per call so the caller is not burdened. [L0682.006]

Set up the post-purchase feedback system, in this order (older advice, 2021-22) [L1052.017]:

  1. Create a post-purchase survey using a tool such as Google Forms, SurveyMonkey, GoSurvey, or Typeform, with about five questions chosen from a template list or written yourself.
  2. Set up an automated email that sends the survey to customers at your chosen point after purchase.
  3. Define the reward for responses and set up the thank-you message, either a template email or a video.

Thanking respondents

  • Thank every customer who responds to the survey, ideally with a personal message. Even a short email reply saying the response was seen and naming the reward is enough. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1052.012]

Thank-you follow-up process (older advice, 2021-22) [L1052.014]:

  1. Receive the survey response, which includes the respondent's name and email.
  2. Trigger the thank-you message for that person, either a pre-recorded Bonjoro message or a reply email.
  3. Mention that the reward is available and where to claim it.

Call-based customer questions

Customer service survey during calls (older advice, 2021-22) [L0682.005]:

  1. Let the caller first get their problem solved or receive the information they called for.
  2. Then say you have a couple of quick questions that are asked of everyone who calls.
  3. Ask whether they noticed anything not working on the site; if yes, ask what it was.
  4. Ask whether they looked at other sites while researching the product; if yes, ask which ones.
  5. Ask which of those they liked better and why, and what they liked about them.
  6. Record the answers in the Q&A tracking sheet.
  • Asking questions during a pre-purchase call is especially valuable because the buying decision is still fresh in the caller's mind. Ask only a few questions per call so the caller is not burdened. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0682.006]

Tracking reviews and feedback

  • Record every customer who was called, who had a conversation, and who promised a review, and whether the review was actually posted. Follow up with customers who committed but have not yet left the review. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1011.013]
  • Track the number of reviews relative to sales volume, and request reviews from happy customers consistently. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0962.130]
  • Use a Q&A tracking sheet (Google Sheets, or any tool such as Trello or Asana) to record customer service answers, especially when virtual assistants handle service. It must be built into the customer service SOPs, and access must be given to the right people. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0682.021]
  • Document each completed review in Trello with a comment that records the content type and date. [L0286.025, L0286.035]

CSAT: measurement and analysis

  • CSAT data is in Gorgias under the Statistics section, in the Satisfaction view. It shows a chronological list of tickets, including ratings of 4 stars or lower. The Satisfaction dashboard has a CSV download button to export all CSAT scores. [L0401.005]
  • CSAT ratings alone have limited value. The written customer comments, especially negative ones, explain what to change in customer service. [L0401.008]
  • CSAT is shaped by what happens before and after purchase, not only by support. Clear product descriptions set expectations; good product photography, faster and more reliable shipping, free shipping for eligible customers, and a helpful post-purchase experience all raise satisfaction. [L0401.012]
  • When a customer gives a low CSAT score without an explanation, proactively reach out and ask for written feedback. This reveals why the rating was given, what the customer expected, and how to prevent lower scores in the future. [L0401.007]

Review negative CSAT responses in this order [L0401.004]:

  1. Read and audit the low-rated responses, not just the positive ones.
  2. Identify recurring themes, such as long wait times, confusing information, or unresolved product issues.
  3. Tally how many tickets mention each recurring issue.
  4. Rank issues by tally count, since the more tallies an issue has, the higher its impact on satisfaction.
  5. Fix the highest-impact issues first, for example through agent training or automation for faster replies.

Acting on feedback

  • Post-sale calls reveal improvement points that may not have been known before. Use them to adjust customer service, product information, and delivery communication. [L1011.003, L1259.053]
  • Insights from calling customers yourself can improve other marketing, content ideas, and emails that answer common questions. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1011.020]
  • Feedback can point to usability problems, missing content, weak customer service, or delivery issues. Act on the answers that recur across customers. [L1052.003]
