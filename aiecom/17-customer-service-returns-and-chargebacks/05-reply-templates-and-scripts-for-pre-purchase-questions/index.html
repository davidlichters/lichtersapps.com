17.5 Reply templates and scripts for pre-purchase questions

Speed and first response before purchase

  • Being the first store to answer a pre-purchase question often decides the sale. Fast responses are a direct sales lever, and speed and quality of service can beat competitors who sell the same product. [L0948.001]
  • Replying within minutes can raise sales, because a quick answer to a product question can convert a visitor into a buyer. Check messages from your phone so questions are not left waiting. [L0661.049]
  • Shoppers who call before buying to check stock or get an answer often choose the store that responds. Answering the phone can win sales that other stores lose. [L0966.094]
  • Phone calls from prospective customers, such as asking whether an item is in stock, are a normal part of selling higher-priced products. Answer or return them as soon as possible. [L0835.103]
  • Pre-purchase questions from website visitors typically arrive by phone, live chat or email. They are a daily task to automate or delegate. Post-purchase follow-up questions come through the same channels and need the same handling. [L0592.004]
  • Shoppers fear buying online and then receiving no answer from customer service. Only promise fast customer service if you can actually reply quickly. [L1207.018]

Answering known questions in chat

  • When an answer is known, give it right away in the chat to close the sale. Shoppers who ask pre-purchase questions should receive a fast reply without extra effort from the store owner. [L0948.018]
  • Example: a shopper asks how fast stickers ship, and the store replies "They ship within two to three days." Quick, specific shipping times answer purchase hesitation directly. [L0948.019]
  • Most customer service questions are simple and concern delivery time, shipping time or discounts. Prepare clear answers to these first. [L0909.021] (Older advice, 2021-2022.)
  • Pre-written answers can be stored in the live chat app, so customers get an immediate reply about a specific product, including shipping time and usage. This is useful when you expect repeated questions about one product. [L1428.002]
  • Reply to a chat question you cannot answer yet with the following text, then send the answer as a follow-up once it is ready:
When a chat question comes in and you don't know the answer yet, respond right away by saying it's a good question, that you're looking into it, and that you'll reply as soon as possible. Once the answer is ready, send it as a follow-up.

[L0948.017]

Handling a question you cannot answer yet

Standard procedure (phone or chat)

  1. Receive a customer question you cannot answer immediately. [L0276.040]
  2. Respond: "That's a great question. I want to make sure you get an accurate answer. May I have your name, email, and phone number?" [L0276.040]
  3. Contact your supplier with the customer's exact question. [L0276.040]
  4. Collect the supplier's answer. [L0276.040]
  5. Reply to the customer with the accurate response. [L0276.040]
  6. Update your product page with this information for future customers. [L0276.040]

Supplier-verified variant

  1. Tell the customer you want to confirm the answer and get the correct information. [L0972.042]
  2. Collect their name, email address and phone number if the question came by phone. [L0972.042]
  3. Ask your supplier rep the question directly, stating the customer's needs and quantities. [L0972.042]
  4. Send the verified answer to the customer. [L0972.042]
  5. If the question is likely to come up again, add the answer to the product page as an FAQ or in the description. [L0972.042]

Use this reply when a customer asks a product question you cannot answer yet:

That's a great question, and I want to make sure I give you the correct answer. Could you please send me your name, email address and phone number? I will check directly with the manufacturer and get right back to you.

[L0972.043]

Product question not answerable from the product page

  1. Tell the customer you do not have the answer right now, and take their name, phone number and email. [L0805.031]
  2. Contact the supplier rep (or email the supplier) with the product model number and the customer's question. [L0805.031]
  3. Call the customer back or reply with the answer. [L0805.031]
  4. Add the answer to the product page so future customers see it. [L0805.031]

Older procedure (2020-2021)

Treat these as older advice. The steps remain valid, but the wording is dated.

Procedure, 2020 (older):

  1. Tell the customer that it is a great question and that you want to make sure you give the right answer. [L0852.108]
  2. Collect the customer's name, phone number and email address. [L0852.108]
  3. Contact the supplier to get the answer. [L0852.108]
  4. Add the answer to your website, so future customers can find it. [L0852.108]
  5. Reply to the customer with the correct information and try to close the sale. [L0852.108]

Procedure, 2021 (older):

  1. Respond promptly: "That's a great question. Let me get you the answer." [L0270.059]
  2. Contact the supplier for the answer. [L0270.059]
  3. Provide the supplier's answer to the customer. [L0270.059]
  4. Capture that information and add it to the product page so future shoppers see the answer without asking. [L0270.059]

Principles for supplier questions

  • Expect customer questions on products you did not know before. Ask the supplier for the answer, then add frequent questions to the product page. [L0806.083]
  • Each store owner needs to build product knowledge in the niche. Questions from customers should be answered by checking with the supplier, then published on the product page. [L0805.032]
  • Phone and support answers for product questions should follow a lesson script. A sales phone call should never end without a follow-up: the customer should be called back with an answer rather than left waiting. [L0971.078]

Responding to pre-purchase hesitation

  • Using fear, threats or crime statistics to push a purchase repels most buyers. Instead, let the buyer recognize the need, then show the product points they care about most, such as reliability and ease of installation. [L1366.068]

FAQ building and product-page content

Building the FAQ

  1. Answer every customer question by email or phone, then add that question and answer to the FAQ. [L0910.025]
  2. Keep building the FAQ over the first months of the store. [L0910.025]
  3. Make new customer service staff read and memorize the FAQ during onboarding, because these are the questions they will receive. [L0910.025]
  4. Link customers to the FAQ so they can find answers without calling or emailing. [L0910.025]
  5. Where possible, add the answer to the product description or to a staff note so the question does not come up again. [L0910.025]

What the FAQ page should cover

  • The FAQ page should collect the questions customers ask repeatedly. Cover price guarantee, product guarantee, shipping policy, returns and support. If one visitor has a question, many others probably do too. [L1130.012]

Preparing support for a team

The following two units describe the same procedure and are merged here.

  1. List the questions customers are likely to ask, both before and after purchase. [L1358.018, L1360.021]
  2. Write a clear, polished answer for each question. [L1358.018, L1360.021]
  3. Put the questions and answers into PDFs (compile them into a PDF document). [L1358.018, L1360.021]
  4. Let the support team copy and paste the answers to customers. [L1358.018, L1360.021]

Turning questions into product-page content

  • Turn customer questions into product page updates so future visitors find the answers without calling. [L0934.181]
  • Product pages should give customers all information they need before buying, so the store reduces customer inquiries over time. [L0964.111]
  • Write detailed FAQs on each product page for every recurring customer question, so the answer is available before the customer has to ask. [L1515.034]
  • Write content that answers questions a buyer has before buying, such as whether to offer a phone number, and link those questions to your products. This keeps the blog focused on purchase decisions rather than general interest. [L0382.051]

B2B and wholesale discount requests

  • Standard reply for a B2B or wholesale buyer who wants a discount: create a multi-use discount code named after the business, give it only to that buyer, and set the percent off as the B2B price. [L0472.054]

Reply template:

Thank you for your question. That's a great question, and I want to make sure I give you the correct answer. Could you please send me your name, email address and phone number? I will look into this and get back to you with the answer as soon as I have it.

[L0472.077]

MAP discount requests

Procedure for customers who request a discount on products with strict MAP policies:

  1. Acknowledge their request professionally. [L0251.036]
  2. Explain that the price is set by the manufacturer to protect brand value and that this is industry-wide. [L0251.036]
  3. Offer alternative value: same-day shipping if possible, expedited service, or reference other benefits. [L0251.036]
  4. If you cannot offer discounts or special terms, maintain confidence in the price and emphasize service quality and expertise. [L0251.036]

Expired promotions

  • Honor a legitimately expired promotion when the customer has a real reason for missing it. Decline expired codes requested from old archives or without a reason. [L0834.088] (Older, 2021.)
