17.4 Support tone, soft skills and handling difficult customers

Support mindset and tone

  • Customer service drives both repeat purchases and five-star reviews. Reviews act as a trust factor that raises the website conversion rate, so service quality directly affects sales. Communication should always be quick, truthful and friendly, across phone, email, chat and social media. [L0404.017]
  • Empathy is the most important skill in customer service (older advice, 2021-22). Understand the customer's situation from their side, listen fully, and reflect back what you heard before you act. [L0909.019]
  • Stay friendly and patient, even with rude customers (older advice, 2021-22). Basic courtesy, such as not talking over the customer and not ignoring their message, matters more than any technique. [L0909.020]
  • Greet every customer warmly, ask what problem you can solve for them, and build a genuine personal connection. Customers who feel cared for are more understanding when problems appear during the order. [L1472.002]
  • Remember details from earlier conversations, such as names and past topics, and use them in later contacts. This familiarity matters most for repeat buyers. [L1472.003]
  • Keep a consistently positive tone with every customer, because a competitor selling the same product is often only one click away. Service is what sets a store apart when the product and price are similar. [L1472.004]
  • Be empathetic by imagining you are the customer waiting for a product that is four weeks late with no clear date. Frustration is natural in that situation, so acknowledge it in every reply. [L1472.011]
  • Even when a delay is caused by a supplier, carrier or processing time outside your control, you remain responsible for solving it. Respond with empathy and focus on the solution. [L1472.005]
  • Customers often accept a long wait or a problem as long as you communicate regularly and stay polite. Rudeness is the main thing that makes customers leave, not the problem itself. [L1472.013]
  • Service that does not lead to a sale still has value, because it builds goodwill and a human connection. Listening to a customer who needs to talk can keep them loyal for future purchases. [L1472.012]
  • Do not sell products you do not carry, but still listen to the customer and help where you can. Being a supportive point of contact can keep the customer loyal even when the sale does not happen. [L1472.061]
  • Treat your customers with respect and aim to improve their lives, since the best dropshipping businesses excel at service. Customer relationships are where you can separate yourself from competitors. [L1480.028]

Chat and proactive contact

  • Staff may start chats with website visitors when they have time, but this is optional. [L0404.013]
  • If a visitor does not reply to the first message, do not send a follow-up, so customers do not feel harassed. [L0404.013]

Regional communication styles

  • Customer service replies should match each market's communication style. Separate reply templates are needed even within the same language. [L0519.039]

Never guess, and admit what you don't know

  • Never guess or make up answers to customer questions. Give information only once it has been confirmed through the owner or the supplier. [L0404.010]
  • Never invent an answer to a product or technical question (older advice, 2021-22). If the customer receives wrong information and finds out after delivery, you will have to pay to fix the problem. [L0909.022]
  • If you do not know a product detail, do not guess. Tell the customer you will check with the manufacturer and call back with accurate information, then use the answer to update your product page. [L0934.180]
  • Admitting when you do not know an answer and following up with the correct information builds trust. Customers appreciate accurate information, and the owner gains a stronger knowledge base in the niche over time. [L1094.093]

Phone reply when you do not know the answer (Source A, 2023):

Good question, [customer name]. I don't know the answer at the moment, so I'll consult our team and return your call as quickly as possible. May I have your phone number and email address?

[L0934.179]

Use this reply on the phone when you do not know the answer to a customer's question. Replace each bracketed placeholder and keep the order of the steps:

That is a great question. I do not have the exact answer right now, but I would like to reach out to our partner at [brand or partner name] so I can get you the correct information. Can I get your name, phone number and email address? I will follow up with you as soon as I have the answer, because I do not want to give you incorrect information.

[L1094.092]

Handling a question you cannot answer (older advice, 2021-22)

  1. Tell the customer you need to check the answer, and do not guess.
  2. Offer a callback within a stated time, such as five or ten minutes, or put them on hold if the phone system allows it.
  3. Check the answer with your supplier, by phone or on the supplier's website.
  4. Call the customer back and give them the confirmed answer.

[L0909.024]

Holding reply:

Thanks for asking. I want to confirm that exactly before I answer, so you get accurate information. Could I call you back in five minutes?

[L0909.023]

Voicemail and callbacks

  • Sending callers to voicemail loses sales, because callers simply phone the next seller on their list. Customers have said they bought because someone answered the phone, so answered calls are a cheap sales safeguard. [L0337.006]
  • Voicemail greeting (older advice, 2021-22):
Hello, you've reached [business name]. We are busy right now and cannot take your call immediately, and we don't want you to have to wait on hold. To use our callback service, leave your name and your contact details after the tone, and we will call you back as soon as we can. Thank you for your patience.

[L0909.008]

Handling a voicemail callback (older advice, 2021-22)

  1. Receive the SMS or email notification that a message has been left.
  2. Listen to the message and note the caller's name and what they need.
  3. Call the customer back as soon as you are able to.

[L0909.009]

When to call customers

  • Avoid calling customers early in the morning. Use the evening and night hours instead, and in most countries keep calls before about 8 p.m. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0909.027]
  • Evening callbacks work because customers are usually at home at that time and can actually talk. Calling after a day of work also fits a side-business owner's schedule (older advice, 2021-22). [L0909.028]

Difficult and angry customers

  • Do not return an angry caller's hostility with hostility, and do not block customers in anger. A single heated reaction can turn a fixable problem into a lost customer and a public complaint. [L1472.006]
  • Angry customers often vent about problems in their own life, so their anger is rarely about the store itself. Stay calm, train yourself to keep your composure, and still follow your own process. [L1472.007]
  • Some customers will stay unreasonable no matter how well you respond, and that is not your fault. As long as you complete your own steps correctly, their reaction does not change the correct response. [L1472.019]
  • Do not take personal offense at customers who react badly to a sensible response. Document each step so you can show you handled the situation correctly. [L1472.020]

Handling objections that products ship from China

  • Some customers object that products come from China. This is rare, and it can usually be handled by giving the tracking number and finding a solution with the customer. [L1347.035]
  1. When a customer objects that the product ships from China, first send the tracking number, since customers often ask for it.
  2. If they remain upset, point out that many everyday consumer electronics, such as iPhones, are also made or shipped from China.
  3. If the problem is serious, offer a solution.

[L1347.038]
