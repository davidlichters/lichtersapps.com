17.12 Reducing return and refund rates

Set the return target and measure it correctly

  • Two metrics are in use, and they are not the same. A return rate is the share of orders sent back. A refund rate is the share of sales refunded. The targets below use different ones, so check which one a benchmark measures before comparing. [L0467.005, L0253.009]
  • For premium products sold at premium prices, a return rate of 1 to 2 percent is normal and a good target. Some niches run higher, but 2 percent is a safe planning ceiling. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0467.005]
  • For refunds, aim for under 1 percent over a long period, calculated over the whole period and not per 10 items. Damaged items that need a replacement are excluded from this calculation. [L0253.009]
  • Track the refund rate per product as a quality measure, because a low refund rate suggests strong product satisfaction. In one example store, about 410 orders were placed without a single refund. [L0492.041]
  • Track returns and cancelled orders, and sales discounts, each as a separate percentage of gross sales every month, so a rise in returns is not hidden inside the sales total. In the example store, returns and cancelled orders of 237,813 equalled about 13 percent of gross sales of 1,826,410, and sales discounts of 34,197 were about 1.9 percent. (Older advice, 2021 to 2022.) [L1046.056]
  • For fashion, check the net sales after returns before deciding whether a product is viable. [L0693.055]

Choose niches and suppliers by return risk

  • Research the expected return rate for your niche before committing. Start with brand reviews on Google, and look up supplier names on retailer sites for reviews. (Older advice, 2020 and 2022.) [L0245.064, L0249.069]
  • Look for patterns of complaints about damage, quality inconsistency and defects. [L0249.069]
  • Before signing a supplier, check its brand reviews for complaints about damage, wrong colours, or items that differ from the photos, because these signal a high return rate. [L0667.025]
  • A supplier with a high return rate is a product quality problem. Stop selling its products rather than covering the losses with restocking fees. [L0667.026]
  • Skip any niche where high returns are expected. Returns cost shipping, support time and revenue at the same time. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0663.004]
  • Avoid building a store around products where delivery and returns depend on slow or unreliable chains, because a poor customer experience leads to disputes and loss of repeat customers. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0357.063]
  • Avoid niches where customers have strong emotional attachment and tight deadlines. A late or failed delivery can ruin an event and cause severe complaints. Products that must arrive by a fixed date carry much higher service risk. [L0766.005]
  • Avoid mattresses, bed sheets and pillows for dropshipping unless the seller is a brand with very high margins. Feel, softness and stiffness cannot be shown on a website, so these personalised items have very high return rates that cost time, labour and money. [L0766.013]
  • Products with high return rates or that customers often ask about, such as electronics and apparel, should be avoided, since they need more support and return handling. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0801.123]
  • A store with a physical showroom can reduce returns by helping customers choose the right product in person. It can sell online only the brands whose return policies allow it. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0801.125]
  • For fashion, returns are common and can be acceptable if sales exceed returns. [L0693.055]
  • Sizing-dependent fashion creates high return rates because customers expect Amazon-level return policies. Dropshipping cannot sustainably support sizing-dependent products. [L0209.038]

Product types with specific refund risks

Low-priced and impulse products

  • Low-priced markdown products and impulse purchases drive very high reported return rates. Premium products bought after more thought return far less often. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0467.006]
  • Very cheap novelty items, around $13 or less, are less likely to be returned, because customers often do not bother sending back a low-value product. Check this against your own return rate before relying on it. [L0655.027]
  • The two observations do not conflict. Items under about $13 are returned less often because return shipping is not worth the effort. Low-priced markdown and impulse purchases are returned more often. Apply the $13 rule only after checking your own rate. [L0655.027, L0467.006]
  • Gimmicky products carry high refund risk because buyers are often disappointed by the product's real quality. Plan for refund handling and dispute management from the start. [L1211.016]

Digital products

  • Refund requests for a digital product are a direct signal of low content quality, and they show where the product needs to improve. [L0513.044]
  • Make sure the content is accurate and useful before launch, because poor content is what triggers refunds. [L0513.044]

Assembly-heavy products

  • Include supplier videos showing assembly time, and photos of what the packaged goods look like when delivered. Set customer expectations upfront to reduce returns and complaints. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0249.067]

Fashion and footwear

  • Some buyers wear fashion items for a weekend and return them with the tags still on, which causes chargebacks. Plan return rules for clothing with this in mind. [L1214.030]
  • Bracketing (buying several sizes to keep one) and wardrobing (buying for a single use and returning) push return costs up. Strategic friction in the return policy or at purchase filters out customers who return for no reason without breaking loyal relationships. [L0955.004]
  • Categories where the product may differ from the photos, such as jewellery or clothing with sizing or quality variance, cause refund-risk hesitation. [L1208.007]
  • For shoes, state clearly on the product page that stock is limited and that returns are not accepted. This discourages buying one pair to send back for another size. Expect that some wrong-size cases will still need a refund. [L1219.007]
  • Sizing issues can cause returns, especially for shoes and footwear. Plan refunds into the margin. [L1439.030]

Electronics

  • Electronics carry a high risk of chargebacks and disputes when quality is poor. Test a new electronic product with the first week of customers, collect reviews and ask about quality before increasing spend. [L1218.029]

Describe the product so the customer knows what arrives

  • Accurate product descriptions and photos, plus staff notes that explain exactly what is in the product, reduce refund requests because customers know what they are buying. Clarity before purchase is the first refund preventer. [L0910.033]
  • Sell only what fits the promises you make. Congruent purchases reduce buyer's remorse, refunds and chargebacks. [L0378.097]
  • The brand with a typical 20 to 25 percent return rate credited its low returns to accurate product advertising, strong product imagery and a quality product that customers do not want to send back. [L1438.030]

Set expectations on the sales page

  • State customer service speed and the returns policy on the sales page, for example "fast customer service" and "easy returns", so the buyer knows who will help if something goes wrong. [L0919.045]
  • Visible support lowers the fear of being left alone with a bad purchase. Place it next to the guarantee and secure checkout messages in the risk-removal section. [L0919.045]
  • Reply to customer questions and complaints quickly to keep returns low. One store had no refunds because support was fast and problems were fixed for the customer. [L1439.028]
  • A money-back guarantee shown on the product page must match the refund process you actually operate. Make sure customer support can process it without extra conditions the buyer was not told about. [L1188.036]
  • A money-back guarantee needs fast, clear refund handling, because the guarantee is part of the offer and affects trust and conversion. [L0871.082]

Choose the return policy: who pays for return shipping

Sources disagree on this. Each position applies in different conditions.

Position When it applies Ids
Customer pays return shipping Free returns raise the return rate and encourage return culture, especially when competitors offer fast delivery together with free returns. L1438.029
Customer pays return shipping (fashion, multiple markets) Paid returns are now common in fashion, and customers in several countries accepted them after a switch from free returns. L0660.032
Customer pays a fee About 64% of brands charged return fees to some extent in surveys cited for 2025. Shoppers accept fees when the process is easy. L0955.002, L0955.003
Seller pays return shipping Use a full refund guarantee with seller-paid return shipping as the answer to dissatisfaction, and refund within the stated trial period. Customers who know the return route is simple are more likely to order. L1369.102
Free replacement, no questions A policy that says no returns are needed and the product is replaced for free reads as a strong guarantee and can lift conversion. L0577.069
  • Shoppers accept return fees when the return process is easy. A fee combined with a hard, slow process damages the relationship. Free returns still make sense for loyalty or VIP customers and to attract first-time buyers. [L0955.003]
  • Many consumers do not know how expensive returns and shipping are, so they abuse free returns when they are unaware. Making the cost of a return visible is part of keeping abuse down. [L0955.005]
  • Surveys cited for 2025 found that in a survey of 1,000 consumers worldwide, about 70% said they would pay for a convenient, premium return experience. [L0955.002]
  • Clear refund shipping costs deter buyers who would otherwise order and return, so some low-intent orders are lost on purpose. This is accepted because returns cost more than the lost sale. [L0910.032]
  • Check reviews for a brand's products across websites to judge its return rate before deciding on a free-return policy. [L0964.102]
  • Free returns can increase the return rate, so decide the policy against your own measured return rate, not on principle. [L1438.029, L0964.102]

Choose refund windows and guarantee lengths

Sources disagree on length. Each applies in different conditions.

Guarantee When it applies Ids
14 days, money back Unopened products or items with manufacturing defects. Show it prominently in Q4 to build confidence without triggering chargebacks, if the terms are clearly stated. L0309.010
30 days, money back Common standard. Must match the refund process you actually run. L1439.030, L1188.036
30 days, with free return Pair with a stated free-returns policy. L0919.026
30 days, reminder Remind buyers of free returns or the 30-day guarantee where product may differ from photos. L1208.007

  • A 90-day guarantee gives buyers a full refund if they return the product within 90 days of receiving it, and it should be stated on the page. [L1194.047]
  • For approval or trial offers, most people are honest when they receive a product on approval, and losses from trials that are not paid for are very small. Offering approval or return rights rarely costs more than it earns in trust and sales. [L1261.121]
  • Before offering a trial with a payment or return condition, check the cost of processing returns and the share of trials that are never paid for. Build those costs into the cost per customer. [L1261.123]
  • Do not charge restocking fees if the main competitors do not charge them. [L0667.025]

Remove the buyer's risk

  • Risk reversal removes the buyer's fear of losing money. Common tools are a 30-day money-back guarantee with a free return, a 100% refund if not satisfied, and a stated free-returns policy. [L0919.026]
  • A money-back guarantee works because buyers are loss averse: they fear losing money more than they value gaining a product. Reducing the downside of a purchase increases conversion more than merely highlighting the upside. [L1209.026]
  • A returns policy that says no returns are needed and the product is replaced for free reads as a strong guarantee and can lift conversion. [L0577.069]
  • Display the 14-day money-back guarantee prominently during Q4 to build confidence, provided the terms are clearly stated. [L0309.010]

Build the return allowance into the margin

  • Build a return allowance into the margin so refunds do not push individual sales into loss. Set the allowance from your expected return rate. [L0436.010]
  • Plan refunds into the margin, because sizing issues can cause returns, especially for shoes and footwear. [L1439.030]
  • Include the cost of processing returns and the share of trials that are never paid for in the cost per customer. [L1261.123]
  • Returns are unavoidable in e-commerce, but they can be reduced, and reducing them is a core task. Plan for returns as a normal part of the business and work to minimise them. (Older advice, 2021 to 2022.) [L1480.026]
  • Plan for refund handling and dispute management from the start, particularly for gimmicky products. [L1211.016]

Keep the customers who return less

  • Attract customers who want to wear the product for a long time and keep coming back, rather than customers who buy for a one-time occasion. Long-term customers are less likely to return items. [L1438.031]
  • Accurate advertising, strong product imagery and a quality product that customers do not want to send back are the main reasons the brand with a 20 to 25 percent return rate gave for its lower returns. [L1438.030]
