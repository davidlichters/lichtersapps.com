# 17. Customer service, returns and chargebacks

## Designing the support operation: scope, channels and ownership

### Scope: which channels support covers

- A store's support scope is live chat, inbound phone and email, plus social messaging if the store uses it. At low volume one person can handle all of these. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0834.090]
- Cover three channels: email, live chat on the store, and social media messages and comments. Social media means Facebook page messages, Instagram DMs and comments on TikTok organic videos. [L1358.020, L1360.023]
- Answer direct messages and comments on time. If you run organic TikTok videos, assign someone to answer the comments on them. [L1358.024]
- Staff live chat or another support channel, because new visitors ask questions before they buy. Publish your support hours and an email address so buyers know a real person answers. [L1346.029]
- At launch, let customers reach the store through live chat, email and phone. Their questions show how the business actually works and what needs to be clarified. [L1446.011]
- A customer service role covers live chat, phone calls, and order shipping and tracking. Social media and content writing go to a separate person, because combining them breaks down quickly. [L0970.134]
- Automating about 30% of tickets lowers first response time and cost, and frees the team for complex cases that can lead to sales. Instant self-service answers also beat making customers wait for an agent. [L0752.034]
- TikTok direct messages now transcribe voice memos automatically as they are sent, which makes person-to-person conversations easier to read and follow. [L1504.013]
- A structured customer service setup answers questions quickly, prevents refunds caused by confusion, builds trust in the brand and supports sustainable store growth. [L0911.002]
- Phone volume in a small dropshipping store can be about one or two calls per day, and nearly all of them can be sales-related. At that volume, phone can be an additional task for a freelance assistant who already works at a computer, rather than a full-time role. [L0910.061]

### Scope of post-sale support in a store sale

- A typical post-sale scope for a store sale is about one month of email support plus two calls of at least one hour each over that month. Agree the scope before the deal closes. [L0553.019]
- Pre-selling means the calls and emails a buyer needs before committing to a purchase. Products that need a lot of pre-selling add workload for the owner, so avoid them when building a low-effort store. [L1517.017]

### Who handles what at each stage

- Early on, the owner answers every chat, email and call personally. Quick replies to stock and order questions turn clicks into sales. (Older advice, 2020.) [L1111.132]
- Handle all customer service yourself at first, so you learn which questions customers ask and how to solve their problems. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1110.040]
- The store owner handles customer contact and makes sure customers are satisfied, while the supplier usually stores and ships the product. Returns reduce the total sales figure in the store report. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0771.051]
- Move customer service off the owner's plate as early as possible, even if only part of it is delegated at first. This covers phone calls, emails, online chat and sometimes supplier contact. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1247.002]
- Typical contacts that interrupt an owner are shipping time questions and order-status questions (where is my order), arriving by phone, email and online chat. These are low-value, unpredictable inquiries and should go to a support person. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1247.021]
- A virtual assistant can handle emails and phone calls as order volume grows. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0468.145]
- Hire a virtual assistant working about 4 to 8 hours a day only after you know the questions and answers yourself. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1110.040]
- Owners who run the business from abroad need phone and email coverage, handled personally or outsourced, to keep buyers who are ready to purchase. [L0923.053]

  - **Pre-purchase** (sales, inquiry response, incentive-based compensation).
  - **Post-purchase** (fulfilment, returns, support).

- Pre-purchase staff must understand order profitability, discount authorisation limits and real-time stock status before speaking with customers. Without this knowledge, they promise excessive discounts or unrealistic delivery dates, creating losses and operational chaos for the fulfilment team downstream. [L0263.050]

### Workload by price point

- Products priced at $2,000 or more typically require higher levels of pre-sale and post-sale customer support. (2024.) [L0253.004]
- High-ticket products raise the customer service workload, mainly in pre-sale questions and post-sale follow-up. Plan for this before selling products above about $2,000. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0854.083]
- Customer service is central in a business with expensive items, since more support is needed as prices rise. Plan staffing and support around the price and return rate of the niche. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0801.124]
- Customer service volume rises with higher-priced items, so plan support for those products. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0468.145]
- Lower-priced products attract more pre-sale calls, such as customers asking whether an item is in stock. Higher-priced products mean fewer calls, but you need to be prepared to answer or return calls quickly. (2023.) [L0835.051]
- Sources differ on higher-priced products. Some describe a higher overall level of support (L0253.004, L0854.083, L0801.124). Others describe fewer calls but a need for fast replies (L0835.051). Both apply: plan for more total support and faster response time per contact, even if the number of calls is lower.
- Expect a slightly higher volume of inbound support requests for electronics and complex products. A high order value, such as an $8,000 sale, can justify handling that extra support. (Older advice, 2018.) [L1274.017]
- Fewer orders mean fewer tracking numbers, fewer shipping problems and less customer service work. This workload reduction is a main reason to sell higher-priced products. [L0474.006]
- A low-ticket store of $40 to $50 products sourced from China generated high order volume but became a support nightmare. High order count with low-value products is not better than fewer higher-value orders. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0962.127]

### Workload by order volume

- Early on, support contacts in a new dropshipping store start slowly, often with only a few calls and sometimes none on a given day. Workload grows only once sales and momentum build, so early volume is usually manageable. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0909.001]
- At high order volume a store can receive hundreds of customer emails per day. This is a reason to outsource support early. [L1358.019]
- Many customers buy without ever contacting support, so slow replies affect only some of your sales, mainly early on. Sales continue while you handle the backlog. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0909.016]
- The share of buyers who call support before purchasing varies by market. In markets where many customers ask questions first, plan more phone capacity. In others, expect most buyers to purchase without contacting you. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0909.029]
- The early small-volume and later high-volume figures describe different stages of the same store. Both apply: plan for a quiet start and for a large inbox once sales grow. [L0909.001, L1358.019]

### Hours, response windows and coverage

- Buyers who are ready to purchase will leave for a competitor if calls or emails go unanswered for more than a few hours. (2023.) [L0923.053]
- Some customers will not wait for a callback, so slow responses will cost some sales. There is no perfect fix, so treat it as an accepted trade-off while the business is small. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0909.005]
- Pick up a call immediately only when you can take it from a quiet place. Otherwise let it go to voicemail and answer emails or chats in set blocks, such as about half an hour during the day. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0909.004]
- Email is easier to handle in batches, because there is a natural gap between a customer's message and your reply. Answer emails after work or in set blocks during the day. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0909.015]
- Daily coverage routine when working in a far time zone:
  1. Check emails right before going to bed, around 21:00 to 22:00 local time.
  2. Fulfil any orders that came in during the day so they do not wait a full day.
  3. Send tracking numbers, either personally or through a VA.
  4. Check emails again first thing in the morning.

  About 20 minutes at night and a morning check cover the start and end of a normal business day, so suppliers and customers see replies during their working hours. [L0910.006]
- Check a competitor's support quality by searching its store name and URL on Google and reading the reviews. Complaints about unanswered calls or emails show a gap you can close by replying quickly. [L0894.014]
- Do not invite overseas customers outside your shipping area to contact you. Shipping costs often exceed the product price, and such inquiries are frequently fraudulent. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0801.120]

### Setting up support step by step

1. Handle all customer service yourself at first, so you learn which questions customers ask and how to solve their problems.
2. Add answers to frequent questions (shipping times, warranties, stock status) to your website, so fewer people ask.
3. Set up automated post-purchase emails with tracking updates, for example with the AfterShip app.
4. Hire a virtual assistant working about 4 to 8 hours a day only after you know the questions and answers yourself.

(Older advice, 2019.) [L1110.040]

### Keeping support separate for each store

- Keep customer service and support separate for each store, because each store has its own customers and its own problems. Mixing support across stores creates confusion that is hard to undo. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0949.034]

### Support and growth: what to expect

- Good customer service protects reputation and can bring referrals and positive reviews. It will not multiply store traffic or customer numbers on its own, so owners should not expect support to be a major growth driver. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1247.020]
- Customer service is a growth lever. Buyers with a good experience come back, while a bad experience stops repeat purchases. Treat service quality as a core part of building a business that lasts. [L1472.001]
- Fast answers to customer questions, especially before purchase, can raise the conversion rate. Customer support therefore affects sales, not just service. [L1358.021, L1360.020]
- Sources differ on how much support can grow the business. One view says support does not multiply traffic or customers (L1247.020). Another says it raises conversion and repeat purchases (L1472.001, L1358.021, L1360.020). Both apply: expect support to protect and convert the customers you already have, not to bring in new visitors.
- Customer service is a core success factor. E-commerce businesses that offer no service either fail or stay small. Expect to answer phone calls and emails, because the belief that you never have to do this is wrong. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1480.027]
- Long shipping times combined with a poor customer experience caused the 2016 to 2018 approach to fail, even when cheap Facebook ads brought in orders. Consider shipping speed and customer experience alongside traffic. [L1459.019]
- For a newly launched dropshipping store, customer service and shipping are the operational areas where owners most often have questions. Sorting out these two areas early is a common starting point for daily store management. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1499.002]

## Setting up contact points: phone, email, chat and contact page

### Business phone numbers

- Use a dedicated business phone number, not a personal cell number, so business and private calls never mix on the same device. [L0700.002]
- Calls are one contact channel alongside email and chat, and a toll-free number can be obtained so customers can call without paying for the call. [L1093.008]
- A toll-free number makes the store look more established and gives customers an easy way to reach support, which can reduce chargebacks caused by unclear order problems. [L0430.014]
- Customers who call before ordering are checking whether the store responds. A visible phone number builds confidence and reduces hesitation to buy. [L1094.089]

| Option | Note | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Local area code number | Fine to start with | L1094.090 |
| Toll-free number (MightyCall 855 numbers) | Looks more professional later | L1094.090 |
| Google Voice free business number | Does not reveal it is Google Voice when the voice option is turned off | L1094.091 |
| GoDaddy 800 number | Another toll-free option | L1094.091 |
| Grasshopper (commonly used for public business numbers) | Older advice, 2021-22 | L1145.006 |
| JustCall.io or Zintel (used in Australia instead) | Older advice, 2021-22 | L1145.006 |

**Testing the number (older advice, 2021-22)**

1. Call the public phone number from a different phone, such as a partner's or friend's phone. [L1145.007]
2. Confirm that it rings through to the phone you will answer. [L1145.007]
3. If it does not, find and fix the cause before launch. [L1145.007]

**Phone hours and forwarding (older advice, 2021-22)**

- Phone or service hours should match when customers actually call, which often peaks in the evening after work. Extended hours can be a differentiator even if round-the-clock calls are rare. [L0411.028]
- Case: in the first business, the seller offered 24-hour phone support staffed by overseas agents while the seller slept. Almost no calls came at 3 a.m., but many came after work around 6 to 7 p.m. Paying agents to cover those hours was worth it because competitors did not answer calls. The seller forwarded the line to their own phone at night, and calls at that hour were almost never received. [L0411.027]
- Forward the business number to your personal mobile during the day so you can take calls whenever you are free. [L0909.026]
- Set the number to divert to a prerecorded voicemail when you cannot answer, so callers always get a message instead of silence. [L0909.026]
- Outsource phone support once the store is profitable enough to justify it. [L0934.182, L0970.138]

### Voicemail greetings and phone handling

- Keep the phone number on the website even if you cannot answer every call, and use a voicemail greeting that captures the caller's name, email, phone and reason. [L0934.182]
- A business phone number should be listed on the store, with a voicemail that takes name, phone, email and reason for the call and promises a callback as soon as possible. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0834.091]
- Without a voicemail on the public number, callers reach nothing when you cannot pick up. Set a pre-recorded voicemail that lets callers request a callback. This matters most if you still work a day job during business hours. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1145.008]
- Most inbound calls are either pre-sale questions from people wanting to order, post-sale questions about shipping dates, or checks that the business is legitimate. A voicemail with a short greeting helps you learn which group is calling. (Older advice, 2020.) [L1111.133]

```

```

**Handling customer calls before hiring phone support (2023)** [L0970.138]

1. Set the phone app's default voicemail greeting to the template. [L0970.138]
2. Turn on transcription so each voicemail is sent as text immediately. [L0970.138]
3. When a transcript arrives, call the customer back with the reason and details. [L0970.138]
4. Once the business is profitable, hire someone to answer the phone. [L0970.138]

### Dedicated support email and aliases

- Set up a dedicated support email for the brand and have a virtual assistant reply quickly, so that every customer gets an immediate response to questions or delivery problems. [L0492.037]
- Create the dedicated support email on Google Workspace (formerly G Suite), for example contact@ or info@ followed by the store domain, at about $10 per month. Connect your domain and set this address as the store contact email in Shopify. [L1173.030]
- Use the business email support address for support requests. The team reviews incoming messages Monday to Friday and almost around the clock, and escalates urgent ones to the owner. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0665.087]
- Set a dedicated returns@ alias. It separates return requests from sales and supplier mail while keeping everything in one inbox. Replies should come from the same address the customer wrote to. [L1197.021]
- Let a virtual assistant also send tracking numbers to customers. [L1431.054]
- Use a dedicated business address for the store, not a personal one. Addresses on the contact page are mailing addresses, and the page should say that returns are not accepted there. (Older advice, 2020.) [L1088.073]

**Email signatures and sending rules**

- Every business email must include a signature with the sender's name, the business name, the business phone number and the sender's personal business email address. [L0404.014]

- New emails to customers or suppliers that are not new orders must be sent only from the staff member's personal business email address, using the same signature as for replies. [L0404.016]

### Spam control and lost messages

**Contact form and address spam**

- A support email address shown on the website attracts spam and irrelevant inquiries. Use a contact form instead, with predefined subject lines, which allows auto responses and rules to be applied. [L0752.024]
- Listing a plain support email address on a contact page leads to spam and irrelevant inquiries; use a contact form instead. [L0752.033]
- Contact form in Gorgias: Settings > Channels > Contact Form. Name the form, connect the store, define subject lines, then publish it on the website using the embed code. [L0752.025]

**Spam procedure for contact form submissions**

1. Delete junk emails immediately. [L0253.048]
2. If the same email addresses appear repeatedly, block them in your email provider settings. [L0253.048]
3. Do not mark them as spam; simply delete. [L0253.048]
4. Expect spam whenever you have public contact forms on your store; this is normal. [L0253.048]

**Checking for missing replies**

- For a support email that has not been answered, check your spam folder and that of the customer, because replies from customers and outgoing responses are both prone to spam filters. [L1285.071]
- If no reply arrives within the 48-hour window, follow up and check the spam folder. [L0934.102, L0934.183]
- A support email system can fail silently, so requests may never reach the team. If you have not received a reply after four to five days, send the request again through another channel. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0960.066]
- A helpdesk switch can lose emails, so a supplier or customer who says they sent a message but got no reply may have had it lost. Ask them to forward or resend the email to the new address. [L0807.039]
- Relying only on email notifications for form submissions introduces delays that competitors overcome. Real-time alerts enable faster contact and win high-value deals. [L0284.003]

**Email deliverability**

- Set up the DMARC record for the business domain to increase trust and deliverability, so customers receive order and account emails. In Shopify: Settings > Notifications > Sender email > Email domain authentication. Until it is set up, Shopify sends via a backup store address. [L1327.048]

**Editing the Shopify contact page**

1. Go to Shopify Admin > Online Store > Pages and open the Contact page that Shopify created automatically with the account. [L0509.005]
2. Change the page title to Contact Us. [L0509.005]
3. Keep the theme template set to Contact, which adds a contact form to the page. [L0509.005]
4. Click Save. [L0509.005]
5. Click View Page to check the result. The page is reachable at /pages/contacts. [L0509.005]

- The Contact template adds a form that visitors can fill out, and each submission arrives in your email with the visitor's contact details. At the start, the template alone is enough; more contact channels can be added later. [L0509.006]
- Shopify admin > Pages > Contact us: keep the contact form and add at least two further contact methods, such as hours of operation, a real phone number and an email address or live chat, then save. Google checks that a storefront shows at least two contact methods. [L0724.028]
- The Contact Us page should be detailed and personal, with the owner's name, a clear invitation to email, and the email address, so customers know who to reach after purchase. [L1293.013]
- A support page must show customers how to contact the business. Contact details that are easy to find reduce pre-sale and post-sale uncertainty. [L1130.011]
- Make contact options (email, phone, live chat), shipping destinations and the refund policy clearly visible. Without them, visitors who have a question leave and buy from a larger marketplace instead. [L0390.027]

**Where the contact details go**

- Show customer service contact details, such as a phone number with opening hours, in the header so they are visible on every page. [L0409.047]
- Contact details in the footer (phone, email and postal address) are recommended. Search engines and paid ad platforms such as Meta and Google tend to view a store as more legitimate when contact information is visible, and the details give customers an easy way to reach the store. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0681.011]

**Reconciling plain email and contact form**

- Public visitors: use the contact form with predefined subject lines as the main route, because a plain address attracts spam. [L0752.024, L0752.033]
- Visible contact options: the store still needs at least two contact methods on the storefront, and a plain email address can be one of them. [L0724.028, L0390.027]

### Live chat on the store

- A live chat widget on the store gives shoppers a real-time support channel, usually used to answer questions before they buy. [L1007.007]
- The online chat widget appears in the bottom right-hand corner of the screen on all devices. [L0404.012]
- Older advice (2021-22): keep the chat widget at the bottom left or bottom right of the page, because shoppers expect the chat there. [L1298.010]
- Before a chat starts, visitors must enter a name and email address. The owner keeps the contact details even if the chat is not answered in time, and can follow up by email. [L1311.012]
- When visitors start a chat, they are prompted to enter their name and email; the email is captured even if you do not reply immediately, allowing follow-up if the chat is missed. [L0151.006]
- The visitor panel in a chat shows the visitor's email address, how long they have been a customer and their location. Shopify counts any email address stored in the system as a customer, even if that person has never bought anything. [L1311.018]
- Mobile notifications let the owner answer chats while away from the computer, which keeps response times short. Slow answers to pre-sale questions cost sales, so the phone app should be set up from the start. [L1311.004]

### Widget hours and offline status

**Going offline when you cannot answer**

1. Open the chat dashboard. [L0404.011]
2. Click the status drop-down in the top left-hand corner. [L0404.011]
3. Set it to "Offline" when you cannot be online for any reason. [L0404.011]

- During working hours, the chat dashboard must stay open while the staff member is at their computer. Outside working hours, the chat is in Offline mode, so customers leave messages, or the owner monitors the chat. [L0404.012]
- When the live chat agent is on break, hide the chat widget instead of showing an offline status, or route chats to a phone if staff can step in. This keeps the site from looking closed. (Older advice, 2020.) [L1088.072]

**Setting hours: two approaches**

- Keep availability as open as is realistic. While a store is run by one person, reaching as many visitors as possible during waking hours matters more than strict business hours. [L0948.013]
- Use 24/7 when visitors who write outside working hours should still be able to leave their name and email for follow-up. Available hours are 24/7 by default and can be edited to limit when the chat icon appears; keeping 24/7 is recommended. [L1311.025]
- Use a waking-hours window when you run the store alone and want staff only notified by phone. Set the window to the hours you are awake, for example 7:00 AM to 9:00 PM, individually for each day of the week. Chat stays on the store during these hours, and staff are not required to sit at the computer. Changes can take a few minutes to appear on the store. [L0948.012]

**Tidio operating hours**

- Turn on operating hours in Tidio and set the times you are available each day, for example Monday 7:30 AM to 6 PM and Tuesday until 4 PM. Match these hours to your target audience, or outsource replies to a VA if you cannot cover them. [L1297.031]
- Turn on operating hours and set hours per weekday, e.g. Monday until 6 p.m., Tuesday until 4 p.m. [L0477.026]

### Shopify Inbox

**What it is and where it runs**

- Shopify Inbox is the live chat app included with a Shopify account. Its mobile app lets you read and answer chats from your phone while customers browse the store. [L1287.011]
- Shopify has built-in live chat on the storefront, and a Shopify mobile app sends a notification to the seller's phone when a visitor starts a chat, so the seller can reply from anywhere. [L1304.006]
- Older advice (2021-22): Shopify stores do not include live chat by default, so a chat app must be installed. Live chat adds a personalised sales channel next to phone and email, and it demands less staff time than phone calls. [L1298.008]
- Shopify Inbox is a built-in live chat option in Shopify that serves the same purpose as Tidio. [L1360.022]

**Mobile app: which app to use**

1. Install the separate Shopify Inbox app on iPhone or Android (not the main Shopify app). [L0948.015]
2. Open it and log in with the store's Shopify account. [L0948.015]
3. Incoming chats appear as push notifications and open in the app. [L0948.015]
4. Type the reply and press send; it appears live in the storefront conversation. [L0948.015]

- The main Shopify mobile app sends a notification to the seller's phone when a visitor starts a chat. [L1304.006]
- Set up the phone app from the start, so the owner can answer quickly. [L1311.004]

**Inbox settings and conversations**

## Hiring, briefing and managing support staff and virtual assistants

### When to outsource

- Outsource customer service first, because a constant flow of negative messages can drain the owner, and even major brands receive negative feedback. [L0780.012]
- Outsource customer support early. Answering customer questions can take a lot of time, especially with high order volume, and some days may bring hundreds of emails that overwhelm the owner. [L1360.019]
- Answering customer emails can take three to four hours per day and blocks growth. A virtual assistant (VA) at $3 to $5 per hour can take over this work and return those hours to the owner. [L1127.034]
- Typical daily tasks in an online store are answering live chat, phone and email questions, handling follow-up questions after a purchase, and processing incoming orders with suppliers until they ship. These tasks require human interaction and are usually the first candidates for delegation once they are understood. [L1447.004]
- Outsource customer service only after you have seen call volumes, call times and how many calls turn into sales. A new hire in another time zone should be fully briefed before taking over. [L1277.093]
- Do not outsource phone support until business profit can pay for it. Until then, use a voicemail with a clear message and return calls and emails the next day. [L0941.082]
- Per-call phone answering services are not recommended at the start. The vendor example is only a starting point to see what is possible. [L0941.083]
- Early on, answer live chat from the owner's phone when possible. Consider a paid 24/7 answering service only once the business is profitable and reinvesting profits. [L0967.091]
- Hire your own employees for customer service and other tasks that need care and attention toward customers. Third-party agents have less interest in your customers and often cost about the same as a direct hire. [L1359.014]
- Where sources differ on structure, use direct hires for work that needs care toward customers, and a virtual team for hours and time-zone coverage. [L1359.014, L0962.129, L0852.110]
- Scope of support hours depends on three factors: product price (higher price means fewer sales and fewer support contacts), customer demographic (older customers generate more phone calls), and budget. [L0721.031]
- Product knowledge questions can be handled by general support staff. Pre-purchase questions call for live chat and email coverage, which may give a competitive edge if others avoid it. (Older, 2019.) [L0663.003]
- Prepare customer service before a major sales peak such as Black Friday. In the lesson's case, a team of three VAs was not enough when orders surged, and tickets fell four to five days behind. Train extra helpers in advance, even family or a VA at around 3 USD per hour. [L1363.043]

### Where to find virtual assistants

- onlinejobs.ph is the go-to platform for hiring virtual staff to answer customer queries in real time when the owner is in a different time zone. Job posting, qualifying, hiring and training guidance is covered in the outsourcing module of the course. (2024.) [L0666.006]
- Staff customer support with VAs in regions with reverse working hours, such as the Philippines, to cover different time zones. Hire them through job platforms such as onlinejobs.ph. (2021.) [L0962.129]
- Customer support can be outsourced to a virtual team in the Philippines, which is a major growth step. Support covers live chat, phone and email. (Older, 2020.) [L0852.110]

### Hiring and vetting

- When hiring customer service staff, look for people with similar past experience who can provide a reference. Treat the job as a real position even though it is virtual. (2023.) [L0970.135]
- For a store selling to US customers, the person answering calls should be a native speaker with an American or Canadian accent. Customers then assume the business is a normal domestic company. Accents from some outsourcing regions can reduce trust for this audience. [L0910.060]

### Onboarding a new hire

- Set up the assistant in this order:
  1. Give access to the support mailbox, sales and orders. [L1173.032]
  2. Write standard operating procedures and reply templates. [L1173.032]
  3. Hold a weekly call to review work. [L1173.032]
  4. Update the instructions in one batch rather than sending small changes continuously. [L1173.032]
- When hiring fulfillment or design help, give clear, complete instructions, since vague briefs lead to unwanted results. Schedule calls regularly and do not wait for the assistant to start the conversation. [L1173.033]
- Onboarding a customer service or fulfillment hire (older, 2020):
  1. Document every customer service and fulfillment step for each supplier and each scenario. [L1277.094]
  2. Set up an open communication channel with the new person, such as Slack or Facebook Messenger. [L1277.094]
  3. Expect questions for the first few weeks, possibly up to a month, because the new hire will not know every answer yet. [L1277.094]
  4. Keep answering questions until the process runs smoothly. [L1277.094]

### Scope of the role

- A virtual assistant for customer service handles three areas: visitor questions before purchase, customer updates after purchase, and order processing. Order processing means contacting the supplier to place the order after a customer order comes in. It does not mean taking orders by email from customers. [L1430.002]
- VAs can handle order processing, tracking and routine customer questions without expert skills, since many customers mainly want to hear a real person. Phone support is often the first role to hand off. [L1515.031]

### Phone coverage and time zones

- Responding to customer queries instantly raises conversion. An owner in a distant time zone should outsource real-time responses rather than rely on a night shift schedule, since working through the night is unhealthy over the long term. [L0666.007]
- When operating from a distant time zone, staff phone support first, then email support, with people who work during your customers' business hours (US business hours in the example). Do not answer calls yourself at night. [L1515.032]
- Answering support calls personally at 3 or 4 a.m. because of a time-zone gap is not sustainable. Plan staffing before sales volume grows. [L1515.033]
- Split phone support and chat or email support into separate people only when volume grows beyond what one person can handle within the response targets. [L1111.126]

**Setting up a first phone helper (small business):**

1. Forward the business phone line to the helper's personal mobile. [L0337.005]
2. Pay per answered call instead of a fixed salary. [L0337.005]
3. Greet callers with "Hi, can I help you?" [L0337.005]
4. Take a message and ask the caller's questions. [L0337.005]
5. Tell the caller that a product specialist will call back. [L0337.005]

### Access rights and permissions

- Give VAs access only to the support mailbox and the sales and order data they need, not to the entire personal inbox. Keep the owner's personal inbox separate from customer complaints. [L1173.031]
- A VA can independently approve discounts up to 10%. Any discount above 10%, or any other customer credit, requires owner approval. [L0230.021]

### Inbox labels and email triage

- Colour-code Gmail labels by priority so the assistant knows which emails to handle first. Customer issues get a red label, since they are the most urgent. [L1000.063]
- An overnight inbox may hold around 20 emails, and an urgent $20,000 order could be buried among them. Priority labels make sure the assistant handles that order before routine messages. [L1000.064]
- Keep customer issues and sales inquiries in dedicated Gmail labels, not mixed into the general inbox, so they can be found later. Customer issues get a red label so they are actioned first. [L1000.068]

### Order boards

**Order Processing board (card per order):**

1. Order Sent: record the product name and shipping address, then send the order to the manufacturer. [L1396.016]
2. Once the manufacturer confirms the item is in stock, move the card forward. [L1396.016]
3. If the item is on backorder, notify the customer first and only then move the card to Backorder with a due date. [L1396.016]
4. Move the card to Shipped once fulfilled, and record the tracking number. [L1396.016]
5. Move the card to Claims and Damages when a claim or damage notice arrives, and add notes about its progress. [L1396.016]

- Keep cancelled orders on the board instead of deleting them, because the same customer may call back and the quote information may still be needed. Put a due date on backorders so they are not forgotten. Move cancelled orders to a cancelled list, not deleted. [L1396.017]
- If a replacement item is out of stock, notify the customer that a new item is coming but is temporarily unavailable. Keep the card in the Claims and Damages list with a due date, so the claim stays visible until the replacement ships. Add status notes to the card with each update. [L1396.018]
- Shopify order notes alone are hard to track once there are many backorders. Staff must scroll through each order and cannot see due dates. Track order status on a visual board with due dates on each card. This matters once volume reaches about 30 to 50 orders per month. Keeping important customer details only in individual Shopify orders makes information hard to find as volume grows, and the owner has to scroll through orders and cannot see all pending items together. A separate board for order tracking solves this. [L1396.019, L1396.063]
- Without a shared board, backorders and customer updates get lost. One customer waited since July, and the follow-up depended on memory. Lost notes from calls and chats also cause missed quotes. Record every customer request on a board that everyone can see. [L1396.020]

**Asana order board (automated order flow):**

1. A new order arrives in the store and is automatically added to the first column of the Asana order board, and the help desk sends a notification. [L1473.010]
2. The VA waits for the outside fraud-screening service ClearSale to approve the order. [L1473.010]
3. After approval, the VA forwards the order to the appropriate supplier or places it on the supplier's website and waits for confirmation. [L1473.010]
4. When the supplier confirms stock, the VA charges the customer's card in Shopify, since the card was only authorized at checkout. [L1473.010]
5. The VA moves the card to the confirmed column in Asana. [L1473.010]
6. When the order ships, the VA adds the tracking number in Shopify and updates the status in Asana. [L1473.010]
7. The VA marks the order complete in Asana with a check mark. [L1473.010]
8. If a shipped order later has a problem, the check mark is removed and the card is moved back to the column that matches the new issue. [L1473.010]

**Backorders on the Asana board:**

1. When a supplier reports a backorder, the VA gets the backorder date from the supplier. [L1473.011]
2. The VA records that date in Asana. [L1473.011]
3. The VA moves the order card to the backorder column. [L1473.011]
4. The VA informs the customer about the backorder using a ready reply template in the help desk. [L1473.011]

**Damaged items and returns on the Asana board:**

1. For a damaged item or a return, the VA asks the supplier for their damage or return procedure. [L1473.012]
2. The VA explains that procedure to the customer. [L1473.012]
3. The VA updates the order status in Asana. [L1473.012]

## Order status, tracking and delivery inquiries

### Why tracking and order status matter

- Order tracking systems prevent orders from disappearing due to supplier delays, lost emails in inbox, or operational oversights. This becomes critical as transaction volume grows and when working with new suppliers whose reliability is unproven. [L0292.001]
- Tracking questions are the most frequent support request, so prepare standard replies for the common cases: order not shipped yet, order shipped, shipped but not received, and shipped with an angry customer. [L0405.001]
- Most customer calls are about legitimacy, order status and stock. Detailed product pages, an FAQ and automated tracking emails remove most of these questions. (2021, older advice) [L1112.033]
- An order tracking tool can send tracking information to customers automatically after shipment, which reduces "where is my order" contacts. (2021, older advice) [L1112.034]
- Automatic shipment updates sent through a tracking app reduce customer inquiries about order status. [L0966.093]

### Order status check before every reply

1. Look up the order status in the store admin and fulfillment system before replying to any tracking question. [L0405.033]
2. If the order is not shipped yet, send the not-shipped reply: it is being processed, a tracking email follows once it ships, and orders take about 10-20 days after shipping. [L0405.033]
3. If the order is shipped and the customer is asking about tracking, send the shipped reply with the shipping date, the 3-5 business day tracking delay, the tracking link and a screenshot of the current status. [L0405.033]
4. If the order is shipped and the customer says they got no confirmation emails, send the junk/spam version of the shipped reply and ask them to check junk and spam first. [L0405.033]
5. If the order is shipped but the customer is angry, send the angry reply with the current status, take responsibility for delivery and promise a free reship or full refund if the parcel is lost in transit. [L0405.033]
6. If the parcel is delivered and the customer wants a refund, offer a free reship first unless they ask for a refund on first contact, in which case process the refund without pushing a reship. [L0405.033]
7. If the tracking cannot be found at all and the parcel appears lost, send the lost-order refund reply and confirm the refund is already initiated. [L0405.033]

### Out-of-stock: handling the order

#### Standard out-of-stock handling

1. Ask the supplier when the product will be available again. Also ask whether a faster special order is possible and whether it costs extra. [L1119.009]
2. Contact the customer as soon as possible. Tell them the product is not immediately available and how long the wait will be, then ask whether they are willing to wait. [L1119.009]
3. If the customer is undecided about waiting, offer an authorised discount by refund if they wait. [L1119.009]
4. If the customer cannot wait, look for a similar product that is in stock and suits their needs. If needed to close the sale, offer an authorised discount on the similar product. [L1119.009]
5. If the customer will neither wait nor accept a similar product, cancel the order in Shopify. If payment was already captured, refund it. [L1119.009]
6. If the customer is willing to wait, confirm with the supplier that you want to backorder the product, and ask the supplier to ship immediately once the product is back in stock. [L1119.009]
7. If the supplier requires partial or full payment to place a backorder, consult the business owner and get authorisation before confirming any payment. [L1119.009]
8. Once a backorder is placed, email the customer to confirm the order is on backorder and give the expected arrival date. [L1119.009]

- Procedure from 2025: when an item is out of stock with no clear delivery date:

  2. Offer similar alternatives at about the same price. Example: between 800 and 1,200 dollars for a 1,000 dollar order. [L0857.040]
  3. If the customer wants a more expensive available item, send a Shopify draft order for the difference. [L0857.040]
  4. If the customer declines, cancel the order and refund it. [L0857.040]
- Rule for supplier failures: offer 2 or 3 alternative products that match the original in size, color, quality and price and are in stock and ready to ship now. Ask whether they want to swap their order for one of them. [L1407.007]

## Backorders, made-to-order items and shipping-delay communication

### Setting expectations before the sale

- Keep accepting orders for an out-of-stock item whose restock is a few weeks away, and add a note at the top of the product description with the estimated restock date. [L0247.096]
- Put delayed-shipping information prominently in the product description, not only in checkout. State assembly requirements, shipping duration and installation responsibility upfront. [L0270.034]
- Tell customers the exact date an out-of-stock product will be back, both on the product page and right after the order. Customers accept a wait when they know what is happening and are far less likely to email later asking where the product is. [L0942.014]
- A customer whose item is delayed should be told on the website and by email that the item is backordered, with the expected ship date. *(Older advice, 2020.)* [L0468.144]
- When a product is backordered, show the expected date on the product page and offer a deposit option so customers can order now. *(Older advice, 2019.)* [L1069.086]
- Make the lead time visible before checkout, and confirm it by email after the order. The risk of a chargeback or complaint rises when a customer does not know a product is built to order. [L0965.071]
- Be transparent with customers and suppliers about production and delivery lead times, because customers often expect immediate fulfilment that production cannot provide. Communicate realistic timelines from the start. [L1438.032]
- Customers who order unique products they cannot find locally usually accept a shipping delay of up to about two weeks. Their tolerance is much lower for products needed for a fixed occasion. [L0766.009]
- Products must be delivered before the promised date to avoid chargebacks. Avoid selling products tied to fixed dates unless delivery is guaranteed in time. [L1184.050]
- Avoid supplier routes with three- to five-week shipping, because long shipping times caused many refunds. [L1108.031]
- Avoid launching during holidays that delay shipping. Late orders lead to complaints and payment disputes, which can put a Stripe account and a Facebook account at risk. [L1127.035]

### Backorders: first contact, escalation and revised dates

Escalation procedure when a backordered product has caused problems:

1. Update the product page with a notice above the Add to Cart button that states the backorder and the expected date.

### Supplier slowdowns, seasonal peaks and Q4 cutoffs

- During a supplier slowdown such as Chinese New Year, some organic orders still come in, so customers should be told in advance about expected delays. This prevents refund requests caused by silence about shipping times. [L0780.045]
- Procedure for a product that goes viral a few days before Christmas:

1. If a product goes viral a few days before Christmas and customers place orders unlikely to arrive by the holiday, accept the orders.
2. Send follow-up emails to every customer confirming their order and shipping timeline.
3. Update them throughout fulfillment with tracking.
4. Even if products arrive after December 25, consistent communication reduces chargebacks and improves customer satisfaction.

[L0309.036]

- In Q4, customers who buy about 5 days before Christmas hoping for delivery in time often file chargebacks when the product arrives late. This is a major Q4 risk for dropshipping stores. [L1220.014]

1. Work out the last order date that still delivers before Christmas, based on supplier and shipping times.
2. State the delivery risk clearly on the store and in emails.

4. Offer a compensation option such as a 50% refund or another gift.

## Writing shipping, returns, refund and warranty policies

### Before you start: supplier terms and policy basis

- Base the store's return policy on each supplier's return policy. If a supplier allows 30-day free returns, the store can offer the same. If a supplier allows no returns, mark those products on the site as not eligible for returns. [L0551.072]
- Read the signed supplier approval documents. They state each supplier's return terms, and the store's return policy is set around them. [L1113.040]
- Your return window must match what the supplier allows. Read the supplier's agreement documents before you set the policy, and show any longer supplier window on the product pages. [L0855.132]
- Return responsibility for heavy items depends on each supplier's policy, which you learn when the supplier approves you. (Older advice, 2019) [L0663.067]
- If suppliers charge return shipping or restocking fees, state those fees in the shipping and returns policy and base the policy on the supplier's terms. (Older advice, 2019) [L1275.033]
- Return policies differ between brands. The store's returns policy should summarise all supplier policies, and each supplier's policy should be noted in the retailer paperwork. (Older advice, 2020) [L1111.128]
- Before listing a brand, verify its return process. Brands with supplier-level support and clear policies reduce return and warranty problems. (Older advice, 2020) [L1111.134]
- Expect higher return rates in some niches, and avoid those niches when choosing products. [L1283.003]
- Compare your return policy with the top two or three competitors in the niche. A policy with refunds within 30 days without a reason, a restocking fee (for example 15% to 20%) and customer-paid return shipping is acceptable if competitors do the same. You can set the fee lower to stand out. [L0805.029]
- To set return shipping fees, look at the return policies of your top future competitors and model yours on them. Choose whether to charge actual cost or a fixed amount. [L1285.068]

### Shipping policy content

- The shipping policy must be specific enough for a reviewer to understand within a minute. It should state when orders ship after purchase (a range is acceptable), the average transit time, and the shipping cost, including whether shipping is free. [L0377.010]
- Vague shipping policies are a known cause of compliance problems. Avoid any wording that could be read as misleading. [L0377.011]
- Use this template for the shipping policy text:

```
Once your purchase goes through, you'll get an order confirmation email. We dispatch every order within [X] days of placement and send you an email with the tracking number as soon as it leaves us. Across the United States, every order ships free through [carrier(s) you typically use]. Choose free shipping and transit takes [X] to [Y] business days. If the store offers faster options, add this line: customers who need quicker delivery can pick [upgraded delivery option] at checkout, and those expedited rates are calculated live at checkout. If the store ships internationally at no charge, add this line: every international order ships free, and with free international shipping transit runs [X] to [Y] weeks.
```
[L0377.012]

- Reference figures from the lesson's US example: dispatch within 3 days, US transit of 3 to 5 business days, international transit of 1 to 3 weeks, carrier USPS. [L0377.012]
- If actual average shipping times are not yet known, check competitors' shipping pages to estimate transit times. The policy can be updated later. It only needs to exist and be accurate from the start. [L0377.013]
- A high-ticket dropshipping store usually does not ship internationally. Publish the international shipping paragraph only if the store actually ships abroad. A store that ships worldwide should state the international transit time in weeks. [L0377.027]
- The shipping page should list the expected shipping time for each supplier or brand, because suppliers ship at different speeds. Update these times as they become clear, for example after a few weeks of order data. [L0910.026]
- Shipping time should be stated in the store's policies, along with contact methods, the return policy and product descriptions, because missing details are a common reason for a store review. (2023) [L0855.139]
- Keep store policies short and clear, not long legal texts. The lesson's policies are only a few sentences each. [L0377.017]
- Many shoppers do not know a new store. Write the shipping policy (cost, delivery time, carrier) and the returns policy in plain English, and make them better than competitors' policies. [L0894.012]
- The contact page must offer at least two different ways to reach the business, such as a phone number and a business email. A contact form may be added, but it does not replace the two methods. [L0377.009]
- Until dedicated support mailboxes exist, use support@ followed by your domain (for example support@yourdomain.com) in the policy templates. Replace it with the real mailbox once it exists. [L1048.013]

### Shipping and returns page placement

- Shoppers read the shipping policy before they spend a lot of money, so it must be easy to find and complete. Place shipping information where buyers look before ordering. [L1130.008]
- The returns and exchange page answers what happens if the buyer is unhappy, which buyers ask before purchasing. Keep it visible before checkout to reduce hesitation. [L1130.009]
- The shipping page can link to the returns policy so customers find return terms next to delivery information. A short shipping statement with a link to the returns page reduces questions. (Older advice, 2021 to 2022) [L1264.012]
- Leaving the return policy off the page leaves shoppers worried about damaged or unwanted items. State easy returns clearly on the page. [L1207.017]
- Most merchandise sold by mail ships subject to return. Return rights are a standard part of direct response selling, and a clear return policy removes the buyer's risk. [L1261.122]
- Do not open the returns and exchanges policy by default, especially a harsh one. Place it in an expandable section for curious buyers, because a long multi-step return process can scare off undecided visitors. [L1016.036]
- Pre-footer trust column: use a clear header such as "Easy returns" and link it to the returns policy page once that page exists. Until then, leave the link empty. [L1309.057]
- Policy pages such as shipping or terms are needed before they are linked in the pre-footer. In the example, a temporary link to terms of service was used and should be replaced with the correct page later. [L1309.058]
- Publish the shipping policy and the returns policy on the store, and make sure everyone handling orders has read both before working on them. [L1291.027]

### Free shipping, add-ons and accessories

- A free standard shipping offer on all products is a common store policy. Expedited shipping or white glove delivery can be offered as paid add-ons. [L1283.058]
- Conditional free shipping is set up by creating separate shipping profiles on the Shopify shipping page, each with the criteria that must be met for it to apply. [L1283.059]
- Accessories with flat-rate shipping should be sold only as add-ons to a main product. Do not list accessories as standalone products or include them in ads and product feeds. [L1283.060]
- When offering free shipping, a customer return means the shipping cost already paid may not be recovered. Design the return policy around each supplier's return rules, not a single store-wide policy. [L0837.042]
- Advertising free shipping while charging customers for return shipping is acceptable if the return costs are clearly disclosed in the policy. Clarity in advance prevents complaints about shipping. (Older advice, 2019) [L1275.034]

### Return window

- Return windows should always have an end date, because unlimited free returns make it impossible to tell when a product is profitable. Match the store's return window to the supplier's policy, for example 30 days when the supplier allows 30 days. (Older advice, 2019) [L1069.093]
- US average return window for physical products is about 30 days. Supplier windows can be 14, 30, 60 or up to 180 days. (2023) [L0855.131]
- No general US legal return period exists for ordinary products that the store must offer. This is general information, not legal advice. (2023) [L0855.138]
- A UK retailer may be required to offer a 14-day return policy for consumer goods, so check local rules before setting the policy. The US has no equivalent blanket rule for most products. (Older advice, 2022) [L0963.135]

  4. If the customer insists on a refund and does not want a replacement, offer a partial refund that is lower than the sale price by the return shipping cost, and let the customer keep the product.

  [L1128.006]
- Example: for a product sold at 50 USD where return shipping would cost the customer 20 USD, offer a partial refund of 30 USD and let the customer keep the item, avoiding the trip to the post office and the wait for the return. The seller stated this may not be the best method, and customers accepted it with mixed reactions. [L1128.007]
- For a low-cost product where return shipping costs 15 to 20 USD, a replacement with the customer paying about 7 to 8 USD for shipping was accepted by most customers. Partial refunds drew more complaints. Lead with the paid-shipping replacement option. [L1128.027]
- When you handle return and replacement emails yourself, read the customer's first message to judge whether they will accept paying for shipping. Offer a paid-shipping replacement to those who seem willing, and a free replacement or a refund to those who do not. [L1128.028]
- Landed cost of a cheap item shipped to the US: supplier price (about 2 to 3 USD) + shipping to the US (about 5 USD) = about 7 to 8 USD. If the customer pays about 7 to 8 USD for shipping on a free replacement, the seller's cost is covered. [L1128.029]

### Store credit and exchange-first returns

- When customers request refunds, offer store credit instead. Store credit keeps revenue on your books, retains the customer within your ecosystem, and lets you fulfil their purchase need with an alternative product or brand you control, maintaining the sale. [L0309.011]
- Return credit options in Loop: store credit delivers a gift card code by email once the return is set up, while a refund to the original payment method is issued minus applicable fees. Refund timing varies. [L0955.032]
- Choose the model that fits the return type:
  - Change-of-mind returns under the 7-day rule are store credit only. [L1291.017]
  - Loop's exchange-first model offers refund or exchange, with bonus credit on refunds. [L0955.033, L0955.034]

### Exchange-first logic in Loop (Checkout Plus)

- Customer return flow with Checkout Plus:
  1. The customer enters the order number and zip code.
  2. Loop detects that the customer is covered for a free return.
  3. The customer selects the return reason and the item to return.
  4. The summary page removes the handling fee for a customer who opted in.
  5. The customer submits the return and chooses store credit or a refund to the original payment method.
  6. The customer buys a label from the preferred carrier, and the label is billed to the brand's FedEx or USPS account.
  7. The customer attaches the label and ships the item back.

  [L0955.031]
- Exchange-first return logic in Loop:
  1. The shopper selects the item to return.
  2. The shopper gives a reason, such as too big, too small or did not like it.
  3. The shopper chooses refund or exchange.
  4. If the shopper wants a refund, Loop offers bonus credit to shop again now.

  [L0955.033]
- A bonus credit of about $5 or $10 for shopping again, added on top of the refund value, can turn a refund into an exchange. The credit works across the full catalogue. [L0955.034]
- Shoppers who want an item in a different size are more likely to exchange than to refund. This is why the return reason question comes before the refund-or-exchange choice. [L0955.035]
- Shoppers who pay for free returns in Loop's model are not charged the handling fee at return. Those who did not opt in pay the handling fee on return. [L0955.036]

### Cross-border returns

- Returns from a customer in another country are usually sent to a local return centre, and the customer pays return shipping. This applies in the UK, the Netherlands and elsewhere. [L0660.034]
- Global return solutions are lacking in dropshipping. Returning a product to a facility where it can be handled matters most for fashion, because fashion return ratios are higher. [L0780.033]
- Returns from overseas dropshipping suppliers are very hard to process. Some stores absorb the cost of a return instead of chasing the supplier, especially when the return is requested after the order has already shipped. [L1315.060]
- Refund process for a customer who requests a refund:
  1. Check whether the customer is in the same country as the store's target market.
  2. If yes, ask the customer to send the product back so it can be resold.
  3. If no, which is the usual case, tell the customer to keep the product.
  4. Refund the customer if the reason for the request is valid.

  [L1439.029]

### Refund abuse and requests outside policy

- Most customers do not abuse refund policies. The minority who do is small, and generous service tends to pay off in brand goodwill. Design the service policy around the honest majority, and handle the few abusers with stricter rules only when they become a real cost. [L0552.004]
- Refund abuse without returning the item is a risk mainly for low-priced items, such as those under about $50. For expensive products it was not seen in practice. [L0970.133] *(2023)*
- Responding to a return request outside the policy:
  1. Send a polite first email that explains the policy.
  2. Include a screenshot of the return policy page and a link to it.
  3. If the customer stays unreasonable, consider accepting the return.

  [L0962.126] *(older: 2021)*
- Customers ask for returns of unopened product during a recall, and they get frustrated when refused. Define a clear recall and unopened-product return process before it is needed. [L1164.022]

## Reducing return and refund rates

### Set the return target and measure it correctly

- Two metrics are in use, and they are not the same. A **return rate** is the share of orders sent back. A **refund rate** is the share of sales refunded. The targets below use different ones, so check which one a benchmark measures before comparing. [L0467.005, L0253.009]
- For premium products sold at premium prices, a return rate of 1 to 2 percent is normal and a good target. Some niches run higher, but 2 percent is a safe planning ceiling. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0467.005]
- For refunds, aim for under 1 percent over a long period, calculated over the whole period and not per 10 items. Damaged items that need a replacement are excluded from this calculation. [L0253.009]
- Track the refund rate per product as a quality measure, because a low refund rate suggests strong product satisfaction. In one example store, about 410 orders were placed without a single refund. [L0492.041]
- Track returns and cancelled orders, and sales discounts, each as a separate percentage of gross sales every month, so a rise in returns is not hidden inside the sales total. In the example store, returns and cancelled orders of 237,813 equalled about 13 percent of gross sales of 1,826,410, and sales discounts of 34,197 were about 1.9 percent. (Older advice, 2021 to 2022.) [L1046.056]
- For fashion, check the net sales after returns before deciding whether a product is viable. [L0693.055]

### Choose niches and suppliers by return risk

- Research the expected return rate for your niche before committing. Start with brand reviews on Google, and look up supplier names on retailer sites for reviews. (Older advice, 2020 and 2022.) [L0245.064, L0249.069]
- Look for patterns of complaints about damage, quality inconsistency and defects. [L0249.069]
- Before signing a supplier, check its brand reviews for complaints about damage, wrong colours, or items that differ from the photos, because these signal a high return rate. [L0667.025]
- A supplier with a high return rate is a product quality problem. Stop selling its products rather than covering the losses with restocking fees. [L0667.026]
- Skip any niche where high returns are expected. Returns cost shipping, support time and revenue at the same time. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0663.004]
- Avoid building a store around products where delivery and returns depend on slow or unreliable chains, because a poor customer experience leads to disputes and loss of repeat customers. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0357.063]
- Avoid niches where customers have strong emotional attachment and tight deadlines. A late or failed delivery can ruin an event and cause severe complaints. Products that must arrive by a fixed date carry much higher service risk. [L0766.005]
- Avoid mattresses, bed sheets and pillows for dropshipping unless the seller is a brand with very high margins. Feel, softness and stiffness cannot be shown on a website, so these personalised items have very high return rates that cost time, labour and money. [L0766.013]
- Products with high return rates or that customers often ask about, such as electronics and apparel, should be avoided, since they need more support and return handling. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0801.123]
- A store with a physical showroom can reduce returns by helping customers choose the right product in person. It can sell online only the brands whose return policies allow it. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0801.125]
- For fashion, returns are common and can be acceptable if sales exceed returns. [L0693.055]
- Sizing-dependent fashion creates high return rates because customers expect Amazon-level return policies. Dropshipping cannot sustainably support sizing-dependent products. [L0209.038]

### Product types with specific refund risks

**Low-priced and impulse products**

- Low-priced markdown products and impulse purchases drive very high reported return rates. Premium products bought after more thought return far less often. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0467.006]
- Very cheap novelty items, around $13 or less, are less likely to be returned, because customers often do not bother sending back a low-value product. Check this against your own return rate before relying on it. [L0655.027]
- The two observations do not conflict. Items under about $13 are returned less often because return shipping is not worth the effort. Low-priced markdown and impulse purchases are returned more often. Apply the $13 rule only after checking your own rate. [L0655.027, L0467.006]
- Gimmicky products carry high refund risk because buyers are often disappointed by the product's real quality. Plan for refund handling and dispute management from the start. [L1211.016]

**Digital products**

- Refund requests for a digital product are a direct signal of low content quality, and they show where the product needs to improve. [L0513.044]
- Make sure the content is accurate and useful before launch, because poor content is what triggers refunds. [L0513.044]

**Assembly-heavy products**

- Include supplier videos showing assembly time, and photos of what the packaged goods look like when delivered. Set customer expectations upfront to reduce returns and complaints. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0249.067]

**Fashion and footwear**

- Some buyers wear fashion items for a weekend and return them with the tags still on, which causes chargebacks. Plan return rules for clothing with this in mind. [L1214.030]
- Bracketing (buying several sizes to keep one) and wardrobing (buying for a single use and returning) push return costs up. Strategic friction in the return policy or at purchase filters out customers who return for no reason without breaking loyal relationships. [L0955.004]
- Categories where the product may differ from the photos, such as jewellery or clothing with sizing or quality variance, cause refund-risk hesitation. [L1208.007]
- For shoes, state clearly on the product page that stock is limited and that returns are not accepted. This discourages buying one pair to send back for another size. Expect that some wrong-size cases will still need a refund. [L1219.007]
- Sizing issues can cause returns, especially for shoes and footwear. Plan refunds into the margin. [L1439.030]

**Electronics**

- Electronics carry a high risk of chargebacks and disputes when quality is poor. Test a new electronic product with the first week of customers, collect reviews and ask about quality before increasing spend. [L1218.029]

### Describe the product so the customer knows what arrives

- Accurate product descriptions and photos, plus staff notes that explain exactly what is in the product, reduce refund requests because customers know what they are buying. Clarity before purchase is the first refund preventer. [L0910.033]
- Sell only what fits the promises you make. Congruent purchases reduce buyer's remorse, refunds and chargebacks. [L0378.097]
- The brand with a typical 20 to 25 percent return rate credited its low returns to accurate product advertising, strong product imagery and a quality product that customers do not want to send back. [L1438.030]

### Set expectations on the sales page

- State customer service speed and the returns policy on the sales page, for example "fast customer service" and "easy returns", so the buyer knows who will help if something goes wrong. [L0919.045]
- Visible support lowers the fear of being left alone with a bad purchase. Place it next to the guarantee and secure checkout messages in the risk-removal section. [L0919.045]
- Reply to customer questions and complaints quickly to keep returns low. One store had no refunds because support was fast and problems were fixed for the customer. [L1439.028]
- A money-back guarantee shown on the product page must match the refund process you actually operate. Make sure customer support can process it without extra conditions the buyer was not told about. [L1188.036]
- A money-back guarantee needs fast, clear refund handling, because the guarantee is part of the offer and affects trust and conversion. [L0871.082]

- Many consumers do not know how expensive returns and shipping are, so they abuse free returns when they are unaware. Making the cost of a return visible is part of keeping abuse down. [L0955.005]
- Surveys cited for 2025 found that in a survey of 1,000 consumers worldwide, about 70% said they would pay for a convenient, premium return experience. [L0955.002]
- Clear refund shipping costs deter buyers who would otherwise order and return, so some low-intent orders are lost on purpose. This is accepted because returns cost more than the lost sale. [L0910.032]
- Check reviews for a brand's products across websites to judge its return rate before deciding on a free-return policy. [L0964.102]
- Free returns can increase the return rate, so decide the policy against your own measured return rate, not on principle. [L1438.029, L0964.102]

### Choose refund windows and guarantee lengths

Sources disagree on length. Each applies in different conditions.

| Guarantee | When it applies | Ids |
|---|---|---|
| 14 days, money back | Unopened products or items with manufacturing defects. Show it prominently in Q4 to build confidence without triggering chargebacks, if the terms are clearly stated. | L0309.010 |
| 30 days, money back | Common standard. Must match the refund process you actually run. | L1439.030, L1188.036 |

- A 90-day guarantee gives buyers a full refund if they return the product within 90 days of receiving it, and it should be stated on the page. [L1194.047]
- For approval or trial offers, most people are honest when they receive a product on approval, and losses from trials that are not paid for are very small. Offering approval or return rights rarely costs more than it earns in trust and sales. [L1261.121]
- Before offering a trial with a payment or return condition, check the cost of processing returns and the share of trials that are never paid for. Build those costs into the cost per customer. [L1261.123]
- Do not charge restocking fees if the main competitors do not charge them. [L0667.025]

### Remove the buyer's risk

- Risk reversal removes the buyer's fear of losing money. Common tools are a 30-day money-back guarantee with a free return, a 100% refund if not satisfied, and a stated free-returns policy. [L0919.026]
- A money-back guarantee works because buyers are loss averse: they fear losing money more than they value gaining a product. Reducing the downside of a purchase increases conversion more than merely highlighting the upside. [L1209.026]
- A returns policy that says no returns are needed and the product is replaced for free reads as a strong guarantee and can lift conversion. [L0577.069]
- Display the 14-day money-back guarantee prominently during Q4 to build confidence, provided the terms are clearly stated. [L0309.010]

### Build the return allowance into the margin

- Build a return allowance into the margin so refunds do not push individual sales into loss. Set the allowance from your expected return rate. [L0436.010]
- Plan refunds into the margin, because sizing issues can cause returns, especially for shoes and footwear. [L1439.030]
- Include the cost of processing returns and the share of trials that are never paid for in the cost per customer. [L1261.123]
- Returns are unavoidable in e-commerce, but they can be reduced, and reducing them is a core task. Plan for returns as a normal part of the business and work to minimise them. (Older advice, 2021 to 2022.) [L1480.026]
- Plan for refund handling and dispute management from the start, particularly for gimmicky products. [L1211.016]

### Keep the customers who return less

- Attract customers who want to wear the product for a long time and keep coming back, rather than customers who buy for a one-time occasion. Long-term customers are less likely to return items. [L1438.031]
- Accurate advertising, strong product imagery and a quality product that customers do not want to send back are the main reasons the brand with a 20 to 25 percent return rate gave for its lower returns. [L1438.030]

## Fraud screening and risky orders

### Shopify fraud analysis levels

- Shopify Payments ranks the fraud risk of each order through fraud analysis. Medium or high risk orders should be checked before they are shipped. [L0360.060]
- Shopify Fraud Analysis scores orders for fraud risk. Use it to review suspicious orders before fulfilling them and to reduce chargebacks. [L1095.011]
- Shopify's built-in fraud analysis flags high-risk orders, which usually have a reason to be high risk. Do not process flagged orders without first contacting the customer. [L0934.099]
- Shopify's fraud analysis rates orders low, medium, or high. A high rating is almost always fraud. A medium rating needs a check of the reason shown plus verification of the customer. [L0856.088]
- Shopify includes risk tools that flag orders likely to be fraud. Use them to reduce stolen-card chargebacks, which are a separate risk from delivery disputes. (Older advice, 2019) [L1086.064]
- The level scales differ between sources. Both are given below, and the 2019 scale is marked as older. [L0467.003, L0856.088]

3. If the customer does not reply, cancel the order and send nothing, since fraudulent orders often use fake phone numbers. [L0360.061, L0960.079]

### Suspicious order patterns

- Suspicious orders often come from emails that ask to pay with an unfamiliar method, such as QuickBooks, and to ship through the buyer's own carrier. Treat this as a likely scam, especially when the buyer wants expensive items shipped to a family member or a vacation home. [L0472.052]

- Bots that add the cheapest product to the cart repeatedly are often Google or Meta testing checkout prices against the product feed. Ignoring them is usually fine. [L0472.053]

- For orders from high-fraud regions, such as the UAE, use Shopify's fraud protection tools, require strong payment confirmation, and remain cautious. (Older advice, 2020) [L0245.062]

- Shopify's native option and the ClearSale app are the two ways to transfer fraud-chargeback risk away from the merchant. [L0467.004]
- ClearSale independently verifies orders and covers the chargeback if an order it marked as safe turns out to be fraud. (Older advice, 2019) [L0467.004]
- ClearSale is a fraud-screening app for e-commerce orders. It reviews transactions to reduce fraudulent orders and chargeback losses. [L0984.005]
- ClearSale reimburses the money if an order it approved is later found fraudulent. One host did not use it personally. [L0804.093]

- Some stores rely only on Shopify's built-in notifications and skip third-party fraud apps, while others use paid fraud tools. Choose based on your chargeback risk. (Older advice, 2021) [L0968.101]
- A chargeback on a stolen credit card is usually lost by the merchant if the order was shipped and no evidence can counter the claim, so the business absorbs the loss. Fraud protection and careful order checks reduce this exposure. (Older advice, 2019) [L0467.001]

### Cancelling when in doubt

- If you think an order might be fraud, treat it as fraud and cancel it. This rule of thumb prevents losses from suspicious orders. (Older advice, 2020) [L0961.053]
- If the customer does not reply to verification, cancel the order and send nothing. [L0360.061]
- If nobody answers or calls back, do not ship the order. [L0467.002]

## Preventing chargebacks and protecting the payment account

### Chargeback causes

- Late fulfillment and customers who believed the product was not authentic or not as described were the main causes in one documented case. Some customers also disputed charges after the brand owner publicly called the seller a scammer. [L0351.009]
- A low-quality product can lead to high chargebacks. Enough of them can get the payment processor to shut the account down, even after the store reaches high sales. Check quality before scaling. [L0335.034]
- Selling a seasonal product outside its season increases chargebacks, because buyers dispute orders for items they do not need in the current weather. Plan the sales window so the product is sold only while its season matches the buyer's need. [L0347.032]
- Dropshipping models with long delivery times and difficult returns carry more chargeback risk, because customers who wait weeks often dispute payments. (Older, 2019.) [L0357.056]
- Customers file chargebacks through their bank or card issuer, not through Shopify. The merchant receives a notification and can respond with evidence, but the card network or the bank decides the outcome. (Older, 2019.) [L1086.062]
- Chargebacks stay rare when products arrive quickly, the store responds to customers, and the product matches what the customer expected. Most chargeback losses come from delays and unclear product expectations. (Older, 2019.) [L1086.065]
- A chargeback can be lost even after delivery if a stolen card was used to order an item shipped to a reachable address. Prevent this with fraud prevention measures on the store, because evidence submitted after the dispute is often not enough. (Older, 2021.) [L1112.036]
- Higher order volume brings more returns, complaints and chargebacks, especially on Shopify Payments or Stripe. Products with thin margins become a risk as sales grow. [L0440.052]
- Orders paid with credit or debt carry higher chargeback risk. The store's economic exposure is higher during a downturn, when customers are panicking. [L1440.048]
- In fashion and streetwear, younger UK buyers (about 21 to 30) converted very well, but refunds and chargebacks were much higher with them. Older UK buyers were easier to sell to. [L1440.047]
- A supplier dispute or a quality problem can raise the PayPal dispute rate. Changing suppliers can therefore cause temporary spikes in disputes. [L1440.049]
- Most German buyers pay with PayPal. Poor customer support leads to many chargebacks in Germany. [L1184.049]
- Chargebacks rise during peak and holiday seasons. A single chargeback can threaten a small merchant. [L1231.047]
- Unsupported health claims may pass for a while, but they eventually cause trouble with platforms. When the promised result does not happen, customers become angry and more likely to return the product or dispute the charge. (Source B course, 2021-22.) [L0416.018]

### Chargeback rate thresholds

The sources give different figures because they describe different contexts. Use the reference that matches your account and payment method.

| Reference point | Value | When it applies | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visa and Mastercard expectation | Below 1%, meaning no more than about 1 chargeback per 100 orders. Above that, card networks issue warnings and the processor may close the account. (Older, 2020.) | General card network rule | [L0468.140] |
| US Stripe and PayPal accounts | About 1% dispute rate described as the point of risk for holds or closure | US accounts, account-specific | [L1512.030] |
| Hong Kong accounts | Up to 6% described as tolerated (account-specific, verify before relying on it) | Hong Kong accounts only | [L1512.030] |
| Reserve case | 3.36% chargeback rate over the last 90 days led Shopify to place 10% of payouts in reserve | Shopify Payments store that had unfulfilled orders charged back | [L1327.017] |
| Example ratio | 17.5% for the period without alert prevention | Lesson example, not a benchmark | [L0351.012] |
| Occasional chargebacks | Two chargebacks across more than 1,000 orders | Treated as a normal cost of business that sales volume covers | [L0910.036] |
| Shopify shutdown trigger | Three chargebacks in one week | Can get a Shopify store shut down | [L1299.030] |

- The chargeback rate is disputes divided by orders. Raising average order value spreads the same number of disputes over more revenue, which is the main practical way to bring the rate back below 1%. [L0351.002]
- Keep chargeback rates low. A high chargeback ratio is one of the main triggers for a merchant account to hold a reserve. (Older, 2021.) [L0671.107, L0671.244]
- Excessive chargebacks can cause a payment provider to stop supporting a store. (Older, 2020.) [L1088.074]

### Account holds and closures

- Shopify rarely closes stores. Closures mostly happen for prohibited products or serious chargeback problems. (Older, 2021.) [L0671.106, L0671.243]
- Account closures for chargebacks are rare. They usually follow from unanswered customers, missed shipments or repeated fraud. To stay within the limits, respond to customers, ship on time and avoid prohibited products. (Older, 2020.) [L0468.141]
- Three chargebacks in one week can get a Shopify store shut down. Shopify may ban the store immediately, without refunding the money it holds. [L1299.030]
- A wrong Disputifier setup can let chargebacks through, which can lead to this situation. [L1299.030]

**Case: Stripe hold.** A seller's Stripe account was placed on a 30-day hold. The seller contacted customers early to resolve complaints, and the disputes were resolved quickly. About 20 days after the hold, the money was released, the dispute rate fell, and no further holds followed. [L1512.053]

**Case: PayPal hold.** A seller with PayPal holds at about 27,000 USD in volume uploaded fulfillment documents when requested, and the funds were released about a week later. The store also found that PayPal made up only about 5% of its payments. The seller paused PayPal for a few days and saw no drop in conversion. Judge holds against how much a payment method actually contributes to sales. [L1512.054]

### Prohibited products

- Products on Shopify's prohibited list, such as supplements, can cause the processor to cancel the account and hold funds. Check the prohibited products list on Shopify's legal page before listing a product. (Older, 2020.) [L0468.142]
- Avoid unsupported health claims. They create platform trouble and lead to angry customers who dispute charges. (Source B course, 2021-22.) [L0416.018]

### Responsiveness and proactive communication

- Not replying to customer emails is the most common cause of chargebacks. Answer every message quickly, reassure the buyer and show empathy. A customer who receives no answer will often dispute the charge. [L0352.022]
- Ignoring customers who ask where their order is leads to chargebacks. Even a reply that only explains a delay keeps the customer from disputing the payment. [L0661.050]
- Contact the customer as soon as a delay is known. Most chargebacks from legitimate customers can be avoided this way, because the frustration that leads to disputes is prevented. [L0360.059]
- Most chargebacks may be prevented with better communication: clear product descriptions, accurate shipping times and a friendly, responsive contact channel. Angry customers who dispute a payment often have unrelated problems, but the store controls what it communicates before the dispute. [L0910.037]
- Unanswered customer tickets make customers angrier. This raises the share of refunds. A rising share of refunds leads to a rising share of chargebacks, so the damage grows faster than sales. (Source B course, 2021-22.) [L0632.013]
- Chargebacks and refunds mostly happen when customers are not answered in time. A fast email reply with a discount code for the next order protects against chargebacks, even with long shipping. [L0820.031]
- Put in the shipping and refund policies that customers must contact the store before filing a chargeback. Keep records of all customer emails. A bank can use a screenshot of the customer's email and an unanswered reply as evidence. [L1381.052]

### Terms checkbox and evidence

- Turn on the terms and conditions checkbox in the checkout area, so customers must agree before checkout. Link it to your terms pages using inline HTML. [L1231.046]
- A recorded terms checkbox and a screenshot of it are evidence to include in a chargeback response. They may not win every dispute. [L1231.047]
- For orders that were shipped as planned, enter the tracking number as evidence to win the dispute. Keeping proof of shipment is what makes this possible. (Source A course, 2024.) [L0360.062]

### Disputifier, Disputely and RDR settings

**Disputifier**

- Disputifier is an app that blocks chargebacks and helps handle disputes when orders go wrong. Set it up before the store starts scaling. [L1299.029]
- A wrong setup can let chargebacks through. [L1299.030]

**Disputely**

- Disputely is a chargeback monitoring and dispute tool. It connects to the store, sends chargeback alerts and fights disputes to recover funds. The lesson presents it as one of the cheapest options. Its billing page lists alert types RDR, CDRN and Ethoca, with prices of $16.00 and $25.00 per alert. [L0351.010]
- Each chargeback costs about $30 in total. Fighting a chargeback with Disputely costs about $20 to $30. The merchant pays these fees even when the chargeback is won. [L0351.011]
- The Disputely dashboard shows the chargeback ratio over time, chargebacks prevented, and the true cost of avoided chargebacks. [L0351.012]
- The Disputes view lists each case with a status such as Enriching or Evidence Ready and a countdown to the deadline. The tool submits evidence automatically about 5 days before the deadline, so review cases and add notes or extra evidence before that point. [L0351.016]

**RDR setting**

- In Disputely Billing, the RDR (Rapid Dispute Resolution) alert setting must show Not active. When it is Not active, the automatic actions read: No refunds will be issued and no subscriptions will be canceled. [L0351.013]
- If RDR stays active, customers can be refunded automatically even after the order has shipped, when the order is seen as not fulfilled fast enough. The merchant then loses the ad spend, the cost of goods and the sale. [L0351.014]

**Procedure: stop automatic RDR refunds**

1. Open the Disputely billing page and check the RDR alert status. [L0351.015]
2. Write an email to Disputely support asking them to disable RDR on your account. [L0351.015]
3. Wait for confirmation, then check that the RDR alert shows Not active and that the automatic refund action reads No refunds will be issued. [L0351.015]

### Pre-dispute refunds and early resolution

- Automatic refunds triggered by RDR are a loss, not a protection, so keep RDR at Not active. [L0351.013, L0351.014]
- Mitigator sends signals before a chargeback is filed to a Stripe or Shopify Payments account. This allows earlier resolution. Third-party gateways with better merchant protection were reported to cost around 400 to 500 (period and currency not stated). Their lower acceptance rate can reduce conversion, so they fit mainly when holds or disputes already occur. [L1512.046]
- Replying quickly with a discount code, before a customer opens a dispute, is the earliest form of resolution available. [L0820.031]

## Responding to chargebacks and payment disputes

### Core principles for every dispute

- Submit far more evidence than seems necessary, such as courier proof of delivery, email threads, order pages, packing slips and the website's policies. The aim is to persuade the payment provider to decide in your favour. [L0350.001]
- Never accept a chargeback. One seller who contested every dispute with delivery evidence reported winning far more disputes than losing them, even with a high chargeback rate. [L1381.060, L1381.051]
- Save the responses from past cases that worked, because they show which evidence to submit and which wording is persuasive. Reuse them as a guide for similar disputes. [L0350.004]
- Answer every customer message, and prepare response templates for each situation. A chargeback is usually lost when a customer messaged and received no reply, because no action was taken. [L1219.053]
- Customer service for chargebacks should not follow a fixed template. First stop the shipment, then decide the next step based on whether the customer responds and whether the chargeback was intentional. [L0856.085]
- Chargebacks can still be lost even with proof of delivery, a customer signature, order emails and shop messages. The case may still be lost if the card was stolen. [L0472.051]
- Chargebacks are relatively rare. With proof of delivery (a tracking number showing delivery), a store is typically in a good position to dispute a claim that the product was not received. [L0971.021]
- Contested chargebacks were won in roughly 50 to 60 percent of cases when the seller had clear policies and responded well. Winning depends heavily on documentation and prompt replies. [L0352.019]

### Ownership and first customer contact

- Assign all chargebacks and payment disputes to one dedicated support team, which also handles high-risk customers in the lesson's setup. That team owns each case from the first customer contact through to the final evidence submission. [L0350.025]
- Check email regularly and respond quickly to any customer who raises a problem or threatens a chargeback. [L0639.038]
- Ask what went wrong and offer a fix first, such as a replacement or a resend. [L0639.038]
- Contact the customer in ways that can be documented, such as email, not only by phone. [L0466.060] *(older: 2018)*
- Keep records of every contact attempt, including unanswered calls and voicemails. [L0466.060] *(older: 2018)*
- On PayPal, if the case has not yet become a chargeback, first try to settle the customer's problem directly, for example with a replacement order or a partial refund. Customers who are satisfied with that outcome often close the case themselves. [L0350.003]
- When a customer stops answering about an incomplete order after a partial shipment, treat it as a warning sign and do not assume the chargeback is lost. Submit proof of delivery such as tracking, and keep all outreach documented. [L0466.071] *(older: 2018)*

### Identify the dispute before you respond

- Chargebacks usually come from three claims: the customer says the order was fraudulent, never arrived, or arrived broken. Each claim needs different evidence, so answer the specific complaint rather than a generic one. [L1381.059]
- Non-receipt disputes differ from fraud claims, where a card was stolen. Identify which kind of chargeback you face before deciding how to respond. [L0466.061] *(older: 2018)*
- The dispute reason shown in the customer's message tells you what evidence to submit. [L0350.024]
- A delivery status can mislead, for example when a parcel is delivered back to the sender. Read the tracking carefully before responding. [L0350.024]
- A sudden cluster of PayPal disputes, such as six in one day shortly after a supplier change, signals that the product or fulfilment being shipped does not match the listing. Check what is actually being delivered before handling the disputes one by one. [L0694.047]

### Matching the ticket and the order

- A customer can have several tickets. Check that the ticket you attach to a chargeback response matches the disputed order before you submit it. [L0350.017]
- Use the customer's details to find their ticket and related correspondence in Gorgias or your email platform. [L0350.015, L0350.005, L0350.018]
- For a ClearPay/AfterPay dispute, the view shows the customer's message, a dispute ID, the merchant order number, the order amount and the dispute amount. Match these against the order in Shopify before you prepare the response, to confirm you are answering the right dispute. [L0350.028]
- In Shopify, the home dashboard counters, such as '1 chargeback to review' and '3 high risk orders to review', are the starting point for finding open chargebacks that still need a response. [L0350.026]

- 'More actions' > 'Print packing slips' produces the packing slip PDF. [L0350.007]
- 'More actions' > 'Print order page' produces the order page PDF with customer details, shipping and billing addresses, order total and payment information. Both are used as evidence. [L0350.007]

### Shopify chargeback response

**Deadlines and set-up**

- Every Shopify chargeback shows a response deadline. If you do not respond, Shopify submits the evidence it holds to the customer's bank on your behalf on that deadline date. [L0350.010]
- Example: a Shopify chargeback for $462.00 showed a deadline of 27 May 2023 at 9:00 am to submit evidence and dispute it. Missing that date means the bank receives only what Shopify has on file. [L0350.011]
- Shopify gives roughly one to two months to contest a chargeback. Example: a 74.81 dollar chargeback had a deadline of November 16 at 3:30 p.m., after which Shopify sends available evidence to the bank automatically. [L1381.050]

### When to refund instead of fighting

**Refund when you were at fault**

- Refund the order instead of fighting a chargeback when the customer was left without a response or shipment. The chargeback may be handled as a refund when you were at fault. [L0804.092]
- Refund a customer rather than let them file a chargeback, because a bank dispute is harder to recover from. A refund keeps the customer and the account in better standing. [L1141.027]
- Refund customers who threaten a chargeback with a valid reason, because chargebacks can lead to holds and blocks on your payout account. [L1219.056]

**Fight when the request has no valid reason**

- Fight chargebacks from customers who ask for a refund without a valid reason. [L1219.056]
- Submit everything for every chargeback, even fraud claims, since many are won. [L0804.091]

## Automation, help desk and self-service tools

### Self-service and proactive support principles

- Omni-channel support brings customer touchpoints from email, social media, phone and SMS into one platform. This lets you serve customers on their preferred channel and move a conversation to a faster channel, such as from email to SMS. [L0401.013]
- Self-service resources (FAQ pages, knowledge bases and chat) let customers find answers themselves. The source states that 88% of customers prefer self-service because it is quick and hassle-free. Self-service also reduces repetitive tickets in the inbox. [L0401.014]
- Proactive support means reaching out before customers contact you, for example with live chat campaigns while they browse the site, or with direct messages to social media followers. This creates chances to answer questions, offer discounts and build happier customers. [L0401.015]
- A shopper who cannot get a fast answer about product fit, shipping or account changes often leaves the store without buying. Instant automated replies across the whole buying journey can keep these visits from ending in lost sales. [L0753.001]
- Self-service quick responses should cover frequent questions that can be answered without a human, so the support team is not distracted from more significant issues. [L0752.010]

### Support channel hierarchy and tool choice

- Start with the chat and automation add-on features (flow builder, quick response flows), then the help center, then the contact form. Tickets that still reach the contact form and could have been solved by the flow builder or quick response flows are handled by rules and auto responses. [L0752.028]
- Use quick response flows for questions with one fixed answer, where one click gives the reply. Use the flow builder only when the right answer depends on a customer detail such as days since the order, region or product, because it asks follow-up questions and branches. Both can run in the same chat widget. [L0752.040]

### Gorgias Automate and the automation ratio

- Gorgias Automate can automate up to 30% of support tickets on average, which lowers first response time and cost. Some brands report higher rates, but 30% is the planning figure. [L0752.001]

### Gorgias AI agent

- Gorgias is a conversational AI platform for e-commerce stores, built both to answer support questions and to drive sales through one-to-one chat conversations. Its AI agent works as an automated front-line team member. [L0753.002]
- AI chatbots can handle customer service 24/7. The stated purpose is to reduce refunds and increase customer satisfaction. [L1459.013]
- AI reply generators inside a live chat app can handle standard customer questions automatically, which reduces response time for common requests. [L1416.008]
- Routine requests, such as changing a shipping address or modifying a subscription, can be resolved automatically by the AI agent without a human handling them. This shortens response times for simple inquiries. [L0753.005]
- When the AI agent hands a conversation over to a human agent, the human sees the full customer history, profile and order data already in the same place. The human can answer personally without asking the customer to repeat information. [L0753.008]
- Quality control of a Gorgias AI support agent runs in this order:
  1. Gorgias runs rigorous quality checks on the agent. [L0753.006]
  2. The store's own team reviews the agent, so that responses are accurate and match the brand voice. [L0753.006]

### Quick response flows

- Quick response flows are set up under `Automation > Quick response flows` (Automation Add-on). Up to 6 buttons can be displayed in the chat widget with common questions. A click gives an instant answer. If the customer needs more help, a ticket is created for an agent. [L0752.008]
- Buttons appear in the order set in the list. Add new flows with `+ Add Quick Response Flow`. [L0752.008]
- Build quick response flows in this order: [L0752.009]
  1. Analyze the first 30 to 45 days of tickets after launch to find the questions asked most often. [L0752.009]
  2. Pick the six most frequent questions. Examples include product mineral-based check, vegan and cruelty-free status, how to create an account, how to pick the right shade, track and manage orders, and return policy or flow builder questions. [L0752.009]
  3. Create one quick response flow per question in `Automation > Quick response flows`. [L0752.009]
  4. Use all six button slots as soon as the brand has enough recurring questions. [L0752.009]

## Post-sale calls, reviews and customer feedback

### Why post-sale contact matters

- Most e-commerce stores see only about 1%, 5% or 10% of customers leave a review. A structured post-sale call program can raise the review rate to roughly 50% or more. [L1011.001]
- Post-sale calls serve two goals at once. They make the customer feel looked after by checking that their needs are met and their questions answered, and they bring in reviews and feedback for the business. [L1011.002]
- Post-sale calls reveal what customers expect, want, or would like improved. This can be information the business did not have before. [L1011.003]
- A customer who receives an unexpected, helpful follow-up call is more likely to talk about the business and to buy again. Sending upsell messages before or just after the call strengthens this effect. [L1011.004]
- Reviews will not come on their own. The business has to ask for them actively, most effectively by phone. Relying on passive hope is not an effective approach. [L1012.009]
- Asking customers for feedback on the business reveals improvement opportunities and is often a core part of conversion rate optimization. It also makes customers feel they have input, which increases their mental investment and the chance they buy again. [L1052.001]
- Customer feedback will not arrive on its own. Ask for it proactively, every time you want to learn something about the business. [L1052.002]
- Feedback can point to website usability problems, content gaps customers want filled, weak customer service, or issues with how products are delivered. Targeted customer answers show where the business can grow. [L1052.003]
- Track customer feedback and queries to find weak points that are not visible in analytics. Be proactive about shipping delays or site issues. [L1259.053]
- After-sale service (refunds, returns, damages and defects) shapes the feedback score and loyal customers. Be proactive about shipping delays and monitor customer feedback for product or page issues. [L1260.054]
- Request reviews from happy customers consistently, and track the number of reviews relative to sales volume. *(Older advice, 2021.)* [L0962.130]

### CSAT: measurement and analysis

- CSAT data is in Gorgias under the Statistics section, in the Satisfaction view. It shows a chronological list of tickets, including ratings of 4 stars or lower. The Satisfaction dashboard has a CSV download button to export all CSAT scores. [L0401.005]
- CSAT ratings alone have limited value. The written customer comments, especially negative ones, explain what to change in customer service. [L0401.008]
- CSAT is shaped by what happens before and after purchase, not only by support. Clear product descriptions set expectations; good product photography, faster and more reliable shipping, free shipping for eligible customers, and a helpful post-purchase experience all raise satisfaction. [L0401.012]
- When a customer gives a low CSAT score without an explanation, proactively reach out and ask for written feedback. This reveals why the rating was given, what the customer expected, and how to prevent lower scores in the future. [L0401.007]

## Reputation management and complaint handling

### Principles of reputation work

- Poor reviews and complaints are inevitable at scale: one complaint in a thousand customers is normal. [L0790.071]
- Respond honestly, and interact with critics while the audience is still small enough to do so. [L0790.071]
- Public negative comments cannot be fully prevented, so the practical response is to resolve each customer's complaint quickly and directly. Consistent service reduces the number of unhappy buyers over time. [L0492.067]
- Happy customers refer other customers, while unhappy customers search out every public channel to complain. [L1472.014]
- Customers judge a brand by how the retailer treats them. Suppliers approve retailers only if they will treat customers fairly, because a retailer represents the brand's name and must protect its reputation. [L1422.012]

### Response standard and speed

- Answer complaint emails quickly. Acknowledge the customer's frustration and offer a concrete solution such as a refund or fix. Response speed is part of the solution and prevents disputes. [L1141.028]
- Reply to negative reviews within 24 hours. Acknowledge the specific problem, state the action taken and give a direct contact route. [L1164.025]
- Trustpilot shows a company's reply rate and reply time. The example brand replied to 99% of negative reviews, typically within 24 hours. Use these figures as the benchmark for your own response standard. [L1164.023]
- Update customers proactively about shipping delays, before they have to ask. [L0637.022]
- Reply fast to Facebook comments, DMs and Messenger messages. Fast replies signal good business practice to Meta and can help move the business up a tier. [L0637.022]

### Triage of negative reviews

- Older advice (Source A, 2021), still valid as a sequence. Handle a negative review as follows: [L0968.097]
  1. Decide whether it comes from a real customer, a competitor or a troll. [L0968.097]
  2. For a real customer with a fixable problem, try to fix it calmly. [L0968.097]
  3. For fake or unjustified reviews, request removal. [L0968.097]
  4. For customers who cannot be satisfied, stop engaging and focus on good customers. [L0968.097]
- The business cannot edit or delete reviews itself. Its only direct action on a review is a reply. [L0694.038]
- Removal, as described in step 3 above, applies only to fake or unjustified reviews. [L0968.097, L0694.038]

### Payment, collections and chargebacks

**Collection letters: persuasion and threat in turn.** Alternate persuasion with the threat of loss in a collection series. A strong threat frightens some debtors into paying, but it angers others, so follow it with a persuasive letter that restores goodwill before the next threat. [L1369.098]

**Collection letters: appeal to honesty.** Formula: remind the debtor of an honest trait he is proud of, such as paying one's debts, and then say that his account reflects that trait. Why it works: a debtor who sees himself as honest is more likely to pay than one who feels accused. [L1369.099]
- Example one: Most of our customers pay their accounts promptly because they value their word, and we are confident you are one of them. [L1369.099]
- Example two: Our records show your history of paying on time, and this one invoice is simply waiting for your confirmation to close. [L1369.099]

**Collection letters: amount, date, consequence and benefit.** Formula: state the exact amount owed, the specific date by which payment must arrive, the consequence if it does not, and the benefit of paying now. Why it works: a precise deadline and consequence are more likely to prompt action than a general reminder. [L1369.100]
- Example one: Your balance of 25.00 dollars is due by the 17th. If payment is not received by then, your account will be recorded as overdue in our credit file. [L1369.100]
- Example two: Your invoice of 340 dollars is due by the 30th. Paying by then keeps your account in good standing and lets you order next month without a hold. [L1369.100]

**Collection letters: specific threats.** Threats in collection letters must be specific: state the amount, the deadline date and the exact next step, such as a transfer to the legal department. A vague warning is easy to ignore. [L1369.101]

**Collection letters: before and after record.** In a payment reminder, show the debtor the exact current entry on his account and the entry it will become if payment does not arrive by a set date. A visible before-and-after record makes the consequence concrete. Keep the consequence limited to actions you are legally entitled to take. [L1369.123]

### Customer targeting

**Aim for the right buyers.** Aim to attract the right buyers rather than the most buyers. Customers who do not fit the product create a poor business, complaints, and extra costs. [L0378.100]

**Pain-point research.** Find out what the product solves, what customers say in reviews, and what problems they face in daily life, to build a precise customer avatar. [L1158.020]

**Customer conversations before private label.** Before private labeling a main product, the store should already have a substantial sales history, many customer service interactions and ongoing contact with customers. These provide the data needed to find differentiation. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22) [L1155.005]

### Platform support, accounts and policy compliance

**Support channels for a training program.** Support is available through the private, members-only Facebook group, where questions are answered by the instructor and experienced high-ticket dropshippers, and by email to support@dropshipbreakthrough.com (the link is in the page header). Before posting, search the group for the question, as it may already be answered. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22) [L1496.009]

**Shopify theme and app support.**
- Shopify Support for theme edits: contact Shopify directly for a theme question, such as where to place a Klaviyo embed code in the Dawn theme, since Shopify built the theme and can give the exact location. (Source A 2024) [L0856.090]
- Store and product issue support: email the support address with the theme version used. The developers can locate code added elsewhere, including custom code, that changed a setting. (Source A 2024) [L0856.091]
- Send technical Shopify questions to the support address of the course provider, which routes to the developer team. This is not the coach's area of expertise. (Source A 2023) [L0964.112]
- When business email support is needed, contact the store's support address and describe the page and the problem. The team then routes the request to the right designer or developer. (Source A 2022) [L1092.098]
- For a support problem with Google Search Console or other tools, email the support team with the exact error message, since the team may already have a fix from earlier cases. (Older advice, Source A 2021) [L1090.121]

**Unplanned Shopify theme code error (procedure, Source A 2024).** Use this when red error lines appear in the theme editor after removing a third-party app's JavaScript.
1. Find the extra or missing bracket or line that was removed. [L0971.036]
2. If unsure, post a job on Upwork for a Shopify developer describing the exact red error. [L0971.036]
3. A five-minute fix is typical; a budget of about $20 is usually enough. [L0971.036]

**Bulk price changes.** Shopify bulk edit and product export are the approach for price changes in bulk. No repricing app is used by the team. (Source A 2024) [L0971.077]

**Digital content protection.** When content is copied, first message the copier once to remove it. If there is no response, file a DMCA takedown with the host, for example Shopify for a Shopify store. (Older advice, Source A 2022) [L0969.039]

**Store name collisions.** If another store uses the exact same name and sells the same products, change the store name and buy a new domain to avoid consumer confusion. (Older advice, Source A 2022) [L0969.094]

**Advertising account restrictions.** Ad accounts and business assets can be restricted when the landing page or creatives break platform policy, for example with before and after claims or weight loss claims. Audit the landing page and creatives against the policy before launching or reusing creatives. (Source C) [L0638.049]

**Google Merchant Center.**
- Google Merchant Center policy violations caused by automated bot scrapes can be contested. Request a manual review, then ask for the domain to be whitelisted so it is reviewed manually before suspension. (Older advice, Source A 2022) [L0665.088]
- Merchant accounts can be suspended if the account is set up from abroad while listing a US address. If this happens, appeal and provide the correct details. (Older advice, Source A 2022) [L0665.089]

**Facebook account bans due to ID issues.** Submit the ID review in Meta Business settings. It usually takes 24 to 48 hours. If it does not work, contact the account seller or a support provider. (Source C) [L0773.052]

**Pinterest.** Pinterest's support team can help with account bans, restrictions and ad insights. The phone number on the business account is used for support, not verification. Do not follow Pinterest's general marketing advice for campaign decisions, because it is not aimed at direct response selling. (Source C) [L1134.036]

### Ads and click quality

**Click-through rate threshold.** Do not scale ad spend when click-through rates fall below 2%. A low CTR indicates poor product-market fit and will exhaust budget without converting traffic. (Source C) [L0324.024]

**Click fraud.** A third-party click fraud service tested at the time was not worth the cost. The author cancelled after 30 days and does not recommend it. Use Google's Invalid Click analysis and account audit first. (Source A 2024) [L0856.089]

### Marketplace delivery commitments (Google Shopping Actions)

Older advice, Source B 2021-22.

**Joining Shopping Actions.** Joining Shopping Actions requires the merchant to state specific delivery time frames for orders and to keep them. Other marketplaces use the same kind of delivery commitment. [L0738.010]

**Delivery reliability before joining.** Before joining, check that you can deliver within the stated time frame at least 90% of the time. Example: if you promise delivery in 7 days, at least 90% of orders must arrive within 7 days. If you cannot, do not join, because suspension will follow. [L0738.011]

**Seller score.** The seller score is driven by buyer feedback on delivery times, return rates and cancellation rates, each with thresholds. A poor seller score is hard to recover from. [L0738.016]

**Retailer Standards score.** The Retailer Standards score is calculated from order defects such as late deliveries and high cancellation rates. A merchant must deliver 20 items to receive a score the following month, and the score is checked on the first day of each month. [L0738.027]

**Defect trends.** Late deliveries and a high order cancellation rate both lower the Retailer Standards score. Check the defect trends in Merchant Center regularly so that problems are corrected before the monthly score is set. [L0738.028]

**Daily order check.** The Shopping Actions Today's Snapshot in Merchant Center flags orders as due today when they are pending shipment, missing tracking information or have wrong tracking information. Clear these flags daily, because late or untracked orders reduce the Retailer Standards score that determines Shopping Actions visibility. [L0738.040]
