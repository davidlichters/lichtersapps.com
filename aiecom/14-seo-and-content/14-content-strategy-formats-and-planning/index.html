14.14 Content strategy, formats and planning

Role of Content in the Funnel

  • Without content, a store depends on paid channels such as Google ads, paid social and affiliate marketing for traffic. Content adds organic traffic that does not depend on ad spend alone. [L0500.012]
  • Search engine optimisation cannot be done effectively without content, because product pages give search engines too little material to rank for informational searches. [L0500.007]
  • Content has four jobs: answering customer questions and solving problems, gaining attention and engagement, generating traffic from search engines and YouTube, and building backlinks to the site. Plan each piece for at least one of these jobs. [L0386.002]
  • Product pages are commercial, while content is educational and entertaining. Product pages fit the product-aware and ninja-aware stages. Buyers at the problem-aware and solution-aware stages need educational content, such as a guide that explains what to look for and recommends specific products. [L1117.014]
  • A store made only of collection pages and product pages can answer questions only at the product-aware and ninja-aware stages. Content lets a store serve the whole awareness pyramid more easily than any other marketing mechanism. [L1117.013]
  • Competitors who reach people in the early awareness stages first gain attention, engagement and trust, and lock those buyers into their own sales funnel. A brand that appears only at the product-aware stage must win attention away from sources the customer already trusts. [L1117.012]
  • To be present everywhere in the buyer's journey, a brand must engage customers with both content and products. Product pages belong where the buyer already knows what they want, but they should not be the entire journey. [L1117.016]
  • Educational content builds know, like and trust and helps buyers decide intelligently. Show both the positives and the negatives of a product, because not every visitor is a potential customer. [L0382.004]
  • Pain-point content can rank in search engines and bring traffic, and it guides people through their customer journey. This supports conversion and authority, so it is a strong starting point. [L0386.004]
  • A buyer's guide raises the authority of a store, educates customers and links to products that fit what the reader wants. It can be offered as a downloadable PDF with a clear format and relevant product links. [L0837.063]
  • Buyer's guides, content on the store, and content co-created with suppliers help organic traffic and build the store's content base. (older, 2021) [L0801.088]
  • Publish unique long-form content as video on YouTube or as articles on a blog, and use social platforms to link people there. These platforms are consumption-focused, so viewers who stay are highly qualified. [L0487.059]
  • Written blog content is a worthwhile format on its own, because readers like to read and search engines favour written content. It can also be combined with video. (older, 2021-22) [L0462.026]
  • Content is one part of a connected marketing system, not a standalone task. The same pieces feed email flows, SEO, link building and retargeting. Automated email sequences can use site content in their first emails. (older, 2021-22) [L0381.002]
  • Content pieces published on the store's own website are later reused for link building and SEO. Plan each piece with a place on the site, not only for social channels. (older, 2021-22) [L0381.018]
  • Produce content from a researched, documented plan made in advance, not from topics picked at random. Random content usually performs poorly, and the failure is then wrongly blamed on content marketing. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.001]
  • Content delivers its value when it is produced and maintained on an ongoing basis. Treating it as a one-off project limits the results it can bring. [L0500.013]
  • Most e-commerce stores have no regular content production system when they start. A store without one should build it from the beginning. A store already publishing content should first check whether the content fits the market, then promote it. [L0500.011]

When to Start Content

  • Start blog content marketing once the store has traffic, so you know which topics attract visitors. Content made for the blog can also be reused on social media. [L0835.096]
  • Buyer's guides and content are most useful after the store has traffic, because the data then shows which products visitors actually want. [L0855.154]
  • Start a done-for-you content package only after the store is complete and has traffic data, so you know which products visitors view. Spending on content before there is data is wasted. [L0804.062]
  • Done-for-you content packages add long-term value through unique content and organic traffic, but they do not produce early sales. If a store still has open site bugs or unprofitable ads, fix the site and make the paid traffic profitable first. (older, 2021) [L0801.184]
  • On a new affiliate site, publish three to five blog posts before buying traffic. The posts show visitors that you know the topic and give you something to link to when you share the site elsewhere. [L0514.008]
  • Sending traffic to a new affiliate site with no posts leaves you nothing to link to when you use free methods such as forum replies or YouTube comments. Publish a few posts before you send people there. [L0514.039]
  • If there are no competitor blogs in your exact niche, use the closest related content, such as blog posts from related products or from the buyer's own interests, as a starting reference for ideas. [L0804.063]
  • Do not spend time deciding the perfect order of content ideas. Pick any idea and start producing, because the cost of waiting is higher than the cost of a suboptimal first choice. Research can start immediately and the plan can be adjusted later. (older, 2021-22) [L0381.008]
  • Trying to fully prioritise content ideas before starting wastes time that could be spent producing content. (older, 2021-22) [L0381.012]

Content Types and Formats

Choosing the format

  • Use one of three content formats: written only, video only, or a combination of both. Pick the format before planning or producing anything. (older, 2021-22) [L0383.005]
  • Suitable content formats for keyword topics include buyer's guides, how-to articles, entertainment or list posts, industry announcements and new product type alerts. Choose the format to match the search intent of the keyword. [L1120.002]
  • A search term does not depend on the format of the page that targets it. The same keyword can be targeted with a video, a blog post or another written format, so the format choice does not change the keyword assignment. (older, 2021-22) [L0877.034]
  • Mix the content themes in your plan. Problem-solving pieces include how-tos, listicles, ultimate guides and product comparisons. Entertaining pieces include best-of lists, breaking news and controversial takes. (older, 2021-22) [L0384.006]
  • Mixing text, images and video suits readers who prefer different formats. Some want the reasons in text, some want photos of real customers with the products, and some want a video that repeats the text. (older, 2019) [L0467.016]
  • Every blog post should include three elements: text, images and a video, with the images and video placed within the text. Videos should come from the suppliers or the store owner. (older, 2019) [L0467.017]
  • Extensive content libraries such as comparisons, how-tos and buying guides are a strong SEO strategy and can drive a large part of a competitor's traffic. Note the content types and count them. (older, 2021-22) [L0893.032]

Type reference

Recommended length and purpose by content type:

Content Type Recommended Length Purpose
Product release (new item announcement with photos) ~500 words Announce inventory and drive immediate interest
How-to guides, repairs, tutorials, tips, user guides 1,000-2,000 words Build authority and rank for informational keywords
Competitor comparisons 1,000+ words Capture 'best of' searches
Product reviews 1,000+ words Rank for specific product queries
News and announcements 500+ words Show fresh inventory and promotions
Testimonials/customer stories 500-1,000 words Build trust and provide social proof

[L0226.011]

  • A listicle can work as top-of-funnel content for a shop that sells related products. A surf shop publishing a top 10 list of local surf spots attracts searchers who are not ready to buy. Product pages remain the commercial targets, and the listicle serves to attract the audience. (older, 2021-22) [L0662.018]
  • Best-of pages and listicles place your own product first, so they only work if the product is genuinely the best option in its category. Build the product quality first, then the pages. (older, 2021-22) [L0768.010]
  • Brand versus brand comparison pages can either compare your lesser-known brand with a well-known brand, or compare two well-known brands and recommend yours at the bottom of the page. (older, 2021-22) [L0768.011]
  • News-jacking content from a source article, such as a medical article:
    1. Find the article.
    2. Quote the relevant parts.
    3. Link to the original source and mention it.
    4. Add your business commentary. Do not use content behind a paywall. [L0804.061]

Length and Depth

  • Use the type-based lengths in the table above as the starting point for each piece. [L0226.011]
  • Blog post length is 300 to 1,000 words depending on the topic. Do not inflate with filler; write what is necessary to explain the topic fully. Blog posting every other week is effective. (older, 2022) [L0272.039]
  • Aim for quality over quantity in each article, because that is what currently ranks. Google increasingly favours authoritative, long pages that cover several related keywords rather than thin pages for one keyword. [L0382.014]
  • To rank for a competitive search term, a new piece should cover its subject in more depth than the pages already on page one. Google tends to favour longer-form content, so a thin article is unlikely to outrank existing pages. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.022]
  • When judging a competitor's content, check its volume and quality separately. Long, in-depth pages with images and links to products suggest higher quality. Short pages that answer simple questions suggest lower quality. Quality gaps reveal opportunities. (older, 2021-22) [L0893.031]

Idea Sourcing

Customers and their questions

  • To find content ideas, work through four questions in this order:
    1. Which questions, problems and frustrations do my buyers run into that connect to what I sell?
    2. Which content subjects related to my products are already being shared on social platforms and through email?
    3. Which content already ranks in search engines for the keywords I am targeting?
    4. Which product-related content topics are other websites already linking to? (older, 2021-22) [L0386.003]
  • Customer service records and customer surveys show real pain points, the questions customers ask before buying, and what they struggle with or wish they knew more about. Ask existing customers directly and treat their answers as sources for early content topics. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.013]
  • Choose which content to create based on keyword research, using the questions real customers ask in the niche. [L1227.024]
  • Keyword-based content should match the questions customers ask, so the question itself can become the post's target keyword. [L1227.024]

Communities and social signals

  • Content research starts with what already performs in your market. Check what popular creators publish and which topics their audiences watch. Use this to model and adapt topics rather than copying content. (older, 2021-22) [L0478.017]
  • Content gaps are topics that large audiences in your market do not cover well. Finding them gives you content opportunities where you can serve buyers with less competition. (older, 2021-22) [L0478.020]
  • Shareable content can be found by looking for existing topic pieces with high social engagement or shares. Create something similar but more in-depth, so it has a good chance of getting the same engagement. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.005]
  • Shareable topics can be found with BuzzSumo, which shows which topics in your niche are trending by displaying Facebook engagement counts, Twitter mentions, share frequency and related articles. Use this research to align content strategy with audience interest. [L0286.028]
  • Topics with many upvotes, likes or comments show what the niche audience engages with and make good content candidates. Reddit threads with question lists can also provide related keywords for a blog post. [L1120.032]
  • Content idea research on Reddit:
    1. Search your niche keyword plus Reddit on Google.
    2. Open the niche subreddit (for example the standing desks community with about 27,000 members).
    3. Look at the recent posts sorted by the number of upvotes, and note topics that could become a blog post.
    4. Click the top sort option and change the time filter to all time to see the most upvoted posts that can serve as evergreen topics. [L1120.029]
  • Example: a Reddit question about whether paint on the legs of a standing desk wears off led to a blog topic on customizing a standing desk, including which paint lasts. The question idea was added to the worksheet as a blog post keyword. [L1120.030]
  • Content idea research on Facebook groups:
    1. Go to Facebook and type your niche into the search bar.
    2. Click Groups.
    3. Join relevant groups and look for posts with many likes and comments.
    4. Add those topics and keywords to the worksheet. [L1120.031]
  • Example: question-style titles from competitor blogs can be used directly as blog topics in a niche such as standing desks. Examples are how long should you stand at a standing desk and tips for buying an adjustable standing desk converter. Each one should be added to the keyword worksheet as a blog post keyword. [L1120.041]
  • Log content ideas from communities:
    1. Join Facebook groups in your niche and note the questions people ask.
    2. Join relevant Reddit communities and collect the discussion topics that come up repeatedly.
    3. Search Google for niche topics to see what people are asking about.
    4. Log each content idea in a Google Sheet, with one column for the idea and one column for the link to the source it came from. [L0472.075]
  • Example of an AI-assisted idea chain, applied to a food brand:
    1. Every Monday, scrape a relevant Reddit forum for the niche, for example r/cooking for a food brand.
    2. Use Chat2T to summarise all new posts and extract key insights.
    3. Send those insights to Claude to generate new marketing angles the brand has not yet tested.
    4. Send each angle back to Claude to write a complete UGC video script.
    5. Add all generated scripts to ClickUp for the production team. This chain turns raw community conversations into ready-to-produce creative briefs automatically. [L0287.020]

Brainstorming the first ideas

  • Plan content topics six months ahead by collecting 12 content ideas as the starting point. Include any ideas already in your head, and brainstorm the rest across different content types for your market or niche. (older, 2021-22) [L0384.005]

Keyword and SERP Research

Keyword discovery

  • Keyword and traffic tools such as Ubersuggest, Ahrefs, Semrush and Moz show, for a search term, what content already exists, its social share counts, its organic traffic and its backlink counts. Use them to judge what already exists for a topic. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.011]
  • Keyword research for the content plan can be done yourself or outsourced. If it has not been done yet, do it before entering the first content ideas. (older, 2021-22) [L0381.001]
  • Keyword pass output: the minimum output of a first keyword pass is a list of terms to target with content. (older, 2021-22) [L0877.034]
  • Build a keyword list from informational queries before writing. [L0380.014]
  • Style-based keyword research for homewares, using a style name rather than a product term:
    1. Search for style-based keywords related to your products, such as an interior style name, and note monthly volume and keyword difficulty.
    2. Choose styles with usable volume and low difficulty, and estimate the number of backlinks needed to rank.
    3. Write a style guide that explains the defining characteristics of the look and how to recreate it at home.
    4. Create product-type guides for the style, such as beds, dining tables or wardrobes.
    5. Link from the guides to lists of your top products for a themed room, so the content leads down to products. [L0719.049]
  • Example of a style-based entry point: a keyword such as Hamptons furniture had 2,000 to 3,000 monthly searches, keyword difficulty of 20, and an estimated need for about 20 backlinks to rank. [L0719.039]
  • Content aimed at style searches sits higher in the funnel and suits homewares, because buyers start with a look or style rather than a specific product. The content can then lead down to product lists. [L0719.040]
  • Keyword research workflow step 1 uses the Ahrefs Content Gap method to choose keywords, and RAW is used to vet them. [L0382.039]
  • Evergreen keywords are optimised once on existing pages such as collections, product pages or permanent pages. Trending keywords can justify one or two new blog posts per month. Plan the two groups separately. [L1120.023]
  • Holiday and promotion keywords are covered in the holiday section below. [L1222.011]

Checking what ranks

  • To check what already ranks for a target keyword:
    1. Search the target keyword in Google.
    2. Note the URLs of the content pages in the top five organic positions on page one.
    3. Record those URLs in the content research document.
    4. Have the person producing the content review those pages before writing and identify where they can go further with a better piece. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.006]
  • If no content pages rank on page one for a keyword, do not drop the keyword. It means ranking should be easier with reasonable effort. If three or four content pages already rank, they are the competition to beat. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.007]
  • Finding no similar online content is not a bad sign and does not mean the content will fail. It only means the topic may be new ground with less existing competition. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.020]
  • Research does not have to be finished for every planned idea, but some research should always be done in advance so that part of the planned content is already researched. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.016]
  • Content research for a niche keyword:
    1. Search Google for your main niche keyword, for example ring light.
    2. Analyse all organic results on the first page and ignore paid ads.
    3. Note the format Google returns for the query, such as top 10 lists, best-of pages or buying guides.
    4. Build your guides, videos and reviews to match those formats and topics, instead of producing large volumes of content blindly.
    5. Over a few months, this content that mirrors what already ranks lifts a new site against established competitors. (2018, older) [L1274.004]
  • Publishing more content on a fixed weekly schedule does not automatically improve rankings. SEO gains for a new site usually build slowly, often over several months. (2018, older) [L1274.003]
  • A niche that is new in a country can have high-volume keywords with little competition, so ranking can be possible with content alone. Content should target those keywords before adding backlinks. [L1153.062]
  • Case: the seller found keywords with high search volume and low competition in the adult coloring book niche, which is new in the US. The seller wrote seven blog articles of about 500 words each, targeting 10 keywords, with no backlinks, press releases or other link building. The result was page 2 rankings for nine of the 10 keywords and a page 3 ranking for one, which the seller saw as the basis for future free organic traffic. [L1153.061]

Competitor Research

  • Check competitor content for every new content piece: have competitors covered this topic? If they have, note it and aim to do it better. If no competitor does content marketing in the niche, this part of the research can be skipped. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.009]
  • The competitor research document made early in the programme asks whether each competitor does content marketing. Check that answer before searching for competitor content. Competitors with no content marketing can be skipped in content research. Those with content get noted and must be beaten. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.023]
  • Do not reinvent the wheel. In markets with existing content, study what works and do everything better. In markets with little content, you are breaking new ground, and organic competition is usually low. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.008]
  • The attention matrix lists the people, pages and organisations the audience already follows. Use it to find out whether those sources have already produced similar content. It can be shared with any writer or producer. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.010]
  • The attention matrix is a document that maps where a store's audience pays attention, and it can be delegated to a virtual assistant. (older, 2021-22) [L0381.010]
  • BuzzSumo and Social Animal work like a search engine for content that was shared on social networks. Enter a topic to see shared pieces and how others approached it. Use this for inspiration rather than copying. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.012]
  • BuzzSumo has a free sign-up that lists the most popular pages and articles for a niche keyword. Results are sorted by social shares and engagement on Facebook and Twitter, which shows what the audience cares about. (older, 2019) [L0467.011]
  • Steps to pick content topics from a competitor's blog:
    1. Use the free version of BuzzSumo to search your niche keywords.
    2. Find which of the competitor's posts have the most shares or comments.
    3. Identify the theme of the best-performing content.
    4. Create your own content on a similar but different angle, rather than rewording theirs. (older, 2020) [L0961.075]
  • Competitor collection pages, step by step:
    1. Search the competitor category on Google (for example L-shaped standing desks).
    2. Note the content types ranking (product pages, collection pages, top 10 lists).
    3. If lists rank, create your own top 10 article on the collection page.
    4. Review the top results and determine the features and topics they cover.
    5. Write your own content covering the same topics, but better.
    6. Add it below the products on the collection page. [L0274.038]
  • Source C research procedure for a product you sell:
    1. Select a product you are selling.
    2. Search Google for articles on that product.
    3. Read the top three to five ranking articles on the first page of results.
    4. Note the repetitive patterns, topics and information covered across those articles.
    5. Combine the best information from all of them.
    6. Write a new article in your own words covering those same topics (you can use AI to assist).
    7. Post it to your blog to acquire Google SEO ranking. [L0280.021]

Pattern Matching

Matching formats that already work

  • Research-based content process that avoids copying competitors:
    1. Find the most popular content of competitors, such as blog posts with the most comments or product pages with strong descriptions.
    2. Describe the intro, the body and the conclusion of that piece in one sentence each.
    3. Have the writer start from scratch with that framework, using their own words. (older, 2019) [L0467.009]
  • Match the format Google returns for your keyword. Build guides, videos and reviews that mirror the top formats and topics. (2018, older) [L1274.004]
  • Pattern for content aimed at solution-aware and problem-aware buyers: a broad educational guide titled as the ultimate guide to a product category for a specific buyer type. It explains the buyer's needs, lists the characteristics to look for and recommends a short set of specific products that meet them. Two new examples: The Complete Guide to Choosing a First Road Bike for Commuters and The Ultimate Guide to Picking a Beginner Espresso Machine for Small Kitchens. [L1117.015]
  • Page types to build on the store for SEO, in this order:
    1. Category collection page with buying-intent keywords, turned into a landing page with your product at the top.
    2. Best-of page for the category with the current year in the title.
    3. Listicle, for example a top seven list for the current year.
    4. Generic category page.
    5. For-sale page.
    6. Top seven page.
    7. Brand versus brand comparison pages. (older, 2021-22) [L0768.008]
  • Brand versus brand comparison pages are covered under Content Types and Formats above. [L0768.011]
  • Style guides lead into product lists, so the top products for a themed room sit after the guide. [L0719.040]

Filling in the planner

  • A content title can start as a broad topic and be refined later. The overview should state who the content is for, what it should teach or achieve, and the goal of the piece. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.017]
  • Target keyword and similar online content are recorded in this order:
    1. Take the primary target keyword from keyword research and record it.
    2. Add the similar terms, close variations and synonyms of that keyword, and plan to include them in the content.
    3. Search the keyword in Google and record the URLs of ranking content pieces.
    4. Check social share data for the topic with BuzzSumo or a similar tool and record strong pieces.
    5. Check competitor websites for content aimed at the same audience and record it. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.018]
  • Research phase output, in order:
    1. Content topic and title.
    2. Overview with the content's purpose and goal.
    3. Target search terms the piece might rank for.
    4. Similar online content that already exists for those terms. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.021]
  • Anyone who produces content must review all recorded similar online content before writing, recording or planning it, and their version must be better than that content. Share the planner with every writer or producer along with this instruction. (older, 2021-22) [L0386.019]
  • Record every asset a piece needs before production starts, such as images, graphs and PDFs, together with where each asset will come from. This lets whoever produces the piece find everything without asking. (older, 2021-22) [L0384.011]
  • Stock photo platforms such as Unsplash and Adobe Stock can supply images for content. Any third-party image used in a piece needs its source recorded and attribution given in the piece itself. (older, 2021-22) [L0384.012]
  • Record the statistics you plan to cite and the existing content you plan to link to in the planner, backed by enough research to support the idea. Empty fields are acceptable early on if the sources are not yet known. (older, 2021-22) [L0384.013]
  • For each planned content piece, choose three to four promotion channels and log them in the content planner. (older, 2021-22) [L0381.007]

Setup Sequence

Gameplan

  • Simple content marketing gameplan, in order:
    1. Decide the content format: written content, video content, or a combination of both.
    2. Plan your first 12 content ideas (topics) in your content planning document.
    3. Decide how and by whom the content is produced. For written content, find a content writer. For video, find an editor and, if someone else records, a person to do the recording.
    4. Produce two quality content pieces per month, continuously and without an end date.
    5. After 12 months of consistent work, either increase output above two pieces per month (for example, add one more per month) or hire another person.
    6. Promote each content piece for at least two weeks, ideally up to 30 days, and build backlinks to content pieces where it is relevant. (older, 2021-22) [L0383.004]
  • Content marketing action plan, in order:
    1. Fill in the attention matrix document. It does not have to be finished in one month, but make a substantial dent.
    2. Do keyword research, or check whether it was already done. If not, do it yourself or outsource it.
    3. Enter the first 12 content ideas into the content planner, which covers about six months of content at two pieces per month.
    4. Complete research for the first four content ideas in the planner.
    5. Every two months, block up to 30 minutes in a calendar to research the next four ideas and add more content ideas, so about six months stay planned ahead.
    6. Post a job to find a content producer, such as a writer or video editor. Give candidates a test, hire one, and produce two quality pieces of content per month.
    7. For each planned content piece, choose three to four promotion channels and record them in the content planner. (older, 2021-22) [L0381.001]
  • Action steps to set up the content plan:
    1. Fill the planner with 12 content ideas that mix problem-solving and entertaining topics suited to your market or niche.
    2. Complete the research columns for the first four pieces.
    3. Add a calendar entry for two months from now to research the next four pieces.
    4. Make sure a producer is ready before content production starts. Keyword research is done alongside these steps. (older, 2021-22) [L0384.017]

Planning horizon

  • Keep a rolling plan of about six months of content in the content planner at all times, so new ideas are always added before the existing schedule runs out. (older, 2021-22) [L0381.003]
  • Plan 12 content ideas in the planner first. That equals about six months of content at two pieces per month. (older, 2021-22) [L0381.004]

Production System

Keyword-led weekly system

  • Content marketing system with seven steps, repeated for each keyword every week:
    1. Define the target market and write the customer profile.
    2. Set up an email opt-in on the store.
    3. Build a keyword list from informational queries.
    4. Write a blog article for one keyword.
    5. Create a custom image for that article that contains the keyword and matches the brand.
    6. Write a plain text email that drives traffic to the article.
    7. Upload the article to the blog, then send the email to the list. [L0380.014]
  • Create a separate blog for the store and publish each article as its own page. The email then links directly to that article page instead of the homepage. [L0380.015]
  • Keyword-based content is produced in this order:
    1. Research the keywords yourself.
    2. Create the content. Most beginners should write it themselves. Later, collaborate with niche influencers who can contribute pieces, or hire an expert.
    3. Make basic graphics yourself in Canva, or outsource images on Fiverr. [L1120.003]

Article workflow and quality control

  • Content production workflow from start to finish:
    1. Choose keywords with the Ahrefs Content Gap method and vet them with RAW.
    2. Create the article in the Google Docs template and brief the writer with the brand guideline.
    3. Have the writer complete the article with an editor.
    4. Edit the article with a native English editor.
    5. Write the headline and meta description, often with a sales copywriter.
    6. Create a custom image with the keyword in its alt tag.
    7. Upload the article and check its SEO with a plugin such as Yoast.
    8. Publish the article.
    9. Track the article's email signups each month and use the best performers to find similar keywords. [L0382.039]
  • Templates for customer surveys, blog post creation guidelines, podcast keyword research and podcast content processes systematise outsourcing and maintain quality at scale. [L0326.002]
  • Writer brand guideline rules are covered under Writers and Outsourcing below. [L0382.057]

Writers and Outsourcing

Who can write

  • Professional writing ability, marketing expertise or native English fluency are not requirements for content creation. These skills are learnable over time or outsourceable to freelancers, which removes a psychological barrier that stops many from starting a content strategy. [L0226.046]
  • Keep research and planning in-house: decide which article titles, About Us format and product page elements the site needs. Outsource only the writing, because the owner must control the content direction. (older, 2019) [L0467.012]

  • When outsourcing content writing, find niche enthusiasts in Facebook groups rather than professional writers. Niche passion produces better content for dropshipping stores than generic writing skill. (older, 2022) [L0249.065]

Finding writers step by step

  • Finding writers for a broad, non-technical niche:
    1. Post a content writer job on Upwork with the outline of the pieces you want.

    2. Join all relevant groups.
    3. Identify members who post often and whose posts get many likes and comments.

  • Outreach template for a niche group member:

Role of Content in the Funnel

Content Compared with Sales Copy

  • Educational writing that only informs, such as a general guide or blog post, is not sales copy and does not move a visitor toward a purchase. It can have a place on an e-commerce site, but product, collection and sales copy should each be written to earn one defined action. [L1460.013]
  • Authoritative, educational content in text, image, video or social form raises conversion, especially for high-priced or technical products. It engages buyers earlier on the customer awareness path than product pages alone. You do not need hundreds of blog posts; you need more useful content than your competitors. [L1522.031]
  • Content is a long-term investment that builds organic traffic and rankings over time. Unique product descriptions, collection page copy and blog posts should include the correct keywords and be formatted properly. [L1283.095]

What Makes Content Worth Producing

  • Great content must be deep, relevant to what is happening now, shareable, well researched and worth linking to. Content that meets these criteria attracts attention, builds trust, shows relevance and authority, gives search engines what they reward, and makes a business look unique. [L1461.001]
  • Original content is a competitive advantage because no competitor can copy it, which makes the business feel more unique to visitors. [L1461.009]
  • Create unique general content such as blog posts, guest posts, and downloadable supplier catalogs, sales sheets and spec sheets. Unpublished text from supplier materials gives a large SEO advantage once you publish it on your store. [L1357.038]

Depth Before Width

  • Shallow content is common online, so producing deep content is the way to stand out in search and social channels. Search engines and consumers can see large numbers of thin pages, so depth is the differentiator. [L1461.010]
  • There are over one trillion individual web pages online, and most of them are shallow, with very little substance. Because thin pages are so common, deep and comprehensive pages stand out to both search engines and consumers. [L1461.026]
  • Content should be deep before it is wide; only a small share of web content is deep, so depth is a differentiator that compounds over time. A practical test is whether the piece gives visitors more value than any competitor's version on the same topic. [L1461.021]

Mixing Content Types by Funnel Stage

  • Problem-solving content is often the best starting point for a business that has no content yet, because the team already knows the customer problems from running the business. It ranks well in search and YouTube and also improves conversion by helping buyers move through the customer journey. [L1461.014]

  • Entertainment content is the most widely engaging content and is the main tool for filling the top of the funnel, since it does not require the visitor to be problem aware. It should still be relevant to the target market and appeal mainly to that audience rather than everyone. [L1461.015]

  • Process for using entertainment content to fill a sales funnel:

    1. Publish interest-based or best-of content that attracts relevant visitors to the website.
    2. Offer an opt-in on the page to add visitors to the email audience.
    3. Place a cookie for retargeting so visitors can be shown ads later.
    4. Serve further content to move them through the funnel toward the desired awareness stage.

    [L1461.017]

  • Example: a beard-care brand's blog mixes content types on one landing page: a product-related review of nose hair trimmers (problem solving), a controversial opinion piece against neckties (entertainment), a personal-story piece about facial hair (entertainment), and a men's hat style guide (a mix of both). This combination of types is presented as a good model for a mixed blog strategy. [L1461.020]

Content Types

Priority Order

Source: Source B, 2021-22 (older advice).

  • These six content types are the most shared and most linked to, and they lend themselves to depth, which makes them the strongest choice for SEO and engagement results. Backlinks from other sites are a key reason to favor them. [L1421.002]
  • Focus first on how-to, listicles and ultimate guides, since they are the easiest to produce and the best starting point. Best of the interwebs and expert roundups come after these. [L1421.030]

Problem-Solving Content

Source: Source B, 2021-22 (older advice).

  • Problem-solving content is usually the easiest type to produce because most problems have known answers, and the product itself is typically the answer. It gives immediate value and can engage people at the problem-aware, solution-aware and product-aware stages of the customer awareness pyramid. [L1461.012]

  • Types of problem-solving content: [L1461.013]

    Type Purpose
    How-to guides Step-by-step instructions to solve a specific problem
    Ultimate guides Most comprehensive resource on a topic, helping choose products and answer purchase questions
    Product comparisons Pros and cons of specific products for middle-of-funnel buyers deciding between options
    Listicles (solution lists) Numbered lists of solutions or products to a problem, such as 'top 10 ways' or 'five ways to avoid'
    Explanatory content Simple explanations of technical terms, industry lingo or product technology to help customers understand
    FAQ content Answers to common questions, optionally combined with a listicle format, such as 'top 5 questions when shopping for X'

How-To Guides

Source: Source B, 2021-22 (older advice).

  • How-to content, built step by step:

    1. Pick a concrete problem or pain point the reader has and make the piece a direct answer to it.
    2. Break the solution into logical steps, one segment per step, presented in order (step one, step two, step three and so on).
    3. Explain each step as clearly as possible in jargon-free language, written for the reader with the most basic knowledge.
    4. Use as many images as possible: in written content, insert images; in video, show each step either with an edited static image or with a hands-on recording of the action.

    [L1421.003]

  • Breaking a how-to into small, simple steps makes it feel easy for a beginner, which is what drives the best result for this content type. [L1421.004]

Listicles

Source: Source B, 2021-22 (older advice).

  • For listicles, the title contains a number and the list covers different items. They suit both problem solving and entertainment, and they are among the most shared and linked content types. [L1421.005]

  • Listicles have a low barrier to entry because most of the information already exists online or offline; the work is mainly collecting, organizing and rewriting it in a unique way instead of copying. [L1421.006]

  • Listicles often work well with a short introduction, a table of contents with anchor links to each section, practical info such as required gear, and a short description per item covering location, who it suits and the conditions. [L1421.010]

  • Listicle research before publishing:

    1. Search for the topic and see how many existing listicles already cover it.
    2. If a competitor has published a top N list, publish a longer or differently sized list instead, for example top 17, top 20 or top 21.

    [L1421.007]

  • Listicle numbers that are not round (for example 7, 17, 23 or 33) tend to get higher engagement and more clicks than round numbers such as 5, 10, 15 or 20. [L1421.008]

  • Title pattern and examples are in Pattern Matching below. [L1421.009]

Ultimate Guides

Source: Source B, 2021-22 (older advice).

  • Ultimate guides are the most valuable content and are highly linkable, which makes them strong for problem solving and link building. Their title must be earned: if a piece is called the ultimate guide, it must be the deepest and best resource on that topic. [L1421.011]
  • Ultimate guides can be very long, from roughly 5,000 to 10,000 words. Even when little competing content exists, the piece should be written as if someone could publish a better one soon. [L1421.012]
  • Ultimate guides suit high-volume, more competitive search terms, which are broader and harder to rank for. These pieces are easier to build backlinks to, and such terms generally need more backlinks to reach top positions on Google. [L1421.014]
  • An ultimate guide can combine how-to and listicle styles, for example an ultimate guide to choosing a product that works as a how-to, or an unlimited-count list where each item is covered in detail. [L1421.015]
  • Not every piece should be an ultimate guide because it takes more work, but some should be included in the mix. [L1421.016]

Best of the Interwebs

Source: Source B, 2021-22 (older advice).

  • Best of the interwebs pieces collect existing online content, which makes them quick to produce. Images can be taken from the original sources, so custom visuals are usually not needed. [L1421.017]
  • Each item in a best of the interwebs piece needs commentary: explain why it is good, what it does particularly well or what you like about it. A simple link with no insight is not enough; personal opinion is acceptable. [L1421.019]
  • Entertainment form: curated lists of the best blogs, YouTube channels or resources in the target niche, bringing relevant visitors to the site. [L1461.016]

Expert Roundups

Source: Source B, 2021-22 (older advice).

  • Expert roundup production:

    1. Choose a popular question or statement in your market.
    2. Identify experts with large audiences using the attention matrix, meaning people regarded as authorities who already have audiences through websites, social media or YouTube.
    3. Find their contact details on their website or social media, then email each one with the simple question.
    4. Contact more experts than you need, because not all will respond.
    5. Keep the question simple so that a few-line answer is enough; for example, ask for one favorite item.
    6. Build the piece around each answer, adding a short explanation and a relevant image sourced from the internet.
    7. When published, email the participants with a thank-you, a link and a request to share the piece.

    [L1421.020]

  • Expert roundups use experts' names and trust to attract attention to your piece, which is useful when your own name is not yet trusted in the market. [L1421.021]

  • An expert roundup should state in its headline that it is based on expert feedback; leaving that out weakens the piece. [L1421.022]

  • Expert roundups can take longer, from a few days to a couple of weeks, because the work depends on experts replying. [L1421.023]

  • Roundup content gains reach through reciprocity: after publishing, send the contributing experts the link and ask them to share it with their audience, which lets the business use their audience to gain exposure. [L1461.019]

  • Roundup content in the entertainment table: expert quotes gathered by emailing experts for a short comment; the published roundup is shared with the experts, who often share it with their audience. [L1461.016]

Controversial Content

Source: Source B, 2021-22 (older advice).

  • Controversial content takes a position that goes against commonly held beliefs. It should be used sparingly, because it is highly engaging but polarizing. [L1421.024]
  • A controversial position must be explained in depth with supporting evidence and a real attempt to convince the reader. A fake position cannot be defended in depth, so it should not be used. [L1421.025]
  • Controversial content in the entertainment table: taking an opposing position on a commonly held belief in the market to attract attention. [L1461.016]

  • Rising volume of AI-generated, recycled and syndicated content causes audience fatigue, and attention is split between creators, streaming services and daily life. Content needs a clear strategy to stop the scroll and hold viewers. [L1457.004]

  • Idea-sourcing procedure:

    1. Open the analytics of the main platform, TikTok being the most detailed for audience insight, and Instagram analytics for audience behaviour.
    2. Read the search terms your audience uses and the videos they interact with.
    3. Note the questions behind those searches.
    4. Build videos that answer these questions before the viewer asks them.

    [L1457.007]

Problem-Based Ideas

Source: Source B, 2021-22 (older advice).

  • Before producing any piece of content, follow these steps:

    1. Research what already exists on the topic by searching for it and reviewing who has covered it.
    2. Identify which aspects the existing top content has not covered.
    3. Plan how to go deeper and provide more value than the existing pieces.
    4. Produce the content so it aims to be the most comprehensive resource online on that topic.

    [L1461.008]

Competitor and SERP Research

Reviewing Existing Coverage

Source: Source B, 2021-22 (older advice).

  • Listicle research: search for the topic, count how many existing listicles cover it, and if a competitor has published a top N list, publish a longer or differently sized list instead (for example top 17, top 20 or top 21). [L1421.007]
  • Ultimate guides target high-volume, competitive terms that need more backlinks to reach top positions on Google. [L1421.014]
  • Shallow content is common, so a depth-first approach is the way to stand out against what already ranks. [L1461.010]
  • Gap test before production: find what the top content misses, go deeper than it, and aim to be the most comprehensive resource. [L1461.008]

Competitor and Keyword Data

Source: Source A, 2022 (older advice).

  • Six-month done-for-you content plan process: [L1281.115]
    1. Research the niche, the site, the competitors and the keywords.
    2. Build a content marketing plan from that research.
    3. Write blog posts and source images.
    4. Publish the content on a schedule over six months to build organic traffic from Google.

Pattern Matching

Listicle Titles

Source: Source B, 2021-22 (older advice).

  • Listicle title pattern: [Number] + [best/top] + [item] + [location or qualifier] + [audience or experience level qualifier]. The qualifier at the end widens the appeal to a segment, such as beginners, advanced riders or first-time visitors, while the base title still captures the broad search. [L1421.009]
    • Travel niche example: "11 Best Hiking Trails in Colorado for Every Fitness Level"
    • Finance niche example: "19 Best Budget Apps for Freelancers Who Are Just Starting Out"
  • Use non-round numbers such as 7, 17, 23 or 33, which tend to earn higher engagement and more clicks than 5, 10, 15 or 20. [L1421.008]
  • Listicle structure: short introduction, table of contents with anchor links, practical info such as required gear, and a short description per item covering location, who it suits and the conditions. [L1421.010]

Trend Patterns

Source: Source E, 2025-26.

  • Trend search example: a skincare brand checks the search terms in TikTok analytics and finds a viral technique, such as slugging, being searched for often. The brand then builds videos that answer the questions viewers are about to ask, before they search for them. [L1457.025]

Content Planner

Starting Plan Structure

Source: Source A, 2022 (older advice).

  • The six-month plan runs research, a content marketing plan, writing and sourcing images, then scheduled publishing. [L1281.115]

Source: Source B, 2021-22 (older advice).

  • Content planning method: for the first 12 ideas, think of a few different versions of each content type and include them in the plan. [L1421.026]

Content Pillars

Source: Source E, 2025-26.

  • Each content pillar should align with the brand's expertise, its business goals and what the audience cares about. Pillars keep content balanced, prevent random posting, simplify monthly planning and show which formats to scale. [L1457.011]
  • Content pillars are assigned to fixed weekdays. Two pillars per week is the minimum, five pillars fit daily posting Monday to Friday, and seven pillars are possible but usually too demanding. [L1457.010]
  • Test a set of content pillars for two months, then review performance data. In the next month, increase the posting frequency of the pillars that perform well and reduce the frequency of those that do not. [L1457.012]
  • Posting without pillars leads to disorganized content and makes batch production much harder. Without a system, the creator also loses motivation more easily. [L1457.013]

Flexibility in the Plan

Source: Source E, 2025-26.

  • Keep some flexibility in the schedule, for example one fixed weekday for trend-based videos. Trend slots are harder to batch but allow a fast reaction to trends while the rest of the plan stays fixed. [L1457.017]

Batching and Production Rhythm

Source: Source E, 2025-26.

  • Batching means filming a whole series in one session. For example, two hours of filming can produce ten videos for one weekday slot, covering the next ten weeks. [L1457.015]

Writer Throughput

Source: Source B, 2021-22 (older advice).

  • Ask writers to focus on one product category per week, for example twelve collection pages for similar products, so they keep the same subject and write faster. A writer needing about 1.5 hours per 500-word page can usually finish about two pages per day. Plan about twelve content items per week per writer as a starting point. [L1396.043]
  • Writing a 500-word collection page took a writer about 1.5 hours, so a typical day allows two or three pages. Set weekly targets based on this measured rate, then adjust after one week of real results. Do not expect a 500-word article in half an hour. [L1396.044]

Writers and Outsourcing

Weekly Assignment Cards

Source: Source B, 2021-22 (older advice).

  • Assign weekly content work to a writer using this structure:

    1. Create a weekly project card, for example for a specific week in October.
    2. Add the target keywords in the card description.
    3. Set a length, such as 300 to 500 words for collection or product page content.
    4. Attach the link to each collection or product page to be written.
    5. Move each card to the done list when the writer finishes, so progress is visible.

    [L1396.042]

  • Create blog or buying guide tasks using this structure:

    1. Write the blog name or topic.
    2. Add reference sources, such as manufacturer pages, reviews or related blogs.
    3. List the product links that must appear.
    4. Set the target keywords and the desired length.
    5. Move the card to done when published.

    [L1396.045]

Writer Capacity

Source: Source B, 2021-22 (older advice).

  • Plan about twelve content items per week per writer as a starting point, one product category per week, adjusted after one week of measured results. [L1396.043, L1396.044]

Holiday and Seasonal Content

Source: Source A, 2025.

Example titles are [niche] Black Friday deals and [niche] Cyber Monday deals, each explaining the offer, when it runs, which products it covers and how to use it. [L1287.034]
