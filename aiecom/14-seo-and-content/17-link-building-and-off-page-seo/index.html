14.17 Link building and off-Page SEO

Backlink Basics and Why Links Matter

  • Backlinks from external websites signal authority to Google. Each link is a vote of confidence, and websites with more backlinks rank higher. [L0226.008]
  • Working with influencers generates backlinks, because they link to your store in their articles, which supports search rankings. [L0226.008]
  • Off-page SEO covers actions outside the website that make search engines see it as trustworthy and authoritative, such as building backlinks, earning branded searches, and gaining engagement and shares on social media. [L0462.033]
  • (older, 2021-22) A 2020 ranking factors study found a clear correlation between total backlinks and Google rankings. When URLs with zero backlinks were excluded, top-ranking pages had more backlinks than lower-ranking pages. [L0462.034]
  • (older, 2021-22) Google has stated that it still uses PageRank alongside many other undisclosed signals. Links are one part of off-page SEO, not the whole. [L0462.035]
  • (older, 2021-22) Google's Quality Rater Guidelines rely heavily on off-site reputation, checked through online reviews, expert recommendations, news articles and awards. A large number of positive user reviews counts as evidence of a good reputation. [L0462.036]
  • (older, 2021-22) Google has publicly named three main ranking priorities: content, external links and the RankBrain algorithm. External links are one of the few ranking factors Google has explicitly confirmed. This statement dates from around 2020. [L0565.001, L0944.001]
  • (older, 2021-22) Google has officially confirmed only these three organic ranking signals. Other factors are known mainly through testing, so treat any specific claim about them as a hypothesis to verify. [L0944.010]
  • (older, 2021-22) Google treats backlinks as social proof. Links from other sites signal that content is good and the site is trustworthy, and sites with weak content rarely attract them. Backlinks also help Google place a site in its wider context. [L0944.002]
  • (older, 2021-22) Google values backlinks as a core signal for understanding the web. If any of the three core signals is missing, strong on-page and technical SEO will not earn significant rankings. [L0944.001, L0947.001]
  • A site can earn some organic traffic without links through technical SEO, content and on-page work. Reaching high organic traffic with no external links is very hard, because Google uses links as third-party votes and combines them with on-page quality signals. [L0565.002]
  • When competing pages have similar content quality, the page with more external links is usually harder to outrank. Links act as a tie-breaker that good content alone cannot provide. [L0565.003]
  • Google also evaluates on-page quality, so pages that look dodgy or carry spammy links are judged negatively. Clean external links from relevant sites support page quality, not just link count. [L0565.016]
  • A backlink passes most of its authority to the exact page it points to, not to the whole domain. Links on that page to other pages on your site pass only part of that authority onward. [L0470.006]
  • Social media shares are nofollow and do not create backlinks that raise search rankings. They still help the trust signals of the site, so they are worth doing. [L0470.007]
  • (older, 2021-22) Once a page reaches the top of the results for its target term, the goal shifts to holding that position. Watch whether competing pages keep adding backlinks and whether you add enough quality links to stay ahead. [L0944.011]
  • (older, 2021-22) The E-commerce Skyscraper Method is the main link-building approach. Smaller supplementary methods can be added alongside it to find more opportunities, but they do not replace it. [L0944.012, L0944.006]
  • (older, 2021-22) Backlink outreach is a form of link building only when all links are genuine. Black hat tactics such as link farms are recognizable to Google and carry a risk of penalties. [L0944.003]

Measuring Authority and Setting Link Targets

  • Domain Authority Checker is a free tool that assesses a website's authority for SEO analysis. [L0173.002]
  • (older, 2021-22) The number of backlinks needed depends on what competing pages already have, not on a fixed target. The goal is more or better-quality backlinks than the direct competitors ranking for the same search term, rather than the most links in the whole market. [L0944.004]
  • (older, 2021-22) Benchmarks from the course differ by how strong the competition is. Use each one where it fits:
    • Weak competition: if the top-ranking competitors have about five backlinks, a page usually needs roughly 5 to 10 quality backlinks, provided its own content is better than theirs. This is a rough benchmark, not a guaranteed result. [L0944.005]
    • Competitive niche: the average top-10 page for "surfboards" had 14 backlinks and a domain score of 54. For "wetsuits" the average top-10 page had 33 backlinks and a domain score of 72. These show how many links a page needs to compete. [L0877.027]
    • No fixed number: there is no fixed backlink number a top result must have. Look for ranking pages with hundreds of backlinks or high-authority linking sites, such as those rated in the 80s or 90s. Accept lower numbers in very small niches. [L0878.017]

Why E-commerce Pages Struggle to Earn Links

  • E-commerce sites find it hard to earn many inbound links because their core pages are product and collection pages, which are commercial in nature. Most websites do not want to link to them unless they gain something, since such a link gives their audience little value. [L0565.004]
  • Resellers face an even harder time than brands selling their own products. A truly unique, self-made product can make commercial pages easier to link to. [L0565.005]
  • Websites are generally unwilling to link to a commercial page for nothing in return. Link outreach to product or collection pages therefore needs an offer that gives the linking site something of value. [L0565.017]
  • (older, 2021-22) Pursuing backlinks to product or collection pages is usually a poor use of outreach effort, because most website owners prefer to link to informational content. A store that wants links needs content worth linking to before starting outreach. [L0944.008]
  • (older, 2021-22) Content pages such as guides and articles are the preferred targets for backlinks, because other site owners rarely link to product or collection pages. Build outreach around content that is created first. [L0944.007]
  • Content pages attract links more readily than product pages, but content type and structure strongly affect how many links it earns. Choose content by researching which formats are most linked to in the niche. [L0565.006]
  • Before producing link-attracting content, research which content types are most linked to in the niche, and produce those alongside the other content the store needs. Targeting and research are part of the link strategy, not an afterthought. [L0565.007]
  • Product or collection links can still matter. Building links to product pages and collections that sit on page two of Google can move them up. It takes a few months before the effect shows, but organic sales then replace part of the margin lost to higher costs. [L0942.026]
  • (older, 2021-22) Sources differ on product-page links. Use the page-two approach from L0942.026 only where a product or collection page is close to page one and the outreach offers the linking site something of value (L0565.017). Otherwise follow L0944.008 and focus on content. [L0942.026, L0565.017, L0944.008]
  • Affiliate link arrangements are a separate topic. They are set aside when analyzing why commercial pages struggle to earn links, and the outreach method for e-commerce stores does not cover them. [L0565.015]

Choosing Content Roles and Projects

  • Not every content piece needs a backlink campaign. Content serves one of three roles: ranking for target keywords, earning backlinks, or helping existing customers on the site. Only pieces meant to earn links need to go through a link-building process. [L0943.001]
  • Build backlinks to content pieces wherever it makes sense for that piece. [L0383.012]
  • (older, 2021-22) A skyscraper project can be run on any suitable high-quality piece, which speeds up link acquisition. It is optional for each piece, so a monthly publishing plan does not require a skyscraper process for every item. [L0943.003]
  • (older, 2021-22) Skyscraper content is built to attract large numbers of backlinks. It is not needed in every piece and should not be the starting point of a beginner's plan. [L0384.007]
  • (older, 2021-22) The practical minimum is one completed skyscraper project, run through the full process including link negotiation, so that the workflow is learned and some links are built. [L0943.004]
  • (older, 2021-22) The skyscraper-style method aims for a very low cost and time investment per link. It is cheaper per link, per piece of content and per outreach than most other online link building. It makes up for lower effort per link with much higher outreach volume. [L0565.008]
  • (older, 2021-22) Only earn genuine links from real websites. Never buy links or build unnatural ones. This white hat approach keeps the risk of a Google penalty or algorithm-related loss very low. [L0565.011]

Operating Sequence

  1. Identify one piece of content to run a skyscraper project on. If no suitable content exists, choose a topic that fits the niche and create the content first. [L0943.005]
  2. Work through the skyscraper project step by step, including the link negotiation process. [L0943.005]
  3. Once the first project is complete, repeat the process on an ongoing basis and review which parts of the work can be outsourced. [L0943.005]
  4. Research opportunities in your niche for resource page link building and broken link building. [L0943.005]
  • (older, 2021-22) Keyword research is the first step before creating content and running outreach. The skyscraper-style method starts there. [L0565.014]
  • (older, 2021-22) Link building is still partly manual and time consuming, so the owner should not keep spending large amounts of their own time on it for months or years. [L0945.002]

Topic Research for Link-Attracting Content

  • (older, 2021-22) For link-focused content, keyword research is really topic research. The goal is to find topics that already attract many backlinks, not keywords to place inside the text. The chosen topic should be one that other sites have linked to repeatedly. [L0878.001]
  • (older, 2021-22) Choose keywords relevant to your niche or product category, even if they are not directly about your products. Focus on informational queries, such as how-to or what-does-this-mean questions. [L0878.002]
  • (older, 2021-22) Avoid commercial keywords (buying intent, such as "where can I buy") and navigational keywords (finding a place or site, such as "near me" or a specific park or brand). Even when their keyword difficulty is very high, they do not produce linkable content. [L0878.003]
  • (older, 2021-22) Use broad keywords for this method, the opposite of typical SEO writing that targets narrow terms. Broad topics appeal to a wider audience and attract more links. The final article topic must still be specific enough to write one complete piece about. [L0878.004]
  • (older, 2021-22) Keyword difficulty (KD) in Ahrefs, Ubersuggest and similar tools estimates how many linking domains a page needs to reach the top 10 organic results. In Ahrefs it is a weighted average of the linking domains of the current top-ranking pages, shown on a logarithmic scale from 0 to 100. A higher KD means the top pages already have many backlinks, which is what this method looks for. [L0878.005]
  • (older, 2021-22) Target informational keywords with a keyword difficulty above 10 as the ideal. Values such as 15, 16, 20 or 30 are all good. In very small or specific niches, KD values somewhat below 10 can still produce usable link targets. [L0878.006]
  • (older, 2021-22) When a KD is slightly below 10 but several ranking articles have decent backlink counts, the topic is still worth pursuing. Borderline values work as long as enough linking sites can be targeted. [L0878.016]
  • (older, 2021-22) Search volume and clicks are not important for this method, because the purpose is to earn links rather than traffic for that exact term. Judge candidates by KD, search intent and the backlink profile of the ranking pages. [L0878.007]
  • (older, 2021-22) Keyword and topic research workflow for the skyscraper method (Ahrefs Keywords Explorer, Google, United States):
    1. Open the keyword tool and select Google with the United States as the country.
    2. Enter the broadest term that describes your niche, for example the main product category, and run the search.
    3. Read the overview for keyword difficulty, search volume and CPC.
    4. Open the Questions report to see question-style searches around the term.
    5. Keep only informational queries and remove commercial and navigational ones, even if their KD is high.
    6. Keep candidates with KD above 10, accepting somewhat lower values in very small niches.
    7. Open the SERP overview of each candidate and check the top results: they should be mostly informational articles with many backlinks, not store, government or navigational pages.
    8. Ignore Wikipedia results when judging backlink strength, because they collect links for being Wikipedia.
    9. If the broad term is too general for one article, return to the question variations and choose a more specific candidate.
    10. Record each promising topic and its top-ranking competitor URL in the research spreadsheet.
    11. Use the Having same terms report to find secondary keywords close to the primary one and record them.
    12. Choose the topic that has the strongest set of linking sites and a clear article idea you can make better than the current top result. [L0878.008]
  • (older, 2021-22) Example: the broad term "camping" in the United States had a KD of 32, a search volume of about 127K and a CPC of $0.80. Ahrefs estimated that backlinks from about 40 websites were needed to rank in the top 10, but the term was too broad to write one specific article about. [L0878.012]
  • (older, 2021-22) Example: question-style variations from the same niche had lower KD values. "What is dispersed camping" had a KD of 17, and "how to cook while camping" had a KD of 26. Both returned mostly informational articles with a good number of backlinks, making them suitable candidates. [L0878.013]
  • (older, 2021-22) Keyword difficulty labels and estimated linking websites needed for the top 10 (Ahrefs, United States):
Example keyword Keyword difficulty Ahrefs label Estimated linking websites needed for top 10
camping 32 Hard ~40
how to cook while camping 26 Medium ~30

[L0878.014]

  • (older, 2021-22) Example: "how to start a campfire" had a low KD and an estimated need of about 8 linking websites, while its top pages still carried many backlinks from individual sites. Because the query had enough linked articles beyond Wikipedia, it was treated as a viable topic despite the low difficulty. [L0878.015]
  • (older, 2021-22) Example of the same topic: for the informational keyword "how to start a campfire" in the United States, Ahrefs showed a search volume of about 1.6K, a CPC of $1.60 and an Easy label with about 8 linking websites needed for the top 10. Its broader parent topic "how to start a fire" had a search volume of about 5.5K. [L0878.031]
  • (older, 2021-22) To find secondary keywords, open the Having same terms report for the primary keyword and record the similar search terms that fit the same article. Sites that link to those variations are also likely to link to your article. [L0878.018]
  • (older, 2021-22) To validate an existing topic idea, enter it into the keyword tool, check its KD and SERP overview, and look at the ranking articles and their backlinks. If several informational articles have decent backlink counts, add the idea to the outreach list as a content piece for this method. [L0878.020]
  • (older, 2021-22) Build the article so it is at least as good as the current top-ranking page, for example as a more complete guide that adds extra items or sections. Better, more useful content is what attracts links away from existing pages. [L0878.019]
  • (older, 2021-22) Choosing a high-KD navigational keyword, such as a "near me" search, wastes effort, because no informational article can satisfy that intent. Always check the search intent in the Questions and SERP results before committing to a topic. [L0878.021]
  • (older, 2021-22) Counting Wikipedia pages as link targets inflates the apparent backlink strength of a SERP. Wikipedia accumulates links for its own reasons, so focus on other websites in the results. [L0878.022]
  • (older, 2021-22) Research spreadsheet layout for recording topics:

Example row: https://www.chowhound.com/food-news/204186/camping-food-guide-ideas/ | how to cook while camping | how to cook biscuits while camping; how to cook baked potatoes while camping; how to cook food while camping [L0878.023]

  • (older, 2021-22) The backlinks of current top-ranking articles reveal the sites that already link to this kind of content. These are the likely linkers to target in outreach for a new, better article on the same topic. [L0878.028]

Building Link-Attracting Content

  • (older, 2021-22) Content built to attract backlinks for an e-commerce store must contain no commercial angle. It should not recommend buying specific products from the store, promote the store's products as better than others, or imply a buy call-to-action. [L0564.001]

  • (older, 2021-22) The main goal of this content is to earn links, not to rank for keywords or serve buyers directly. Keywords and customer relevance appear naturally, but they do not decide the topic or purpose. [L0564.002]

  • (older, 2021-22) Middle-of-funnel content that compares the store's own products or recommends the best product in a category is excluded from link-attracting content. That content should still be produced for customers, but it cannot be used for outreach. [L0564.003]

  • (older, 2021-22) Many websites refuse to link to overtly commercial pages, and link outreach works on volume. Promoting commercial content to many potential linkers wastes outreach effort. [L0564.004]

  • (older, 2021-22) The best topics for this method have broad appeal and sit at the top of the funnel. The wider the audience a topic reaches, the more linkers it attracts. [L0564.005]

  • (older, 2021-22) Only four content types are used for this method: ultimate guides, how-to posts, list posts (listicles), and infographics. Research into non-homepage pages shows these attract the most links. [L0564.006]

  • (older, 2021-22) An ultimate guide is a very large, very detailed page that delivers a lot of value on one niche topic. It should cover a subject related to the store's niche, not the store's own product or buying process. A cornerstone page can be promoted this way if it fits the ultimate guide format. [L0564.007]

  • (older, 2021-22) A how-to post explains how to do one specific thing, usually by fixing a common problem people have in the niche. It can be less detailed than an ultimate guide but is still highly linkable. A how-to can also be expanded into an ultimate guide on the same subject. [L0564.008]

  • (older, 2021-22) A list post, also called a listicle, presents a numbered set of items, such as "the top 12 places" for a niche topic. Longer and more detailed lists attract more links. List posts are among the easiest of the four types to produce. [L0564.009]

  • (older, 2021-22) An infographic is visual content that people embed in their own articles with a source credit, which creates a backlink. Existing articles can be converted into infographics and shared. Even if the infographic is not promoted, it can serve as the source for a skyscraper outreach campaign that earns a link back to the site. [L0564.010]

  • (older, 2021-22) Title formula for how-to pages:

    How to [fix or do a specific thing] [a common problem in the niche]

    It works because people searching for a concrete fix tend to link to the page that solves that exact problem. Examples in the same pattern: How to Stop Dog Harness Chafing Under the Front Legs; How to Keep a Camping Stove Flame Steady in Wind. [L0564.016]

  • (older, 2021-22) Any skyscraper content must be at least as good as the content ranking on page one of Google for the same topic. Thin sections, such as a few lines per list item, do not meet this standard. [L0564.011]

  • (older, 2021-22) Each piece of link-attracting content should go deeper on its subject than any existing page has gone. Depth of coverage is the main quality measure for these pages. [L0564.012]

  • (older, 2021-22) Before creating new link-attracting pages, audit the existing site for content that already matches one of the four formats and can be promoted as it is. Commission new pieces only in the formats the site is missing. [L0564.013]

  • (older, 2021-22) Check whether these four formats attract the most links rather than assuming it. Look up which non-homepage pages in a specific niche have the most backlinks and note their formats before choosing one. [L0564.014]

  • (older, 2021-22) For a camping-gear store, an ultimate guide to choosing tent stakes for different soil types, or a directory of campsites by US state, fits the ultimate guide format. A guide to buying the store's own tents does not, because it is commercial. [L0564.015]

  • (older, 2021-22) Original statistics attract links. People link to pages to cite specific statistics on them, not only because the content is high quality. Publishing original statistics or a data list can attract backlinks automatically. [L0462.037]

  • (older, 2021-22) Case: the author wanted more backlinks and suspected that people link to pages that cite specific statistics, not only to high-quality writing. A list of 80 email marketing statistics for 2019 was built at considerable time and money cost and published as a page. The first backlink arrived a few days later, which supported the hypothesis that original statistics attract links automatically. [L0462.043]

  • (older, 2021-22) Whenever content links out to or uses material from another source, such as quotes, graphs or research data, the original source must be credited on the page with a backlink. Then email the source to let them know. [L0385.006]

Prospecting Linkers

  • (older, 2021-22) Only contact sites that are a likely linker: a site within your broad industry category or niche that already publishes resource or content pages with outgoing links. Sites outside your niche are not worth outreach, even if they have broken links. [L0332.029]
  • (older, 2021-22) A likely linker list is built from backlink data. The referring page is the page that links out, and the link URL is the page on your site or a competitor's site that it links to. Both are needed to know which domains to contact. [L0601.003]
  • (older, 2021-22) Likely linker lists can contain thousands of referring pages, so an automated email search can produce a large pool of outreach contacts from one list. [L0601.002]

The Referring Page URL and Link URL columns are the two inputs needed later to match contacts to each linking site. [L0601.035]

  • (older, 2021-22) While doing skyscraper research or reviewing resource pages, run Check My Links on every relevant page you visit. Add any invalid link you find to your outreach list, because you can offer the site owner a fix before you ask for a backlink. [L0332.028]
  • (older, 2021-22) Each outreach batch should contain only prospects whose content is relevant to your article's topic. Pair each prospect with the URL of the article you want them to link to, so the recipient can read it from the email. [L0946.016]
  • (older, 2021-22) Contact details can usually be found for about half of the likely linking domains, not for every one. Plan outreach lists with that loss in mind. [L0601.001]
  • (older, 2021-22) Searching each website by hand for contact emails is very slow and often fruitless, because many domains do not publish email addresses on their site. [L0601.012]

Finding and Choosing Contact Emails

  • (older, 2021-22) Hunter.io finds email addresses for sites. Sites where no email address is found are recorded in the No Email Database, since other methods may find an address for them later. [L0422.012]
  • (older, 2021-22) Contact first names can be found with Hunter.io and with the Norbert tool, which can also find names linked to email addresses. Paste Hunter results into the Hunter Output tab of the email filtering sheet. Where no name is found, leave the Name cell blank so the Mailshake fallback text is shown instead. [L0946.041]
  • (older, 2021-22) In one example, 50 of 78 requested domains returned addresses. A success rate around 50 percent of requested domains is considered an average result. [L0601.020]
  • (older, 2021-22) Send only one outreach email per domain, because repeated emails to the same company look spammy and raise the chance of spam filters. [L0601.025]
  • (older, 2021-22) For a content or link request, choose an editorial or named contact over a sales or advertising inbox, even when the sales address has a slightly higher confidence score, because the request is unlikely to be answered there. [L0601.027]
  • (older, 2021-22) Personal, named addresses are generally more likely to get a reply than generic addresses such as info, admin or customer service. Prefer a named person when the score is comparable. [L0601.028]
  • (older, 2021-22) Use first names where available to personalize outreach emails. Names are not found for every address, especially for generic inboxes, so leave those without a name. [L0601.031]
  • (older, 2021-22) Choosing the final address for each domain is a manual judgment task that takes time on large lists. It is the step that cannot be left to automation. [L0601.030]
  • (older, 2021-22) Selecting one outreach email per domain and transferring it back into the template:
    1. Open the Hunter.io CSV and review every address listed for each domain.
    2. Keep one address per domain, using judgment that combines the confidence score with the best fit for the outreach purpose.
    3. Delete all other rows.
    4. Copy the remaining domain names into the Domain name column of the Hunter Output tab.
    5. Copy the remaining email addresses into the Email Address column in exactly the same order.
    6. Copy the first names into the Name column, in the same order.
    7. Check that the domain, email and name entries line up row by row.
    8. Check the main sheet, where each email and name should now match its referring page root domain.
    9. When finished, remove the URLs from columns A and D. The remaining columns refresh automatically. [L0601.029]
  • (older, 2021-22) In the email filtering sheet, each Root Domain cell is calculated from its URL. The domain starts after the protocol, skipping a leading www. when present, and ends at the first slash. After the batch is built, paste only values into the outreach batch sheet, because formulas stop working once they are copied there. [L0946.042]
  • (older, 2021-22) For skyscraper link-building outreach, bulk sending and automated follow-ups are handled by the outreach tool. The sending mailbox must have good deliverability first, because follow-ups and campaign results depend on it. [L0574.023]

Broken Link Building

  • (older, 2021-22) Broken link building works by helping the owner of another website. You find a dead link on their page, tell them about it, and offer one of your own relevant pages as the replacement. Because you solve a problem for them first, the backlink request is easy to accept. [L0332.001]
  • (older, 2021-22) Dead links that return HTTP 404 errors are common on older content, often around two years old. Owners stop updating resource lists, linked pages get deleted, or linked businesses close. Brand-new articles rarely contain broken links, so focus the search on older content and resource pages on sites in your niche. [L0332.002]
  • (older, 2021-22) Do not assume a site has no broken links just because its newest article is clean. Older content is where broken links cluster, so check older articles and resource lists as well. [L0332.023]
  • (older, 2021-22) A site with many valid redirecting links and no invalid links is being maintained, because the owner updates links when they expire. Such a site is unlikely to offer a broken link opportunity, so move on to the next site. [L0332.012]
  • (older, 2021-22) On content pages, check only in-content links, the links inside the article body. Navigation and sidebar links are not relevant for outreach. In-content links on a fresh article usually come back green, so check older articles first. [L0332.013]
  • (older, 2021-22) Before outreach, make sure your site has a suitable page to receive the link, such as a content page that covers the same topic. Without a good replacement page you have nothing useful to offer the site owner. [L0332.014]
  • (older, 2021-22) Choose the target page on your site to match the broken link. Use your homepage for general links, and a content page or your blog collection for topic-specific links. A product page can work in some cases, but only when it is a sensible replacement for the product being linked. [L0332.015]
  • (older, 2021-22) Affiliate links are acceptable, but your own site must run an affiliate program before you ask for a link that is part of an affiliate deal. The site owner may then need to negotiate placement. If a site links to a product page for a product it no longer sells, the link is likely broken and outreach is still worth it. [L0332.016]
  • (older, 2021-22) Example: on an Australian home-building site, a resource page with 111 links showed 94 valid, 17 valid redirecting and 0 invalid links. The owner was keeping it current, so it was not a good outreach target. A recent article on the same site with 177 links showed 89 valid, 10 valid redirecting and 0 invalid links, which fits the pattern that new content rarely has broken links. [L0332.030]
  • (older, 2021-22) Broken link outreach workflow:
    1. Install the Check My Links Chrome extension from the Chrome Web Store.
    2. While browsing relevant sites in your niche, open a resource page or an older content page.
    3. Click the extension and let it run. The counter shows the links still in the queue.
    4. Read the results panel: Links, Valid links, Valid redirecting links, Warnings and Invalid links.
    5. Look at the page itself. The links highlighted red are the invalid ones.
    6. Keep only in-content links and confirm each broken link fits your niche and your replacement page.
    7. Choose the replacement page on your site: homepage, content page, blog collection or, in some cases, a product page.
    8. Find the site owner's email address on the site. If none is usable, search for it with hunter.io.
    9. Send the outreach email: point out the broken link, offer your page as a replacement, and make clear there is no pressure if they decline.
    10. Send follow-up emails until you get a reply or conclude there will be none. [L0332.017]
  • (older, 2021-22) Broken link building works best combined with resource page link building and the skyscraper method. Each tactic adds only a few links per month, but together they accumulate into meaningful domain authority and link juice over time. [L0332.024]

Broken Content Linking and Resource Pages

  • (older, 2021-22) Broken content linking is a variation of broken link building. It uses a content search tool, such as Ahrefs Content Explorer, as the search engine for link targets instead of hunting for dead links directly. Traditional broken link building is used first to build the initial links. [L0462.039]

Backlink Basics

  • A backlink is any clickable link on another website that leads to your store's homepage or any other page of the store. [L1227.009]
  • Backlink quality depends on the authority of the linking site. One link from a strong, relevant site with high domain authority can give a bigger boost than hundreds or thousands of links from sites with low domain authority. [L1227.010]
  • A healthy link profile mixes several link sources, but the links are not equal in value. [L1227.011]
  • Social profiles on YouTube, Facebook, Instagram and Pinterest that link to the store feed into domain authority. Posts by other people that share product pages or blog posts with a link back to the store count too. [L1227.012]
  • Search engine friendliness, meaning how well the site is optimized on-page, is a third factor that feeds into domain authority alongside backlinks and social signals. [L1227.013]
  • In a Backlinko and Ahrefs analysis, the number one Google result had 3.8 times more backlinks than positions 2 to 10. [L1461.006]
  • Page authority is a third-party score, such as the one Ahrefs publishes, that estimates a page's strength. Google does not use it, and the closest Google concept is PageRank. Domain Authority is likewise a third-party metric rather than a Google one. [L1465.041]
  • Organic backlinks usually point to the home page, while proactive link building should target content pages and cornerstone pages. As a store grows, its home page collects most of the backlinks, and that link value reaches other pages through the main menu. [L1465.042]

Domain Authority

  • Domain authority is a score from 1 to 100 built from on-site optimization together with external links pointing to the store. A higher score improves the chances of ranking well, especially for competitive keywords. [L1227.001]
  • Broad, competitive keywords need a high domain authority. Niche keywords, collection-style keywords, most product pages and some blog posts need much less, because competition for them is lower. [L1227.002]
  • Track domain authority over time and aim for steady upward movement as more websites link to the store. Check it periodically rather than once. [L1227.003]
  • Running competitor sites through the same tool shows which domain authority level you should aim for when targeting broad keywords in your niche. [L1227.006]

Steps to check a domain's authority

  2. Click check authority. [L1227.005]

Finding Prospects

Procedure: finding likely linkers for a target keyword

  1. Pick the target keyword and check that its SERP contains many ranking pages with many backlinks.
  2. Open the SERP overview in an SEO tool (Ahrefs Keywords Explorer in the example, but any SEO tool works) and export the full results list as CSV.
  3. Repeat step 2 for every similar secondary keyword, since more keywords uncover more likely linkers.
  4. Merge all exported CSV files into one sheet in Excel or Google Sheets.
  5. Remove duplicate rows based on the URL column only, keeping the first instance of each.
  6. Sort the sheet by the backlinks column from highest to lowest.
  7. Work down the sorted list only while domains have at least 10 backlinks.
  8. For each domain, paste its URL into Site Explorer, open the Backlinks report, filter to Dofollow, group similar links as one link per domain, and export the full list as CSV.
  9. Save all per-domain exports in the same folder.
  10. Merge the per-domain exports into one master sheet.
  11. Filter the master sheet by Domain Rating and keep only domains between 10 and 75. [L1443.001]

Principles and rules

  • A likely linker is a site that has already linked to a page on the same topic as your content, so it is the most probable source of a new link to your better piece on that topic. [L1443.002]
  • A keyword can still be worth using for link building even if its keyword difficulty is above the target of 10, as long as many ranking pages for it have many backlinks. [L1443.003]
  • Use as many secondary keywords as possible with the same topic when collecting SERP results, because each extra keyword surfaces additional likely linkers. [L1443.004]
  • For this link-building method only dofollow backlinks are pursued; nofollow links are excluded from the list of likely linkers. [L1443.005]
  • Process only domains with at least 10 backlinks to the ranking page. Below that threshold the time spent per link drops sharply, and in the example about 35 domains exceeded 10 backlinks. [L1443.006]
  • Do not manually research what each referring page is about or who runs it at this stage; exporting the data and moving on saves a lot of time. [L1443.010]
  • In one example the first domain had 137 dofollow backlinks from unique domains, and the second had 19. Each domain's full list is exported separately before merging. [L1443.011]

Resource pages

  • A resource page is a section of a larger website that links out to other valuable pages, websites and tools on one topic. Its labels are typically resources, useful resources, links or useful links. Getting listed there is a supplementary link-building tactic, not a main driver, and works best as an extra link source for content pages, blog pages or the home page. [L1237.001]
  • Resource page link building is not a volume tactic, because a given niche usually has only a few such pages. Expect a small number of wins and treat the search as a recurring task rather than a campaign. Broader adjacent niches can also yield relevant pages. [L1237.002]
  • Build search terms from the topics and use cases people have for your products rather than from product names, because the people who write resource pages think in topics. For example, a bathroom products store should search on the home renovation topic rather than on bathtub or vanity. Product-name searches are less productive for this tactic. [L1237.003]
  • Google results vary by country, so run the searches in the Google version for the market you sell to. Results in another country may return irrelevant pages for the same search terms. [L1237.016]

Procedure: finding and qualifying resource pages (2021-22, older advice)

  1. Choose topic-based keywords that describe what your customers use your products for.
  2. Search Google for each keyword combined with resources, helpful links, useful links and useful resources, for example keyword followed by the word resources.
  3. Search keyword followed by inurl:links to find URLs that contain the word links, and try resources as well.
  4. Go through pages two and three of the search results, since useful pages often appear there.
  5. Quickly assess the quality of every candidate page, starting with its domain authority.
  6. Discard candidates with a domain authority of zero or below 10.
  7. Check that the page is relevant to your topic and that its audience matches your target market; discard irrelevant pages.
  8. Decide which of your own pages fits the page (homepage, blog page or blog collection page, or a newly written blog post) and pitch only that page.
  9. Find the email address on the site, or use Hunter.io if none is listed.
  10. Send a personalised outreach email from your template and fill in the specific details.
  11. Handle any link negotiation (payment or link exchange requests) with a prepared response.
  12. Send follow-up emails at a regular interval. [L1237.004]

Search operator and tools

  • The Google search operator inurl:links returns pages with the word links in their URL, which often reveals links pages that are resource pages. The same operator works with the word resources. Combine it with the topic keyword, for example keyword followed by inurl:links. [L1237.005]
  • Ubersuggest is used to check the domain authority of a candidate resource page's website before you pitch it. Run every candidate through an SEO tool, such as Ubersuggest, as part of the quality check. [L1237.006]
  • Hunter.io finds email addresses for a website's owner or contact when no email is published on the site. Use it as the fallback for locating outreach contacts. [L1237.007]
  • The tools are used in sequence: first keyword and competitor research to identify content and likely linking sites, then email extraction to find contact addresses for those sites, and finally cold email outreach to request links. [L1388.012]
  • Hunter.io can be searched with a website URL to return the email addresses it holds for that domain, or with a single email address to check whether it can be found. You are charged only for each address that is successfully returned, so searches with no result cost nothing. [L1388.014]

Domain authority thresholds for resource pages (2021-22, older advice)

  • Domain authority below 10 should be avoided for resource page link building, and above 10 is generally worth pursuing. A domain authority of zero passes no SEO value, and accumulating many such links can hurt your ability to rank. [L1237.008]
  • Check the quality of a resource page's site before you pitch it, using an SEO tool to confirm its domain authority is above 10. Only proceed to pitching once the site passes this check. [L1237.009]
  • The procedure discards 10 and below, while the rule text says above 10; treat exactly 10 as the boundary case and check it against the other filters. [L1237.004, L1237.008, L1237.009]

Choosing which of your pages to pitch

  • If a resource page lists businesses, suppliers or similar companies, pitch your homepage as the page to be linked. If it links to individual content on other sites, pitch a blog post or your blog collection page instead. [L1237.010]
  • Pitch a blog collection page that holds many useful articles when the resource page links to blogs or useful content in general. Choose a page that gives the audience real advice relevant to the resource page's topic. [L1237.011]
  • If no existing page of yours fits a content-linking resource page, write a new blog post on a topic the audience is interested in, ideally one that can rank for keywords. Pitch that post to the resource page after it is published, and do not rush it. [L1237.012]
  • If a resource page mixes trade bodies, government sources and service providers without a single clear theme, pitch either your homepage as a trusted supplier of the product category or your blog collection page with practical how-to content. Choose the page that best matches the mix of links already on the list. [L1237.022]
  • Do not pitch product pages or collection pages to resource pages. These are commercial pages, so the request reads as a sales ask rather than a value offer for the site's audience, which lowers the chance of being listed. [L1237.013]
  • Do not pitch pages on your site that are only loosely related to the topic of the resource page. An irrelevant page significantly reduces the chance of being added to that page. [L1237.014]

Judging neglected sites and mixed lists

  • A site that looks neglected or dated is not automatically disqualified. If the blog has been online for many years and the author still publishes new posts, it can still be worth pitching after you pass the domain authority check. [L1237.021]
  • Some blogs offer guest post exchanges, where they publish your guest post in return for one of yours. If the link is to a commercial site, the blog may charge according to rates published on its advertising page, so check that page before agreeing. [L1237.019]

Example of search volume

  • A search combining a topic keyword with inurl:links in Google Australia returned about 202,000 results. Many hits were ordinary business or service pages rather than resource pages, so every result needs a manual check of what it actually is before you spend time on it. [L1237.024]

Outreach Emails

  • All outreach emails should be short, to the point, and polite. Long messages or off-topic digressions reduce response rates, so keep each message to the request and a clear question. [L0947.020]
  • Resource page outreach does not require a bulk email or mail-merge tool. Send each email individually, because the volume of relevant resource pages in one niche is small. [L1237.023]
  • The pitch should frame your page as a valuable resource for the site's audience rather than as a way to promote your products. Your page's usefulness to the audience is the reason the owner should add the link. [L1237.015]
  • Many blogs do not publish a public email address. Look for a contact page, an about page or an author page, where a named author's email may be listed. If none is found, use Hunter.io. [L1237.017]
  • Prepare a standard response to payment and link-exchange requests before you start outreach, and use it consistently. [L1237.018]
  • For backlink outreach, send no more than two to three emails to an influencer or publication. If there is no response, switch to a different channel such as a Facebook page or Instagram direct message, then check back in the future. [L1094.099]

Opening formula for a resource page outreach email

Formula: name the specific resource page, give one brief reason it helps its readers, name the single page of yours that adds value, and ask directly whether they would consider adding it. It works because it shows you read the page, moves the request from self-promotion to audience value, and gives the owner an easy yes or no.

  • Example 1: Hello [Name], your links page on [topic] is a great find for people researching [subtopic]. I have written a guide on [subtopic] that would fit well alongside your listings, so would you consider adding it?
  • Example 2: Hi [Name], your resources list on [topic] saves readers a lot of searching. I put together a practical checklist for [subtopic] that could be useful to your audience, and I wondered if you would be open to including it. [L1237.025]

Replies and Negotiation

Hard no versus soft no

  • Respond differently to two kinds of rejection: a hard no (rude or hostile) should not be answered, and the contact should be removed from the outreach list and tagged as do-not-contact for future campaigns. A soft no (polite refusal, often with praise) should be answered to keep the relationship open. [L0947.010]
  • Replying to a hostile hard no escalates the situation and rarely changes the outcome. Do not reply; delete the contact from the list, record it as do-not-email, and move on. [L0947.011]
  • Outreach is about building relationships with sites in your industry and niche, so a no today does not rule out a link later. Continue to respond politely to soft no replies because other collaboration opportunities may come from the same contact. [L0947.014]

Declining a paid link while keeping the relationship

Template: declining a paid-link offer and offering a guest post

Thanks for your reply. Unfortunately, I don't have the budget to pay for links of this kind. If you'd still like to work together, could I write a guest post for your site instead? I'm confident I could create something that adds genuine value for your readers. What do you think?:)

[L0947.004]

  • Declining a paid link while keeping the relationship works by naming a budget or policy limit, then offering a no-cost alternative that still gives the other site content value and gives you a backlink. The offer is framed as mutual benefit, and the reply can be yes or no.
    • Example 1: Thanks for the quote. Our budget is set for content only this quarter. Would you be open to a short guest article from me instead? Happy to draft a few ideas.
    • Example 2: We don't pay for placements right now, but I can contribute an in-depth guide to your resource section that your readers would find useful. Interested? [L0947.005]

Link exchanges

  • Link exchanges, where the other site asks you to link back, are generally avoided because search engines may view reciprocal linking unfavorably over time and it spends your own link authority. When a site proposes an exchange, the preferred response is to decline politely and redirect to a guest post. Declining is acceptable and not a sign of a failed outreach. [L0947.006]

Template: declining a link exchange and redirecting to a guest post (version 1)

Thanks for getting back to me. Sadly, I avoid link exchanges because they can cause problems with Google. I understand you need to gain something from these arrangements, so I was wondering whether I could write a guest post for your site. It would be a mutually beneficial way to collaborate. Any thoughts?:)

[L0947.007]

Template: declining a link exchange and redirecting to a guest post (version 2)

Thanks for getting back to me. Regrettably, I steer clear of link exchanges. My SEO specialist has warned me that these can sometimes lead to trouble with Google. I completely understand you want to get something out of such exchanges, so I had the idea of writing a guest post for your site. It would be a win-win collaboration. Any thoughts?:)

Template: closing reply when a site will not write a guest post and you decline the exchange entirely

Totally get it, don't blame you. We'll just have to leave it there, but if you do still want to link to my site, please feel free to do so.

[L0947.009]

Asking for feedback after a soft no

Template: requesting feedback

Hey, thanks for getting back to me. No problem:) I wanted to check whether you had any feedback. I just want to be sure I wasn't too far off track with this. Thanks

[L0947.012]

  • After a soft no, asking for feedback opens a second chance. If feedback points to a different content direction, offer to write a guest post on that topic at no cost to them. The pattern is: acknowledge, ask for feedback, then offer a guest post aligned with what they want.
    • Example 1: Thanks for the honest reply. What topic would fit your readers better? I could draft a piece on that and send it over.
    • Example 2: Appreciate you letting me know. If you'd like something more practical for your audience, I can write a guest article on that angle. [L0947.013]

Clarifying a misunderstood request

  • When a contact did not understand the request (confusion or suspicion they must pay), send a short polite clarification that asks them to update an existing piece of content to include a link to your article. Use the batch spreadsheet to find the target URL before writing. If they stay unclear, offer a phone call to walk them through it. [L0947.015]

Template: clarifying the request

Thanks for writing back. I had a smaller exchange in mind. Would you be open to editing your article at [Their URL] so it links to my article at [Your URL]? I think it would be a valuable addition for your readers. What do you think?

[L0947.016]

Follow-Up and Verification

Procedure: follow-up when an agreed link never appeared

  1. Check the page for the link; confirm it is missing, and check Google Search Console for the backlink.
  2. Reply within the same email thread so the earlier commitment is visible.
  3. Write a short, polite message saying you looked but could not find the link and ask whether you looked in the wrong place.
  4. Make it sound like you may have missed it, not like they broke a promise, to keep the relationship friendly.
  5. Keep the tone polite and light; they usually add the link after this reminder. [L0947.017]

Template: follow-up reminder

That's great! I just checked, but I couldn't find the link. Maybe I was looking in the wrong place?:) Thanks

[L0947.018]

  • Use Google Search Console to verify whether an agreed backlink has actually gone live on the partner's site. [L0947.019]
  • Send follow-up emails at a regular interval. [L1237.004]

Risk, Anchor Text and Disavowing

  • Disavow spammy backlinks, such as links from adult or unrelated spam sites, before a sale. Buyers look closely at the link profile for these. [L1468.029]
  • If anchor text is heavily over-optimized, add more varied and natural anchors to dilute it. Make these link changes early, because a month-before change can damage the site's look to a buyer. [L1468.030]
  • Buying backlinks is risky because it can lead to a penalty. Stick with guest posting on sites that already publish guest content. [L1094.101]
  • Do not pitch paid links to link farms; remove them from the list instead of negotiating. [L0947.022]
