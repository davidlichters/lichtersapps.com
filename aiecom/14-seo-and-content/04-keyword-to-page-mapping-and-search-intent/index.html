14.4 Keyword-to-Page mapping and search intent

Core rule: one keyword, one page

  • Assign each target keyword to exactly one page on the site. Google generally shows only one result per website for a given query (branded searches are the usual exception), so several pages competing for one term split ranking signals, waste effort, and leave Google unsure which page is primary, so none ranks well. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0758.001, L0877.001, L0879.001, L1120.036]
  • Pointing five or more pages at one search term is a common error, based on the belief that one of them will rank better. In practice, none of them ranks well. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0879.004]
  • Ten pages each optimized for one term is the same error. Optimize one dedicated page per keyword and spread the rest of the keyword list across other pages. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.002]
  • A single page with focused, deliberate keyword use has a much higher chance of reaching page one than many pages with scattered keyword targeting. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.003]
  • Two pages aimed at the same keyword compete and weaken each other. Search engines treat them as duplicates, so keeping both splits relevance. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0949.050]
  • Do not optimize the whole site around one keyword. Assign each keyword to one specific page, post, collection or product, with as little overlap as possible, so the site can rank for many keywords across different pages. [L1120.013]
  • Do not put the same keyword on multiple pages. The overlap weakens the ranking of each page. [L1120.036]
  • For a keyword with search volume, only one page from a site typically ranks on page one of Google, about 95% of the time. Consolidate similar pages rather than expecting several of them to rank on the same results page. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1465.028]

Splitting the keyword list and mapping it to pages

  • Split the keyword list into two groups. Category and product keywords are kept for category and product pages. Informational keywords go into the article list. Exception: a product keyword with strong search volume can also be used in product titles. [L0380.011]
  • Map each keyword to a content type before the content is written. A product group or sub-category keyword becomes a Shopify collection. A comparison or educational keyword becomes a blog post. An evergreen tool, such as a calculator, becomes a page. [L1120.014]
  • Content strategy for ranking keywords (Source A, 2025):
    1. Choose the main keyword (e.g., "hybrid surfboards").
    2. If the keyword is relevant to a product collection, make that collection page rank for the keyword.
    3. In blog posts and other content, link to the collection page.
    4. This focuses link authority and ranking power on the collection. [L0255.063]
  • Keyword workflow from research to tracking (older, 2021-22):
    1. Build a large keyword list through keyword research, which can be outsourced to a freelancer for a spreadsheet.
    2. Select the keywords you want to target.
    3. Reduce them to a top 20 or top 50 list.
    4. Map each keyword to an existing page, or add a planned page to your SEO plan if no page fits.
    5. Track the ranking of the top keywords over time with a tool such as Ubersuggest, and watch organic traffic alongside it. [L0879.005]

Choosing the page type by intent

  • Search intent determines the page type. Commercial or non-branded product-type searches with no specific item in mind lead to collection or content pages. A precise product search leads to a product page. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.010]
  • A shopper who searches a generic product type without a specific item in mind is better served by a collection or content page that shows options and guidance. Sending them straight to one product page forces extra navigation, and each extra click raises the chance they leave. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.023]
  • Judge the user's intent behind each term, not only the product name. A broad term such as "surfboards with motors" is a middle-of-funnel search, so it fits a content or collection page rather than a single product page. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0877.020]
  • Search intent must match the landing page. Informational queries should land on pages that answer the question. Queries for a named product model should land on a page about that exact product, not on a general category or content page. [L1099.002]
  • For organic traffic, identify the search terms that trigger each page. If a page receives the wrong searches, either adapt the landing page to the terms that should bring buyers, or rank a separate page for those other terms. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0473.008]
  • Map terms to page types by intent (older, 2021-22):
    • A term asking what kinds of a product exist, or how to choose one, such as "types of surfboards", fits a content piece.
    • A low-difficulty tool or calculator term with real volume, such as "surfboard volume calculator", fits a content piece.
    • A term for one specific model or product number fits a product page.
    • A broad group term for a buyer segment can go to a guide page, or to a collection page with added content. [L0877.033]
  • Buying-intent pattern (older, 2021-22): combine the product category with a buying-intent modifier, such as "for sale", "best", or "top seven", plus the year. It works because people close to a purchase search with these modifiers, so pages built this way match high-intent searches. Two new examples in this pattern: "standing desk converters for sale" and "top seven espresso machines for 2026". [L0768.009]

Checking the SERP before you write

  • Before writing, check the top 10 Google results for a search term and note their format, such as product pages or guides. If most of the top results are product pages, a product page is needed to compete for that keyword. [L0382.023]
  • Pattern matching: the page you publish must look like the pages Google already ranks for that keyword. Transactional product or collection pages do not rank for informational queries, and blog posts do not rank for commercial queries. Check the top 10 results first to see which page type wins. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L0883.005]
  • Check search intent with a Google search before choosing the page type (older, 2021-22):
    1. Search the target keyword in Google.
    2. Look at the first page of results (the first five results are used as the reference).
    3. If most results are product pages, Google sees commercial intent, so target a product page with the best matching product.
    4. If most results are content pages, Google sees informational intent, so create a content page for the keyword. [L0881.014]
  • Choosing between a collection page and a content page (older, 2021-22):
    1. Search the keyword in Google and review the results on the first page.
    2. If the first page is mostly collection pages, Google wants to show product listings, so a collection page fits.
    3. If the first page is mostly content pages such as listicles, build a content page that is better than the existing results.
    4. Check the backlink counts of the top results in Ahrefs or Ubersuggest.
    5. If the top results have many backlinks, choose a content page, because backlinks are easier to earn for content than for collection pages. [L0879.003]
  • Manual SERP check to confirm a keyword tool's SERP picture (older, 2021-22):
    1. Search the candidate keyword in a normal Google search with the same country setting used in the keyword tool.
    2. Compare the organic results with the SERP overview from the keyword tool.
    3. Confirm that most top results are informational articles with many backlinks, not store, government or navigational pages.
    4. Keep the topic only if the manual search confirms the tool's SERP picture. [L0878.029]
  • For a "best [product]" search term, check the first page of results. If most results are listicles, a single product page is unlikely to rank. Target that term with a listicle and place your own product at the top. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0949.067]

Case, product-group keyword "L-shaped standing desk" (Source A): the team checked the organic results and ignored sponsored ads. The first organic result was a manufacturer's product page optimized for the keyword. The second was an Amazon page that looked like a product but was a collection of L-shaped standing desks. A Wayfair result, reached through its brand section, was also a collection page for L-shaped standing desks. Because the top pages for this product-group term were mostly collection pages, the keyword was assigned to a Shopify collection rather than a blog post. [L1120.039]

Product pages

  • Product pages should target brand name, product name and SKU terms. These have little competition and often rank in the top three with minimal work. Category and blog pages should keep targeting their own broader topics. (Source A, 2023-2024.) [L0855.149, L0965.080]
  • Product pages target branded terms, specific product names, model names or product numbers, and very specific product-type searches that one product can fully answer. Do not assign broad terms to product pages. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0758.005, L0877.006]
  • Product pages rarely rank for anything beyond the product name, because Google reads the product name first from the page title. For broader terms, use a blog, content page or collection page instead. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0879.002]
  • Ranking a product page for a broad product-type term is much harder than ranking a content piece for it, although it is not impossible. Product pages should therefore be assigned only narrow, specific search terms. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0877.007]
  • Product pages are poor targets for broad, non-branded search terms. Many other pages on the site also use those terms, and a product page has limited text space to optimize for them. A product page works best for terms tied to its own title, model or part number, and brand plus product type. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.022]
  • Product pages can rank for a brand name plus a specific product type, such as a brand and model-type combination, but not for the brand name alone. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.008]
  • Broad group terms with many suitable products are not product-page targets. A term such as "surfboards with motors" is mid-funnel and belongs on a content or collection page. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0758.012]
  • Do not send a search for a broad group, such as "surfboards for beginners", to one specific product page. If the shop holds dozens of matching products, the chance that one page is the right product for that searcher is very low. A visitor may leave and not return if the page does not match. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0877.021, L0758.012]
  • A term that matches one or several products is best left off the product-page list when the business has many suitable items. Such terms belong on content or collection pages. Product pages are reserved for brand names, model names, product numbers and very specific single-product searches. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0758.025]
  • On e-commerce sites with more than about 50 products, especially grouped in a few product types with 30 or more items each, product pages should target specific keywords: product title, model or part number, or brand plus product type, not broad generic terms. On sites with only one or two products, a more liberal keyword approach is acceptable. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.006]
  • Keyword-to-product exception: a product keyword with strong search volume can also be used in product titles. [L0380.011]

Collection pages

  • Collection pages target broad product-type terms, brand names, and specific product-type terms. They suit users looking for a type of product, or a brand, that has a selection of products. Competitive collection-page terms need substantial written content on the page to rank. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0758.004, L0877.005]
  • Collection or category pages suit product-type keywords and, for resellers, brand names or brand plus product type combinations. They are the right page when a shopper searches for a type of product without knowing which exact item they want. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.009]
  • For a product-group term where the top organic results are mostly collection pages, assign a Shopify collection rather than a blog post. [L1120.039]

Content pages

  • Content pages target informational terms, problem-based terms (searches where the user seems to be looking for an answer to a problem), and broad product-type and product-use terms (what a product is used for). (Older, 2021-22.) [L0758.003, L0877.004]
  • Content pages such as blog posts, guides and resource pages suit informational keywords (for example, how to choose a product), broad product-type keywords, and specific product-type keywords. Collection and product pages are not suited to informational queries. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.011]
  • Content pages allow longer text, so they can hold more keyword variations and synonyms and can go deeper on a term than a collection page. A content page can reach about 10 or more keyword occurrences when the post runs around 3,000 words. A product page with little text usually needs only 2 to 3 mentions beyond the first 100 words. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.012]
  • Deep informational targeting, such as how-to guides or buying advice, belongs on content pages. Collection pages carry some content, but within a tighter limit. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.025]
  • Prefer content pages over collection pages for broad, educational terms, such as "surfboards for beginners". Content pages attract backlinks, while collection and product pages rarely do. Link acquisition is needed for competitive terms, and content is the most practical page type for earning links. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0758.013]
  • Broad, high-volume product-type keywords are more competitive and need high-quality content and backlinks to rank. Content pages are the usual choice because they attract backlinks more easily than product pages. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.013]
  • When a broad "for beginners" term fits many products, a content page such as an ultimate guide works better than a product or collection page. The guide should explain which product type the visitor needs, then link to the matching collection or specific product pages. If a collection page is used instead, it still needs written content to rank. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0758.024]
  • For a term that asks for guidance, create one ultimate guide page that educates the visitor on the right product choice, then link from it to the matching collection or product pages. If no content piece is planned, a collection page for the same term is the fallback. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0877.022]
  • Lower-volume variations of a head term, such as adjective versions like "hybrid", "retro" or "cheap", can sit on the same page when no other page targets that exact phrase. This lets one page rank for many terms, each with smaller search volume. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.024]
  • Example: a laptop stand for desk keyword (14,800 monthly searches, high competition, $2.16 CPC) can support a blog post comparing a laptop stand with an adjustable-height desk, even if the store does not sell laptop stands. A related keyword about ergonomics can support a second post comparing an ergonomic laptop stand with a standing desk. (Source A.) [L1120.034]

Keyword placement on a page

  • Google uses on-page text (headings, body and the words visible on the page) as its most basic relevance signal, so a page meant to rank for a term should contain that term. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.004]
  • A target keyword should appear roughly 5 to 10 times across the whole page, counting every on-page element: title, product or post heading, image alt text, H2 subheadings and body copy, not just the body text. The range exists because a page has many places where keywords can sit. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.001]
  • Use synonyms and close variations of the focus keyword to avoid repeating the exact phrase too often. If the exact phrase would have to appear more than about 10 times, switch to close variations that no other page on the site is targeting. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.016]
  • Keyword placement workflow for a new page (older, 2021-22):
    1. Start from the keyword research list and pick one focus keyword for the page.
    2. Confirm the page type fits the keyword intent (product, collection or content page).
    3. Place the focus keyword in the title tag, ideally at the front without making the title read awkwardly.
    4. Place it in the first 100 words of the body text, plus 2 to 3 more occurrences depending on the page length.
    5. Use it in at least one H2 subheading (not every H2 needs it).
    6. Use it in at least one image file name.
    7. Use it in at least one image alt text.
    8. Include it in the page URL.
    9. Include it in the meta title.
    10. Include it in the meta description.
    11. Check the total count stays within about 5 to 10 occurrences across the page, and use synonyms or close variations for further mentions. [L0881.005]

Reviewing existing pages in Search Console

  • Case, hoodie store (Source A): the store checked Google Search Console for its product queries over the last three months. The query "Source A hoodie" had 88 total impressions with an average organic position of 5. A unisex zip hoodie page appeared for this query with 85 impressions at position 5 but received zero clicks, which made it the page to optimize first for that keyword. [L1271.014]

Mapping an existing site to keywords

  • Mapping an existing large site (older, 2021-22):
    1. Map each page to one keyword it should target.
    2. Start with the most important pages.
    3. Make sure no other page targets the same keyword.
    4. Work through the rest of the site gradually, since this cannot be done overnight on a large site. [L0881.020]
  • Case, surf shop (older, 2021-22): the shop has about 30 surfboard variations across brands, sizes and configurations, and one product-type term appears on many of its pages. Choose one page for that head term, preferably a content or collection page rather than a product page, and use the term 5 to 10 times on that page only. Other pages keep it at one or two uses, so Google can see the chosen page as the most relevant match for the term. [L0881.021]
  • Among very similar product pages that compete for the same keywords, choose one as the main page and point more internal links to it. This tells search engines which page to rank and stops them from splitting ranking power across similar pages. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1465.027]
  • Link content to the collection it should rank for. Blog posts and other content should link to the collection page, which focuses link authority and ranking power on the collection. [L0255.063]

Merging competing pages

  • Merging competing blog posts (older, 2021-22):
    1. Identify the posts that target the same keyword.
    2. Choose the stronger post to keep as the winner.
    3. If the weaker post has no links and no authority, delete it and redirect its URL to the winner.
    4. Copy any useful information from the deleted post into the winner.
    5. If the weaker post has links and authority, reassign it to a different keyword instead of deleting it.
    6. If the content fits another term, update the post for that term rather than deleting it. [L0949.051]

Category mapping and topical authority

  • A 12-month content plan should aim at topical authority: becoming the go-to source for one subject area. Topical authority is built over time through a connected set of pages, not single articles. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1389.002]

  • One product can belong to several categories at once. A men's black running short, for example, fits men's running shorts, black running shorts, and running shorts with pockets. A category for each relevant search variant lets the store target more specific keywords and reach shoppers who search for exactly that item. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1389.003]

  • Broad head terms, such as "running shorts", face heavy competition and often attract shoppers with a different intent, such as a woman searching for women's products. Specific category terms are easier to rank for faster and convert better, which brings sales sooner. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1389.004]

  • Build a mix of broad parent categories and narrower niche categories, with narrow ones based on specific use cases. Furniture example: sectional categories for piece count (two-piece, three-piece, four-piece), color (brown, leather, black, canvas, white), and room (living room sectionals). A single product can sit in several of these at once. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1389.017]

  • Attribute categories pair a product kind with one or more traits, such as shade, cut, or purpose. In clothing, the structure runs gender, then product, then trait:

    • men's running shorts with pockets
    • men's running shorts with a lining
    • men's running shorts without a lining
    • men's compression running shorts
    • women's running shorts with a high waist

    For a swimwear shop, the same structure yields women's high-waisted swim bottoms with ruching and men's quick-dry board shorts with a zip pocket. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1389.018]

  • Granular category pages capture shoppers with very specific searches, and these shoppers are more likely to buy. Most competitors do not build pages at this level of detail, so the store gains a ranking and sales advantage. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1389.019]

  • Blog articles or Shopify pages can both serve as topical authority content, and either format works. Each article should link to the relevant collection pages, since that internal linking is how the collections are ranked. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1389.022]

  • Hybrid categories, such as a non-toxic version of a product, are only worth creating if people actually search for them on Google. SEO captures existing demand rather than creating it, so a hybrid category without search volume will gain little traction. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1389.012]

  • Claim-type modifiers, such as non-toxic or PFAS-free, can be tested as hybrid categories at the broad product level, for example "non-toxic running clothes", rather than at a narrow level such as "non-toxic running tights". Narrow claim-plus-product combinations often show too little monthly search volume on Google, so check the volume before creating them. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1389.025]

  • Map out every category you can think of, including an authority cluster for each, while skipping top-of-funnel and AI Overview-type queries. [L1389.011]

  • Leave out top-of-funnel terms that everyone already understands, such as the basic product name in its most obvious form. Also leave out query types that an AI Overview would likely answer directly, because articles on those queries rarely earn clicks. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1389.005]

  • Put the exclusion rules into the prompt from the start, such as telling the AI to skip AI Overview-type queries and top-of-funnel terms. Do not let the AI generate every query and then delete the unwanted ones afterward. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1389.006]

  • Give the AI a list of direct competitors when building the map, since better input produces better output and speeds up the work. Choose competitors you actually want to model your categories on. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1389.007]

  • Firecrawl can scrape competitor websites so that their category structure and page content can be analyzed. The scraped data serves as input for mapping the competitor's categories. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1389.008]

  • Before creating pages, remove any category you do not stock or sell. Example: if the store does not sell high-waisted running leggings, drop that category from the map. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1389.021]

  • Once the category list is cleaned, put the final map into one shared document in a fixed format, so the whole team can read and review it before any collection page copy is written. This document is the handover point between the category map and the content work. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1389.024]

  • Category mapping procedure (Source E, 2025-26):

    1. Map out every category you can think of, including an authority cluster for each one, while skipping top-of-funnel and AI Overview-type queries.
    2. Give the AI your chosen competitors and have it map the categories against them.
    3. Refine and trim the map inside the chat.
    4. Validate each category's search demand with a keyword tool.
    5. Remove the categories you do not sell.
    6. Export the cleaned map.
    7. Create a collection page for each remaining category.
    8. Create blog articles or Shopify pages that link to these collections to build topical authority. [L1389.011]

  • AI-generated map exports can contain multiple tabs and hundreds of rows with hallucinated, wrong, or irrelevant entries. Do the analysis and trimming inside the chat first, then export a cleaned version instead of reviewing a raw spreadsheet. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1389.009]

  • Case, hypothetical running brand (Source E, 2025-26): the brand had hundreds of non-toxic SKUs across all running subcategories, and the category mapping was tested on them. The competitors came from an athleisure strategy built a month earlier, with Happier Earth and Allware named as the main examples, and the AI was asked to map categories against them. The lesson reports no traffic or sales result for this map, only the planning setup. [L1389.026]

Pitfalls

  • Do not optimize one page around a single item from a set of similar topics, such as one fun fact out of many. Create separate content for each item instead, because a rewrite around one sub-topic can move rankings backward. (Source E, 2025-26.) [L1269.021]
  • Common mistake: keyword stuffing across many pages. Keep one dedicated page per keyword and use the rest of the list on other pages. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0881.002, L0879.004]

Further details

  • Product pages should rank for product-specific terms such as a model name, but not for broad category terms that are very hard to rank for on a product page. Broad terms are targeted with separate content pages that link to the product page. [L0396.028]
