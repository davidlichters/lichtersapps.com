7.17 Value ladders, subscriptions and repeat purchase

Value ladders: structure and principles

  • A value ladder puts value on the vertical axis and price on the horizontal axis, so the highest-value, highest-price offer sits in the top right corner. Each rung must give customers real value so they naturally want to move up. Build the ladder before building any sales funnel. [L0523.006]
  • A stranger will not pay a top-tier price before receiving value from you. A cheap, low-risk entry offer lets people experience your value first, and the higher rungs then sell themselves. Never lead with your most expensive offer to cold prospects. [L0523.007]
  • Most businesses have only one or two parts of a complete value ladder. Fill the gaps by adding a frontend bait offer at the bottom and premium backend services at the top. A missing rung usually explains why growth stalls. [L0523.010]
  • The frontend offer must be attractive enough to get people through the door, not just a necessary service. A dull entry offer for something people buy only when in pain keeps the business struggling. Choose a popular free or low-cost entry that draws traffic, then upgrade customers to the core service. [L0523.013]
  • Every value ladder needs a continuity offer you can bill every month, such as software, a membership or ongoing coaching. Recurring billing creates residual income that keeps the business stable and gives customers a reason to stay in the system. [L0523.009]
  • Build the ladder so that each rung gives the customer a clear next step. Keep adding higher-value tiers, because there is no natural ceiling on what people will buy. [L0523.012]

  • A low-price digital product can be extended up the ladder with do-it-yourself plans and a physical version of the same item. A digital guide to building a homemade novelty gadget, for example, can be paired with plans and the finished physical product. Each added piece gives buyers another step up. [L0523.011]
  • In one group of high-ticket members, nine of eighteen people bought an additional 30,000 USD offer when it was presented to them that same evening. A percentage of any audience will always pay for premium access. [L0523.012]
  • Premium access can also be earned rather than bought at once. Pair a low-cost starter product with a premium item that the buyer earns through a small per-unit credit, such as 5 cents per pound of product bought, until the premium is paid off. Repeat purchases then fund the premium while the customer keeps buying. [L1261.140]

Case studies

  • A chiropractic clinic charged about 50 USD per adjustment. Each visit lasted about ten minutes, and the patient paid and left, with no other offers beyond occasional multi-visit continuity plans. The owner mapped a complete value ladder and created a wellness program at 5,000 USD, giving clients about ten times more value per visit, which became the top rung. Because a standard adjustment was not an attractive entry point, the clinic added free massages as bait. Two full-time massage therapists gave the massages and were trained to spot spinal or rib misalignment, then upgrade clients to adjustments and to the wellness program. After the full ladder was in place the business turned around noticeably; no revenue figure was given. [L0523.014]
  • A dentist offered a free teeth cleaning through a mailed postcard to a new patient. During the appointment he noticed yellowing and offered custom whitening trays, then noticed the lower teeth shifting and offered retainers. Together these cost the patient over 2,000 USD in less than an hour. Cosmetic surgery, which would have cost 10,000 USD or more, sits at the top of his ladder. He also booked the patient for a return visit six months later, adding the patient to a continuity program on the way out. [L0523.015]
  • A jewelry brand built a product ladder of three kits. A decision kit serves buyers who do not know the shape. A comparison kit serves buyers who know the shape but not the size. A confirmation kit lets buyers see the final choice on their hand before buying the main product. Each kit moves the buyer to the next stage of the funnel. [L1016.043]
  • A try-on decision kit priced at $156 is credited toward the full purchase. This lowers the risk for undecided buyers and moves them into the next step of the buying process. [L1016.042]

Front-end entry offers

Low-cost entry products (older source)

  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): The front-end offer funnel uses a low-cost entry product, sold near break-even, to attract customers already buying a competing category. The brand then recovers margin through a sample, a discount card or an add-on product in the same package. Formula: entry product sold at cost or below plus a path to the core product. It works because the customer acquisition cost is paid back by the repeat purchase of the core item. Example 1: a supplement brand gives a travel sample of a shake inside a reusable bottle. Example 2: a skincare brand ships a mini serum in a sleeve with a 15 percent code for the full size. [L0511.009]
  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): Protein powder funnel case: a reusable container that holds protein powder on a carabiner costs about $3 to make and about $5 to get into the marketplace. It sells for $10 and is designed to break even at $5 per unit. A sample packet and a store discount card are included, so the buyer enters the brand's customer base at roughly no net acquisition cost. [L0511.010]
  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): A low-cost reusable container for protein powder can be made from a 20-ounce soda bottle. Cut off the top, seal the cut edge and attach a screw cap so the container can hang from a carabiner. This kind of cheap, useful add-on can serve as an entry product sold near cost. [L0511.026]
  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): A product customers buy before the main product, such as a protective or storage accessory for an item in the same industry, can serve as a lead magnet. Its job is to bring in future buyers who can then be remarketed to. [L1152.008]
  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): A low-cost accessory can carry a printed insert or postcard recommending the main product, such as a pellet grill, and listing its benefits. The card can link to a content page, which moves a buyer of a basic version toward the upgrade. [L1152.024]

Trip wires and free-plus-shipping

  • Use a trip wire to filter out freebie seekers. Instead of a free bribe, charge a small amount such as 7 dollars, or place a low-price payment step at the bribe or on the next page. Visitors who only want free information leave, while buyers stay on your list. [L0514.033]
  • A trial product can be given away for free with the buyer paying only the shipping. The payment covers the product cost while contact data is collected. The buyer is then moved to a larger paid offer, so the first step is a lead capture, not a profit source. This works best when the free item is cheap to ship and the follow-up offer is clearly relevant. [L0919.039]
  • Make a buyer qualifying offer immediately after the opt-in, not days later. The offer should be a no-brainer priced around 5 to 7 USD or sold as free-plus-shipping, so nearly every subscriber can say yes. Once buyers are identified, keep the subscriber list and buyer list separate and market to each differently. [L0523.061]

Buyer offer types

  • A tripwire is a smaller offer that is a splinter of the core product, such as one module or training session at a large discount. It usually costs around 7 USD per item and can be physical or digital depending on the market. Its purpose is to turn subscribers into buyers quickly. [L0523.086]
  • A self-liquidating offer costs between 37 and 97 USD and is designed to cover the cost of the ads that brought the buyer in. Upsells then become profit. Free-plus-shipping, trials and tripwires often lose money initially, while a self-liquidating offer is meant to break even on its own. [L0523.087]
  • A straight sale is a regular sale of a high-ticket item priced from 97 USD to 5,000 USD or more. It requires more selling, so it is usually offered only to warm traffic or to buyers who have already been through earlier funnels. Stronger trust is needed before buyers pay these prices. [L0523.088]
  • Do not assume one buyer offer works for every product. A supplement that failed to convert with free-plus-shipping succeeded as a straight sale. Test each product with different buyer qualification offers before giving up on a market. [L0523.089]
  • Hyperactive buyers are people in an urgent state of need who purchase several related items at once. They are the highest-value segment of a buyer list, so identify them fast and present several upsells and downsells while their need is strong. Interest fades within days, so the window is short. [L0523.062]

Upsell paths after the first order

  • A supplement brand case study: base product at $9.99, average order value of $99.99 (10x markup). Structure: buy base at $9.99, then a first upsell offer at $30 (additional supplement plus brain-capacity PDF) for $39.99 total, then free shipping at a $49 threshold, then a second upsell offer at $500 (8-week coaching program, one-to-one VA accountability sessions and money-back guarantee). Total revenue exceeded $250,000 from $9,000 in ad spend. The domain was bought in October and list building started late October, still during Black Friday. The email list grew to 180,000 addresses over time, enabling plug-and-play launches. [L0307.009]

Continuity and subscriptions

Subscription principles

  • Build subscription and recurring offers so revenue continues after the first purchase, instead of depending only on one-time sales. [L0914.012]
  • Subscriptions turn one-time buyers into repeat buyers by creating recurring purchases. They are a starting point for raising customer lifetime value, not a complete strategy on their own. [L1232.003]
  • Subscription options such as monthly, every two months or every three months can be offered next to a more expensive one-time purchase. This pushes repeat orders and raises customer lifetime value. [L1131.008]
  • Upsells, cross-sells, win-back messages and customer lifetime value are essential when a store only breaks even. Raising product prices where possible is also a key lever for creating real profit. [L1215.031]

Subscribe and save: setup

  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): A subscribe-and-save option was shown next to the one-time price, making the saving visible: one-time $12 versus $10 with subscribe and save 17%. The same store labeled a pack SAVE 17% and offered a Travel Sample Pack, a Build Your Pack option and a seasonal collection. [L0409.016]
  • Subscribe and Save offer on the product page: 3, 6 or 9 units per delivery, with the 6-unit option preselected as the default. Example: six bars at $42 against $8 per single bar, a saving of about 12.5% on a six-pack. [L0524.023]
  • Set the subscription to repeat every three months on the product page, and make the subscription the default choice. This makes the repeat purchase the easy path. [L0524.024]
  • Show the subscription saving as an explicit currency amount next to the quantity selector, for example "save £5 every month". A concrete monthly figure is easier to grasp than a percentage and makes the subscription look like a clear deal. [L0919.038]
  • On subscription pack pages, a competitor placed a "Flexible delivery schedule" note near the add-to-cart button. A visible note like this tells the customer that recurring deliveries can be changed, which makes the recurring offer easier to accept before checkout. [L1164.056]
  • Subscribe-and-save offers are common in competitor listings and can be used to show a lower effective price per period. Compare subscription and one-time options together when you decide whether to offer a subscription. [L1167.014]

Fit: which products suit subscriptions

  • Subscribe-and-save only fits products that customers reorder regularly, such as daily supplements (e.g. AG1 / Athletic Greens). Avoid it for products with no repeat purchase need, because it does not suit most customers. [L1404.024]
  • Subscribe-and-save only fits products that customers reorder regularly, such as daily supplements. A related retainer model gives subscribers ongoing services or discounts over time, similar to a membership, and suits products with a steady repeat need. [L1404.036]
  • Seasonal or recurring packages of consumables and recipes, shipped on a schedule and linked to holidays and sporting events, suit niches where customers need regular refills. Build the subscription only after the content audience is already engaged. [L0487.051]
  • A physical subscription can be a monthly calendar sold only to US buyers, with a new design on each month's page that the buyer can hang up. The planned price was about $19 to $20 per month. Offering it after a first set purchase turns one-time buyers into recurring revenue. [L1153.080]

Memberships and exclusive programs

  • Membership example: an exclusive membership for $129 gives 25% off every order, 10% cash back, free shipping and a free sample pack, so the total stacked value looks far above the fee. [L0919.024]
  • Exclusive memberships and loyalty programs: customers join, sometimes for a fee, and receive exclusive deals in return. This is another offer model to consider alongside the others. [L1404.029]
  • Exclusive rewards, such as cashback, early access to special products or other member benefits, are used to bring customers back for more purchases. They reward repeat buying rather than the first order. [L1232.005]

Gift cards

Gift card as a front-end or upsell product

  • Sell gift cards at $5 each during Q4; production cost is about $0.50. The margin is 90%, making this one of the highest-profit SKUs during holiday sales. [L0309.009]
  • Gift cards were offered on the store but not pushed, and sales from them were weak, so they did not deliver meaningful extra revenue. Focus on discounts and bundles first. [L0519.035]
  • Gift card upsells work in two ways: they lift the average order value on the current purchase, and they make a second order more likely because the customer holds a credit to use later. [L1413.010]

Discounted post-purchase gift cards

  • Post-purchase gift cards can be sold as a separate product in the thank-you email, for example a $50 card sold for $20 to $40. The customer gets a saving and is likely to return, while the store's extra margin on the second order covers the discount on the card. [L0605.030]
  • Product margins should be at least about 20% so that a discounted gift card or upsell does not put the store at a loss. Check this margin before offering gift cards. [L0605.031]
  • A discounted gift card gives the customer a saving equal to the card's face value minus the price paid. For example, $50 for $20 saves $30, and $50 for $40 saves $10. The store receives the price paid up front, so the margin on the products the customer later buys must cover that discount. Check that the product margin is at least about 20% before offering a larger discount. [L0605.053]

When each applies

  • Use the $5 Q4 card where the margin is near 90% and gift cards are actively promoted during holiday sales. [L0309.009, L0519.035]
  • Where gift cards are not being pushed, fix promotion first, because unpromoted cards produced weak sales; focus on discounts and bundles first. [L0519.035]
  • Use discounted post-purchase cards only when the product margin is at least about 20%. [L0605.030, L0605.031, L0605.053]

Accounting

  • Older (Source A, 2021): A spend-and-get gift card promotion needs an accounting check. Ask a CPA how unredeemed gift cards are reported, because they can inflate revenue in the next year if not handled correctly. [L1090.080]

Post-purchase offers

  • Offer existing customers an extra 10-20% off other products in the thank-you email. Because the acquisition cost was already paid, purchases by the same customer add profit with no extra acquisition cost. [L0605.032]
  • Choose the size of a post-purchase reward based on what the margin can afford, whether a dollar amount such as $15 off or a percentage such as 15% off. The reward is for the next order, so it should not erase the profit on the current one. [L0476.042]
  • After a first purchase you have the buyer's address, phone number and email, so you can follow up with direct mail or a phone call to offer a related product. Winning the first sale is usually the hardest step, and later sales to existing buyers are easier. [L0643.067]
  • A supplement seller sold a protein shaker on Amazon, then noticed that buyers wanted a way to carry their meals. The seller released a meal preparation box and sold it to the existing customer database instead of on Amazon. A meal carry bag that also fits the shaker followed later and was sold to the same buyers, so each new product built on the first purchase. [L0643.068]
  • Promotions to existing audiences generate sales without new traffic, which adds value to the business. Run them consistently once sales and remarketing audiences are growing, not as an early-stage tactic. [L1205.027]
  • Target a bundle offer at leads who viewed the product one, two or three months earlier and have not bought. Their standard remarketing has already run its course, and a new package gives them a reason to return. [L1205.029]
  • Flexible bundles help customers discover a new favorite product and can raise average order value. Bundles work best when they are easy to change and are presented as a way to find products the customer will reorder. [L1232.004]

Retroactive offers

  • Starting point: a free digital guide was given away with the product, and some buyers paid 19.95 USD for it. Later, previous buyers who had skipped the add-on were offered the guide free with a "you missed it" message. Reviews from these buyers were five stars. [L0773.064]
  • Retroactive value offer: after a free add-on has been tested at a small price, past customers who skipped it can be offered the same item for free. This recovers sales from people who did not buy the first time and builds goodwill. Example: a free sizing chart offered to customers who ordered a month earlier. Example: a free care guide sent to buyers who ignored the upsell at checkout. [L0773.065]

Referral programs

  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): Create an automated referral program with associated emails. Depending on your email platform, the program can be linked to loyalty or operate separately. [L0203.010]
  • Referral reward structure: for each referral, the friend gets $10 off and the referrer gets $10 back. The reward is valid through five referrals, and the fifth referral earns a $75 coupon, which is roughly break-even when the product sells for $85. [L0790.025]
  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): Without a loyalty point system, a store can give a customer $100 off the next order for each successful referral. Alternatively, the store can pay $100 for each referral made. [L1234.011]
  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): A referral program can be tied in so that referrers earn bonuses. Not every customer will use it, but it adds extra value to the customer journey. [L1522.028]

When each applies

  • The $10 friend and $10 referrer structure with a five-referral cap fits a product sold at around $85, where the fifth-referral coupon stays near break-even. [L0790.025]
  • The flat $100 per referral model fits a store without a loyalty point system; it can be run as credit on the next order or as a payment. [L1234.011]

Loyalty and points programs

Program design (older source)

  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): Build an automated loyalty program using email marketing templates entered into your email system to reward repeat purchases and encourage customer retention. [L0203.009]
  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): Loyalty points should be given for more than purchases. Common earning actions are creating an account, joining the email list, leaving a product review, making an additional purchase, and referring a friend or family member. [L0956.002]
  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): Most loyalty programs are points-based, where customers redeem points for a fixed dollar amount off a purchase. The app handles earning and redemption automatically, so the store owner only needs to set the rules and values. [L0956.004]
  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): Reward actions that create value for the store, such as leaving reviews, not only purchases. A good review can earn points, which encourages more social proof at little cost. [L0956.013]
  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): Credit loyalty points already on a customer's first order, not only from the second purchase onward. This lets the customer see the program working immediately, and the first program message can report a balance they already hold. [L0956.015]
  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): A loyalty program can reward each purchase with store credit or with a points system that shoppers redeem for a discount. [L1522.028]

Economics

  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): A loyalty purchase carries no paid acquisition cost, so the normal cost per acquisition does not apply to it. When deciding how much reward to offer, add back the acquisition cost saved on a first-time sale, then check the result against margins. [L0956.005]
  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): Do not reduce the reward on each individual order to save money. Loyalty rewards are judged across the whole customer relationship, including referrals and reviews, so penny-pinching on single orders can cut the program's value. [L0956.012]
  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): A repeat order with almost no margin can still be worth it if the customer refers three new buyers, since each referred first order brings in full-price revenue. When judging a reward, compare its cost with the total revenue from the referrals and reviews it produces, not with the repeat order alone. [L0956.017]

When to build a rewards program

  • Older (Source A, 2022): A rewards or points program is worth considering only when customers buy several times per year. For products bought every two to five years, the program is rarely used. [L1092.056]
  • Older (Source A, 2022): A rewards program should only be built when repeat buying is frequent, because the value of points is forgotten between long purchase cycles. [L1092.103]

Repeat business and customer research

Customer research

  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): Customer research helps determine which upsells to offer, when to offer them, and how to generate repeat business from existing customers. [L0872.011]
  • Test each product with different buyer qualification offers before deciding a market will not work. Track which offers convert for each product. [L0523.089]

Survey rewards

  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): Offer a small reward for completing the survey, typically around $10 to $15. It does not need to be large, since the goal is to thank customers for their time. [L1052.009]
  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): If you run a loyalty program, reward survey completion with loyalty points, because points encourage a return purchase. Without a loyalty program, use a one-off discount code, a cash-back offer on the order, or an Amazon gift card. [L1052.010]
  • Older (Source B, 2021-22): Cash-back and gift card rewards do not keep the money in your store, since customers may spend them elsewhere. Rewards that work inside your shop, such as loyalty points that can be used on upsell offers, drive repeat sales. [L1052.011]

High-value repeat customers

  • Reward the best customers with a $50 coupon to encourage a fourth or fifth pair purchase; the discounted sale still carried about a 50% margin. Target this group as a VIP list, for example the top 700 customers who had bought four or more pairs. [L0790.026]
  • Hyperactive buyers, identified fast after purchase, are the highest-value segment of a buyer list and should receive several upsells and downsells while their need is strong. [L0523.062]
  • Keep subscriber and buyer lists separate, and market to each differently. [L0523.061]
