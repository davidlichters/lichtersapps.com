7.22 Promotion calendar, seasonal sales and Q4

Offer arsenal and monthly rotation

  • Build an offer arsenal: a list of at least four offers you can pull from whenever you need one. The list is the pool you rotate through over the year. [L0757.015]
  • Brainstorm ideas without pricing them first, then check each idea's cost and margin before scheduling it. Keep the list written down so you can reuse, adjust and pull from it each month. [L1204.023]
  • A monthly offer-brainstorming exercise can produce around 12 unique offers, more than the five needed for Q4. Keep a running list so you always have spares. [L1224.008, L1287.056]
  • Plan Q4 offers in this order:
    1. Brainstorm about 12 possible promotions. [L1285.081, L1223.008]
    2. Select five and assign one to each holiday. [L1285.081, L1223.008]
    3. Record the offer, eligibility and theme for each promotion in an offer calendar planned a year in advance. [L1285.081]
    4. For each promotion, check the historical best-performing email subject lines and ad headlines, ranked by opens, clicks and sales, and pick those that fit the brand. [L1223.008]
    5. Use the same message in the email subject lines and in the remarketing ad headlines. [L1223.008]
    6. Set up the email segment and the remarketing audiences, then launch. [L1223.008]
  • Promote one offer per month. Offers can be recycled and reused throughout the year. [L0757.024]
  • Run only one offer per month. Proven offers can be recycled every quarter or every six months, ideally with a new twist, but not repeated every month. [L1205.017]
  • Older advice (2020): Rotate offers monthly rather than repeating the same promotion. Two promotions in the same month, such as during November, are not too much. [L1088.054]
  • Older advice (2022): Rotate the type of promotion each month, such as a bundle, a fixed percentage off, or something else, instead of repeating one offer. [L1281.036]
  • Vary promotions across the year instead of repeating the same discount on each holiday. Choose the deal, such as a free gift, a free item, or a buy-one-get-one-free offer tied to a supplier deal, based on what the target customer wants. [L1076.071]
  • Monthly promotions usually run for three to five days, announced to the email list and on social media. [L1281.036]
  • Each month can follow a fixed theme. Example rotating offer calendar, one offer per month and reused across the year:

  • Four promotion types exist: monetary promotions, bundles, physical product bonuses and digital product bonuses. Each is used to turn existing leads who have not bought into customers. [L1205.004]

Offer types and gift structures

  • Percent-off offers are the simplest monthly promotion. Raise your usual coupon for a short window, for example from 5 percent to 10 percent off all orders. [L1204.026]
  • Fixed-amount offers work best on higher-priced products, for example save 50 dollars on all orders over 500 dollars. [L1204.026]
  • Free expedited shipping works well in November and December, when customers worry about delivery before the holidays. Promote a cutoff time, for example 5 p.m. for same-day or 24-hour dispatch, or a supplier ship-by date about a week before Christmas. It can be run for only part of the catalog if the supplier does not offer it store-wide. [L1204.029]
  • Buy-one-get-one-free offers work especially well in November around Black Friday and Cyber Monday. Shoppers buy extra units as gifts, or buy two and keep one and give one away. [L1205.007]
  • A bundle promotion can run for roughly one week within a chosen month, not the whole month. Example: a golf bag sold at $400 with a $100 carrier upgrade. A single $500 bundle that adds golf balls, a glove and a cleaning kit is a plausible one-week offer. [L1205.028]
  • A bundle can include a free gift and a free ebook download, shown clearly in the ad so the customer sees the deal immediately. [L0692.054]
  • Choice offer: the customer picks one of two free gifts. It keeps a product offer fresh and can rescue results when the old offer is worn out. Examples: "Pick your free gift: a travel pouch or a bottle of cleaner with any order." and "Choose one free bonus: an extra case or a matching cable with your purchase." [L0950.043]
  • Seasonal relabelling: keep the same discount and shipping offer, but rename it for the season, such as Black Friday or Cyber Monday, to make it look new. Examples: 10% off plus free shipping for Black Friday; 10% off plus free shipping for Cyber Monday week. [L1085.063] Older advice (2018).
  • Tie promotions to current events, holidays or seasonal moments where possible. The more timely and relevant the promotion, the more interest and urgency it creates and the more likely older leads will convert. [L1205.023]
  • Any seasonal reason can justify a discount event, including Halloween, a spring sale or a holiday. Short, recurring sales give buyers a reason to act now. Keep the offer clear and time-limited so the reason to buy stays believable. [L1363.058]
  • Non-discount promotions can compete with large retailers, for example free white glove shipping, free expedited delivery for holiday timing, a raffle entry, or a bonus gift valued at around 100 dollars. [L1113.071] Older advice (2022).
  • Gift-with-purchase offers work during the holidays because the buyer can keep the gift or give it to someone else. The perceived value of the gift is often higher than its cost to the store. [L0467.060] Older advice (2019).

Gift-with-purchase formula for expensive products (older advice, 2019)

  • Pattern: a percentage discount combined with a free gift above a spend threshold.
  • Formula: Save [X]% and get a free [gift] on orders over $[threshold].
  • Example 1: Save 10% and get a free gift box on orders over $500.
  • Example 2: Save 15% and get a free set of RGB lights on orders over $1,500.
  • It works because the discount keeps the price appealing, the gift adds value the buyer can also give to someone else, and the threshold raises the average order. [L0467.058]
  • Case: a store with high-margin products ran a holiday promotion giving a discount plus a free gift. The gifts included an $80 item and a set of RGB lights costing the store about $50. The RGB lights offer was designed to push orders toward roughly $2,000 per customer. [L0467.059]

Free gift threshold (older advice, 2018)

  • Pattern: a percentage discount plus a free gift on orders above a set amount, used only in peak sales periods by stores that already know their numbers.
  • The gift should be worth about $50 to $100 and be something the buyer can keep or give away.
  • Example 1: 10% off plus a free $80 gadget on orders over $1,000.
  • Example 2: 15% off plus a free $60 accessory on orders over $500. [L1085.064]
  • Use it only where the store knows its average order value and margin, and only around holidays and sale days, not all year. Set the threshold above the average order value so the gift is affordable. [L1085.065]
  • Gift-friendly offers can sell products that would not normally be bought as gifts. During holiday shopping, buyers are already choosing presents for family, so a free gift removes one decision and pushes them toward your store. [L1085.066]
  • Current figures (2024): A low-cost gift unrelated to the product, sent to buyers before the holiday deadline, can drive sales when inventory is limited. The gift must be worth more to the customer than its cost of about $80 shipped. [L1285.082]

Recycling offers

  • Recycle proven offers two to three times a year, or every quarter or six months, rather than always creating new ones. Repeat the offers that keep converting with the audience. [L1204.042]
  • Sources differ on frequency. [L1204.042] says two to three times a year; [L1205.017] says every quarter or every six months, with a new twist; [L1404.032] says cycle offer types at most about once a year, and a BOGO that performed strongly at launch can fail on a repeat run. Use the once-a-year limit for offer types that have already faded.
  • Over 90% of visitors to a dropship store are first-time visitors who came from a paid ad for a specific product. Because the audience is new each time, the same offer can be recycled very frequently without losing effect. [L1085.062] Older advice (2018).
  • An offer that was used before can still work if it is packaged as a Black Friday or Cyber Monday deal. [L1223.013]
  • Vary offer types and cycle them at most about once a year. [L1404.032]
  • Repeated offers stop converting after a few weeks. Use a different offer for each promotion instead of the same discount, such as 5% off. [L1223.007]
  • Repeating a 5 percent offer five times gets fewer responses because customers have already seen it. [L1285.080]
  • Repeating the same discount on each promotion trains the audience to ignore it. Plan the offers in advance so none is recycled within the same quarter. [L1224.007]
  • A holiday promotion using a repeated discount with a holiday name, such as 10 percent off with a holiday name, loses impact after the first use. [L1287.056]
  • Each promotion in the quarter needs its own unique offer. Thanksgiving has no separate offer when it runs together with Black Friday. [L1222.003]
  • Keep the offer you already run, such as free shipping, 5% off or 10% off, but change the coupon code to a holiday-related word. Announce the holiday promotion on Facebook and send the same promotion to your email list. Holidays are a sales opportunity and not a reason to pause making money. [L0357.068] Older advice (2019).

Audiences for promotions

  • A promotion works best when it is shown to a warm audience that has engaged for months, because the goodwill makes the offer feel natural. A buy-one-get-one or free-add-on promotion timed to a holiday converts far better in front of that group than cold. Examples in the same pattern: "Order a set of two candles this week and get a third free for the holidays" and "Buy the starter brewing kit now and get the seasonal ingredient pack free." [L0487.050]
  • Time product offers to promotions and seasonal peaks such as summer, Black Friday and Christmas. Keep building the audience between them. Regular content can mention products with a link in the description without being a sales video. [L0487.048]
  • Wait before making offers to a new audience so it can grow and trust the brand. The example suggested about six weeks. Making offers right away is possible if resources exist to sell and grow at the same time. [L0487.049]
  • Promotions to move less popular products or seasonal items work best when sent to warm audiences such as your email list and remarketing audiences of past visitors. New visitors from cold traffic usually buy what they searched for, regardless of promotion. [L0801.052] Older advice (2021).
  • Example warm audience size: around 2,000 people on your email list and around 10,000 people in a remarketing audience. Push promotions to those groups first rather than to new visitors. [L0801.053] Older advice (2021).
  • Run a discount or flash sale every month, targeted at one segment of the email list. Revenue was higher in every month with a significant discount, so brands that fear discounting for premium image should test it. Online competition for attention and inbox space makes a sale a strong tool. [L0334.018]
  • Sales event opt-ins can stop visitors from buying immediately, because they wait for the event. Run them close to the event rather than too far in advance to limit that effect. [L0571.023] Older advice (2021-22, Source B).
  • Run giveaways before Black Friday. This builds early interest at low CPM, and the engaged audience can then be converted during Black Friday. [L0797.021]
  • Source conflict on channel priority: for Black Friday and Cyber Monday in the first month of a new store with a small email list, storefront promotions matter more than email campaigns. Make the store's promotions match the holiday, and add them once the first suppliers are live. [L1116.061] Warm-audience targeting in [L0801.052, L0801.053] applies to older stock and promotions aimed at existing buyers.
  • Promotions aimed at the store's existing buyers did not move slower products in one 2021 case. Results were mediocre, and select brands on sale up to 20 percent off sold few units, while regular best sellers sold more. [L0801.050] Older advice (2021).
  • Case, 2021: a store ran a tiered Black Friday promotion spending 400 dollars for 15 percent off, 800 dollars for 20 percent off and 1,200 dollars for 25 percent off, with average order value around 350 dollars. The next month without promotions still produced more sales than December. [L0801.050] Older advice (2021).

Measuring and tuning promotions

  • Store promotions perform differently from one another and affect monthly revenue, so plan a promotion calendar and track results per promotion. Use the best-performing promotions as a baseline for future months. [L0807.048]
  • Coupon codes on the store were valid on Wednesdays and Sundays, chosen because traffic is similar each day. The host did not split-test the days. [L0804.089]
  • Use coupon codes tied to the holiday season, such as Black Friday, Cyber Monday or Xmas. Promote them in email, on social media pinned posts and in cover images. [L1111.089] Older advice (2020).

Promotion duration and timing rules

  • Each offer promotion should run 3 to 5 days. Use the shorter end when quantity is limited, and the longer end when you need more hype or when the purchase is a large decision for the audience. [L0757.025]
  • Each promotion runs for 3 to 5 days, and the rest of the month the normal percentage discount applies. Example: a promotion may run from January 1 to January 5 only. [L1205.018]
  • Use shorter promotions when quantity is limited. Example: when a supplier allows only 20 units, the offer runs for three days or until the 20 units sell out, whichever comes first. [L1205.019]
  • Use a five-day promotion for big purchase decisions, high-ticket items or when more hype needs to be built. [L1205.020]
  • For larger purchases, send one pre-promotion email before the offer starts to tell the audience something big is coming and prepare them for it. [L0757.026]
  • Monthly promotions of 3 to 5 days can be run, tied to a holiday or event relevant to the niche. Promotions are typically used with promo codes rather than price changes. [L1094.078]
  • A promotion with a real deadline, such as a holiday, creates urgency. It can run for a single day or through a weekend, and it should be promoted with a sale announcement on the website and social media. [L0837.035]
  • A 3-day Halloween promotion example: October 29 to October 31, 2024, with 10% off the whole store using the coupon code "happyhalloween", and one promotional email each day of the sale window. [L1105.005]
  • A holiday promotion can be run as a short fixed window, such as Monday to Friday of one week, with a percentage-off discount code for all customers. Set the start and end dates before writing any promotional copy. [L1286.004]
  • Source conflict on length: a 3-to-5-day window is standard; a five-day window is for big-ticket items [L1205.020]; a week-long window is used for Black Friday [L1115.105, L1224.005]; a one-day window is used for Cyber Monday [L1224.004, L1287.055]; a 48-hour window is used for flash sales [L1400.002]. Each applies to its own case.
  • Weekday rule: end offers on a weekday, and use Friday for the more expensive offers. Tie the promotion to current events or holidays where you can. [L0757.027]
  • Plan promotions at least three months ahead, covering Halloween, Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year, with one specific offer per event. [L1113.069] Older advice (2022).
  • Holiday promotion routine, from 2019, applies when you have no unique offer ready: rename the existing discount code, and keep the same offer under a holiday code. [L0357.068] Older advice (2019).
  • A holiday promotion can still be worthwhile when planned only about one week ahead. In that case, a simple change such as renaming the existing discount code to a holiday word is enough, while a unique offer requires more lead time. [L0623.024]
  • A holiday promotion can be assembled, configured, announced to your email list and posted to social media in approximately one hour. A discount of 5% or 10% paired with a holiday-specific code (for example "MemorialDay" instead of a generic discount) has measurable impact even with a small audience; converting one or two additional sales justifies the effort. [L0294.008]

Holiday planning for the US calendar

  • U.S. holidays such as Memorial Day and the 4th of July, and monthly occasions, provide recurring promotional triggers throughout the year. [L0294.008]
  • A national holiday such as the 4th of July is a reason to run a special promotion that differs from everyday discounts. This applies only to stores that sell to customers in the US. [L0623.001]
  • If there is no time for a unique holiday offer, the fastest starting point is renaming the existing discount code to a holiday word such as "July 4th", "freedom" or "fireworks". Set that code to expire at the end of the promotion window. [L0623.002]
  • A holiday promotion in the lesson ran for five days, from Monday 1 July to Friday 5 July, with the code expiring at midnight on the final day. Normal promotion codes in the same business typically ended on Wednesday or Sunday. [L0623.003]
  • 4th of July promotion plan:
    1. Create a holiday-themed promotion, or rename the existing discount code to a holiday word such as "July 4th", "freedom" or "fireworks". [L0623.004]
    2. Set the code to expire at the end of the five-day window, Friday 5 July in the example. [L0623.004]
    3. Show the code on the store and promote it to new visitors coming from ads. [L0623.004]
    4. Send promotion emails to the existing email list on every day of the window, from Monday to Friday. [L0623.004]
    5. Run static holiday remarketing ads to older website visitor audiences. [L0623.004]
    6. Run dynamic product ads to recent visitors who have not bought, excluding recent customers. [L0623.004]
  • Start holiday promotions weeks early, since suppliers get busy, some close, and shipping carriers are backed up in the peak shipping period before Christmas. Holiday offers should state the gift benefit clearly, such as a Christmas gift or a holiday promotion. [L0467.057] Older advice (2019).
  • Holiday promotions were run on all dropshipping stores, so promotional calendars should be planned per store. [L1002.037] Older advice (2019).
  • Holiday-specific bundles can be promoted earlier than the holiday, for example during Black Friday or Cyber Monday, since many people shop for gifts early. [L1285.083]
  • Holiday bundles or special products do not need to wait until the holiday itself to be promoted. Testing them earlier, for example during Black Friday or Cyber Monday, is worth doing because many people shop for Christmas during those events. [L1223.015]

Q4 planning

  • Prepare five unique offers for the Q4 promotion season. Because Thanksgiving runs inside the Black Friday promotion, the five offers cover Halloween, Black Friday with Thanksgiving, Cyber Monday, Christmas and New Year. [L1221.002]
  • Run five unique offers across five promotions in Q4 instead of repeating the same discount. [L1285.080]
  • Use five unique offers for the five holiday promotions instead of repeating the same discount, such as 5% off, because repeated offers stop converting after a few weeks. Each offer should give customers a different reason to buy now. [L1223.007]
  • Plan a promotion for each Q4 event: Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Christmas and New Year. [L0626.023]
  • Create the promotional images and promotional email copy for each event before it starts. [L0626.023]
  • If a store has no Black Friday promotion ready, it is getting late, but it is still possible to plan and launch one before the event. [L0626.023]
  • Existing store offers, such as a site-wide discount code or free premium white-glove shipping, can be the basis for one of the five holiday promotions. Each promotion still needs a different offer, not the same discount repeated. [L1223.033]
  • Plan at least the next three months covering the holidays. To prepare holiday promotions:
    1. List every holiday that can carry a promotion. [L1113.070]
    2. Choose one offer for each event. [L1113.070]
    3. Create the Klaviyo emails for each promotion. [L1113.070]
    4. Create the images for Facebook remarketing ads. [L1113.070]
    5. Create the images for Google display ads. [L1113.070] [L1113.070] Older advice (2022).
  • Set the start and end dates of the promotion before writing any promotional copy. [L1286.004]
  • Use a single discount code applied to all customers, such as 10% off, combined with a delivery promise before a holiday deadline. Only promise a delivery date you can actually meet with your supplier and shipping times. [L0620.003]
  • Q4 is estimated to add about 30 percent to monthly revenue. The actual gain scales with the current revenue level. [L1116.041]
  • Black Friday and Cyber Monday sales rise even without specific promotions. Once the first suppliers are live, add holiday-relevant promotions on the store to boost sales further. [L1116.040]
  • Change the sale reason every three to four days, following a calendar: early November sale, 11.11, pre-Black Friday, Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Christmas and end-of-year sale. [L1194.040]
  • A monthly offer-brainstorming exercise can produce around 12 unique offers. Keep a running list so you always have spares. [L1224.008]

Halloween

  • A 3-day Halloween promotion example: October 29 to October 31, 2024, with 10% off the whole store using the coupon code "happyhalloween", and one promotional email each day of the sale window. [L1105.005]

Black Friday and Thanksgiving

  • Black Friday and Cyber Monday sales start before Black Friday and end at midnight on Cyber Monday. Long promotions tend to perform well, and one planned campaign included seven emails. [L1090.078] Older advice (2021).
  • Start Black Friday promotions and ads at least one day before Black Friday. [L1092.054] Older advice (2022).
  • Start the Black Friday sale on the Monday before Thanksgiving and run it through Black Friday, rather than extending it after the event. Longer sale windows produced more sales in this approach. [L1285.102, L1224.005]
  • Black Friday promotions perform better the longer they run, so start the sale before Thanksgiving rather than extending it after Black Friday. A five-day email run from Monday before Thanksgiving through Black Friday covers the Thanksgiving shopping window. [L1223.011]
  • A Black Friday promotion can run as a week-long event that includes Thanksgiving Day as one of its days, rather than as a single day. [L1115.105]
  • Combine Thanksgiving and Black Friday into one roughly week-long promotion instead of a month-long Black Friday event. Start it on the Monday before Thanksgiving and end it on Black Friday. [L1224.005]
  • Thanksgiving is a US-specific holiday, so it is folded into the Black Friday promotion instead of getting its own campaign. The Thanksgiving-day email is themed around the holiday and repeats the discount code. [L1223.012]
  • For Black Friday and Cyber Monday, use aggressive discounts. Discounts of 60 to 70 percent perform best; discounts above 80 percent tend to be seen as too good to be true and convert worse. You may need to raise the product price beforehand so the discount still leaves a margin you can afford. [L0387.008]
  • Escalating discount ladder for an event: start one month before the event with 20% off, then increase weekly to 30%, 40%, 50% and 60%. Raise prices incrementally as discounts increase to maintain margin and justify the perceived value of the offer. [L0309.001]
  • Run your main event (Black Friday or Christmas) with increasing offer pressure. [L0309.004]
  • Black Friday pricing on Google Shopping depends on the supplier agreements. Without a MAP restriction, lower the price in Shopify so the lower price shows in Google Shopping. [L1092.052] Older advice (2022).
  • A bundle offer can run across the Black Friday to Cyber Monday window on the store, while Google Shopping ads stay unchanged. Promote the bundle on-site and through email and remarketing rather than as a Shopping promotion. [L1088.093] Older advice (2020).
  • Pre-sale momentum: build it with a smaller offer that grows toward Black Friday, rather than a long sale that runs for weeks. Keep the largest offer for Black Friday itself. [L1225.005]
  • A 24-hour sale extension is a common Black Friday tactic, especially for brands that rely on acquiring new customers, but it weakens urgency if the messaging is careless. [L1225.001]
  • Black Friday and Cyber Monday are a sales dip-and-peak cycle. A sales dip before Black Friday and again in January and February is a normal ebb and flow for e-commerce, so plan cash flow around it rather than reacting with panic. [L1225.013]
  • Free express shipping is used near shipping cut-off dates, for example before Black Friday, to let customers receive orders earlier and squeeze a few extra days of sales. [L1404.022]
  • Source note on Black Friday timing: the minimum for ads is one day before (2022) [L1092.054]; the planned start is the Monday before Thanksgiving [L1224.005, L1285.102]. Both apply, with the Monday start as the plan.

Cyber Monday

  • Cyber Monday works as a separate promotion just two days after Black Friday in the lesson's wording, because shoppers actively look for deals and respond to repackaged offers. The 2024 calendar places it on Monday 2 December. [L1223.013, L1223.009]
  • The 2025 calendar runs Cyber Monday on Monday 1 December as a one-day promotion, with 2 emails (one in the morning and one in the evening) and remarketing all day. [L1224.004, L1287.055]
  • Cyber Monday 2024: early morning 2 December and about 17:00 on 2 December, two emails, one day only. [L1223.009]

Christmas planning

  • Christmas promotions run before Christmas rather than on Christmas Day, with a message that the buy-by date for delivery before the holiday is closing. Allowing about two weeks for delivery, the emails go out in early-to-mid December. [L1223.014]
  • The target is gift buyers and customers who want high-ticket or bulky items, such as furniture or appliances, in time for the holiday. [L1223.014]
  • The Christmas offer message is "order now to get your product before Christmas". It drives a large spike, and it only works if suppliers can deliver within about two weeks. Confirm delivery times first. [L1224.009]
  • The Christmas promotion runs early in December because shoppers have usually finished their spending by Christmas and are less active online then. The main message is to order now to receive the product before Christmas, which drives a sharp spike in sales. [L1287.058]
  • Example Christmas offer: discount code "xmas" gives 10 percent off, combined with a guarantee that the product will be delivered before Christmas. This only works if the supplier can deliver within about two weeks. [L1287.059]
  • The 2025 September call paired a 10% off code with a guarantee that orders arrive before Christmas Day. [L1286.004]
  • The 2024 Christmas calendar uses emails on Monday 9, Wednesday 11 and Friday 13 December, with the message as the last order date before Christmas. [L1223.009]
  • The 2025 Christmas calendar runs Monday 8 December to Friday 12 December, with 3 emails on 8, 10 and 12 December. [L1224.004, L1287.055]
  • Plan a Christmas sale for the second week of December so products can be delivered before the holiday. [L1090.079] Older advice (2021).
  • Christmas offers can be smaller than Black Friday because most of the buying has already been done. [L1225.006]
  • For a gift product, frame Christmas offers as gifting rather than as a buy-one-get-one deal. Use an experiential gift angle for buyers purchasing for a partner or themselves. [L1225.014]
  • Example Christmas structure: free express shipping above a spend threshold plus around 20% off site-wide, with an added gift to keep the momentum. This should be lower than the Black Friday deal so the Black Friday buyers are not upset. [L1225.016]
  • Gift wrapping: in Q4, offer free gift wrapping on high-tier bundles for giftable products to increase appeal to customers buying gifts for others. [L0232.009]
  • Christmas packaging upsell: offer a simple checkbox on the checkout cart for "Add holiday gift packaging". It costs $0.50 to produce and sells for $3-4, adding $3-4 profit per order with no friction if presented as a non-intrusive option. [L0309.008]
  • Gift-friendly holiday bundle example: a holiday offer can bundle the main product with a free gift and a free ebook download. [L0692.054]
  • Source note on gift wrap and packaging: free gift wrapping on high-tier bundles [L0232.009] and a paid packaging checkbox at $3-4 [L0309.008] are both valid; the first is a bundle benefit, the second an add-on.

Holiday shipping cut-offs

  • Stop selling Christmas-delivery products by December 10-12 to guarantee shipping arrives before the holiday. Cutoff dates prevent logistics bottlenecks in late December when carrier capacity is exhausted and packages routinely arrive after December 25. [L0309.007]
  • Non-seasonal products (jewelry, gadgets, apparel, lifestyle items) can continue selling past December 10-12 for Christmas gifts if you clearly state on the order page that Christmas delivery is not guaranteed. Manage customer expectations through follow-up emails. [L0309.043]
  • Seasonal products (Christmas ornaments, holiday decorations) must stop by December 12 to prevent chargebacks. Organic channels with higher margins tolerate late-delivery complaints better than paid ads with lower margins. [L0309.043]
  • Procedure for the cutoff:
    1. Add a clear cutoff date to your website and order confirmation page stating "Orders placed after [date] cannot be guaranteed Christmas delivery." [L0309.012]
    2. Include a 24-hour cancellation window on the confirmation email for customers who miss the cutoff. [L0309.012]
    3. Update your refund policy to reflect that late-placed orders are ineligible for refunds due to shipping timing beyond your control. [L0309.012]
  • Holiday deadline: for the 2025 calendar, Christmas runs 8 to 12 December, within the 10-12 December cutoff range. [L0309.007, L1224.004]
  • Expedited shipping offers: promote a cutoff time or a supplier ship-by date about a week before Christmas. [L1204.029]

New Year

  • Plan a New Year sale running from December 31 into January 1 as an end-of-year promotion. [L1090.079] Older advice (2021).
  • Avoid ending a promotion on New Year's Day, when buyers are usually least active. [L1224.006]
  • Promotions should end on a Friday, because people are typically paid on Friday and spend more then. Avoid ending a promotion on New Year's Day, when many shoppers are not buying. [L1224.006, L1287.057]
  • The 2024 calendar runs New Year from Monday 30 December with Wednesday 1 January and Friday 3 January emails, closing Friday 3 January. [L1223.009]
  • The 2025 calendar runs New Year from 31 December, ending on Friday 2 January 2026 (see calendar version A, inferred). [L1224.004]
  • The 2025 calendar version B runs New Year from 31 December to Friday 2 January. [L1287.055]
  • Source conflict: the 2021 advice ends the New Year sale on January 1 [L1090.079]; the 2025 rule avoids ending on New Year's Day [L1224.006, L1287.057]. The later rule applies for new plans.

Closing the Friday rule for promotions

  • Close promotions on a Friday whenever possible, because people are paid on Fridays and will otherwise ask for more time. If a promotion must end on Wednesday or Thursday, send an extra email on the Friday after with the same offer extended by one day. [L1223.010]
  • Promotions should end on a Friday because people are typically paid on Friday and spend more then. [L1224.006]

Flash sales

  • Flash sales are time-limited promotions with a strong offer, such as a high-ticket item at a deep discount, a product bundle, or a trending seasonal product. Pairing the time limit with limited stock creates urgency and triggers fear of missing out. [L1399.002]
  • Flash sale pattern: a promotion limited to a fixed time window, built around a high-value offer such as a high-ticket item at a deep discount, a product bundle, or a trending seasonal product. Pairing the time limit with limited stock creates urgency and taps into fear of missing out (FOMO). Example: a 48-hour sale on a premium bundle with only 50 units available. [L1400.002]
  • Pair flash sales with a fixed window: a 48-hour window on a premium bundle with 50 units, or a three-day window when quantity is limited. [L1400.002, L1205.019]
  • A monthly flash sale aimed at one email segment raises revenue in each month with a significant discount. [L0334.018]

End-of-season clearance

  • End-of-season discounts clear stock that needs to leave quickly. Shoppers also tend to wait for a sale before buying big-ticket items to get the largest savings. [L1399.011]
  • End-of-season discounts clear out stock that needs to leave quickly. Shoppers also tend to delay purchases of big-ticket items until a sale, so a seasonal discount captures that demand. [L1400.008]
  • Mystery box: a "trust us" bundle used to clear unsold or weaker stock. It only works if the brand keeps enough real quality in the box, otherwise the offer produces many returns and complaints. Because it can usually be run only once, the box can be a limited edition created for that one promotion. [L1404.020]
  • A brand that runs frequent sales can keep its image by building a strong brand experience around promotions, including the ordering experience and merchandising, planned in advance so that the discounting is part of the brand. [L1404.021]
  • Clearance pricing for an extension: 70% off, with the message "Almost out of stock: Black Friday stock still available". [L0309.004]
  • Sell out each collection, then move on to the next collection, rather than discounting constantly. [L1438.033]

Extending a promotion

  • Continue the promotion for two weeks after the event concludes. In the extension period, message as "Almost out of stock: Black Friday stock still available" and offer 70% off to clear inventory. [L0309.004]
  • A 24-hour sale extension is a common Black Friday tactic. It weakens urgency if the messaging is careless. Extend only the same offer under the same terms, because adding deeper discounts or a new offer for late buyers beyond early loyal customers creates trouble. [L1225.001]
  • Only change or extend an offer if the team can execute it fully, since a new offer means a website redo, updated email flows and new ad creatives. Extending a sale without proper execution hurts more than it helps, while a long sale period risks offer fatigue. [L1225.003]
  • If you extend the sale or change the offer after Black Friday, make it exclusive to BFCM so the Black Friday offer keeps its value. [L1225.006]
  • Extension email: if an event ends on Thursday or Wednesday, send a Friday email with the same offer extended by one day. [L1223.010]
  • The 2024 Halloween calendar uses a Friday extension email on 1 November, which is also the close. [L1223.009]
  • Source conflict: the extension of the 2024 Halloween and Black Friday sales is one day in the Friday rule [L1223.010]; the two-week extension at 70% off is a clearance tactic [L0309.004]. Use the one-day extension for offers still in their promotion, and the two-week clearance only for stock to be cleared.

Closing a promotion

  • Close an expired offer:
    1. Swap the expired offer for your default offer. [L0757.039]
    2. Revert every change made for the offer: remove it from the notification bar and the exit intent overlay. [L0757.039]
    3. Revert the offer-dependent changes: custom product pages, content pages, edited product pages, and the Bold Sales Motivator threshold display. [L0757.039]
  • Leaving an expired offer on the site after it ends, including banners, overlays, pages and progress trackers, sends shoppers to a dead promotion. Always swap in the default offer and undo every promotion change. [L0757.040]
  • Set the code to expire at the end of the promotion window so it stays time-bound and relevant to the holiday. [L0623.002]
  • Always keep a default offer, such as 5 or 10 percent off, live after a promotion ends. People click promotional emails weeks later, and they should still find something to buy at a fair price. [L1204.053]
  • Promotions should end on weekdays, with Friday used for the more expensive offers. [L0757.027]
  • Use a sell-out close for limited stock: the offer runs for three days or until the 20 units sell out, whichever comes first. [L1205.019]

Default offer after promotions

  • Keep a default offer, such as 5 or 10 percent off, live after every promotion ends. [L1204.053]
  • When an offer expires, swap in the default offer and undo every promotion change on the site. [L0757.039, L0757.040]

Discount depth

  • Escalate by week before a large event: 20%, then 30%, 40%, 50%, 60%. [L0309.001]
  • For Black Friday and Cyber Monday: 60-70% performs best; above 80% converts worse. [L0387.008]
  • For monthly promotions: raise the usual coupon for a short window, for example 5% to 10% off all orders. [L1204.026]
  • A repeated 5 percent offer loses responses after several uses. [L1285.080]
  • Source conflict: 60-70% is the best range for Black Friday and Cyber Monday [L0387.008]; the 70% clearance is for stock after the event [L0309.004]; the monthly rule is a 5-10% coupon [L1204.026]. Each figure applies to its own promotion type.

Further details

Run holiday promotions each month, with ad campaigns built for the biggest dates:

  1. Plan a promotion for each month, with the largest ones around the holiday season.
  2. Build ad campaigns for the specific promotion, such as Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Christmas or New Year.
  3. Create the promotion in Google Shopping promotions where available. [L0470.064]

Running a bundle promotion that is not a Google Shopping promotion:

  1. Leave the Google Shopping ads unchanged for the promotion period.
  2. Show a notification bar on the website that promotes the bundle during the promotion window.
  3. Use an exit intent overlay that announces the bundle price and links to the bundle page.
  4. Email the store's list about the bundle.
  5. Run remarketing ads on Facebook and Google to visitors who have not yet purchased. [L1088.053]
