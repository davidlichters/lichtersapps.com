7.13 Designing bundles and quantity-break ladders

Bundle types and definitions

  • A bundle combines more than one product into one offer, such as "buy this and this item and get an extra 20% off." A bundle that contains several units of the same product is a quantity break, such as "buy one and get 20% off, buy two and get 30% off." [L0434.010, L0435.012]
  • Bundle pricing sells two or more units for less than the sum of the single prices, so the customer gets an automatic saving and average order value (AOV) rises. The bundle price must still leave a margin that covers all costs. [L0436.016]
  • Quantity-break offers raise AOV because customers who buy more get a bigger discount, and the store earns more per order. [L0635.027]
  • Automatic quantity discounts raise both AOV and conversion rate because the total looks lower for multiple units. A short urgency line such as "only 5 left in stock" can strengthen the effect. [L0773.018]
  • Bulk pricing or a discounted second unit raises AOV. Buyers who want more of the same product are easier to convert than new buyers. [L0378.030]
  • Bundles raise AOV because a customer who buys two items plus a free third spends more per order than someone buying one item. Use bundles to make the store more profitable per visitor, not only to win the first sale. [L0562.008]
  • Bundles and volume discounts increase the number of items per order. A free-gift option can reduce the perceived price without a full discount. Test which offer type fits the margin of your products. [L0476.031]

Bundle types in use

Quantity breaks and buy-X-get-Y

  • Buy two, get a percentage off the second unit or the whole order: buy two and get an extra 30% off raises AOV and margin per order, and suits customers who want more units of the same item. [L0434.012, L0435.011]
  • Buy one get one free (BOGO): strong with customers, because people respond to a perceived free item. [L0440.045]
  • Buy two, get the third free: a typical bundle offer; choose the version by testing which sells best while still leaving a good margin. [L0562.007]
  • Buy two, get the third free, for products customers already buy in stores: buy two get a third free raises order size and margin; buy two with free shipping also raises both. [L1176.027]
  • Buy any two, get the third 50% off: encourages multiple items per order while keeping per-unit profitability. [L0232.006]
  • Buy-X-get-Y offers: customers buy more items to qualify for the free item. They work best when the product is logically bought in multiples, such as home decor. [L0650.037]
  • Tiered BOGO-style ladders: Buy 1 Get 1 Free, Buy 2 Get 1 Free, Buy 3 Get 2 Free, with the savings amount shown under each tier. Keep the visual layout simple so customers are not overwhelmed. [L0232.004]
  • Common offers for low-priced products: buy two get one free and buy three get two free. A store running several of these is usually testing which quantity customers accept. [L1172.024]
  • Buy two, buy three or buy four and get one free: works well for small, cheap products. Test several and keep the one that suits the margin. [L1171.025]
  • Buy-two offer with a second item at a percentage off: a gifting variant, used with free US shipping. [L1225.019]
  • Buy one at 20% off, with a second item at an extra 15% off: one of the offer structures that work on TikTok. [L1183.018]
  • Buy one and get the second at 50% off, with free shipping above a threshold: a bundle structure, with free shipping above a threshold such as 55 dollars. [L0789.028]
  • Buy two get 50% off or 30% off: tested structures among several others (see the list below). [L0650.034]

Complementary bundles

  • A complementary bundle pairs two different products that solve a related problem. Replace a second identical item with a complementary product, such as a sleep-related item with pyjamas or an organiser with a wallet, because a second identical item has little value to a buyer who needs only one. [L1131.013]
  • Complementary items work better than pushing customers to buy more of the same product to reach a free-shipping threshold. [L1172.026]
  • Complementary bundles work well when the add-on solves a related need and is cheap to ship. Shipping both items in one parcel is cheaper than two separate orders to the same address. [L1345.022]
  • Complementary-product bundles can convert better than standard discounts. A bundle with a complementary item made with the Bundle Bear app roughly doubled conversion rate compared with buy 2 get 20% off, with a 40 to 50 percent bundle take rate and about $40 extra profit per order. [L0650.038]
  • Complementary items are added when customers rarely buy more than one of the same product. [L0440.045]
  • Offer a complementary item when the offer complements the solution to the customer's problem or makes the product more attractive. For example, a candle seller can give two free when a customer buys four. [L0549.020]
  • For a cheap product that complements a main product, test buy two get two free instead of a small percentage discount, since this can raise AOV. [L0549.022]
  • Pair a main product with a part it needs from the same list, such as Fish Tanks with Aquarium Stands, Koi Ponds & Filtration Systems, and Above Ground Pools with Pool Filtration Systems and Pool Vacuums. Selling them together raises order value and saves the buyer a separate search. [L0546.020]
  • Bundle a product with a complementary product the same buyer needs. Example: a neck pillow for someone with neck pain who has trouble sleeping. [L0871.043]

Build-your-own, sets, avatar and family bundles

  • Build-your-own: up to 15% off, free shipping, and any combination of products the customer chooses. Show it as a pop-up offer that splits the screen so the product choices stay visible. [L0524.025]
  • Sets (sold together, older advice, Source A 2021): matching sofas and love seats should be sold together with the recliners they match, because customers buy complete sets. Keep unrelated products out of the store, with set-based exceptions. [L0801.057]
  • Sets, rule (Source A 2023): sell bundles only when the product naturally comes in sets, such as dining chairs sold in packs of four at about $200. Do not turn single items priced around $50 into bundles just to reach a higher price. [L0804.088]
  • Avatar bundles: packages designed around a specific type of buyer, such as entrepreneur, traveler, biohacker, essential and longevity. Use them in promotions to match the offer to each customer type. [L0334.021]
  • Family packs: bundles such as a family pack of 3 to 4 products work well and can be promoted on a specific collection page. [L0605.033]
  • Two-family bundle: instead of $70 for one unit, offer two units for $60 total. This appeals to families buying gifts for multiple households. [L0209.033]
  • Multi-pair bundles for shoes: buyers often purchase several pairs in different colors or variants at the same time, so offer multi-pair bundles next to the single-pair option. [L1219.009]
  • Subscriptions as a bundle layer: offers can scale with quantity and subscription, with bigger discounts for more units, extra savings for a monthly subscription, and a "frequently bought together" add-on at a discount. [L0919.022]
  • Buy More Save More: suits direct-to-consumer consumable brands such as food, beverage and makeup. It can also lead first-time buyers into a subscription, because they try an offer before deciding to subscribe. [L1231.043]

Bundling with an upgrade

  • Bundling combines a main product with an add-on you already sell as an upgrade and gives it one bundle price. Example: a $400 golf bag and a $100 trolley sold together for $450, promoted as a single package for a few days. [L1204.035]
  • A bundle works as a re-engagement tool because it is a new offer the customer has not seen before. Its price should be close to the cost of the main product plus the upgrade, with low-cost accessories added, so the margin stays acceptable. [L1205.013]
  • Case: a golf bag sold for $400 and a carrier upgrade for $100. For one month, the store offered the bag, the carrier, 24 golf balls, a glove and a cleaning kit together for $500. The extra cost to the store was only slightly higher than the bag and carrier alone, and the package gave a reason to contact old leads who had viewed the bag but not bought. [L1205.012]

Choosing bundles by stage and starting point

  • Start with one product. Add a bundle or a second product once the first one shows demand. Selling several products can also build trust, depending on the niche. [L0774.020]
  • When starting out, focus on one product at a time rather than a bundle. Bundles are harder to sell and harder to test for a beginner. [L0772.013]
  • Start with simple bundles that make sense for the product. Do not add complex bundle structures until the store is scaling. [L1299.002]
  • Keep one clear offer per store rather than several different offers for different categories, to avoid confusing customers, unless the store is large enough to manage it. The overall goal is to raise AOV. [L0650.036]
  • Replace standard time-limited percentage sales with bundle offers, such as buy two get 20% off plus a free gift, or buy one and get a complementary product free. In one store this increased conversions and AOV. [L0549.019]
  • Changing an offer from 50% off plus free shipping to buy one get one free or buy two get two free made a major difference in one seller's sales compared with the earlier offer. [L0549.021]
  • Bundles at the first step of the shop raise AOV. A product priced at $39 can be sold as two or three units to bring the order value to $70 to $80. A bundle raises margin per order without raising the ad cost per click. [L0645.024]
  • Bundles plus post-purchase upsells are the way to increase margin when a single product is priced too low. Reported results: about 70 percent of customers chose a bundle, and AOV rose from $39 to $110 across the store. [L0645.025]
  • Bundle at the first step, then upsell: a single-product store that averaged $40 per order introduced buy two get extra 20% off, so two items cost about $70. Shipping per unit fell with two units shipped together, and AOV rose from $40 to $70 with higher profit per order. [L0434.013]
  • Expanding a single-product store into a multi-product store with bundles and extra items raises AOV. Multi-buy offers encouraged customers to buy more. [L0519.033]
  • Bundles raise AOV for the store, so a store with higher AOV can justify a higher cost per acquisition when scaling. Increasing AOV through bundles, upsells, higher-priced offers or a higher price raises profit per order. [L0999.015]
  • Quantity bundles aim to raise average order value, revenue per customer and profit per order at the same time. They work as a conversion lever because each order contains more units. [L0860.002]
  • Bundle structure matters more than the single-unit price: a visible quantity ladder with a clear saving per tier pushes multi-unit orders. Keep the ladder short so a buyer can compare the tiers in a few seconds. [L1188.019]
  • Bundles raise AOV and also take up more space in Google Shopping ads, because each bundle is a different product listing. Bundles can be given their own UPC codes, which allows listing them on eBay. [L0920.021]
  • A bundle is made by building one product page that includes several products, with the bundle price above the individual sum. No app is needed for a basic bundle. [L0855.047]

Bundle strategy by price point

  • Low-priced products (about $5 to $10) in paid ads: a bundle can raise AOV to a level that covers ad costs. The bundle must still leave a margin of about $20 to $25 per customer. [L1174.016]
  • Low-priced products in general: bundle offers such as buy two, buy three or buy four and get one free work well for small, cheap products. Test several and keep the one that makes sense for the margin. [L1171.025]
  • Low-priced kitchen products: quantity discounts can encourage customers who want multiples to buy more. Test quantity offers with the product's natural use cases. [L1172.027]
  • Competitor example at low price: a competitor selling a cheap item priced one unit at $24.99, two units at $29.99 and a larger pack at a higher price used bundling to raise margin per order. The product was judged a potential winner because the competitor ran 280 active ads for it. [L1174.017]
  • Cheap add-on check: if a cheap add-on of about 2 to 3 dollars shows a good take rate of around 40 to 50 percent, a bundle with a total price up to about 40 dollars can make sense. The extra margin from the add-on can justify the bundle. [L0708.008]
  • Mid-priced products: a bundle offer for two items is typically priced about 20% below the single price. The second item can be a different variant of the same product, for example another size or color. [L1173.021]
  • Higher-priced products: a seller of a $79 device... see the bundle example below. Price the bundles so the best-selling option is the obvious deal. [L0789.026]
  • Thin-margin products (cheap items): see the pricing procedure below. [L0298.032]

Designing the tier ladder

Tier structures

  • Create tiered offers with a simple layout. Buy 1, Buy 2 and Buy 3 quantity tiers with progressive discounts are used for a single-product store. [L0860.005, L1149.006]
  • Multi-tier discounts such as "buy any 2 pieces, get the 3rd one 50% off" encourage multiple items per cart order while maintaining per-unit profitability. [L0232.006]
  • Pre-selected starter kits: present the six most popular combinations with the items included, rather than making the customer pick from a long list. Customers who face around 70 stone options and 11 setting options cannot judge which choice is best. [L1225.020]
  • Replace a custom bundle builder with ready-made bundles and an extra 10% off the pre-created bundle. Fewer decisions and clicks can help lift AOV. [L1225.017]
  • Keep the single-unit price low for entry, then make the larger quantity or the subscription the default that drives value. Visitors are more likely to accept a bigger option when it is preselected. [L0524.028]
  • Price a single unit intentionally higher than the cost structure suggests (for example $25) so the bundle offer (for example 2 units for $30) looks like an exceptional value. This raises bundle attachment rate even for low-margin products. [L0298.058]

[L0502.014]

Case behind the ladder: an African-map necklace was sold in a Shopify store at 19.90 EUR, aimed at people with an African background who want to show their origin. The dream outcome was to look good in it and show love for one's roots, and the basic single item was offered first. The price was raised to 29.90 EUR and a complementary second necklace valued at 29.90 EUR was added as a free gift, which raised conversion. The next tested offer was buy one for 29.90 EUR and get two more different necklaces free, shipped in one package, with a cost of goods of about 13 USD. The final Big Boss offer added a jewelry box (about 1 USD), a free ebook or lookbook for styling and care, and free shipping. Each stage raised conversion, and the bundle kept a margin of about 17 at 29.90 EUR. When cost rises, the price must rise too. [L0502.028]

Necklace variant: the necklace sold for 29.99 with a free choice of country, and bundles were offered in which customers who bought four items received two for free. [L0519.032]

Badges and labels

  • Competitor stores mark the best-value bundle with a Most Popular badge. Use the badge to guide buyers toward the bundle with the highest margin or volume, once you know which one it is. [L0600.023]
  • Label the 2-pack as "Most Popular" and the 3-pack as "Best Value". Add small icons such as a gift and a truck to make the tiers easier to scan. [L0860.006]
  • Label one bundle as "most popular" and highlight it visually to steer shoppers toward the higher-value option. A clearly marked default reduces choice effort and lifts AOV more than an unmarked list of equal options. [L0435.021]
  • Highlight the middle bundle tier with a most popular label and add free shipping to it. This steers most buyers toward the bundle with the higher order value and better margin for the store. [L0434.015]
  • Badge sets seen in the sources:
    • Most Popular on the 2-pack, Best Value on the 3-pack. [L0860.006]
    • Most Popular on Buy 2, Best Offer on Buy 3, with Buy 2 at Save 50% and Buy 3 at Save 55%. [L1013.015]
    • Best Offer on the top bundle at $89.99. [L0560.034]
    • Best Seller on the two pack in the sock ladder. [L0347.017]
    • Most Popular on the 60-day tier and Best Deal on the 90-day tier. [L1164.040]
  • A badge sits on the bundle that tests as best (margin or volume), not on the bundle you want to push by default. Competitors' badge placement is a signal, not a rule. [L0600.023]
  • Show the regular price next to the bundle price to make the saving clear. [L0871.044]
  • Show the saving on each tier so the customer can compare them in a few seconds. [L1188.019, L0232.004]

Naming tiers and bundles

  • Creative names create psychological appeal and justify premium pricing. Examples: "Elite Growth Kit," "Stylist Bundle," "The Complete Acne Killer," "Twin Pack." Generic names such as "Bundle 2" leave perceived value on the table. [L0232.014]
  • Example: a luxury hair laser at $1,999 alone can be repackaged as an "Elite Growth Kit" with a 5-month supply and extended warranty, justifying approximately $300 additional cost through distinctive naming. [L0232.015]
  • Generic bundle names fail to create perceived value or justify premium pricing compared with names that suggest benefits, status or use cases. [L0232.022]
  • Name bundle tiers to reflect shared use cases or paired scenarios, such as "Twin Pack," "Room for Two," "Apartment Bundle," "Partner Kit." This appeals to customers buying for multiple users and increases AOV through contextual appeal. [L0232.025]
  • Give each bundle a name that describes the outcome, such as a "sleep bundle" or "back and neck bundle," and show the regular price next to the bundle price. Combined bundles with a shared problem (for example neck and back pain) sell well. Sleep bundle example: priced at $79.95 against a regular combined price of $126, shown clearly on the landing page. [L0871.044, L0871.043]
  • Label each bundle by the length of supply it covers, such as 10-day, 30-day, 60-day, 120-day or one-year supply, not only by quantity. This lets buyers compare the cost per day across tiers. [L0600.039]
  • Tier labels for a three-tier pack: 30-day, 60-day, 90-day supply. [L1164.040]

Pricing each tier from its own cost

Rules

  • Before launching a bundled offer, calculate the per-unit costs for all items combined and verify that the bundled price still allows healthy margins. Bundling power collapses if unit economics do not support profitability. [L0233.024]
  • Set bundle pricing from cost of goods and margin: first calculate cost and margin for each option, then ensure the best bundle still gives a healthy margin even when a second unit is sold at 50 percent off. [L0789.027]
  • Each bundle quantity must be priced on its own supplier cost, not as a simple multiple of the single-unit price. A two-piece bundle is priced from the two-piece cost, which is why $45.95 for two is not double the single price. [L1182.010]
  • Use a fixed bundle total instead of a percentage discount when setting bundle prices, so the customer sees a clear per-unit price and the seller avoids discount math. Pick a total where the per-unit price drops clearly from the single price to make the saving obvious. [L0861.005]
  • Check the bundle saving against the sum of the single prices. Saving percentage = (combined value minus bundle price) divided by bundle price. Example: a bundle priced at 500 with a combined value of 680 gives (680 minus 500) divided by 500 = 36 percent. [L1088.052] (older advice, Source A 2020)
  • A bundle at 29.99 for two items against 59.99 full price gives a $10 saving on the pair, about 16.7% off the full price. [L0436.025]

Example: a single item at $29.99 and two items for $49.99 instead of $59.99. Before launching a bundle like this, check that the bundle price still covers product cost, acquisition cost and fees. [L0436.025]

  • A bundle with a total price up to about 40 dollars can make sense if a cheap add-on has a 40 to 50 percent take rate (see the add-on check above). [L0708.008]
  • Profit check: a buy-4-get-4-free offer on a home decor product left about $30 margin per order for one student. [L0650.043]
  • If the profit is too thin at the current price, improve the offer rather than only changing ads, for example by giving two items at a discounted total price while keeping a larger margin. The offer change must still leave enough margin after the cost of the extra item. [L0661.028]

Pricing examples

  • Thin-margin procedure: set the single unit at 25 dollars (or 21, or 19.99), and pre-select a 2-unit bundle at 30 dollars. Mark the bundle with labels such as "lowest price" or "most popular" and add value indicators. Customers often purchase bundles because the offer feels compelling; test with bundle apps such as Catching Bundle, Bundle Bear or White Bundle. [L0298.032]
  • Two-item bundle priced about 20% below the single price (see mid-priced section). [L1173.021]
  • A single bottle at $19.95 paired with a Buy Two fixed total of $29.99, which works out to $14.98 per bottle and saves about $10 against two singles. A Buy Three fixed total of $38.95 brings it to $12.98 per bottle, and the saving against a $60.00 compare-at price shows as $21.05. [L0861.004]
  • Book pricing formulas (standard price 41.95 USD):
    • Bundle price per tier = unit price × quantity × (1 − discount rate). [L0860.027]
    • Worked example: with a 39.99 unit price, a 15% discount on two units gives 67.98 and a 25% discount on three gives 89.98. The displayed prices are rounded to 67.99 and 89.99 so they end in.99. Divide the bundle total by the quantity to see the effective price per unit (about 33.99 for the 2-pack and 30.00 for the 3-pack). [L0860.027]
    • For a book priced at 41.95 USD: a 2-pack at 10% off totals 75.51 and at 15% off totals 71.32; a 3-pack at 20% off totals 100.68 and at 25% off totals 94.39. Before choosing the exact percentage, check that the bundle total still covers COGS, shipping and ad spend per order. [L0860.028]

Pricing procedure: Buy-One-Get-One-Free bundle

  1. Ask the supplier for the cost of two pieces. In the example, one piece costs $8 and the second unit adds $4, so the total for two pieces is $12.
  2. In the bundle row of the sheet, check that the market label reads US and not UK.
  3. Enter the total product cost for two pieces ($12) and set the quantity to 2.
  4. Raise the selling price until the margin reaches 70%; in the example this gives $45.95 for two pieces.
  5. Check that the margin is at least 67% before using the price.

Pricing procedure: Buy-Two-Get-One-Free bundle

  1. Ask the supplier for the cost of three pieces, or add one more unit to the two-piece cost. In the example, two pieces cost $12 and the third unit adds $4, so the total for three pieces is $16.
  2. In the sheet, change the quantity to 3 in the bundle row and enter the total product cost of $16.
  3. Check that the market label reads US.
  4. Raise the selling price until the margin reaches 70%; in the example the final price is $64.95, after first trying a higher price of about $69.95.
  5. Check that the margin is at least 67% before using the price.

Pricing procedure: full sequence for a single product with bundles

  1. Price the product at roughly the same level as competitors.
  2. If the margin is at least 70%, price slightly lower to undercut them; if the margin is strong but tighter, price slightly higher to protect profitability.
  3. Replace any Buy-one-get-one offer with Buy 1, Buy 2 and Buy 3 bundles that carry progressive discounts.
  4. Add a free gift to the Buy 2 and Buy 3 bundles to raise perceived value.

[L1149.011]

  • Bundle pricing with a margin reference: "Bundle pricing sells two or more units for less than the sum of single prices, so the customer gets an automatic saving... Calculate that the bundle price still leaves a margin that covers all costs." [L0436.016]
  • Price of a low-cost add-on: if a cheap product (about $5 to $10) is competing in paid ads, a bundle can raise AOV to a level that covers ad costs. The bundle must still leave a margin of about $20 to $25 per customer. [L1174.016]

Price-raising before a BOGO offer

  • BOGO works when margins allow it. If margins are thin, raise the product price by $5 to $10 first and then test BOGO, provided branding is strong enough to justify the higher price. [L1260.017, L1345.013]

  • Procedure: set up a buy-one-get-one-free offer when margins are thin:

    1. Raise the product price by 5 to 10 USD.
    2. Strengthen branding so the higher price is credible.
    3. Offer buy-one-get-one-free on the higher price.

    In niches such as jewelry, good product images can justify a higher perceived value (example: a necklace going from 12.99 USD to 19.99 USD with BOGO). [L1259.026]

  • In niches such as jewelry, strong images can raise perceived value, so a necklace listed at 12.99 could be shown at 19.99 and then offered as buy-one-get-one-free. Results depend on niche and margin, so test. [L1260.018]

  • Raise the price before the bundle is made: a single product priced too low can be lifted by bundles and upsells (see the first-step bundle section above). [L0645.025]

  • The price should also be set so the single-unit price is raised before the offer and not tested for margin only: "Test which offer type fits the margin of your products." [L0476.031]

  • Test offers for thin margins: see the offer change above. [L0661.028]

Bundle offers and where to put free shipping

Sources disagree on where free shipping belongs. Both values are given below with the condition under which each applies.

Never on the lowest tier (this is listed as a mistake) [L0232.021] Removing free shipping from the lowest tier keeps the upgrade incentive and avoids giving margin away to price-sensitive buyers

  • Bundles with complementary items work better than pushing customers to buy more of the same product to reach a free-shipping threshold. [L1172.026]
  • Bundles reduce shipping cost per unit, since one parcel carries several items. This improves profit per order while the bundle price still feels like a saving to the customer. [L0435.013]
  • Bundle shipping saves money because two items shipped to one address cost less than two separate orders, so bundles can improve margin while appearing to be a deal. [L1259.032]
  • When an offer includes several items, ask the supplier to ship them in one package. Otherwise shipping costs multiply and the margin disappears; the extra cost should only be the product cost of the extra item. [L0502.018]

Gifts placement

  • Free gifts go on the bundles, not the single-unit tier. Adding a free gift to the Buy 2 and Buy 3 tiers raises perceived value and conversion rate. [L1149.008]
  • Offering free items or strong discounts such as buy one get one free or buy two get two free attracts customers, because people respond strongly to a perceived free item. [L0440.045]
  • The offer should either complement the solution to the customer's problem or make the product more attractive. For example, if you sell candles, give two free when a customer buys four. [L0549.020]
  • Place the free ebook on the 2-pack and the 3-pack, and place free shipping on the 3-pack. This gives the larger bundle the most visible extras. [L0860.007]
  • Free gift on a bundle with a gift-friendly message: a single tier with "buy 1 and give 1 to someone you love" wording. [L1141.010]
  • Free gifts raise perceived value, and the free-gift option can reduce the perceived price without a full discount. [L0476.031]
  • Gift-season offers should sound like a gift, not a bargain. [L1225.019]
  • Gift-friendly niches can benefit from buy-one-get-one or buy-2-get-items offers, because customers can give the items to friends or partners. [L0650.039]

Bundle strategy by brand and niche

  • Buy-one-get-one-free can downgrade a brand that sells a premium or identity-based product, because it cheapens the perceived value. Use a different offer for such brands. [L1131.012]
  • Buy-one-get-one-free can be a high barrier when many customers only want one gift, as seen with a previous Mother's Day mask offer that required buying two. Test percentage-off or a second-item discount, which gives more flexibility to single-gift buyers. [L1225.015]
  • The best offer type depends on the product and niche. Gift-friendly niches can benefit from buy-one or buy-2-get-items offers. [L0650.039]
  • BOGO and similar offers are niche- and margin-specific and must be tested. Products that seem unlikely to sell in multiples often do sell that way when the offer is tested. [L1345.015]
  • Bundle offers such as "buy two, get one free" plus a clear black or multi-pack option (see the example above). [L0655.015]
  • Start a new store with one product and add a second product once the first one shows demand. [L0774.020]
  • Products bought in stores: a bundle offer such as buy two get a third free works well for products customers already buy in stores. [L1176.027]
  • Bundle the products that buyers would purchase together. Bundles work best when the products are related and buyers would purchase them together. Use a bundle app such as Bold Product Bundles for these combinations, though any bundle app works. (older advice, Source A 2021) [L1112.070]

Sourcing and supplier bundles

  • Create product bundles from the same supplier, such as kits made of components that come together, rather than making customers assemble cheap separate components to reach a price point. (older advice, Source A 2021) [L0671.055, L0671.194]

  • Before building a bundle from related products, test a tiered quantity discount on the same product:

    1. One item at the normal price.
    2. Two items at a special discount price.
    3. Three items at a better discount price.

    This raises AOV with no extra product research, and the bundle idea can be tested later if it still needs a boost. [L0774.053]

  • Test a second product as a bundle with the supplier before running ads on it:

    1. Choose the main product and a second product that is selling well for other stores.
    2. Ask the supplier agent to create a bundle SKU with the main product and the second product.
    3. Measure which bundle buyers pick most, then run ads for the second product on its own.

    [L0774.018]

  • Discuss tiered pricing options with your supplier before launching a bundle. [L0240.011]

  • Bundles and quantity levels with the supplier: ask the supplier for the cost of two or three pieces before pricing the bundle (see the pricing procedures). [L1182.007, L1182.008]

Bundles and Google Shopping

  • Bundles with a single item's page: create the product page for the single item that Google Shopping shows most, and offer quantity options such as 2, 4 or 10 on the same page. This keeps your listing aligned with what buyers already search for. (Source A 2023) [L0934.050]
  • Do not repackage a product into a higher-priced bundle SKU, such as an $800 product sold as a $1,200 bundle. Google Shopping shows the higher price next to competitors at $800, which lowers click-through rate and sales. (older advice, Source A 2018) [L1109.032]
  • Unique bundles raise AOV and also take up more space in Google Shopping ads, because each bundle is a different product listing (see the channel section above). [L0920.021]

Bundles on TikTok and other channels

  • Offer structures that work well on TikTok include buy one get one free, buy two get one free, buy one at 20% off with a second item at an extra 15% off, and bundles. Building a strong offer is the most important investment when creating a unique selling angle. [L1183.018]

Unbundling and debundling

Principles

  • Unbundle high-priced products into separate components to lower the entry price. This improves click-through rates on Google Shopping ads while generating add-on revenue. [L0240.011]
  • A debundled listing with a lower price can raise Google Ads click-through rate because it is cheaper than competitors' bundles. It is not false advertising when the listing photo and title show only the single item. [L1076.029]
  • Unbundling sells the core product without an accessory at a lower price, and the accessory is offered as an optional add-on. Confirm the MAP limit with the supplier before listing it. [L1204.037]

Examples

  • Product bundle: two separately priced items, one at $400 and one at $100, can be sold together as a single $500 bundle. The bundle packages the products into one purchase with a combined price. [L0757.020]
  • Product un-bundle: a single higher-priced package is split into parts that can be sold separately. The lesson's example pairs a $1,000 price with an $800 price for the un-bundled version. [L0757.021]
  • Paddle board example: a paddle board listed at 800 dollars without a paddle, which is 200 dollars below the 1,000 dollar competitors' price, with the paddle added for plus 200 dollars. Confirm the MAP limit with the supplier before listing it. [L1204.037]

Procedure: debundling a product for a cross-sell

  1. Check the supplier price list for a single-item price, or ask the supplier for the separate MAP, wholesale and retail prices of the item sold in the bundle.
  2. If the single item can be bought separately, list it at its lower price.
  3. Use a photo of the single item only.
  4. Let the customer add the accessory on the product page through a cross-sell offer.

[L1076.028]

Procedures for complementary bundles from a low-cost accessory

  1. On AliExpress, search for accessories related to the main product (for example fitness, health, jewelry, hair or nail accessories) and set a price filter from 0.01 USD to about 4 USD (for example min 0.01 and max 4 dollars).
  2. Pick a low-cost complementary item (example cost around 2 USD) that solves a related problem.
  3. Bundle it with the main product and ship as one order to the same address, which saves on shipping costs because two items ship to one address.
  4. List the complementary item at a higher value (example 20 USD) while its cost is about 2 USD.
  5. Offer the bundle with a visible saving, about 15% or a dollar-equivalent saving (example: main product 39.99 USD plus complementary product at 20 USD with a 15 USD discount, so buyers see the second item as almost free). Use wording such as "save big with our bundle."

[L1259.030, L1260.022]

Testing and rollout

  • Offers should be tested, and the one that makes the most sense for the margin should be kept. [L1171.025, L0562.007]
  • Test BOGO and similar offers before committing, because the best structure depends on niche and margin. [L1345.015]
  • A quantity-break offer and a buy-two offer both raise AOV, so test which one lifts AOV more. [L0635.027, L0434.012]
  • Use bundles to make the store more profitable per visitor, not only to win the first sale. [L0562.008]
  • Multiple bundle structures: several tested offers exist on the same product; keep the one that makes the most sense for the margin. [L0650.034, L1171.025]

Mistakes to avoid

  • Offering free shipping on the lowest tier removes the incentive to upgrade and wastes margin on buyers who would buy anyway. [L0232.021]
  • Generic bundle names fail to create perceived value. [L0232.022]
  • Repackaging a product into a higher-priced bundle SKU lowers click-through rate. [L1109.032] (older advice, Source A 2018)
  • Buy-one-get-one-free can downgrade a premium or identity-based brand. [L1131.012]
  • A second identical item has little value to a buyer who only needs one. [L1131.013]
  • Bundling does not work when the store offers only one product in BOGO form (for example one book), so use Buy 1, Buy 2 and Buy 3 quantity tiers with progressive discounts instead. [L0860.005, L1149.006]
  • Buy-one-get-one can be a barrier for single-gift buyers. [L1225.015]
  • Turning single items around $50 into bundles only to reach a higher price is not advised. [L0804.088] (Source A 2023)
  • Bundles can lose money if the bundle price does not cover product cost, acquisition cost and fees. [L0436.025, L0233.024]

Build-your-own and offers that look like free items

  • Build-your-own bundle: up to 15% off, free shipping, and any combination of products the customer chooses. [L0524.025]
  • Show the add-on as almost free: list the complementary item at a visible value (e.g. $20) while its cost is about $2, and show a bundle discount so the add-on looks almost free. [L1260.022]

Pricing for Google Shopping and bundle take-rate

  • Bundle take-rate and profit: complementary bundles with a 40 to 50 percent take rate and about $40 extra profit per order (see the complementary section). [L0650.038]
  • Bundling three related items into one set is a way to raise perceived value and average order size. [L1153.030]

Bundle types

  • Complementary add-on bundles: a low-cost accessory is paired with a main product and shown as one offer. [L1345.024]
  • Quantity bundles (one, two and three packs): each larger pack gives more savings per unit. These are a core way to raise order value, alongside volume discounts and cart upsells. [L1410.003]
  • Buy-one-get-one and free-item bundles: a second unit or a second item is offered free with the first. [L1404.018, L1436.043, L1439.034]
  • On-page bundle packages: a group of products tied to a core product, sold at one combined price. [L1409.010]
  • Value bundles: one item is discounted to roughly break-even and profit comes from the other bundled items. [L1522.017]
  • Bundles are often combined with percentage-off or dollar-off discounts. [L1404.023]

Complementary add-ons

  • Example: a complementary item costing about $2 is listed at $20. Bundled with the main product, the bundle shows a $15 discount on the add-on, so the buyer perceives the add-on as $5. [L1345.024]
  • A bundle discount of about $15 on a $20 complementary item is considered an attractive discount level. Set the actual discount according to margin. [L1345.025]
  • Create bundles for popular add-ons and accessories as separate Shopify products. This gives more listings in Google Shopping, where the single product and several bundles can appear together for one search. [L1357.021]
  • Start with bundles for the best-selling accessories and test them before adding more. Bundles for every combination create too many options and overwhelm customers. The best approach is an educated guess followed by testing. [L1357.022]
  • Bundling only works where the product is naturally bought with something else. It is not possible for products never sold alongside another item. Where it works, a bundle can add roughly one or two hundred dollars of profit per sale. [L1456.015]

Tier naming and badges

  • Name a bundle tier, such as Gold, by using the same product name plus a suffix that makes it sound like a better deal. [L1456.014]
  • Multi-pack tiers with a "Best Value" tag and a visible savings amount raise the average order size. [L1479.023]

  • Pairing multi-pack tiers with an automatic refill discount, such as 20% off, adds repeat revenue from the same customer. [L1479.023]

Buy-one-get-one and free-item offers

  • Multi-purchase deals such as 2 for 1 or buy 1 get 1 free make the customer feel they get a great deal, which raises the chance of buying. This format has worked for years and still works today. [L1399.010]
  • Multi-purchase deals such as buy 2 for 1 or buy 1 get 1 free make shoppers feel they are getting a strong deal, which raises the chance of buying. This is an old promotion that still works well today. [L1400.007]
  • BOGO works best on the same product as the one purchased, because customers who want that item already have an emotional attachment to it. A variant such as Buy One Get One at 30% off is also possible. [L1404.018]
  • A buy-one-get-one-free offer works well for the same product. It also works for very simple products that are understood from one image. For a different or complex product, the offer lowers conversion because the buyer must check the landing page. [L1412.040]
  • Buy-one-get-one-free and "buy this, get this free item" bundles have been working for TikTok dropshipping. Buy-one-get-one may fail with electronics, while jewelry may suit it. [L1436.043]

Cart-page quantity ladders

  • On the cart page, a quantity ladder can reward larger baskets: one unit gets no discount and two units get 10% off. This gives a buyer already in checkout a simple reason to add a second unit. [L1412.055]

Free shipping and gifts

  • A free second item can be attached to a purchase, and social actions can unlock extra rewards. [L1439.034]
