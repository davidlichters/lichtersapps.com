7.12 Bonuses and free gifts

Types of bonuses

  • Bonus offers have four types: physical products (low-cost items customers need), digital products (online lessons, guides, e-books), expedited shipping upgrades (white-glove delivery), and extended warranties. [L0314.001]
  • Physical product bonuses have historically given a stronger conversion lift than digital bonuses. Digital bonuses are covered under the digital versus physical comparison below. [L0314.001]
  • A bonus is a free item tied to a purchase trigger, such as spending above a threshold or buying a specific product. Physical bonuses are shipped goods, and digital bonuses are delivered online. [L0757.022]
  • Bonus items can include free ebooks, video guides, styling tutorials, complimentary gifts, or storage boxes. They increase perceived value at minimal cost and make rejection emotionally difficult for customers. [L0233.009]
  • Bonuses such as ebooks, video tutorials on how the product works, or videos on how it improves the buyer's life increase the value of an offer. Add them to the landing page as part of the offer. [L0502.011]
  • Free gifts, free upsells, installation services, and valuable content such as recipe books add perceived value. Consistently helpful free content throughout the buyer journey can build a higher perceived value than competitors. [L0411.031] (Source B, 2021-22: older advice.)
  • Free gift card: a gift card can serve as the free gift when a store wants to add value to an order and encourage a larger purchase. It works as an upsell because the buyer gets extra value without a physical product from the supplier. Show the gift clearly on the offer image so the visitor sees what they get. [L1363.059]
  • Premium with its own value: add a premium that carries its own value, such as a one-year magazine subscription included with a set, so the same payment looks like more value. Name the premium's price or worth in the letter. [L1369.016]
  • Gift with purchase from another range: buying one product gives a free second product from a different range, typically a smaller, high-margin item that adds little weight, so shipping cost stays low. [L1404.019]
  • Services the business already provides can be presented as bonuses at no extra cost to the seller. [L0683.009]
  • White-glove delivery means the delivery company brings the product inside, unpacks it, removes all packaging, and positions it. It costs an extra $100-$150 per order. [L0314.012]
  • Physical bonuses should be items the buyer would need anyway, such as a seasonal ingredient pack, a protectant for leather furniture, or a book drop-shipped with the order. [L1204.039]

Deciding whether a bonus pays

  • Bonus offers must be strategised before traffic launch so they are built into the sales funnel as a proven conversion multiplier. [L0040.015]
  • Offer free gifts or bonuses only when the margin allows it, such as on a best-selling product with strong profit per sale in a competitive niche. Free gifts make sense when competitors do not offer them and the product margin covers the cost. [L0941.052]
  • Example: a free bonus gift worth about $50 can make sense on a product that earns roughly $400 to $500 profit per sale in a competitive niche, because competitors do not offer it and the gift drives extra orders. If the margin on the product is thin, do not offer a gift. [L0941.108]
  • Include free gifts only when the margin still works after the gift cost. If a supplier charges state tax on shipments and this cannot be resolved, drop the free gifts. [L0968.078] (Source A, 2021: older advice.)
  • Keep the gift's cost low enough that it does not eat into the margin, and skew the value by setting a higher price point for the bundle. [L0999.065]
  • Bundling a low-cost item with the main product, or adding a free gift that costs little, can raise perceived value and average order value while keeping margin healthy, as long as the gift is relevant and the customer wants it. [L0999.064]
  • The bonus strategy is worth more when product margin is high. On a $300 or $800 profit product, a $20 bonus is negligible and can be given freely. [L1293.026]
  • Very low-priced add-ons whose shipping cost is close to their price should not be sold alone. Offer them as a free bonus gift with high-ticket purchases, or sell them in multi-packs. [L0966.054] (Source A, 2025.)
  • Do not sell low-ticket accessories on their own. If the margin allows, include them as bonus gifts with a main product instead. [L0968.112] (Source A, 2021: older advice.)
  • Decide between a free bonus and a cross-sell by comparing the main product price with the price of the add-on. [L1110.028] (Source A, 2019: older advice.)
  • Cheap accessories on an expensive product should be listed as free bonuses on the product page, without their own product pages. Example: a 5,000 USD bike with a reflector and water bottle holder costing about 30 USD retail and 15 USD to the seller. [L1110.028] (Source A, 2019: older advice.)
  • Gift bonuses reduce the need for price cuts: a free accessory that costs the store $25 but is valued at $200 can be given with every order. [L1109.031] (Source A, 2018: older advice.)
  • Competitor stores add a free bonus item such as a guide ebook to a bundle. Use a bonus to raise perceived value without changing the base price. [L0600.021]

Choosing gifts by cost and relevance

  • Pick bonus items that cost little for you but solve buyer problems, such as storage, styling, cleaning, or wearing advice. Low-cost items like a jewelry box or an AI-drafted ebook can carry a large share of the perceived value. [L0502.016]
  • Bonus items should be inexpensive for you to offer, even if they look valuable to the buyer. Do not choose items with high wholesale costs or ones that threaten your margins, because bonuses are incentives for customers to choose you, not revenue-builders. [L0314.015]
  • Bonus items that customers would need anyway with the product are the best choice. Ideally the bonus comes from the same manufacturer, so it can be added to the supplier's order at no extra effort. [L1293.027]
  • Physical bonus pairings that work: a book for spending over $50; free ingredients or free bottles with a starter kit; a free protectant with a leather sofa. [L0757.023]
  • Physical bonus ideas: a homebrewing starter kit can include a free ingredient sample pack or free bottles if bought before a deadline. Leather sofas can include a free leather protectant shipped with every order during a few promotion days each month. [L1205.015]
  • The bonus should be closely related to the product's use or benefit. An unrelated free ebook is weak. For example, a shower filter should get an ebook on skin care for sensitive skin, linked to hard-water skin problems. [L0577.016]
  • A free gift that is unrelated to the main product but valued by buyers can make an offer irresistible and help differentiate the store from competitors. Pick a low-cost gift that buyers want, such as a shaker or water bottle. [L1173.022]
  • Test whether the gift matches what the target buyer wants. Free packaging or free shipping insurance can be offered as a free-gift tab on some themes, but these are not always what buyers value. [L1173.024]
  • Choose a gift item that matches what the buyer needs, such as a cleaning product for a sofa. [L0963.068]
  • Choose a gift the buyer will value, such as a popular consumer electronic item unrelated to the store's products, and check that its cost fits the margin on the main sale. [L1223.032]
  • Bonuses should be designed specifically for the main offer, not thrown together. Each one should be valuable enough that the prospect would pay for it alone. Start by asking which bonuses would perfectly complement the main product and add enormous value to the offer. [L1365.037]
  • Choose a promotion that feels valuable to customers and is still profitable. A free gift with high perceived value often moves more customers than a slightly larger money-off discount. [L1112.068] (Source A, 2021: older advice.)
  • A bonus gift with a perceived value of about $100, such as a leather desk mat, can persuade buyers to buy from your store instead of the supplier. Its cost to you is only the wholesale price of the gift, so it works best when the supplier does not offer the same extra. [L0934.003] (Source A, 2023.)
  • Choose bonus items from the same supplier as the main product to avoid extra shipping costs. Bonus items from different suppliers arrive separately and add shipping. [L0962.106] (Source A, 2021: older advice.)
  • Add-ons do not need to come from the same supplier as the main product. Choose the add-on that is cheapest to buy and has the highest perceived value for the customer. [L0577.012]
  • Add-on pricing: the add-on's real cost is irrelevant to the buyer. Only the stated value matters, so label it with a clearly higher value. [L0577.015]
  • Cheap free gifts such as a $1 item from AliExpress can be used as a bonus to entice buyers, as long as the business keeps a profit margin. [L0650.042]
  • Adding a complementary free item, for example socks with hoodies, or a free digital ebook raises perceived value and allows a higher offer price, as long as the item is cheap to produce or obtain. [L0650.040]
  • A free digital bonus, such as an ebook, can be added to every order as a zero-cost extra that makes the purchase more attractive. It works when the product margin is too thin for a bigger discount. [L0562.041]
  • If a margin is too thin for a $20 gift, give something that costs nothing, such as a digital recipe e-book, a video tutorial, or a free online training course, when it adds value for the customer. [L1293.030]
  • Educational bonuses such as ebooks raise the perceived value of an offer, especially in health and wellness, kids, and parenting niches. This can allow a slightly higher offer price while still giving the customer a feeling of extra value. [L0650.069]
  • Example: a necklace offer gave the buyer one main product plus a second necklace of their choice for free. Most buyers chose a design linked to their home country. The offer raised perceived value while keeping the free item's cost low. [L0661.027]
  • Physical bonuses should be items the buyer would need anyway. Free books were also used as a bonus on orders over $50 in one store. [L1204.039]
  • Two other examples of bonus items that carry perceived value: a free trending product offered with any order before a deadline, and a bonus of a snorkel with a mask. [L1111.090, L1293.028]
  • Bonus pairings that match the main product's use: a free snorkel with a scuba mask, a free carrying case with a camera lens, a free air pump with a $300 air mattress (a $30 to $40 pump, since no air mattress comes with one and customers must buy it separately). [L1293.025, L1293.028, L1293.029]
  • When a supplier includes a bonus item such as a bag or accessory, show it prominently as a "Free gift" block with a picture and its value. This makes the included extra visible and increases the perceived value of the offer. [L1130.030]
  • Free accessories bundled with a main product are generally acceptable if the accessories are also sold separately at MAP. Check each supplier's policy if in doubt. [L1069.054] (Source A, 2019: older advice.)
  • Bundle low-cost extras as free bonus gifts rather than raising the base price, so the advertised price stays the same and MAP is not violated. [L1109.030] (Source A, 2018: older advice.)

Digital bonuses and free ebooks

  • Digital bonus ideas: an e-book version of a guide given free for orders over $50; a free lesson, e-book, or downloadable video that matches what the customer needs; or a free extended trial of a related service through an affiliate partnership, such as a treadmill store offering a weight-management program trial. [L1205.016]
  • Digital bonus pairings: spend over $50 for a free eBook; buy a drum set for free online lessons; buy a treadmill for an extended free trial of a weight-loss program such as Weight Watchers. [L0757.023]
  • Digital bonuses (online lessons, guides, e-books) underperform physical bonuses in conversion lift, based on practical experience. When using digital bonuses, partner with external instructors or platforms rather than creating your own content. This reduces cost and leverages their expertise. [L0314.009]
  • Example: a drum-set seller partnered with an online drum lesson platform to offer one or two free introductory lessons. The platform gains new customers, the store differentiates, and the bonus has minimal or no cost to the store. [L0314.010]
  • Example: treadmill sellers can offer physical bonuses such as a free water bottle, towels, or apparel, or digital bonuses such as a free meal plan or an e-book on running technique. Test which types customers value most. [L0314.011]
  • Educational bonuses should be checked against the product: the best bonus is directly linked to the buyer's use or benefit. [L0577.016]
  • A free ebook worth $20 with a purchase increases conversion and can be delivered automatically after the order. [L1184.048]
  • A digital bonus such as an ebook was tested as extra value but did not beat a relevant physical free gift in one store. Prefer a physical gift that is closely related to the main product and its customer problem. [L1315.062]
  • Free digital add-ons such as guides or templates can be listed at a stated value and given free with purchase, which makes the gift feel worth more. Customers who missed the add-on earlier can be offered it free later as a reason to buy again. [L0774.055]
  • Free digital gift pattern: add a simple free guide, for example one made with ChatGPT and a couple of images, to the order to add value without cost. Some customers may pay a small price for the guide, after which the offer can be given free to previous buyers. [L0773.055]
  • A free digital ebook that costs nothing to create can justify a $5.99 higher price for a bigger bundle tier, increasing perceived value at no cost. [L0232.008]

Creating free ebooks

Scope and content

  • Keep the ebook short: 15-25 pages with 4-6 chapters of substantive, useful content. Each chapter must deliver real value, and the ebook must contain no placeholder text. [L0860.011]
  • Ebooks cost almost nothing to deliver but noticeably raise perceived value. Customers feel they are getting expert information, and the offer feels more complete even if they never read the ebook. [L0860.012]
  • Page count guidance: a free ebook should have around 15 to 30 pages to deliver perceived value, while 5 pages is too few. Show the page count on the landing page, for example "a 28-page PDF." [L1439.050]
  • Choose an ebook topic that matches the product's niche and the buyer's problem. A dog product gets a dog health guide, a fitness product gets a home workout plan, and a sleep product gets a sleep guide. [L0860.013]
  • The ebook should read as a thoughtful gift, not a sales pitch. Use a warm, direct voice, short paragraphs, subheadings, and bullet points where they help readability. [L0860.014]
  • Filler chapters and placeholder text make the bonus look cheap and weaken the offer. Every chapter should be useful on its own. [L0860.015]
  • Ebook bonus titles should match the product's buyer. For a beauty product, a skincare guide such as "Five-Step Skincare Routine For Dry Skin" fits. For a posture product, a habits guide such as "6 Daily Habits For Sitting Taller At Work" fits. [L0860.030]
  • For a book seller, a good free gift is a short e-book that teaches the reader how to create an e-book, offered as a bonus on the Buy 2 and Buy 3 bundles. [L1149.009]
  • Make the ebook about how to properly use or optimise the main product. This justifies higher bundle prices and increases the perceived value of the tier. [L0232.027]
  • In a free-gift ebook, place a short tracker or overview of the whole challenge, such as a 7-day reset map, before the day-by-day breakdown. The reader knows the full path before the detailed steps begin. [L0862.039]
  • Example of a challenge ebook step: before bed, take a small flashlight and a small mirror. Spend about 30 seconds on three checks: the gumline where teeth meet gums, the back of the tongue for any white or yellow coating, and the gaps between the back teeth. Note one simple observation only, without changing your routine. [L0862.040]
  • A short usage guide for a product that buyers may ask about can be made as follows. [L1432.043]
    1. Find a store that sells the same product and has a strong product description.
    2. Use that description to understand the product's features and how it is used.
    3. Write and design a PDF of three to four pages that explains how to use the product.
    4. Deliver the PDF to buyers automatically, with a delivery app or a download link in the order confirmation.
    5. Prefer creating the guide yourself over buying Amazon seller material to reuse, since your own version is safer and more specific. [L1432.043]
  • Adding a small, unannounced free gift to the parcel costs almost nothing if it is printed cheaply, for example three items per A4 sheet in black and white with a simple recipe. Customers who receive such a surprise return more often, refer others, and leave better feedback. [L1437.005]

Sourcing and rebranding ebooks

  • Ebook topics that buyers can use: a free ebook such as a resistance band exercise guide, an ab workout guide, or a 15, 30, or 60 minute workout guide. A resistance band seller added a mesh bag to solve an odour complaint and included three free ebooks. [L1153.031]
  • Buy a PLR (private label rights) ebook on a health-related topic from a site such as plr.me, typically around $5 to $10 or less. [L0871.047]
  • A free-ebook sourcing procedure is set out below. [L0650.068]
    1. Choose a topic related to your product or your customer's needs, such as wellness or a product-use subject.
    2. Search that topic on a free-ebook website and download a matching ebook.
    3. Hire a freelancer on Fiverr to change the cover and branding to match your store.
    4. Offer the rebranded ebook as a free bonus with a purchase, and do not sell it directly. [L0650.068]
  • A free ebook rebranded with a new cover and logo can be offered as a bonus. It should not be sold directly because of copyright concerns. The lesson describes this as a gray area and recommends the rebranding. [L0650.041]
  • Disagreement on selling rebranded PLR ebooks: one lesson says the ebook can be edited in Canva and kept as delivered, since the rights allow reuse, and then either added as a bonus or sold as a product on the site. [L0871.047] Another says rebranded ebooks should not be sold directly because of copyright concerns and should be offered only as a free bonus. [L0650.041, L0650.068] Use it as a free bonus in every case. Sell it only if the PLR licence you bought explicitly allows resale.
  • A cheap-upsell strategy with a free ebook as the add-on follows these steps. [L0577.011]
    1. Search the supplier marketplace (AliExpress example) for a product related to your main product. Filter by reviews and find an add-on that costs between 10 cents and 1 dollar.
    2. Offer the add-on for free on the store, either beside the main product or as a free bonus when customers buy.
    3. Find a PLR ebook related to the product's benefit (for example, skin care for sensitive skin for a shower-filter product) on a PLR site.
    4. Buy the PLR ebook for about 2 dollars 70 cents, rebrand it with your own logo, and give it to customers as a downloadable bonus.
    5. State the ebook's value as 20 dollars in the offer, for example "Receive a 20 dollar ebook free with your order."
    6. Make the offer very visible on the site (banner, dropdown, or product gallery image as second image).
    7. Set up the add-on as a variant of the main product's Shopify listing, and use an automation app (Auto DS was named) so the supplier receives orders for both items together.
    8. Tell customers in the order confirmation email that the items may arrive on different days and not together. [L0577.011]
  • A custom add-on packaging made specifically for a product can be expensive: if it must be manufactured separately, suppliers often require a minimum of 500 to 1,000 units. Only use custom add-ons if the supplier already has the item in stock. [L0577.017]

Tiered and selectable gifts

  • Tiered gifts make the buyer add more items. Each extra item unlocks an additional free gift, up to a set maximum. Removing an item removes the gift, which uses loss aversion to encourage larger orders. [L0919.021]
  • Example: a tiered gift offer in the cart unlocked three gifts as the buyer added bottles: a recipe book, a mystery gift, and a milk frother. Removing a bottle removed the matching gift. Gift tiers were tied to quantity and capped at a set number. [L0919.046]
  • Example: a necklace offer and selectable gifts with a choice of design, as above. [L0661.027]
  • Include a card that new customers return with their order to claim a gift, and give existing customers a different reason to receive it. The gift helped the catalogue beat competing catalogues and paid for itself several times through larger orders. [L1261.159]
  • Free gift price guidance: a base product at $45 with a free gift gave a total perceived price of around $60, which was a good sweet spot. A total of $80 to $100 was harder to sell because buyers did not see enough value. [L1315.030]
  • Example: a free gift that costs about $2 was added to a product sold at $59.99 instead of $60, which skews the perceived value upward. This was the most profitable offer for that store. The gift must still be relevant to the customer and must not eat too much of the margin. [L0995.021, L0999.016]
  • Bundle-stacking with value display, where the bonus is shown at its dollar value: offer the main product plus a free bonus item, show the bonus's original retail price in parentheses (for example "$19.99 value"), and add urgency language. This works because the free item increases perceived bundle value at minimal cost, and the specific dollar value makes the gift tangible. [L0313.004]
    • Example one: "FREE meal planning guide ($24.99 value) with every 90-day nutrition package."
    • Example two: "FREE stretching routine video ($14.99 value) with every recovery device-today only." [L0313.004]
  • Example: a posture corrector bundle with a free gift. The free "Posture Fix Guide" was displayed at a $19.99 value and included with the corrector purchase. The ad used "limited time only" and "today only" language. The free guide was a low-cost value-add that increased perceived bundle worth without significant cost to the seller. [L0313.014]

Presenting gifts on pages

  • Show the free gift clearly on the product page and in the cart, with a headline, an image, and a claim message after adding to cart. Customers who do not know about the gift may not add the product. [L1315.032]
  • Add free bonus items, such as a digital cheat sheet or e-book, as a visible line near Add to Cart and in the bundle block. State the bonus's stated value when it has one. Only promise a bonus that is actually delivered automatically after checkout. [L1188.018]
  • Place an image labelled with the gift and its value under the product description on the product page. Use wording such as "free gift with your order, 39 USD value." [L0963.068]
  • Bonuses should be shown on the product page as free gifts, not hidden behind extra steps. Do not make customers call or take extra steps to receive an offer, such as a gift voucher for large orders, because extra steps cause distractions and lost sales. [L1110.030] (Source A, 2019: older advice.)
  • Show the bonus on the landing page as part of the offer, next to the main product. [L0502.011]
  • Make the offer very visible on the site with a banner, a dropdown, or a product gallery image as the second image. [L0577.011]
  • Present a bonus-offer pattern on an expensive product page: "If you buy [expensive product], you get [free item] included." The customer sees the bonus as added value to a purchase they already want. Two examples: "Buy the 4-seat sauna and get a free thermometer kit included." and "Order the commercial espresso machine and receive a free descaling set with it." [L1002.034] (Source A, 2019: older advice.)
  • Threshold gift pattern: "spend over [threshold] before [date] and get a bonus [gift] valued around [amount]." It works because buyers already plan to spend the amount, and the gift feels like extra value even when it has nothing to do with the store. [L1113.078] (Source A, 2022: older advice.)
    • Example one: "Order over [threshold] this [holiday] and receive a free [gift] shipped with your order."
    • Example two: "Shop before [date] with over [threshold] in your cart and we add a bonus [gift] at no cost." [L1113.078]
  • Gift-with-purchase promotion: offer a free trending product with any order placed before a deadline, such as 11:59 pm on Cyber Monday. It works even in niches that are not gift products, because buyers shopping for the main product also consider the bonus item as a present for someone else. [L1111.090] (Source A, 2020: older advice.)
  • Offer the gift as a bundle choice in the bundle block: the main product with the free bonus and the stated value shown at the bundle price. [L0313.004]
  • Bundle presentation: list the offer as separate components stacked on the main product, not as one item. [L0683.007]
  • Bonus stacking for a 59.99 USD product: show the old price 119.99 USD next to the new price 59.99 USD, list a free ebook worth 19.99 USD, free shipping instead of 9.99 USD, and 24/7 customer service. Add a second unit for 39.99 USD instead of 59.99 USD to raise average order value. [L0683.008]
  • Each bonus should be given a stated value, such as the price it would cost on its own. Customers then see the total package as worth more than the price. [L0683.009]
  • Lead magnet pattern: keep the same product and the same price, and add a small free gift that matches the buyer's interest to raise conversion rate. Rotate gifts every two days so each gift gets a fair test. Example: a sold-out-style fitness band at the same price with a free water bottle; a kitchen knife with a free cutting board. [L0773.015]
  • Bonus items can be presented as the "premium" in a sales letter, with the premium's price or worth named in the letter. [L1369.016]
  • Free gifts should be placed where the buyer sees them before deciding; the same bonus that is hidden in the footer does not increase conversion. [L1315.032]
  • Add a free ebook with the shape of the offer: "Receive a 20 dollar ebook free with your order." [L0577.011]
  • Show the gift image in the offer image, so the buyer knows exactly what they get. [L1363.059]
  • Bonus delivered after purchase must be listed truthfully on the offer, so the buyer knows the bonus will arrive. [L1188.018]

Fulfilment of gifts

Same-warehouse and separate shipments

  • Fulfilment depends on where the gift ships from. [L0358.033]
    1. If the free gift ships from the same warehouse as the main product, bundle the gift into the product on your store so the supplier ships both together.
    2. If the free gift ships separately or from another warehouse, set up an automation in Zapier.
    3. Set the Zapier trigger to fire when a new order is placed with an order value above the threshold, for example above $2,000.
    4. Set the action to send an email to the fulfilment contact with the bonus gift and the customer's shipping address. [L0358.033] (Source A, 2020: older advice.)
  • Gift shipped separately from the main order: a store bought the gift directly from the brand and shipped it separately, with the business address as return address. The email list and ads promoted it. [L1113.079] (Source A, 2022: older advice.)
  • Gift-with-purchase fulfilment when a low-cost gift is added to a high-ticket order: [L1223.032]
    1. Pick a gift the buyer will value (a popular consumer electronic item unrelated to the store's products) and check that its cost fits the margin on the main sale.
    2. When a customer completes a qualifying order, take the customer's shipping address from the order.
    3. Order the gift from the retailer and enter the customer's address as the delivery address, so the gift ships straight to the buyer.
    4. Let the buyer receive the gift as extra value on top of the main purchase. [L1223.032]
  • Gift-with-purchase value: a low-cost gift with purchase can drive pre-Christmas orders. The lesson's example was a roughly $80 iPod shipped to the buyer's address on top of a high-ticket purchase. The buyer sees the gift as extra value, and the seller pays only the gift and shipping cost. The gift should be desirable to the buyer and worth it to the seller at the chosen spend level. [L1223.016]
  • Check regularly that every order includes the free gift. A free gift that is not added to some orders creates refund and support problems. [L1173.023]
  • Thresholds such as $2,000 apply to the trigger for the Zapier automation step. [L0358.033]

Dropship SKUs and automation

  • Setting up a free-gift or buy-one-get-one offer with a dedicated supplier SKU: [L1428.020]
    1. Ask the supplier, for example on CJ, to create a variant that combines two items under one SKU.
    2. On the supplier's product page, choose the two-item variant. The total dropshipping price is shown at the bottom.
    3. Copy the SKU number of that variant.
    4. Add the variant to your store as the offer.
    5. Check that your margin covers the cost of two items before setting the price. With this setup the supplier ships both items on each order without any app. [L1428.020]
  • A temporary free-gift offer can use an automatic website discount instead, for example one free item when two are added to the cart. The supplier then ships each item separately, so the supplier must know the two items to ship. A permanent offer is better served by a dedicated SKU, because the supplier ships it automatically. [L1428.021]
  • Bonus setup steps: [L1293.033]
    1. List your top three bestsellers and the high-margin products you want to sell.
    2. For each, identify the accessory the buyer would normally need.
    3. Check the supplier's price list for cheap items you have not added to your store.
    4. Add the bonus to the supplier order confirmation, or to the SKU, so it ships with each order.
    5. Offer the bonus as a free gift once the purchase reaches the threshold. [L1293.033]

Shipping and compliance

  • Customers may receive the add-on and the main product on different days, so tell them in the order confirmation email. [L0577.011]
  • Free packaging, shipping, or extras that are not in the supplier's standard shipment need to be planned before launch so the order can be fulfilled. [L1173.023]

Digital versus physical performance

  • Physical product bonuses have historically delivered stronger conversion lift than digital bonuses. [L0314.001] (Source A; this is the source's general view.)
  • Digital bonuses underperform physical bonuses in conversion lift, based on practical experience. [L0314.009]
  • In one store, a digital bonus was tested as extra value but did not beat a relevant physical free gift. Prefer a physical gift that is closely related to the main product and its customer problem. [L1315.062]
  • Physical bonuses can change with the purchase month. Each monthly window gives a different item that fits the season, such as a summer ingredient pack in one month and an autumn pack in the next. Buyers get a new reason to act, and the bonus is something they would probably buy anyway. [L1204.071]
  • Physical bonus case: a merch store ran a five-day promotion in which every order over $50 received a free physical book. The offer raised average order value and turned old leads into customers. The bonus was given without any upsell and without extra charge. [L1205.014]
  • Test different bonus types and combinations with different products over time. Do not expect to find the perfect bonus strategy immediately. Continuously measure which bonuses increase conversion and refine based on sales data. [L0314.017]
  • Test whether the gift matches what the target buyer wants, and compare a physical gift with a digital gift on the same offer. [L1173.024, L0314.011]
  • In one store owner's experience, adding a bonus gift worth about $20 to the buyer tripled the conversion rate for the specific products it was attached to. The effect was tested on products the owner was already selling, not on new listings. [L1293.035]
  • The $20 bonus strategy: give a low-cost item with each purchase to convert buyers who are comparing with Amazon and competitors. The formula is a product-specific bonus that the buyer would normally need anyway, worth about $20 to the buyer but cheap to the seller. A $50 to $100 margin product still earns up to $80 after the bonus. The reason: it removes the need to compete on price and makes the offer more valuable than the competitor's. [L1293.025]
  • Scuba mask example: the bonus is a snorkel (wholesale about $5 to $10), offered free with any mask over $99. A podcast microphone can be bundled similarly with a stand, pop filter, and carrier case. A coffee maker example: a standard AeroPress versus a bundle that includes a reusable metal filter costing well under $1 to make, which the speaker chose over the competitor. [L1293.028]
  • Free gift value comparisons: a free gift with high perceived value often moves more customers than a slightly larger money-off discount. [L1112.068] (Source A, 2021: older advice.)
  • Premium and bonus guidance from Source A 2022: free gifts add perceived value without proportional cost. A guide valued at $19.99 costs the seller almost nothing to create, but the stated dollar value anchors customer perception of bundle worth. This is more effective than reducing the main product price by the same amount. [L0313.024]
  • Free bonuses in product bundles work because they increase perceived value without proportional cost. This is more effective than reducing the main product price by the same amount. [L0313.024]
  • A resistance band seller paired a $35.97 product with a $50 gift card for a partner weight-loss product, used to offer perceived value. The bonus was paired with an affiliate code so the seller could earn $50 to $150 when the buyer redeemed it. [L1153.031]
  • Physical bonuses should match the buyer's need; a bonus that only looks valuable can be a waste. [L0314.002]
  • Bonus items in a physical sense work when they directly address a real customer need related to the main product and cost much less than their apparent value to the buyer. Identify what customers need to use the main product effectively, then source an inexpensive version as a bonus. [L0314.002]
  • Home-brewing starter kit: include a free ingredient pack (enough for one small batch) with purchases. Wholesale cost is minimal, customers see it as essential to getting started, and they are more likely to buy from you than from competitors not offering it. [L0314.004]
  • Paddle boards with leashes: leashes cost $10-$15 each wholesale. Offering free leashes with board purchases provides genuine customer value while protecting margins. [L0314.005]
  • Chandeliers priced at $1,000: offer a free pack of light bulbs with a wholesale cost of $20. Customers need bulbs and perceive high retail value. If competitors do not offer this, you gain a clear advantage. [L0314.006]
  • Leather sofas: offer a free leather protectant to help customers maintain their purchase. This addresses post-purchase care concerns and adds perceived value without harming margins. [L0314.007]
  • Treadmills: offer physical bonuses such as a free water bottle, towels, or apparel, or digital bonuses such as a free meal plan or an e-book on running technique. Test which types customers value most. [L0314.011]
  • Free gift value: a bonus gift can also be a physical gift paired with a larger order. [L0999.064]
  • Digital bonus versus physical gift: test both on the same product before choosing. [L0314.017]
  • Airplane-style or large product purchases: the buyer may prefer a physical gift they need. [L0314.002]
  • A physical gift that is cheap to print and delivered in the parcel can increase return visits and referrals. [L1437.005]
  • Digital bonuses can be used when they are partnered with an external provider, so the store does not need to create the content. [L0314.009]
  • Free ebook as a loss-prevention bonus: a free ebook on a related topic is a low-cost bonus that can be delivered automatically after purchase. [L1184.048]
  • A physical bonus would not be justified if the margin is too thin. [L0941.108]
  • White-glove delivery: offer it free as a bonus for high-ticket items ($2,000 or above), where margins are strong and most competitors offer only curbside delivery. White-glove delivery costs an extra $100-$150 per order. [L0314.012]
  • Do not offer expensive bonuses ($100-$150 upgrades) across your entire site. Target them to your highest-margin products or those priced $2,000 or above to protect overall profitability. [L0314.013]

Common mistakes

  • Do not choose bonus items that carry high wholesale costs or threaten your profit margins. Bonuses are incentives, not revenue-builders. [L0314.015]
  • Offering a free ebook that is unrelated to the product is weak. The bonus should be directly linked to the product's use or benefit. [L0577.016]
  • Filler chapters and placeholder text make the bonus look cheap and weaken the offer. [L0860.015]
  • Free gifts that are not added to some orders create refund and support problems. Check regularly that every order includes the free gift. [L1173.023]
  • Customers who do not know about the gift may not add the product. Show the gift clearly on the product page and in the cart. [L1315.032]
  • Do not promise a bonus that is not delivered automatically after checkout. [L1188.018]
  • Bonus delivery must not be left to customers to claim through extra steps. [L1110.030] (Source A, 2019: older advice.)
  • Custom packaging made for one product is expensive. Only use custom add-ons if the supplier already has the item in stock. [L0577.017]

Further details

  • Free gifts like 'Free mystery gift when you spend $X' or complementary items (travel bag with hair dryer bundle) increase perceived value at zero cost and make higher-price tiers more attractive. [L0232.018]

  • A low-cost bonus closely tied to the main product, such as a recipe book included with an air fryer, can raise perceived value even when it costs little to give. The bonus works best when the buyer would naturally want it alongside that product. [L0411.036]

  • Lead magnet test: 1) Keep product and price unchanged. 2) Add a free gift to the order. 3) If conversion does not rise, switch to a different gift every second day (e.g., Day 1 ring, Day 2 necklace, Day 3 ebook). 4) Keep the gift that produces the most sales; the winning gifts can suggest future bundles. [L0773.016]

Test a free gift as a lead magnet while keeping the same product and price:

  1. Add a free gift to the product, starting with a low-cost item that fits the product, such as a yoga mat with a 29.99 USD stretching band, which sharply raised conversion in one case.
  2. If conversion does not rise, change the gift every two days.
  3. Rotate options in order, for example a ring first, then a necklace, then an e-book.
  4. Keep the gift that produces the most sales; the winning gift also shows which bundle idea to test next. [L0774.013]
