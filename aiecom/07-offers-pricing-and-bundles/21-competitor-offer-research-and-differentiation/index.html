7.21 Competitor offer research and differentiation

Why competitor research comes first

  • Skipping competitor research leads to guessing about differentiation, because you cannot judge whether your offer is unique until you know the offers competitors already run and that your target customers already buy. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0898.003]
  • A quality offer is one that customers perceive as unique, or at least partly unique, compared with what competitors sell in the same market. Perceived uniqueness counts, not the seller's own view of the product. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0898.002]
  • The offer does not need to be the best in the world, but it must be better than what competitors offer, ideally clearly better, because buyers can open several competitor tabs at once and a weaker offer is easy to spot. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0408.008]
  • Competitors selling the same product should not stop you from selling it. Victory comes from a superior offer structure (bundled bonuses, guarantees, benefit-focused naming) and faster fulfillment, not from product exclusivity. With a strong offer and operations, you outperform competitors with identical products but weak offers. [L0233.021]

Identifying real competitors

  • Do not treat products sold by low-volume one-off sellers, such as many Etsy shops, as direct competitors, because they usually have no large inventory. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0968.083]
  • If competitors sell near Amazon prices, they are often Chinese sellers. Avoid very large brands, but small competitors whose ads get about 500-3,000 likes or engagements can be out-marketed. [L1217.061]
  • When competitors buy cheap and fulfill through AliExpress with very low margins, the same product can often be sold at a higher price. Their short-lived stores do not build a brand, which leaves room for a stronger store. [L1171.027]

Building the competitor offer map

Core rule and procedure

  • Compare your current offer with the offers of competitors, using the same offer elements, to see where you stand in the market. Where you are behind, improve the offer first and record the changes in the curve offer builder. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0399.006]

Procedure (older advice, 2021-22):

  1. Pull out the competitor map you built earlier, with each competitor's offer and USP, and review it. If it does not exist yet, first run a competitor-research process and fill it in.
  2. Compare your current offer with the competitors' offers, using the same offer elements, and judge where you stand in your market.
  3. Where you are behind, improve your offer toward the strongest curve offer you can and record the changes in the curve offer builder. [L0399.021]

Comparing offer elements

Multi-buy tiers

  • A competitor used tiered offers: buy 2 get 1 free for visible improvement with free shipping, and buy 3 get 2 free for real results with a free post-serum jelly. Free shipping applied to orders over $40. [L0374.026]
  • Competitor stores commonly use multi-buy offers such as buy 2 get 1 free and buy 3 get 2 free, as well as buy 2 get 2 free and buy 3 get 3 free for longer supplies. Test which tier structure sells best before expanding it. [L0600.018]
  • Bundle offers seen in the field include:
    • buy 1 get 1 free (2 bottles in total)
    • buy 2 get 2 free (4 bottles in total)
    • buy 3 get 3 free (6 bottles in total)
    • a two-bottle pack with free bonus champagne [L1164.039]
  • Buy-one-get-one-free offers appear on competitor stores and are used in the fashion niche as a visible hook, which can be tested in a similar form. [L1161.032]
  • Common competitor offers include buy one get one free. A stronger differentiator is a free ebook or guide given with the purchase. Offers should be paired with an authority signal to make the store look more credible. [L0335.042]

Subscriptions

  • Competitor stores offer an automatic refill or subscription option with a saving of around 20 to 25 percent. Show the saving next to the subscription option so it is clearly compared to the one-time price. [L0600.019]
  • A competitor's serum page combined multi-unit tiers (Buy 2 Get 1 FREE, Buy 3 Get 2 FREE) with an Auto-Refill subscription that saves 25 percent and a 90-day guarantee. Multi-unit tiers plus a subscription discount are a common structure to model when pricing a bundle. [L0772.026]
  • The same competitor's tiered offer also included a monthly auto-refill at 25% off with cancel anytime. [L0374.026]

Shipping thresholds and delivery

  • Competitors often advertise free shipping only above a threshold, such as $49. If you offer free shipping on all orders, show that as the main message and place an expiring coupon code in the header bar above it. [L0894.017]
  • Estimated ship date and delivery window are easy to add to product pages and can separate you from a competitor that never mentions arrival time. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0243.012]
  • A competitor charged $150 for white glove delivery and $99 for Room and Choice service, while basic threshold delivery (delivered off the truck) was included. [L0894.018]
  • A $1,500 chandelier with free white glove delivery beats a competitor that adds $150 for the same service. Shoppers comparing both sites will choose the store with the free option. [L0894.019]
  • If margins allow, include white glove free on selected products. Otherwise sell it as a paid premium upgrade. [L0894.018]

Guarantees and warranties

  • A competitor's serum page paired its add-to-cart button with a visible 90-day guarantee and a free-shipping message, both placed near the price. Placing the risk reversal next to the buy button is a structure worth copying when you test a product page. [L0772.048]
  • If a competitor mentions a one-year warranty but does not emphasize it, highlight the warranty prominently. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0243.012]
  • Guarantees are part of a superior offer structure that competitors selling identical products often lack. [L0233.021]

Bonuses, accessories and gifts

  • Include accessories such as light bulbs as a free gift when the supplier provides them at a good price per order. Free inclusion beats a competitor that says the item is not included. [L0894.034]
  • Bonus items such as wrist rest pads with desk purchases are a way to add value. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0243.012]
  • If a competitor's top sellers in Google Shopping have similar photos, plain websites, a bundle and a pop-up, you can beat them with an offer they lack, such as a free extra accessory or a free gift on the first order. [L1508.033]

Price, total cost and margin

  • A competitor's low item price may be offset by a separate shipping charge, so compare the total cost at checkout rather than the displayed product price. Otherwise the margin estimate for a cheap product can be wrong. [L0655.026]
  • A competitor listed a product below MAP. Compare the full price a customer would pay, including sales tax, shipping and any discount the competitor adds. A lower listed price can still end up higher than yours at checkout, which changes whether the listing is a real threat. [L0470.103]

Discounts

  • When a competitor runs a discount on the same product, beat it with a stronger offer, such as moving from 50 percent off to 70 percent off, raising the compare-at price, or adding a buy-one-get-one-free deal. Keep the ad copy the same while improving the offer. [L1382.073]
  • If a competitor offers 50 percent off, beat it with 70 percent off, buy-one-get-one-free, or a higher compare-at price so that the offer looks larger. [L1436.045]
  • In the most competitive product brands, find out which discounts your top competitors give and be ready to match them. Experts disagree on best practice for discounts, so the right level depends on your specific competition. [L1357.007]

Customization

  • Products with customization options can sell at higher prices, and competitors often do not build the full options. This can be a differentiator for a store. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0967.073]

Verifying competitor prices

  • The same product showed different prices across one brand's pages and ads, so check the price on each page and each ad before you rely on it. A two-formulation bundle priced under $40 was promoted with free shipping on TikTok, and the bundle price was a repeated USP. [L0374.028]
  • The hosts recalled a starting price of about $48 for a blender but were unsure whether the price had gone up or down as the product became popular, so verify exact price history before relying on it. [L0657.002]
  • Price points from research as examples:
    • a nostalgia gift toy at $39.99 USD or CHF 34.99 with a buy-one-get-one-50%-off offer
    • a customized sister jewelry gift at $19.99 with 148 active ads running in Canada, Australia and the US [L1185.008]

Finding gaps

Feature gaps

Pattern (older advice, 2021-22):

  • Feature-gap differentiation: identify a need that recurs in customer feedback and that no competitor addresses, then build the product around solving it.
  • Pattern: find the single complaint or request mentioned repeatedly that the only available option does not solve, and make the core of your version the solution.
  • Example 1: a camping stove seller notices customers complain about bulk, so they launch a compact version that packs into a single pouch.
  • Example 2: a kitchen scale seller hears that users want to weigh liquids without a cup, so they launch a scale with a tare-friendly, removable bowl. [L1155.012]

Competitor weak spots

  • Competitors without promotions, urgency, bundles or bonus offers leave room to win. [L0894.016]
  • When a competitor offers free shipping on heavy or large items, check how they make money on it by reviewing their individual reviews, since some competitors lose money without knowing it. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0967.074]
  • Read competitor reviews to see why a competitor's price is low. [L0358.015]

Creating a unique offer

Improve the offer, not only the price

  • Differentiate from competitors by improving their offer: provide lower pricing, bundle multiple units, or reframe the product message for new use cases. [L0209.034]
  • To beat a competitor in a crowded niche, improve the offer rather than only the price. Keep the same product and add value such as a free gift or a lower price with an extra item. [L1220.036]
  • Example: a competitor sells a necklace at 40 USD. A competitor-beating offer might be 30 USD plus a free ring. [L1220.036]
  • Improve the offer to compete in a new niche, such as jewelry, instead of copying competitors. Offer more value for the same or less money, for example a lower price with a free extra item. [L1220.037]
  • A competitor charges 40 USD for a necklace. Undercutting to 30 USD and adding a free ring is an offer that positions the store above the competition. [L1220.038]
  • Check what competitors offer before setting up your own bundles, so your offers are not weaker than the market standard. [L1299.006]

Brand value

  • If the same product is sold cheaper on Amazon, a brand-owned store can still charge more by adding complementary items or a stronger offer. One seller reported pricing about 15 USD above the Amazon average and still selling well. Perceived value and branding are the levers, not matching the lowest price. [L1428.019]

Tested differentiation

  • Customized offers that competitors do not make are a stronger way to stand out than free shipping, free white-glove delivery, bonus gifts or 10% off. They should be tested and measured before wider rollout. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1086.004]

Avoiding easily copied terms

  • Avoid offers that let competitors copy the same terms, such as matching guarantees or undercutting, because the advantage disappears when rivals adopt them. Find an offer structure that is hard to match. [L1261.103]
  • Free shipping, free white-glove delivery, bonus gifts or 10% off are easy for competitors to copy and do not create separation. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1086.004]
  • Shoppers often value a strong bonus offer more than a competitor's longer history, so compete on the offer rather than on store age. [L0857.025]
  • When supplier copies happen, run the same offer with suppliers that do not sell direct, or build a more premium bundle with extras that the supplier does not offer. Offers cannot be copyrighted, so differentiate with better bundles. [L1204.059]

Keeping the map current

  • Expect competitors to answer a successful store with bundles, packages, or a redesigned site. Plan periodic reviews of offers and design so the store does not lose ground while it is being run. [L0826.025]

Responding when competitors sell at MAP or below

Competitors sell at the same MAP price

  • When competitors all sell at the same MAP price, price cannot be the reason a shopper chooses you. Win on services, shipping, included accessories, bonuses and clear product information instead. [L0894.043]
  • When selling at MAP, stand out through bonus offers and expiring coupon codes. These create differentiation without breaking the MAP policy. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0967.068]
  • When a competitor sells the same product at the same price, add a bonus offer that the shopper values highly to win the sale. [L0857.024]

Competitors sell below MAP or non-MAP, and you are on MAP

  • If a competitor sells a product below the MAP or non-MAP price, do not match them on price, because that leads to a race to the bottom. Revisit your own numbers for room to offer a special deal, and research the competitor's reviews to see why their price is low. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0358.015]
  • If a non-MAP competitor sells the same product for less, do not start a price war. Run paid traffic only on products where the margin remains healthy, and send free traffic to the products where competitors undercut you. [L0835.054]
  • When a competitor sells below your landed cost, their per-sale costs (card processing fees, paid traffic, hosting, and staff for live chat, phone and email) are usually higher than your margin. Their price most likely reflects cheaper sourcing or shipping. Ask your supplier about better pricing before you react with a price cut. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1002.078]

Retailer breaks MAP and the manufacturer does not enforce it

Options (older advice, 2021-22):

  1. Document that you have followed the MAP policy and raised the issue with the manufacturer on several occasions.
  2. Remove the product from your store and sell alternatives from other suppliers with better margins and support, if the gap is too large to compete with.
  3. Or match the lower price to stay on a level playing field, accepting that the retailer may drop you from its supply.
  4. Or, instead of bidding harder in Google Shopping, drive more organic or other traffic to that product page. [L0879.039]
