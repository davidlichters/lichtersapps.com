7.20 Writing and displaying the offer

Offer copy formulas and patterns

  • Make the offer explicit in ad copy. Generic offers such as "50% off + free shipping" are now expected and will underperform. The offer directly affects both CTR and CVR, so choose an enticing, differentiated offer. [L0206.006]
  • Use one offer per ad variant so the results can be compared. [L0392.010]
  • Most-aware customers already want the product and only need a reason to buy now. Push a direct offer (percentage discount, free gift or promotion) with a clear buy-now action. Extra explanation is usually unnecessary at this stage. [L0516.010]
  • For a prospect who already knows the product and wants it, the headline needs only the product name and a bargain price, with the price as the news. The body summarises the strongest points and ends with a store name or coupon and a call to buy. This is a mechanical job and should be paid accordingly. [L0330.020]
  • Value equation: value rises when the dream outcome and the perceived likelihood of achieving it go up, and when the time, effort and sacrifice needed to get there go down. Copy should raise the first two and shrink the last three at the same time. [L0516.014]
  • Higher-priced products need more copy to justify the price and create the need. A low price that looks like clear value needs less copy. A low price that seems too cheap to be credible needs more copy to build trust. [L1366.055]
  • Vivid, detailed pictures of the satisfaction a product gives reduce how much the buyer cares about the price. The more concrete scenes of use you can present without repeating yourself, the stronger the demand and the weaker the price resistance. [L0330.101]
  • If a product seems to need several units to be useful, such as a small accessory a buyer might think only needs one, frame it as a gift for someone else to remove that objection. Gift framing can support single-unit purchases alongside multi-unit offers. [L1172.055]
  • Offer call-to-action formula: pair one concrete price benefit, such as a percentage discount, with a second version that gives a free gift with purchase today. A clear, time-bound incentive turns interest into action. [L0392.010]
    • Examples: "Get your set at 25% off before the weekend ends." and "Order today and get a free travel case with your purchase." [L0392.010]
    • The example call to action used a 40% discount on a pair of the product, and a second version offered a free gift with purchase on the same day. [L0392.011]
  • Stacking pattern: stack a percentage discount, a free bonus and a shipping or time condition in one line, so the buyer who already knows the product gets more than one reason to act now.
    • Formula: discount % + bonus item + condition (free shipping, online only, or a deadline). [L0794.055]
    • Examples: "Take 30% off today and get a travel case free with your first order." and "Save 15%, ship free, and add a refill pack at no cost, online orders this weekend only." [L0794.055]
  • Spend-threshold headline formula: Save $AMOUNT on all orders over $THRESHOLD! It works because it states the exact saving and the condition to unlock it in one line, so the shopper knows what to spend. [L0757.019]
    • Examples: "Save $25 on all orders over $200!" and "Save $80 on every order over $400!" [L0757.019]
  • Seasonal sale formula: [season or event] sale: free shipping plus [discount percentage] off with code [short word tied to the store theme]. The event name creates timing, free shipping removes the cost objection, and a concrete percentage with a themed code makes the offer specific and memorable. [L1325.008]
    • Examples: "Summer sale: free shipping plus 20% off with code SURFSIDE" and "Winter sale: free shipping plus 25% off with code FROSTBITE". [L1325.008]
  • Dynamic day coupon text formula: a short call to action that names the discount code, states the percentage off and ends with a day name that limits the offer. The code, the saving and the deadline are visible in one line, which lowers hesitation at checkout. [L1450.015]
    • Examples: "Enter SPRING15 now and save 15% through Sunday only." and "Use code BREW20 today to get 20% off until Friday only." [L1450.015]
  • Bundle title pattern: name the outcome the bundle gives and the exact saving in the same line, so the discount is visible before the shopper reaches the cart. [L0559.020]
    • Examples: "Start your morning and save 12%" and "Build your tea set and save 15%". [L0559.020]
  • Combined value formula: show the combined value of the items, then the bundle price, then the saving in dollars or percent. Buyers judge a deal by the amount they save, so a visible saving makes an add-on feel almost free. [L1260.064]
    • Examples: "Complete the set: total value 74.99, today 59.99, you save 15." and "Add the second item and save 20% on the pair, shipped to one address." [L1260.064]
  • Keep the offer simple. An offer that confuses visitors hurts sales more than it helps. Use one clear offer with a simple structure that people can follow straight through to checkout. [L1017.024]

Offer naming

  • Magic Offer naming structure: MAGNET + AVATAR + GOAL + INTERVAL + CONTAINER. You do not have to use all five elements. [L0549.023]
    • MAGNET is the hook, such as free, giveaway, percent off, spring, summer, new or holiday. [L0549.023]
    • AVATAR is the target customer or interest. [L0549.023]
    • GOAL is the problem solved or benefit, such as pain free, stress relief, hair cure or thicker hair. [L0549.023]
    • INTERVAL is the time to the result, such as 5 minutes, 30 days, 21 days or 6 weeks. [L0549.023]
    • CONTAINER is the format, such as bundle, program, shortcut, rebirth, attack, blueprint, sprint, catapult, comeback pack or journey. [L0549.023]
  • Keep offer names short and punchy. Words that rhyme or start with the same letter sound better, and the name should signal the target customer, the problem solved and how quickly the result comes. [L0549.024]
  • Example of a combined offer name using an interval, a goal and a container: "five minute hair enhancer bundle." [L0549.026]

Ad copy and headlines

  • Ice Cap migraine relief ad: 25% discount plus free shipping. It uses multiple short testimonials for social proof ("Finally real relief without meds", "I feel in control again", "Stops my migraines fast"), a pain-point opening ("Sick of the migraines?"), and emphasises 50K+ users relieved. Holistic positioning ("wholistic and calming solution") appeals to health-conscious customers. [L0313.013]
  • Ice Cap pricing: 25% discount plus free shipping, with the exact price not shown in ads. This suggests a sufficient profit margin to absorb shipping cost while keeping margin on the 25% discount. [L0313.018]
  • RYZe Mushroom Hot Cocoa ad: single image with minimal copy ("NO MORE SLEEPLESS NIGHTS"), an ingredient list with benefits (Reishi for stress relief, Melatonin for sleep support, etc.) and a simple CTA ("TRY IT"). This format works for supplement and wellness products where ingredient proof is strong enough without testimonials. [L0313.017]
  • Bottom-of-funnel static ads for offers use 25% or 50% discounts (BWF ads). They need little research because the offer itself carries the message. [L1195.043]
  • Point the ads at the single collection where a buy-X-get-Y offer applies, so the ad message and the discount match. In the example the offer needed two items from that one collection, and the free item also came from it. [L0650.067]
  • Cut any offer shown in a copied video that does not appear on your landing page, such as buy one get one free, so the ad promise matches the store. [L0704.007]
  • AI-generated product names and descriptions should not be copied and pasted without editing. The output needs human judgement and split testing before it goes live. [L0683.021]
  • Full buyer guides target people who are not ready to buy, so create them only after ads are profitable and dialled in. Comparison charts are the better early option for buyers who are ready to purchase. (Older advice, Source A 2020.) [L0664.055]
  • Compare discounted and undiscounted listings side by side. A product shown with a discount in an ad usually gets a much higher click-through rate than the same product at MSRP beside it. (Older advice, Source A 2021.) [L0671.058]
  • Rule for discount claims in ads and banners: state the exact percentage, the product scope and whether a promo code is needed. List any excluded categories or products, and never show a discount larger than the one you actually run. [L0972.102]

TikTok

  • Buyers arriving from TikTok rarely buy a regular-priced product, so the offer must be strong. Show the offer in the ad text or within the first three seconds of the video. [L1382.016]
  • TikTok buyers expect a deal before they consider the product, so show the offer in the ad text or within the first three seconds of the video. [L1436.041]

Sale banners and product page graphics

  • A product page graphic sits right above or below the add-to-cart button and is used for sales or urgency. It can be removed, changed or swapped out with the season, and works best with a promo code and an expiry date. [L0342.030]
  • Sale banner formula: a large heading with the discount percentage, a line showing the coupon code as the action to take, and a small expiry line in a smaller font. Use white text on a bright background that matches the brand colours. [L0342.031]
    • Examples: "Summer Clearance: 15% Off All Boards" with "Use code SUMMER15 at checkout" and "Ends Sunday", and "Flash Sale: 20% Off Gear" with "Enter code FLASH20 at checkout" and "Expires Friday 11:59 PM". [L0342.031]
  • A product page sale graphic should show the offer, the coupon code, where the code is entered and, optionally, an expiry date to create urgency. Keep the wording short so it reads in a second. [L1045.026]
  • Sale graphic formula: a bold headline with the offer and the discount, a line with the coupon code, a line saying where to apply the code (such as at checkout), and an optional smaller line with an expiry date. The buyer sees the offer, the code and the next action together, right next to the buy button. [L1045.027]
    • Example 1: "Spring deal: take 20% off every order / Use code SPRING20 / Apply at checkout / Ends 31 May." [L1045.027]
    • Example 2: "Free gift with every order / Add code WELCOME at checkout / Limited to this month." [L1045.027]
  • Do not put a sale graphic over a product image in Google Shopping ads, because the ad can be disapproved. (See Google Shopping and promotion display.) [L0470.063]
  • Use a product-page pop-up for items with long lead times, such as six to ten weeks. The pop-up tells the visitor the wait before they add the product to the cart and asks them to confirm before they continue, which prevents disappointed customers later. [L1472.041]
  • Use "Add-on" or "Bundle" imagery: create an image in Canva (for example with the "product offer" template) showing the main product with the free bonus, and place it in the product photo gallery as the second image. [L0577.014]
  • On the product page, show the main item in the default photo and display the add-on items being worn or used by the model in other photos. The upsells are visible without cluttering the main gallery. [L0838.013]

Procedure: unique offer template for one product (Shopify)

  1. In Shopify, go to Online Store > Themes and click Customize. [L0667.008]
  2. Open the product template dropdown, choose Products, and select the default product page template. [L0667.008]
  3. Click Create template, name it for the offer (for example "product page 50 off"), and base it on the default product page. [L0667.008]
  4. Click the expiring discount code block, change its text to the new offer (for example "$50 off and free shipping"), then save. [L0667.008]
  5. Go to Products, open the target product, and in the right sidebar under Theme template change the default template to the new one. [L0667.008]
  6. Save and preview to confirm the unique coupon shows only on that product. [L0667.008]
  • A template assigned to zero products is not visible to shoppers. [L0667.008]

Procedure: free-gift banner on a product page

  1. Design a free-gift banner image that matches the site's layout and colours. [L0667.034]
  2. Show the product image, the product name and its value, for example with a message that it ships free with the order today. [L0667.034]
  3. Place the banner above the product description, close to the add-to-cart button, so every visitor sees it. [L0667.034]
  4. To insert it, press Enter above the description in the product page editor, click insert image, then save. [L0667.034]

Announcement bars and site-wide offers

  • A store-wide discount code can be placed in the announcement bar at the top of the site. A code that applies only to specific products is shown on those products' pages through a dedicated product page template. [L0667.090]
  • A promotion bar at the top of the homepage that offers free shipping plus a percentage discount with a code gives visitors a reason to act sooner. The reviewed example suggested free shipping plus 15% off during a seasonal sale. [L1325.007]
  • Announcement bar and exit-intent discounts can be swapped for free shipping offers when the margin does not allow a discount. [L0855.051]
  • Use announcement bars to explain promotion rules where shoppers are focused, for example to say a free gift applies without discount codes. Announcements can also link to collections or blog pages using inline HTML hyperlinks. [L1231.024]
  • Show the offer (what exactly the customer receives) near the top of the page, because visitors want to know what they are getting before they read further. [L1363.005]
  • Homepage sections can be added and filled later to highlight product promotions. Start with only the basic homepage blocks at launch and add promotional sections as campaigns require. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1371.051]
  • Show the offer, the price and the call to action on the homepage, product pages and About Us page at minimum. The curve offer can also appear on the cart page, in emails and in ads, but it must always be clearly visible. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0399.007]
  • Give customers with a specific need a direct link from the product page to a matching collection, such as a sensitive-skin collection introduced with the question "Sensitive skin?". This keeps the main product page focused while still catching niche buyers. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0409.017]

Trust rows and reassurance

  • Pre-footer trust-building headlines: Free Shipping $100+ (incentive), Surfer Owned & Operated (authenticity), Fast & Secure Checkout (convenience), Easy Returns (confidence). [L0101.014]
  • Example trust row from a reseller homepage, placed right below the header, with four points:
    • Free Shipping ("all the time, every time") [L0409.045]
    • Give Us a Call (phone number shown) [L0409.045]
    • 30 Day Easy Returns ("don't like or need it, send it back") [L0409.045]
    • Price Match ("find a better price, we'll beat it") [L0409.045]
    • (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.)
  • Show free shipping on orders over $50 and a 90-day 100% money-back guarantee in the offer area of the product page. [L1164.041]
  • Free-shipping promotions can be shown on product pages with images and graphics. The shipping option itself appears in the cart. (Older advice, Source A 2022.) [L0963.070]
  • Show free extras such as white glove delivery or bonus gifts on the product page, the same way you show bonus gifts, so buyers see the full value of the offer. Free white glove is worth offering only when the product price is high enough that the margin covers its cost. (Older advice, Source A 2020.) [L0961.007]
  • Note: a no-sales-tax policy can be shown in ads by default if competitors add sales tax and yours does not. Mention the policy on product pages or in the header announcement bar, not in ads. [L1094.079]

Sales page structure (long-form offer)

  1. Reveal the special report as the main offer. [L1365.040]
  2. Introduce the subscription service, under which the report is received when the prospect joins. [L1365.040]
  3. Show what others say, using testimonials for the service. [L1365.040]
  4. Use a false close that anchors a high price. [L1365.040]
  5. Present extra bonuses and show how each one has value. [L1365.040]
  6. Show the value stack with the total value of everything. [L1365.040]
  7. Cut the value down and reveal the final price. [L1365.040]
  8. Add a guarantee as the risk reversal. [L1365.040]

Price display and savings math

  • Compare-at price display: show the higher compare-at price (for example 79.99 USD) struck through, show the sale price (for example 39.99 USD) in a larger font, add a "50% OFF" label, and optionally add a time-limited scarcity line such as "Limited time" or an event sale. Page builders such as PageFly or GemPages allow custom background colours for this highlight. [L1259.027]
  • Highlight the discount in the price block in this order: product name first, then the compare-at price, then the sale price in a larger font, then the text "50% off". An event or time-limited scarcity line such as "limited for today" or a seasonal sale can be added. [L1345.017]
  • Visitors and shoppers read from left to right, so the first price they see should be the higher reference price, and the real sale price should be larger. The contrast between the two numbers makes the saving visible at a glance. [L1260.062, L1345.019]
  • Caution on heavy percentage banners: avoid heavy 50 percent off banners with limited-time urgency, because many consumers now distrust them. Show clear prices and present the store as a trusted brand instead. [L1251.043]
    • Reconciling sources: the "50% OFF" label in the price block is used in [L1259.027] and [L1345.017], while [L1251.043] warns against heavy 50% banners. Use the label with clear, real prices and a restrained banner; reserve heavy urgency banners for offers you can defend. [L1251.043, L1259.027, L1345.017]
  • In an offer lead, state the regular price, the special price and the dollar saving together, for example a $59 cover price offered at $19 saves $40. The dollar amount makes the value concrete for the reader. [L0756.076]
  • Shopify savings label: enter the sale price and the MSRP (compare-at price) in Shopify. Example: a sale price of $800 and an MSRP of $900 show the $900 struck through, $800 as the sale price, and a "Save $100" label, displayed automatically on every product page without extra work. [L1130.018]
  • Do not list the sale price alone as the minimum advertised price. Always show the discount against the MSRP, and keep the expiring coupon promotion visible in addition to it. [L1130.019]
  • Listing only the sale price gives visitors no sense of how much they save. This weakens conversion even when the price is competitive. [L1130.020]
  • Keep the time-limited coupon promotion in a separate banner on the product page, above the price, rather than merging it into the MSRP display. Visitors can then see the MSRP saving and the extra coupon saving at the same time. [L1130.035]
  • Price-tag caution for supplier pricing: with MAP suppliers, the displayed price cannot be lowered. Use the Google Shopping promotion feature to show a discount tag, and apply the actual reduction with a coupon code at checkout. (Older advice, Source A 2022.) [L1092.053]
  • Higher-priced products and sale copy: a free gift or discount described on the page should match the price logic. Free white glove and similar extras only pay when the margin covers their cost. [L0961.007]

Keeping the math clear

  • Calculate the exact saving before you write it on the store. The 20% example given for two 34.99 USD items does not work out to a 10 USD saving; 20% off 69.98 USD is about 14 USD. A wrong figure damages trust. [L0774.015]
  • Savings label formula for a compare-at price: (compare-at price minus sale price) divided by compare-at price, then rounded. Example: a $69.99 compare-at price with a $49.99 sale price gives (69.99 minus 49.99) divided by 69.99, which is about 28.6% and is shown as "SAVE 29%". [L1188.016]
  • Savings label calculation: MSRP (compare-at price) minus sale price. Example: a sale price of $1,199.99 with an MSRP of $1,299.99 displays "Save $100" automatically on the product page. [L1130.034]
  • Discount email and checkout copy must match the math: state the exact percentage and the expiry, and never promise more than the store runs. [L0972.102, L1188.016]
  • Discount claims in visuals and text must match the stored discount exactly. [L0972.102]

Bundles and add-ons

  • Bundles and free add-ons make the customer feel they receive several items for the price of one, which lifts offer appeal. Make bundles visible on the site as a bundle block, dropdown or image gallery. [L0577.013]
  • In a bulleted value recap, place a small product image beside each bullet rather than text alone, so the stack of items looks physically substantial. Show each item's dollar value next to its image so the total is easy to follow at a glance. [L1356.075]
  • Merchandising matters: the order in which products appear on the site influences results. Keep the offer simple and the website design clear so customers understand how the offer works, because confusing offers fail even if they are strong. [L1404.031]

Emails for offers

  • Pattern for the discount email: state the action and the exact percentage, then the code and the expiry time.
    • Examples of my own: "Save 10% on your bag, valid until midnight tonight" and "Take 20% off your basket before the timer hits zero". [L0567.026]
  • Do not upsell an abandoned cart by default. When a discount cuts your margin, add a best-seller product block to the offer email as an AOV booster. The block lets the shopper add items with one click and updates the cart. [L0567.029]

Cart and checkout messages

  • Cart message for a pre-purchase return option. Replace the placeholders, then show the message at the bottom of the cart with a link to a short details panel that explains the fee. [L0955.050]

Google Shopping and promotion display

  • Google Shopping promotions can show offers such as free shipping or a discount code. Do not put a sale graphic over the product image, because the ad can be disapproved. [L0470.063]
  • A Merchant Center promotion displays the offer in the Shopping ad, where the shopper can hover over or click it to see the promotion details. Shopping ads get the same extra incentive that promotion extensions give search ads. [L0712.014]
  • Promotion extensions show a sale offer, such as a Black Friday sale, directly under the search ad, giving searchers an extra incentive to visit the site. The lesson calls them a strong way to increase CTR and notes the feature was recently rolled out, so check its current availability in Google Ads. [L0712.011]
  • Google Merchant Center promotions: add a promotion such as a 5 percent off coupon code in Merchant Center. Google then shows a promo tab on your Google Shopping ad with the promotion details, which can increase click-through rate. (Older advice, Source A 2021.) [L0801.054]
  • Price-tag caution for MAP suppliers: see the MAP note under price display. [L1092.053]

Shopify setup and store-wide settings

  • A store-wide discount code can be placed in the announcement bar. A product-specific code is shown through a dedicated product page template. [L0667.090]
  • Use the product template procedure above for a unique offer on one product. [L0667.008]
  • Use the free-gift banner procedure above for a free gift on a product page. [L0667.034]

Further details

  • A free product offer that requires a $9.95 shipping and handling payment, placed prominently on the homepage, does not fit a store selling gifts and undermines trust. Review offers like this for fairness and fit before publishing them. [L0355.020]
