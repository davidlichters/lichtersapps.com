8.6 Naming the business and brand

Core criteria for the store name

  • Keep the name to three words or fewer, because shorter names are easier to remember and better for branding. [L0336.006]
  • For a general store, aim for 5 to 10 letters that are easy to say and spell. Run the radio test: a listener should be able to hear the name once and spell it correctly. [L0989.003]
  • Make the name short, memorable and easy to say, without hyphens. Including the niche helps but is not required. [L0468.104] (Source A, 2020, older advice)
  • Make the name short, memorable and easy to recall so customers can find you again. Avoid random letters or numbers, and make the name sensible. [L1109.062] (Source A, 2018, older advice)
  • Say the candidate aloud in normal conversation and imagine someone repeating it to a friend. A good name is one people naturally reference when they talk about the brand. [L0990.013]
  • Keep the name neutral or positive. Only avoid names that are outwardly negative or offensive, because the actions, service and conduct of the business shape brand perception far more than the name does. [L0990.001, L0990.011]
  • Do not give the name a deliberately misspelled or creative spelling, such as Kandi instead of Candy. Suppliers and customers see it as unprofessional, and people who search from memory type the correct spelling and miss the store. [L0336.007]
  • Spell every word as it appears in the dictionary. In a non-English market, use the correct spelling in the local language. [L0336.008]
  • Choose a name that is clear and simple, not clever. It does not need a logical link to the products, because many successful companies have names unrelated to what they sell. [L0336.010]
  • Avoid frivolous or overly unique names for a serious product, since they damage its perceived seriousness. Choose a name that suits the seriousness of the purchase. [L1261.109]
  • Keep the store name neutral and not vague, and do not tie it to the owner's personal name, because these stores are not meant to be personality-driven. [L0834.113] (Source A, 2021, older advice)
  • Time-box the naming decision. Overthinking delays the launch and has only a small effect on success. [L0336.011] (Source B, 2021-22, older advice)

Sound, meaning and tone

  • If the brand should mean something, the sound matters. Soft, rounded sounds suggest friendly, bubbly or feminine brands. Sharp, hard sounds suggest aggressive, extreme or masculine brands. Choose the phonetics deliberately to match the intended tone. [L0990.002]
  • Sound-shape associations are consistent across people. When asked to match a rounded shape and a spiky shape to the made-up words "bouba" and "kiki", most people pair rounded with "bouba" and spiky with "kiki". Soft-sounding names suggest rounded, gentle brands, and sharp-sounding names suggest angular, intense brands. [L0990.014]
  • Define the brand tone with specific adjectives before you brainstorm. Tone words such as playful, manly, competitive, moody, exciting, sweet, bubbly, feminine and aggressive each call for different sounds and word choices. [L0990.016]
  • Choose the name only after the niche and the initial product range are settled, so it reflects the market the store actually serves. [L0336.015] (Source B, 2021-22, older advice)
  • Avoid building the name around a word many competitors already use, such as "pro" in golf. Common niche words add little distinctiveness, so check how often a candidate word appears in the niche's product names. [L0990.015]
  • Check that the name has neutral or positive meaning in major languages. [L0989.004]
  • A brand name that describes a benefit or tells a short story can be part of the advertising itself, because names that tell a story are usually shown prominently. [L1261.106]
  • Names that describe a benefit or product in a way that is memorable can be shown prominently. [L1261.106]

Brandable names and word construction

  • Pick a brand name made of two simple words: one describing the product and one describing the differentiator. This makes it easy to remember and lets it extend to a family of products. [L0334.004]
  • Keep product formulas simple, for example 4 to 5 ingredients each, so customers can combine several products into one routine. [L0334.004]
  • Store names work best when they include the niche keyword in the brand name plus a brandable word, and the name must be available. Descriptive words about the product that do not contain the keyword can also work. [L0359.062]
  • A good brandable word can be a term that people already inside the niche recognise, such as a local gesture or slang word. This makes the name feel authentic while staying short, unique and easy to remember. [L0504.020]
  • Use a brandable store name and domain instead of an exact-match name that copies the product keyword. Exact-match domains are the outdated approach. Brandable names are easy to remember and do not lock the store to one product, so the niche can widen later without renaming. [L0356.001] (Source A, 2019, older advice)
  • A brand name that suggests the product's main benefit, such as speed, victory or care, works because customers link the name to the product without extra explanation. Example pattern: a name built around "swift" for a fast-delivery product, or "haven" for a home-comfort product. [L1071.010]
  • Names that only describe an ingredient or category invite substitution and make it hard to hold price, because rivals can use the same name. Avoid names competitors can claim as their own. [L1261.108]
  • A coined name with no meaning is exclusive and protectable, but it needs long, consistent advertising before it carries meaning. Most coined names never reach that status, so choose them with care and budget. [L1261.107]
  • A pioneer who creates a new product category should give the product an exclusive name that can become the common term for the category, so rivals cannot use it. Launching a generic brand lets rivals take the demand. [L1261.112]
  • A name tied to a patent may become common property when the patent expires. Plan names and brand protection separately from patent protection. [L1261.110]
  • When a product must be sold under a common name, add a person's name as a secondary identifier. A named creator signals pride and authority and gives a generic product a distinct identity. [L1261.111]

Generic versus niche-signaling names

Generic names (not tied to one category)

  • Brand names for dropshipping stores must be generic and not tied to a specific product category. Naming a pet store "Paw Paw Friend" restricts future expansion into other categories. [L0320.001]
  • A generic, catchy brand name with a simple font logo is a flexible starting point, because it can be used for any product. [L0361.043]
  • Generic brand names are easier to come up with and are a valid choice. [L0990.003]
  • Choose a flexible name that is not tied to one product category, because a category-specific name limits the store once more products are added. [L0989.002]

Niche-signaling names

  • The business name should signal the market it serves, so a target customer who reads it can immediately tell the business is about their interest. Pick a name tied to the market's category rather than a single item. [L0336.001] (Source B, 2021-22, older advice)
  • Niche-relevant names make it immediately clear which market the brand belongs to. [L0990.003]
  • Brand names built around a product description (for example "golf swing trainer") are good for niche or product-led stores, because the name itself explains what is sold. [L0990.008]
  • A niche-specific name and logo must be replaced when the product changes. [L0361.043]

When the sources differ

  • Generic is the rule for a multi-category store. [L0320.001, L0989.002]
  • For a niche or single-product store, a niche name is acceptable. It must be replaced if the product changes, and it is the better fit when the product description is the main selling point. [L0361.043, L0990.008]
  • Both generic and niche names can work with either a generic or a niche store. [L0990.003]
  • The store name should reflect the niche but does not need to name every product category. Buyers still click and buy a product they searched for. [L0801.175] (Source A, 2021, older advice)

Structure of market names

  • Formula: [local marker, such as a country nickname] + [market keyword] + optional [broad category word]. The local marker signals a national business, which many buyers prefer. The market keyword tells the target customer what the store is about. Examples: "Nordic Pet Supply Co." for a Nordic pet market, and "Texas Grill Depot" for Texas grilling products. [L0336.013] (Source B, 2021-22, older advice)
  • Geographic and cultural references such as country names, mountain ranges and region names create generic brand names with no product association, which allows expansion into any category. Examples: Tatran (Tatra Mountains), Karpatia (Carpathian Mountains), Orava (region), Danubia (Danube River), Liptov, Spis. [L0320.002]
  • Any country works equally well as inspiration, whether it is a major nation, a lesser-known country, a region or a geographic feature. Names derived from Slovenia, Peru or Estonia perform as well as names from Slovakia for multi-category stores. [L0320.007]
  • A name built on one product, such as Logsplitters.com, makes little sense once the store adds snowblowers or generators. A name built on the market word, such as Power Pros or Power Superstore, works across the whole range. [L0336.016] (Source B, 2021-22, older advice)
  • A name built on one product type, such as surfboards, locks the store into that product and makes new categories feel unnatural. A broader market name, such as one that implies water sports, leaves room to grow. A single-product name is workable if necessary but not ideal. [L0336.009] (Source B, 2021-22, older advice)

Case: Oz Off-Grid (Source B, 2021-22, older advice)

An Australian off-grid living store started from an approved niche covering solar energy, waste disposal, water treatment and off-grid food alternatives. The owner considered several names and chose Oz Off-Grid. Off-Grid names the market, and Oz signals an Australian business that local buyers often prefer. The alternative Off Grid Warehouse was considered, but the matching domain osoffgrid.com.au was already owned, so the business name and domain matched exactly. The store was planned to serve the Australian market first, with the name leaving room for many more product categories. [L0336.017]

Generating names with AI

Tools

  • Use ChatGPT or Gemini (Google's AI) to brainstorm brand names for dropshipping stores. [L0320.003]
  • An AI assistant such as ChatGPT can help generate unique brand and product names when naming is difficult. [L1015.011]

Basic prompt

Give me 10 brand names for my branded drop shipping store

For a different quantity or geographic inspiration:
Give me 15 brand names similar to [country name]

[L0320.004]

Procedure: basic brainstorming

  1. Open ChatGPT or Gemini.
  2. Submit a prompt requesting brand names, optionally specifying a number and a country for geographic inspiration.
  3. Keep testing with different parameters to generate more options.
  4. Select and use your chosen brand name.

[L0320.005]

Deep research prompt (system role)

You work as a senior brand namer and strategist.

[L0989.007]

Deep research procedure (AI research model)

  1. Open a ChatGPT thinking model with deep research enabled.
  2. Paste the brand name prompt and fill in the product description and the product list.
  3. Run it, then review the 12-name longlist, the 4 positioning routes and the Top 3 shortlist.
  4. Search each candidate's.com in a registrar's domain search (GoDaddy was used) and confirm it is available.
  5. Add the chosen domain to the cart to confirm it is still free.
  6. Search the name on Trademarkia and on Google for conflicts or unwanted meanings.
  7. Choose the brand name and its matching.com domain.

[L0989.009]

Input and output rules for AI research

  • Deep research often asks what exactly you sell, so put the actual product list into the prompt rather than a general category. Pair the list with a short description of the quality and the feeling the customer should get, because the names are built from those benefits. [L0989.008]
  • Ask for two groups in one run: about six premium single-word names and about six names that are very likely to be free, such as two-word combinations of a brand word and a descriptive word. This gives aspirational options and realistic fallbacks in one list. [L0989.028]
  • If the generated positioning routes all sound alike, the prompt is too vague. Ask again for bolder positioning and a more specific target audience. [L0989.011]
  • For a broad niche store, the first name and positioning are provisional. Launch a basic store, test products, and sharpen the positioning later based on what sells and what the ads say. [L0989.010]

Framework for a meaningful name (Source C)

  1. Define the niche, since it can shape the name.
  2. Identify the community the product serves and what that audience likes to do.
  3. Look for puns or wordplay usable with those interests.
  4. Describe what the product does and how it helps people.
  5. List the product's benefits and pull some of them into the name.
  6. Define the brand tone or brand values, for example moody or exciting, as this strongly affects the name.

[L0990.005]

Worked example for a golf swing aid: the product is a swing trainer in the golf niche. The community is amateur men who want to improve their game. The product leaves a mark on a pad showing swing accuracy and direction. The benefits are a better swing, fewer strokes per hole and beating friends. The desired brand tone was playful, manly and competitive. [L0990.006]

Product-specific name pattern

Combine a word for the core benefit or improvement (such as "pro", "swing" or "precision") with the product type word ("trainer", "pad", "aid", "mate"). Optionally add a word that signals the desired tone. Examples: "Putt Rhythm Pro" for a putting-rhythm trainer, and "Iron Line Coach" for an iron-shot alignment guide. [L0990.007]

Variations of the product name, such as swapping the product word or reordering it, quickly produce a large list of candidates. [L0990.008]

Iterating on AI output

  1. Review the list and mark the names you like or dislike.
  2. Tell the AI what you dislike about the rejected names.
  3. Ask for another batch based on that feedback.
  4. Repeat until the candidate list is large enough.

[L0990.010]

Do not accept the first suggestion. Choose the final name yourself from the AI's list by picking the most memorable one that fits the niche best, and check the others first. [L0521.008]

Checks before you commit

Domain match

  • The business name must match the domain exactly. Customers usually remember the name and type it into the browser with a suffix such as.com,.com.au or another country-specific ending. If the matching domain is already taken, choose a different name. [L0336.004] (Source B, 2021-22, older advice)
  • Brandable names beat keyword-copy domains, so a matching domain should be brandable rather than an exact keyword match. [L0356.001, L0336.004] (Source A, 2019, older advice)
  • In high-ticket dropshipping, buyers visit the site several times before purchasing. The first visit often comes from an ad, but later visits typically happen by typing the remembered name, so the name must be easy to recall and type. [L0336.005] (Source B, 2021-22, older advice)

Competitor similarity

  • The name must be fairly unique within the intended market. Search the product keywords on Google and Google Shopping, review the names of the sellers in the results, and avoid names or URLs that are close variants of a competitor's. [L0336.002] (Source B, 2021-22, older advice)
  • Avoid names that look like or closely copy a competitor's name, since rivals can use the same name and make it hard to hold price. [L1261.108]
  • A store name that matches an unrelated local business, such as a gym with the same name, is not a major concern. Clarity and memorability matter more. [L1109.063] (Source A, 2018, older advice)

Trademark and languages

  • A quick trademark check should be part of the naming step. [L0989.004]
  • Search the name on Trademarkia and on Google for conflicts or unwanted meanings. [L0989.009]
  • Choose the name carefully, because it appears on the store, invoices, ads and domain. Check domain availability and possible trademark conflicts before launch. [L1007.008]
  • A trademark-style name, which is a unique invented name combined with a product word, gives a unique domain for the Shopify store and ring-fences competition. Searches for the brand name face less competition than searches for the generic product name. Add the TM symbol to the end of such a name, since it is trademarked. [L0990.004]

Multi-store and second-store naming

  • Several websites selling the same products in the same niche split the effort. Put all products under one brand and one domain. According to the example given, one site with the same total work builds about ten times the authority in Google's eyes compared to ten separate sites. [L0467.056] (Source A, 2019, older advice)
  • When one owner runs several related services or product lines, one brand usually gets a higher response rate because customers see a single, consistent company. Separate brands are an option, but the single brand is easier to run and tends to convert better. [L1076.025] (Source A, undated)
  • A second store in a related niche can be named after its category with a "plus" suffix. For example, a dining room furniture store paired with a home lighting store. Both stores can share one theme, with only the logo and colour scheme changed. [L0623.027] (Source A, undated)

Test stores that become brands

  1. Start with a general, catchy brand name on one store to test multiple products.
  2. When a product proves to be a winner, keep the same website and change the domain, name and logo to build a real brand.

[L0361.045]

Company and LLC names

  • Choose the company name carefully, because it appears on the store, invoices, ads and domain. [L1007.008]
  • A company name does not have to match the website name. Many businesses run one or more stores under a different name, which is not a problem for approvals or operations. [L0968.114] (Source A, 2021, older advice)
  • Choose a generic company name for the LLC so that several related stores can later share it, rather than naming the company after one store or domain. [L1090.082] (Source A, 2021, older advice)
  • Avoid the word "consulting" in a company name chosen from the start, since it does not help the business. If an existing company already has it, keeping the name is generally harmless. [L1090.083] (Source A, 2021, older advice)
  • Create one legal business entity for each group of related businesses, for example one for fitness equipment and one for office furniture. This keeps accounting separate and makes selling a group easier. [L0468.105] (Source A, 2020, older advice)
