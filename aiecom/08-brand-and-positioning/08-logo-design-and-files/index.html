8.8 Logo design and files

Logo requirements

  • Define the brand before designing the logo: its values, what it offers, the customer problems it solves and its tone of voice. These answers guide the logo's shape and style. [L0952.001]
  • The logo is the face of the brand and is often the first thing customers link to it. It reinforces the feelings, beliefs and attitudes people already hold about the brand, which is why it matters for trust. [L0952.002]
  • A good logo meets four criteria: simplicity, memorability, versatility and relevance. Each one is a test to apply when judging a design. [L0952.003]
  • Simplicity: the logo is easy to recognize and remember. Simplified versions of objects work well, as the Apple and Twitter bird examples show. A simple mark is recognized at a glance and recalled later. [L0952.004]
  • Memorability: the logo makes a strong impression at first glance and stands out enough that a viewer instantly recognizes it on later encounters. [L0952.005]
  • Versatility: the logo must work in many formats (social media, websites, letterheads) and on both light and dark backgrounds. Test it in each context before finalizing. [L0952.006]
  • Relevance: the logo should visually match the niche. For a dropshipping brand, use concepts from the product's niche, for example pet symbols for a pet brand or baby symbols for a baby brand, so the brand looks like it belongs to that niche. [L0952.008]
  • A logo should be as simple as possible, because simpler logos produce better results. Minimal designs look more professional and more luxurious than busy ones. [L0953.002]
  • A logo should be simple: text plus one small graphic, nothing showy, so it is noticed but does not compete with the page. Stores whose products are already colorful and busy benefit most from a simpler logo. [L0342.007, L1045.007]
  • When unsure about a logo design, choose the simpler option and avoid over-engineering or overcomplicating it. Less is more. [L0952.009]
  • A logo does not need to be professional at the start. It should be clean, the right size and not pixelated, and a simple typed wordmark is sufficient and cheap to make. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0663.069]
  • The logo should use the same words as the domain name, so the store name is the most visible element. Check that the spelling matches the domain exactly. [L0342.006]
  • The logo should use the same words as the domain name, with the store name front and center. This keeps the brand name consistent across the site. [L1045.006]
  • Logo design should be primarily text-based, displaying the business name clearly and legibly as the dominant element. Decorative logo marks are optional and not necessary for success. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0323.007]
  • The logo must be rectangular (wider than tall) to fit into website headers without consuming excessive vertical space. Circular logos are difficult to size and display properly. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0323.006]
  • Your website logo appears in the header, footer and a few other pages. Rectangular orientation is critical because it fits naturally in the header space without consuming excessive height. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0323.024]
  • A logo should be rectangular and no more than about 200 px high, with a width of around 400 to 500 px, so it loads quickly and displays well in multiple places on the site. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1371.030]
  • Logo text style should match the target customer base. Male-dominated markets such as power equipment benefit from bold, chunky, thick fonts. Water sports and movement-focused markets suit softer, flowing typography. Luxury segments need elegant, refined letterforms. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0323.026]
  • Logo design quality comes from a brand colour palette and a chosen typeface defined before the logo is built, so colours can be swapped quickly to test how the logo looks. [L0952.012]
  • Build logos from core basic shapes (circles, squares, ellipses, rectangles) instead of drawing everything freehand. Complex icons can be reduced to a few simple shapes that fit together. [L0952.013]
  • Taglines in the logo are optional and make no measurable difference to business success. Include one only if it genuinely resonates with the target market's values or positioning. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0323.008]
  • Branding is not permanent. The logo design and colours can evolve over time without changing the business name or domain. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0323.009]

Wordmark or icon: where to start

  • A simple typed wordmark in a nice font is enough as a logo at the start. Logos matter less than the brand name, so do not spend much time on logo design. [L0361.044]
  • At the early stage, use text-only wordmarks with no icon or symbol, because a mark is not needed for versatility yet. Each wordmark option should differ only in its letter case or type style. [L0954.006]
  • Paying for a logo is optional during setup. A logo can cost about USD 20 or up to USD 100, and the choice adds to the USD 100 to 500 launch budget. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0438.007]
  • A consistent symbol paired with the brand name builds recognition over time. When later products launch, customers who saw the icon before connect it with past purchases and expect to like the new product. [L1047.004]
  • A brand logo must work across many surfaces, such as a website, packing slips, social media ads and other materials. It needs to be versatile and adaptable, not a single image that only fits one place. [L1047.001]
  • Test a logo by mocking it up on the real surfaces where it will appear, such as an Instagram profile, before deciding. A wordmark can fit poorly in a small profile circle while a compact icon fits well. [L1047.003]
  • There are two ways to build an icon: an automated route where AI brainstorms concepts, and a manual route where you sketch the icon yourself and have AI refine it. Use the AI brainstorm when you need options, and use the sketch when you already have a specific idea in mind. [L1047.005]
  • Logo workflow in four steps: [L1047.002]
    1. Develop an icon that pairs with the brand name.
    2. Export the logo as a transparent PNG.
    3. Convert the PNG into a vector file.
    4. Finalize the logo into the variations you actually need.
  • A logo concept should express the product's core meaning in a simple visual idea, such as motion for speed or growth for progress. A simple symbol is easier to remember and connects to the brand story. Example: a leaf-shaped mark for an eco-friendly cleaning product. Second example: an arrow shape forming a 'U' for an upgrade-focused electronics store. [L1071.011]
  • Logo design pattern: a simple mark made of two or three basic shapes, where one shape is cut out of another (negative space) to suggest a recognizable object. The viewer completes the image in their mind, which makes the mark memorable and easy to recognize. Example of the same pattern: a coffee cup whose handle is formed by a cut-out circle in a solid square, and a leaf built from a single cut-out ellipse on a circle. [L0952.041]
  • A pet-friendly brand logo can pair a rounded display typeface with a lighter, softer secondary typeface. Choose the typefaces together with the brand colours before building the mark, so the text and symbol share one visual tone. [L0952.052]

  • Logo, colour and typography can be made in Canva, through a graphic design class, or by hiring a freelancer on a third-party service such as Fiverr. [L1071.012]
  • Canva is a free design tool that can produce a simple logo. A professional designer is an option when more polished branding is needed. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0853.094]
  • Canva can hold a brand kit that combines the logo, colour palette and typefaces in one place. [L1071.012]
  • You do not need a finished logo concept before contacting a Fiverr designer. Provide three colour hex codes, the business name, what the business sells and the target market feel you want, then let the designer create options from that foundation. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0323.025]
  • A logo can be built from overlapping shapes in Illustrator: [L0754.057]
    1. Set each new shape to about 50 percent transparency so overlapping shapes stay visible.
    2. Draw the basic shapes with the Shape tool and the Pen tool, and shape anchor points with the Direct Selection tool.
    3. Group the shapes you want to combine and open the Shape Builder tool.
    4. Drag across the areas you want to keep to merge them into one shape, and hold Alt or Option and drag across the areas you want to remove.
    5. Lock finished layers using the lock icon in the Layers panel so they cannot be moved by accident.
    6. Polish the outline with the Direct Selection tool, then set the transparency back to 100 percent.
    7. Collect several reference logos for inspiration and iterate until the shape feels balanced.

Canva: store logo for the Source A theme

Canva procedure with the 800 x 300 px template: [L0342.005]

  1. Open the logo template for the Source A theme (800 x 300 px) and click Use Template.
  2. Click Text, then Add a heading, and type the store name exactly as the domain name.
  3. Save the brand colors in Brand Kit first, then select the text and apply a brand color with the text color (A) icon.
  4. Enlarge the text until it fills the template, choose a bold or extra-bold font and optionally capitalize it.
  5. Adjust letter spacing and line spacing so the text fills the frame and the lines sit close together.
  6. Open Elements, search Graphics for a simple icon such as a letter S, drag it in, resize it to fill the template and recolor it to match the brand colors.
  7. Keep the logo to text plus one small graphic.
  8. Download the logo as a PNG. On the free plan it keeps a white background.

A second version of the same Canva procedure: [L1045.009]

  1. Open the logo template (800 x 300 pixels) through the linked template and click Use template.
  2. Click Text, then Add a heading, and type the store's domain name.
  3. Select the text and use the A text color button to pick one of the brand colors from the Brand kit.
  4. Change the font, make it bold or extra bold, capitalize it if wanted, and enlarge it to fill the template. Adjust letter spacing and line spacing as needed.
  5. Go to Elements, then Graphics, search for a simple letter or symbol and pick a free item (items with a crown are paid).
  6. Drag the icon into the template, enlarge it to nearly the full template size and change its color to the brand palette.
  7. Click Share or the download button, choose PNG and download.

Canva: square template (Source D method)

  • The Canva logo template used in this method is a square 500 × 500 px design. Keep the logo on a square canvas of this size. [L0953.007]
  • Procedure in Canva Premium: [L0953.008]
    1. Decide the brand name first.
    2. In Canva Premium, open Designs in the top-left area.
    3. Search "Logo" in the "Search Logo templates" field and scroll through the results.
    4. Choose a simple, minimalist template that matches a professional look.
    5. Replace the template text with your brand name.
    6. Edit the template's letters to fit the brand name, for example by changing the first and last letter of the template text into an O-shape.
    7. Adjust the position of the logo elements (move up or down) until they are balanced.
    8. Adjust the size of the logo so it is clearly readable.
    9. Check the result against the simplicity rule and finish if it is simple and professional.
  • Pick a template whose style is black and white, minimalist and simple, because that style matches the professional look a logo should have. Reject templates with many elements or strong decoration, even if they catch the eye first. [L0953.006]

Wordmarks generated with ChatGPT

  • For logo generation in ChatGPT, select the ChatGPT 5 Thinking model and the Heavy thinking mode in the composer before pasting the logo prompt. The full set of four wordmarks, four favicons and a palette took about one minute of thinking in this setup. [L0954.015]
  • Before generating, finalise the brand name and its spelling. The wordmarks and favicons are built from the exact spelling. If the name changes later, all four wordmark options, favicons and the palette must be generated again with the new name. [L0954.017]
  • Keep the wordmark to the brand name only and leave out the domain extension such as.com, even if the domain is pasted into the prompt. The domain is an input for the brand positioning, not part of the logo text. [L0954.016]
  • Steps to create a logo set and palette with ChatGPT: [L0954.003]
    1. Build a master brand document with the product list, the chosen brand name, the domain name and one brand positioning (three short pillars, each a label with one sentence, plus a short mini story paragraph).
    2. Open a new chat in ChatGPT using the thinking model.
    3. Paste the logo generator prompt and replace the placeholders with the brand name, the domain and the positioning text.
    4. Wait until the generation is finished.
    5. Click download and download all assets: the 4 wordmark PNGs, the 4 favicon PNGs and the palette swatch PNG.
    6. Open the downloaded files in Finder to check that the wordmarks, favicons and palette swatch are all there.
    7. Use the logo, favicon and text options in the Shopify theme.
  • Logo generator prompt, to be pasted with placeholders filled in: [L0954.004]
Act as a senior logo designer. Produce 4 clean wordmarks for the brand [BRAND NAME] that use type alone: no icon, symbol, mascot or other mark, only the name set in lettering. Leave out drop shadows, gradients, 3D effects and ornaments. The background must be transparent.

Mandatory baseline rules:
- Every letter sits on one shared baseline. No letter may be moved vertically on its own.
- Reject any bouncy or playful baseline. Switch off any stylistic set that offsets glyphs.
- Keep the font's default kerning. Adjust tracking evenly across the whole word, never letter by letter.
- Fonts may overshoot for optical reasons, but the baseline must still look straight, with no stair-stepping.
- Run a baseline check before export and regenerate any option that shows vertical jitter.

Four style options:
- A: lowercase
- B: Title Case
- C: UPPERCASE, bold
- D: soft serif (small caps, expanded)

Inspiration, not imitation: draw on recent, high-quality brand aesthetics from the last 10 years. Choose new, free fonts that suit the brand positioning and the products sold.

Deliverables: PNG wordmarks on a transparent background, 3000 x 1200 px, wordmark centred with even padding on all sides. Also create 4 PNG favicons, one per option, each showing the first letter of the wordmark.

Spelling must match the input exactly. Use consistent tracking with optical kerning turned on.

Output: download links for the 4 wordmark PNGs and 1 palette swatch PNG. Under a 'Palette' heading, list 5 colours with HEX and RGB values. Include at least one dark and one light colour with strong contrast for buttons, and make sure the colours work with white. Under each option (A, B, C, D), give a short spec line.

Inputs (paste below):
Brand name: [BRAND NAME]
Domain: [DOMAIN]
Brand positioning: [POSITIONING 1: label and one sentence] [POSITIONING 2: label and one sentence] [POSITIONING 3: label and one sentence]
Mini story: [SHORT MINI STORY PARAGRAPH DESCRIBING THE PRODUCTS, THE CUSTOMER FEELING AND THE BRAND PROMISE]

  • Logo export checklist for wordmarks and favicons: [L1331.007]
    1. Use a single straight baseline with no per-letter vertical shifts.
    2. Keep default kerning and even tracking, with no manual glyph nudging.
    3. Make the canvas 3000 x 1200 px, centered, with at least 12% clear margin on all sides.
    4. Check that no letters overlap, there are no unwanted ligatures, and the mark stays clean at small sizes.
    5. Export transparent PNGs for wordmarks and favicons.

Icon development with AI

  • Run the icon or logo brainstorm in a chat that already holds the brand details, such as the name, target customer and style words. The AI then has the brand context and returns concepts that fit the brand instead of generic symbols. [L1047.042]
  • Suggest icon ideas with this prompt: [L1047.006]
Suggest several icon ideas that could stand for [BRAND NAME]. For each one, explain its shape, the feeling it conveys, and where it would appear, such as hang tags, waistbands, packaging, an app icon, or embroidered details. Keep the look consistent with [BRAND STYLE WORDS, e.g. soft, minimal, luxe, neutral, sporty, playful].

[L1047.006]

  • If you want a full logo rather than a separate symbol, ask for logo concepts instead of icon concepts in the brainstorm prompt. This produces a cohesive logo design that already includes the brand name. [L1047.007]
  • Choose the concept whose shape and mood match the target customer and the brand direction. A refined, luxury-leaning silhouette can fit a women's brand better than a more generic symbol, even if the generic one looks more striking at first. [L1047.008]
  • Pick icons that match brand maturity. A sophisticated, techy or luxury brand fits abstract or geometric marks, while cutesy, cartoon-like graphics suit a playful, girly brand. Compare each candidate with competitor logos in the same niche to see where it lands. [L1047.017]
  • An abstract symbol built from three flowing lines can carry several meanings at once, such as breathability, movement and luxury. This gives the brand a story it can reference in marketing and product messaging. [L1047.018]
  • The icon must fit the existing brand elements, such as the font and existing icons. A geometric symbol pairs well with a squared-off typeface, while a playful rounded font suits a more cartoonish mark. [L1047.019]
  • Judge an icon by how it looks at small sizes, such as a profile picture circle or an app icon, not only at full size. These are the places where the symbol appears alone, so it must stay recognizable there. [L1047.043]
  • Manual icon method: [L1047.010]
    1. Decide on the exact icon idea you have in mind before you open the chat.
    2. Draw the logo on a sheet of paper. You do not need artistic skill, but expect several tries.
    3. Take a clear photo of the sketch.
    4. Drag and drop the photo into the ChatGPT chat.
    5. Ask ChatGPT to turn the sketch into the icon.
    6. Compare the AI result with the sketch and refine it if needed.
  • Sketch-to-icon prompt: [L1047.011]
Can you turn this sketch into the icon? Keep the exact outline, proportions and key details of my drawing, and turn it into a clean, simple shape that works as a logo.

[L1047.011]

  • AI image generation can ignore details described in the text, such as a leg silhouette, and return a plain letter shape instead. For a specific shape, upload a sketch so the AI follows your drawing rather than a written description. [L1047.012]
  • Smoothing prompt for a sketch-based icon: [L1047.015]
Smooth this icon so the edges are clean and even. Then make it even smoother and more minimalistic, keeping the core shape and proportions unchanged.

[L1047.015]

  • Logo pattern in use, from a case: [L1047.039]
    • Starting point: a women's luxury-leaning fleece and activewear brand had an original stylized logo that was judged not versatile enough for everyday brand use. A mockup on an Instagram profile showed the wordmark fit poorly where a compact icon would fit well.
    • Step 1: ChatGPT brainstormed icon concepts, including a curved C with a leg silhouette, a spiral C, a flame C, a soft mountain, a crescent moon, a monogram and a wave C. The curved C with leg silhouette was the first favorite, and it was generated as concept 1.
    • Step 2: Concept 1 ignored the leg shape and came out as a plain C. The team drew the leg by hand, photographed the sketch, and uploaded it so ChatGPT could turn it into an icon. The sketched leg icon was more recognizable but its first AI version was rough.
    • Step 3: The team went back to an earlier message and branched the conversation to generate the mountain concept as concept 6. The mountain concept was judged fine but without much personality. The crescent moon concept 9 was rejected, and the monogram concept 8 looked like a copyright symbol.
    • Step 4: The leg sketch was refined by asking for smoothing and a more minimalistic look, which made it cleaner. The wave C concept 4 was judged abstract, luxurious and suitable as a profile icon, with three lines that could suggest breathability, movement and luxury.
    • Step 5: The wave C was chosen over the leg icon. The leg icon was seen as more cutesy and playful, while the brand was judged more mature and techy, and the geometric wave C paired well with the existing geometric icon and squared-off font.
    • Step 6: ChatGPT regenerated the chosen icon as a transparent PNG, which was then vectorized in Kittl and tested by scaling up.
    • Step 7: Variations were built in a grid of icon, wordmark, stacked and left-aligned logos, each in black, white and color, plus black and white text over a color icon. The stacked logo used center alignment and a larger text than symbol.
    • Result: all variations were exported at 3x size as PNG and SVG files, and screenshots were collected on a FigJam board. The next planned step was a branding deck.

Icon shape and style patterns

  • A curved C icon: centered and left-aligned versions look strongest, though other alignments such as right-aligned are possible. [L1047.034]
  • Choosing the alignment that suits the icon shape is a principle to follow. [L1047.034]

Variations

  • Build a logo variation grid in Kittl. Rows are layouts and columns are colour versions. [L1047.028]

Icon black icon white icon color icon not used not used
Wordmark (text only) black text white text color text not used not used
Stacked (center aligned) black white color black text, color icon white text, color icon
Left aligned black white color black text, color icon white text, color icon

Black/Color means black text with a color icon, and White/Color means white text with a color icon. [L1047.028]

  • Plan colour versions from the start. A colour icon with black text or with white text is very common and is needed for many backgrounds. You will not realise you need these combinations until you try to use the logo. [L1047.033]
  • Choose a logo colour version for each background type: a light version for light backgrounds and a dark version for dark backgrounds. A colour that works on white often needs to be lightened to work on black. [L0952.010]
  • A minimum set of logo versions to launch a brand is one light version, one dark version and one social media profile version. [L0952.011]
  • In a stacked logo, make the text larger than the symbol for a more balanced look. [L1047.030]
  • Align the stacked and left-aligned logo versions by eye, judging by how they look visually, rather than following strict alignment hacks. This optical approach usually produces the best result. [L1047.031]
  • Test versatility by checking the logo on each background and in each context before finalizing. [L0952.006]

Formats and files

  • A transparent PNG is an image with no background, so it can be placed on any colour or photo. A normal image with a white box cannot be used on top of other designs or surfaces. [L1047.020]
  • A raster file such as PNG becomes pixelated and blurry when enlarged, so it fails on large uses such as building signs, vehicle graphics and billboards. A vector version is required for any logo that must be used at large sizes. [L1047.023]
  • Use PNG files with a transparent background for logos and favicons. JPG files do not support transparency and show a white box around logos placed on a dark background. Only PNG and JPG files are generally needed for a Shopify site. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1371.028]
  • Export the logo variations: [L1047.035]
    1. Click each variation in the group.
    2. Choose Quick Export.
    3. Pick the format: PNG for the transparent raster version, SVG for the vector version.
    4. Set the size to 3x.
    5. Click Export.
    6. Repeat for every variation in every group.
    7. Screenshot the finished variations and add them to a FigJam board for review.
  • Name the downloaded logo files as [brandname]wordmark[A-D].png for the wordmarks, [brandname]favicon[A-D].png for the favicons and [brandname]_palette_swatch.png for the palette, all in lowercase. On a Mac these files land in the Downloads folder, where they can be checked in Finder before uploading. [L0954.018]
  • Prepare the store logo for Shopify: [L1371.029]
    1. Open the transparent PNG logo in PicMonkey.
    2. Use Crop canvas to remove the extra white space around the logo.
    3. Use Resize to set the width to about 500 px, keeping the logo rectangular.
    4. Download as PNG.
    5. Upload it under the header logo and checkout logo in Shopify. (Older advice, 2021-22.)
  • In the Shopify admin, the logo and the wide homepage header image can be downloaded from Content > Files by opening each file and clicking its download button. The wide header image is a separate file from a square image, so pick the wide version for the Facebook cover photo. [L0654.023]

Favicons

  • Create one favicon per wordmark option: a PNG showing the first letter of the brand name. The favicon is what appears in the browser tab, and a store without one looks unfinished. [L0954.009]
  • A favicon is the small icon shown in the browser tab, and a site without one shows a default blank globe icon. Use an element of the logo so the browser tab looks polished and recognizable. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1371.027]
  • Favicon image size: the Shopify Favicon field asks for a 32 x 32 px PNG, while the lesson resized the favicon to 40 x 40 px. Use 32 x 32 px where the Shopify field specifies it. The favicon should be a square PNG with a transparent background that represents the brand. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1371.026]
  • Create a favicon in Shopify with PicMonkey: (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1371.025]
    1. Open the logo PNG with a transparent background.
    2. Use Crop canvas to make a square around one element of the logo, such as an icon, and click Apply.
    3. Click Resize, enter 40 in both width and height in pixels, and click Apply.
    4. Click Download, keep the PNG file type, and save the file.
    5. In Shopify Theme settings under Favicon, click Select Image, upload the file, and select it.
  • Theme settings > Favicon provides several colour variants of the favicon image in the Files list. Pick the variant that matches the logo colours so the browser tab icon stays consistent with the brand. [L1309.070]

Social profile versions

  • For social media profile pictures, the logo should be square and have a lot of empty space around it so it fits the circular or square crop without being cut off. A simpler, centred version is usually better for this use. [L0952.007]
  • A square profile version and a wide header version serve different places. The profile uses the square version, and the Facebook cover photo uses the wide header version from Content > Files. [L0654.023, L0952.007]
  • Mock up the logo on the real profile before deciding. A wordmark can fit poorly in a small profile circle while a compact icon fits well. [L1047.003]
  • Judge the profile version at small sizes such as a profile picture circle, because the symbol appears alone there and must stay recognizable. [L1047.043]

Logo don'ts

  • Avoid busy, cartoonish or sports-team style logos, because they look childish and unprofessional. A logo that looks amateur undermines trust in the store from the first impression. [L0953.004]
  • Do not use a busy or heavily decorated template, even if it catches the eye first. [L0953.006]
  • Do not over-engineer the logo. When in doubt, choose the simpler option. [L0952.009]
  • Do not spend much time on logo design at the start. [L0361.044]
  • Do not write the logo in lowercase where the brand expects capital letters. A logo written in lowercase can look careless. (Lowercase is a valid option where it is the brand's chosen style, such as option A in the wordmark prompt.) [L0656.024, L0954.005]
  • Do not repeat the same element inside a logo, because it is distracting and weakens the mark. A monogram-style letter mark can also look like an existing copyright sign, so check how it reads before using it. [L1047.016]
  • Do not make the symbol larger than the text in a stacked logo, because it often looks wonky. [L1047.030]
  • Do not leave colour versions until later. Plan them from the start. [L1047.033]
  • Do not expect AI to follow a written description of a specific shape. Upload a sketch instead. [L1047.012]
  • Do not include the domain extension such as.com in the wordmark text. [L0954.016]
  • Do not use a circular logo in a header-bound layout, because it is difficult to size and display properly. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0323.006]
  • Do not add a tagline unless it genuinely resonates with the target market's values or positioning. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0323.008]
  • Do not use a raster PNG at large sizes such as signs, vehicle graphics or billboards. Use a vector version. [L1047.023]
  • Do not place a normal image with a white box on top of other designs or surfaces. Use a transparent PNG. [L1047.020]

  • Premium brands often use a single clean mark or wordmark with no extra decoration, which signals luxury. To apply this, pick one clear element, such as a monogram or a single elegant serif wordmark, and leave out ornaments. Example 1: a black serif wordmark with one small letter-based symbol on a plain background. Example 2: a white minimal monogram of the brand's initials in a thin circle. [L0953.003]
