8.21 Trust-based brand ethics and reputation

Honesty in claims and copy

  • Honesty shows through copy. Hidden flaws or evasive claims are noticed by readers, while a candid tone about the product builds trust and makes the buying environment feel real. [L1366.111]
  • Describe your business, where it comes from and what you sell accurately and transparently. When a problem appears, address it honestly instead of hiding or spinning it, because trust is the baseline for any brand. [L0534.001]
  • Do not promote the business with personal or obscure brand claims. Use direct, factual statements about what the store offers. (Older advice, Source A 2022.) [L0665.072]
  • Promotional copy that claims months of investigation or a secret discovery to justify a set of products reads as fake and erodes trust. Keep product-section headlines literal and short, and keep them far away from trust elements such as charity pledges. [L0355.021]
  • Some print-on-demand stores use religious or emotional messaging, and claims of donating 10% to a charity may be false. Verify such claims before copying the approach or promoting the brand. [L1214.058]

Product accuracy and specifications

  • Misrepresenting specifications makes the buyer blame the brand after delivery and harms the supplier's reputation. Example: listing a 48-inch 4K Philips TV as a 50-inch 6K model. [L1422.014]
  • Describe every product exactly as the supplier defines it, including size, resolution and other specifications, and present the brand as it is. [L1422.015]

Sourcing transparency

  • Show full label disclosure and explain how to read a label on the site, so customers can check every ingredient and amount themselves. The reasoning is that if a customer cannot tell what goes into the body, it may not be a good decision. [L0334.048]
  • Start with small transparency steps and increase them as the brand gains confidence. Example sequence from the source:
Stage Action Trigger
1 Publish the label and its contents Start
2 Publish all suppliers After the first roughly 1,000 customers showed demand

[L0334.008]

  • Transparency about product and sourcing can be used as a long-term defense. Copiers may copy formulas and marketing, but they usually do not keep up because they are not committed to the same values. Make the brand's authenticity the reason customers stay. [L0334.006]

Origin stories and founder claims

  • Do not present an invented origin story or a fake inventor in your ads, such as students who supposedly created the product. Claims of this kind damage trust and can get accounts restricted. Use a true founder story or describe the product's real benefits instead. [L0706.066]

  • If you sell someone else's product, use this procedure to get a true backstory. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0580.006]

    1. Look for a backstory on the brand's About Us page.
    2. If none is published, ask the manufacturer how the product came about and whether they allow you to use the story on your site.
    3. If they agree, put the story on the product page word for word.
    4. Alternatively, ask customers who had a positive experience to share how the product improved their life in a way that matches the main problem of the market.

    The story must be true, but names and personal details may be left out. [L0580.006]

Reviews and social proof

  • Only use product reviews that the supplier gives you permission to use. Never import reviews from other websites or from Amazon. (Older advice, Source A 2021.) [L0671.045]
  • Product reviews can serve as a source for customer stories, since they show real positive experiences with the product. Customers can be asked directly for a story, and it does not need full names as long as it is true. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0580.007]

Audience and community targeting

  • Running a campaign aimed at a community that does not match the brand's real origin can cause public backlash and negative reviews. In the documented case, the ads were shut down and the brand kept selling only organically on Instagram. [L0519.031]
  • Customers and critics can often see where a store is actually operated, for example from the visitor's IP address. If the store's location does not match the community it targets, critics can publicly question its legitimacy, which can bring negative reviews and force the paid ads to be shut down. [L0519.044]
  • Before launching a campaign aimed at a specific group, do the following:
    1. Choose target communities you can credibly serve.
    2. Prepare a response plan. [L0519.044]
  • Avoid publicly posting store revenue claims or store links for copying in online communities. People may copy one store without the underlying principles and face risk from that. (Older advice, Source A 2022.) [L0429.078]

Originality and design

  • Build your store with a unique angle rather than copying another store, even when a competitor sells in the same niche. Copying another store's content or layout is a serious problem. (Older advice, Source A 2021.) [L0968.086]
  • Copying other brands' designs is a mistake for a clothing brand and should be avoided. [L0779.002]
  • Do not display logos on product images if the physical product does not have them. This creates a trust violation when customers receive unbranded items, triggering complaints and chargebacks. [L0324.020]

Persuasion limits

  • Persuasion techniques should only be used for products that reliably deliver the result the copy promises. Psychological triggers can win a first sale, but they cannot keep a product that fails the buyer from being returned and reviewed badly. [L0340.078]

Business choice and reputation protection

  • Sell only products you believe in, and avoid selling to people who do not need or want them. Trust takes a long time to build and very little time to lose. [L0378.103]
  • Short-term profit is never worth lasting damage to your reputation. Business made through practices you feel bad about often disappears, because you may not feel you deserve it. [L0378.104]
  • A business that creates problems for others, causing as much harm as good or more, is a sign of bad business. Keep the business aimed at solving problems rather than creating them. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0872.002]
  • The ecommerce dropshipping community is very small, and a person's reputation travels faster than their ads. Treat every customer as if the whole niche is watching, and never run deceptive or no-ship operations. [L1038.017]
  • Build the brand's network and reputation through reliability and quality, and be cutting-edge with designs so they attract attention. Reliable quality is what makes customers and partners return. [L1438.038]
