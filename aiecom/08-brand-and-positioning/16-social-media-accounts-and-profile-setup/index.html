8.16 Social media accounts and profile setup

Strategy and platform priority

  • Social accounts must be active before paid traffic is launched, so visitors who research credibility before buying find a complete brand presence. [L0040.011]
  • Business social media accounts (Facebook and Instagram business pages) take about 30 minutes to set up and are required for most supplier approvals. [L0278.030]
  • Social accounts work mainly through indirect sales. People who search for a store and find active social pages are more likely to trust it. [L0429.070] (Older advice, 2022.)
  • Organic social with few followers is rarely worth the effort. Basic paid campaigns can build a few hundred likes for social proof. [L0429.070] (Older advice, 2022.)
  • Expensive products are usually bought after research, so do not pay for social management to grow a large organic following. [L0470.087]
  • Focus effort on Facebook and Instagram, the two primary accounts, instead of spreading effort across networks such as Twitter, Pinterest or YouTube (80-20 approach). [L0653.004]
  • Facebook organic reach has been low for several years, so page posts are hard to show even to followers who like the page. Instagram offers more chance of virality and suits experimental, meme-style content. [L0653.015]
  • Humorous meme-style content is an advanced step and is not needed at launch. Start with plain business updates such as products, promotions and restocks. [L0653.021]
  • Instagram Reels help build a following for a new brand, since a few strong reels can gain a few hundred followers quickly. Do not rely on Reels alone for sales: in the owner's tests, Reels converted lower than TikTok, and the audience may be more skeptical. [L1077.037]
  • Snapchat's audience spans a very wide age range, not only young users. It suits founder-led brands that want to connect personally with their audience. [L1504.018]
  • Pinterest is covered in its own section below. [L1136.041]

Profile photo, name, username and bio (all platforms)

Profile photo

  • Use the same profile photo on every social platform so users recognise the account across apps. [L0915.003, L0915.009]
  • The profile photo should clearly represent the brand or person, be eye-catching and colourful. [L0915.003]
  • If the photo shows a face, zoom in so the face fills the circular avatar. On a phone the picture is tiny, so a far-away or off-centre shot and group photos are not recognisable. [L0915.004]
  • A profile picture that cannot be identified at a glance while scrolling on a phone is a red flag. Use a clean, single-colour background with the subject clearly visible. [L0915.005]
  • For a product or brand profile, use a high-quality product image, the product logo or the brand name, not a selfie. A profile video is optional. [L1380.010]
  • Keep logos, colours and the profile photo consistent across all social pages, so users who find the brand on one platform recognise it on another. [L0915.009]

Name and username

  • The account name (display name, not username) can be set to the store domain, for example brand.com, so people who tag the account see where to buy. [L1380.011]
  • The Facebook page name should match the store name exactly, including the domain-style name the store uses, so customers can find the page when they search. [L0653.010]
  • Use the store name as the Facebook page name, not the full website URL. [L0654.009]
  • Instagram name should match the store name. [L0653.017]
  • Usernames should match the store name, for example shakasurfboards. If the username is taken, try a close variation, for example shakasurfboard. [L0654.011, L0654.013]

Bio: content

  • A bio should contain the main offer or where to shop, the location if the business has a physical location, the niche, a short catchy tagline and a contact email for brands or press. [L0915.006]
  • Location in the bio strongly affects discoverability when people search for profiles, so include it when relevant. [L0915.007]
  • A bio that only says "follow me", gives no information about the account and pushes people off the platform is weak. Give users a reason to follow instead of asking for it. [L0915.008]
  • The platform reads the bio as a signal of what the page is about and which viewers to show it to. A bio that states the niche clearly helps the algorithm target the account, not only the human reader. [L0915.024]

Bio: formula and examples

  • Bio formula: niche, then location (if there is a physical base), then a short catchy tagline that states the benefit, then a contact email for brand deals or press. A relevant emoji helps the bio stand out when scanned on a phone. [L0915.023]
  • Example in the same pattern: Vegan skincare for sensitive skin | Berlin 🇩🇪 | Routines that take 3 minutes ✨ | Brands: collab@yourbrand.com [L0915.023]
  • Second example in the same pattern: Pet grooming tips for Labradors | Denver, CO 🐾 | New tip every Tuesday | Press: press@yourbrand.com [L0915.023]

Bio: length and structure (differing sources)

Facebook page setup

Overview

  • Quick path to create a Facebook page: log in, click the plus (+) symbol at the top, select Page, complete the setup wizard, and the page is ready. [L0653.014]
  • Overview of steps: create a Facebook business page; upload the business information (profile photo, cover image and about-us section); post two to three times a week; create an Instagram business account; upload its business information; post on Instagram two to three times a week. [L0653.005]

Create the page (alternative path)

  • Create a new public Facebook Page by opening the Facebook profile switcher, choosing See all profiles, and then selecting Create Page. [L0651.009]
  • On the Create a Page form, the Page name is required and should be the brand name. The Category field is also required and should be the category that best describes the business. The Bio field is optional and can be filled in later. [L0651.010]

Create the page (full step list)

  1. Go to Facebook, click See more in the left navigation and click Pages. [L0654.010]
  2. Click Create page. [L0654.010]
  3. Choose Public page and click Next. [L0654.010]
  4. Click Get started. [L0654.010]
  5. Enter the store name as the page name, not the full URL. [L0654.010]
  6. Choose a relevant category, for example Sporting goods store. [L0654.010]
  7. Write a short bio based on the About Us page and paste it into the bio field, which appears under the profile picture and header. [L0654.010]
  8. Click Next to the next page and enter the live store URL as the website. [L0654.010]
  9. Enter the business phone number as the phone number. [L0654.010]
  10. Enter the store email address, for example sales@ followed by the store domain. [L0654.010]
  11. Leave the address empty; it is not necessary. [L0654.010]
  12. Set the hours to Always open, because the store is e-commerce only. [L0654.010]
  13. Upload the logo as the profile picture and the wide homepage header image as the cover photo. [L0654.010]
  14. If the logo does not fit the profile picture, crop the symbol out of the logo so it fits in the middle. [L0654.010]
  15. Click the action button, choose Learn more, enter the homepage URL as the link and click Save. [L0654.010]
  16. Skip the WhatsApp number and skip inviting friends. [L0654.010]
  17. Click Next, turn on emails about updates to the page and click Done. [L0654.010]

Steps 1 to 17 [L0654.010]

Finish the page

  • After creating the page, upload the brand profile picture and the banner (cover photo), add a short store description and add the website link. [L0651.011]
  • The Links section of a new Facebook Page accepts up to 10 links, such as websites, blogs or portfolios. [L0651.012]
  • Edit page info via the sidebar option Edit Page Info. Use the store's logo as the profile photo and a cover image uploaded as the background. [L0653.012]
  • Facebook image size requirements for the profile photo and cover image change over time. Check the current recommended sizes on a current source before uploading. [L0653.013]
  • On the page, the account switcher at the top right toggles between your personal profile and any page you manage. To post as the business page, switch to the page and type in the post box while acting as that page. [L0654.024]

Page fields

  • Fields: Name (must match store name), Username, Description (short synopsis of company and mission statement), Phone (optional, only if the business is live), Best contact email address, Best website, Address (only if a physical address exists). [L0653.011]
  • Local-business fields such as service areas and opening hours can be skipped for an online store. Extras such as privacy policy and product information are not necessary at the beginning. [L0653.011]
  • The bio should be a shorter version of the About Us page, and it must be short enough to show completely under the profile picture and header. [L0654.009]

Page category (differing sources)

  • For an online store, select Product or service as the page category rather than a local business type. Local-business fields can then be left empty. [L0653.019]
  • Example category used in the setup walkthrough: Sporting goods store. [L0654.010]
  • The category is not the most important setting, but it is shown on the page, so pick one that matches the business. Facebook does not show every option in a complete list, so AI can help find a suitable category. [L0654.008]

Prompts for the page bio and category

Bio prompt:

I am creating the bio for a Facebook page for [your store name]. Here is the URL of the live store: [your store URL]. Please read the store and help me write a bio for the page. Please help me write this, and keep the bio short enough to fit in the bio section of the page without being cut off.

[L0654.006]

Category prompt:

What category should I use on a Facebook page for my store that sells [your product or niche]? Please suggest the most relevant categories for the page and briefly explain why each one fits.

[L0654.007]

Facebook username

  • Username steps, while logged in as the page:
  1. Click the three dots on the page. [L0654.011]
  2. Click Settings and privacy. [L0654.011]
  3. Scroll down the left navigation and click Page setup. [L0654.011]
  4. Click the top link, Name. [L0654.011]
  5. Click Edit next to the username. [L0654.011]
  6. Enter the store name as the username, for example shakasurfboards. [L0654.011]
  7. If the username is not available, try a close variation, for example shakasurfboard. [L0654.011]
  8. Click Save and enter your password if asked. [L0654.011]
  9. Check the page URL, which should now be facebook.com/ followed by the username. [L0654.011]

Path: Settings and privacy > Page setup > Name > Username > Edit > Save. The username appears in the page URL, so it should match the store name. A username can be unavailable if someone already used it, in which case use a close variation. [L0654.012]

Instagram account setup

Overview and fields

  • Quick path to create an Instagram business account: go to instagram.com while logged out, select Sign up, go through the signup process and choose the business account option, then upload business info and post two to three times a week. [L0653.016]
  • Profile fields: Name (should match the store name), Username, Category, About us (short bio) and Website (link). These are edited via Edit profile. [L0653.017]
  • Profile category, as listed in the fields: Brand for a store. [L0653.017]
  • Category chosen when converting to a professional account: Shopping and Retail. Both are reported. The Brand value is the profile category, and Shopping and Retail is the category selected during conversion. [L0654.013, L0654.014]
  • Instagram is mainly a mobile app, so posts are typically published from a phone. Posting from desktop is still possible using the create button, which accepts image uploads. [L0654.017]
  • The website field can only be added in the mobile app, not on desktop. Log in to the account on a mobile device to enter the store website. [L0654.016]

Create and convert the account

  1. Open instagram.com on desktop or use the mobile app. [L0654.013]
  2. Click Create new account. Do not use your personal account, because this is a brand-new account for the store. [L0654.013]
  3. Enter the store's official sales email address. If you cannot access the verification email, use another email address that you can access. [L0654.013]
  4. Enter a password and a date of birth. [L0654.013]
  5. Enter the business name as the full name. [L0654.013]
  6. Enter the store name as the username. If it is taken, add a variation. [L0654.013]
  7. Click Submit and enter the verification code sent to your email. [L0654.013]
  8. Click More, then Settings. [L0654.013]
  9. Scroll down the left navigation and click Account type and tools. [L0654.013]
  10. Click Switch to professional account and select Business. [L0654.013]
  11. Click Next twice. [L0654.013]
  12. Choose the category Shopping and Retail and click Done. [L0654.013]
  13. Confirm that you want to switch to a professional account. [L0654.013]

Path: More > Settings > Account type and tools > Switch to professional account > Business > category Shopping and Retail > Done. [L0654.014]

Profile photo and bio on Instagram

  • Use the same profile photo as on Facebook. [L0654.015]

Pinterest profile

Setup

  1. Set the username to the store name for a general store, or to the product name if the store sells one product. [L1135.020]
  2. Replace the profile picture with the store logo or a product image. [L1135.020]
  3. Write an About section of five to six lines describing the store. The lesson suggests using ChatGPT to draft it. [L1135.020]
  4. Add a cover image, such as a store banner or a product-related image. [L1135.020]

Treat the profile like a small website

  • Treat the Pinterest profile like a small website and include the same types of content you would show on your store. [L1136.014]
  • Every board and pin should contain links back to the store, because a profile without links sends no traffic. [L1136.014]
  • Each board works like its own niche category. The board a visitor saves a pin to determines where that pin is organised on the profile, so organise boards by category so each one is relevant to a specific audience segment. [L1136.038]

Board types

  • Product boards cover products, new releases and best sellers, but they are not enough on their own. Storytelling or inspiration boards perform better because they show the result a customer wants and place the products inside that story. [L1136.015]
  • Build a board around the outcome a customer wants, such as better sleep, and fill it with relevant images and tips. Place the product as one piece of that routine, so it is seen as the means to the outcome, not just an item for sale. [L1136.016]
  • Resource boards collect useful how-to and tip content related to the products, either from your own blog or from other good sources. Their purpose is to add value and build trust, which makes visitors more likely to buy. [L1136.017]
  • Behind-the-scenes boards show real people, such as the founders, team or daily life behind the brand. This humanises the store and builds trust, because people prefer to buy from people. [L1136.018]
  • Testimonial pins combine a customer quote with a photo of the customer, designed as an image. Place them on a dedicated testimonial board on the profile, the same way testimonials appear on the website. [L1136.019]

Reach

  • A single viral Pinterest pin can drive enough traffic to build a business, and some brands have built most of their marketing on Pinterest. Pinterest should still be integrated into places where the audience already visits, such as the site, email and other social networks, rather than becoming the main focus of the homepage. [L1136.041]

  • Posting an inactive profile leaves a visible gap that undermines the credibility purpose of the accounts. [L0653.018]
  • Routine posts are enough to support trust and remarketing, so content can stay simple. [L0963.076]

Organic content ideas

  • Content for a small store's organic posts follows a simple pattern: show a product, announce a seasonal or holiday promotion, announce a newly approved supplier, announce a product back in stock, or announce a product now available in another colour. No large content production is needed. [L0653.007]
  • Examples: a product photo with a short lead line for a winter sale, a post about a new supplier that was just approved, a restock notice for a bestseller, and a note that a product now comes in a new colour. [L0653.008]
  • Reviewing the Facebook pages of large dropshipping stores is a useful source of ideas for what type of posts a store should publish. [L0653.009]
  • Instagram feed: fill the normal feed with images, not reels, such as lifestyle images, offers and product details. [L1367.005]
  • Use Instagram Highlights for social proof and offers. Keep a Reviews highlight with supplier reviews and an Offers highlight that gets a new announcement each week. [L1367.017]

Instagram setup procedure for high click-through rate

  1. Before creating the account, connect to a USA VPN and keep it on at all times while using the account. [L1367.005]
  2. Create a brand new Instagram account using a unique phone number and email address that have not been used before. [L1367.005]
  3. Convert the account to a business page. [L1367.005]
  4. Write a clear offer in the bio that states the product or brand, the shipping promise, social proof, scarcity and a call to action pointing to the link. [L1367.005]
  5. Create two highlights: Reviews, where you post the supplier's reviews to build trust, and Offers, where you post the new offer each week as an announcement. [L1367.005]
  6. Fill the normal feed with images, not reels, such as lifestyle images, offers and product details. [L1367.005]

Reels

  • Before running any reels on a new Instagram page, the feed should already hold about 20 images. After that, add 1 to 3 posts per week. [L1367.007]

Follower count and authenticity

  • A page with many followers but very few likes and comments suggests bought followers. A healthy page should show about 10 percent engagement on posts, so 100,000 followers would mean roughly 6,000 to 10,000 likes and comments. [L0645.041]
  • Buying a few thousand followers for an Instagram or Facebook page was reported to cost under $50, often around $10. This is a trust factor, not a sales driver, and it does not affect the ad account. [L0645.042]
  • Do not buy followers. [L0857.048] (2025.) This conflicts with the cost report in L0645.042. The 2025 source advises against it, and the Source C source reports it as a trust factor with no effect on the ad account. Choose based on the trade-off between the cost reported in L0645.042 and the advice in L0857.048.
  • Paid campaigns to build a few hundred likes for social proof are a different approach from buying followers. [L0429.070] (2022.)
  • A low follower count of about 5 to 10 on a new page did not cause problems in practice. Ad viewers mainly decide based on the ad and the website, so more followers are only a benefit later. [L1108.004]

Linking social accounts to ads

  • Accounts must be active before paid traffic is launched. [L0040.011]
  • When launching test ads, do not connect an Instagram profile to the Business Manager. Clicks from Instagram ads then go straight to the website instead of an inactive profile. [L1108.001]
  • An Instagram profile that has not posted for a long time loses trust once it is linked to running ads. Keep it unlinked until there is a plan to grow a long-term brand audience. [L1108.002]
  • Before testing, publish a few posts on the Facebook page so it looks legitimate. [L1108.003]
  • Ad sign: enter the store name and upload the store logo in the ad sign field. [L1378.020]

The two sources differ on linking. L0040.011 requires active accounts before paid traffic for a complete brand presence. L1108.001 and L1108.002 advise keeping an inactive Instagram profile unlinked during testing. Both apply: the first to a finished brand presence, the second to test campaigns on a profile that is not yet active.

Brand name search signals (older advice)

Older advice (2022).

Procedure for a store name that resembles a well-known store:

  1. Create profiles on Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest, YouTube, TikTok and Snapchat, even if you post little. [L0963.074]
  2. Write a 100-character description of the store with a link to the homepage on each profile. [L0963.074]
  3. Get listed in shopping directories with the store name as link text pointing to the homepage. [L0963.074]
