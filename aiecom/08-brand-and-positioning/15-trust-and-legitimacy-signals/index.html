8.15 Trust and legitimacy signals

Trust foundations

  • Trust comes mostly from professionalism, which shows in bugs, speed, design and service. Quick tricks and low-effort shortcuts do not build lasting trust. [L0390.023]
  • Trust grows when you remove the reasons customers would write or call before buying. Professional design, correct grammar, a visible phone number, a strong About page, a clear contact page, personal product notes and trust badges all serve this goal. [L0910.017]
  • Visitors to a new store trust it more when it is visibly linked to brands, companies or associations they already recognize, because authority transfers by association. A brand with no recognition can borrow credibility this way. [L1050.001]
  • Trust factors such as reviews, video reviews and user-generated content reduce perceived risk because buyers see satisfied customers. They work as both social proof and risk reversal, so they belong in the offer. (older advice, 2021-22) [L0411.029]
  • With a cold audience, trust grows when you educate people on the problem and the solution before you sell. Deep product knowledge and expert-backed proof make customers feel loyal to the brand. [L1377.050]

Business identity: email, phone and address

Business email

  • Use professional business email addresses, not personal accounts, for supplier communication. This establishes legitimacy and credibility and supports long-term business partnerships. [L0119.001]
  • Business buyers can be recognized by their email domain. Orders from company addresses rather than generic free providers such as gmail.com suggest a B2B customer. Track these companies so you can list them as customers once you have enough of them. [L1050.018]

Virtual address

  • Register a virtual business address to satisfy supplier approval requirements and appear legitimate. Do not display it prominently everywhere on the website; use it for approvals while keeping your privacy. (older advice, 2020) [L0268.077, L0268.035]
  • Do not post a physical location everywhere on the site. (older advice, 2020) [L0268.035]
  • If a customer asks for a showroom, answer: "We do not have a physical location; we ship direct from the factory." If the product needs touch to decide, ask suppliers for swatches or samples (about 2 x 2 inch pieces) to send to customers. (older advice, 2022) [L0665.071]

Phone numbers and voicemail

Showing a phone number

  • Display a phone number on your website to increase trust, even if you cannot answer every call. A visible phone number signals credibility to customers. (older advice, 2022) [L0249.044]
  • Put a dedicated business phone number on the website and give it to suppliers instead of a personal mobile. Buyers expect a business to have a separate contact line, and a personal number lowers perceived trust. You can still answer business calls on your own mobile, since the business number only needs to look professional. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1198.002]
  • Show the phone number on the website so visitors can call before buying. (older advice, 2022) [L0429.005]
  • Show the phone number prominently in the header for expensive products. (older advice, 2020) [L0268.035]
  • For business-to-business sales, show your phone number prominently on the trade page and on the quote request form. (older advice, 2021) [L0853.026]
  • Open Phone (open phone.com) provides US virtual phone numbers for non-US residents doing business in the United States. It is the alternative to Mighty Call if you cannot obtain a domestic number. [L0251.028]
  • Mighty Call is a US virtual phone number service. Source A members receive a discount code in the bonus section of the members area. [L0251.029]

Voicemail

  • Use this voicemail greeting template:
Welcome, this is [Your Name] at [store name]. We're currently unable to answer your call because we're helping other customers. Please leave a message with your name, email address, phone number, and the purpose of your call-we'll get back to you as quickly as possible.

[L0249.045] (older advice, 2022)

  • Set up voicemail to be delivered as an email transcript or text message. You see the customer's request immediately and can prioritize urgent calls, such as large orders, during business hours. (older advice, 2022) [L0249.046]

Outsourcing phone support

  • Outsource phone support through a virtual call center billed per call or per minute, rather than paying for fixed full-day staff. This gives 24/7 coverage while you work another job. (older advice, 2022) [L0249.047]
  • Design your support around calls, texts and direct answers rather than purely automated channels if your core buyers are older and prefer personal contact. Know your buyer profile before you choose your service style. [L1472.045]

Presence beyond the website

  • To look legitimate to customers who doubt a new store, build a presence beyond the website. (older advice, 2022) [L0429.005]
    1. Create and keep active a Facebook page, an Instagram account and a Pinterest profile. [L0429.005]
    2. Post a couple of times per week on each, without needing daily posting in large communities. [L0429.005]
    3. Show a phone number on the website so visitors can call before buying. [L0429.005]
  • A store with no social media links looks like a burner store to visitors who check whether a real business stands behind it. Missing social profiles weaken trust. [L1346.027]

Support and service promises

  • State the support you actually provide, such as phone availability, email response time and shipping time. Competitors selling at a loss tend to fall behind on service, so accurate service statements build credibility. (older advice, 2022) [L0665.068]
  • Respond to positive reviews sent by email. Do not respond to public positive reviews on judge.me. (older advice, 2022) [L0665.070]
  • Respond publicly to negative reviews only when the business genuinely failed. Ignore abusive reviewers. (older advice, 2022) [L0665.070]

Supplier logos and authorized retailer status

Authorized retailer statements

  • Display that you are an authorized retailer for the brands you sell. This builds your own authority by association and tells customers the products are genuine. (older advice, 2022) [L0272.027]
  • Show only an authorized retailer badge for a brand you are actually approved to sell. A badge on a product page of a brand you do not represent is a false claim. [L1050.004]
  • Place the authorized retailer statement on that brand's product pages in the product description, for example "We are an authorized retailer for [brand]". [L1050.004]
  • Show the authorized retailer badge on each brand's collection page in Shopify, with a statement that you are an authorized retailer for that brand. [L1050.005]
  • Use this statement near the product title or price, not only in the footer:
We are an authorized [official brand name] retailer.

[L1281.055] (older advice, 2022)

  • When a brand approves you as a reseller, it may send an "authorized retailer" image. If it sends only its logo, create the badge yourself by adding the words "authorized retailer" under the logo. [L1050.017]
  • When you request reseller approval, be ready to explain how you plan to position the supplier's products on your site. Brand owners want to see the intended positioning before they agree. (older advice, 2021-22) [L1060.012]

Creating the badge

Creating an authorized retailer badge (procedure merged from two units that describe the same process; the 2021 version gives the same four steps):

  1. Get the brand logo. [L1050.002]
  2. Open Canva (canva.com; the free version is enough) and load the logo. Add the supplier logo. [L1050.002, L0962.041]
  3. Add the words "authorized retailer" under the logo. [L1050.002, L0962.041]
  4. Save or export the result as your badge image and place it on the store. [L1050.002, L0962.041]

(older advice, 2021 for L0962.041)

  • Keep logos simple. Overloaded logos with cartoon characters, large circles and taglines underneath look messy and reduce trust. (older advice, 2021) [L0962.042]

Logo placement

  • Show supplier logos on product pages, not in the footer on every page. The logo is most useful where the buying decision happens. Footer logos add many images that slow the site without helping sales. (older advice, 2022) [L1281.054]
  • Display the brand names and logos of your suppliers prominently and include a "Shop by Brand" navigation section. This captures customers searching for those brand names directly and builds your authority as an authorized retailer. (older advice, 2022) [L0272.063]
  • Supplier trust badges can go on product pages, with a different badge for each brand. [L0667.029]

Trade associations, B2B and customer logos

Trade associations

  • If you or your business belong to a trade association, show that membership on the About Us page. For example, show an NRA membership for a store selling gun safes, or membership in the International Interior Design Association for a store selling curtains. New stores usually have no such membership unless the owner has one personally. [L1050.012]

B2B trade page

  • For business-to-business drop shipping, add a trade page that shows companies you have worked with and offers bulk discounts. (older advice, 2021) [L0853.026]
  • Include a quote request form that asks for name, email, phone and products of interest. (older advice, 2021) [L0853.026]

Customer logos

  • Once businesses start buying, add their logos to a "Past Clients" or "To the Trade" section to build social proof. Add a logo only after the client has ordered, and build the section over time. (older advice, 2020) [L0961.014]
  • Create an "Our Customers" page on Shopify only once you have business customers, not from day one. List the companies that have bought from you. Visible business clients signal that your business sells to other businesses and raises authority. [L1050.015]

Reviews, testimonials and supplier reviews

Supplier reviews

  • Use supplier reviews on your store only with permission. (older advice, 2021) [L0853.050]
    1. Ask the supplier for permission to use their reviews. [L0853.050]
    2. Once they agree, copy the approved reviews into your review app. [L0853.050]
    3. Never import reviews from other websites without permission. [L0853.050]
  • To get early reviews without waiting for sales, first ask the supplier to share its existing review database for its products. Then use the lesson's method to request reviews from customers who buy from you. [L0941.057]

Testimonials

  • Quoted testimonials can serve as a starting point before real reviews exist. Replace them with real customer testimonials once sales come in. [L1345.039]

Press mentions

  • If a brand has been featured in the press, mention it in the brand's collection page content, not on product pages. Add it in the collection SEO section. [L0667.031]

Charity partnerships

Choosing a partner

  • Charity Navigator is a free website for checking charities. Use it to confirm that a partner is a registered 501(c)(3) and to review its finances before you donate. [L0790.031]
  • When choosing a charity partner, pick three candidates and list the pros and cons of each. Make sure the partner has no downside that could harm your brand or the local economy. [L0790.032]
  • Start with a domestic or local charity rather than an international one when you are new. Local partners are easier to verify and can strengthen trust with domestic customers. [L0790.033]
  • A good benchmark is that about 5% of donations go to administration and executive salaries. Check this before donating and review the partner's past use of funds. [L0790.034]

Permission and wording

  • Mention a charity you support, whether as a fixed percentage of sales, a fixed amount per month or ad hoc donations, on the store only with the charity's explicit permission. Smaller charities or ones you already have a relationship with are more likely to agree. [L1050.013]
  • Naming a charity on your store without its explicit permission is a mistake. Charities are careful about who they are associated with because of charity scams. Ask for approval before you show the association. [L1050.014]
  • A holiday charity pledge should state the percentage of sales, the exact holiday period it applies to, and the named beneficiary with its logo. Limiting the pledge to a defined period makes it verifiable and avoids an open-ended claim. [L0355.024]
  • Humorous or off-brand copy next to a trust element, such as a charity donation message, undermines its seriousness. Keep copy near trust signals consistent with the store's tone. [L0355.004]

Donation amounts

  • The source gives two values for the per-sale donation, and they apply to different situations:
    • The minimum donation per sale was set at 50 cents in the campaign described. [L0790.035]
    • A start at 25 cents is enough to make a charitable claim credible. [L0790.035]
  • Use 25 cents as a credible starting point, and the 50-cent minimum as the value set in the campaign described. [L0790.035]
  • A single sales day that donated 100% of revenue, split across three charities after a hurricane, totaled $75,000. [L0790.035]

Transparency and campaigns

  • Be transparent about your donations and tell customers how much is given, because this drives conversions. The message must be genuine: it should accurately describe the product and the donation, and never misrepresent the brand. [L0790.036]
  • A collaboration framed around social good, such as a promised donation match within a 24-hour window, earned coverage from major publishers. You do not need to announce final donation totals up front, but be ready to be transparent when asked. [L0790.037]
  • Brands with a real cause tend to attract customers, especially when the cause is authentic. Profits can be donated, or customers can be offered an optional extra payment that goes to a specific charity or cause. (older advice, 2019) [L1068.076]
  • Reading glasses donations were first routed through Vision Partners. A later distribution partner could handle about 200,000 pairs globally, shipped in batches of roughly 1,000 units. [L0790.102]

Badges, claims and honesty

Fake and purchased badges

  • Many dropshipping stores display fake or purchased trust badges, such as Google Trusted Store, Forbes, FDA approval or a TM trademark symbol. These badges can be bought cheaply, so they do not prove legitimacy, and you should not copy them. [L0372.042]
  • Paid placements on sites such as Fox or Forbes can be bought through Brand Push Up. The lesson quoted about 195 to 200 USD for one featured placement. This is an optional, pricier way to gain authority. [L1363.012]
  • Big-name brands can create brand association even when margins are low. Including them can strengthen brand recognition and a larger catalog. (older advice, 2019) [L1068.077]

"Not sold on Amazon or eBay" badge

  • Some competitor product pages add a "not sold on Amazon or eBay" badge to signal that the brand sells only through its own store. Use this badge only if it is true for your supply, and check the marketplaces before publishing it. [L0600.037]

Niche and generic trust badges

  • Trust badges that speak to the buyer's own world show that the store understands its customers and fits the niche theme. Generic badges, such as free shipping or a guarantee, fit any store, but a niche-specific badge builds credibility faster. Examples of the pattern: "Built by hikers for hikers" for a trail gear store, or "Tested by dog owners" for a pet bed store. [L1346.025]
  • Show trust badges that cover shipping, product sourcing and a guarantee, such as free shipping, direct-from-manufacturer delivery and a 30-day guarantee. These help new visitors who do not know the brand yet. [L1346.026]

Communicating about delays

  • Send proactive customer communication. If delivery exceeds 2 weeks, email customers every 3 days with shipping status updates, explain the delay and confirm that a free gift is included. [L0324.021]
    1. Monitor shipment tracking daily. [L0324.022]
    2. If expected delivery exceeds 2 weeks, email the customer within the first week. [L0324.022]
    3. Explain the reason for the delay. [L0324.022]
    4. Confirm that a free gift is included in the parcel. [L0324.022]
    5. Send a follow-up email 3 days later with a tracking update. [L0324.022]
