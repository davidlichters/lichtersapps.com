8.22 Brand protection and trademarks

Trademark decisions

  • Trademarking the business name is optional for most dropshippers. It gives peace of mind but is not a requirement to operate. [L0249.043]
  • If you have not invested much in branding, change a name that is very similar to a competitor's. The risks include cease-and-desist letters, customer confusion, and remarketing ads that mix up the brands. In Shopify, a domain can be changed easily by linking a new one. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0357.044]
  • A visible TM or ® mark next to a product name does not mean the trademark is registered. Customers may believe it, but do not use these symbols without a real registration. [L0372.043]
  • A unique mechanism often carries a trademark so that competitors cannot use the same term, which protects the uniqueness of that attribute. Before you write your own name for a mechanism, check whether the brand already holds a trademark for it. [L0465.011]
  • A trademarked logo showed no measurable conversion difference in the owner's experience. Trademark the brand mainly for protection, and consider it at larger scale. [L0941.056]

Registration by jurisdiction

  • Trademark protection depends on the registration. A trademark must be registered in the jurisdiction where the infringement targets customers before a report can succeed. [L0991.020]
  • Brand names can be registered as trademarks in several regions (for example EU, Australia, Cambodia, US), so a trademark check should cover every market where the brand sells. [L0991.021]
  • Do not send reports for a trademark that is not registered in the jurisdiction where the infringer targets customers. Confirm the registration before reporting. [L0991.026]

Copyright

  • Copyright protection covers original product photos and images created by the brand, such as studio shots with models. Keep the original files and the date of publication as proof. [L0991.022]
  • A licensed product (for example, a product made under a license from a major media company) should be stated as licensed in the report. The licensor's name can be included to support the claim that the infringing copies are unauthorized. [L0991.027]
  • If a book is currently run by a main author, a listing risks a DMCA takedown from that author. This can lead to a Meta ad ban or a Shopify DMCA action. Reduce the risk with a custom cover page or an internally customized book. [L1175.024]

Copying competitors' assets

  • Never copy a competitor's unique store assets, such as their logo, custom website copy, or custom-made images. Taking these is treated as stealing intellectual property and is not an acceptable shortcut in dropshipping. Create your own brand assets. [L0365.001]
  • Selling the same type of product as other stores is fine, because similar products are not the problem. The line is crossed only when you copy their original creative work, such as brand assets or written and visual content. [L0365.002]

Infringement detection and reporting

Detection

  • Choose the platforms to monitor based on where the brand's customers and copycats are. For a brand selling apparel and blankets, Facebook and Instagram are the main hotspots. Marketplaces such as Amazon, eBay and Etsy, plus standalone websites, are also searched. Other brands may find different hotspots based on their products. [L0991.018]
  • Detection methods:
    1. Search keywords on each platform.
    2. Search the Meta Ad Library for ads that claim to sell the brand's product or use its name.
    3. Run a general search across platforms to see what appears. [L0991.019]

Reporting process

  • Platforms are sometimes reluctant to act and may suggest doing as little as possible. A complete report with proof of ownership, links, and a clear legal basis gives the platform what it needs to act. [L0991.017]
  • Keep a detailed log of every infringement and report. The process is manual, often slow (days to months), and involves many back-and-forth exchanges with the platform. The log is also the basis for measuring trends. [L0991.016]

Reporting mistakes

Mistake Why it matters Do instead Source
Using a generic or personal email address (e.g. Gmail) Platforms may consider it unauthorized Use a company-domain address for reporting [L0991.024]
Linking private or unpublished material (e.g. shared cloud folders with raw photos or marketing videos) The accused party receives the full report Link only to published material [L0991.025]
Reporting a trademark not registered where the infringer targets customers The report cannot succeed Confirm the registration first [L0991.026]
  • A repeat infringer may return after a takedown with a new page. Keep the older complaint records and original publication dates to show prior ownership, and report the reappearing content again. [L0991.023]

Defense against copycats

  • Create custom content (your own product shoot, creators, or actors) and give the product a custom name to reduce direct copying by competitors. [L1436.065]
  • Use the store or domain name inside custom creator videos, not only the product name. Clips that copy the videos then carry your brand name, which makes them harder for competitors to pass off as their own. [L1436.092]
  • Keep subtle watermarks on product page images, but not on the primary variant image, because Google Merchant Center can reject listings with visible watermarks. The watermark is also evidence: copycat stores often scrape whole image sets, so a watermarked photo on a copied page can support a later takedown. [L1357.050]
  • A private brand is much harder to copy than a dropshipped store because nobody else owns the brand. Becoming the brand suppliers and customers want reduces the risk of being dropped by a supplier. Copying a store is a common worry, but this is the stronger position. [L0511.017]
  • Fast fashion brands can copy a design within about two to three months of its release. Stay ahead by moving on to the next design and relying on quality, brand identity and new ideas rather than one product. [L1438.036]
