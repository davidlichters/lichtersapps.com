8.23 Localization, expansion and exit value

Localizing for a new language market

Recommended procedure for entering a market with a different language:

  1. Test the product with English-language pages in the existing store. [L0298.035]
  2. If results are profitable, create a separate new store on a country-specific domain (.de for Germany). [L0298.035]
  3. Set the shop's default language to the target language so checkout, header, footer, and all pages display natively. [L0298.035]
  4. Use a Shopify store at 1 dollar (new account) with a domain like yourdomain.de. [L0298.035]
  5. Separate stores enable full localization and typically yield higher conversion than single-store multi-language pages. [L0298.035]
  • Many Germans speak English. If a product converts profitably with English pages, it will convert better with German pages, because localization increases confidence and checkout completion. [L0298.036]
  • For German-speaking markets (Germany, Austria, German-speaking Switzerland), offer the website in German. German customers prefer shopping in their native language and have more confidence buying on German-language sites than English ones. Options are a language converter or a separate store instance per language. Separate stores allow language-specific optimization and better conversion rates. [L0307.023]
  • Shoot product photos in recognizable local places and use the words and spelling locals use on the store. Both build trust with buyers in the target market. [L0549.037]
  • Copying a proven competitor's product page and creatives in a new market rarely works. In one France test, a copied page brought only one sale. Instead, research local brands, then build your own images, your own creatives, and a full native translation. [L1158.047]

Expanding to a second country

  • Expand a store to a second country (for example Canada) only after the first market is working well. Duplicating campaigns for each market is a lot of extra work, and the US market alone has room for growth. [L0806.059]
  • A store running as a niche store with a strong brand can still be tested with international markets, but review the brand each time a new market is added. Branding a product with a logo adds cost and requires bulk inventory, so brand only once the product is proven. [L0694.041]

Domains and separate stores per market

  • A second store domain should be nearly identical to the first, with a small addition such as the word "shop" or the suffix "co", so customers recognize it as the same brand. This makes the backup store easy to switch to. [L0352.036]
  • A winning product should get a domain name that reflects its niche, such as a pet name for a pet product, instead of a generic store name. Once it shows consistent profitability for one to one-and-a-half weeks, turn the store into a niche store with more products in the same niche. [L1382.058]
  • When a product works, buy a domain name that matches the product's niche (for example a pet-related name for a pet product) and build a niche store. Add more products in the same niche over time. [L1436.068]
  • Rebrand to a niche store only after the product shows consistent profitability for about one to one and a half weeks. [L1436.069]

Multi-store branding

  • Each store can have its own brand name and domain. Localize the branding to the market language, such as German or English. [L1431.032]
  • Keep products in the same store when they are related to the niche. Unrelated product categories go into a separate store. [L1111.093] (Older advice, 2020.)
  • A store can be pivoted to a different product type within the same niche without renaming it or building a new store. [L0671.169] (Older advice, 2021.)

Niche expansion and product sets

  • Build 3-5 complementary products in the same niche or category rather than jumping to unrelated niches. This enables cross-promotion: customers buying your first product (e.g., resistance bands) will see "frequently bought together" suggestions for your second product (e.g., ab wheel), accelerating organic ranking without additional ad spend. [L0228.014]
  • Adding similar niche categories builds a broader niche and authority in one theme, such as silk products. The brand then attracts a wider set of customers than a single product would. [L0434.009]
  • After one product gains traction, add similar products from the same category that fit the same audience. Building a set of related items in one niche is a practical path from a single product toward a brand. [L1380.060]
  • Consider a niche where current products are not enough and average order value is about $300. Adding higher-priced products within the niche is preferable to starting a new store. [L1111.095] (Older advice, 2020.)

Procedure for building a product set:

  1. Pick products that belong to one niche, not random categories. [L1153.036]
  2. Choose a set of items that a single audience buys together, for example resistance bands, yoga mats and ab wheels in Sports and Outdoors. [L1153.036]
  3. Use this shared audience to cross-promote, for example by emailing a customer who bought resistance bands about an ab wheel. [L1153.036]

Building a brand across platforms

  • Build a brand across multiple platforms, not only on Amazon. A brand is a recognizable experience, so the seller should also operate a Shopify store, a Facebook group, an email list and subscription upsells in the same niche. [L1153.037]
  • A recognizable brand protects the business from new competitors who try to enter the market. It also diversifies revenue, because income from Shopify, eBay or subscriptions adds to Amazon sales. [L1153.038]
  • A community is valued more than a count of social followers. A closed group of about 10,000 engaged members and an email list with open rates above 70 percent add more value at sale than cheap likes, which can be bought in bulk. [L1153.039]
  • Paid social likes are cheap, for example about 1,000 likes for about $5 on freelance marketplaces, and buyers acquiring a brand do not pay for them. Engaged email subscribers and members of a closed community carry much more weight in a brand's sale value. [L1153.084]
  • Specialist stores can build assets buyers value, such as a Facebook group, an email list, and a push notification list. These assets raise valuation because they show an existing audience. [L0554.021]
  • Local agency positioning is built on being the best-known helper in one city, which makes the brand recognized by many businesses there before scaling abroad. [L0440.047]

Brand value at sale

  • A store brand has value beyond its net profit and is worth more when the site is sold. It must be built as a brand, not just a logo, a domain name or a tagline. Start by defining the unique selling proposition (USP), which explains why your store differs from competitors. [L0413.022]
  • A brand built for resale or long-term value is run differently from a dropshipping store. Its priority is audience and brand awareness, so it may run at break-even for the first year or two while building customers. [L0661.055]
  • Buyers of online stores often avoid pure dropshipping stores because their revenue is seen as volatile and the store has no unique product of its own. To make a store more sellable, add private-label elements such as your own logo and labels, and ideally create your own product variant. [L1469.045]

Exit value and the long-term goal

  • Build your store and choose products with the long-term goal of transitioning to a branded store and selling the business. A branded store has significantly higher exit value than a commodity dropshipping operation. [L0280.030]
  • A successful branded Shopify store typically sells for $1 to $1.5 million. Example: a store with $2.6 million in total revenue had an estimated sale value of $1 to $1.5 million. [L0280.031]
  • Monthly profit from actively running a branded Shopify store is typically around $10,000. Life-changing money comes from selling the store for $1 to $1.5 million, not from monthly operations. [L0280.032]

Marketplace listings and broker sales

  • On auction platforms, your listing appears among many scam or low-quality sites, which lowers how premium the site looks. That positioning pushes the multiple down. [L1468.044]
  • Public listings reveal your domain, niche, keywords and traffic, which invites copycats who can rebuild the site. Sites without a strong moat, such as a fledgling dropshipping store, are the most exposed. [L1468.045]
  • Brokers keep the URL and keywords private and share them only with buyers who pay a deposit. This reduces copycats and keeps the marketplace premium. [L1468.046]

Monetizing the brand through an ecosystem

  • Use tracking pixels and email to keep visitors inside your own ecosystem, so each visitor can be sent to related stores and offers. Without this, a visitor who leaves after one purchase is lost to the store. This is the web ring idea. [L0923.051]
  • Diversified revenue from Shopify, eBay or subscriptions adds to the income from a single marketplace, which strengthens the brand's value as an asset. [L1153.038]
