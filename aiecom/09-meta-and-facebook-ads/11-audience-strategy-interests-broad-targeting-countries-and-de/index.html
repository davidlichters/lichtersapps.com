9.11 Audience strategy: interests, broad targeting, countries and demographics

Choosing the Audience Type: Broad, Interest, or Single Interest

Decision by Pixel Maturity

Situation Audience approach Source
New pixel or completely new product; Meta does not yet know the buying audience Single-interest testing, with broad interests of at least 5M L0375.005, L0376.005, L0376.006
Pixel has 1,000 or more purchases Also test a broad audience with no interest targeting L0375.025
Warm audience foundation exists (email list of 100K+ and months of pixel data from customer conversions) Broad campaigns become profitable L0307.032
Broad campaigns run without a warm audience CPCs will spike L0307.032
Small budget, broad underperforms A narrow or broad interest audience may be the better starting point L0644.020, L0999.009
  • Broad campaigns (not custom audience or lookalike) become profitable only after a warm audience foundation exists. That requires a large email list (100K+) and pixel training data from months of customer conversions. Once the pixel has enough behavioural data and an engaged audience to retarget, broad works because the algorithm identifies similar high-value users. Test broad only after the warm audience is established. [L0307.032]
  • A new pixel or a completely new product does not yet know the real buying audience, so single-interest targeting works best there. Single-interest targeting gives the algorithm a clear audience and gathers better data faster. [L0375.005, L0376.005]
  • Once the pixel has 1,000 or more purchases, you can also test a broad audience with no interest targeting. [L0375.025]
  • Broad works straight away for some ad accounts. Others need a large daily spend for a while to collect data, such as £50 per day for a month. This is hard on small budgets, so interest-based ad sets may be the better starting point. [L0644.020]
  • When broad underperforms, move to broad interest audiences, such as camping or pets, before falling back to no-interest targeting. Broad interests can still reach millions of people. [L0999.076]
  • With a small budget, a small narrow interest audience with a relevant ad can still be the better starting point. Broad should be tested but not assumed to work. [L0999.009]
  • Broad targeting with no interests is worth testing, for example in a single ad set, especially for gift-type products during peak gifting seasons. Interest targeting still works for many accounts. [L0692.035]
  • Broad demographic targeting is a valid option that Meta encourages. Run demographic and interest tests both. [L0661.032]
  • Broad is not automatically the best start. In one new-store test with ad sets targeting only one country, the broad ad set was the weakest performer. [L0650.058]
  • Broad is what Facebook currently pushes, so creative quality matters more than fine interest selection. [L0644.019]

Older Advice (Source B course, 2021 to 2022)

  • Older advice (2021-22): For social ads, review the targeting in place for each ad set and check whether it is too broad. If it is, narrow the audience and compare the conversion rate against the broader version. [L0473.007]

Audience Research Before Launch

Facebook Audience Insights

Facebook Audience Insights is a free Facebook tool that shows the size, interests, age distribution and gender split of an audience. It is used to segment an audience before running ads and to see how much reach each interest combination offers. [L0487.029]

  • Facebook Audience Insights (facebook.com/ads/audience_insights) shows the size and common interests of a chosen audience by country, age, gender and interests. Use it even without running ads to learn about the market and find where your customers already spend their attention. [L0643.020]
  • Audience Insights results depend on the location the account appears to be in, so results can show a different country when a VPN is not set to the target market. Check the location before drawing conclusions from the data. [L0487.028]
  • Narrowing the age range too early can hide the real audience. Start with the broad range and let the data show the characteristics. Narrow only after the interest data shows a clear pattern. [L0487.027]

Procedure, research an audience in Audience Insights:

  1. Set the location to the target country. [L0487.026]
  2. Start broad: keep the age range at 18 to 65, keep all genders, and do not narrow the audience yet. [L0487.026]
  3. Type relevant interest keywords into the interests field one at a time and watch the audience size drop with each addition. [L0487.026]
  4. Read the gender split and the age distribution that Facebook shows for the audience. [L0487.026]
  5. Add more specific keywords to refine the niche, then narrow the age range to the bracket that most of the audience falls into. [L0487.026]
  6. Use the resulting audience as the seed audience for ads. [L0487.026]

Reference numbers:

  • With the location set to the United States and ages 18 to 65, the interest 'home grilling' alone showed about 25 to 30 million people. Each added interest shrinks the audience. Restricting the age range to 18 to 24 dropped it to about 1 to 1.5 million in one test. Check the gender split at the broad level first, since that grilling audience was roughly 42 to 46 percent men. [L0487.067]
  • With the United States as location and no interests, the potential audience is about 150 to 250 million people. Adding one large niche brand page narrowed it to about 500,000 to 600,000 people. Adding several related pages raised it to about 700,000 to 800,000 people. [L0641.003]

Procedure, research with brand pages:

  1. Open Audience Insights from the gear/tools menu in Facebook Ads Manager. [L0641.002]
  2. Enter a precise interest, such as a well-known brand in your niche, in the precise interests field and watch the potential audience size on the right. [L0641.002]
  3. Open the Pages suggestions list at the bottom to see pages ranked by relevance to that brand. [L0641.002]
  4. Add each relevant page to the additional interests field and repeat until you have a large master list of related pages. [L0641.002]
  5. Save the audience so you can reuse it when building lead campaigns. [L0641.002]

Scale check before cold campaigns:

  • Before launching cold Facebook campaigns for a niche product such as running gear, check the potential audience size. In the example, roughly 33 million people in the United States could be reached through Facebook ads. [L1203.038]
  • Lists of pages and interest groups found through the Facebook search can be used as targeting interests in Power Editor, to reach new cold audiences that have not yet visited your store. [L1243.005]

Market and Customer Research

  • Define a dream customer before creating ads, then target that exact group by gender, age and country in each ad set. In the example, the target group was women over 40 in specific countries. [L0519.025]
  • Use persona research to choose ad targeting and creative angles. The platforms and communities where the audience spends time (Reddit, Quora, Facebook groups, Instagram) show where to test ads and which language to use. [L0395.008]
  • Favorite Online Spaces should list where target customers spend time online. Facebook is a common answer, but specialist forums, relevant subreddits with active members, Quora for question-based communities, and LinkedIn or Twitter for business audiences often reveal the more precise places. [L0872.025]
  • Older advice (Source B course, 2021 to 2022): Favorite Online Spaces should list where target customers spend time online, as above. [L0872.025]

Target people before they become product aware:

  • Target people before they become product aware, because the auction for people already searching for your product type is crowded and expensive. Look for the earlier interest that leads to your product instead. [L0643.021]
  • An Australian health and fitness business targeted people who follow a popular weight-loss TV show, roughly 500,000 people in Australia. The common interests Audience Insights showed included celebrity trainers, clean eating recipes, a 12-week transformation program and an activewear brand. These were less contested, so bids around 39 cents (against a maximum of 19 cents) brought strong sales. [L0643.022]

Procedure, chain from one interest to the next:

  1. Start with a broad interest that matches your customer's situation. [L0643.023]
  2. Pull the common interests from Audience Insights. [L0643.023]
  3. Test these interests with a small budget to see if the audience buys. [L0643.023]
  4. If an interest converts, run the same process again from that interest to find the next audience. [L0643.023]

Avatar Research from Ad Data

Procedure, ad data analysis for avatar research, in order:

  1. Gender: spend share per gender, CPM, CPC and CTR per gender, conversion difference, the likely primary buyer, and whether a proxy buyer is involved. [L0613.037]
  2. Age: spend, performance and conversion by cohort; under-served cohorts with high value; likely life-stage explanations; likely distress by cohort. [L0613.037]
  3. Time: peak engagement, conversion peaks, late-night activity as a sign of distress, and workday versus weekend differences. [L0613.037]
  4. Hypotheses: primary avatar, secondary avatars, proxy-buyer signals, and distress indicators from timing and engagement. [L0613.037]

Proxy buyer add-on:

  • Identify who buys for whom and the relationships involved. Estimate the percentage of buyers who are proxies from ad data. [L0613.042]
  • Build a full avatar for the proxy with extra sections covering their relationship to the end user, how their emotions differ from the direct user, what 'saving' the loved one would mean for their own identity, and the hurtful things they fear hearing about the loved one. [L0613.042]

Finding Interests

What Interests Are

  • Interest targeting is about what the audience likes, follows, shares, comments on and engages with on Instagram and Facebook. The aim is to find interests that lead to engagement, website visits and purchases. There is no universally right or wrong interest as long as it matches the target audience. For example, do not target men and football for a makeup store. [L0650.004]
  • Interests are based on what people follow on social media, not what they buy. [L0642.007]
  • An interest includes everyone who engaged with the topic, including people who dislike it. Interest targeting therefore does not guarantee the right audience. [L0639.019]
  • Interest targeting should follow what the ideal customer follows on social media, not only the product itself. The interest does not need to be related to the product, as long as it reaches the right buyers. [L0661.029]
  • Find interests where your buyers already spend time. Think of the product, the customer it serves, and the brands, TV shows, magazines or celebrities that customer follows, then use those as interests. A broad interest related to the product or the audience is enough. Deep research is not needed. [L0647.020]
  • Look beyond the product category. Think about what else the target audience does: hobbies, parenting, work, brands, astrology, sports, food. Start with obvious interests, then add less obvious ones. [L0642.007]
  • Using interests that are only loosely related to the product leads to poor results. Interests should reflect what people actually follow on social media and match the niche, for example culturally specific interests for a specific audience. [L0678.016]
  • Starting with interest targeting that is too broad, and running it for about a month without results, wastes budget. Narrow the interests to match the audience once the product and offer are in order. [L0678.017]

Non-Obvious Interests That Sold

  • A shoe store found that its best interests were astrology, Kim Kardashian and makeup, none of them directly related to shoes. Its target audience was highly active on astrology content, and when they saw the shoe ads they visited the site and bought. [L0642.008]
  • A shoe brand running ads in Australia found that its winning interest was astrology, because women over 40 who were interested in the brand were also interested in astrology. The advertiser could scale that interest a lot. A non-obvious interest linked to the actual buyer can outperform the interests that look most relevant. [L0650.059]
  • For women over 45 in Australia, the research surfaced health, fashion, spirituality and astrology as interests. Astrology performed best for a shoe product. For men's grooming products in Germany, football clubs such as FC Bayern and Dortmund worked as interests. [L0661.030]
  • Interests that performed well for an Australian fashion store targeting women over 45 included discounts, Black Friday and astrology. [L1212.029]
  • Very broad celebrity interests, such as famous singers, reached large audiences that did not buy a niche jewelry product. Interests tied to the community the product serves, such as a culture-specific style interest combined with a demographic filter for that community, produced results. [L0678.044]
  • A travel accessory product tested broad first and then switched to an outdoor and camping audience. The landing page and creatives were changed to show the product in mountains and by the sea, and results improved. [L0999.010]
  • For a floor mat product, the interest search used cleaning terms: 'Clean house' and 'Carpet cleaning', the latter with an audience of about 13 million. Further ad sets then used other broad floor-related interests such as 'Carpet dust mop', 'Mop' and 'Floor', one interest per ad set. [L0647.021]
  • For a kids product, use interests such as kids, family, fatherhood, motherhood and parenting. Unrelated interests, such as magazines, TV programs or cartoons, can also work and are worth testing. [L0692.032]
  • Problem-solving household and washroom products can work on Facebook with audiences aged about 25 to 30 and older, because they recognize the everyday problem right away. [L0708.055]
  • Target interests similar to the problem being solved, such as the game, the platform or related hobbies, and match the audience age and gender to the buyer profile. [L0513.034]

Interest Types by Affinity

  • For a higher-affinity audience, look for interests tied to big brands, influencers that are still available as interests, and TV shows that match the product. These show the user has a real connection with the product, not just a single click on an interest. [L0645.039]
  • Engaged shopper audiences have higher purchase intent but also raise CPM, because many brands target the same group. [L0363.059]
  • Interests where other brands and dropshippers already convert have high CPMs because of competition. Low-intent audiences such as engagement or link clicks have low CPMs but rarely sell. Start with the high-converting interests even though they cost more per impression. [L0648.010]
  • Do not exclude dropshipper or AliExpress interests. The exclusion adds a filter that raises CPM and shrinks the audience (example: from 100 million to 68 million). It also made the ads more visible to spy tools. [L0648.020]
  • If audience filters like engaged shoppers perform poorly, switch to broad audiences. Keep a main broad audience running alongside any interest audience. [L0363.060]
  • Each extra filter on Facebook also increases the cost per impression. [L0363.059]

Procedure: Finding the Next Interest

The four sources below describe the same workflow, merged here. Keep the ad set name in sync with the interest.

  1. Open the ad set that already has a working interest, or duplicate the ad set that is working. [L0691.019, L0648.038]
  2. In the ad set's detailed targeting, search a broad term such as home decor, or open the interest section of the ad set. [L0642.052, L0650.005]
  3. Click 'Suggestions' to get interest ideas from Meta, and under the working interest, choose one of the related interests Meta lists. [L0650.005, L0691.019]
  4. Select one suggested interest and copy its exact name. [L0642.052, L0650.005]
  5. Replace the interest in the copied ad set, or add it. Paste the name at the top of the ad set name, so you always know which interest each ad set contains. Rename the ad set with the new interest name before publishing. [L0648.038, L0642.052, L0650.005]
  6. Repeat with other interests, including adjacent themes such as flowers or bonsai for a home decor store. Click the cross in the top left and publish. [L0650.005]
  7. Keep the first ad set Broad and repeat with a different interest for each additional ad set. [L0642.052]
  8. Repeat until you have 12 to 15 tested interests. [L0648.038]

Additional rules:

  • Test several interests for the same product, even with a good CTR. Some interests bring visitors who never buy, while others convert. Keep only the interests that produce sales. [L0388.037]
  • Test many interests and ad variants to find audiences with a lower cost per acquisition and a higher margin. Some interests bring strong results while others barely cover costs, so each one needs its own test. [L0661.031]
  • Use Peeksta (peeksta.com) to find niche-specific interests for single-interest targeting. [L0375.006]
  • Interest choices matter even when platforms push broad. In one test, a brand-specific interest such as a snowboard brand audience consistently beat broad targeting, and a pets interest performed better than a narrower dog-owner interest. Test several interest levels before choosing one. [L0999.075]
  • A high attention rate alone does not show that an audience is buying. In one test with two interests, the home and garden interest had a poor attention rate but produced the sales. A PC-focused interest had about 40 percent attention and no add-to-carts. Judge interests by sales. [L0999.077]
  • Interests can still be used with broad-level choices, such as 'cats' or 'pets' for a cat toy. They matter less than before. [L0691.018]
  • Facebook builds a profile of the audience from the ad creative, copy and who responds to it. Naming the target audience in the copy, for example an architect using a specific piece of software, helps the system find the right people. [L0644.021]

Discovering Interests from Adjacent Products

  • For a pet product, a single-interest test ad set can target a broad interest such as 'I love dogs'. Each test ad set then uses a different interest of the same kind, with an audience of at least 5M. [L0376.033]
  • Interest targeting for a female fashion test: use Gender = female; Age = 40+ (the lesson uses 40 to 65+ in its search); Country = one or more target countries. Use the same age and gender settings as the research search, so the test matches the product's proven audience. [L0661.033]
  • Early tests used women 40+ as the audience. Younger audiences were excluded when data for them looked weak. When scaling, the winning group was narrowed to women 45+ interested in astrology in two cities, and that audience produced most of the sales. [L0388.038]
  • For a fishing niche outside the usual women-focused audience, the test audience was set to men over 30. Testing a demographic that differs from your core customer is a deliberate step when a niche shows strong results elsewhere. [L0388.045]
  • Interests for a gendered product, such as men's jewelry, when the seller has no prior success with that audience: see the procedure in the Gender section. [L0773.060]
  • Postpartum audiences suit hair loss products. Targeting parents zero to 12 months after childbirth with a motherhood interest performed well. Seasonal holiday audiences work only briefly. [L0549.048]
  • Facebook's audience estimate for a new parents interest in Canada was about 30,000 to 40,000 people. That size is too small for some products, while the same audience performed well for a hair product. [L0549.047]
  • Small interest audiences can produce a few early sales and then stop, possibly because the platform exhausts the easiest buyers first. This is one possible explanation, and the ad account data should decide. [L0524.041]
  • Target the top-level interest related to the product. Find where the buyer lives online and what they follow, then move from broad to specific. [L0647.020]
  • On Facebook, you can target people travelling between two countries for an event. Open Facebook Ads detailed targeting and search for a travel behavior filter, such as people who travel from one country to another. Another option is the filter for people who recently moved from the origin country to the destination country. If neither returns a usable audience, a custom audience has to be built. [L1218.032]
  • Instagram boosts can target a defined audience, such as women aged 25 to 55 interested in luxury products, tea and organic goods, to find likely buyers instead of accumulating followers. [L1016.063]

  • Pick interests with an audience size above 10 million, starting with big interests related to the product. Then add Facebook's suggested interests from them and test one at a time. [L0645.038]

Stated Size Is Not the Active Audience

  • An interest audience stated as 30 million is not shown in full. With a purchase objective, the platform selects a much smaller pocket of users who showed buying-related behaviour, so the active pocket is far smaller than the stated size. [L0639.005]
  • Different interests can each reach different pockets and may overlap. Meta offers an audience overlap check for this. [L0639.005]

Testing Single Interests

Structure

Initial test structure for a new Facebook campaign with a limited budget:

  1. Start with five ad sets, each with one interest. [L1216.017]
  2. Alternatively, start with four interest ad sets and one broad ad set. [L1216.017]
  3. Once sales come in and the store converts well, expand the number of interests or ad sets. [L1216.017]
  • Start Facebook interest testing with four or five interests, to spend the budget wisely in the beginning. Scale the number of interests only once the store has sales and a good conversion rate. [L1216.018]
  • An initial interest test usually covers about 5 to 10 interests, from which 2 to 3 or more winners often emerge. Keep adding new interests after that, because the goal of horizontal scaling is a steady stream of new winning pockets, not only the first winners. [L1335.041]
  • The five ad sets compete against each other. Use one broad, related interest per ad set. [L0647.037]

Launch settings for a new interest test:

  • Set the launch time to 12:00 AM (midnight) of the next day. [L1335.007]
  • Use a starting budget of about $10. [L1335.007]
  • Name the ad set after the interest. [L1335.007]
  • When testing new interests, duplicate an existing ad set and change only its targeting. [L0692.049]
  • For a test ad set in Switzerland, set location to Switzerland only, minimum age 25+, and language German when the ads are in German. Start with a Broad ad set without interests, then add one interest per ad set. [L0642.004]
  • Use one ad per ad set during testing. In Facebook testing, a single creative per ad set is used. [L1212.041]

One Interest per Ad Set

  • Each testing ad set should hold exactly one interest, so that its results can be traced back to that single interest. Multiple interests in one ad set blur which audience is responding. Keep the other ad set settings identical so that only the interest differs between them. [L0647.036]
  • Test one interest per ad set and do not stack several interests. With stacked interests you cannot tell which one produced a sale, while Meta keeps spending on the weaker ones. [L0648.018]
  • Adding several interests to one testing ad set makes it impossible to tell which interest produced the purchases. [L0647.037]
  • Interest stacking is not required. Test each interest in its own ad set first, and then optionally combine winners into one ad set to see which setup performs better. There is no fixed right answer. [L0642.009]
  • Meta has moved away from narrow stacked interests and now pushes broad targeting. Choose one interest per ad set only if that interest gives better return on ad spend than broad targeting. [L0353.028]
  • Keep creatives that perform acceptably across ad sets, and drop only those that perform badly in a consistent way. [L0692.049]

Test Design Examples

  • For a new-store or new-product test, test ad copies and different interest audiences, because the winning audience is found through testing. Specific interests often outperform broad targeting. The ad copy here is the primary text in the Facebook ad, not the website copy. [L1185.027]
  • Test a new product with an interest test, then use the interest that works best for scaling. [L1212.042]

Broad Targeting and Creative as Targeting

Broad Is Valid, With Conditions

  • Facebook's algorithm finds the right audience for a broad campaign on its own if the creative is strong. Broad targeting is therefore a valid start, with country as the main control. You learn the audience from the data the ads generate. [L1218.012]
  • Meta recommends broad targeting, where the creative itself should identify the buyers. Once the creative does that well, you can go fully broad with only country selection. [L0639.019]
  • Broad targeting should be tested but not assumed to work. [L0999.009]
  • Facebook's broad targeting is improving and interest targeting may be phased out. The ad and creative relevance matter more than fine interest selection. Even when platforms push broad, a relevant narrow test can still outperform. [L0999.062]
  • Facebook keeps removing detailed interest options and pushes toward broad targeting, so the relevance of the creative and landing page matters more. In one store, uploading a customer list into an Advantage+ campaign still produced broad delivery that underperformed the niche approach. [L0995.051]
  • Once an interest or niche produces sales, narrow the creative and targeting to that niche. A catch-all ad is acceptable when the budget is low and the problem is common to many people. [L0644.022]
  • After Apple's 2023 iOS update, tracking became weaker, so broad targeting with expanded audiences became the norm, and the creative now does most of the targeting. Meta expanded audience sizes by turning on detailed targeting in most accounts, and bigger audiences give the pixel more data to optimise with. [L0398.001]
  • Since the iOS 14 update, interest targeting has lost much of its precision, and Meta pushes advertisers toward broad targeting. Broad targeting works best when the pixel has a lot of sales data. [L0691.018]

Conflict: the tracking change is dated to the 2023 iOS update in one unit [L0398.001] and to the iOS 14 update in another [L0691.018]. Both describe the same shift toward broad targeting.

Creative Does the Targeting

  • The strongest long-term strategy is a creative that wins on its own inside broad targeting, regardless of the learning phase. An ad that hits and sells well is more important than optimising the algorithm. [L0353.029]
  • The creative itself does much of the targeting now. The people, text and problem shown in an image or video tell Meta who to show it to. For example, an image of a 50+ person with the text 'foot pain' pushes the ad toward that audience. [L0691.020]
  • Facebook builds a profile of the audience from the ad creative, copy and who responds to it. [L0644.021]
  • Keep producing new creatives with different hooks, angles and narratives. [L0644.019]
  • Keep the ad text, video and visuals relevant to the chosen interest, because the algorithm reads them to decide who sees the ad. [L0999.076]
  • Broad Advantage+ campaigns depend on the quality of the ads, because Meta chooses the audience itself. The direction of Meta is toward broad targeting without interests, so creative quality becomes the main lever. [L1316.019]
  • Since 2026, changing only the hook or primary text of a duplicated video ad no longer reaches a different audience. To target several audiences you need several distinct concepts, because viewers respond differently to short videos (around 30 seconds) and long videos (around 15 minutes). [L0374.008]
  • Meta's Andromeda delivery system is described as LLM-based and shows ads to users at different awareness levels. A campaign therefore needs messages for several awareness stages rather than one format repeated. [L1195.057]
  • Running the same creatives across many accounts or sellers raises the risk that Meta flags the business. [L1195.057]

Advantage+ Audience and Audience Controls

  • Advantage+ audience lets Meta's AI find people for the ad. Optional audience suggestions steer it toward the people you expect to respond. When no suggestion is added, the AI searches on its own. [L0614.019]
  • Ads Manager's estimated audience size does not include Advantage+ audience options. [L0614.019]
  • Under Suggest an audience, Meta can reach people beyond the audience controls when it expects better results. The suggestion field is optional and can be left empty if strict targeting is wanted. [L0865.063]
  • Use Advantage+ audience with broad targeting for the standard setup. Leave the default age range of 18 to 65+ and all genders unless there is a very specific reason to restrict them. [L0865.030]
  • Ad set > Audience > Audience definition: keep Broad. Narrow and Further limit the reach of your ads are not recommended. Only location and audience exclusions should normally be changed. [L0865.031]
  • Ad set > Audience > Languages: a language restriction is available if ads should run only in one language. Leave it at the default for a standard setup. [L0865.034]
  • In a product test, leave the ad set audience broad. Do not add detailed targeting or other audience controls, and only restrict audience settings for legal or practical constraints. [L1193.015]
  • For worldwide targeting, Facebook can search wider than your strongest markets, see the Countries section. [L0067.025]
  • When testing multiple countries at once, break the data down by country. If one country pushes CPM and CPC up sharply (e.g. the US), remove that country from targeting. [L1316.036]

Country Testing Workflow

Country testing workflow for a new product:

  1. Start with the Big Five markets: USA, Australia, New Zealand, Canada and UK. [L0361.024]
  2. After a couple of days, break down the results by country. [L0361.024]
  3. Remove countries with high CPM and no sales. [L0361.024]
  4. Keep scaling the countries that work. [L0361.024]

Procedure, per-country ad sets for a clean comparison:

  1. Create a separate ad set for each country you want to test. [L0644.024]
  2. Use the same ad and the same copy in every ad set. [L0644.024]
  3. Keep the ad sets in the same campaign and give each the same budget. [L0644.024]
  4. Compare results per country after enough spend, so the test shows which country responds best. [L0644.024]
  • Targeting several countries in one ad set lets Facebook send most of the budget to the country that engages first. In one test about 90% of the reach went to the UK, while Australia and New Zealand got almost no spend. [L0644.023]
  • Meta delivery optimises toward the chosen conversion goal and moves spend to the ad set or country it predicts will convert best. A multi-country ad set can therefore send nearly all budget to one country. [L0644.044]
  • If the goal is to compare countries rather than let the system choose, put each country in its own ad set with the same ad, the same copy and an equal budget. [L0644.044]
  • Conflict: one ad set with several countries lets Meta choose the market and will concentrate spend [L0644.023, L0644.044]. Separate ad sets per country are required to compare countries cleanly [L0644.024, L0644.044].
  • When running several countries in one campaign, run them in one campaign and break the data down by country to find the best performers, rather than running a separate campaign per country. [L0774.037]
  • When running several countries in one campaign, break the results down by country in reporting so each market can be judged separately. To put the full budget behind one market, target only that country. [L0661.034]
  • In a test, only the variable under test should differ between ad sets. Unequal budgets or different creatives make the comparison of countries or audiences invalid. [L0644.025]
  • When a product sells in multiple countries, test it in several countries and find the one where it converts best. Then focus the budget there. [L1176.016]
  • If CPM is high in one country while sales still come from it, remove that country from the ad targeting and keep the others. Compare the country breakdown of the ad account to find the expensive market. [L1216.001]
  • If one country pushes CPM and CPC up sharply, such as the US, remove that country from targeting. [L1316.036]

Testing Outside the Main Market

  • Launch new ad sets targeting countries outside your main market (for example UK, Canada, Australia without USA) to test if geographic mix improves performance. Test this in parallel, not by cutting existing ads. [L0298.030]
  • German-speaking neighbouring countries such as Switzerland and Austria are a logical next step after a German market performs well, because the German-language store and creatives can be reused there. Test them as separate markets with their own spend rather than assuming the German results carry over. [L0694.043]
  • For very small countries, remove as much targeting as possible and go broad, since narrow targeting limits the audience. For larger markets such as the US, Australia, Germany and other top-tier countries, specific interests can still help find relevant buyers. [L0639.018]
  • When CPC or CPM is too high, the fix is an alternative, broader target audience instead of one narrow audience. For example, expand from only the United States to a group of top-five English-speaking countries such as the UK, New Zealand and Canada. [L0874.013]

Worldwide Targeting

  • For worldwide targeting, keep the strongest English-speaking countries in the audience while letting Facebook search wider. Exclude countries with low buying power or high scam risk: Russia (hit or miss), India (scammy) and Brazil (scammy traffic). Include the top five English-speaking countries. [L0067.025, L0640.032]

Country Data Example

  • On Facebook, mobile converted best among devices, followed by tablet and then desktop. In one example, desktop was three times as expensive per click and per lead as mobile, roughly nine times as expensive overall. iOS converted higher than Android. [L0643.048]

Geo-Targeting and Locations

  • Geo-targeting in Meta ads can target zip codes, whole states, or a radius around a city instead of an entire country. This lets you limit ads to customers near a supplier's warehouse. [L0942.022]
  • In the US, Facebook ads can target people by income, household size, political party and the last car they test drove, and geo-targeting can reach people by location in real time. This is useful for higher-priced offers, but it is not available in every country. [L0643.027]
  • In the Locations field, typing a country name returns a list of matching countries, regions, cities, states and addresses. Each suggestion must be selected from the list. [L0614.020]
  • The Include dropdown adds the location to the audience. Browse or bulk entry can add several locations at once. [L0614.020]
  • In the ad set's Locations field, add each target country with the Include control. Under the field, Meta shows the default option 'People living in or recently in this location', which is left as it is. [L1193.025]

Language

  • Choose all languages or English as the language setting, unless the landing page and ads are in another language. If the store is in German, select Germany together with the German language. [L0648.019]
  • Run ads in the language of the target market, for example German for a German-speaking audience. English ads weaken trust with local buyers. [L1108.018]
  • If a product's language version is only English, run English ads. Match the ad language to the language of the product or game in each country. [L1176.017]
  • Ad set > Audience > Languages: a language restriction is available if ads should run only in one language. Leave it at the default for a standard setup. [L0865.034]
  • For a test ad set in Switzerland, set language German when the ads are in German (Switzerland has German, French and Italian parts). [L0642.004]

Gender

  • Keep gender set to all genders and do not narrow it, because Meta optimises by performance. Narrow gender targeting only when the creative and product are clearly for one gender, such as a men's grooming kit. [L0648.015]
  • Gender breakdown: in Ads Manager, open the breakdown menu and select Breakdown by gender. Check where budget and sales go, because a single gender often takes 70 to 90 percent of the budget, even with open targeting. Other breakdowns in the same menu are By time and By delivery. [L0648.042, L0648.064]
  • If the product is not clearly gender-specific, test open targeting and a single-gender split test (for example 10 tests) against each other. Gifts can cross genders, so the data decides which gender converts. [L0648.043]
  • Default gender in the standard setup is all genders. [L0865.030]
  • Testing a gendered product, such as men's jewelry, when the seller has no prior success with that audience:
  1. Start with broad interest targeting. [L0773.060]
  2. Confirm the CTR is good and that the target gender engages with the ad. [L0773.060]
  3. Test interests that the target group typically follows, such as cars or sports, adjusted to each country. [L0773.060]
  4. Run multiple countries in the same campaign and break the data down by country to see which performs best. [L0773.060]
  • In Audience Insights, the gender split at the broad level should be checked first, before narrowing. A grilling audience was roughly 42 to 46 percent men. [L0487.067]

Reading Breakdowns: Demographic and Time Analysis

  • Target Evidence Prompt uses your Facebook ad account metrics to refine customer targeting and messaging. Use it with 2 to 3 days of product running data. It covers:
    • gender breakdown with performance by gender [L0221.004]
    • age breakdown with performance by cohort [L0221.004]
    • time-of-day and day-of-week spending patterns [L0221.004]
    • top performing ad creative themes [L0221.004]
    • geographic concentration data [L0221.004]
  • Ads Manager breakdowns: open the Breakdown menu and choose By gender, By time or By delivery to see where budget is spent and which segment converts. Check the gender breakdown after tests. [L0648.064]
  • Use the ad data analysis procedure in the Audience Research section above for gender, age, time and hypotheses. [L0613.037]

Keeping Variables Isolated

  • In a test, only the variable under test should differ between ad sets. Unequal budgets or different creatives make the comparison of countries or audiences invalid. [L0644.025]
  • Each testing ad set should hold exactly one interest, and the other settings stay identical, so only the interest differs. [L0647.036]
  • Test a single variable per run: country, interest, gender or age. For a country test, use the same ad and copy in each ad set with equal budgets. [L0644.024, L0644.025]
  • When testing several interests with the same creative, keep the creative identical across ad sets where the test is about audience. [L0692.049]
  • Keep creatives that perform acceptably across ad sets, and drop only those that perform badly in a consistent way. [L0692.049]
  • Test different ad copies and different interest audiences because the winning audience is found through testing. [L1185.027]
  • Keep ad set settings identical except the interest. Duplicate an existing ad set and change only its targeting. [L0647.036, L0692.049]
  • Single-gender split tests are run against open targeting, so the gender variable is the one under test. [L0648.043]

Scaling Winning Audiences

  • Once an interest or niche produces sales, narrow the creative and targeting to that niche. [L0644.022]
  • Interests that convert are used for scaling. The interest that works best is used for scaling. [L1212.042]
  • When scaling, the winning group can be narrowed. In one case the group was narrowed to women 45+ interested in astrology in two cities, and that audience produced most of the sales. [L0388.038]
  • Targeting that works should be expanded horizontally: keep adding new interests after the first winners, because the goal is a steady stream of new winning pockets. [L1335.041]
  • Scale the number of interests only once the store has sales and a good conversion rate. [L1216.018]
  • Scale the winning audience. Keep the winners at the same ad settings and expand the number of interests once sales are steady. [L1216.017]
  • Interest choice is judged by sales, not by attention. [L0999.077]
  • A small narrow audience can produce a few early sales and then stop. Check the ad account data to decide. [L0524.041]
  • Broad targeting underperforms on some products. If broad underperforms, move to broad interest audiences before falling back to no-interest targeting. [L0999.076]

Broad Versus Interest Targeting

Situation Approach Source
Product is not very specific or niche, and you target top five or e-packet countries Broad targeting with an audience of at least about 5 million people [L1469.034]
Product is narrow Go narrow with targeting [L1440.021]
Sales already come in and a niche is working Narrow the creative to that niche [L1426.026]
Budget is low and the problem is common to many groups Catch-all ads are acceptable [L1426.026]
  • Meta has been pushing broad targeting in recent years and has removed many interest options, so broad audiences often beat very narrow interest targeting. Advantage+ shopping campaigns follow this direction by using the account's purchase and engagement history rather than manual audience choices. [L1335.049]
  • Broad targeting lets the algorithm choose the audience without interest restrictions. It works immediately for some ad accounts, but other accounts may need a large spend over roughly a month before the data supports good results, so the budget must be able to absorb that test. [L1426.006]
  • On Facebook you can target broad and still do well, because the algorithm uses the ad copy to find the audience. [L1440.021]
  • Run separate campaigns for broad, niche and specific targeting, run them in parallel, compare which performs best, and keep the winning targeting. [L1469.031]
  • A broad-targeting test on Facebook was suggested at about £50 per day for one month for accounts that need large volumes of data. That level is high for small stores, so run it only when cash flow can absorb the test. [L1426.049]

Audience Size

  • For products that are not very specific or niche, use broad targeting with audiences of at least about 5 million people, especially when targeting top five or e-packet countries. Let strong creatives define the audience through engagement data and pixels. [L1469.034]

Creative as Targeting

  • Facebook builds a profile of who responds to an ad from its copy and creative. [L1426.025]
  • A niche message, such as a specific profession with the problem named in its language, lets the algorithm find that audience faster and makes the message resonate more. [L1426.025]
  • When sales already come in and a niche is working, narrow the creative to that niche, for example nurses or snowboarders in parks. [L1426.026]
  • Catch-all ads are acceptable when budget is low and the problem is common to many groups. [L1426.026]

Finding Interests

  • Facebook interests are determined by what people like and follow on their social media, not by what they say about themselves. An unrelated interest such as astrology can produce the best results if that is what the buyers follow. [L1439.017]
  • To find new interests similar to a winner, open the ad set, scroll to Detailed targeting and click Suggestions. Pick related interests with a good audience size, for example a category interest such as home appliances for a cleaning product. [L1335.044]
  • Look for interests that match the customer's life, not only the product. Examples include family, hobbies or astrology, so the audience reflects who buys the product. [L1469.032]
  • A post-purchase survey can ask why the customer bought and what they plan to use it for. The answers can refine interest targeting for Facebook ads. [L1412.039]

Interest Testing Process

  1. Find out who has already bought, including age, gender and country. [L1439.018]
  2. List possible interests from their everyday life, such as hobbies, food, parenting, health, TV series and streaming shows. [L1439.018]
  3. Test many different interests in parallel, within the daily ad budget. [L1439.018]
  4. Keep the interest that brings sales and scale it over a longer period. [L1439.018]
  • Do not test too many interests at the same time, because the number that can be tested depends on the daily ad budget. Running ten interests with a small budget gives too little data per interest. [L1439.019]
  • For gift products, the person who pays is often not the person who uses the product. Test interests that match the likely gift buyer's own life, such as what they follow and do, rather than only the recipient's profile. [L1439.053]
  • For a jewelry store, interests built around the product itself or around occasions, such as jewelry, anniversaries and relationship status, produced weak results. Fashion accessories worked better, and a fitting interest was found only by testing many options. Do not assume that buyers follow an interest just because it matches the product or occasion. [L1439.054]
  • For a known buyer profile of UK women over 35, possible interests to test include parenting, motherhood, cooking, health, work and career topics, and popular UK TV programmes or Netflix shows. Use such a list as a starting set and keep only the interests that bring sales. [L1439.055]

Interest Cases

  • A shoe store found that the strongest interest was astrology, which had nothing to do with shoes. Women above 45 living in Australia who were interested in astrology became very good customers. [L1469.033]
  • A jewelry store had zero sales before joining mentoring. After the store focused on a clear offer, changed the product images, added a two-for-one bundle and tested interests, sales began to rise. About 20 sales came in one week, but sales stalled at one or two per day afterward. The winning interest for this brand was one that unexpectedly fit the buyers, and the sales came mainly from UK women over 35. [L1439.020]

Country Selection

  • For a clean country test, place each country in its own ad set with the same ad, copy and campaign, and control the spend per ad set with equal budgets. Putting several countries in one ad set lets Facebook pick the winner by engagement and can starve smaller markets. [L1426.027]
  • When countries share one ad set, Facebook can send 90% of the spend to one country, here the UK, and leave Australia and New Zealand with almost nothing. Separate countries into their own ad sets to avoid this. [L1426.028]

Age, Gender and Demographics

  • Facebook audiences skew toward users over 30, while TikTok and Snapchat attract younger users. Facebook ads need higher production quality than UGC-style content, which fits younger audiences better. [L1431.041]
  • Use the buyer profile from existing sales, including age, gender and country, as the starting point for demographic choices. [L1439.018]
  • Demographic examples from tested stores: UK women over 35 for a jewelry store [L1439.020], and women above 45 in Australia for a shoe store [L1469.033].

Keeping Variables Isolated

  • In a valid test, all variables must stay equal except the one being tested. Unequal spend between two countries, such as 2 versus 5 per day, distorts the comparison. [L1426.029]
  • When you duplicate a winning interest into a new ad set to test a different interest, delete the older interest from the duplicate, so the test isolates the new interest and each test runs on its own. This keeps the results of each test clean. [L1382.049, L1436.080]

Further details

  • Target newer smartphone models as a proxy for buying power, since people who can afford a new phone are more likely to buy higher-priced products. [L0643.052]

  • To target phone types, use Facebook's option to target Android users rather than limiting the audience to one phone brand, so that the product still reaches a broad group of customers. [L1161.026]
