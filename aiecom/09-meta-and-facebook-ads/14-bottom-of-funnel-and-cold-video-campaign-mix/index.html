9.14 Bottom-of-Funnel and cold video campaign mix

Bottom-of-Funnel Ads: When They Work

  • Bottom-of-funnel (BOF) ads work for products whose audience already understands the category and can buy without explanation, such as phone cases, water bottles, posture belts and validated books. These products skip the education phase and go straight to conversion. [L0315.004, L0316.001]
  • Before you launch BOF, run this test: "Can this product sell without explanation?" If yes, launch BOF. If no, warm the audience first with educational video ads, so the pool is large enough before BOF retargeting starts. [L0315.015, L0315.004, L0316.001]
  • If your product needs to solve an unfamiliar problem, warm cold audiences with video first. More than 50% of viewers automatically join the BOF pool. [L0316.001, L0316.015]
  • For books: if other brands have already run and validated the same book, BOF can work, because the audience is already solution-aware. If the book is niche or new, warm it with video first. If competitors have already built awareness, BOF image ads can capture sales from their audience that did not convert. [L0316.025]
  • BOF is a harvesting channel, not a discovery channel. It shows the offer and benefits to people who already know the solution or have engaged with you. Because no spend goes toward explanation, CPMs stay low and ROAS rises. [L0316.002]
  • Competitors' video ads can prepare buyers for you. If they have already warmed an audience to a solution, such as posture belts, and some people did not buy, your BOF image ads can capture those almost-converted customers. They did the education; you harvest the sale with a simpler, cheaper approach. [L0316.024]
  • Example: one personal brand earned $100,000 in its first six months using only BOF image ads, starting from zero. The product was easy to understand (not a niche supplement) and the audience was solution-aware, so no warm-up video campaign was needed yet. [L0316.003]

Writing BOF Creative

  • BOF ads should read like direct sales ads: simple, straightforward and selling immediately. Tell the customer what they are buying and why, then ask them to buy. Skip storytelling and narrative, which is the opposite of educational ads. [L0316.040]
  • For wearables and health products, BOF ads succeed by addressing emotional transformation, not technical specifications. Cold buyers respond to "you can have your life back" more than to "compression ratio of 360 degrees." Testimonials, pain-point questions and risk removal all convert cold audiences. [L0313.022]
  • Guarantees work in BOF because they remove the main objection of cold traffic: "I don't know if this works." [L0313.025]
    • Specific terms are more credible than open-ended guarantees, for example 30 nights or a 7-day benefit window. [L0313.025]
    • The trial period must be long enough to test (30+ days). The benefit timeline must be realistic: 7 days for pain relief, not 1 day. [L0313.025]
  • Pattern: BOF targets people who already know the product and are close to buying. It converts with a direct, number-based offer and a clear reason to act now, because that removes the last reason to hesitate. Formula: aware audience + specific number-based offer + clear reason to act now. [L0794.030]
    • "Still comparing? Take 35% off your first order this week." [L0794.030]
    • "Your size is still in stock: 20% off when you add the second copy." [L0794.030]
  • Meta's algorithm assigns different concepts to different customer types based on scrolling behaviour and purchase history. Skeptics see testimonials, procrastinators see urgency and fence-sitters see value offers. You do not need to segment manually. Create several concepts in separate ad sets and let Meta optimise. [L0316.026]
  • Static hot ads do not link to product pages. They build trust and authority with customer voices and link to the store. They complement the dynamic product ads that run alongside them. [L0649.021]
  • Advertorial retargeting: visitors who read an advertorial but did not click through to the product can be retargeted with Meta ads. This is a more qualified audience than cold traffic. It works best as a step in a warm-up path for lower-ticket products, before building a longer funnel for expensive items. [L0552.050]
  • Cold Facebook traffic should go to an editorial page, not directly to the product page. The editorial page's call-to-action button links to the product page. Facebook ads are good at catching impulse buyers, but sending all traffic to a product page leaves most of the prospects unconverted. [L0578.006, L0578.033]

Campaign Structure and Separation

  • Keep BOF as a separate campaign from cold video campaigns. [L0316.004, L0315.001]
  • Within one BOF campaign, create one ad set per concept: testimonial, free gift, benefit, social proof and urgency. Each ad set targets the same warm audience from a different psychological angle. [L0315.001, L0316.004]
  • Put 4 to 8 ad variations with different hooks in each ad set. Meta measures which concept converts best and spreads budget across ad sets so no single concept dominates spend. [L0315.002, L0316.004]
  • Use separate Campaign Budget Optimization (CBO) campaigns for each format:
    • one for cold video
    • one for BOF (warm)
    • one for native ads, from week 2 onward, if used [L0316.014]
  • Never mix formats in one CBO. If a high-performing video campaign shares a CBO with BOF ads, the BOF ads get no budget and no fair test. [L0316.014]
  • Put the BOF statics and videos for the same product in different campaigns. Video ads in the same campaign can starve the statics, which then die. [L1476.036]
  • Grouping, which differs from the rule above: when deciding campaign structure, group native and BOF ads together as statics and keep video ads in a separate campaign. [L1476.059]
    • Conflict: L0316.014 says one CBO per format, including native. L1476.059 says native and BOF share a static campaign. Both are valid structures. Use one CBO per format to keep each format's budget separate. Use the statics grouping when you want native and BOF to compete for the same static budget. [L0316.014, L1476.059]
  • ABO or CBO matters less than having ads that actually win. Separate campaigns per format let you control budget and scale each format on its own. They do not guarantee better results. [L1476.058]

Budgets and Test Length

  • Starting budget for BOF testing: $50 USD per day. Minimum viable test: $15-20 AUD (about $15 USD) per day. Lower budgets often give higher ROAS, because the algorithm has more time to find your best audience. [L0316.012]
  • Test BOF for 2 to 3 days. If it is not profitable by day 3, stop. Try new concepts or give up on this approach. Do not keep spending on an unprofitable test beyond this window. [L0316.013, L0316.031]
  • Starter setup for a product: [L0316.034]
    • Kalodata rips at $50/day (priority; validates the product)
    • BOF image ads at $50/day (side test; low cost; quick results)
    • Both in separate CBOs for 2 to 3 days
    • Native ads come in week 2, if profitable
    • This spreads risk and tests three angles at once.
  • If your Kalodata rip campaign is not profitable after 2 to 3 days, test BOF image ads instead of pausing. The audience may be solution-aware and tired of competitors' videos but has not bought yet. BOF is faster and cheaper to produce than video and can capture warm-but-not-buying segments. [L0316.047]
  • Scaling limit: when you scale to $200/day and reach breakeven (1.0X ROAS), do not raise the budget further. The constraint is the ads, not the budget. Test new formats (BOF, native, advertorials, listicles), new concepts or AI UGC. The biggest jumps come from new creative that works, not from scaling mediocre ads. [L0316.037]

Ad Set Rotation and Creative Fatigue

  • Typical BOF structure on an active brand: 30+ ad sets in total, with 15 to 20 active at any moment. [L0316.041, L0316.048]
  • Introduce new concepts every 2 days. Keep winners and pause losers. [L0316.041]
  • Pause fatigued ad sets rather than deleting them. Do not delete old ad sets; disable them and retest later if needed. [L0316.041, L0316.048]
  • As new warm audiences enter your pool from video campaigns, retest disabled sets to see whether they perform better on the fresh audience. [L0316.048]
  • Procedure for a new BOF campaign: [L0316.031]
    1. Launch a separate CBO campaign for BOF (not in the same CBO as videos).
    2. Create three starting ad sets: testimonial, free gift, benefit.
    3. Add 4 to 8 ads to each ad set.
    4. Set the daily budget to $50 (or a minimum of $15-20 AUD).
    5. Run for 2 to 3 days.
    6. If ROAS is profitable, keep running and introduce new concept variations weekly. If it is not profitable by day 3, stop and test new concepts or different products.
  • Ad type: BOF image ads perform as well as video ads at capturing warm, solution-aware audiences. Images are faster and cheaper (about 20 minutes with AI prompts). Videos take longer. Use images to test quickly and introduce video concepts once images prove the market. [L0316.042]

How Video and BOF Work Together

  • Every viewer who watches 50% or more of a video is automatically added to a warm audience pool. These viewers then move into the BOF campaign, which grows the pool of buyers for your image ads without extra work. [L0316.015]
  • BOF profitability compounds as the warm pool grows. A larger pool gives BOF more impressions at low CPMs, which raises ROAS. Sustained cold spending increases BOF returns without extra effort, creating a self-reinforcing cycle. [L0315.012]
  • Video builds the audience and BOF harvests it. BOF is a harvesting channel with a hard ceiling, typically $80K-$100K monthly revenue. The real scale comes from video ads building a larger warm pool, with BOF monetising the result. Together they can produce 5X or more revenue growth. [L0316.017]
  • Example (personal brand): video campaigns were introduced at scale alongside existing BOF ads. ROAS doubled and budget scaled from $300 per day to $1,000 per day using the same BOF ads, with no new creative. The video campaign warmed the audience and BOF captured the results. [L0316.016]
  • Example (Brand A): the brand hit an $80,000 monthly revenue ceiling running only BOF ads. When video ads at scale were added alongside the same BOF campaign, monthly revenue rose 5X to $400,000+. Video prospected cold audiences and warmed them, and BOF captured the ready-to-buy segment. [L0316.018]
  • A high number of BOF ads for a product signals existing demand, because those ads target warm audiences who already know the product. It is a sign the product is easier to sell. [L1187.017]

Funnel-Stage Creative Mix

  • Top-of-funnel (TOF) ads educate cold audiences who do not yet know the product. They simply show what it is. [L1187.018]
  • A healthy product ad set includes a mix of top-, middle- and bottom-funnel ads, not only one stage. [L1187.018]
  • Typical length: BOF product ads run about 1 to 10 seconds. A much longer ad deserves a closer look before you assume it is BOF. [L1187.019]
  • Skip a product if almost all its ads are BOF with no top- or middle-funnel variety. It gives too little variety to test with. [L1187.020]
  • Inside one CBO, the mix is heavily BOF: about 90% of ads at that stage, with the rest at middle and top. Plan creatives around that mix. [L1195.056]

Native Ads

  • Native ads are ads with a long primary text that reads like an article. They are a strong top-of-funnel format. Spot them by the long primary text and copy their structure. [L1164.032]
  • Native ads scale less than UGC or BOF ads, so run them as a side campaign for extra revenue. Start them only after a winning product is confirmed and the pixel is warmed up, and only with budget set aside for testing. [L1476.055]
  • Timing: native ads run from week 2 onward, in their own CBO, if the product is profitable. [L0316.014, L0316.034]

Whitelist and Persona Pages

Concepts

  • A Facebook Page is the public identity above an ad: a page name, a profile picture and posts. For the customer, it is the face that speaks to them, so the page you run an ad from shapes how it is perceived. [L1474.001]
  • A brand page is the official Facebook page of your store or product brand, usually carrying the store name. Ads from it show the company speaking directly about its product. [L1474.002]
  • A whitelist page is another Facebook page or creator identity that has given permission for ads to run under its name. The product stays the same, but a different person or page appears as the speaker. Meta calls this arrangement Community Handled Partnership Ads and manages permissions through its partnership tools. [L1474.003]
  • Two whitelisting types: [L0906.013]
    • Personal whitelisting uses a creator, influencer or advocate persona that the audience trusts as a peer.
    • Third-party whitelisting uses an authority figure, such as a doctor for supplements, to lend credibility.
  • Whitelist ads are usually written in the first person, from a named persona describing their use of the product. Some use a third-person page voice that describes the product. Both differ from the brand page's company voice. [L1474.015]

Brand Page versus Whitelist Page

Brand page Whitelist / partner page
Who is talking Your brand Another approved page or creator
Product being sold Your product Can be the exact same product
How it feels The company is advertising A person or page is recommending
Main benefit Builds your brand Gives another angle or identity to test

[L1474.007]

  • Customers see ads all day, and an ad straight from a brand often reads as plain advertising. An ad from a creator or relevant page feels more like a recommendation and more organic, so the creative feels more native. [L1474.008]
  • Whitelist pages are used to: [L1474.009]
    • test a different identity without changing the product
    • make creatives feel more native or personal
    • borrow a creator's credibility or style, once permission is granted
    • compare which page identity gets better results for the same product
  • A brand page and a whitelist page can promote the same product at the same time. This lets you compare two identities side by side. [L1474.012]
  • Procedure: [L1474.010]
    1. Start with the product you want to sell.
    2. Choose the face of the ad: your brand page or an approved partner page.
    3. Run the ad from the chosen page.
    4. Compare results across the pages.

Rules and Compliance

  • Only run ads under another person's or business's identity when they have granted permission through Meta's partnership or advertising tools. Never pretend to be someone else, copy their page, or use their identity without consent. [L1474.006]
  • An invented persona can be your own whitelist page, so it does not have to be an existing creator. An invented persona has no real access to your ad account and must be treated as a separate identity. [L1474.004]
  • A whitelist identity may not use the name of a real celebrity. Meta rejects those ads and they are not approved. Use an invented name. [L1474.005]
  • A whitelist page you build and own is presented as a fictional character instead of a company logo. The ads run under that character's identity. You keep the page, its audience and the pixel in your own account. [L1475.001]
  • A character page is treated as a branded page with a personality, similar to a mascot brand. It stays compliant only if the character is clearly a brand persona. It must not imitate a real, identifiable person, and standard Meta ad policy still applies. Check Meta's current Page authenticity and ad policies before scaling, since enforcement can change. [L1475.002]
  • Mistake: using a real person's name, photo or likeness for the character can get the page or its ads rejected or disabled. Keep the persona fully fictional, with an AI-generated or licensed stock portrait. [L1475.003]
  • Invented personas are often shown with stock photos as profile pictures. A stock photo does not change the permission and compliance rules for whitelist pages. [L1474.016]
  • Checking a page before you treat it as a possible identity: open its Facebook page view and check the Details section, the Links and Contact info fields, the follower count and the Photos section. The page view also shows a "Not yet rated" or review status, which gives a quick sense of whether the identity looks like a real, active page. [L1474.020]

Building the Page

  • Procedure, character assets built with AI: [L1476.017]
    1. Attach your customer research and product documents to Claude.
    2. Paste the character-generator prompt.
    3. Let Claude read the research, pick the archetype and generate the character profile and assets.
    4. Copy the profile photo prompt, the cover photo prompt and the bio.
    5. Paste the two image prompts into ChatGPT to generate the images.
    6. Upload the images and the bio to the new Facebook page.
  • Procedure, creating the page in Facebook Business Manager: [L1476.019]
    1. Create a new Facebook page inside Business Manager.
    2. Upload the profile photo.
    3. Upload the cover photo.
    4. Upload the bio.
    5. Start running ads from that page.
  • Setup is the same as a brand page: create the page, upload profile photo, cover photo and bio, then run ads from it. The AI prompts do most of the character work, so this is beginner-friendly. [L1476.034]
  • One whitelist character can run all of a brand's ads without extra impersonation risk. A different page per person shown in UGC is also possible, but each character must not imitate a real person. [L1476.035]

When to Use Whitelist Pages

  • Start with a whitelist page from day one of testing, rather than waiting for a winning ad. This is recommended on a tight budget, unless brand pages have already proven better for the product. [L1476.020]
  • Make the whitelist page the default for the whole ad account, rather than running brand pages. A face instead of a logo means more people watch, fewer scroll past and more click, and this adds up at scale. [L1476.026]
  • Whitelist pages suit static ads well, since a static image without a face needs only the character page. UGC videos without faces can also use the whitelist page with no extra work. Video ads with a face need the matching-look consideration. [L1476.030]
  • Split test whitelist against brand page: run the same five ads once with each page. This splits the budget in half, and both tests need identical ads for a fair comparison. With a small budget and few ad sets, run only the whitelist page. [L1476.021]
  • If only a few ads are tested on a small budget, use the whitelist page for all of them and drop the brand page. This avoids splitting the budget across two pages. [L1476.022]

Researching Competitor Pages

  • To see how competitors run whitelisting or persona pages: [L0906.011]
    1. Open the Meta Ad Library at facebook.com/ads/library.
    2. Set active status to Active.
    3. Set ad type to All ads.
    4. Set country to All.
    5. Set search type to Page.
    6. Sort by total impressions, descending, to see which pages run the most ads.
    7. Search by the competitor's page name, not by a product keyword.
