9.3 Account trust, warm-up and ban prevention

Ban Rates and What They Mean for Planning

  • Older sources put the ban rate for new advertisers very high. One source said Facebook bans beginners about 90% of the time, because bots are placed to protect user experience [L0296.004]. Another claimed 90-95% of beginners are banned, but this is an unverified estimate [L0637.001]. A speaker estimated 80-90% at that time [L1440.019]. These figures are older and unverified.
  • A newer source says bans for new advertisers have become less common and are no longer a universal problem. Recovery solutions exist for properly set-up accounts that do face suspension [L0296.033]. Plan from the newer position, but do not treat any percentage as a guarantee [L0296.033, L0637.001].
  • Meta's ban systems are largely automated. New advertisers are screened by algorithms before any human looks at the account [L0637.001].
  • Prevention steps lower the risk. No step guarantees that a ban will not happen [L0637.001, L1440.019].
  • Meta reacts to new accounts that show unstable or suspicious behaviour, such as changing devices, IP addresses, payment details, or sudden spend jumps. Keeping device, IP, proxy, payment and spend patterns consistent makes the account look like one stable, legitimate user [L1477.010].

Profile Age and Identity

  • Use an older personal profile, at least 2 years old, not a new dummy profile [L0637.003, L0637.059].
  • A brand-new dummy profile usually gets the account banned right away, because Meta has no activity history to trust [L0637.059].
  • Set up a new business account using an existing personal profile that already has history, not a brand-new account. A new account with no data and sudden trading can look suspicious [L0950.054].
  • Do not run ads from a bare personal account. Set up a business profile in Meta Business [L0341.027].
  • Running ads from a personal profile without a business account and business details raises the risk of a flag. Meta reviews new advertisers for legitimacy, so set up the business and provide as much verifiable information as possible [L0343.022].
  • Confirm the profile identity early, before any problem. When Meta asks for ID verification after a ban, it checks that a real, legitimate person runs the ads, so the platform should already know who you are [L0637.002, L0637.003].
  • Profile identity confirmation is reached from the personal profile via Settings & privacy > Settings > identity confirmation. Upload a government or national ID document there. The menu path is as stated in the source; check the current Meta UI, since menu names change [L0637.004].
  • Meta can suspend a new ad account, sometimes instantly, if identity verification is not completed in time. Submit the requested ID promptly [L0341.026].
  • Meta may suspend a new ad account right after creation and may ask for identity confirmation within about 48 hours. Respond in time, because missing the deadline can lead to a ban [L0343.021].
  • Older (2018): Meta can grant account verification on Facebook and Instagram after substantial ad spend, usually through an account representative who requests the verification [L0466.057].

Two-Factor Authentication

  • Enable two-factor authentication on the personal profile via profile > Settings & privacy > Settings > Security and login > Two-factor authentication. The purpose is to show Meta that the same person who verified the identity is the one running the ads [L0637.005].
  • Set up two-factor authentication for every admin on a Meta business account, especially admin accounts. One account was reportedly banned because a non-admin user had no two-factor authentication set up [L0343.023, L0341.027].
  • Under Business settings > Business options, the Two-factor authentication setting can require a login code to access the business portfolio from unknown browsers. The option was set to No one in the example. The Access recommendations setting suggests extra assets to people who manage permissions [L0651.008].

Business Manager and Business Verification

  • Keep the Business Manager separate from the personal profile. A ban on the ad account or Business Manager should not take down the personal profile. In the worst case only the Business Manager is shut down and a new one can be created. A personal profile ban can block Facebook use entirely [L0637.006].
  • Create the Business Manager at business.facebook.com/create, not as a personal ad account [L0637.003]. Create it with business details rather than the default personal one [L0650.012].
  • Create the Business Manager and verify it with a legitimate business. A business account without a real business can cause verification problems and more ad account bans [L0950.053].
  • Business verification is optional but strongly increases trust with Meta. Verify in Business settings by submitting one business document, such as an LLC, Ltd or similar. The review took about 4-5 days in the source. Verification does not restrict which countries you can target [L0637.007, L0637.003].
  • Verified businesses get more flexibility and access to live chat support. Verification also helps Facebook see the business as legitimate rather than as a scam risk [L0709.032].
  • One account went through a 13-step verification process and had no restrictions or bans for about 1.5 years afterwards [L1440.020].
  • Add 1-2 trusted people to the Business Manager with page and pixel rights [L0637.003]. Extra people add trust and protect the assets. If the main personal profile gets banned or loses access, they keep access to pages, pixels and ad accounts [L0637.009].
  • Keep the same ad account and Business Manager for a store, rather than creating new ones for each product or test. A stable, consistently used account is less likely to be blocked [L0650.010].
  • Separating Business Managers by product risk is a containment practice. Use a separate Business Manager for products that carry ban risk, such as products with health claims, revealing skin or controversial topics. Keep the main Business Manager for safe products and for other stores [L1347.021]. Do not use a separate Business Manager to run a product that the account is already restricted for.
  • Do not link your personal profile to a new Business Manager, and do not send invitations between the old and new accounts. In one reported case this led to both accounts being blocked [L1347.022].
  • Browser for Business Managers: use Brave for new Business Managers and keep Google Chrome for existing ones. Microsoft Edge and Safari (on a Mac) are also options for separating accounts [L1347.023].
  • Do not create a brand-new personal profile to get around limits. Profiles with no history are often banned. The approach is a trusted friend or family member with an established profile who creates the Business Manager and adds you as admin or partner. This avoids the need for their profile login [L1213.013, L1218.013]. Do not use this arrangement to get around a restriction on an account, because that is circumvention of Meta's enforcement.
  • Creating a second Meta Business Manager soon after the first can trigger a security restriction or suspension. If the account is restricted, request help through Get support on the Meta business help page and complete the identity verification Meta asks for. Do not open more accounts [L0806.105].

Page Warm-Up

  • A warmed-up Facebook Page and profile help, because Meta and visitors trust an established account more than a new one. Skipping the warm-up makes it harder for ads to run and convert [L1021.011].
  • Fill the page with business information: phone number, business address, description and website link [L0650.012].
  • Fill the About section in detail, including the website. Before running conversion campaigns, a new page should have around 10-20 likes. Avoid running ads right after the page is created [L0637.010, L0637.003].
  • Warm up the page with 2-3 simple posts over 1-2 days before starting conversion campaigns. Good posts are happy or product-related content, or a screenshot or banner from the store, without hard sales messages or scammy claims such as weight-loss promises [L0637.011].
  • Engagement posts can be a product photo with a positive quote and no traffic link [L0650.012].
  • Keep warm-up creatives simple: product-only images or a positive quote with no people in them, because an image that shows a person can cause problems during warm-up [L0650.064].
  • Make the page look legitimate and branded, so it does not look scammy to Meta or to users. A bare or generic page raises the risk of problems [L1477.003].
  • The page is the asset that gets restricted first when ads are banned. One profile can run several products under one page, but each product needs a clear business identity behind it. Build the page and Business Manager as the trusted front of the store [L0637.058].
  • Older (2021-22): Paid traffic networks are more likely to trust a store that shows a physical postal address in the footer. An address supports ad platform review, even though it may not be kept long term [L0681.012].
  • Meta tends to prioritise conversion campaigns that send traffic off the platform, so new pages, Instagram accounts and Business Managers attract extra scrutiny. Every new asset linked to the ads adds risk of a temporary block [L0650.063].
  • Launching a conversion campaign with purchases on a brand-new Facebook page and account, without any warm-up, was linked to a temporary block. A new page, a new Instagram account or a new Business Manager each adds risk, because Facebook does not trust new assets yet [L0650.013].
  • A newly created Facebook page and Instagram account can be blocked from running ads, including remarketing, until the accounts show some activity. Post regularly on both before planning paid campaigns. Look in community groups for others who had the same block (2023) [L0804.112].

Instagram

  • Creating a new Instagram account or connecting Instagram to the ads adds work and risk. A connected account must be posted to regularly and needs a real follower base [L0650.015].
  • Running traffic-only ads without connecting Instagram sends visitors straight to the website [L0650.015].
  • Messenger requires only the Facebook Page. Choosing Instagram as a message destination requires connecting an Instagram account first through the Connect account option [L0614.017].

Page Warm-Up Procedure

Warm up new Facebook assets before running conversion campaigns:

  1. Secure the personal profile with two-factor authentication [L0650.012].
  2. Fill the Facebook page with business information such as phone number, business address, description, and website link [L0650.012].
  3. Create a separate business manager with business details rather than using the default personal one [L0650.012].
  4. Complete business verification [L0650.012].
  5. Run an engagement campaign of about $1 to $5 for one to two days with simple, positive posts (for example a product photo with a positive quote) and no traffic link [L0650.012].
  6. Then move to conversion campaigns [L0650.012].

Source D 2026 Engagement Warm-Up

  • A small engagement campaign with a low budget can warm up a new or inactive Meta ad account before running campaigns that matter. The goal is account activity, not sales [L0614.001].
  • Simple setting: after creating the pixel, open the ad account, press Create, then start an Engagement campaign with recommended settings and a budget of about $10. Later ads from the account should go active much faster [L0348.001].
  • Ad set: target four countries (United States, Canada, Australia, United Kingdom), leave the custom audience field blank, keep the Advantage+ audience setting on, and use a daily budget of $10 [L0348.002].
  • Age 18-65+ with Advantage+ audience switched on lets Meta show ads beyond the suggested audience when that is likely to improve results [L0348.026].
  • Warm-up ad: untick the Advertiser Ads option, remove the media, upload any non-explicit simple image such as a company logo or a random neutral photo, publish, and wait for it to finish processing. This spends roughly $10 [L0348.003].
  • Campaign objective: Engagement. Create a Manual engagement campaign instead of the recommended setup for finer control. The campaign name can stay at its default [L0614.002].
  • Budget: set the Daily budget under Ad set budget to $20.00 USD. Ads Manager then shows a maximum daily spend of $35 and a maximum weekly spend of $140 for that setting [L0614.003].
  • Budget strategy: Campaign budget (Advantage+ campaign budget, which spreads spend automatically across the campaign) or Ad set budget (set per ad set, with its own bid strategy and budget schedule options). For a single-ad warm-up, Ad set budget gives direct control over the Daily budget [L0614.015].
  • Message destinations: choose Manual destination, not Automatic (recommended), then select Messenger. Automatic lets Meta send people to whichever messaging app they use most, so Messenger is not guaranteed [L0614.004]. Automatic can send people to Instagram instead of Messenger. Set Manual destination when Messenger must be the target [L0614.011]. Automatic sends people to whichever messaging app Meta expects will lower ad cost, while Manual limits the ad to the apps you tick [L0614.017].
  • Audience definition: keep the setting on Broad rather than Narrow. Under Locations, add United States, which the tutorial recommends, but any audience works for a warm-up. Ads Manager showed an estimated audience of 73,700,000 to 86,700,000 with the broad setting [L0614.005]. Broad audiences can improve performance and reach more people likely to respond. Meta notes that its estimates do not include Advantage+ audience options and can change over time [L0614.006].
  • Audience controls can be set for all campaigns in the ad account to reflect legal or practical constraints only, such as restricted locations or age ranges. They are not needed for a standard warm-up unless a constraint applies to the account [L0614.018].
  • Ad format: image ad with one image. Ads Manager accepts one image or video per ad, and the image may be cropped automatically for Square, Vertical and Horizontal placements [L0614.009].
  • Call to action: Send message. People who see the ad can message the Page directly, which matches the goal of an engagement ad that starts conversations in Messenger [L0614.010].
  • Identity: the field sets which profile appears in the ad, usually the Facebook Page that represents the business. Messages started from the ad are routed to the chosen destinations, so the Page must be the one that will answer the customer [L0614.021].
  • Partnership ads and Multi-advertiser ads are separate toggles that default to Off. Keep both Off for a plain warm-up ad. Partnership ads use signals from both accounts, and Multi-advertiser ads can appear beside other advertisers and may crop or resize the creative [L0614.022].
  • Switch the warm-up engagement ad off once it has spent about $8 to $10 in total. Letting it keep running after that spends budget without any further purpose [L0614.008, L0614.012].

Warm-up engagement campaign in Meta Ads Manager:

  1. Go to Campaigns, click + Create, and choose the engagement objective [L0614.007].
  2. Select Manual engagement campaign instead of Recommended settings [L0614.007].
  3. Leave the campaign name at its default [L0614.007].
  4. Set the Daily budget under Ad set budget to $20.00 USD [L0614.007].
  5. Under Message destinations, select Manual destination and tick Messenger [L0614.007].
  6. Under Locations, add United States and keep Audience definition on Broad [L0614.007].
  7. Under Ad creative, choose Create image ad, select one picture of an animal (for example a dog), and upload it [L0614.007].
  8. Enter a short headline (see the pattern unit for the headline style) [L0614.007].
  9. Click Publish and accept Meta's terms [L0614.007].
  10. Turn the ad off once it has spent about $8 to $10 [L0614.007].

Source C Optional Warm-Up

Ad account warm-up, as an optional safety step:

  1. Create a single campaign with one ad set and one image ad [L0637.012].
  2. Set the budget at $1-5 per day for an engagement campaign [L0637.012].
  3. Target either worldwide or the United States, Canada and the top four countries, keeping the targeting broad, which gives an audience of about 100 million to 500 million [L0637.012].
  4. Use a simple image such as a store banner, smiley faces or a smiling dog, with a short positive quote as copy [L0637.012].
  5. Run it for at least 1-2 days [L0637.012].
  6. Then start conversion campaigns [L0637.012].
  • Post engagement campaigns used only to warm up a new ad account or page cost about $5 to $10 per day and are optional. Run them for one to two days at most, because longer runs add low-quality audiences to the pixel data [L1213.001].
  • To run a warm-up engagement ad without publishing it on the main page, use the normal ad creation flow, select the page as the advertising identity, and upload the image directly. The ad is not published to the page's timeline and can be switched off after 2 days [L0637.014].
  • Running conversion-objective campaigns directly, without a warm-up engagement campaign, can still produce good results and engagement. Engagement campaigns can help in pet, kids and some fashion niches but are not required [L1213.002].

  • Large, established accounts with a long payment history can skip warm-up, but new accounts should not [L0637.013].

Warm-Up Sequence and Timing

Full Warm-Up Sequence (Source D 2026)

Warm-up sequence for a new Meta ad account:

  1. Log in to the Facebook profile only from one fixed device and one fixed IP address, using the same proxy every time, with no VPN switching [L1477.001, L1477.002].
  2. Set up the page so it looks legitimate and branded, not scammy [L1477.001, L1477.003].
  3. After the ad account is created, wait 1 to 2 hours before launching any ad [L1477.001, L1477.004].
  4. Start with a $20 budget and let it spend about $8 to $10 before launching sales ads [L1477.001, L1477.005].
  5. Spend small amounts first and avoid aggressive scaling: Day 1 spend $10 to $20, Day 2 spend $20 to $30, Day 3 onward run normal campaigns [L1477.001, L1477.006].
  6. Do not run high budgets during the warm-up and do not edit business settings too much too early [L1477.001].

Rules that apply to this sequence:

  • Always log in to the Facebook profile from the same device and the same IP address, and use the same proxy every single time. Switching VPNs or IP addresses between logins is a risk factor for Meta [L1477.002].
  • Start a new ad account with a small spend, for example a $20 budget, and wait until about $8 to $10 has been spent before launching sales ads. Avoid aggressive scaling in the first days [L1477.005].
  • Do not run high budgets right away on a new or warming ad account. Increase spend gradually, following the schedule of $10 to $20 on Day 1 and $20 to $30 on Day 2, before running normal campaigns from Day 3 [L1477.006].
  • Before moving to sales campaigns, run an engagement campaign first to warm up the pixel and collect website activity data [L1027.005].

Full Account Setup Procedure (Source C)

To reduce account suspension risk on Meta: complete account verification and profile verification, provide detailed business information, wait 3-7 days before running any ads (do not advertise on day one), and scale spending gradually over weeks rather than jumping to large budgets immediately [L0296.031]. Meta can ban a new advertiser, and the steps below are the setup sequence for it [L0296.004]:

  1. Do not run ads from a bare personal account [L0341.027].
  2. Set up a business profile in Meta Business [L0341.027].
  3. Enter your business details and provide as much accurate information as you are willing to share [L0341.027].
  4. Set up two-factor authentication, especially for any admin accounts [L0341.027].
  5. Complete identity verification when requested [L0341.027].

Meta trusts accounts that look legitimate, and it associates bad experiences with the platform [L0341.027].

Payments and Spend Limits

  • Payment failure is a serious risk on Meta. A card declined automatically by Facebook can lead to an immediate account ban. Always have two payment methods on the ad account, a main one and a backup if the first fails, because repeated failures lead to a ban [L0691.013].
  • Do not add payment methods repeatedly in a row. If a payment method does not work, wait about 1 hour before trying again, because repeated attempts lead to a ban [L1477.007].
  • Adding a card for ad payments can fail with an insufficient-funds error even when the card has enough money, which can then restrict the ad account. The cause was unconfirmed, so check the payment setup before spending on a new account [L0709.033].
  • Pay every bill on time [L0637.003].
  • A personal profile that cannot add a valid payment method may be banned, and the linked business account can then be banned too, even after business verification is completed. Verification alone does not protect an ad account if its payment method is invalid or fails [L0691.032].
  • A new Facebook ad account has a spending limit that increases only after payments are made on time. If the ad account stops spending on a day, check the payment method in the payment settings, because a failed payment can silently stop delivery [L1216.009].
  • A CBO or ABO campaign that spends far below its daily limit can have a payment or account-history problem. Check that the ad account is set up correctly, and that no payments to Facebook are overdue in the payment gateway. If the problem persists, duplicate the campaign and run the copy [L1220.001].
  • Suspensions for unusual activity may require verifying your card again. Keep business details and payments fully legitimate to reduce risk [L0950.055].

Spending Limit Ladder

  • A personal-profile Business Manager starts with a 50 USD spending limit and rises progressively to 100, 150 and 250 USD before the limit is removed [L1347.018].
  • Spending limits are raised by making payments on time and staying consistent, without billing failures. Add two payment methods, so that if the first one fails the second one is charged [L1347.019].
  • A seller's credit card was compromised and replaced, which caused several payment failures on Facebook. The seller believed these failures kept the spending limit low for longer, and then added a second payment method to avoid this in the future [L1347.020].
  • Late payment on the ad account can impose a spending limit. In one case the limit was 50 USD per day, rising about 130 USD per day weekly, but it reset to 15 USD per day after just a few account actions such as adding an ad set or duplicating a campaign [L1212.033].
  • Making changes to a restricted Facebook account, such as adding an ad set or duplicating a campaign, can drop the daily spend limit back to a lower level within 24 hours [L1212.035].

Trust Tiers and Asset Ranking

  • Meta assigns advertisers spend tiers based on account age, Business Manager history and successful payment history. Very large advertisers spending roughly $100k to $1M or more can skip warm-up and launch campaigns directly [L0637.061].
  • Meta ranks business assets such as the Business Manager, Facebook pages and ad accounts by their spend history and places them in trust tiers. An asset with a longer, steadier spend record is trusted more [L0650.062].
  • Facebook was described as relatively lenient with copied creatives in general-store testing. TikTok and Pinterest were described as banning accounts much faster for the same behaviour [L0660.027].

Feedback Score and Shipping Time

  • Meta assigns the feedback score after a store has about 50-100 sales, on a scale of 0 to 5. It is judged from product quality, shipping times and customer communication, and is collected from direct customers via feedback forms [L0637.017].
  • Feedback score thresholds [L0637.018]:
    • Below 2 restricts advertising for a page that is less than 1 year old.
    • A page below 2 can be restricted from running ads.
    • A score above 3 is considered good. Scores of 3-4.5 are the typical healthy range.
    • Accounts below 3 for several weeks risk a ban.
  • Check the feedback score and any restrictions in Business Manager > Account Quality. You can also see ad rejections and each asset's status there. Shipping time is set in the same Account Quality area, below the feedback score [L0637.019].
  • Set shipping time in Business Manager > Account Quality, on the page's section, for your store. Set it to more than 6 weeks, up to 8 weeks, because Meta sends the feedback form only after the shipping time has passed [L0637.020].
  • If the shipping time is set too short, feedback forms reach customers before the parcel arrives and generate bad ratings [L0637.021].
  • Keep the real shipping time under 2 weeks with good suppliers and agents, but set the platform setting longer as a buffer [L0637.021]. The platform setting (6-8 weeks) and the real shipping time (under 2 weeks) are two different values for two different purposes.
  • Keep the feedback score above 3 by communicating well and setting shipping time correctly [L0637.003]. Reply fast to comments and messages [L0637.003].

Policy-Risk Habits

Creatives and Claims

  • Ad creatives and missing payments are the most common causes of Meta bans. Follow Meta's rules on prohibited content such as nudity and before-and-after images [L0691.017].
  • Avoid health promises or other claims in ad text that Facebook may flag, since this can lead to the account being blocked [L0650.011].
  • Do not make scammy claims such as weight-loss promises in page posts or ad copy [L0637.011].
  • Check how a product looks in ad creatives and listing photos before running it on Facebook. Facebook can flag a product that looks like a weapon, such as a walking stick that resembles a gun, and the flag can lead to another account ban [L1440.058].
  • Running random, unvetted ads can get the whole ad account banned. Strict product research and ad checks protect the account from bans [L1131.022].
  • Selling branded products or running copied product images from AliExpress is a common cause of Facebook bans. Use only unbranded products [L1431.038].
  • Do not copy competitor creatives exactly [L0637.003].
  • Meta does not algorithmically compare new ad videos against other advertisers' videos to detect copies. The practical risk of reuse comes from a competitor reporting the ad, either through a DMCA notice or an ad report. This usually happens when the competitor is already in a dispute or was banned earlier [L1218.001].
  • Posting an identical ad file from a different account can link the accounts, because the ad files share metadata. If one ad is banned, the linked ad can be banned too [L0361.028]. The source suggested changing the file metadata or making a small change to the video before uploading. This is not recommended, because it is an attempt to hide a connection that Meta can detect. Run each account's ads from that account's own original creatives [L0361.028, L1195.025].
  • Running identical creatives across several accounts or with many sellers can trigger Meta to restrict the Business Manager. In severe cases, copyright complaints can take down the Shopify store and ad accounts within days [L1195.025].

Products and Testing

  • Use a separate ad account, or a separate Facebook account, for testing new products instead of your personal ad account. Testing products that get flagged can ban the account [L1173.011].
  • Products that carry ban risk, such as products with health claims, revealing skin or controversial topics, belong in a separate Business Manager (see Business Manager section) [L1347.021].

Compliance Habits

  • Facebook accounts that did not follow Facebook's ad prevention practices have a higher chance of being banned. Following the practices reduces the ban risk. The coach reported few ban problems among mentorship members who follow them [L0363.056].
  • Complete the free Facebook Advertising Policy Blueprint course and keep its certificate [L0637.003].
  • Keep business details and payments fully legitimate [L0950.055].

Login, Device and IP Hygiene

  • Log in to the Facebook profile only from one fixed device and one fixed IP address, using the same proxy every time [L1477.001].
  • Always log in from the same device and the same IP address, and use the same proxy every single time. Switching VPNs or IP addresses between logins is a risk factor for Meta [L1477.002].
  • Logging in from multiple devices is a common cause of instant bans. Avoid it in the first phase of an account [L1477.008].
  • Using a VPN or proxy on Meta is a fast route to a block. For a US target, choose USA in the ad set targeting and do not use a VPN [L0691.016].
  • Conflicting advice: one source suggested that, after a flagged IP or card history, a fresh agency account and a VPN could be used to get back to scaling. This is not recommended, because it evades Meta's enforcement and leads to permanent loss of assets. Use the consistent-device, consistent-IP approach above instead [L0351.036, L1477.002, L0691.016].
  • A flagged payment card or IP history, such as a DMCA record tied to an area, can cause repeated bans on new Business Managers and ad accounts [L0351.036].
  • Before creating a Business Manager and ad account, one source recommended scrolling the news feed for about 15 to 30 minutes. This was described as reducing bans on new accounts from a flagged IP or card [L0351.035].

What Causes Instant Bans and Cascades

Common Triggers

  • Common causes of instant Meta account bans are logging in through a VPN, logging in from multiple devices, repeatedly adding payment methods, running high budgets immediately, and editing business settings too much too early. Avoid all of these in the first phase of an account [L1477.008].
  • Launching 10-15 ad sets or campaigns on day one of a new ad account [L0637.013].
  • Payment failure, where a card declined automatically by Facebook can lead to an immediate ban [L0691.013].
  • Ad creatives and missing payments, the most common causes of bans [L0691.017].
  • Not completing identity verification in time, which can lead to an instant suspension [L0341.026, L0343.021].
  • A non-admin user without two-factor authentication on a business account [L0343.023].
  • Running random, unvetted ads [L1131.022].
  • Selling branded or copied products [L1431.038].
  • A product that looks like a weapon [L1440.058].
  • Creating a second Business Manager soon after the first [L0806.105].
  • Launching a conversion campaign on a brand-new page and account without warm-up [L0650.013].

Cascades

  • Account bans on Meta can cascade. A personal profile that cannot add a valid payment method may be banned, and the linked business account can then be banned too, even after business verification is completed [L0691.032].
  • A ban on the ad account or Business Manager should not take down the personal profile, but a personal profile ban can block Facebook use entirely [L0637.006].
  • Linked accounts can be banned together when identical ad files share metadata [L0361.028].

Recovery

  • If an account is restricted, request help through Get support on the Meta business help page and complete the identity verification Meta asks for, instead of opening more accounts [L0806.105].
  • When Meta asks for ID verification after a ban, it checks that a real, legitimate person runs the ads. Verify identity early so the platform already knows who you are [L0637.002].
  • Recovery solutions exist for properly set-up accounts that do face suspension [L0296.033].
  • Keep the Business Manager separate from the personal profile so that a Business Manager shutdown does not take down everything else. In the worst case, a new Business Manager can be created [L0637.006].
  • Do not open a new personal profile to get around a restriction, and do not open more accounts after a restriction. Both approaches are circumvention of enforcement [L1213.013, L0806.105].

Further details

  • Account blocking occurs when ad quality scores drop below Facebook's normal threshold; Facebook determines quality through satisfaction surveys sent to recent customers about their purchase experience. Maintain account health by monitoring and improving performance. [L0238.005]
