9.13 Ad creative, copy and placement formats

Ad Text Anatomy and Field Priorities

  • A Facebook ad has three text elements: Primary Text above the creative, Headline in bold below the creative, and Description in small text below the Headline. [L0204.001]
  • The Description is often hidden on Reels and Stories placements. [L0204.001]
  • Primary Text and Headline are the two most important elements because they stay visible in all placements. Description visibility is unreliable, so optimise the first two first. [L0204.002]
  • Viewers see only the first 1-2 lines of Primary Text before deciding to watch the video or skip it. These lines must stop the scroll with a bold claim, a direct question about a pain point, or a benefit statement. [L0204.003]
  • Put the website URL in the Primary Text field so users can click from the copy straight to the store. [L0205.007]
  • Users see the video first and the text overlay second. Ad copy must not look pushy, so the whole ad reads as organic and native to the platform. [L0205.008]
  • (Older, 2021-22.) In a Facebook video ad, the headline and some call-to-action buttons below the image disappear, so the website link belongs at the top of the Primary Text. [L0488.010]

Primary Text Length and Templates

  • Long-form Primary Text runs more than 5-6 lines. Short-form runs 3-4 lines. [L0204.005]
  • Select copy length by audience age: for 40+, use long-form copy; for under 40, test short-format copy. [L0204.006]
  • Conflicting age thresholds exist in the sources. L0204.006 gives 40+ for long-form and under 40 for short-format. L0637.055 gives above 40 or 50 for Facebook-style captions with product, solution and benefit messaging, and under 40 or 30 for TikTok-style UGC. Use the threshold that matches your audience data. [L0204.006, L0637.055]

Long-form Primary Text template [L0204.007]:

Line 1 (bold claim or problem): Introducing [Product Name], the ultimate solution for [Customer Problem].

Line 2-3 (agitation): Are you tired of [specific pain point]? We have got the perfect solution for you. Say goodbye to [problem] once and for all with our innovative [product category].

Line 4 (benefits): Key benefits: [Benefit 1] • [Benefit 2] • [Benefit 3]

Line 5 (offer): [Discount]% off, limited time only / [Discount]% off with code [CODE]

Line 6 (action + outcome): Shop now and transform your [problem] into [desired outcome].

Short-form Primary Text template [L0204.008]:

Line 1 (problem question): Struggling with [pain point]?

Line 2 (solution intro): Say hello to [Product Name], your answer to [problem].

Line 3 (benefits): [Benefit 1] • [Benefit 2] • [Benefit 3]

Line 4 (exclusive offer): [Discount]% off, exclusive offer

Line 5 (CTA): Shop now

Headlines and Descriptions

Headline formula library [L0204.013]:

**Attention-grabbing:** Discover [Product Name] [your solution to problem] OR Get ready to transform your life with [Product Name].

**Urgency + offer:** Limited time offer: [Discount]% off OR Hurry, [Product Name] sales ending soon. Grab [Product Name] now!

**Benefit-driven:** Experience [key benefit] with [Product Name] OR [Key benefit] way to [solve problem]

**Problem-solving:** Say goodbye to [problem] with [Product Name] OR Tired of [customer pain point]? Try [Product Name].
  • The Description is a small, often-hidden field. Fill it with limited-time offer details (for example "Free shipping" or "30-day money-back guarantee") or social proof (for example "Rated 4.8 out of 5 by 15,000+ happy customers"). Avoid long text. [L0204.014]
  • (Older, 2021-22.) On the Facebook news feed, the ad copy can be more detailed and name both the problem and the solution the content offers. The Headline and CTA stay the same as in other placements. [L0415.006]

Writing and Sourcing Copy

  • Gather copy inspiration from three sources: customer psychology research (deepest desires and insecurities), direct customer interviews and feedback, and competitor ad analysis via the Facebook Ads Library. [L0204.009]
  • ChatGPT and Copy.ai are free tools for drafting Primary Text and Headlines. Enter your product details and customer pain points, or paste a competitor ad and ask for a rephrased version for your product. Generate 3-5 variations. [L0204.012]
  • Lead with the customer's transformation, not the mechanism. "Includes 360-degree compression technology and cooling materials" is weaker than "Migraine relief you can feel in minutes" because the customer cares about the result. [L0313.011]
  • Symptom-led headlines name the visible problem the buyer already feels, such as leaking, damage or lost time, rather than the root cause, because people recognise symptoms more easily than causes. [L0701.034]
    • Formula: [symptom the buyer sees] + [what it costs them in time, money or image] + question. [L0701.034]
    • Examples: "Shoes still soaking after your morning walk?" and "Keep finding ruined shirts after the rain? Stop losing money on replacements." [L0701.034]
  • Avoid direct claims such as weight-loss guarantees or fitness guarantees in captions, voiceover and on-screen text. Rephrase them so they do not read as a violation. [L0871.073]

Hooks

  • Meta defines the hook as the first one to three seconds of an ad. Other platforms and sources use different time definitions, so the exact length matters less than the element that grabs attention immediately. [L0508.011]
  • Hook length in the sources:
    • First 1-3 seconds: Meta's definition. [L0508.011]
    • First 1-5 seconds: the proven hook to reuse from a competitor's ad. [L0637.015]
    • First 3-5 seconds: the part that differs between creative variations in testing. [L0648.060]
  • Test idea, style and hook before smaller variables. These three have the largest impact on results even though they take more effort to change. After Meta's Andromeda update, this lets the platform test a wide range of very different ads. [L0508.015]
  • Use the Hook Test strategy on Meta, Facebook and Instagram: run several different ad hooks or angles and keep the ones that scale. [L0914.010]
  • Judge hooks by hook rate to find the best-performing hook. [L0902.022]

Offers, Urgency and Guarantees

  • Risk-reversal structure (works because it removes fear of wasted money and sets explicit expectations for when to judge results) [L0313.005]:
    1. State the specific benefit timeline ("relief in as little as 7 days").
    2. Include a money-back guarantee with an exact period ("30-night risk-free trial").
    3. Show the price clearly and prominently.
    • Examples: "Feel the difference in 5 days. 60-night money-back guarantee, $44.99" and "Sleep better by night one. 90-night satisfaction guarantee, $59.99 today." [L0313.005]
  • Competitor comparison with negative indicators (works by making the alternative look worse and clarifying your unique safety or benefit advantage) [L0313.007]:
    1. Display your product's key attributes with checkmarks or positive phrasing.
    2. List what alternatives include that customers want to avoid, marked with X, "no" or "risk of".
    • Examples: "Natural formula ✓, Zero chemicals X, Long-lasting ✓, No rebound effect X" and "Effective immediately ✓, No grogginess X, No prescription X, No dependency ✓." [L0313.007]
  • Urgency with specificity (vague urgency such as "soon" creates no action, while specific deadlines trigger fear of missing out) [L0313.008]:
    • State the end day, for example "Offer ends Sunday".
    • Or use "Today Only" or "Limited time only".
    • Or show inventory scarcity.
    • Examples: "Launch offer ends Thursday. Get 20% off now" and "This weekend only: buy now at launch price, $39.99." [L0313.008]
  • Offer testing: start without an offer, check visitor behaviour, then test offers such as Buy one get the second item 20% off, Buy one get the second 50% off, or Buy two get the third free. The presenter's winning offer at the time was Buy one get the second item 20% off. [L0642.027]
  • Place the offer text in the Primary Text of a single image ad, the most visible spot, where it also appears as the first line on Instagram. For traffic from both Facebook and Instagram, put the offer in the Primary Text. It can also go in other parts of the ad. [L0650.018]
  • Run the first ads as conversion campaigns with urgency and fear-of-missing-out messaging so first-time visitors buy and the product is validated. Use retargeting and email to win back visitors who did not buy. [L0661.036]
  • Use only evergreen promo codes, such as a permanent 3% or 5% code created in Shopify. Expiring or changing codes break remarketing ads once they stop working. Confirm codes are active before publishing. [L1029.025]

Social Proof, Reviews and Comments

  • (Older, 2021-22.) Include text-based customer reviews as quotes under the initial copy in Facebook ads and other ads with enough copy space. This adds credibility and increases curiosity and click-through motivation. [L0241.007]
  • (Older, 2021-22.) Review-led ad structure [L1398.005]:
    1. Place a headline that summarises a review above the body copy.
    2. Start the body copy with a review text, so the shopper sees social proof first.
  • (Older, 2021-22.) A Facebook ad can carry four or five reviews in the top text, ending with a link back to the product. Choose reviews that highlight different aspects of the product or service so all key points are covered. [L1398.006]
  • (Older, 2021-22.) If many reviews praise the business itself, such as seller service or the buying experience, the trusted advisor ad can focus on the business instead of one product. Product images can still be used, but the text should say plainly what the business is known for. [L1398.007]
  • Social proof scale: "50K+ users" stated as "relieved and relaxed" gives concrete credibility, compared with an unspecified "thousands of happy customers." [L0313.021]
  • Reply to every comment with helpful answers such as sizes or shipping times. Do not pitch or post sales links. Ask a follow-up question to keep the conversation going, which builds engagement that lowers CPM. [L0645.027]
  • CPM was reported to fall from about $15 at launch to $5-$8 after building engagement with comments. Typical dropshipping CPMs are $20-$30. A new campaign can still show $20-$30 before it gets engagement. [L0645.028]
  • An ad with zero comments looks untrustworthy, so people do not ask questions or comment. Seed the first comments and reviews yourself from the brand page at launch. [L0645.030]
  • Ads with high engagement, such as comments, likes and shares, get a lower CPM and reach more people for the same spend. Creatives that invite viewers to comment or share can produce this effect. [L1171.013]
  • Example: an ad inviting viewers to post a photo of their own pet in the comments triggered many comments and likes. The higher engagement lowered the CPM, so the same budget reached more people. [L1171.054]

Single Image Ads

  • Image ads are cheaper on Facebook and often convert better than video. Use images for products such as jewellery, fashion and some lifestyle products. [L0577.039]
  • Image ads often have a lower CPM than video, so they reach more people for the same budget. Facebook may also show image ads more to users with a slow internet connection. Video ads cost more but can be useful for retargeting. [L0642.019]
  • Products that need no explanation, such as home decor, clothing, shoes and jewellery, can be sold with picture ads without video. Videos are needed for products that must be explained or demonstrated. [L0642.020]
  • Scaling with image ads alone is possible. One operator reached seven-figure revenue over six-plus months, with roughly 99% of sales from static image ads and no video ads. Image ads are easy to produce in Canva and carry better targeting than many alternatives. [L0349.001]
  • For Facebook ads promoting clothing, image ads perform best compared with other formats. [L0779.014]
  • Include natural, non-graphic photo ads alongside designed graphics, because designed ads can still look like ads. Carousel ads suit lifestyle-heavy products such as fashion, and single image ads can also be strong. [L0341.020]
  • For a single image ad, Meta's Ad Setup > Format offers Single image or video. Use Carousel only when several cards are needed on purpose. [L0865.039]
  • Meme ad procedure: place a text on top of the image, add the product picture below, and add an emoji. The presenter builds these in Canva. Test multiple texts with one image. [L0642.023]

Video Ads

  • (Older, 2021-22.) A Facebook video ad is built in almost the same way as a static image ad. A static image version also works, though video is generally preferred by audiences. [L0488.012]
  • Video length in the owner's clothing test: ads from 15 seconds to under 20 seconds, with the benefit delivered immediately, tended to outperform one-minute videos, with better click-through and conversion. A before-and-after format showing the result early, then the product, drove more clicks. A one-minute video still has value for retargeting viewers who watched more than 50% of it. [L1077.014]
  • Repurposing a standard Facebook ad into a TikTok aspect ratio performed very badly. Even uploaded as an organic video it got fewer than 100 views. Build creative native to each platform instead. [L1077.013]
  • Ad owner's early lesson: the early Facebook ads worked because the advertiser was also a target customer who knew the audience's interests and followed the same creators. Use a hook-pain-solution-benefit structure and show the product's benefits directly. [L1077.012]
  • Paid ads follow the same content logic as organic posts, because the ad only adds paid distribution and audience targeting. Content that performs organically tends to perform as ads with the right targeting. [L1217.046]
  • Transcript procedure for video ads (older, 2021-22):
    1. Get the video transcribed with an automatic online transcription tool such as Rev.
    2. Paste the exact transcript into the ad copy at the top.
    • Reason: some people do not listen to audio, so they can read the message instead. Always include a link to the page you want visitors to reach. [L0488.011]
  • Rev is an online transcription tool that automatically turns video audio into text, useful for producing ad copy from video. [L0488.013]
  • (Older, 2021-22.) Facebook ads do not offer a click-to-call button. A call-to-action that asks people to phone the business must link to the website's contact page instead. Use the contact page URL as the ad destination. [L0488.015]
  • To promote an existing post, use the version that already has the engagement. Likes, comments and views carry over from the original post, but the other platform's copy starts at zero. Example: use the Facebook post if it has far more views than the Instagram copy. [L0865.038]

Carousel, Catalog and Collection Ads

  • Carousel ads perform best on Facebook, and carousels with five cards perform very well. Catalog ads also work well on Facebook. [L0577.038]
  • Carousel setup:
    • Pick 3 to 5 product images. The presenter uses 4. [L0642.021]
    • Add an offer and a link to each card. Free shipping text is optional. [L0642.021]
    • Use the collection page link as the destination. [L0642.021]
    • Add a headline and description to each card. [L0642.021]
    • Use the same Primary Text as the single image ad. [L0642.021]
    • CTA: Shop Now or See more. [L0642.021]
  • Carousel ads perform well for product tests and show several product angles in one ad. [L1184.032]
  • (Older, 2021-22.) Use a single image or video for most Facebook retargeting ads. Collection ads tend to underperform, and a carousel is an option only when several product views need to be shown. [L1342.030]
  • Collection ads combine a video or slideshow at the top with dynamic product ads underneath. Shoppers can click through to the exact product they viewed, which makes them suitable for retargeting. [L1243.022]

Slideshow Ads

  • Slideshow ad procedure in Meta Ads Manager [L0642.022]:
    1. Choose Create video instead of image or video.
    2. Select 4 product images.
    3. Click Edit and set the display time per image to 1 second.
    4. Arrange the image order.
    5. Click Play to preview.
    6. Click Save to computer and wait for the video to render.
    7. Upload it, then add headline, description and Shop Now CTA, and check the link.

Specifications, Ratios and Safe Areas

  • Feed ad ratio is 4:5, which is 1080 x 1350 pixels. Reels, Stories and TikTok use 9:16 portrait. Placing a 9:16 edit into a feed placement crops it to 4:5, so text outside the 4:5 area can be cut off. [L0344.034]
  • Change the project ratio with the ratio button next to the playhead. 9:16 is the usual portrait ratio for Reels, Stories and TikTok. Build the edit in 9:16 and keep all text and graphics within the 4:5 area so the feed crop does not remove them. [L0344.035]
  • Placing text high on the frame, near the face, is a common mistake because feed placements crop the top and bottom of a 9:16 edit. Move the text down so it stays inside the 4:5 area. Use a 4:5 overlay or a locked guide layer while editing. [L0344.036]
  • Blend modes for an overlay [L0344.037]:
Blend mode Effect
Screen Removes dark areas and keeps light ones
Multiply Keeps dark areas and removes light ones
Soft light A softer version of these two
Darken Keeps only dark areas
Brighten Keeps only light areas
  • Lower the overlay opacity if the effect is too strong. [L0344.037]
  • Facebook crops portrait video to 4:5 in feed placements. Keep the main subject and captions near the centre of the frame and place captions higher than the bottom edge, so the crop does not cut them off. [L1423.017]
  • Overlay banners at the very top of a video can be cropped out when Facebook shows the feed version at 4:5. Export a 4:5 feed version with sale banners inside the visible area, and use a 9:16 version for Stories and Reels where the top banner stays visible. [L1363.056]
  • Ad image specification for link ads: exactly 1200 by 627 pixels, with no more than 20% of the image as text. Ads with more than 20% text on the image are rejected immediately. [L0641.007]
  • Images with more than 20% text get the ad denied right away, so keep text on the image well under that threshold. Ads are also commonly denied in review, which is why a support ticket is the next step. [L0641.008]
  • Keep text on Facebook ad images under 20% of the image area (older, 2021-22). Check with a grid overlay before launch. Images that pass this rule have the best chance of approval. [L0799.008]
  • (Older, 2021-22.) Facebook ad images should be JPG or PNG. Use square 1:1 for feed and in-stream video placements, and fullscreen vertical 9:16 for Stories. Use an image with little or no overlaid text to maximise delivery. [L1342.031]
  • (Older, 2021-22.) In Ads Manager, Automatically Crop to Square creates a square version of an uploaded image for placements that need it. Crop Image lets you adjust the crop manually for each placement. [L1342.049]
  • A square ad uses a 1080 x 1080 pixel frame, a 1:1 aspect ratio. [L1427.020]
  • Set the frame size in the sequence settings of your editor, entering the horizontal and vertical pixel values for the ad format. Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve and other editors have an equivalent setting. [L1427.019]
  • When Meta's ad preview shows dimension warnings for a 4:5 video, the warnings can be ignored in most cases, because a 4:5 file is served to most placements. Check that the upload shows the dimensions as fine, then continue. [L0647.035]

Placements, Previews and Destinations

  • Preview each ad on Instagram, the Facebook feed and Facebook Marketplace before publishing. The same carousel or picture can look different in each placement. [L0642.053]
  • Separate formats for placements (procedure) [L0773.030]:
    1. Upload the horizontal video.
    2. Click Edit video, then Edit placements.
    3. Upload the story-format version for Stories.
    • Using the same video in two formats (feed and story) is also acceptable. [L0773.030]
  • For a video that runs on Facebook and Instagram in different formats, upload the horizontal version first. Then click Edit video > Edit placements and upload the story version. Feed placements on Facebook and Instagram were the best placements for the lesson's account, so switch from automatic to manual placement once data shows feeds perform best. [L0774.038]
  • (Older, 2021-22.) The same image, headline and body copy can run on Instagram news feed placements. Keeping one creative across Facebook and Instagram supports consistent recognition. [L0415.007]
  • (Older, 2021-22.) Facebook creates the Instagram version of a Facebook feed ad automatically, so a separate Instagram ad does not have to be built. The Instagram version is identical to the Facebook version unless you change it. [L1169.011]
  • (Older, 2021-22.) Facebook feed ads can use a short or long copy version. Long copy gives more room for product detail, and a View more link lets readers expand it, so the most important text should sit at the top. [L1169.012]
  • (Older, 2021-22.) On Instagram, only the first line of body copy shows before the more link. The opening line must summarise what the ad is about so the viewer clicks to read the rest. [L1398.008]
  • Set the ad destination to the product page or landing page for one-product stores. Use a collection link only when customers should choose between several products, such as fashion or jewellery. [L0648.027]
  • Set the ad CTA to Shop Now when the ad sends traffic to a collection page. Use Shop Now with the product page link when the ad sends traffic to one product. [L0642.024]
  • Multi-advertiser ads is on by default in the ad format section. Meta can personalise the ad and resize the creative for each viewer. Check the creative in the preview because it can be resized. [L0865.061]
  • Ad-level Languages can translate the ad text based on the viewer's location or default language. It is optional and can stay off in a standard sales setup. [L0865.062]

Meta Setup Settings

  • Under Ad creative setup, Meta allows up to five Primary Text versions (shown as "Primary text (1 of 5)") and several headlines, added with the Add text option and Add headline option. When more than one text is entered, Meta shows the version it predicts will perform best. The label reads "Not optimised" while that setting is off. [L0348.028]
  • The Set up test option under the creative section compares up to 7 different versions of one creative in a single test. It is meant to make sure new test ads receive delivery. [L0348.029]
  • Advantage+ creative enhancements: the default is 6 of 8 enabled. Switch to All optimisations to turn all 8 on, which shows 8 out of 8 after saving. [L1029.021]
  • Dynamic creative testing (DCT) combines many images with copy and headlines, and Facebook finds the best combinations. Adding a DCT ad as a new asset in a scaling CBO shows whether new creatives get spend. If it gets almost none, the creatives are likely not good enough. [L1107.023]
  • Dynamic creative is being phased out by Meta. While it is still available, using it switches on the Flexible ad format, so the Flexible option is turned off in this test setup. [L1193.017]
  • Some advertisers build creatives as dark posts, which are unpublished posts that appear only inside ads and not on the Page timeline. The speaker still preferred this older method for setting up his ads. [L0643.061]
  • Promotion and sandbox ads run as dark posts and never appear on the Facebook page. Post publicly only the content you want public, to the page first. [L0649.040]
  • Ad setup procedure for a creative test [L1193.018]:
    1. Name the ad with today's date, product name, CBO, ad set name and the ad number, following the naming formula.
    2. Choose Manual setup.
    3. Select the Facebook Page and the Instagram account that should appear as the ad identity.
    4. Choose the single image or video format and turn the Flexible option off.
    5. Open the Media box, choose Videos, and upload all the video creatives you have collected. Uploading takes time, so wait for it to finish.
    6. Paste the Primary Text and headlines, add one description, paste the landing page as the website URL, and select Shop Now as the CTA.
    7. Duplicate the ad once for each creative so every creative has its own ad, then publish all ads. [L1193.018]
  • Creating the other two creatives in one ad set [L0647.010]:
    1. Click the three toggle buttons on the first ad, choose Duplicate, and repeat once more so there are three copies in total.
    2. Rename the copies Video 2 and Video 3.
    3. In each copy, open Ad creative, click Edit media, click Change video and select the next video (image ads also work).
    4. Keep the Primary Text, headline, description and call to action identical to the first ad. [L0647.010]

Creative Testing Structure

  • Andromeda retrieval engine (deployed December 2024): creative now does the audience targeting, so tight audience restrictions can hurt performance. Campaigns need genuinely different creative concepts, since small changes such as a word or a colour do not count. Campaigns with 8-15 truly different concepts are expected to outperform those with 2-3 variants of one idea. [L0613.044]
  • A concept pairs one avatar with one angle: a specific pain point, a variation in messaging, or a demographic segment such as ethnicity, age group or life stage. [L1019.006]
  • Test a new creative with the best existing audience, such as a winning interest or stacked interests, so only the creative varies. Reduce all other variables, including the offer and audience, to keep the test readable. [L0646.043]
  • Moving a winning ad into a new ad set and forcing spend on it, while other creatives get almost no spend, can raise CPM and CPC. Test untested creatives in a separate ad set so Facebook can learn which one performs, instead of pushing one ad. [L0645.016]
  • Ads that get almost no spend in a campaign with a clear winner usually have weaker results in Facebook's data, so this is normal. Test them separately in their own ad set before deciding they are dead. [L0645.017]
  • Putting several ads into one ad set makes Meta concentrate spend on one or two ads while others get only cents. Creative test results are then unreliable, so use one ad per ad set during creative testing. [L1316.011]
  • Creative testing structure with ABO [L1316.012]:
    1. Create one new campaign with budget set at ad set level (ABO).
    2. Create nine ad sets, each with a daily budget of 5 USD.
    3. Put exactly one new ad into each ad set.
    4. Target a winning interest in some ad sets and a broad audience in others.
    5. Let the campaign run and compare the nine ads on performance, then keep the best one.
  • Creative testing with proven audiences [L0648.060]:
    1. Build one creative testing campaign whose ad sets are your winning audiences, one ad set per winning interest or custom audience.
    2. Add one new creative as three variations that differ only in the first 3-5 seconds (the hook).
    3. Keep the same three variations in every ad set so the results can be compared across audiences.
    4. Treat a creative as a likely winner only if it has the best CTR in all winning ad sets, not just one.
    5. Replace the older creative in the main campaigns with the confirmed winner.
  • If one creative wins across all ad sets, use only that creative for scaling. If no creative wins everywhere, let the audiences decide: different creatives may be relatable to different audiences, so give the campaign several strong options. [L1177.045]
  • Ad structure inside each ad set: one concept with its three hook variations, so each ad set holds three ads and every ad set carries the same three ads. Two ads per ad set is the minimum. Two to three is the recommended structure, and three to four are also possible. [L0902.021]
  • Let each creative test run for two to three days before judging it. Judge the concept by profitability, and judge the hooks by hook rate. [L0902.022]
  • Avatar testing procedure [L0906.018]:
    1. Keep the problem concept fixed, and choose one audience to test it on.
    2. Create several avatar variants of the same audience, for example by ethnicity, language or gender, such as English-speaking, Spanish-speaking, Latino, or Spanish-speaking men and women.
    3. Test each avatar version with the same problem concept.
    4. Keep the version that performs best and build more variants from it.
  • Test several creative formats at once, such as carousel, meme-style image, slideshow and single picture ads. Compare their CTR and spend, and keep the format with the strongest CTR. Scale that one once it has enough spend. [L0650.057]
  • Testing multiple creatives is effective. Changing the pictures and adding the offer inside the image improved results for an ad set that had been showing the same creative for a long time. [L0650.017]
  • The Sandbox is a separate campaign for split-testing creatives. CBO tends to concentrate spend on the early winner, so new ads in core campaigns rarely get a fair test. [L0649.025]
  • Launch the Sandbox only after the core campaigns are live. Test new creatives against the best-performing cold audience. When a new ad wins, move it into the core campaign and turn off the old one. [L0649.026]
  • Launch a new campaign with a meaningful number of creatives, ideally between 10 and 20. Use the time before launch to produce as many creatives as possible while saving cash. [L1225.054]
  • A single ad set with about 13 creatives, including four videos and nine static images, across different angles is a reasonable amount. Diversify angles, such as by gender, and add variants over time. [L1225.061]
  • Beginners should aim to launch more than 20 ads per month. Use a monthly ad spend divided by 500 to 1,000 USD as a rough check of that number. [L1235.016]
  • Creative and conversion matter more than the ad budget at the start. Fix the page and the creative first, then increase spend so the test produces useful data. [L1016.070]
  • Creative quality and offer quality have a bigger effect on ROAS and scalability than the choice of scaling strategy. A strong creative with a good offer and a strong sales page makes the audience and budget structure matter less. [L0999.033]

Creative Fatigue and Refresh

  • Update and test creative ad variations weekly, and refresh campaign creatives every week. [L0238.010]
  • Ads that get almost no spend in a campaign with a clear winner should be tested in their own ad set before being judged dead. [L0645.017]
  • Do not copy a competitor's full creative. Meta can detect duplicates and penalise your ad when the competitor is penalised. Reuse only the proven hook (about the first 1-5 seconds), then change slides and clips, add your logo and product name. Alternatively use custom or UGC content when budget allows. [L0637.015]
  • Meta's algorithm updates, such as Andromeda, are increasingly wary of AI-generated ads that look like spam. Generic or mass-produced ads can lose reach, so AI output must meet a real quality standard before going live. [L0498.003]
  • A Dynamic Creative ad that gets almost no spend in a scaling CBO means the creatives are likely not good enough. Replace them rather than adding more of the same. [L1107.023]
  • Use the Sandbox to bring new creatives into core campaigns when they win, and turn off the old one. [L0649.026]

AI-Generated Creative

  • Meta's algorithm may penalise or raise CPMs on AI-generated bottom-of-funnel ads uploaded directly, because it prefers original content and reads image metadata. If you make ads with ChatGPT, Claude or similar tools, upload the image to Canva and download it again before uploading to Meta. [L0316.011]
  • The AI creative approach taught in this series applies across product categories, including pet, fashion and pain relief. The principles are not tied to one niche, so an advertiser can adapt them to any product. [L0498.005]
  • A default AI answer, asked which resources it would study to build a system for effective Meta ad creative, relies mainly on Meta's own guidance plus generic inputs such as reviews, the product itself, competitors and ROAS. Generic sources like this do not reveal what actually works, which usually comes from testing over years. [L1478.006]
  • Generic AI-made ads can lose reach on Meta; the quality bar in [L0498.003] applies to every AI output before it goes live. [L0498.003]

Further details

  • Image and video ads should be placed in separate ad sets; when they share an ad set, the algorithm may stop delivering the image and push all budget to the video. Duplicate the ad set and place the winning image in its own ad set to test it. [L0642.018]
