# 13. Influencers and UGC

## Role of Influencers, Creators and UGC in the Business Model

### Role of Influencers, Creators and UGC in the Business Model

#### Where Influencer Marketing Fits

- Influencer marketing is an advanced lever for later stages of a business, not a starting tactic for a new store. [L0544.012]
- Influencers are one of the core traffic sources in this model and are used alongside SEO and paid search, not instead of them. [L0759.005]
- Consistent revenue comes from paid ads, while influencers add brand and social proof. [L0661.059]
- Influencer marketing is a long-term channel that requires patience, consistency and admin work. Over time it tends to be more profitable, consistent and reliable than relying on paid traffic alone. [L1251.016]
- Affiliates, influencers and high-authority blogs rarely send meaningful sales early on. Focus first on Google Ads and Meta remarketing. Once the business has momentum, use paid affiliate deals and a referral program. [L0804.057]

#### When to Start (Stage-of-Business Timing)

- Validate the product with Facebook ads before investing in influencers. Then choose influencers with an engaged audience. Influencer work is best used to build social proof and brand awareness once sales exist. [L0661.057]
- Start commissioning UGC after a product is validated, meaning ads on Facebook or TikTok already bring real buyers and the goal is to improve conversion rate and prepare for scaling. [L1402.002]
- When building a brand with purchased stock instead of dropshipping, hire creators from the start to make authentic videos that connect people with the brand faster. [L1402.003]
- Early growth was built by finding the most ideal customer, growing an Instagram page organically, and getting people and influencers to promote the brand. This worked well only in the earlier social media environment. [L1438.042]
- Influencer marketing and referral marketing suit later stages, after validation, when sales already exist. [L0544.012, L0661.057, L0804.057]

#### Why Influencers Work

- Influencer marketing transfers the trust an influencer already has with followers to a brand. Recommendations often feel more genuine than traditional advertising. [L0479.017, L0480.001]
- Influencer marketing is a useful way to reach a new audience and bridge the gap to consumers, especially for new brands, because the creator already has an established audience and the content feels authentic. [L0630.011]
- Influencer marketing suits new brands because influencers already have an audience, which helps bridge the gap between a new brand and its customers. Its authentic, relatable content tends to build brand awareness and visibility, though its effect must still be measured. [L0786.014]
- Influencer partnerships drive brand growth mainly because they put a recognizable face behind the brand, which raises visibility and credibility. [L0505.001]
- Shoppers often check a brand's social profiles to confirm it is legitimate and to see real people who have used the product. [L0505.001]
- Content posted on the creator's own page carries more trust than content they only deliver for paid media, because it uses the audience relationship the creator has built. [L0505.001]
- An influencer's large reach can expand brand awareness and visibility on social platforms, bringing the product in front of people who would not otherwise see it. [L0480.003]

#### Creative Reuse

- Influencer collaborations give brands access to creative content that already resonates with an audience. That content can be reused as shareable material to amplify the brand's message. [L0479.018]
- Influencers are skilled at making content that resonates with their audience. Brands can reuse that content as shareable, compelling material to amplify their message. [L0480.004]

#### Expected Returns and Volatility

- Influencer marketing has high ROI potential if successful. When it works, the return can exceed any other paid platform. Success is not guaranteed and requires strong research skills. [L0296.027]
- Influencer marketing gets traction more cheaply and quickly than paid ads. [L0561.038]
- Influencer sales come in spikes and are hard to predict, so they are a gamble for direct sales. [L0661.059]
- Influencer-driven sales are volatile and come in spikes. Do not expect steady daily revenue from influencers alone. [L1440.040]
- Example: an influencer-led store sold about 16,000 euros in one day with a 50% profit margin on a one-day spike. The sales came from influencer marketing only, with no other ads running. This is one case and a spike, not a steady baseline. [L1440.039, L1440.040]
- A survey figure cited for influencer marketing states that 86% of people who use it effectively report an immediate or long-term effect on their brand. No survey source is named. Use it only as a rough indication and verify it before relying on it in a plan or report. [L0630.012]
- About 50% of consumers look for recommendations from others before they buy, so influencer content works best high in the sales funnel to bring in qualified traffic. [L0790.091]

#### Trends by Period

- Influencer marketing was described as regaining strength in 2023 after a weaker period in 2021 and 2022, so it was treated as a channel to test again. Check the current market before relying on older assumptions about any channel. [L1214.060]
- Older advice from 2019 to 2022 on influencer effectiveness should be treated as dated; the 2023 recovery means it must be re-tested rather than assumed. [L1214.060]

#### Brand-Image Tradeoffs

- Influencer marketing gets traction more cheaply and quickly than paid ads, but may devalue the brand image. Paid ads give more control over how the brand is perceived. [L0561.038]
- Celebrity endorsements paid for across many unrelated products have damaged the credibility of testimonials. Prefer ordinary customers describing their own experience over famous names praising products they have not truly used. [L1369.038]

| Channel | Speed to traction | Cost profile | Brand control | Revenue pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Influencer marketing | Faster, cheaper traction | Lower entry cost | Lower; may devalue brand image | Spiky, hard to predict [L0561.038, L0661.059] |
| Paid ads | Slower to start | Ongoing spend | Higher control over perception | Consistent revenue [L0561.038, L0661.059] |

#### Affiliates and Referral Partnerships

- Affiliate partnerships with blogs and creators can bring traffic and sales. They are a useful growth channel once a product is validated. [L0661.060]
- Once the business has momentum, use paid affiliate deals and a referral program. [L0804.057]

#### Testing Influencer Traffic

- When paid ads produce few add-to-carts, influencer content can be tested as an alternative source of traffic. In the discussed case it generated more add-to-carts and at least one purchase. [L0361.030]

#### Seeding and Budget Split

- In a first year with almost no money, one brand split its marketing budget 75% to influencer seeding and 25% to remarketing. The reasoning was that traffic it paid for had to be captured again. This is one brand's choice in a low-budget situation, not a general rule. [L0790.088]

#### Choosing Influencers by Niche

- Influencers are useful for a print-on-demand niche only when the niche is open to influencer marketing and the influencers are not too large (not macro). Choose influencers whose audience fits the niche. [L1438.041]

#### Digital Products

- Digital products such as eBooks can be promoted through influencers instead of paid ads. This approach was planned but not yet tested. [L1431.048]

#### UGC Practice

- User-generated content builds trust because prospects believe other buyers' experiences more than polished brand ads. In dropshipping, raw clips from real people often move a viewer to the next step faster than traditional advertising. [L1402.001]
- For UGC after validation: give the creator the product and let them name the store in the video to build more trust. [L1402.002]

#### Common Mistake

- Celebrity endorsements paid for across many unrelated products have damaged the credibility of testimonials. [L1369.038]

#### Further details

- For holiday campaigns, influencer marketing on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube and Twitter was described as the most profitable approach, ahead of paid ads. Twitter ads and influencers were described as especially strong. [L1214.036]

## Setting Objectives and Choosing Platforms

### Setting One Campaign Objective

- Define one specific objective before starting an influencer campaign, such as launching a new product, promoting an upcoming sale or building brand awareness. Tell the influencer this objective so both sides work toward the same goal. [L0787.001]
- Set the campaign objective before choosing an influencer, because brand awareness, engagement and conversions each require a different kind of influencer. [L0479.008]

| Objective | Influencer to look for | Reason | Source ids |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand awareness | Broad reach and a large, diverse audience | A high-profile influencer can introduce the brand to many new potential customers. | [L0479.009] |
| Engagement | An active audience whose followers regularly comment, like, share and re-post | This audience may be smaller than a broad-reach audience but is more involved. | [L0479.010] |
| Sales and conversions | A track record of shaping purchase decisions, such as in-depth product reviews, product usage demonstrations or exclusive discounts for followers | Purchase decisions are the target. | [L0479.011] |
| New product launch | An influencer who has previously introduced new products to their audience | Consider a promotion or giveaway with them. | [L0479.012] |

- For awareness, a high-level influencer may still be worth it when you need impressions, brand recognition and press. Press features and backlinks from these collaborations can also drive traffic and improve search visibility. [L0790.050]
- Creators match different objectives. A creator with a highly engaged community who starts conversations on most posts suits community engagement. A creator who dedicates videos to product reviews suits driving sales conversions. [L0505.008]

### Defining the Target Audience First

- Define the target audience's demographics, interests and behaviour before searching for influencers, because these determine which influencer will resonate most with your customers. [L0479.002]
- Examples of audience-first matching:
  - A fitness brand targeting health-minded young adults should look for a fitness, nutrition or wellness influencer whose followers already share that interest, not only an influencer with a large following. The influencer then aligns with the brand's values, and their followers are more likely to share the buyer's interests. [L0479.003, L0480.024]
  - A tech brand should look for an influencer who does gadget reviews, tech tips and gaming content, or who regularly publishes product reviews and covers the latest gadgets. That audience is tech-savvy and more likely to be interested in technology products. [L0479.004, L0480.025]
  - A fashion brand with bold, edgy designs aimed at young buyers should look for a streetwear or experimental fashion influencer whose aesthetic matches the brand. That style fits the brand's look and can attract new customers who match its designs. [L0479.005, L0480.026]
- The right influencer depends on the brand's niche and region. A kitchenware brand may work with food bloggers, while a wellness brand may work with fitness coaches or lifestyle creators. There is no universal list of influencers for every brand. [L0505.018]
- Start with micro-influencers, and test at least five different influencer categories before choosing a focus. Categories can include fashion, beauty, fitness, pets, mommy bloggers and photographers. [L0790.047]

### Influencer Selection Procedure

Follow these five steps in this order:

1. Define the target audience by demographics, interests and behaviour. [L0479.001, L0480.023]
2. Set a clear campaign goal: brand awareness, engagement, conversions or another specific objective. [L0479.001, L0480.023]
3. Use an influencer marketing platform to search for candidates that fit the audience and goal. [L0479.001, L0480.023]
4. Check each candidate's social media channels on every platform they use, and check whether their content style suits your audience. [L0479.001, L0480.023]
5. Analyze the influencer's engagement metrics before collaborating. [L0479.001, L0480.023]

- The influencer marketing platforms in step 3 are search tools for finding creators. They are a different thing from the social platforms covered in the next sections. [L0479.001, L0480.023]

### Platform First or Influencer First

- Audiences differ significantly by platform, so a creator's audience on TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat or YouTube should be evaluated separately. Choose the platform for the campaign first, then find creators present on it. [L0505.010]
- Start influencer work by choosing the social platforms your brand will use, and complete your research on those platforms first. Only after that, identify potential influencers who are active on them. Then evaluate each candidate on content, engagement and past collaborations. [L0782.014]
- These two rules agree on the order of platform and creator. The selection procedure above uses "platform" for influencer search tools, so keep the two meanings apart when briefing your team. [L0505.010, L0782.014, L0479.001]

### Instagram

- Instagram skews toward millennials and young adults aged 18 to 34 and is strongly visual. [L0479.019, L0480.014]
- Instagram works well for photos, very short videos, Stories and links in Stories. It is a strong choice when you want high engagement. [L0479.019, L0480.014]
- Instagram has a shop feature. A shop can be attached to the account so products can be sold directly from the platform, and influencer content can drive direct product sales. [L0479.020, L0480.015]
- Instagram's "Your Algorithm" control accepts plain-language requests about the content a user wants to see. A baby-product brand could ask for parenting advice, baby care and children's DIY videos, which surfaces creators whose audiences match its target customers. [L1504.026]
- Instagram remains the largest audience for most influencers, even when they also post on TikTok. [L0789.018]

### YouTube

- YouTube has a broader audience and favours long-form content such as tutorials, reviews and entertainment. [L0479.021, L0480.016]
- Viewers who watch a full video are more likely to buy, so YouTube has a high conversion rate. [L0479.021, L0480.016]
- YouTube viewers often arrive with search intent, looking for a specific product or solution. This high purchase intent gives YouTube influencer content strong conversion potential, which is why YouTube suits product-focused influencer content. [L0479.033, L0480.028]
- YouTube is sometimes used as an SEO platform, and YouTube videos can support SEO. [L0479.021, L0480.016, L0480.028]
- A YouTube creator with large view counts can still underperform for a physical product if the audience is young and often lacks its own payment method. Check the buyer age before paying for a YouTube collaboration. [L0790.096]

### TikTok

- TikTok's core audience is Gen Z. Its short-form content spreads trends quickly and favours very short, fast content. [L0479.022, L0480.017]
- TikTok's perceived authenticity is high, and users often buy directly from authentic creators. [L0479.022, L0480.017]
- TikTok Shop allows a shop to be attached to a TikTok account. It is a newer function, and it has shown strong sales results and growth. [L0479.023, L0480.017]
- TikTok influencers can deliver about three times the results of an Instagram influencer at about half the price. Once they have a following, they start receiving sponsorship offers themselves. [L1440.038]
- TikTok creators can be cheaper than Instagram creators, so a TikTok-focused campaign can be proposed as an alternative to Instagram. [L0789.018]
- The two figures above come from different sources and make different claims. The first is a results and price claim. The second is a cost and reach comparison. The units do not state a measurement basis for either, so test with your own data before relying on them. [L1440.038, L0789.018]

### Snapchat and Pinterest

- Snapchat and Pinterest also exist but were left out of the main comparison. Instagram, YouTube and TikTok were chosen for their engagement, sales and conversion results. [L0480.019]
- Snapchat and Pinterest are additional social channels. For influencer vetting, check Instagram, YouTube and TikTok first, and add other channels only when the target audience clearly uses them. [L0479.034, L0480.019]
- For a fashion sunglasses brand, Pinterest was used only as a repost channel and Snapchat was hard to penetrate, so the paid focus went to Facebook and Instagram. [L0790.095]
- Pinterest group boards have more than one contributor and are marked by a multi-person icon in the corner. Some have thousands to over 100,000 followers, so getting pins onto them can reach a much larger audience than your own profile. [L1136.024]
- To move existing audiences onto Pinterest, post a message on your other social accounts, such as Twitter or Facebook, inviting followers to follow your Pinterest profile or a new board. [L1136.036]

### Matching Content Style to Platform

- Match the platform to the content style and the audience. TikTok suits quick, short-form, entertaining content for a younger, trend-driven audience. Instagram suits visually appealing images, videos and Stories. [L0479.024, L0480.018]
- Let the chosen platform shape the influencer brief, so the format delivered fits how the audience on that platform consumes content. [L0479.025]
- For influencer marketing of clothing, try-on hauls perform best, followed by unboxing content, which can be used as reels and Stories. [L0779.016]

### Brand's Own Channels

- The platforms used to vet influencers, Instagram, YouTube and TikTok, can also serve the brand's own presence. Plan the brand's own channels alongside influencer partnerships. Each platform's format and audience should guide what the brand posts there. [L0479.031]

## The Dream 100 Partner Strategy

### The Dream 100 Concept

- The Dream 100 strategy identifies the top players already established in your niche, such as celebrities, websites, blogs, YouTubers, course creators, events, Instagram accounts, podcasters and associations. You form relationships with them so they send their traffic to your store, instead of competing with them for the same audience. This traffic source is relationship-driven and requires constant nurturing and tweaking, but it can generate consistent revenue as a gold mine channel. [L0183.001]
- The approach means identifying people, businesses or organizations that already have an engaged audience of your target customers, then building relationships with them. This lets you gain some of the reach and trust of their audience while growing your own. [L0412.003] (older, 2021-22)
- Partnerships with influencers work because target customers trust, respect and listen to that person. The "influencer" label describes this narrowly. Think of it as borrowing an existing audience's trust. [L0412.004] (older, 2021-22)
- The concept has been described in sales and marketing literature for a long time and is a common way many businesses grow through relationships with others who serve the same target customer. It is used less in e-commerce than it should be, but it is a well-tested process. [L0412.006] (older, 2021-22)

### The Four-Step Process

(older, 2021-22) [L0412.009]

1. **Research:** Identify the people, businesses or organizations that already have an engaged audience of your target customers.
2. **Follow and contact:** Follow them and make contact with them.
3. **Build a relationship:** Build a relationship with them.
4. **Explore collaboration:** Explore collaboration options with them.

### Building the Dream 100 List

- A Dream 100 list is the top 100 people and entities in your industry that already reach your ideal customers. It works like a master supplier list, but for influencers and referral partners. Blogs, companies and communities count as well as individuals. [L0528.004]
- The method builds a fixed list of about 100 high-reach partners in a niche, such as influencers, community administrators or companies. You then approach them for partnerships, instead of sending individual cold emails or posting ads directly in their communities. [L0837.067]
- Any online place where your target audience already spends time belongs on the list. [L0528.005]

### Partner Types

- Dream 100 partners can be celebrities, websites, blogs, YouTubers, course creators, industry events, Instagram accounts, podcasts, online stores that are not direct competitors, and associations or online communities centered on your niche. [L0528.005, L0183.001]

| Partner type | How to find or approach it | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Course creators | Teachers of your niche on learning platforms such as Udemy. Search the platform for niche courses. [L0528.006] | L0528.006 |
| Niche competitions and industry events | Valid partner types for your list. [L0528.006] | L0528.006 |
| Communities (Facebook groups, Reddit communities) | Where a whole segment already gathers, such as interior designers. Approach the group administrators, not the group. [L0837.003] | L0837.003 |
| Blogs and YouTube channels | Approach the owners of the channel or blog about a partnership. [L0837.003] | L0837.003 |
| Instagram pages | Approach the owner of the page about a partnership. [L0837.003] | L0837.003 |
| Associations | Find them by searching Google for your niche name plus "association." (older, 2018) [L1274.083] | L1274.083 |

- To reach a whole segment at once, such as interior designers, find the communities where they already gather: Facebook groups, Reddit communities, blogs and YouTube channels. [L0837.003]

### Partnership Forms

- Free relationships require no upfront payment. They include guest podcast appearances, guest blog posts, speaking at industry events while representing your store, and affiliate deals where the partner earns a commission. [L0528.010, L0183.002]
- Paid relationships mean putting money or product in upfront. They include sponsored YouTube videos, Instagram posts, event sponsorships, celebrity video collaborations, and paying a creator for a recorded video. [L0528.010, L0183.002]

### Association Partnership Procedure

(older, 2018) [L1274.083]

1. Search Google for your niche name plus the word "association."
2. Contact each association by email or phone. Introduce yourself and say where you are calling from.
3. Offer the association's members a bonus gift or a discount on your products.
4. Ask the partner to add a link to your store, with your promo code, on their website.

The link brings customers from the partner and gives you a backlink for SEO. This works whatever you sell. [L1274.083]

### Why Relationships Beat Cold Outreach

- Partner credibility transfers into purchase decisions because customers recognize the name and face of a partner they trust. The partner's endorsement can move buyers who would not find the store through search alone. [L0528.016]
- One-to-one cold emails to individual companies are manual and require long back-and-forth conversations. Partnership-based one-to-many outreach is usually more efficient, because many inbound cold emails get ignored. [L0837.004]
- Posting ads directly in groups usually gets them removed. Approach the group administrators and the owners of channels or Instagram pages about a partnership instead. [L0837.003]

### Approaches to Avoid

- Do not reach out to media or as-seen-on companies proactively, because the cost is high and the benefit is small. (older, 2022) [L1281.114]
- If a company asks for free products in exchange for a credit, offer the products at wholesale cost instead of giving them away. (older, 2022) [L1281.114]

## Building and Qualifying the Prospect List

### Setting Up the Tracker and Channel Mix

Follow these steps to set up the tracker. *Older advice, 2021-22.* [L0202.001]

1. Create a tracker document to organize Dream 100 prospects.
2. Identify prospects across multiple channels (social media, email, communities, forums).
3. Follow them on their platforms.
4. Subscribe to their email newsletters.
5. Join their Facebook groups and online forums.

- Target platforms for Dream 100 are blogs and websites, online forums (including the forum and its owners or moderators), Facebook groups, Instagram, YouTube, and LinkedIn for B2B businesses only. Other channels relevant to a niche, such as Pinterest for interior design, can be added. *Older advice, 2021-22.* [L0527.008]
- Facebook pages with large followings are excluded from Dream 100 targets, because page posts reach under 10% of followers and page audiences are largely unengaged unless you run ads. *Older advice, 2021-22.* [L0527.009]
- Facebook group admins are valid targets, because their posts reach at least around 50% of members as notifications. *Older advice, 2021-22.* [L0527.009]
- A creator who posts on several platforms can be entered more than once, and a single person can have several entries. Some niches only have YouTube, Instagram and Facebook groups, which is acceptable. Aim for a reasonably balanced spread across channels when the market allows it. *Older advice, 2021-22.* [L0527.010]
- Instagram reach is moving closer to Facebook reach but is still much better than Facebook pages. LinkedIn and YouTube also reach more of their followers than Facebook pages, so an active creator on these platforms is more valuable. *Older advice, 2021-22.* [L0527.026]
- A large forum can be split into subsections. When one section matches your target market better than the whole forum, enter that subsection as its own tracker row next to the parent forum. *Older advice, 2021-22.* [L0527.023]

### Pure Creators and Sources Without a Website

- Pure creators with no website or other online presence usually earn from ad revenue, sponsorships, viewer donations or membership platforms. They typically form a single entry in the matrix with only a YouTube or podcast property. ### Sourcing Experts and Creators

- Experts in a niche include anyone with a large audience there, such as large YouTube channels, large blogs, or pro athletes with big followings. Identifying them is useful for ad targeting and for partnership opportunities. *Older advice, 2021-22.* [L0872.021]

- Build a list of potential influencer and authority partners:
  1. Search Pinterest or Google for people already using or showing your product.
  2. Note each person's name and category, such as social media influencer, actor, artist, celebrity or doctor.
  3. Record each person's social media handle next to their name.

### Verifying AI-Generated Lists

- AI-generated partner lists can contain broken websites, wrong or missing emails, and names that do not match. Every entry must be verified manually before outreach. [L0528.024]
- One AI run returned only 30 rows, and a rerun asking for 100 rows returned 42. If the output is short, keep the usable rows and build the rest through manual research. [L0528.025]

Record this list in a spreadsheet with four columns: (A) the category describing the partner type (Public Figure, Media Property, Writer, Video Creator, Course Developer, Event, Social Media Creator, Podcaster, or Professional Group), (B) the person's or organization's name, (C) their web presence address if available, and (D) their contact email if obtainable. - Verification procedure for each AI-listed entry:

  2. Confirm the site matches the name and note the audience location, for example a site based in one country with a US audience.
  3. Find the contact page or email, using Cmd+F (Ctrl+F on Windows) to search the page for *contact*.
  4. If there is no email address, record the contact form or an Instagram account for direct messages.
  5. Check blog posts for named outside authors. If none appear across several posts, mark guest posts as no.
  6. Check the sidebar, footer and blog for current ads. If none are running, mark paid ads as NA and note any empty ad space.
  7. Look for an advertising or media section and for emails shown as images, which AI tools cannot read. Copy those addresses manually.
  8. Enter the contact details, dates and notes in the sheet.

  [L0528.023]

### Expanding the List and Qualifying Partners

- Before outreach, open the partner's about or partners page and look for other companies they already work with. Add those linked companies to the list as new rows, since they are likely to be relevant to your niche too. [L0528.047]
- If a prospect gets annoyed when you respectfully disagree with a specific point in their content, treat that as a sign they may be a poor long-term partner. Creators who care about delivering value to their audience usually welcome honest, constructive feedback. *Older advice, 2021-22.* [L0453.021]

## Finding Influencers and Creators

### Choosing a Discovery Method

- Start with manual search on native platforms. It is the primary method because it lets you review each creator's engagement and the conversation around them directly on the platform. [L0505.012, L0505.013]
- Use third-party tools as a secondary option, mainly when time is short. [L0505.013]
- Once you find one suitable influencer, find more through the creators they collaborate with and the accounts the platform suggests on their profile. This builds a chain of relevant creators. [L0505.012, L0505.017]
- AI-generated lists are only one source. Also search Google, YouTube and Instagram for your niche terms and check which sites, channels and accounts already publish for your audience. This shows real web presence and quality that an AI list cannot reveal. [L0528.049]
- Start with your own network. Use your local contacts to reach creators in the target market and ask them to promote the product there. [L0549.042]

## Vetting Influencers and Spotting Fakes

### Choosing Influencer Tiers and Follower Ranges

#### Tier definitions

- Micro-influencers generally have 1 to 100,000 followers. On TikTok, where follower counts run higher, a large following is worth less per follower than on Instagram. [L0505.014]
- Macro-influencers have 100,000 to 1 million followers and offer a broader reach. [L0505.015]
- Celebrity influencers, such as Kim Kardashian, usually produce lower engagement but add strong reputational credibility to a brand. [L0505.016]
- The follower ranges for micro (1 to 100,000) and macro (100,000 to 1 million) influencers are a working convention, not an industry standard. Platform matters: the same follower count carries less value on TikTok than on Instagram. [L0505.020]
- Mid-level influencers were grouped at roughly 100,000 to 600,000 followers, and they usually expect payment rather than free product. [L0790.089]
- Older advice (Source A, 2020 to 2022 material, as labelled in the source): the ranges above differ from the creator's own grouping, so use them only as orientation. [L0790.089]

#### Tier comparison

| Tier | Followers (source values) | Typical behaviour | Source ids |
|---|---|---|---|
| Micro | 1 to 100,000 | Engaged audience, accessible, often free product | L0505.014, L0226.001 |
| Mid-level | ~100,000 to 600,000 | Usually expects payment | L0790.089 |
| Macro | 100,000 to 1 million | Broader reach | L0505.015 |
| Celebrity | No range given | Lower engagement, strong reputational credibility | L0505.016 |

#### Where the sweet spot lies

- Influencer sweet spot is 20,000 to 50,000 followers: they have an audience but are not yet demanding. [L0789.017]
- Between 50,000 and 80,000 followers influencers know their value and may need extra cash up front. [L0789.017]
- Above 100,000 followers deals usually require substantial cash flow and the influencer often has managers or agencies. [L0789.017]
- Target influencers with about 10K to 50K followers, since they usually charge less and often have loyal audiences. Verify their engagement rather than judging by follower count. [L1213.046]
- Newer creators who have just seen success often produce the most sales per dollar spent. Large celebrity deals were reported to give poor returns. [L1214.039]
- Instagram creators under about 30,000 followers gave good results, and they usually do not ask for payment. If they do ask, the fee is typically around 100 or less. [L0788.007]
- Best follower range in one tested mix: roughly 30,000 to 300,000, because these accounts have a more targeted and engaged audience. Above about 1 million followers, the audience becomes too broad and conversion drops. [L0790.048]

#### Micro-influencers versus larger publications

- Small micro-influencers with 1,000 to 2,000 followers rarely convert into customers for a new store. Affiliate partnerships are better with larger online publications, such as blogs, digital magazines and companies, that already reach people interested in the product. [L0471.024] *(Source A, 2024.)*
- Micro-influencers with niche authority and smaller but engaged audiences are more accessible and effective than celebrities. A creator with 1,000 followers in your niche who posts regularly can drive trust-based conversions better than a celebrity with no connection to the product. [L0226.001]
- These two statements conflict in the number they name (1,000 followers is effective in L0226.001 and rarely converts in L0471.024). Apply L0226.001 when the creator is in your niche and posts regularly; apply L0471.024 when judging a small account for affiliate sales for a new store. [L0226.001, L0471.024]

#### Tier mix from one tested campaign

- The influencer mix used was 20% micro-influencers, 50% mid-level and 30% high-level. Micro-influencers work for free product, mid-level usually expects payment, and high-level influencers are expensive. [L0790.045]
- Use high-level influencers only after testing and only with a budget that can absorb the risk. Keep the first posts with micro and mid-level creators until you know which ones convert. [L0790.065]
- High-level influencers with very large followings tend to have lower engagement and are less likely to convert. Their posts reach only a fraction of followers, and they are costly and often require a manager or agency. [L0790.049]

### Choosing by Audience Fit, Not Size

#### Demographic match first

- Choose influencers by whether their followers match your customer demographic, not by follower count, because a large following does not guarantee that the audience will buy from you. [L0479.006]
- Assuming that a large follower count means an influencer can sell effectively to your customers is a common error. [L0479.007]
- Partnering with an influencer whose followers match your buyer demographic lets you reach likely customers quickly. Matching demographics matter more than raw audience size. [L0480.002]
- An influencer's audience must match the brand's target customer. For example, a brand selling to middle-aged women should avoid a child toy reviewer whose audience is about 10 years old. [L0505.009]
- Influencers in different niches can have completely different audiences, so an influencer's demographic must be compared with your target customers before any collaboration. A large following in the wrong demographic is not a sales channel for your product. [L0480.027]
- Choosing the wrong influencers can do more harm than good to a brand, so selection matters more than volume. The right creators increase engagement, awareness and conversion rates. [L0505.002]
- An influencer whose audience matches your demographic can be worth working with even if you personally do not align with them. Decide in advance which content you are willing to repost. [L0790.054]
- Very niche creators, such as eyelash or gym-focused influencers, and YouTube creators can deliver loyal audiences at lower cost. The speaker reported about a 10x return on influencer spend when research was done carefully. [L1213.048]

#### Niche and style fit

- Do not assume an influencer successful with one brand will generate results for your product; influencer performance is inconsistent across different brands and products. [L0296.026]
- Study the influencer's content on every platform they use, not just one, and check that their style, tone, theme and brand message align with yours. Misaligned messaging is a reason to skip the collaboration, even when the audience is large. [L0782.002]
- Pet-focused accounts can engage well with their followers and promote products, but some animals or accounts over-promote many brands. Assess the account's posting history before working with one. [L0790.055]
- Read the influencer's past promotions to judge whether the style feels authentic and engaging before partnering. [L0789.005]

#### Audience demographics to check

- Review the audience breakdown by country, gender and age. [L0789.005]
- Compare the demographics (age, location, interests) and audience countries with your product before committing budget. [L0296.025]
- Compare the demographics and engagement rates on the platform before deciding. [L0480.013]
- Platform data allows a data-driven, informed choice of influencers. [L0480.013]

### Evaluating Engagement

#### Engagement rate benchmarks

- Assess influencer quality by evaluating followers count, engagement rate on posts, content relevance to your niche, posting frequency, and audience demographic alignment with your target customers. [L0226.004]
- Use engagement rate, not follower count, as the main selection metric, and require at least 5 percent. Engagement can be faked too, so verify it with a second analytics tool before reaching out. [L0789.002]
- Healthy influencer engagement is about 5% to 10% of followers as likes. Also check that the comments are positive and that followers actually interact with the posts. [L1213.044]
- A high engagement rate (likes, comments and shares relative to the audience) suggests a dedicated, responsive audience and is a positive signal when choosing influencers. Use this metric to judge audience activity, not follower count alone. [L0782.003]
- Judge a creator by engagement and by the conversation under their posts, not by follower count alone. A large following on TikTok, or a celebrity with millions of followers, often comes with lower engagement per follower. [L0505.019]
- Check each partner's engagement before reaching out and skip those with fake engagement or overpriced rates. Not every large audience is worth paying for. [L0528.008]

#### Recent engagement and views

- When hiring influencers or creators on Twitter or other platforms, check engagement in the last three months rather than only follower count. Creators with good recent engagement are a better bet than those with large but inactive audiences. [L0561.062]
- Follower count is a weak predictor of views because TikTok reach is highly volatile. A small account can out-view a much larger one, so judge creators by actual recent views rather than by followers. [L1379.002]
- An account with about 50,000 followers should typically generate at least about 3,000 views per video, so a sponsored post with a steady audience can reach a meaningful number of people. [L1379.037]
- Example: a channel with 100,000 subscribers should have a recent video with at least 20,000 to 30,000 views. If it only gets views from one or two viral videos, the channel is considered dead. [L0788.012]

#### Follower-to-engagement example from a real account

- A genuine creator with about 113,000 followers had likes that varied widely from roughly 6 to 1,500 per post, with specific and relevant comments. Uniform, inflated engagement with generic emoji comments is the warning sign. [L0790.093]

### Detecting Fake Followers and Comments

#### Principles

- Fake or bought followers are common and can make a profile look successful. Verify engagement carefully before sending product, because some fake profiles reach out to brands constantly asking for free product. [L0790.053]
- Follower count alone is not a quality signal, because about 30,000 followers can be bought cheaply on freelance marketplaces. Fake profiles built this way then pitch brands constantly for free product. [L0790.092]
- Many influencers buy fake likes and comments, so judging them by appearance or follower count is unreliable. Evaluate their audience engagement and comment quality instead. [L1213.047]
- Views and likes are easy to fake, so check the comments on an influencer's posts. Real, content-specific comments indicate genuine engagement. [L0671.099, L0671.236]

#### Comment and like check

Red flags:
- Generic emoji-only comments and irrelevant replies usually mean bots or bought followers. [L0790.052]
- Very consistent or inflated like counts can indicate fake engagement. [L0790.052]
- Uniform, inflated engagement with generic emoji comments is the warning sign. [L0790.093]

Green signals:
- Real, content-specific comments indicate genuine engagement. [L0671.099, L0671.236]
- Likes that vary from post to post and comments that are specific and relevant. [L0790.093]
- Comments are positive and followers actually interact with the posts. [L1213.044]

### Growth Curves and Follower Trends

- Check the follower growth curve over recent months. A steady decline in followers, especially after a public controversy, signals lost engagement. A small drop of around 5 percent can be accepted, while larger drops are a red flag. [L0789.004]

### Past Collaborations and Authenticity

#### Reviewing past brand work

- Review past brand collaborations and check that the content looks genuine. [L0782.001]
- Check past collaborations to see whether the influencer's content looks genuine: people should interact with it, and their earlier ads with other brands should show how well they work with brands like yours. This indicates whether their audience will respond to your product. [L0782.004]
- Count how many paid placements a brand has run with an influencer. Three or more previous placements by different brands signal the influencer is profitable, while a single one-off promotion is a warning sign. [L0789.003]
- Look at the posts they made for other brands and judge how well those posts performed. [L0790.052]
- Ask the brands they have worked with about how reliably they executed. [L0790.052]
- Check whether the audiences of the influencer and of similar brands overlap. [L0792.001]

#### Vetting order before outreach

Vet a potential influencer in this order before reaching out:

1. Study their content across all platforms and check their style, tone, theme and brand message for alignment with your brand. [L0792.001, L0782.001]
2. Evaluate engagement rate from likes, comments and shares; high engagement signals a dedicated, responsive audience. [L0792.001, L0782.001]
3. Review past collaborations and authenticity: look at their previous ads, how they worked with other brands, and whether their audience overlaps with the audiences of similar brands. [L0792.001]
4. Contact the influencer only after these checks are done. [L0782.001]

#### Loyalty and trust

- For mid-level creators, a loyal following matters more than raw reach: check whether followers trust the creator and want to be like them. That trust is what turns viewers into buyers. [L0790.097]

### Full Vetting Checklist

Use this checklist in order before offering product or budget. [L0790.052]

1. Confirm the follower count is in the range that fits your budget, for example 30,000 to 300,000 for micro-influencers. [L0790.052]
2. Check the post count and compare it to the follower count; a very low number of posts for a large following is a warning sign. [L0790.052]
3. Read the comments: generic emoji-only comments and irrelevant replies usually mean bots or bought followers. [L0790.052]
4. Check whether likes look organic, because very consistent or inflated like counts can indicate fake engagement. [L0790.052]
5. Look at the posts they made for other brands and judge how well those posts performed. [L0790.052]
6. Ask the brands they have worked with about how reliably they executed. [L0790.052]
7. Confirm that their audience matches your target demographic before offering product. [L0790.052]

### Requesting Analytics and Media Kits

#### Ask before you commit

## Outreach Messages and Sequences

### Channel: DM or Email

- Send outreach as a direct message where possible, since DMs are faster, get higher response rates, and reach the creator directly instead of a manager who may slow the reply. Use email only when DMs are not possible. [L0782.005, L0792.002]
- Use email as the first contact when the creator lists an email address, and follow up with a DM. Instagram DMs are often buried and rarely read. [L1379.005, L1379.007]
- Use email for influencer outreach because it looks more professional. A DM can work only when the sender has a verified account with a large follower base; otherwise lean toward email. [L0783.002]

### Vetting Before Outreach

1. Build a list of influencers that fit the criteria: one country or language, one niche, follower range, minimum 5 percent engagement rate, and no own product yet. [L0789.015]

### Personalisation

- Tailor every outreach message to the individual creator. Reference one specific piece of their content they did well. [L0782.007]
- Never send the same copy-pasted message to many influencers. Each outreach should pick out something specific the creator did well and explain why that content fits your brand and campaign. [L0782.007, L0792.004]
- Address each creator by name and mention a specific aspect of their work that matches your brand, because creators can immediately tell a personal message from a mass one. [L0783.003]
- Mass emails with unfilled merge fields in the greeting, such as a placeholder where the creator's name or handle should be, are recognised at once as copy-paste outreach and are sent straight to spam by creators. [L0783.004]
- Each outreach message should name a specific reason the creator fits, such as their style or feed, instead of generic praise. This makes the request look personal and gives the creator a reason to respond. [L0791.003]
- Clearly state how the collaboration fits your brand and also how it helps the influencer reach their own goals. This alignment is a key reason creators accept. [L0792.006]
- A handwritten note from a founder or CEO that refers to a specific recent video of the creator makes a PR package far more persuasive and increases the creator's willingness to work with the brand. ## Gifting and Seeding Campaigns

### Gifting campaign phases and funnel benchmarks

1. Gift products to a broad list of creators and record their details in a spreadsheet (Google Sheets), starting with 30 to 50 creators. [L0915.016]
2. Expect attrition. From 50 outreach DMs, roughly 30 reply, 20 want the product, and 10 post on Instagram. [L0915.016, L0915.017]
3. Track which creators engage with the brand on their own. These smaller creators are the ones to move into paid partnerships. [L0915.016]

- Funnel benchmark for creator outreach: of 50 DMs sent, about 30 reply, about 20 want the product, and about 10 post on Instagram. [L0915.017]
- Plan for roughly a 60 percent loss from reply to posting, so start with more creators than you need. [L0915.017]
- Arithmetic from the same benchmark: reply to post is a loss of about 67 percent (30 to 10), product request to post about 50 percent (20 to 10), and DM to post about 80 percent (50 to 10). The stated 60 percent does not match the 30-to-10 figure exactly, so keep both and use the stage figures to see where creators drop out. [L0915.017]

Numbered campaign procedure:

1. Gift the product to a broad list of 30 to 50 creators and record each creator's details in a Google Sheets list. [L0915.022]
2. Send outreach DMs and track replies. From 50 DMs, expect roughly 30 replies, 20 who want the product, and 10 who post on Instagram. [L0915.022]
3. Watch which creators engage with the brand on their own, without prompting. These are the genuinely interested ones. [L0915.022]
4. Invite the genuinely engaged, usually smaller, creators into paid partnerships. [L0915.022]
5. For a challenge or campaign, choose the paid creators from the pool of people already gifted and engaged with the brand. [L0915.022]
6. Secure usage rights before running any creator content through paid media such as Facebook ads. [L0915.022]

### Gifting outreach messages and offers

- Common mistake: a custom cushion brand sent a short email offering a free custom-designed cushion in exchange for showing it on Instagram. The email had only a website link and gave no details about what the brand wanted, the product options, or the deliverables. Creators receive many such emails every day and will rarely do gifted campaigns for an unknown brand, so generic gifting outreach like this is ignored. [L0783.010]
- Template for a gifting offer:

```
Hi [CREATOR NAME],

I saw your [SPECIFIC SERIES OR VIDEO] and loved [SPECIFIC DETAIL]. My name is [YOUR NAME] and I work on creator partnerships at [BRAND].

We would like to send you [GIFT PRODUCT] for your next [PROJECT]. In return, we are looking for one Instagram story and one TikTok post showing how you use it. Please let me know whether you are interested, and feel free to ask any questions before deciding.

Best regards,
[YOUR NAME]
```
[L0783.013]

- Formula for a gifting offer: the creator's name, a specific reference to their work, the gift and what it will be used for in their project, and the exact return asked (for example one Instagram story and one TikTok post). Stating the gift and the expectation together lets the creator judge the offer in one reading. [L0783.014]
  - Example 1: Hi Lena, your upcycling series on small bathrooms is great, and we would like to send you an oak wall panel for your next project. In return we would love one Instagram story and one TikTok post. [L0783.014]
  - Example 2: Hi Sam, your cookware reviews are exactly the style of our customers, so we would like to send you our new cast-iron pan. We are looking for one Instagram reel and one story in return. [L0783.014]
- Gifting procedure for influencer outreach:
  1. Find the creator's PO box address in their Instagram bio or YouTube description. [L1214.038]
  2. Send a product personalized for them, such as a version with their name or a cutout of their photo. [L1214.038]
  3. Include a short note saying the gift is from you, with your social media handle. [L1214.038]
  4. Do not ask for promotion. Smaller creators often promote the gift for free as a goodwill gesture. [L1214.038]

### Seeding relationships before any request (older advice, Source B 2021-22)

### Selecting creators for paid work

- Gifting cannot guarantee coverage, so use it to find genuinely interested creators. Those who engage organically are the best candidates for paid collaboration. [L0915.018]
- For a challenge or campaign, choose creators from the pool of people already gifted and engaged with the brand. They are more authentic and need less briefing on brand image and voice. [L0915.019]
- Do not pick creators only because they are viral at the moment. Choose creators who genuinely invest in the brand. [L0915.021]
- Content produced by campaign creators can be pushed through paid media such as Facebook ads, but only if you have the usage rights to that content. [L0915.020, L0915.022]

### Selling at cost instead of free product

- If free products are not feasible, offer an influencer the product at cost in exchange for shout-outs and links. For example, a $1,000 paddle board can be offered at $500. This is older advice from Source A (2021). [L0671.100, L0671.237]
- Influencer marketing is rarely done with free products. Instead, sell the product to influencers at cost, so you lose no money and still get exposure. This is older advice from Source A (2020). ### Shipping and packaging

- Influencer marketing works best when the product ships quickly, since slow shipping damages the influencer's reputation. [L1184.047]
- A custom box can be designed for the product by a freelancer on Fiverr for about $5 to $10, then quoted by a supplier at about $0.50 per box for around 100 units, which costs about $50. Creators showing the custom box in their videos build a stronger brand impression than plain shipping. [L1382.057]

### Launch seeding with multiple creators and markets

- Launch sequence with untapped-market creators:
  1. Pick three or four influencers in an untapped market, such as certain European countries, Australia or New Zealand. [L1213.039]
  2. Send each influencer a brief specifying the posting day, time and the points to mention. [L1213.039]
  3. First video: the influencer uses the product without introducing it, which creates a rumor and vibe around it. [L1213.039]
  4. Second video: announce the launch date and offer a discount, such as 20%, to people who sign up to the email list before launch. [L1213.039]

## Pricing, Budgeting and Negotiation

### Fee drivers

#### Audience size and engagement

- Follower count alone does not set an influencer's fee. A larger audience usually commands a higher price, but that link is not reliable, so audience size must be weighed against the other factors. [L0785.001, L0793.001]
- Engagement rate often matters more than follower count. Example: an account with about 500,000 followers and a 20% engagement rate can ask for more than an account with 1 million followers and a 5-6% engagement rate, because its audience is more active and more likely to buy. Engagement rate is a strong signal of what a creator can charge. [L0785.002, L0793.002]
- Price the whole package, not audience size alone. Niche fit, content quality, engagement rate and the campaign's brand objective together determine what is fair and justified. [L0785.005, L0793.005]
- For UGC that the creator posts on their own account, follower count and engagement rate significantly raise the price. The engagement rate on the creator's Instagram or TikTok account should be above 4-5%. If the brand only uses the video itself, follower count and engagement matter little. [L1402.006]

#### Niche and expertise

- Creators who serve a narrow niche or are true specialists charge more, because few creators cover that niche and the market is less saturated. Confirm that the creator's niche and expertise level match what you are budgeting for. [L0785.003, L0793.003]
- Influencers who are one of only a few covering a specific task usually charge more. True specialists in their domain charge even higher rates. [L0793.003]

#### Content quality and track record

- Content quality can justify a higher fee. Relevant factors are production value, brand alignment, how long the creator has worked as an influencer, how much content they have produced, and how well they produce it. [L0785.004, L0793.004]
- A creator with a proven track record, high engagement and a recognised name in the industry, who works in content full time, will usually ask for a higher fee. Leaders in their niche, such as a well-known tech creator, can command a premium because their expertise adds to the collaboration's success. [L0785.010, L0793.006]
- A fair fee is a judgment across audience size, engagement, niche expertise, content quality and the brand's goal. Weigh all of these before deciding what is justified. [L0785.005, L0793.005]

#### Usage rights

- Define the usage term clearly: state the exclusivity and the usage rights you need for the content. Exclusive use, or the right to repurpose content across channels and ads, raises the fee. [L0793.006]
- Price exclusive usage rights, and the right to reuse content in paid ads or across several channels, separately. Charge a higher fee than for organic use on one channel. Write the granted rights into the contract. [L0793.007]
- Document in writing which rights are granted to whom. [L0793.006]
- Royalty alternative: a content creator who makes ad material for a brand can be paid about 3% of the revenue the creative generates in paid ads, instead of a flat fee. This works only when the brand can attribute ad revenue to that content. [L0789.055]

#### Deliverables and campaign complexity

- Define deliverables in detail: the exact scope of posts, stories, costs and creative work the creator must deliver. Each additional element raises the compensation. [L0785.009, L0793.008]
- Assess campaign complexity when pricing. Low-effort campaigns leave room to negotiate a lower fee. Campaigns that need photographers, videographers or specific locations justify higher compensation. [L0785.008, L0793.006]
- Price UGC by use case. Videos for paid ads need higher production quality and justify a higher budget. Organic TikTok content can use lower production quality if it is engaging and attention-grabbing. [L1402.005]
- The platform or company used to source the creator is a major price factor, alongside the use case and the creator's reach. [L1402.007]

#### Pricing procedure

1. Define the usage term clearly: state the exclusivity and the usage rights you need for the content. Exclusive use or the right to repurpose content across channels and ads will raise the fee. [L0793.006]
2. Negotiate the terms openly with the influencer so both sides are satisfied, and document in writing which rights are granted to whom. [L0793.006]
3. Assess the campaign's complexity: low-effort work leaves room to negotiate the price, while high effort such as photographers, videographers or specific locations requires higher compensation. [L0793.006]
4. Define the deliverables precisely (posts, stories, creative work). Each additional element should increase the compensation. [L0793.006]
5. Evaluate the track record: well-known leaders in their niche who make their living from this work charge more, because their expertise contributes to the success of the collaboration. [L0793.006]
6. Negotiate the final compensation with all of these points factored in, and check that the price fits your budget. [L0793.006]

### Market benchmarks

- Influencers charge anywhere from about $50 to about $1 million per collaboration. Before reaching out, determine where your budget sits within that range. [L0793.009]
- Know the market benchmarks for audience size, engagement rate, content quality and your niche before negotiating. Without them you risk overpaying or underpaying. [L0793.012]
- Adjust the market rate for campaign-specific factors such as the influencer's expertise, the complexity of the work and any exclusive rights. Reflect these adjustments in the contract. [L0793.013]

### Budget sizing

#### Budget procedure

1. Assess the overall marketing budget and set a reasonable portion for influencer marketing without compromising the strategy or the business. [L0793.018]
2. Prioritise influencers within that budget. If an influencer is out of reach, choose another who fits both budget and goals. [L0793.018]
3. Research industry standards for audience size, engagement rate, content quality and your niche so you know the usual rates. [L0793.018]
4. Adjust the market rate for expertise, campaign complexity and exclusivity, and reflect this in the contract. [L0793.018]
5. Communicate openly about the budget and any constraints during negotiation. [L0793.018]
6. Show flexibility through long-term collaboration, mutual promotion or affiliate options. [L0793.018]
7. Emphasise the mutual benefit, especially the unique value your brand brings to the influencer's audience. [L0793.018]
8. Discuss a long-term partnership, in which the rate can be lowered for an extended period. [L0793.018]

#### Sizing rules

- The influencer budget should be only a reasonable portion of the total marketing budget. Size it so that it does not compromise the overall marketing strategy or the business. [L0793.010]
- Never choose an influencer whose price is outside your budget, even if you strongly want them. Look for an influencer who fits both your budget and your campaign goals. [L0793.011]
- Test with smaller creators before committing large budgets. A high fee for a large influencer can return far less than expected: one reported $15,000 post returned about $2,000 in sales. [L1214.040]

### Flat versus performance pay

- Flat fee: pays a fixed amount per deliverable. Use it when the deliverables and usage rights are fixed in advance and the brand cannot attribute sales to the content. [L0793.006, L0793.007]
- Royalty: about 3% of the revenue the creative generates in paid ads. Use it only when the brand can attribute ad revenue to that content. [L0789.055]
- Small fixed payment plus affiliate commission: works when the budget is limited. The creator earns a base amount for the post and more for each sale, which aligns their incentive with yours. [L1379.054]
- Affiliate commission for micro-influencers: the offered range is 6-7%. [L0226.005]
- Worked example: a kayak seller offers a local kayaker with a blog or Instagram account 3-5 free kayaks plus a 6% affiliate commission per referral. The creator profits from promoting to their niche audience without large upfront costs. [L0226.006]
- Flat rate for all creators: if a brand pays one flat rate to all creators, for example $500 per creator, state in the brief or negotiation that there is no room to negotiate. [L0579.025]

### Story versus feed pricing

- Story placements are usually much cheaper than feed posts for the same creator. For accounts with roughly 30,000-50,000 followers, a story post is often the better buy for predictable reach. [L1379.003]
- Story posts give predictable reach near the creator's average story views. A feed or TikTok post can go viral but depends on video quality. [L1379.034]
- Choose story posts when consistency matters. Choose feed posts when you want high variance. You can also negotiate both. [L1379.034]

### Seasonal rates

- Creators charge more during the fourth quarter and around holidays. Negotiate and book posts before the season. [L1379.036]

### Negotiation tactics

#### Opening and concessions

- Start with a low offer and increase only if the creator pushes back. Creators who work with you once often accept lower rates the next time. [L1379.012]
- Open communication about your budget and any constraints often leads influencers to come down in price, especially when they want to work with the brand. [L0793.014]
- Negotiate the terms openly so that both sides are satisfied. [L0793.006]
- Ask for the creator's rate directly. For a paid Instagram post, contact the account by direct message and ask for the creator's rate for a post that shouts out your store. [L0528.014]
- For industry event sponsorships, ask for the costs first, because some are expensive and others are affordable. [L0528.014]

#### Non-cash and incentive offers

- Non-cash offers such as long-term collaboration, mutual promotion or affiliate marketing show flexibility and can bridge a gap in the fee. [L0793.015]
- Micro-influencers can be offered a combination of incentives:
  - free products;
  - affiliate commissions of 6-7%;
  - exclusive coupon codes for their followers;
  - free content creation help (guides or product reviews);
  - interviews featuring their expertise. [L0226.005]

#### Mutual value and partnership

- If your brand gives the influencer's audience unique value, the influencer may be more willing to work with you and adjust their rates. Emphasise this mutual benefit in the negotiation. [L0793.016]
- Long-term partnerships lower the cost per collaboration. Influencers value regular income, and the brand gains a reliable partner who keeps working with them over time. [L0793.017]

## Briefs, Contracts and Usage Rights

### Why the Brief Comes First

- An influencer brief should tell a creator the brand voice, the type of video wanted and the goal of the content from the first contact. This matters most with creators the brand has not worked with before, who need more guidance. [L0579.001]
- A precise brief at the start reduces the number of edit rounds and revision loops later. The more the creator understands before filming, the fewer changes are needed. [L0579.002]
- Sending complete information before filming is the most reliable way to avoid wasted time, keep the creator aligned with the brand and get a video that matches what the brand wants. [L0579.027]
- A brief must clarify expectations: the key message, the target audience and the brand tone, for example family friendly with no swearing, luxury and polished, or playful with bright colours. Doing this upfront prevents videos that clash with the brand. [L0579.004]
- The fee must always appear in the brief. It is normally agreed before the brief is sent, which happens once the collaboration and the rate have been discussed. [L0579.024]

### Brief Structure

Build an effective influencer brief in this order: [L0579.008]

1. **Campaign overview:** campaign name, goal, purpose, key dates and deadlines for deliverables. [L0579.008, L0579.009]
2. **Brand background:** tone of voice, target audience, demographics and interests that inform the video. [L0579.008, L0579.010]
3. **Key messaging:** the core message, any call to action, and the specific points the creator must mention. [L0579.008, L0579.011]
4. **Content guidelines:** format ratio, whether on-screen text is allowed, required video length, and rules such as no swearing. [L0579.008, L0579.012]
5. **Content freedom parameters:** a springboard of video types and options, example content, and room for the creator to brainstorm and add their own ideas. [L0579.008, L0579.013]

Section-level rules:

- The campaign overview states the campaign name, the goal, the purpose, and the key dates and deadlines for each deliverable. The creator needs this from the first message. [L0579.009]
- The brand background section defines the tone of voice, the target audience, and the audience's demographics and interests, so the creator can film for the right viewers. [L0579.010]
- Key messaging names the core message and any call to action, and lists specific points the creator must mention, such as a product's number of blades or settings. [L0579.011]
- Content guidelines set concrete rules: the format ratio, whether on-screen text is allowed, the video length (for example 30 seconds), and language limits (for example no swearing). Be as specific as the brand needs. [L0579.012]

### Creative Freedom and the Springboard

- Set firm rules where the brand needs them, but leave the creator room to keep their own voice, since that voice is why their audience follows them. Give tighter guidance at the start and loosen it once the creator understands the brand. [L0579.003]
- Give the creator a springboard instead of a shot-by-shot script: offer one video type or a choice of types (for example day in my life, get ready with me or tutorial), and include example videos from other creators whose look fits the brand. [L0579.005]

## Running and Optimizing Influencer Campaigns

### Campaign Setup and Objectives

**Campaign procedure.** The two Source C versions of this procedure are merged here. Each step lists the ids it draws on.

1. Define one specific objective for the campaign: a new product launch, an upcoming sale, brand awareness, or a new product promotion. [L0787.002, L0868.001]
2. Tell the influencer the objective so both sides understand the goal. [L0787.002, L0868.001]
3. Request a draft of the influencer's content before anything is published. [L0787.002, L0868.001]
4. Review the draft and confirm it matches the campaign objective before publication. [L0787.002, L0868.001]
5. Create the content together with the influencer, as a joint post published alongside their own posts. Agree in advance on what will be posted and which results it should generate, and confirm both sides are aligned on the message and intended result before the campaign goes live. 6. Publish across your own social media and the influencer's social media at the same time. [L0787.002, L0868.001]
7. Analyze the results, document what worked, and use the insights to choose future influencer collaborations that resonate with your audience. [L0787.002, L0868.001]

**One clear objective per campaign.** Every influencer campaign needs one clear, specific objective before it starts. Typical objectives are a new product launch, an upcoming sale, or promoting new products. Without a defined objective, the influencer's content has no direction and the campaign cannot be measured properly. [L0868.002]

**State the objective explicitly.** The objective must be communicated to the influencer so they understand what the brand is trying to achieve and which product or sale is involved. The influencer should know the specific objective before content is created. [L0868.003]

**Review every draft against the objective.** Never let influencer content go live without first reviewing a draft. Check the draft against the campaign objective, not only for general quality, and confirm its message serves the chosen goal (brand awareness, sales, or a specific product) before it goes live. [L0787.003, L0868.004, L0868.005]

**Co-create the post.** Creating the post together with the influencer, and agreeing on the message and intended result before launch, keeps the content aligned with the campaign goal. This shared understanding reduces the risk of off-message posts. A joint post also gives the brand control over the campaign message. [L0787.004, L0868.006]

**Respond quickly during the campaign.** Answer influencers quickly and handle their questions and scheduling issues in time. Slow or unavailable brand contacts make the campaign feel badly run to the creator, which can hurt both the result and the working relationship. [L0782.016]

### Coordinated Posting and Timing

**Post on both channels at once.** Publishing the same campaign simultaneously on your own channels and the influencer's channels maximizes reach and impact. Coordinated posting across both audiences works better than posting on only one side, and running both channels together is more successful than either alone. [L0787.005, L0868.007]

**Protect the measurement day of a major post.** For a major influencer post:
- Do not run other promotions on the same day.
- Do not let other influencers post on that date.
- Ask the influencer to post within their own two-hour window so traffic can be measured clearly. [L0790.057]

**Approve the first post with a new influencer.**
- Approve the photo and caption before publication.
- Require your company name in the first eight words of the caption.
- Approve all photos and captions for high-priced products. [L0790.058]

**Seasonal window.** The period between Black Friday and Valentine's Day is a strong season for influencer marketing. The influencer receives a product, a script and a coupon code to drive sales. [L1194.051]

**Scale with micro-influencers in bulk.** The goal was about 1,000 micro-influencers available to post at any time. Micro-influencers work best in bulk because each one reaches a small, targeted audience. [L0790.046]

### Promo Scheduling and Story Frameworks

**Five-story Instagram promotion framework.** Each campaign runs over five stories, in this order:
1. Introduce the product, its benefits and the problem it solves, framed as a personal story, such as answering questions about where the product came from. [L0789.041]
2. Demonstrate the product in use, such as showing how to apply a cream or use a hair tool. [L0789.041]
3. Share a coupon code for a limited number of buyers, such as 50 discount codes, plus any free-shipping offer, and mention scarcity. [L0789.041]
4. Ask the influencer to show how to apply the coupon code on the website, by screen recording or on a smartphone. [L0789.041]
5. Repeat the coupon code and remind followers of the deadline. [L0789.041]

Timing of the follow-ups:
- 24 hours later: post a story saying the coupon has sold out and promise another batch later. [L0789.041]
- 24 hours after that: post a story announcing an extra batch of 30 codes, a last-chance message, and extra value such as a bonus. [L0789.041]

**Story script template.** Adapt the wording to the product.

```
Story 1: 'Hey guys, I got a lot of questions about this. People kept asking where I got this. I got it from this brand. It does [benefit] and it helped me with [problem]. This is the product, here is what it does and which issues it solves.'
Story 2: The influencer demonstrates the product in use, for example applying a cream or using the hair tool, on camera.
Story 3: 'Hey, a lot of you have been asking, and I have good news. I reached out to the brand and got a discount code for the next 50 buyers. You get 50 percent off with my code, plus free shipping on orders above [amount]. Enjoy.'
Story 4: Screen recording or smartphone video showing how to go to the website and enter the coupon code.
Story 5: 'Just a reminder, here is the coupon code again.'
Follow-up after 24 hours: 'A lot of you have asked for this coupon. Sorry, it has expired. We will have to wait for the next batch, but I will keep you posted if I can get another one.'
Another follow-up after 24 hours: 'Good news, I got 30 extra codes and I cannot get any more. If you missed it last time, this is your last chance, plus extra value from the brand.'
```
[L0789.042]

**Serial progress videos.** A creator posting a daily check-in on a product's results brings viewers back to follow the next update. This format works best for products where results can be shown over several days. [L0784.026]

### Partnership Ads, Spark Ads and Whitelisting

**Spark Ads boost content designed to convert.** A Spark Ad can give an early boost to a video designed to convert, and organic reach may then take over. Videos can be posted organically first and promoted later, or created from the start for promotion. [L0352.047]

**Spark Ads procedure (TikTok).**
1. Have an influencer create and post content featuring your product on their own TikTok page. [L1374.026]
2. Ask the influencer to whitelist that specific post. This requires only the authorization code for that one post, not their account login. [L1374.026]
3. Run the influencer's post as a Spark Ad from your ad account. [L1374.026]
4. Keep your own organic posts separate from the Spark Ad to avoid damaging their organic reach. [L1374.026]

**Do not Spark-boost your own winning organic posts.** Spark Ads run on the same brand page can kill organic reach. Run Spark Ads from an influencer's post or from a separate page, not from your own successful organic posts. [L1374.027]

**Spark Ads are TikTok-only.** Spark Ads can only run on TikTok placements. If a campaign also uses placements such as Pangle or Audience Network, Spark Ads require a separate campaign limited to TikTok. [L1374.028]

**Partnership ads.** Partnership ads run a collaborator's post as a paid ad. They need the other account's permission before they are set up, and the ad runs through the partner's post under that account's identity. [L0865.036]

**Your own character page versus creator whitelisting.** Running ads from your own character page avoids paying a creator revenue share and removes dependence on a page owner who can withdraw or renegotiate. [L1475.004]

**Whitelist pages for video ads.** Whitelist pages also work for video ads, especially UGC. A video that looks like a real person testing the product feels native and gets watched rather than scrolled past. [L1476.027]

**Matching faces.**
- When a UGC video shows a face, pair it with a whitelist character of similar look, such as the same gender and similar hair.
- With several UGC videos featuring different people, use different whitelist pages and match each page to its UGC face.
- A similar look is acceptable. A copy of a real creator's face is not. [L1476.028]

### Handling Audience Saturation

**Move to paid and UGC when an audience is saturated.** Influencer partnerships depend on the audience. When one influencer's audience is saturated, shift to paid ads and UGC to reach new people. [L0789.050]

### Repurposing Creator Content

**Why repurpose.** Reusing creator content reduces production cost, since the video already exists. It also increases reach and authenticity, because the audience sees different people with different experiences instead of the same brand voice every time. [L0784.008]

**Organic videos from unpaid creators.** When a product becomes a trend, creators often post organic videos about it without compensation. If people spontaneously talk about the product, the brand can ask to reuse those videos, as long as the rights are agreed in writing first. [L0784.009]

**Case: a viral candida cleanse product on TikTok.** The brand that drove the movement worked with several creators, each taking a different angle. The lesson does not give the brand's figures or say which creators were paid, but many creators posted without compensation. Five example videos:
1. A creator tried the viral cleanse and posted a daily check-in, with day 4 as one update. [L0784.010]
2. A creator made a comedic video with a funny trending audio about sugar cravings going away. [L0784.010]
2b. The second creator's video was trend-based with no voiceover, using a trending audio. [L0784.010]
3. A creator unboxed the product and walked through her expectations. [L0784.010]
4. Another daily check-in on day 4, which viewers returned to follow. [L0784.010]
5. An instructional video about what to eat and which practices to follow. [L0784.010]

Lesson: when customers organically join a conversation, the brand can repurpose those videos on its own social channels after getting rights. [L0784.010]

**Choose candidates by engagement.** Check engagement metrics, both among followers and among non-followers, to see which content performs best with the broader audience. [L0784.011]

**Prefer evergreen content.** Choose video that still makes sense when shown years later. Avoid renewing rights to trend-based videos, because they lose relevance once the trend ends. [L0784.012]

**Check brand fit before reuse.** Confirm each creator video matches the brand's values and aesthetic before reusing it. The question is whether the video represents how the brand wants to be seen. [L0784.013]

**Re-publish on other platforms.** A UGC video can be reused on another platform by changing the title, description or thumbnail, since these affect click-through rate. The video itself can stay the same. [L1214.052]

### Long-Term Partnerships and Scaling

**One creator over many one-offs.** A long-term partnership with one creator usually delivers more value than many short one-off collaborations with different creators. The relationship produces more connected and authentic content. [L0783.001]

**Ambassador deals and the fade-out.** Long-term ambassador deals can keep producing sales while a creator stays popular, because their audience keeps trying to copy them. Expect the partnership to lose value when the creator's personal brand fades, and plan the budget for that window. [L1214.059]

#### Finding Content Creators via Google

**Principle**

- Affiliate content creators produce material for the information-gathering stage of the buyer journey (reviews, how-to guides, top-10 lists) rather than commercial-intent keywords, because substantial content performs better with research-phase audiences. [L0215.004]

**Search procedure**

1. Search Google using research-stage keywords: "best [product type]" and "top 10 [product type]" to locate content creators reviewing or comparing products in your category. [L0215.005]
2. Examine the results for review and comparison guide websites. [L0215.005]
3. Click through to these sites to identify which products they recommend and whether they use affiliate links. [L0215.005]

**Mapping the full landscape**

- Test multiple search term variations on Google, such as "best [product]", "top 10 [product]" and related modifiers, to identify all affiliate review and comparison sites active in your product category and geographic market. This comprehensive mapping reveals the full landscape of potential affiliate partners. [L0215.013]
- To map the landscape, search with these variations:
  1. "best [product type]"
  2. "top 10 [product type]"
  3. "[product type] reviews"
  4. "[product type] comparison"
  
  For price-sensitive categories, also search "best [product type] under $[price]" (e.g., "best headphones under $200"). Review the first 2-3 pages of results for each search to identify all major affiliate publishers in your niche. [L0215.020]

**Evaluating candidates**

- Look for reviews comparing multiple products (typically 7 or more), with detailed feature breakdowns, score ratings, and pros/cons for each product. High-effort, comprehensive content indicates a serious publisher worth approaching; thin comparison pages are lower priority. [L0215.014]
- Major media publications (e.g., New York Times, PC Magazine) frequently rank in "best [product]" searches but typically do not use visible affiliate offers due to editorial policies. Focus outreach on independent review sites and content creators where you can confirm active affiliate links, rather than spending effort on large publishers. [L0215.015]

**Confirming an active affiliate link**

1. Hover your mouse over the "See Price" button or product link and read the URL shown in the bottom-left corner of the browser. [L0215.016]
2. If the URL is unclear, right-click the link and select "Inspect" or "Inspect Element". [L0215.016]
3. In the HTML code, locate the `href=` attribute containing the full destination URL. [L0215.016]
4. Look for an affiliate tag parameter in the URL (e.g., "tag=rtings" for Amazon or a similar pattern for other platforms). [L0215.016]

### What UGC Is and Why It Matters

- UGC stands for user-generated content: videos filmed by a real user or creator on a phone, with no professional studio. Such content is often more trusted than polished brand production, so it is a common format for high-performing video ads. [L0707.028]
- Polished ad-style videos made for TikTok ads got almost no views in the referenced case. Reading the feedback and the zero-view results showed the format was wrong. Switching fully to a UGC style fixed the problem. [L0821.018]

### When to Invest in UGC

- Do not spend £120 or more on UGC creators until cheaper ad tests have shown sales. Use image ads or basic videos first. Pay for expensive UGC, such as Billow-style professional creators, only after product-audience fit is proven. [L0308.024]
- Outsource full scripted creative production only for products already proven as winners, or for brands with inventory and data. Do not invest in this production for pure dropshipping tests, because clips and footage are easy to source for those. [L0638.040]

### Raw scene clips

- Ask creators for raw 3-5 second scene clips, not a finished 60-second video. Each scene is quick to record, which makes the work cheap for creators and gives you more material to assemble. [L0398.046]

Creative brief math:

- One 60-second brief is split into 12 scenes of about 5 seconds each: 3 hook scenes, 3 product-introduction scenes, 3 benefit scenes and 3 CTA scenes. [L0398.047]
- Choosing one of 3 hooks, one of 3 intros, one of 3 benefit clips and one of 3 CTAs gives 3 x 3 x 3 x 3 = 81 combinations. [L0398.047]
- About 20 high-quality videos can be picked from these combinations, so a $60 creator fee works out to about $3 per video. [L0398.047]

Raw clip request template:

```text
Subject: Raw clips for [PRODUCT NAME]

Hi [CREATOR NAME],

We would like to work with you on [NUMBER] short scene videos for [PRODUCT NAME]. Please do not send a finished 60-second video. Instead, record separate raw clips of about 3-5 seconds each, one scene per clip, with no editing and no music.

Please record these scenes:
- 3 hook scenes: [HOOK ANGLE 1], [HOOK ANGLE 2], [HOOK ANGLE 3]. Keep the first moment strong and simple.
- 3 product-introduction scenes: the product in your hand, an unboxing or first use of [PRODUCT NAME].
- 3 benefit scenes: show [BENEFIT 1], [BENEFIT 2] and [BENEFIT 3] in use. Speak or show the benefit clearly.
- 3 call-to-action scenes: you holding the product and saying [CTA LINE, e.g. shop now or check the link].

For each scene, use a plain background and keep the phone steady. Send all clips separately so we can combine them ourselves.

Thank you,
[YOUR NAME]
```

## Producing Your Own UGC and AI Content

### Planning and Principles

- Own UGC is the starting point when no budget exists. Film it yourself first, then move to paid creators once the product shows consistent profit. [L0308.028, L1219.049, L1030.024]
- Phone footage is usually good enough when testing a product early. Shoot the product from several angles: held up, a slow slide across a surface, and slow zooms, so the editor has enough material for the narrative. [L0344.049]
- Customisation costs time and money because each custom UGC video needs creator credits or production. Spend on it only after the original product shows consistent profit. [L1030.024]
- Custom content performs better than existing content on TikTok, so prioritise your own videos over reused viral clips. [L1377.024]
- Custom content recorded by the owner on a phone, with simple scripts and no studio, performs better than stock drop-shipping footage, because viewers relate to a normal person explaining the benefit. [L0871.053]
- Authentic creator footage filmed on a phone outperforms polished company production, because viewers trust a regular person more than a brand. [L0707.012]
- Strong video ads combine three things: showing how the product works, using UGC as social proof, and a first 5 seconds that grab attention. The opening sets the base for the rest of the video. [L0777.011]

- If there is no budget for paid UGC, the creator can film the product personally, add a voice-over and short text overlays. Reusing other people's content is possible but not advised. [L0561.037]
- Warehouse or packaging videos with your own logo add a layer of trust for customers. A freelancer can create this kind of corporate video. [L0950.047]
- Customer or staff-filmed product videos can serve as user-generated content. Film real use, explain the product's feel and fit, and post them on the store and on YouTube. [L0801.130]
- Point-of-view (POV) style content works well on Instagram for product marketing. Learn this format before running paid creator campaigns. [L1367.013]
- Clients often find it useful to be shown a real video of a procedure from the business itself, such as a dentist's own footage of a treatment, because it adds credibility to ads. [L0440.048]

### Scripting and Shot Structure

- Ask for a UGC piece about the product built on the problem-agitate-solution framework, then refine the output through follow-up prompts before passing it to creators or clients. [L0353.045]
- Create short, exaggerated reaction clips of the presenter using the product in different settings, such as at a desk, in the bath, or at a friend's place. Use the funniest or most surprising clip as the ad hook and keep the others as alternative openings for testing. [L1403.037]
- The answer to "how would you describe the brand to a friend" contains the sales language buyers use without prompting and the order in which they rank benefits. Use these exact phrases and benefit order in UGC scripts, testimonial copy and word-of-mouth ads. [L1139.012]
- Hooks and benefit scenes: the creator performs an action without speaking on camera, and the voiceover is added in editing. Speaking on camera is needed only for scenes such as a testimonial or a call to action. [L0638.039]

- Speaker profile: the speaker should sound like someone who was skeptical before and only changed their mind after trying the product. [L0707.012]
- Naming a personal insecurity: testimonials that describe a personal insecurity remind the viewer of their own insecurity and how it makes them feel. Selling works by solving a problem or insecurity the buyer already feels, so the speaker should name it openly. [L0707.013]

### Testimonials, Personas and Storytelling Patterns

#### Testimonial Sources and Credibility

- A named, credible person speaking for the product, such as a respected user or expert, carries more weight than an anonymous company voice. Their personal authority can make a claim believable. [L1261.117]
- Let a third party such as a doctor or expert recommend the product in the story instead of the brand claiming its own superiority. This keeps the tone editorial and makes the recommendation more credible. [L0578.021]
- Use testimonials from named, real customers who live in the same town or area as the prospect, quoting a specific outcome they experienced. Local recognition of the customer name gives the claim more weight than the seller's own words. [L1369.035]
- Testimonial clips of real people describing a visible result can be reused as the proof part of a new creative, including clips from ads for other products in the same category. Choose a speaker the buyer can identify with, because that is what the buyer wants to see. [L0901.013]

#### Celebrity, Expert and Everyday Voices

- Bring in other people to see the product through their eyes. Use a celebrity testimonial for a famous endorsement, or an ordinary person's case history or narrative for identification. Their actions and reactions make the performance feel new. [L0330.075]
- Show experts reacting with surprise. Professional or sophisticated people who are impressed by the product add surprise, competition and discovery to the image, and their astonishment is more convincing than the ad's own claim. [L0330.076]
- Use both celebrity testimonials and ordinary people's case histories to reaffirm the benefits. Celebrities give status, while everyday people give the reader an easy identity to share; both show the product from a new angle. [L0330.093]
- Showing envied or admired people in ads can increase desire, because readers want the status or outcome the person represents. Use the buyer's aspirations to select who appears. [L1261.118]

#### Chained Persona Testimonials

- A strong testimonial ad can chain several short creator testimonials, each speaking from a different persona such as a parent, a relative of someone with a specific problem, or a professional. Each persona names a different problem or angle, so one ad covers several viewer segments while the product stays the same. Keep each speaker's story short and tied to one concrete result. [L0707.029]

#### Contrast Testimonial Pattern

Pattern: a relatable person, such as a parent or partner, describes a contrast between age or a condition and how they look or feel, and credits the product as the cause. It works because a third person's visible result feels like proof rather than a sales claim. [L0901.018]

- Example 1: "My husband is fifty, but he keeps up with the kids on weekends since he started this protein mix." [L0901.018]
- Example 2: "My grandma was always cold until she began taking this iron supplement." [L0901.018]

## Using UGC in Ads, Store and Social Channels

### Paid ads: why UGC works

- UGC means real customer material, such as reviews and photos of people using the product. It builds trust and works especially well in retargeting ads, where it answers the doubts of people who already saw the product. [L0648.059]
- Meta favors content that looks natural. Over-produced ads still run but cost more, so real customer photos and videos, or phone-shot footage, often perform better. [L0649.036]
- Use real customer videos or photos in retargeting where possible. A customer describing their experience often outperforms a brand message. [L0649.060]
- Spark Ads from influencer posts tend to outperform regular brand creatives because they look like organic content from a trusted creator rather than a sales message. This lowers viewer resistance and makes the ad feel more credible. [L1374.036]

### Paid ads: where the traffic goes

- Turn collected UGC into paid ads. Cold audiences convert poorly on direct product pages, so send paid traffic to the influencer's profile or to a lead magnet page instead of the product page. [L0789.036]

| Audience | Creative | Send traffic to | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold | UGC video ads with real creators | Influencer's profile or lead magnet page | [L0789.036] |
| Retargeting (people who already saw the product) | Real customer videos or photos | Not specified in the source | [L0648.059, L0649.060] |

### Paid ads: whitelisting and brand pages

- Brand pages perform well with UGC video ads that feature real creators, sourced from TikTok Shop creator programs or official Instagram influencers. [L0906.012]
- Whitelisting means running these ads through a creator or persona page instead of the brand's own page. [L0906.012]
- The brand's page can still host the UGC content, so both approaches are used together. [L0906.012]

### Paid ads: boosting organic content

- Paid traffic such as Coins or Spark Ads on a brand-new account can speed up reaching the right audience during the new account advantage window, which lasts roughly the first one to two weeks. Use this boost only on new accounts. [L1374.031]
- Do not boost old accounts with established organic performance. Boosting old successful organic posts has been reported to kill organic sales. [L1374.031]
- Use organic videos as ad creatives only if they already reached strong views (around 50,000 to 100,000) and carry real social proof. Very new organic videos can lose organic reach when boosted through Spark Ads. [L1377.025]
- Both conditions apply together: the view threshold decides which videos qualify, and the account-age rule decides whether boosting is safe at all. [L1374.031, L1377.025]

### Paid ads: borrowed clips and copyright

- Using UGC clips taken from other brands or TikTok for ads carries a risk of copyright strikes. Keep this short term, such as a couple of weeks at 10 USD per ad set per day (50 USD total), and replace it with your own UGC once the product gains traction. [L0693.047]

### Ad iteration: fixing low-attention ads

- Ads with low attention can be improved rather than discarded. Layer a UGC video with a voice-over or new hooks, because a single hook change can lift attention and sales. [L1426.024]

### Converting winning UGC into other formats

- For books, UGC AI ads produced higher purchase intent and better traction than static image ads in the tests. Static image ads still work, but UGC AI ads are the better default for this product type. [L1030.022]
- Convert a proven UGC AI ad concept back to image ads, in this order:
  1. Confirm the UGC AI concept has produced many purchases and steady profit. [L1030.023]
  2. Build image ads that use the same concept, angle and pain point. [L1030.023]
  3. Run the image ads alongside the UGC AI ads and compare soft and hard metrics. [L1030.023]

### Product pages and testimonial sections

- Place customer photos in a dedicated section on the product page, not mixed into supplier product photos. This section is one of the most important elements for conversion. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1086.048]
- Customer photos also work in blog posts where a product image fits. A standalone customer photo gallery page is a weaker use of the photos than product pages and emails. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1086.050]
- In testimonial sections and product images, use raw UGC photos of real customers in real-life settings instead of high-quality studio shots. Visitors relate more to real people, and the lesson reports that such content converts better than polished lifestyle images. [L0916.029]

### Review proof blocks

- Pattern: place review screenshots from the store, a marketplace or a product page over a blurred version of the product, and show the store URL in the same frame. It works because it puts several genuine reviews in one image while the product remains visible in the background. [L0398.020]
  - Example 1: five screenshots of five-star reviews with the URL of the store at the bottom. [L0398.020]
  - Example 2: three reviews from a marketplace over a blurred kitchen counter with the product. [L0398.020]

### Messenger social proof

## Tracking, Measuring and Iterating

### Measuring Influencer Results: Principles and Priorities

- Tracking traffic from influencer partnerships is the most critical step in determining profitability. It shows whether a partnership returns enough to continue long-term. Some influencers become so profitable that they stay active advertising partners for multiple years. [L0183.010]
- Track referrals from brand ambassadors as their own traffic source, separate from paid social and search. This traffic is expected to be of higher quality than cold paid traffic, and it can feed back into the top of the funnel to create more customers. [L0676.019]
- Judge TikTok performance over several posts rather than a single post, because engagement on influencer content can rise and fall sharply: attention spans are short and trends move quickly, so expect results to fluctuate. [L0480.029]
- Organic Instagram posting that reached over 200 followers within a week signals content interest, but it is not sales evidence on its own. [L1016.065]
- A cited survey claims that 86% of businesses using influencer marketing effectively report an immediate or long-term effect on their brand. The source and the exact definition of "effective" are not stated, so treat this figure as a rough indication only. [L0786.013]

### Platform Analytics and Engagement Data

- Check the built-in analytics of the platform for each influencer post:
  - Instagram Insights
  - Facebook Insights
  - Twitter Analytics
  - YouTube Analytics
- Record for each post: comments, shares, likes and interaction rate. [L0630.001, L0786.002]
- Ask each influencer to send their engagement figures (comments, shares, likes, interaction rate) for the campaign posts, so you can compare them with your own platform data. [L0630.001, L0786.002, L0786.004]

### Influencer Platforms and Campaign Analytics

- Influencer marketing platforms such as Influency, Aspire IQ and Tracker provide detailed campaign analytics, including reach, engagement and metrics specific to each collaboration. [L0630.006]
- These platforms are most useful when the whole campaign is managed through them. [L0786.007]
- If the campaign ran through such a platform, pull its analytics for reach, engagement and collaboration-specific metrics. [L0630.001, L0786.002]

### Brand Mentions and Audience Feedback

- Track brand mentions over the campaign period, either with a social listening tool or by manually searching for the brand, keyword, hashtag or campaign name. [L0630.001, L0786.002]
- Survey the audience and collect feedback (surveys, social media polls) to find out where they first heard of the influencer and how they perceive the campaign. [L0630.001, L0786.002]

### Sales, Conversion and Unique Codes

- Track sales and conversion rate for each campaign. [L0630.001, L0786.002]
- Give each creator a unique discount code, ideally built from their name, so that sales can be traced back to that creator. A 25% code was affordable at that brand's margins. Any unique code, even free shipping, serves the same tracking purpose. [L0790.094]
- Coupon codes unique to each influencer track campaign results and strengthen the relationship with the creator. Any coupon works, even free shipping, as long as it is tied to a specific influencer. [L0790.056]
- Use one identical spelling of each creator name across all ads. Consistent creator names allow reports per creator and make creator commission payments easier to match. [L0885.006]
- An influencer post that does not produce a sale can still be valuable if it earns a follower, an email or a visit that can be retargeted. Plan this fallback before paying for the post. [L0790.061]

### Campaign Evaluation Sequence

Measure each influencer campaign in this order:

1. Check the platform's built-in analytics for each influencer post (Instagram Insights, Facebook Insights, Twitter Analytics, YouTube Analytics). [L0630.001, L0786.002]
2. Record comments, shares, likes and interaction rate. [L0630.001, L0786.002]
3. Ask the influencer to send their engagement numbers as well. [L0630.001, L0786.002]
4. Monitor brand and hashtag mentions with a listening tool, or search manually for the brand, keyword or campaign name. [L0630.001, L0786.002]
5. Pull detailed campaign metrics from the influencer marketing platform, if one was used. [L0630.001, L0786.002]
6. Track sales and conversion metrics. [L0630.001, L0786.002]
7. Survey the audience. [L0630.001, L0786.002]
8. Compare before and after the campaign. [L0630.001, L0786.002]
9. Compare results with your other marketing channels. [L0630.001, L0786.002]

### Deciding Which Partners to Repeat

- Use the results and audience insights from each campaign to decide which influencer strategies to repeat. [L0868.008]
- Choose future partners and formats based on what has resonated most with your audience. [L0868.008]
- Use the sequence above on every campaign before deciding, so that comparisons across creators rest on the same measurements. [L0630.001, L0786.002]
- Influencers whose traffic and sales prove profitable can remain active advertising partners for multiple years. [L0183.010]
