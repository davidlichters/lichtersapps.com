16.2 When to start and how much to spend

Timing: Launch Day or After Traffic and Sales

Sources differ on when paid retargeting should start. Launch-day setup is recommended in most sources. Several sources also require store traffic and sales before paid retargeting is funded.

Set up at launch

  • Set up Facebook remarketing from the first day, alongside Google Ads, because most first-time visitors from search do not buy on arrival (figure cited: about 98%), so they need to be brought back (older advice, 2019). [L0663.017]
  • Facebook ads can be set up from day one, but only for remarketing to people who already visited the store, not for cold prospecting (older advice, 2019). [L0356.054]
  • Set up remarketing campaigns on Google, Bing and Facebook at launch, so visitors who leave without buying can be shown the products again. [L0393.017]
  • Start remarketing campaigns on Google and Facebook early in the launch, so visitors who did not buy can be reached again. [L0532.021]
  • Set up Facebook remarketing campaigns to start as soon as the first store traffic arrives, not later, so retargeting can convert from the launch day (older advice, 2021). [L0671.015, L0671.291]
  • Even one extra remarketing sale every few days can be very profitable early on. This is why remarketing should be live at launch (older advice, 2021). [L0671.016, L0671.292]
  • Run small remarketing campaigns on both Google and Meta from the start, alongside Shopping (2025). [L0667.003]
  • On Facebook, start with remarketing only. Do not spend on cold audiences or brand awareness initially (older advice, 2020). [L0245.037]

Wait for traffic, sales and performance

  • Retargeting requires store traffic and sales first, because the audiences are built from visitors and buyers. Without early sales there is nothing to retarget. [L0661.062]
  • Abandoned cart emails and exit-intent capture are the first retargeting layer. Paid remarketing (Google and Facebook) comes after the store has traffic (older advice, 2020). [L0468.136]
  • Start retargeting after about two weeks of consistent performance from the product. Before that, the audiences are too small and the data too thin. [L0648.052]

How to read the conflict

  • The launch-day sources describe setting up campaigns and capturing the first visitors. [L0663.017, L0393.017, L0532.021, L0671.015, L0671.291]
  • The traffic-first sources describe when paid budget should be funded and grown. [L0661.062, L0468.136, L0648.052]
  • The only dividing line given in the sources is the two-week performance rule. [L0648.052]

  • Source A recommends about 10%. The 10-15% range (Source C) and the 10-20% range (early phase) are wider. Use about 10% as the default and treat the higher ranges as the sources' upper limits. [L0245.037, L1092.099, L0961.063, L0375.026, L0376.026]

  • Google and Meta remarketing should both run from the start. [L0667.003, L0532.021]
  • Bing remarketing is also set up at launch. No Bing split is given in the sources. [L0393.017]
  • Shift budget toward Google Ads for new prospects when remarketing takes too much of the total. [L0961.065]

Warning Signs of Over-Weighting Retargeting

  • Putting most of the budget into remarketing is a warning sign (older advice, 2020). It means the store is not finding new prospects. [L0961.065]
  • Response: shift the budget back toward Google Ads for new visitors. [L0961.065]
  • Keep the remarketing share closer to 10% in the early phase. [L0961.063]
  • The warning sign applies while the store is still finding its first buyers. Source B's growing share (2021-22, older) describes a later stage, once returning visitors and buyers exist. No threshold is given in the sources. [L0961.065, L0563.036]

When to Scale

Conditions before scaling

  • Retargeting starts after about two weeks of consistent performance from the product. [L0648.052]
  • Scale only once traffic and sales are growing. [L0733.015]
  • Increase the budget only once it brings sales. [L0468.137]

Scaling steps

  1. Confirm about two weeks of consistent product performance. [L0648.052]
  2. Confirm that traffic and sales are growing. [L0733.015]
  3. Duplicate the campaign to target only 7-day website visitors. This audience usually converts best because those visitors were on the store recently. [L0733.015]
  4. Increase the budget only where it brings sales. [L0468.137]
  5. Add longer audiences, such as the 180-day audience, later for promotions. [L0733.015]
