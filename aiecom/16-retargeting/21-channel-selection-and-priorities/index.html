16.21 Channel selection and priorities

Start with search, then retarget

  • Search-based campaigns come before retargeting. Google Search, Google Shopping and Bing are the preferred sources of store visitors because people actively look for the product there. [L1020.005, L1243.001]
  • Facebook retargeting starts only after Google Search, Google Shopping and Bing are maxed out. Facebook is a discovery platform, not a search platform, so it is the next channel to add, not the first. [L1243.001]
  • Cold Google campaigns target people searching for the product, while remarketing runs on both Google and Meta. This does not change based on whether your audience is more active on Google or Facebook. [L0471.015]
  • Retargeting should run across several networks with the same core messages. Using only one channel loses customers whose attention is split across platforms. (2021-22, older) [L0563.023]
  • Aim for a subtle, omnipresent presence across relevant networks so visitors meet your ads wherever they go without feeling followed. Reach cold traffic this way, and retarget warm and hot audiences across the same networks so competitors do not dominate attention. (2021-22, older) [L0563.025]
  • Scale retargeting to your traffic volume. [L1251.044]

Channel order

  1. Start with Google retargeting, which has the highest priority. Set up dynamic remarketing first, then standard remarketing, then video/YouTube remarketing. Build these audiences before any other retargeting channel. [L0920.013]
  2. Add Facebook, Instagram and Bing next, using the same audience logic on each platform rather than separate strategies. [L0920.014]
  3. Add the next bottom-of-funnel campaigns: website retargeting on the Google Display Network and retargeting on Shopping. Both target people who have already visited the store. [L0867.004]
  4. Keep YouTube low priority until other channels are fully used. (2021, older) [L0247.071, L0962.158]
  5. Test TikTok last, and only after the Meta and Google remarketing budgets are fully used. It is not a main revenue source. (2025) [L0837.055]

Conflicting advice on Facebook dynamic remarketing versus Google remarketing. The 2021 advice (older) gives Facebook dynamic remarketing ads the remarketing budget before Google remarketing or YouTube placement ads. It says Google remarketing is not worth the added complexity until monthly sales reach the high five or six figures. The undated Source A advice puts Google first without a sales threshold. [L1112.026, L0920.013]

Google retargeting

  • Google Ads offers several retargeting options, and Facebook (Meta) is the second main retargeting platform. [L1239.007, L1240.007]
  • Run retargeting natively inside Google Ads and do not pay for third-party retargeting apps. (2022, older) [L0803.052]

Meta: Facebook and Instagram

  • Use Meta for remarketing to people who have already visited the store. [L1116.057]
  • Use Facebook for remarketing rather than prospecting new audiences. Remarketing is the part of the Facebook budget that works best. (2021, older) [L1090.117]
  • Use Facebook ads to re-engage visitors after they leave the store, because most of the profit comes from this once Google brings in the first traffic. (2019, older) [L1069.085]
  • Facebook retargeting showed a slightly higher return on ad spend than Google retargeting in 2020, though not by much. Run both rather than choosing one. (2020, older) [L1277.091]
  • Remarketing is a high priority and should run on both Google and Meta. A good audience can help conversion and return on ad spend, especially for high-ticket products. (2023) [L1094.055]
  • Use Google remarketing alongside Meta, because some visitors never use Facebook or Instagram. Audience Network also shows ads on partner websites and apps. [L0649.049]
  • Facebook dynamic remarketing ads get the remarketing budget before Google remarketing when monthly sales are below the high five or six figures. (2021, older) [L1112.026]
  • Use carousel ads for the product in Facebook remarketing. [L1251.044]
  • Remarketing to non-buyers runs through Google Ads, Facebook, Instagram and email. Each channel can restart the funnel from the store visit. [L0676.015]

If your target audience has low Facebook usage, use retargeting campaigns on Google, Instagram or other advertising networks instead of relying solely on Facebook. [L0238.014]

YouTube and video

  • YouTube ads work well for remarketing. Target website visitors from the last seven days and exclude customers. Video production is a barrier, so YouTube is generally lower priority than Google Shopping. (2021, older) [L0247.071]
  • A short video, filmed as a selfie or recorded as a screen share, can remarket to visitors who viewed the store in the past seven days without buying. It works well on small budgets. Keep YouTube ads low priority until other channels are fully used. (2021, older) [L0962.158]
  • Video/YouTube remarketing comes third in the Google priority order, after dynamic and standard remarketing. [L0920.013]

TikTok

  • Test TikTok remarketing only after the Meta and Google remarketing budgets are fully used. It is not a main source of revenue. (2025) [L0837.055]
  • A separate TikTok retargeting campaign is generally unnecessary, because prospecting already reaches people who saw the product. Retarget only with a specific creative built for existing brand audiences, such as a discount-code offer. [L1374.025]
  • Adding Google ads to Facebook and TikTok campaigns lets you retarget visitors from those channels who did not buy. This recovers customers who would otherwise never return. [L1213.019]

Bing

  • Retarget on Bing after Google, using the same audience logic as on Facebook and Instagram. [L0920.014]
  • Bing search campaigns belong with Google Search and Google Shopping as search-based campaigns to max out before Facebook retargeting starts. [L1243.001]
  • Bing is a preferred search source of store visitors. [L1020.005]

Pinterest

  • Pinterest remarketing can add some revenue if your target audience uses Pinterest. It is unlikely to double a business on its own. (2020, older) [L1111.122]

Running Google and Meta as one sequence

  1. A visitor clicks a Google Shopping product listing ad and visits the store. [L1031.012]
  2. The visitor leaves without buying. [L1031.012]
  3. The visitor sees Facebook retargeting ads for those products. [L1031.012]
  4. Plan Google and Facebook campaigns together as one sequence, not as separate projects. [L1031.012]
  • Use Google ads after TikTok or Facebook ads to reach people who visited the site but did not buy, across channels such as YouTube and Google. This creates an omnichannel presence. [L1436.075]
