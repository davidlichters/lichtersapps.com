Startup, store setup and expansion

  • Startup costs in 2020 were small: a Shopify plan at about 29 USD per month, a domain at about 14 USD per year, and a start credit of 100 USD each from Google Ads and Facebook. After that, reinvest profit into ads. (Older advice, 2020.) [L1277.110]

  • Judge a store's performance by its setup goals first: suppliers approved, conversion-optimized site, Google Ads optimized, Bing Ads set up, SEO foundations, email campaigns and remarketing running. Then keep improving those, since a store is never finished. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0801.136]

  • Use this checklist before starting another store (older advice, 2021):

    1. Get approved with every supplier in the niche, aiming for 20 suppliers.
    2. Optimize the site for conversions.
    3. Optimize Google Ads.
    4. Set up Bing Ads.
    5. Optimize the site for search engines.
    6. Set up email campaigns.
    7. Run remarketing.
    8. Keep auditing and improving the first store before building a second one.

    [L0801.137]

  • Start a second store only when the first is set up and its budgets are tuned, and keep improving the first store while doing so. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0801.138]

  • Use this setup sequence for a new store (Source A, undated):

    1. Follow the Compliance Checklist.
    2. Setup Google Analytics.
    3. Setup Google Search Console.
    4. Submit Your Products To Google Merchant Center.
    5. Create Your Google Remarketing Campaign.
    6. Set Up Your Bing Webmaster Tools Account.
    7. Create Your Microsoft Merchant Center Store Account.
    8. Submit Your Products To Microsoft Merchant Center.
    9. Create Your Bing Shopping Campaigns.
    10. Create Your Facebook Ads Account.
    11. Connect Your Store To Facebook Ads.
    12. Create Your Audience For Facebook Ads.
    13. Create Your Facebook Remarketing Campaign.
    14. Locate and Contact Top Blogs in Your Niche.
    15. Create Your Promotions.

    [L0188.001]

Shopify and platform settings

  • Under Online Store > Preferences in Shopify, the Customer privacy section offers options to limit tracking for customers in Europe (GDPR, consent required before tracking) and to limit the third-party sale of California customers' data (CCPA). Enable these options if the store targets customers in those regions. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0370.015]
  • Track quantity must be turned on for each product under its inventory settings, and the exact number in stock entered there, before the inventory status can display any count on the product page. [L0976.031]
  • Enable Shopify order notifications on the phone so that new sales are visible as they happen; this was the alert the seller used during the viral phase. [L0821.009]
  • Google Analytics custom dashboards and custom Google Ads reporting are optional. Google's support documentation has current, screenshot-based guides for these. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0429.071]
  • Source A tracking sends store data to the course provider so the theme's performance can be monitored. To opt out, untick this option and click save. [L0973.013]
  • If the reviewer must examine paid traffic data, grant access to your Google Analytics and Google Ads accounts before the call. If access has not been granted, tell the reviewer explicitly that they need it. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0364.009]
  • Use Zapier at about $20 per month for abandoned cart recovery and order tracking. It recovers at least one additional sale from existing traffic, which makes it cost-effective for shops with a consistent abandon rate. [L0286.030]

Supplier, order and lead tracking

  • Track supplier outreach in a supplier tracking sheet with notes on who was contacted, when to follow up, and reminders. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0834.100]

  • Keep a tracking sheet for supplier conversations, with date, contact, what was said and the next follow-up step. This turns early outreach into a reliable record for later follow-up. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0803.092]

  • Keep detailed notes for every supplier contact in the tracking sheet. With many suppliers, you will otherwise forget who said what and what the next step is. [L0928.011]

  • Master Supplier List tracking columns to fill for every contact:

    Column Field Content
    E Date of contact Date you reached out to the supplier
    F Contact person Name of the person you spoke with at the supplier
    G Notes What happened on the call or email, plus next steps, such as a callback date, that the form will be emailed, or that a website dealer application must be submitted

    Always record the next step and any follow-up date, for example "contact us again in two months", so it does not get forgotten. [L0928.010]

  • When receiving multiple orders with products from different suppliers, each supplier typically ships separately with its own tracking number and packaging. Customers expect this; they understand they may receive 2-3 packages from 2-3 different suppliers on different days. (Source A, 2023.) [L0251.021]

  • Use the paid orders list to identify fulfillment bottlenecks. Cards with dates older than your typical supplier response time (usually 1-3 days) signal a delay, either in sending orders to suppliers or waiting for supplier response. The visual board reveals exactly which stage orders are accumulating, enabling quick investigation and intervention before delays cascade to slower shipping for customers. [L0292.017]

  • With more than around 100 orders a month, orders are easy to lose track of without status labels. Use labels so that every order's stage is visible at a glance. [L1000.067]

  • A glance at the prospect sheet shows the day's activity, for example 15 calls received, three closed sales and 30 prospects still open. This is enough to forecast the business's near-term workload. [L1000.054]

  • Measuring and recording all custom order requests in a database enables continuous optimization: it reveals which follow-up timing works, which approaches convert, and why deals succeed or fail. [L0284.009]

  • Add outcome columns to your custom order tracking sheet: record whether each lead resulted in a sale (won/lost/pending) and the specific reason (budget, timeline, feature fit, competitor choice, follow-up timing). This enables you to identify patterns in conversion failures and pinpoint which process improvements matter most. [L0284.015]

  • Roughly 80% of sales come from about 20% of products, and within that group a further 80/20 split applies. Monitor live inventory only for your best sellers, and do not try real-time inventory updates for the whole catalog. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0852.036]

  • The store in the lesson received fewer than one refund per month, and sometimes about one refund every three months, across thousands of orders. These numbers depend on clear descriptions and clear policies. [L0910.034]

  • The store received about two customer inquiries per week against five or more sales per week, which shows the effect of pre-sale and post-sale communication. These figures reflect the store's own experience, not a benchmark. [L0910.035]

Changelog, baselines and test records

  • Record a baseline figure, such as the 90-day average of a metric, in the notes before a change, so the result of the change can be compared against it. Without a baseline, a result like an open rate of 50% cannot be judged as an improvement. [L0410.008]
  • Use the changelog to explain sales movements: when sales rise or fall, check which logged experiments were running at that time before making unrelated changes. Attribute a rise to a test to double down on it, and stop a test that appears to cause a drop as soon as possible. [L0410.009]
  • Fill the Expected Results cell before a change goes live, because a written prediction is what shows later whether the change met the target or only looked like it did. Without a pre-recorded number, a result such as sales staying flat tends to be explained after the fact instead of judged against the goal. [L0410.015]
  • Track every test in a consistent system, because a documented library of past tests supports future decisions and adds value to the business when it is sold. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1364.030]

    (blank) Month name 1 2 3 ... 31 Total Goal Complete Reward
    (blank) Agenda (blank) ... (blank)
    (blank) 30 Mins Development TRUE/FALSE ... TRUE/FALSE 0 0.75 0
    (blank) New Idea To Growth Guide TRUE/FALSE (only on scheduled days) ... 0 0.75 0
    (blank) Launch New Ad Creative TRUE/FALSE (only on scheduled days) ... 0 0.75 0
    (blank) Attend Weekly Call TRUE/FALSE (only on scheduled days) ... 0 0.75 0

    The weekday header row (M T W T F S S) sits above each month's date row, and the first weekday is shifted per month to match the calendar. [L0456.006]

  • Each habit row ends with four columns: Total (count of completed days), Goal (set to 0.75), Complete (completion ratio) and Reward (what you give yourself when the goal is reached). Goal 0.75 means a 75% completion target. [L0456.007]

  • Each day's cell for a habit holds a TRUE/FALSE dropdown. Set it to TRUE when the habit was done and FALSE when it was not. [L0456.008]

  • Aim for a completion ratio of at least 75% per habit. When a habit reaches 75% completion, give yourself a reward that is small and affordable, such as a nice dinner or a pair of shoes. [L0456.011]

  • To use the tracker, open the Google Sheet and choose File > Make a copy. The copy saves automatically to Google Drive, so your own version keeps your daily entries. [L0456.013]

  • To record why a habit was missed, right-click the cell in Google Sheets and choose Comment, which is shown with the shortcut Ctrl+Alt+M. Write the concrete reason in that comment. [L0456.019]

  • The habit tracker spreadsheet is split into tabs named Q1 24, Q2 24, Q3 24 and Q4 24. Each tab holds three monthly blocks, one for each month of that quarter. [L0456.020]

  • Each priority needs measurables: hard, trackable data points with a target value at the end of six months. Measurables can be traffic, number of keywords ranked, number of backlinks, sales count, revenue, profit, profit margin, conversion rate, number of approved suppliers or number of uploaded products. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0447.012]

  • Set measurable targets that are achievable rather than aspirational extremes, because a target far out of reach for the period tends to discourage progress. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0447.014]

  • Typical business weaknesses to test for include: paid ads that were not profitable, no organic traffic so all visits depend on paid traffic, unreliable suppliers or too few supplier options, and too few working hours put into the business by the owner or the team. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0800.015]

  • Complete a time-tracking exercise before starting the annual review, so that you can check how you actually spent your hours. The review then relies on recorded time rather than memory. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0800.023]

  • Identify your best-selling product from data rather than memory:

    1. Open your Shopify store's sales reports and view sales by product.
    2. Find the product with the highest sales.
    3. Copy that product's URL for your records.

    If the store is new and has no sales yet, this step does not apply. [L1268.006]

  • A founder who works 6 hours a day for weeks and sees no progress can give AI the product, the price and the ad results. AI can then diagnose which element is holding the results back and what to change, so the next step is based on data. [L0887.014]

  • A monthly creative output report can be shown as a stacked column chart with one column per month, where static and video output stack within each column. Pair the chart with a table holding the same monthly figures so that the growth or drop in total output is visible at a glance. [L0885.028]

  • The store review videos are published every other week, so store owners should expect the next review about two weeks after each publication. [L0838.019]

Ad performance and direct response

  • When an ad exploits existing mass desire, each dollar of ad spend can produce roughly 50 to 100 dollars in sales. When an ad has to create the desire first, it behaves like education and returns about 1 dollar in sales per dollar spent. [L0330.002]
  • A mentor can interpret account-specific metrics such as CTR and CPM, including the time window to check and when to analyze ad sets. General videos cannot tell you these thresholds for your own product. [L1213.080]
  • Winning products often cannot be predicted in advance and must be tested in the market to see whether they sell. Ad creative should create a problem or desire the buyer feels, because the product itself is usually simple and the demand is created through the ad. [L0780.003]
  • Historical Facebook ad example: a seasonal product was launched with about 2,000 to 3,000 dollars in budget, and about 50,000 dollars was reinvested in ads from 211,000 dollars in revenue within 30 days. This was in an unsaturated market with cheap cost per click and image ads; it is not a current benchmark. [L0561.043]
  • Early internet scam-style ads (e.g., "win free iPhone" or "get free trip") achieved 10 to 30 percent conversion rates because users clicked readily and could not identify untrustworthy websites. This is a historical example, not a guide for current ads. (Source A.) [L0257.002]
  • The 2021 Bing figure is 37.5% of U.S. desktop searches, the most recent figure cited at the time. This is a 2021 desktop-only share. [L1034.005]
  • Do not use the 37.5% Bing share as the share of total search traffic. It covers only U.S. desktop searches, not all U.S. searches including mobile. [L1034.006]
  • To check whether readers finish an ad, offer a small cash reward, such as 5 dollars, to anyone who writes in saying they read it through. Very few replies show that most readers skip the copy, and the headline or opening needs to be stronger. [L1261.152]
  • When you focus on B2B, remove low-priced products from paid ads and keep the same ad approach for the rest. Advertise in trade publications, or set up affiliate deals with them, to reach business readers. (Older advice, 2020.) [L0961.015]
  • A friend's drop-shipping store generated about $476,900 in revenue in 30 days, and nearly all of its traffic came from Facebook (referrals from mobile.facebook.com, facebook.com and l.facebook.com). The result came from running multiple stores with Facebook as the main traffic source. [L0643.045]
  • One client grew from zero to $1.1 million in sales in 10 months. Another grew from $12,000 to $60,000 in monthly sales. Both results were given as examples of Facebook ads working with careful targeting, and the method was not fully described in the lesson. [L0643.046]

Copy and response measurement

  • Track the hit ratio, meaning the share of sales letters or promotions that generate sales. One copywriter reported a hit ratio of 20 to 40 percent before using a structured copy method and 60 to 80 percent after applying it to every project, so measure it per promotion to compare methods. [L1365.052]

  • Breakthrough Advertising Marketing Plan: a Google Sheets template to map your market positioning and messaging strategy. Complete it after reading the Breakthrough Advertising copywriting framework to align your positioning with your market's awareness level. https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1ocIg8b57icetsD1lAyLUpYlbZCXc3dHLzFHuUs9p4xE/edit#gid=1874141477 [L0258.018]

  • Readability is measured with Flesch Reading Ease, calculated manually in this order:

    1. Count the words, treating contractions, hyphenated words, abbreviations, figures, symbols, and their combinations as single words.
    2. Count the syllables in each word as it is normally pronounced, counting abbreviations, figures, and symbols as one syllable; if a word has two accepted pronunciations, use the one with fewer syllables.
    3. Count the sentences as units ending in a period, colon, semicolon, question mark, exclamation point, or dash, ignoring paragraph breaks and internal punctuation.
    4. Divide total syllables by total words to get average syllables per word.
    5. Divide total words by total sentences to get average words per sentence.
    6. Combine both averages in the formula to get the score.

    [L0340.060]

  • Readability comparison of two ad paragraphs about the same offer:

    Paragraph Sentences per paragraph Words per sentence Characters per word Flesch Reading Ease Flesch-Kincaid grade
    1 (plain, short sentences) 7.0 13.1 4.1 72.1 6.4
    2 (dense, long sentences) 3.0 25.3 5.3 34.1 14.7

    [L0340.064]

  • Test a new mailing with a small list before committing a large budget, for example 5,000 names instead of 50,000. A small test shows whether the offer, the copy and the list work before the full cost is at risk. [L1366.098]

  • Measure readership by how many people travel through the full copy, not by circulation or impressions. Only readers who go through the copy can buy, so a large audience that reads little makes a weak ad. [L1366.099]

  • An illustrated four-page letter with a stamped post card cost about 4 cents per piece including postage in 1913 US mail, sent first class with no enclosure. (Historical example.) [L1369.087]

  • Follow-up response for a direct mail sequence to the same list of old leads (historical example):

    Mailing Content Orders per mailed piece
    1 First letter to old leads 1 to 2 years old 4.5%
    2 Free examination letter with enclosed circular 2.5%
    3 Later letter with circular enclosure 1.5%
    4 Large folder with only a post card 1%
    5 Long four-page mop-up letter 2%

    [L1369.088]

  • Record orders per mailing on a record sheet for every letter, headline, envelope and variant, and let that record decide which versions stay. A letter's worth is measured only by the orders it brings back. [L1369.089]

  • Test one element at a time, such as the headline, envelope, letterhead, stamp or opening, and compare each against a plain control letter. Keep only what increases orders and drop what does not. [L1369.090]

  • Judging a letter by polish or by how well it follows traditional rules is misleading, because a plain letter can outsell a polished one. Only the response measured in orders should decide. [L1369.091]

  • In the first six months of one mail-order campaign, the profit on sales reached 34.5 percent. [L1369.092]

  • Track response by test cell and count the response over time, because most sales letter response comes in quickly and a trickle follows weeks later. Compare the immediate response with the expected profit, since the trickle is usually small in profit terms. [L1370.112]

  • Use no-cost pretests before paying for a mailing test, since a small direct-mail test of 5,000 to 10,000 pieces can cost a lot. Pretest results sometimes reveal a letter is so weak that it must be rewritten entirely. [L1370.113]

  • Use split tests to compare variations, such as a neat, organised layout versus a messy, busy one, because the result is often the opposite of what designers expect. Test pricing formats, guarantees and premiums the same way. [L1370.114]

Customer contact and reputation

  • Entrepreneurs should expect odd or hostile customer contacts once they open the business to the world, such as calls at odd hours or suspicion about free shipping on heavy items. These usually do not reflect the business's legitimacy. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0429.075]

Brand protection and infringement tracking

  • Tracking log for brand infringement reports (one tab per platform, same column setup on every tab):

    Columns: Item (the infringer or page name) | Complaint ID (received after filing) | Person (who handled the report) | Detection Date | Reporting Date | Removal Date | Location (if detectable) | Link (to the infringing content) | Infringement Type (Copyright or Trademark) | Status (Detected, Reported, Resubmitted, Escalated, Removed).

Also keep a brand information tab with the details needed for reports: brand name, rights owner, official online presence, registration numbers, mailing address and reporting email.

Use the status 'Resubmitted' when the platform asks for more details, and 'Escalated' when outside help is needed.

    [L0991.014]

  • Monthly, weekly or yearly analysis of the infringement log:

    1. Count the infringements by platform.
    2. Count the infringements by type (copyright or trademark).
    3. Identify the most often infringed products.
    4. Identify where the infringers are located.
    5. Use the results to refine the detection and reporting process.

    [L0991.015]

  • Tabs in the infringement tracking board:

    Tab Purpose
    Brand Information Brand name, rights owner, official online presence, registration numbers, mailing address, reporting email
    Amazon, eBay, Bol.com, TikTok, Etsy, Shopify One tab per marketplace or channel searched
    Facebook, Instagram One tab per social platform, sorted by year groups such as 2024
    Other Marketplaces Other channels not listed above
    Stand Alone Websites (current year) Standalone shops found in the current year
    (CLOSED) previous year tabs Archive of finished years, for example (CLOSED) 2023 Stand Alone Websites

    Every platform tab uses the same column setup. [L0991.035]

Finance

  • Amex Gold points on advertising spend = 4 x advertising spend, counted only up to $100,000 of spend per year. The maximum is therefore 400,000 points per year. [L0699.015]

Case results and examples

  • Case study: Resistance Bands, Day 18. Revenue: $507 on 17 orders. Average order value: $29.85 (full price, no discounts or giveaways). This demonstrates that sustainable growth does not require artificial tactics like free giveaways or deep discounting. [L0228.035]
  • Case study: Resistance Bands, Week 5-6: 96 units sold, $2,900 revenue, 10-18 units per day (average daily orders remained above 10 for 6 of 7 days). The product price increased from an initial $24.95 to a $30.17 average, then to $35.95, without significant order drop-off. Week 6 single day: 12 orders at the raised $35.95 price. Net profit after Amazon fees: $22 per unit. Within 6 weeks, the initial investment was fully recovered and a reorder was funded. Weeks 7-8 produced profit payouts from the two-week Amazon payout cycle. Key lesson: price increases do not necessarily reduce orders in a low-supply scenario with high demand. [L0228.036]
  • Success case: 108 orders in one month = $325,000 in sales; average order value $3,000 per order. (Older example, 2020.) [L0268.043]
  • Profitability timeline: the first sale typically comes within 30 days of launch if the 21-day launch plan is followed. Timeline to consistent profitability depends on reinvestment rate and ad optimization. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0270.045]
  • Example: A competitor added extra features to their weighted blanket (making it Queen-size by default), which doubled product cost. You kept it simple (single size), spent the margin savings on ads, and won the market. [L0041.007]
  • Creative and product-page measurement, for a product description: aim for a product description of roughly 600 words; this length supports SEO and avoids thin content. Count words in the product copy and aim for that range. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0461.015]
  • Snapchat's SPECS AR glasses were reported to cost around 2,000 US dollars. The price is an estimate from the update commentary, not an official figure. [L1504.019]
  • At the stage described, the brand had about 185 products on sale and an annual revenue of around 1 million pounds. The founder reported that the business had reached a multi-seven-figure level. [L1438.048]
