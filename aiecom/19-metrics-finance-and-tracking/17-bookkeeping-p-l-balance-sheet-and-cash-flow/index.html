19.17 Bookkeeping, P&L, balance sheet and cash flow

Bookkeeping foundations and when to start

  • Bookkeeping means setting up accounting software, importing bank transactions, classifying them and reconciling them. It is complex and time-consuming, so many owners hand it to a monthly bookkeeping service. [L0632.004]
  • Set up sound accounting practices from the first sale, either yourself or with an accountant or bookkeeper. Basic bookkeeping is enough at the start to track profit per sale and where costs go. [L0702.025]
  • Leaving accounting until sales are high usually results in messy books. Without knowing where money comes from and where it goes, the business is harder to grow. [L0702.026]
  • Solid accounting shows the profit on each individual sale and where marketing spend is too high. This clarity supports sound business decisions and reduces financial stress. [L1405.033]
  • The general ledger is the foundation of business finances. KPIs, budgets, cash management, financing and loans should only be built on top of it, because without that foundation advanced financial analysis is not reliable. [L1246.011]
  • The general ledger does not answer every financial question by itself. It answers the basic ones, and more advanced needs are addressed with additional layers built on it. [L1246.013]
  • The general ledger can be maintained by the owner in accounting software or handed to an outside bookkeeping firm. Either way, the point is to have the reports it produces available for decisions. [L1246.022]

Accounting software and bookkeepers

Choosing a bookkeeping approach

  • When hiring a bookkeeping service, choose one that specializes in ecommerce operations. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0107.009]
  • Professional ecommerce bookkeeping services cost a few hundred dollars per month. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0107.010]
  • To handle bookkeeping, choose one of three approaches (older advice, Source B 2021-22): [L0107.011]
    1. Hire a bookkeeping service specializing in ecommerce for complete done-for-you management.
    2. Enroll in a self-paced bookkeeping course with group coaching. Dropship Breakthrough members receive free access.
    3. Recruit and train a virtual assistant from Upwork or Online Jobs using bookkeeping course materials.
  • Do not hire cheap or unvetted bookkeepers because you lack expertise to audit their work. Problems remain undetected for months, and fixing poorly maintained books costs far more than hiring professionals initially. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0107.012]
  • The monthly P&L, balance sheet and cash flow statement are generated by general ledger bookkeeping software if the owner does the books, or delivered by a bookkeeper if one is hired. The owner should ask the bookkeeper to walk through these reports each month. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0484.020]
  • If the owner cannot read the monthly reports, or the reports do not explain where the cash went, the bookkeeping is weak. Replace or improve the bookkeeper rather than relying on unclear numbers. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0484.021]

  • Xero is used for accounting and bookkeeping with Shopify integration, typically with the help of an accountant. A simple profit-tracking spreadsheet can replace it while the business is very new. (Source A 2022, older advice.) [L1281.093]
  • Google Sheets is suitable for day-to-day bookkeeping and monitoring a small business. Send the records your accountant needs to Xero, which they use to manage the books and taxes. (Source A 2018.) [L1274.054]

  • An accounting platform such as Xero or QuickBooks, also called a general ledger, records all transactions and provides a P&L, a balance sheet and a cash flow statement. It gives a more complete and balanced view of the business than a P&L spreadsheet. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1246.010]

Spreadsheet P&L setup (build phase)

Template and structure

  • The "Financial statements" template by Xero is available in the Google Drive template gallery and runs in Google Sheets. It is a free basic P&L template for a drop shipping store, with tabs for Instructions, Transactions, Dashboard, Profit & Loss, Categories and Balance Sheet. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0763.002]
  • The spreadsheet template is a build-phase tool. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0763.003]
  • The Transactions sheet has the columns Date, Description, Amount and Category. Category is a drop-down that must be set for every row, and the Profit & Loss sheet only reads transactions that have a category. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0763.005]
  • Track business expenses on dedicated cards so that month-end reconciliation between bank records, card statements and the spreadsheet is quick and reliable. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0903.026]
  • Ignore the balance sheet tab during the build phase. It needs manual entries in shaded boxes and does not help with tracking income and expenses. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0763.021]
  • Review the P&L month by month, because the monthly layout shows how revenue, cost of goods sold and expenses change from one month to the next. The growth rate projection and Dashboard charts are optional extras. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0763.022]

Setup steps

Setting up the P&L template in Google Sheets (older advice, Source B 2021-22): [L0763.004]

  1. Open the Transactions sheet and delete the sample transactions.
  2. Enter all business transactions (date, description, amount) in the Transactions sheet.
  3. Select the matching category for each transaction from the drop-down menu.
  4. The Dashboard and Profit & Loss sheets update automatically from the transactions.
  5. To rename or change a category, edit it on the Categories sheet.
  6. To add a new category, first insert a new row below the last row of that category type on the Categories sheet, type the category name and select the category type from the drop-down. Then go to the Profit & Loss sheet, select the row showing #REF! and insert a new row below it. The new category then appears on the Profit & Loss sheet and in the Transactions sheet drop-down.
  7. The balance sheet step (enter values in the shaded boxes) is not needed in the build phase.

In the Google Sheets financial statements template, open the Profit & Loss sheet and select cell F4 to choose the first month of the financial year. The other month columns update from that choice. Revenue, cost of sales and expenses are summarized automatically from the Transactions sheet into a Total YTD column. An optional Growth Rate field then creates a Projected column for a projected P&L. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1246.020]

On the Profit & Loss sheet, select cell F4 to choose the first month of your financial year, and the month columns update from there. Negative amounts are displayed in parentheses, and the Total YTD column sums the year so far. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0763.008]

Sign convention and checks

  • Enter money you spend, including cost of goods sold, as a negative amount. Enter money you receive, such as sales, as a positive amount. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0763.006]
  • Entering expenses and supplier payments as positive numbers gives impossible results, such as a gross profit larger than sales minus supplier costs and a wrong total profit. Check the signs of all amounts before reading the P&L. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0763.007]
  • A small orange warning triangle that appears in the template after an entry signals an input problem. In the lesson's example it flagged amounts entered with the wrong sign. Check that all costs are negative and all incoming money is positive before trusting any profit figure. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0763.025]
  • Sign error example in a build-phase drop shipping P&L (older advice, Source B 2021-22): a Shopify payout of 2,000 dollars is recorded as positive revenue, but the 1,200 dollar supplier charge and the 500 dollar Google Ads charge are entered as positive numbers too. The sheet then shows a gross profit of 3,200 dollars and a total profit of 3,700 dollars, which cannot be right for these transactions. After both costs are changed to negative amounts, gross profit correctly becomes 800 dollars and the total profit after the 500 dollar advertising expense is 300 dollars. [L0763.024]
  • Starting point: a new drop shipping store with no sheet data yet (older advice, Source B 2021-22). On March 1, a Shopify payout of 2,000 dollars was recorded as a Sales transaction (positive). On March 15, Supplier A charged 1,200 dollars, recorded as a negative Cost of Goods Sold. On March 17, Google Ads charged 500 dollars, recorded as a negative Advertising expense. The result is a Gross Profit of 800 dollars (2,000 minus 1,200) and a total profit of 300 dollars after the 500 dollar ad expense. [L0763.010]

Projection

  • The Profit & Loss sheet has a Growth Rate field next to the Total YTD column. Entering a percentage there, for example 20%, adds a Projected column based on the year-to-date total. In the example, a Total YTD of 3,100 with 20% growth gives a projected 3,720. This projection is optional and not needed during the build phase. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0763.023]
  • The projected figure equals the Total YTD value multiplied by (1 + growth rate). For example, 2,000 with a 20% growth rate projects to 2,400. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1246.021]

What belongs on a P&L

  • A profit and loss statement (P&L, also called an income statement) lists revenue, costs and expenses for a period and ends with the profit or loss. In the build phase, when a store is preparing to launch or has just made first sales, a basic P&L is enough to track money in and out. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0763.001]
  • A P&L (profit and loss sheet, also called an income statement) lists income, expenses and the resulting profit after all expenses are subtracted. It is the basic financial overview a store owner needs from the start. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1148.003]
  • The profit and loss statement (income statement) is the first report to review each month and for the full year. It lists total income, cost of goods sold, gross profit, operating expenses, operating income, other income and net income for the period. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1046.001]
  • A P&L shows income, expenses and profit, where profit equals income minus expenses. It shows overall profitability and where money is spent, but it is not sufficient on its own to judge the financial health of a business. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1246.007]
  • Looking only at the P&L gives a skewed view of the business, especially when a business is large enough to be scrutinized by a buyer. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1246.008]
  • The following transactions do not belong on a P&L (older advice, Source B 2021-22): owner draws, owner contributions, transfers between own accounts, credit card payments, inventory purchases, loans and COVID stimulus payments. Recording inventory as an expense can have a serious impact on taxes. [L1246.009]
  • The P&L does not show cash flow, owner withdrawals or profit distributions, loans or credit card payoffs, investments into the business, or inventory purchases. Its bottom line will not match the bank account balance. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0484.002]
  • In a drop shipping business, income means sales and expenses mean every outgoing payment, such as supplier costs, advertising, hosting and co-working spaces. Keep only income and expenses in the sheet during the build phase and leave out internal transfers, unless they go to a supplier. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0763.020]
  • Cost of goods sold is what you pay the supplier for the products, including the shipping they handle for you. Large supplier payments made by ACH or wire transfer are recorded in the same way as card charges, as cost of goods sold. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0763.011]
  • Money moved between your own accounts is neither income nor an expense, so do not record it. The exception is a transfer sent to a supplier, which is an expense and goes into the sheet as cost of goods sold. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0763.012]
  • The P&L follows bank activity, meaning the date money arrives or leaves, not the date an order was placed. A sale may therefore appear in the following month if the payout or supplier charge comes later. Do not expect the monthly totals in the order profit tracker to match the monthly P&L, because the order profit tracker shows when orders were taken while the P&L shows when money came in or went out. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0763.013, L0763.014, L0763.026]
    • Example: a sale made on February 28 with a Shopify payout arriving about three days later lands in the bank in March, so the P&L shows it under March. Monthly differences like this are normal because the P&L follows bank activity, not order dates. [L0763.026]
  • Formula, Total Cost of Sales = Cost of Goods Sold + Custom COGS 1 + Custom COGS 2. Gross Profit (Gross Margin) = Total Sales minus Total Cost of Sales. Total Expenses = sum of all expense categories. Total Profit or Loss = Gross Profit minus Total Expenses. Negative results are shown in parentheses. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0763.009]
  • Gross margin on the P&L equals total sales minus total cost of sales. For example, sales of 2,000 with cost of sales of 1,200 give a gross margin of 800. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1246.021]
  • Track each advertising cost to the specific product or department it sells, and require every unit of spend to justify itself within a short cycle. Unattributed budget lines are where money is most often wasted. (Source C, undated.) [L1261.153]

Annual P&L structure

  • Use a profit and loss sheet that deducts Shopify fees as its own line, because a P&L without platform fees overstates your real profit. Replace older versions of the sheet with the updated one. (Source D 2026.) [L1323.006]

Reading the P&L

  • Reading a P&L from top to bottom (older advice, Source B 2021-22): [L0484.018]
    1. Total income (sales).
    2. Cost of goods sold (COGS).
    3. Gross profit (total income minus COGS).
    4. Operating expenses, listed by category.
    5. Net ordinary income (gross profit minus operating expenses).
    6. Other income and expense, such as interest income, miscellaneous income and credit card rewards.
    7. Net income, the bottom line, which is the profit.
  • Monthly net results in a seasonal dropshipping business can swing negative because supplier payments follow earlier orders. Judge profitability by the full year and expect it to rise or at least hold steady year over year. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1046.008]

Sales reports and margins

  • In Shopify's sales breakdown, total sales equals gross sales minus returns. In the example month, $915,682.28 across all channels minus $47,519.91 in returns gave $868,162.37, and the online store alone showed $763,095.30 minus $42,630.12 = $720,465.18. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0400.016]
  • Formulas (older advice, Source B 2021-22): net sales = total sales minus returns (for example, $251,671.35 minus $23,721.80 equals $227,949.55). Net profit percentage = net profit divided by net sales, where net profit is what remains after product, fulfillment, shipping and marketing costs (for example, $50,148.90 divided by $227,949.55 is about 22%). Average order value = net sales divided by orders, and conversion rate = orders divided by sessions. [L0458.042]
  • Read the store's sales report in this order: total sales, returns, net sales, orders, sessions and visitors, and profit after all costs. Returns must be deducted before calculating the profit percentage, otherwise profit looks higher than it is. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0458.043]
  • Gross margin = gross profit divided by total income. The course example shows 14.14 percent by dividing by gross sales before discounts and returns (258,322 divided by 1,826,410). On the same 258,322 gross profit and 1,554,399 total income, the margin is about 16.6 percent. Pick one denominator and use it consistently when comparing months and years. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1046.003]
  • Net margin = net income divided by total income. The example gives 138,142 divided by 1,554,399, which is about 8.9 percent (about 9 percent). (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1046.004]

Accrual and cash accounting

Definitions and examples

  • Under accrual accounting, an order is recorded as income on the day it is placed, regardless of when payment arrives. Under cash accounting, income and expenses are recorded only when money actually reaches or leaves the bank account. For example, an order placed on March 31 with a Shopify payout three days later would appear in March under accrual but in April under cash. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0484.003]
  • Cash-based accounting records income and expenses when money moves in or out of the bank account. Accrual-based accounting records income when it is earned and the matching product cost in the same month, which evens out dips. The broker prefers accrual accounting for online businesses, though both work for dropshipping. (Source A, undated.) [L0554.039]
  • Cash-based example: in month 1, 10 units sell at $100 each with a $50 cost per unit paid on 30-day supplier terms, so cash profit is $1,000. In month 2, 3 units sell for $300 and the bill of $500 for the month 1 units is paid, so cash shows a $200 loss. (Source A, undated.) [L0554.040]
  • Accrual example: the same 10 units in month 1 show $1,000 income and $500 cost, so profit is $500. In month 2, the 3 units show $300 income and $150 cost, so profit is $150. (Source A, undated.) [L0554.041]
  • Cash-based accounting becomes a serious problem for businesses with inventory. For example, buying 1,000 units for $20,000 can show a very large negative month, so accrual accounting makes more sense in that case. (Source A, undated.) [L0554.042]

Which method applies when

  • For dropshipping, record sales income on the order date but record cost of goods (supplier cost) only when money leaves the bank or credit card to pay the supplier. This cash-basis approach to COGS is the practical standard, as opposed to tracking profit per order. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0484.010]
  • Most small dropshipping stores record product cost when the supplier charges the card rather than on the order date. This is a hybrid of accrual and cash bookkeeping. Full accrual bookkeeping for every invoice is usually too time-consuming and expensive at scale. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1046.009]
  • Where the sources differ: the broker recommends accrual for online businesses, while the Source B course treats cash-basis COGS as the practical standard for small stores and hybrid as the norm. Use accrual where inventory is large and lumpy, as in the 1,000-unit example, and the hybrid or cash-basis COGS approach for small dropshipping stores where full accrual is too costly. [L0554.039, L0554.042, L0484.010, L1046.009]

Balance sheet

  • Assets are anything a business owns that can generate economic benefit, such as cash, inventory, investments and real estate. Liabilities are amounts owed to others, such as credit card balances, loans, unpaid wages and taxes owed. A rule of thumb is that assets put money in your pocket and liabilities take money out of it, so a home you live in is not counted as an asset in that sense. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0484.004]
  • Buying inventory is not an expense. It is a trade of cash for an equal value of inventory, so the net position does not change at the time of purchase. Inventory can be thought of as cash sitting on a shelf, and it only becomes income through the P&L when it is sold. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0484.005]
  • The balance sheet is a snapshot of account balances at one point in time. It lists assets (bank and credit account balances, inventory, and other items not on the P&L) against liabilities (loans, grants, and other amounts owed), with equity making up the rest. It shows balances, not increases or decreases, and its totals must reconcile as assets minus liabilities equals equity. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0484.006]
  • Check the balance sheet with Assets = Liabilities + Equity. In the example, liabilities of 149,000 plus equity of (88,165) give 60,835, which matches total assets of 60,834 within rounding. If the two sides differ by more than rounding, a line is missing or entered wrong. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1046.049]
  • Current year earnings in equity shows the net income of the current year to date. Retained earnings holds profit kept from earlier years after owner distributions. Review both together when judging whether the business is building equity over time. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1046.052]
  • Year-end cash in the example was 31,631 (business checking 31,176 and savings 456). Business cash rose over the year by a figure the course puts at about 29,000 to 34,000 after owner draws. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1046.018]
  • A balance sheet and a cash flow statement are two additional key reports that accompany the P&L, and both are critical for understanding the business. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1246.012]

Cash flow statement

  • The cash flow statement shows every cash movement into and out of bank and credit accounts, covering incomes, expenses, asset purchases and liability payments. It is always recorded on a cash basis and is the link that connects the P&L and balance sheet to the bank account. It is divided into operating activities (customer receipts, supplier and employee payments, interest, taxes), investing activities (equipment sales, inventory purchases) and financing activities (dividends, credit card payoffs or borrowing). (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0484.007]
  • Cash flow statement arithmetic: net cash from operating activities + net cash from investing activities + net cash from financing activities = net increase (or decrease) in cash. Ending cash equals beginning cash plus that net increase, and the ending figure must match the bank and credit card balances. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0484.019]
  • Net change in cash equals operating cash flow plus investing cash flow plus financing cash flow. Ending cash equals beginning cash plus that net change, so the cash flow statement should close exactly to the bank and clearing balances on the balance sheet. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1046.050]
  • The cash flow net from operations should roughly match profit but will differ because of timing. Sales are recorded when a customer buys, while cash only arrives when the payment processor pays out. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1046.010]
  • Net cash from operating activities = cash receipts from customers minus payments to suppliers and employees plus other operating cash receipts. The example gives 1,557,243 minus 1,421,467 plus 23,515, which equals about 159,290 (the course's operating cash figure). This three-line view summarizes the whole profit and loss for cash purposes. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1046.011]
  • Inventory purchases and similar non-P&L cash payments belong in investing activities, not operating activities. For dropshipping stores, the main investing item is payments in transit, which is cash already earned from payment processors but not yet in the bank. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1046.012]
  • Read the signs in the cash flow statement as direction of cash. Negative financing cash means net debt repayment or owner distributions leaving the business, while positive financing cash means new borrowing or owner money coming in. A negative financing line is not automatically bad when it reflects paying down card debt. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1046.051]

Three reports together

  • The three monthly financial reports (P&L, balance sheet, cash flow statement) can be used together to cross-check one another. If two of them are available, the third can be derived. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L0484.008]
  • A P&L can show profit while the bank account does not match it, which is a common problem for growing e-commerce stores. Reconciling the P&L against bank balances is a key check. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1516.005]

Monthly reporting routine

  • Monthly financial review order (older advice, Source B 2021-22): [L1046.013]
    1. Pull the profit and loss statement for the month and year to date.
    2. Calculate gross margin and net margin with one consistent denominator.
    3. Review the monthly gross profit trend against sales to spot timing effects.
    4. Review the balance sheet: cash, clearing accounts, credit card and loan balances, and whether liabilities are growing faster than assets.
    5. Review the cash flow statement: operating cash, investing (payments in transit and inventory), and financing (card balances, loan payments, owner draws).
    6. Check the large cost lines: outbound shipping, advertising spend, freelancers, interest expense and travel.
    7. Turn the findings into actions, starting with the biggest margin lever.
  • Accounting software such as Zero can produce these monthly reports from connected store and bank data. A spreadsheet can imitate the same layout for practice or for quick what-if calculations. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1046.014]
  • A growing store creates predictable financial questions: where the money is going when the P&L shows profit but the bank balance does not match, how much is owed to suppliers, and why the bank balance is unexpectedly high. Knowing the answers to these questions is what bookkeeping is for. (Older advice, Source B 2021-22.) [L1516.004]

Reconciliation and data checks

  • Export no more than six months of transaction data at a time to avoid confusion during AI classification. Longer periods require too much context and reduce accuracy. (Source E 2025-26.) [L0218.001]
  • Processing 22,000 transactions from a six-month period takes approximately one hour with Claude Cowork on Opus 4.6. (Source E 2025-26.) [L0218.002]
  • Export Xero transactions (Source E 2025-26): [L0218.003]
    1. Open Xero and reconcile transactions through your target end date (max six months back).
    2. Select all accounts and add Contact as a column.
    3. Click Update.
    4. Select Excel and export the transaction list.
    5. Save the file for use in Claude Cowork.
  • Flag any Xero export data more than 30 days old as potentially stale before drawing financial conclusions. Check export dates immediately upon receipt. (Source E 2025-26.) [L0218.015]
  • Treat mismatches as urgent priority: revenue recorded on platforms not matching bank deposits, COGS not matching supplier invoices, or ad spend not matching bank statements. Flag these as potential discrepancies immediately. (Source E 2025-26.) [L0218.016]
  • Check that the Shopify tax settings are configured, since the store then shows tax data in its reports. (Source A, undated.) [L0970.151]

AI-assisted finance reports

  • Do not copy-paste transaction data into Claude manually. Drag the exported Xero file into the Claude Code project folder, where the AI reads it directly. This avoids manual entry errors and maintains data integrity. (Source E 2025-26.) [L0218.022]
  • Ask the AI CFO system questions directly rather than external tools such as ChatGPT. The Claude instance has full context of your financial structure, transaction history and business decisions, enabling more accurate and specific answers. (Source E 2025-26.) [L0218.021]
  • Use Excel instead of Google Sheets for Stage 2 (Claude Code forecasting). Excel has better integration with VS Code, superior context window handling, and formatting precision that Google Sheets cannot yet match. (Source E 2025-26.) [L0218.011]
  • In the Claude Cowork interface (the source reads Cord/Claude Cowork), click "Work in a folder" so that all output files are automatically organized in a single project folder, which persists across sessions. (Source E 2025-26.) [L0218.026]
  • After the AI builds the P&L, require a summary with the following (Source E 2025-26), then review for accuracy before using it in forecasts: [L0218.007]
    1. Total revenue per month (actual vs forecast).
    2. Total expenses per month.
    3. Net profit per month.
    4. List of rows added that were not in the template.
    5. List of rows removed due to no data.
    6. Any transactions the AI remained uncertain about.
  • If Claude Cowork formulas break during the first P&L generation, reinforce formula-building requirements explicitly in the prompt and regenerate. The AI will correct this and ensure every total row contains functional SUM formulas. (Source E 2025-26.) [L0218.027]
  • For any bulk financial model change in Claude Code (Source E 2025-26): [L0218.018]
    1. Create a PLAN.md file outlining the proposed changes.
    2. Wait for explicit "Approved" confirmation from the user.
    3. Execute only after approval.

Annual financial examples

  • Fictional but realistic example (older advice, Source B 2021-22): a US-style summer seasonal outdoor dropshipping store with 2020 total income of about 1.55 million dollars (sales 1,826,410, less discounts of 34,197 and returns and cancellations of 237,813). Cost of goods sold was about 1.3 million, made up of product cost 1,151,214, outbound shipping 98,131 and merchant fees 46,733. Gross profit was 258,322, which the course presents as about 14 percent (see the margin note above for the 16.6 percent figure on total income). Operating expenses were 143,694, including advertising of about 62,000 and freelancers of about 18,000, leaving operating income of 114,627. Card rewards of 23,514 brought net income to 138,142, about 9 percent.
    • Monthly pattern: sales were low in January and February, with a January net loss of almost 10,000. Revenue jumped by about 100,000 in April, reached 110,000 to 115,000 in May and peaked at 287,000 in June. It then fell to about 164,000 in July, rose to about 220,000 in August, dropped to 85,000 to 86,000 in September, recovered to 100,000 to 107,000 in October, fell to 67,000 in November and was about 98,000 in December.
    • In the fall the store paid suppliers for earlier orders faster than new sales came in, producing a gross profit deficit of about 32,000 in one fall month.
    • Year-end balance sheet: cash 31,631, total assets about 60,834, and liabilities of 149,000, made up of credit card A 20,359, PPP loan 3,056, credit card B 84,402 and a long-term bank loan of 41,184. Liabilities were more than twice assets. Operating cash flow was about 159,290.
    • The diagnosis was that profitability, not sales, was the main problem. The planned fixes were higher-margin suppliers, supplier pricing and shipping renegotiation, discount limits, price tests and reduced marketing focus. [L1046.019]
