19.1 Why numbers come first: profit, cash and knowing your business

Why numbers come first

Knowing your numbers is a precondition for growth

  • Accurate financial data is the basis of growth. Without it you cannot see the causes behind your results, so growth stalls at a ceiling you cannot diagnose. (older, 2021-22) [L0449.002, L0599.001, L0632.001]
  • Bookkeeping is not mainly about paying taxes or having records for an audit. Its main value is showing cause and effect, so you can see how each change in the business affects results. (older, 2021-22) [L0449.001, L0599.002]
  • Knowing your numbers means understanding cash flow, profitability, revenue and cause and effect, not just recording expenses. (older, 2021-22) [L0449.006]
  • Budgeting and strategic planning based on your financial numbers can take a business into seven- and eight-figure revenue. They work as a framework on top of marketing and sales, not as a replacement for them. (older, 2021-22) [L0632.003]

Numbers replace guessing

  • Reviewing actual figures replaces emotional guessing about the business's health. It stops self-doubt and anxiety from building up on false assumptions and gives a factual basis for decisions, which brings confidence, stability and stress relief. (older, 2021-22) [L0449.004, L0599.003]
  • Base every campaign decision on strict data analysis rather than emotion. Reacting emotionally to campaign numbers is one of the most costly mistakes in paid traffic. (Source C) [L1024.023]
  • Making ad and store decisions on gut feeling instead of analysing actual ad and sales data is the outdated approach that AI is meant to replace. (Source D 2026) [L1459.018]
  • Early paid traffic gives a new store real visitor data about how customers behave on the site. This lets the operator decide from measured behaviour instead of personal assumptions, since the owner is usually not the typical customer. (older, 2021-22) [L1065.003]
  • Track your own numbers closely, including traffic, sales, margins and conversion, rather than relying on reports from others. Most stores that struggle have figures that are poorly organised. (older, 2021-22) [L0949.029]
  • Track all customer and order metrics, because what gets measured gets actively managed. Unmeasured data invites oversights and failures. (Source A) [L0236.006]

Knowing your numbers early

  • Even if the numbers look worse than you expect, you must know them so you can change something. A decline you do not see keeps running until it surfaces later and hits harder. (older, 2021-22) [L0449.005]
  • Cash flow problems occur both at the current stage and later as the business grows. Regular review of the numbers lets you anticipate them before they become a crisis. (older, 2021-22) [L0449.007]
  • Accurate conversion tracking shows which sources and campaigns generate sales. This lets you make evidence-based decisions about spend allocation, bidding and whether you are hitting profitability targets. (Source B, 2021-22; older) [L0256.001]
  • Three distinct management systems are needed to build a healthy business: bookkeeping to track financial numbers, Profit First to manage cash flow, and strategic planning to achieve scaling. (older, 2021-22) [L0107.008]
  • In the build phase, track four things: pricing and profit margins, profit per order after shipping and fees, a basic profit and loss statement, and a strict separation of business and personal accounts. Check profit per order before scaling any product. (older, 2021-22) [L1148.002]

Revenue, profit and cash flow

Definitions

  • Profit is defined as income minus expenses and is also called net income. It is not the cash that lands in the bank account, so a profit figure should never be compared against the bank balance. (older, 2021-22) [L0484.001]

  • Profit = revenue minus expenses, where expenses include cost of goods, ad spend, refunds, chargebacks and operating costs. Only the cash left after all of these is actually money you keep. (older, 2021-22) [L1458.006]

    Profit = Revenue − (cost of goods + ad spend + refunds + chargebacks + operating costs)

  • Net sales equal total sales minus returns, and this net figure is the one to track. In a Shopify-style total sales breakdown, returns are subtracted from gross total sales. Example: $236,080.79 total sales minus $2,877.60 returns gives $233,203.19 net sales. (older, 2021-22) [L1458.014]

  • Profit cannot exist without revenue, so revenue is a necessary input. The useful question is which revenue actually produces profit after all costs, not whether revenue should be pursued at all. (older, 2021-22) [L1458.016]

  • Revenue is not profit. To find profit, subtract all variable costs (cost of goods, shipping and customer acquisition cost) and all fixed costs from total sales. (Source A) [L0759.014]

  • E-commerce profitability depends on selling each item for more than its full landed cost, so the margin must be known before scaling. Profit per order is the basic number to understand at any stage of the business. (older, 2021-22) [L1246.001]

Net profit versus revenue

  • Judge every sale by its net profit, not its gross value. Missing a few sales a month can cost $1,000 or $1,500 in net profit. (Source A, 2020; older) [L0468.169]
  • Judge a store by net profit after all expenses, including product cost, shipping, marketing and overheads, not by gross sales. Gross sales can look far larger than what the owner keeps. (older, 2021-22) [L0771.043]
  • Net profit matters more than revenue. A store with lower revenue but equal profit avoids much of the work of fulfilment, bulk buying and customer service, so high revenue figures alone are not a sign of a healthy business. (Source C) [L0819.005]
  • Net profit, not revenue, is the figure to track. It pays bills, funds growth and can become a second income stream that makes a job optional. Monitor cost of goods, shipping cost, customer acquisition cost and software costs every month to see how net profit moves. (Source A) [L0759.026, L0759.034]
  • Revenue alone is a misleading success metric for an e-commerce store. Profit and cash flow should drive decisions about growth, since gross sales can rise while the business loses money. (older, 2021-22) [L1458.001]
  • Revenue alone can mislead. A business can see revenue fall while profitability, meaning profit relative to revenue, rises. Track profit margin alongside top-line sales and compare profit and margin before judging performance. (older, 2021-22) [L0449.003, L0599.004]
  • Revenue growth alone does not prove a business is profitable. A store can keep making sales while losing money. Check profit and cash flow separately from top-line sales when judging whether growth is healthy. (older, 2021-22) [L0889.002]
  • Lower revenue with higher margins can be easier to run than higher revenue with thin margins. It means fewer orders, less customer service and less fulfilment work for the same or better profit. Margin per order is therefore a stronger target than sales volume. (older, 2021-22) [L1458.007]

Cash flow versus profit

  • Net profit on the P&L does not show how much cash is available. Money can look like profit while it is already committed to supplier invoices, taxes or refunds, so cash must be tracked separately from profit. (older, 2021-22) [L1200.001]
  • Profit on the P&L and cash in the bank are different measures and can diverge for a growing e-commerce store. Track cash flow separately from profit, and treat any gap between the two as something to investigate. (older, 2021-22) [L1516.018]
  • Cash is what keeps a business alive, so cash flow timing matters as much as the profit figure. A store can be profitable on paper and still fail if money arrives too late to pay suppliers and ad platforms. (older, 2021-22) [L1458.002]
  • Dropshipping is a cash flow business. A store can show a healthy paper profit while running short of cash after paying suppliers, so know your real profit and loss before scaling. (Source C) [L1213.021]
  • Cash flow can destroy a business even when profit looks healthy on paper. Misreading cash flow is a common way owners get into serious trouble. (older, 2021-22) [L0632.002]
  • Cash flow and profit are the core measures in the grow phase, not revenue. Review profit and cash position in every reporting period, not only top-line sales. (older, 2021-22) [L1458.011]
  • Profit is the easier figure to set as a growth goal and for most people to understand. Cash flow determines whether suppliers and ad platforms can be paid on time. Set the headline target in profit and monitor cash flow in the same report rather than choosing one. (older, 2021-22) [L1458.015]

Cost structure and the monthly profit calculation

Variable and fixed costs:

Cost type Behaviour Examples and notes
Variable Occurs only when a sale happens or when you choose to spend. With zero sales, cost of goods and shipping are zero. Cost of goods, shipping, customer acquisition cost [L0759.015]
Fixed Due every month regardless of sales. Platform subscription, domain [L0759.015]
Customer acquisition cost (CAC) Counted as variable because you choose whether to spend it, but it must be spent to keep sales coming in. Stopping ad spend usually stops new sales. (Source A) [L0759.020]
Domain About 12 USD per year, roughly 1 USD per month as a fixed cost. (Source A) [L0759.013]
Virtual assistant or paid software Add to the expense list once you use them, for example a virtual assistant at 400 USD per month. Beginners often don't need them at first. (Source A) [L0759.022]
  • A fixed monthly platform fee becomes a smaller cost per sale as volume grows. For example, 29 USD spread over one sale is 29 USD each, but over 10 sales it is 2.90 USD each. (Source A) [L0759.021]

Monthly profit steps (Source A) [L0759.024]:

  1. Add up the total sales revenue for the month.
  2. Subtract the cost of goods (COGS) for every product sold.
  3. Subtract the shipping fees paid to suppliers for every order.
  4. Subtract customer acquisition cost (the ad spend).
  5. Subtract fixed costs such as the Shopify subscription and the domain.
  6. Subtract card processing fees (0.30 USD per transaction plus 2.9% of sales).
  7. The remaining amount is the net profit for the month.

Formula (Source A) [L0759.025]:

Net profit = Revenue − (COGS + shipping + customer acquisition cost) − fixed costs − card processing fees
Card processing fees = number of transactions × 0.30 USD + 2.9% of total sales

Worked example for one 1,000 USD sale in a month (Source A) [L0759.023]:

Item USD
Sale 1,000
COGS 400
Shipping 100
Customer acquisition cost 100
Shopify 29
Domain (approx.) 1
Total spending 630
Net profit 370

The example leaves out card processing fees.

Common errors:

  • The per-unit fulfilment figure of 600 USD does not match its components of 400 USD COGS plus 100 USD shipping, which is 500 USD. At 500 USD per unit, 50 units cost 25,000 USD, and profit before ad spend and card fees is 24,970 USD, not 19,970 USD. (Source A) [L0759.028]
  • Profit figures that leave out customer acquisition cost and card processing fees overstate real profit. Subtract both before judging how a month went. (Source A) [L0759.029]

Judging a store: success metrics and optimisation metrics

Which numbers decide success

  • Success metrics are the final source of truth for whether a business is on track. Optimisation metrics show whether you are moving toward a goal and where you can improve, so they support the success metrics rather than replace them. (older, 2021-22) [L0400.002]
  • For an e-commerce business, the two core success metrics are profit per sale and cost per sale. These two numbers are enough to judge whether the business is succeeding. (older, 2021-22) [L0400.003]
  • Judge the business by profit, not by the conversion rate alone. A store can have a low conversion rate and high profitability, or a high conversion rate and no profit. (older, 2021-22) [L0400.005]
  • A high conversion rate does not automatically mean profit. Average order value and returning customer rate often grow revenue with less effort than squeezing the conversion rate further. (Source A) [L0390.015]
  • A high conversion rate does not guarantee profit. Also track average order value, returning customer rate, customer acquisition cost and customer lifetime value. A store can convert well and still lose money if these numbers are weak. (Source A) [L0423.015]

Vanity metrics

  • Vanity metrics such as raw revenue, video views or subscriber counts can mislead. Focus on profit, paid installations and numbers that reflect what you actually keep. (Source C) [L0378.083]
  • Publicly shared revenue screenshots rarely show costs, refunds, chargebacks or cost of goods, so they say nothing about real profitability. Judging your own business by someone else's gross sales is a trap. (older, 2021-22) [L1458.003]
  • A benchmark claimed for high-ticket dropshipping is an annual revenue run rate of $10 million or more for some stores. This is the instructor's stated target and was not independently verified, so treat it as an upper ambition rather than a planning figure. (older, 2021-22) [L1014.016]

Early reading targets

  • After about three months a beginner should read these numbers without help: CTR, CPM and CPC in Meta Business Manager, and Add to Cart, Initiate Checkout and Sales in the Shopify dashboard. These numbers show which decision to take next. (Source C) [L1017.002]

Store-level example cases

  • A Shopify store had about $720,000 in online store revenue from 366 orders in one month, with a conversion rate of 0.56%. Total sales across all channels were about $868,000, with roughly 60 to 80 orders from other channels such as Amazon and draft orders. The store normally converts about 0.4% of its traffic, so this month was above its usual rate. (older, 2021-22) [L0400.006]
  • Case: a high-ticket dropshipping store run by a small team with very low overheads. In one month it generated about $720,000 in online store revenue, with a conversion rate of 0.56% against a usual rate of about 0.4% of traffic. Profit margin on those sales was about 12.5%, which came to close to $100,000 profit that month, using roughly $14,000 in ad spend. The result was judged a very successful business despite the low conversion rate and thin margin. (older, 2021-22) [L0400.008]
    • Note: 12.5% of $720,000 is $90,000, so "close to $100,000" does not follow exactly from these two figures. Check the underlying numbers before quoting it.
  • Infrared sauna store, July (example year 2021): sales were 227,949.55 USD from 134 orders and 23,382 sessions. Returns were 23,721.80 USD, so gross sales were 251,671.35 USD. Net profit after all expenses was 50,148.90 USD, a 22 percent margin. (older, 2021-22) [L0771.047]
  • Single-day result from a hardtop gazebo store (July 15, example year 2021): 9 orders produced 23,175.20 USD in sales, with 100 USD returns. The store needed only to forward the 9 orders to the supplier. Profit after all expenses was 4,171.50 USD, an 18 percent margin, mostly from marketing spend that day. (older, 2021-22) [L0771.048]
  • Air purifier store, about 12 months old (July example): 137 orders produced 65,170 USD in net sales and 69,607.60 USD in gross sales before returns of 4,437.60 USD. The margin was 19 percent with 12,336.68 USD net profit. The owner needed about one to two hours per day to manage it. (older, 2021-22) [L0771.049]

Monthly review in the Shopify report

  • For a monthly review, read Total sales, Returns and Sessions in the Shopify Online Store report. Compare Total sales minus Returns with the net profit after expenses. Visitors are shown next to sessions to check conversion. (older, 2021-22) [L0771.044]

Red flags of hidden losses

  • A store can look successful on sales while barely breaking even or losing money once all expenses are counted. Review the full cost picture regularly so you do not discover this only during an emergency. (older, 2021-22) [L0599.005]
  • A store can spend $1 million on ads to generate $1 million in revenue and still lose everything if it then owes about $500,000 in cost of goods that it cannot cover. Check ad spend and product cost against revenue before scaling. (older, 2021-22) [L1458.004]
  • Spending $100 on ads without any add-to-cart actions or inbound interest is a red flag. Use it to optimise the campaigns and the site rather than to close the store. (Source A, 2019; older) [L1068.072]
  • Plan for losses in early months. A store may be negative for months while testing, but profitability should be reached through data-driven tests. (Source C) [L1184.057]
  • Missing a decline is the main risk. Know the numbers so that a falling trend is spotted in the period it starts. (older, 2021-22) [L0449.005]

Revenue drop case

  • In 2018 the speaker's store revenue declined and he panicked, aiming to win back sales. Later analysis showed he had been more profitable on the lower revenue, and his lower revenue meant less work. The lesson: check profit per period before reacting to a revenue drop. (older, 2021-22) [L1458.008]

Chair store case

  • An online chair store had a record year, then sales fell sharply starting in January of the following year and kept falling for months. By May the owner had lost confidence and feared the business was failing. The owner was advised to sell the store before it reached zero, since a buyer would still pay something for it. Selling required four years of complete financial records, but the owner's accountant's reports were hard to understand and seemed inconsistent, so the owner reviewed the figures personally. The review showed that revenue was declining but profit was rising on lower revenue, so the business was healthier than the owner believed. The store was then listed on a brokerage marketplace and sales picked up within weeks of the listing. (older, 2021-22) [L0449.014]

Owner finances

Check the numbers yourself

  • Accepting accountant reports you do not understand is risky, especially when something looks off and you cannot get clear answers. Learn to read the financial statements yourself so you can verify what you are told. (older, 2021-22) [L0599.006]
  • If your accountant's financial reports are not understandable to you, they are not supporting your decisions. Ask for plain-language explanations, and change accountant if they cannot give answers you can act on. (older, 2021-22) [L0449.008]

Recurring financial questions

Check these questions regularly and answer each one with numbers (older, 2021-22) [L0632.005]:

  1. Are my products profitable?
  2. Are my sales profitable?
  3. Where is my money going? If my P&L shows profit, where did that profit go?
  4. Is there more cash in my bank account than expected, and is that money actually available?
  5. How much do I owe my suppliers?
  6. How much can I afford to pay a virtual assistant, and can I afford three or ten?
  7. Can I afford a store manager or a PPC agency?

Monthly and period review

Review each period in this order (older, 2021-22) [L1458.013]:

  1. Review the store's profit and loss report for the period.
  2. Check the cash position and when money arrives versus when it must be paid out (supplier costs, ad spend, refunds, chargebacks).
  3. Compare profit and cash flow with the previous period rather than comparing revenue alone.
  4. Decide on scaling or cost cuts only after profit and cash flow have been checked.

Owner's personal finances

  • Describe the owner's personal financial situation and personal income separately from the business figures. This shows what the business is meant to provide personally. (older, 2021-22) [L0439.014]
  • Business revenue and business profit are never passive income. The goal is to generate cash flow from the business and invest it in assets that produce passive income. (older, 2021-22) [L0439.024]

Early-stage expectations

  • Profitability within the first month of operation is a realistic early expectation for a high-ticket dropshipping store. Earning a few months' extra profit is a shorter goal, while reaching millions in annual revenue may take a couple of years. (older, 2021-22) [L0438.012]
  • Example profitability curve from a Shopify store (Source C): month one earned $4,000 in revenue with a loss of $2,000; month two earned $6,000 with a loss of $600. Profitability was targeted about two months after launch. [L1184.054]
  • A beginner plan can pair the 3,000 USD profit goal over about two months with operational targets of 100 live products and 40 unique visitors per day. (Source A) [L0826.003]
  • Set a target net margin and adjust it over time. Example: aim for a 20% net margin in the first three months, then raise the target to 25% for the next three months. (Source A, 2020; older) [L1111.150]
  • Milestone targets for this roadmap are first to scale beyond $10k in monthly revenue, then to aim for six-figure income. (Source D 2026) [L0914.019]

Note on timing: the sources differ on when a store should reach profitability. The 2021-22 high-ticket course says the first month is realistic (older). The Source C example targets about two months after launch. The Source A beginner plan uses about two months for its 3,000 USD profit goal. Use the first-month figure only for high-ticket stores with very low overheads, and plan for about two months otherwise. [L0438.012, L1184.054, L0826.003]

Growth goals as measurable numbers

Set the vision figures

  • Set the 10-year vision figures for annual revenue, profit per year and profit margin. These are the main business-level targets to put in the vision. (older, 2021-22) [L0439.013]
  • The 90-day revenue and profit targets and other measurables are calculated backward from the one-year plan. Example: a one-year revenue target of $1,000,000 can translate into a 90-day revenue target of $300,000. (older, 2021-22) [L0679.022]
  • In the example, a one-year revenue target of $1,000,000 is broken into a 90-day revenue target of $300,000, which is 30 percent of the annual figure. The 90-day profit and any other measurables are then calculated backward from the same one-year plan. (older, 2021-22) [L0679.032]

Goals and rocks tracker, sheet layout (Google Sheets) (older, 2021-22) [L0679.027]:

Section Fields Notes
10 Year Picture Date, Revenue, Profit, Measurables, Paint The Picture Long-term vision
3 Year Picture Revenue, Profit, Measurables, Paint The Picture Fed from the 10-year picture
1 Year Plan Revenue, Profit, Measurables, Paint The Picture Target for the year
90 Day Rocks Revenue, Profit, Measurables, Up to 7 rocks Next 90 days, rebuilt every quarter
Barrier Issues Free text column Obstacles to each goal

In the example, the one-year revenue target is $1,000,000 and the 90-day revenue target is $300,000. [L0679.027]

Make measurables concrete

  • Measurables should be defined as concrete numbers and not left vague. For example, a 1-million-dollar revenue target with a 300,000-dollar profit goal should be translated into specific customer counts and per-order figures. (older, 2021-22) [L1519.015]
  • Estimate the number of customers needed by dividing the revenue target by the price of the product sold. Example: 1 million dollars in revenue at a product price of 1,000 dollars needs 1,000 customers, or about 750 customers if repeat business accounts for part of the revenue (a 25% repeat share in that example). (older, 2021-22) [L1519.014]
  • Growth must stay profitable. Sustainable growth in an active store means building a clear understanding of financial numbers, including bookkeeping, profit and cash flow, before and during expansion. The aim is to grow without losing profitability. (older, 2021-22) [L0889.003]

Beginner versus advanced finance path

Which module to take

  • Every member should complete at least one of the two finance modules, regardless of stage, because the finance content is not optional. Which module to start with depends on whether bookkeeping is already in place. (older, 2021-22) [L0545.012]
  • The beginner finance module is for owners who are brand new to business or who have just launched but do not yet have a solid bookkeeping method. It must be studied as soon as the business launches. (older, 2021-22) [L0545.013]
  • The advanced finance module is for owners who already run a basic bookkeeping system that tracks profit, losses, invoices, sales and costs. It focuses on using financial numbers to grow the business profitably. (older, 2021-22) [L0545.014]
  • The Know Your Numbers Advance module is aimed at businesses that already have a live store with sales and want to grow. It is more advanced than the earlier Know Your Numbers material, which is the Build Phase module 7 in the Source B program for high-ticket dropshipping. (older, 2021-22) [L0889.004]

What the beginner module covers

  • The beginner finance module has 15 lessons. Among them are an introduction to knowing your numbers, a lesson called What You Need Now, profit per order, generating a profit and loss statement, and when to level up. (older, 2021-22) [L0545.020]
  • The beginner finance module covers calculating profit per order, generating a profit and loss statement, and recognising when to level up. These basics are needed before scaling any high-ticket dropshipping business. (older, 2021-22) [L0545.015]
