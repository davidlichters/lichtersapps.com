19.16 Profit tracking systems: per-order sheets, daily P&L trackers and profit apps

Why profit tracking is required

  • Always track profit with either a Shopify app or a spreadsheet. Without profit visibility you cannot scale effectively, and you risk going negative without knowing it. [L0037.010]
  • Automate profit tracking once the store runs, so margin is measured per order rather than estimated from totals. [L0532.023]
  • Spreadsheets are hard to keep accurate for profit tracking because every payment and cost source must be connected. Profit apps automate this by pulling data from payment providers. [L1213.024]
  • Track profit for each individual order, not only total revenue, so that losing products, pricing problems and winning products become visible. A simple spreadsheet is enough while order volume is low, for example a few orders per day. [L1202.001]
  • Track the net profit of every sale in a profit spreadsheet, so decisions about which products to scale or cut are based on real per-sale results. [L0970.149]
  • A clear view of gross spend and net return is essential once sales start. Without it, a seller cannot tell whether a product is making money or losing it. This matters most when a product is being tested and when a store moves into the scaling phase. [L0678.006]

Choosing a tracking system

  • Older (2021-22): Review the current financial tracking system and check whether it gives a clear view of the business. If it does not work or leaves room for improvement, compare it with a proven tracking system and implement that one if it fits the business. [L0441.012]
  • Older (2021): Google Sheets is enough for margin tracking at the start. Accounting software such as Xero can come later. [L0962.111]
  • Older (2020): The December 2020 coaching call lists Wholesale Cost Tracking as a resource for tracking supplier (wholesale) costs. The lesson names it without describing its setup. [L0426.007]
  • 2023: A profit tracking spreadsheet is enough for a new store's bookkeeping. It costs nothing and tracks revenue, cost of goods, shipping, ad spend and profit. [L0855.033]
  • 2023: Use a profit tracking spreadsheet to track business profit. Xero is the accounting platform used for the business. Google Sheets was used for years before that and supported over $10 million in sales. [L0835.121]
  • 2024: Use Excel for product-level profit tracking to see which products to scale or drop. The team later moved accounting to Xero, which is not day-to-day work. [L0971.067]
  • Shopify's own analytics shows all store reports and activity. When Shopify Payments is used, payments and finances are also visible in the same admin. [L0934.082]
  • Profit apps are the alternative to spreadsheets; see the profit apps section below. [L0037.010, L1213.024]

Per-order profit sheets (Google Sheets)

  • Module 7.9 (Profit tracking) calculates profit margin on every individual order, not just blended profit across all sales combined. Apply it retroactively to all January and February orders. [L0208.015]
  • Older (2022): Module 7 includes a downloadable Profit Tracking Worksheet, used to track ROI indicators, monitor profitability by product and campaign, and make data-driven scaling decisions. [L0249.080]
  • Older (2020): Track sales price, COGS, shipping cost and profit per order in separate columns, and calculate profit margin order by order. [L0268.039]
  • Older (2020), procedure:
    1. Track profit per order in Google Sheets with columns: order date, sale price, COGS, shipping, total profit. [L0268.041]
    2. As order volume increases, assign this tracking to a virtual assistant. [L0268.041]
    3. Periodically review overall business profit. Take distributions based on monthly or weekly profit, not on a per-order basis. [L0268.041]
  • Older (2022), procedure:
    1. Build a sheet with one row per order. [L1281.095]
    2. Record the sale price, product cost, merchant fees, shipping and ad cost. [L1281.095]
    3. Compare profit per order to see which products make real money and which need a higher price or removal. [L1281.095]

Setting up the order sheet

  • Setup procedure:
    1. Open the template link and choose File > Make a Copy. Keep the default name "Copy of" and click OK so the file is editable in your own Google Drive. [L1202.002]
    2. Right-click the tab labelled Duplicate this PNL and choose Duplicate to keep an untouched backup of the formulas. [L1202.002]
    3. Right-click the working tab and choose Rename to give it your own name. [L1202.002]
    4. Enter all order data only in the working tab and keep the backup tab as the reset copy. [L1202.002]
  • Keep the blank template tab "DUPLICATE THIS - P&L" unchanged and copy it for each new tracking period or store, so the column structure stays the same across all reports. [L0394.006]
  • Column structure of the order-level profit sheet, tab "DUPLICATE THIS - P&L", with one example row. [L0394.001]
Order # Date Net Profit Net Margin Supplier Product QTY Customer Paid Promotion Applied COGS Shipping Tax Collected Merchant Fees
1200.0 36526.0 307.7 0.3077 Supplier ABC Product 12345 1.0 1000.0 10% Off 500.0 100.0 63.0 29.3
  • The Date column stores a spreadsheet date serial (36526 = 2000-01-01 in Excel), so format it as a date before reading it. [L0394.001]
  • Net profit per order = Customer Paid − COGS − Shipping − Tax Collected − Merchant Fees. Example: 1000 − 500 − 100 − 63 − 29.3 = 307.7. [L0394.002]
  • Net margin per order = Net Profit ÷ Customer Paid. Example: 307.7 ÷ 1000 = 0.3077, i.e. 30.77%. [L0394.003]
  • Tax collected from the customer must be deducted from order revenue, because it is passed on to the tax authority and not kept as income. Leaving it out overstates net profit and margin on every sale that carries sales tax or VAT. [L0394.004]
  • Record every order on its own row with the supplier, product, quantity and any promotion applied. This shows which suppliers, products and discounts actually produce profit, instead of total revenue only. [L0394.005]

Entering one order

  • Entry procedure:
    1. Replace the example data with the Shopify order number and the date the order was placed. [L1202.003]
    2. Enter the supplier name and the product name. [L1202.003]
    3. Enter Quantity as the number of units the customer bought. [L1202.003]
    4. Enter Customer Paid as the amount shown in the Shopify orders section. [L1202.003]
    5. Enter any promotion used in Promotions Applied. [L1202.003]
    6. Enter COGS from the supplier invoice. [L1202.003]
    7. Enter Shipping and Tax Collected, and leave Merchant Fees to the formula. [L1202.003]
    8. Read Net Profit and Net Margin at the top of the sheet, which update automatically. [L1202.003]
  • Customer Paid must be the amount the customer actually paid as shown in Shopify, not the product list price. If the customer paid for shipping, that amount belongs in Customer Paid, not in the shipping field. [L1202.004]
  • Promotions Applied is only a reference column for spotting trends, such as a 10% off storewide sale. It does not change any calculation. Record each discount so you can later see which promotions were linked to higher or lower profit. [L1202.005]
  • To add more orders: highlight the full row with all order data and formulas, then drag the fill handle at the bottom down to copy the row. The copied rows keep the calculation formulas, so you only overwrite the input cells for each new order. [L1202.012]
  • Ad spend is not entered in the per-order sheet because it varies from order to order. Track it separately and analyse it with return on ad spend (ROAS) alongside the net margin figures. [L1202.019]
  • Older (2021), period-level formulas:
    • Profit per order = sale price − Shopify card processing fee (2.9% for Shopify Payments credit card transactions in the source year 2021) − supplier cost − shipping cost. [L0962.109]
    • Profit after ads and overheads = total profit per period − Google Ads, Facebook Ads and Bing Ads costs − 29 dollars per month for Shopify − other fees. [L0962.109]
  • Older (2021), margin tracking in a spreadsheet:
    1. Create a sheet for each week or month. [L0962.110]
    2. For every sale, enter the sale price, Shopify fees, supplier product price and shipping cost. [L0962.110]
    3. Calculate profit per order. [L0962.110]
    4. Add that period's Google Ads, Facebook Ads, Bing Ads, 29 dollars Shopify fee and other fees. [L0962.110]
    5. Calculate the margin for the period. [L0962.110]

Acting on per-order results

  • Example: After two months with 12 sales, six sales earned $400 net profit each, three earned $200, and three earned $50. The $50 sales should be investigated for too much ad spend or higher shipping cost than expected. Then ad spend can be cut or increased on the products that earn most. [L0970.148]

Order-level tracker from Source B (older, 2021-22)

  • Tool: The Order Profit Tracker is a spreadsheet that calculates net profit and net margin for each order from the sale amount minus supplier cost, shipping, payment processor fees and other costs. It is used to know profit per order, and its instructions include a way to automate the data entry. [L1246.003]
  • Each order is entered with the order amount, any discount, the payment processor fee, sales tax, the supplier total, the cost of products, shipping, other process fees and the amount received. Net profit and net margin are calculated from these entries. [L1246.019]
  • The tracker reports a gross profit margin alongside the net margin. Rely on the net margin for decisions, because it shows what remains after shipping costs and all other order costs are subtracted. [L1246.018]
  • During the early build phase, calculate the profit of every single order, because margins only become clear once real supplier costs, shipping, processor fees and fraud fees are included. [L1201.001]
  • Enter and check the profit of at least the first 5 to 10 orders manually yourself before handing the task to someone else. [L1201.002]
  • Logging procedure for one order in the Order Profit Tracker:
    1. When the store sends a new-sale notification, note the order. [L1201.004]
    2. Place the order with the supplier and wait for the invoice or order confirmation that lists all costs. [L1201.004]
    3. Open the Profit Per Order tab and add a new row with the order number and date. [L1201.004]
    4. Enter the customer name, the product and the quantity. Quantity is for reference only and does not change the calculation. [L1201.004]
    5. Enter the order amount: the total you actually received from the customer. [L1201.004]
    6. Enter the discount you gave. It is recorded for reference; the order amount already reflects what was received. [L1201.004]
    7. Select the payment method so it matches what Shopify fills in automatically. [L1201.004]
    8. Enter the sales tax. Leave it at 0 if no sales tax was charged. [L1201.004]
    9. Enter the supplier total: the product cost plus the shipping cost from the supplier invoice, plus the cost of any shipping you paid yourself through a freight forwarder or UPS on your own account. [L1201.004]
    10. Keep the shipping cost in its own separate cell as a reference note. [L1201.004]
    11. Read the automatically calculated values: processor fees, fraud insurance, total cost, gross profit, net profit and net margin. [L1201.004]
  • For an order split across several suppliers, enter the supplier total as one sum of all invoices, including each separately paid shipping cost, using a typed equation such as =1500+500 (or =1400+100+500 if a 100 shipping cost is paid separately). Example check: 3,000 − 2,000 − 87.30 − 30 = 882.70. [L1201.014]
  • Fraud insurance is entered in the insurance section: Signifyd at 1% of the order amount, with a checkbox column "Insure Checks/Financed Orders?". If you do not use fraud insurance, delete that fee. [L1201.017]
  • Older (2021-22), dropshipping COGS: Recording COGS on an accrual basis for each individual order is effectively impossible for dropshipping. It requires accurate supplier invoices for every order, invoices change over time, and suppliers do not standardize their data. The work is very large, the benefit is small, and the return on investment is negative. [L0484.009]
  • Older (2021-22): Using an order profit tracker to calculate profit on every individual order is not recommended for dropshipping. For the same reason as per-order COGS accrual, supplier invoices change and are hard to keep accurate, so the effort is large while the information gained is small. [L0484.022]
  • Older (2021-22), growth phase: Recording every single order has diminishing returns, because the profit per additional sale of the same item barely changes while the time to collect invoices and costs is large. Weigh tracking time against what it returns. [L1246.004]
  • Older (2021-22), growth-phase procedure:
    1. If you do not yet have a clear profit per product or order, use the tracker for a while without entering every order. [L1246.005]
    2. Spot check periodically to confirm that a product line, brand or best-selling product is still profitable. [L1246.005]
    3. When adding a new supplier or new product, track it for a while until you know how it performs. [L1246.005]
    4. Return to the tracker after supplier price changes or your own price changes to see how things have developed. [L1246.005]
    5. If you are still not confident about profit per order, use the tracker for a week at a time instead of continuously. [L1246.005]

Break-in ROAS and status

  • Break-in RAS is the minimum daily return target. A day only counts as profitable when its ROAS is above that number. The example target is 1.3, so each ad dollar must bring back more than $1.30 in revenue. [L1123.002]
  • Enter your break-in RAS in the Breakeven ROAS cell at the top of the Daily P&L Tracker tab. It is an editable parameter, so replace the example value 1.3 with your own target. [L1123.003]
  • A day with ad spend but no revenue shows red, because a ROAS of zero is below the break-in target. [L1123.005]
  • Cell colors signal status: green means the day is above the target and winning, red means the day is negative and losing, and zero means exact breakeven. [L1123.006]
  • The tracker labels each day with one of three statuses: LOSING (red, negative profit), BREAKEVEN (zero, exact break-even) and WINNING (green, above target). Read the color together with the PROFIT value to see the status. [L1123.021]
  • Judge each day by profit and net margin, not by ROAS alone. A ROAS of 3.0 can still leave a thin margin once COGS and fees are deducted. [L1123.017]

Cell categories and formulas

  • The tracker uses three cell categories: input cells that you fill in each day, AUTO-CALC cells (dark purple) that must not be typed into, and PROFIT/MARGIN cells that are calculated automatically and marked separately. Only the input cells change when you enter daily data. [L1123.022]
  • Dark purple cells are auto-calculated. Never type into them. Fill only the input cells, because overwriting formulas corrupts the calculations. [L1123.007]
  • Some auto-calculated cells pass a value through with an IF formula of this form, so they stay blank until the source input is filled:
=IF(source="","",source)

Do not overwrite these cells, because replacing the formula breaks the link to the input. [L1123.023]

  • ROAS (RAS) = Revenue ÷ Ad Spend. Example: revenue $300 with ad spend $100 gives a ROAS of 3.0. [L1123.010]
  • PROFIT = Revenue − COGS − Ad Spend − Fees. [L1123.011]
  • Net Margin % = PROFIT ÷ Revenue. Example: profit $140 on revenue $300 gives a net margin of 46.67%. [L1123.012]
  • Cost per metric = Ad Spend ÷ count of the event. Cost/ATC = Spend ÷ ATC, Cost/IC = Spend ÷ IC, Cost/Purchase = Spend ÷ Purchases. Example with spend $100, 14 ATC, 10 IC and 6 purchases: $7.14, $10.00 and $16.67. [L1123.013]
  • Worked day-1 example: spend $100, CPC $1.00, CTR 3%, CPM $60, 14 ATC, 10 IC, 6 purchases, revenue $300, COGS $60, ad spend $100. Results: ROAS 3.0 (green, above the 1.3 target), profit $140, net margin 46.67%, cost per ATC $7.14, cost per IC $10.00, cost per purchase $16.67. [L1123.016]
  • Cost per ATC, IC and purchase show where money is lost in the funnel. Compare them day over day: a cost per IC that rises while cost per ATC stays flat points to the checkout step rather than the ad. [L1123.018]

Simple finance sheet for a dropshipping test

  • Procedure, in order: [L678.001]

Why apps and what they miss

  • Profit apps typically capture about 80 to 90% of order costs automatically from connected payment accounts. The remaining share, such as extra returns, disputes, chargebacks and payment-provider charges, must be added manually to get a true margin. [L1213.081]
  • Spreadsheets are hard to keep accurate because every payment and cost source must be connected. Profit apps automate this by pulling data from payment providers. [L1213.024]

Shopify reports and billing

  • Shopify Settings > Billing lists every paid app subscription, what each costs, and when each bill is due. Check it regularly to track recurring app costs. [L1301.013]
  • Paid apps that are no longer used keep billing every cycle. Review the Billing page and uninstall or cancel any app that is no longer needed. [L1301.014]
  • Shopify analytics shows all store reports and activity. With Shopify Payments, payments and finances are also visible in the same admin. [L934.082]

Fixed costs and expenses

  • Shopify monthly subscription costs and app subscriptions are fixed costs and are not included in the per-order profit sheet. The sheet measures the actual profit per sale, so fixed costs must be reviewed separately. [L1202.018]
  • List every recurring cost as an expense with its status and payment frequency, for example the Shopify plan, other paid apps, a VA and fixed company costs. [L477.030, L1297.036]
  • The monthly budget sheet holds fixed costs on their own lines, so the monthly result reflects them. [L550.001, L550.002]
  • A personal-finance style spreadsheet can also show which recurring subscriptions are unused, marked in red as they are entered, so they can be cancelled. [L678.004]

Review cadence

  • Track profit daily at first, then switch to weekly reviews to see the average weekly profit. Weekly data smooths out daily noise and gives a steadier basis for scaling decisions. [L875.024]
  • Weekly tracking gives a clearer view of average profit than single-day numbers. [L445.020]
  • Compare cost per IC and cost per ATC day over day to locate the funnel step where money is lost. [L1123.018]
  • Judge a product on whether its daily profit holds above the threshold across consecutive days, not on the running total. [L1030.033]
  • Periodically review overall business profit. Take distributions based on monthly or weekly profit, not on a per-order basis. [L268.041]
  • Spot check periodically to confirm that a product line, brand or best-selling product is still profitable. [L1246.005]
  • Return to the tracker after supplier price changes or your own price changes to see how things have developed. [L1246.005]

  • Keep a duplicate backup tab with the formulas, and do all entry in the working tab. [L1202.002]
  • Only enter data in the input cells. Dark purple (auto-calculated) cells must not be typed into. [L1123.007, L1123.022]

Template mistakes

  • Typing a fixed number over a calculated cell, such as gross profit, contribution margin or break-even ROAS, breaks the formula chain and makes every later figure wrong. Change the input cells instead and let the calculator recalculate. [L0433.019]
  • Typing values into the colored, automatically calculated cells breaks all calculations in the tracker. Enter data only in the white cells. [L1201.020]
  • Entering the product's list price in Customer Paid instead of the amount the customer actually paid. Discounts and paid shipping then distort the profit result and make the per-order numbers unreliable. [L1202.014]
  • Entering shipping that the customer paid in the shipping column. The shipping column should hold only what you paid out, so customer-paid shipping belongs in Customer Paid. [L1202.015]
  • Leaving Tax Collected empty or treating collected sales tax as revenue. It must be entered and deducted, otherwise net profit is overstated. [L1202.016]
  • Working in the original template tab without keeping a duplicate backup. If the formulas or automations break, a clean duplicate is needed to restore the calculation. [L1202.017]
  • Amounts entered with a currency symbol, such as "$250", are stored as text and are not counted in the totals. [L550.007]
  • A date typed with a wrong year, such as 0203 instead of 2023, places the entry outside the month and breaks any date-based filtering. [L550.008]
  • A log row without a category, like the 100 Facebook Ads entry without a category, will not appear in the category totals of the monthly sheet. [L550.009]
  • The Shopify 1% Fee column header does not match the sample value, which equals 0.5% of revenue. Check the fee rate before relying on the column. [L900.019]
  • A PayPal figure sits next to the profit column but is not part of the profit formula. Confirm whether PayPal fees are already in the payment processor fee column. [L900.020]
  • The total cost of running the business is entered as 400 USD, but the listed monthly expenses add up to 606 USD (605 without the designer value of 1). The expense list and the total must be kept in sync. [L900.021]
  • Entering the wrong cost of goods in BeProfit distorts profit figures. [L477.032]

When per-order tracking stops paying off

Sources disagree on per-order tracking. Each position applies to a different stage and case.

  • Per-order tracking applies while the store is still learning which products make money. A simple spreadsheet is enough while volume is low, for example a few orders per day. [L1202.001]
  • Per-order tracking applies during the early build phase. Calculate the profit of every single order, because margins only become clear once real supplier costs, shipping, processor fees and fraud fees are included. Older source (2021-22). [L1201.001]
  • Once the store is established, tracking each order matters less. Older source (2021-22). [L1201.001]
  • Before handing the task to someone else, enter and check the profit of at least the first 5 to 10 orders yourself. Older source (2021-22). [L1201.002]
  • Per-order tracking has diminishing returns in the growth phase, because the profit per additional sale of the same item barely changes while the time to collect invoices and costs is large. Older source (2021-22). [L1246.004]
  • Per-order COGS accrual is effectively impossible for dropshipping, and the return on investment is negative. Older source (2021-22). [L484.009]
  • Per-order tracking for dropshipping is not recommended, because supplier invoices change. Older source (2021-22). [L484.022]
  • When volume grows, assign the per-order tracking to a virtual assistant, and review overall profit periodically. Older source (2020). [L268.041]
  • Automate profit tracking once the store runs, so margin is measured per order rather than estimated from totals. [L532.023]
  • Per-sale tracking is the rule in the 2023 source: track the net profit of every sale in a spreadsheet. [L970.149]
  • Adding a new supplier or product: track it for a while until you know how it performs. Older source (2021-22). [L1246.005]
  • If you are still not confident about profit per order, use the tracker for a week at a time instead of continuously. Older source (2021-22). [L1246.005]
  • Working rule that reconciles the positions: while product profitability is still unknown or changing, track per order; once products and supplier costs are stable, switch to periodic spot checks and weekly or monthly reviews. [L1202.001, L1246.004, L1246.005, L268.041]

  4. Add the inventory source: choose the dropshipping provider (CJ Dropshipping, AliExpress or Auto DS can be connected automatically to fetch cost prices), or select Other for manual entry.
  5. Select the fulfillment provider, or choose Other.
  6. Enter the shipping prices and product cost per item (example: cost per item $5, handling cost $0).
  7. Click Next and connect any external payment gateway such as Stripe or PayPal.
  8. Shopify plan fees are fetched automatically; click Finish and start with the free plan to see gross profit and costs on the dashboard. [L0875.022]

  • B Profit is a Shopify app that automatically tracks profit, KPIs and profit margins, and shows whether the seller is at loss or profitable. Automatic cost fetching is possible for CJ Dropshipping, AliExpress and Auto DS. It can be used for free with a start plan. [L0875.023]

  • Track profit daily at first, then switch to weekly reviews to see the average weekly profit. Weekly data smooths out daily noise and gives a steadier basis for scaling decisions. [L0875.024]
