20.8 Onboarding and training new team members

Onboarding principles and the owner's role

  • Onboarding is the key step with new hires and virtual assistants. Show the work, set expectations, check progress, and revisit the role from time to time, because a good resume does not guarantee delivery from day one. (Older: 2021.) [L0801.153]
  • Expect to be closely involved in the first one to two weeks of a new hire. Answer their questions, review their work and give feedback until they work the way you want. [L1431.023]
  • Before delegating a recurring task, spend substantial time at the start showing the assistant exactly how you want it done. This upfront training is what later lets a short instruction, such as switching an offer to buy one get one free, be carried out without follow-up. [L1432.040]
  • Prepare a new hire to succeed by setting up boards and processes before they start, so they are not overwhelmed. Tested templates and clear procedures let new staff work well from the first day. Practise the routine with the new hire for about two weeks before they work alone. (Older: 2021-22.) [L1396.056]
  • Incomplete training blocks a person from starting their assigned work. Depending on how soon you need them productive, training may be urgent and a top priority. (Older: 2021-22.) [L0265.016]

Before the first day

Onboarding SOP

  • Create a separate onboarding SOP for every new hire. It explains what the business does, how the website works, which brands are sold and who the customers are. Do not assume an overseas VA or freelancer understands the business model, such as high-ticket dropshipping, unless you explain it. (Older: 2021-22.) [L1339.014, L1339.016]
  • The onboarding SOP can be a simple recorded video of the owner explaining what the website does and how it works, which brands are sold, and who the customers are. (Older: 2021-22.) [L1339.016]
  • The onboarding SOP should explain how the business works, what the team looks like and how the new hire can contact you. Task-specific SOPs go alongside it, and the new hire's first action item is to work through them. (Older: 2021-22.) [L1054.007]
  • An onboarding SOP makes a new hire feel part of a real, legitimate business. This encourages loyalty and higher-quality work, especially for remote contractors who can feel isolated. It can also include any onboarding tasks every new person must complete on joining. (Older: 2021-22.) [L1339.015]
  • If an existing training course covers the task, give the new hire access to it together with the SOP. This can cut a lot of detail from your own SOP and save time. (Older: 2021-22.) [L1054.008]

Onboarding questionnaire

Steps for the first contact (older: 2021-22):

  1. Provide your onboarding SOP and any SOPs or training related to the task. [L1054.006]
  2. Send an onboarding questionnaire to collect the new hire's details. [L1054.006]
  3. Once they have gone through the SOPs and/or training, hold a meeting to set expectations, get to know them better and make sure they understand the task(s). [L1054.006]

Content and format:

  • Use a standard form with the same fields for everyone you hire. Collect payment details, contact details, an emergency contact, a phone number, and their account for a messaging app such as WhatsApp or Viber, which are common in the Philippines. (Older: 2021-22.) [L1054.009]
  • Collect several contact channels, not just one. Natural disasters such as earthquakes, typhoons or landslides can cut power and internet and make the usual channel unavailable. (Older: 2021-22.) [L1054.010]
  • Google Forms can serve as a standardised onboarding questionnaire. All responses are stored in one place, so you can look up details such as a bank account for TransferWise or a PayPal email without searching old chat messages. (Older: 2021-22.) [L1054.011]

First day and first contact

Standard onboarding tasks

  1. Prepare 5-7 standard onboarding tasks in Asana, such as creating an email, accessing live chat, submitting payment info and completing the profile. [L0263.018]
  2. For each new hire, copy and paste these tasks into a new Asana project. [L0263.018]
  3. The new employee completes them on day 1-2. [L0263.018]
  4. The tasks prevent first-day confusion and make the person productive immediately. [L0263.018]
  5. Without clear tasks, new hires sit idle and lose momentum. [L0263.018]

Onboarding call

  • Hold the onboarding call on day 1 or 2. Give a brief overview of the company, provide technical training, and explain team structure and reporting. This kickstarts the relationship and ensures the person understands the context. [L0263.023]
  • In the get-to-know call with a new outsourced hire, ask about their family, where they live and what they enjoy outside work. This makes the working relationship personal, builds trust and supports long-term retention. (Older: 2021-22.) [L1054.043]

End-of-trial meeting

  • Confirm pay, working hours and communication expectations, and check the new hire is still happy with them. Then get to know them personally, answer questions about the SOPs and training, and send them back to work to see how they perform. (Older: 2021-22.) [L1054.013]

Training plans

Standard VA training procedure

  1. Teach company details: about us, products, and shipping and returns policy. [L0230.018]
  2. Document and review all SOPs. [L0230.018]
  3. Provide a workspace overview and record a video walkthrough. [L0230.018]
  4. Create a shared Google Doc for training notes and questions. [L0230.018]
  5. Show training videos from apps such as Asana, Shopify and Reamaze. [L0230.018]
  6. Record custom training videos with free software. [L0230.018]
  7. Use learning-by-doing with real tasks. [L0230.018]
  8. Explain the reasoning behind each process. [L0230.018]

Workspace and learning-by-doing training (up to two weeks)

  • Initial training for a new VA lasts up to two weeks and ends earlier if practical checkpoints are passed. During the training period, both the new VA and an existing VA training a replacement receive half pay. [L1473.022]
  1. Have the new VA read about the company, its products, and its shipping and return policies. [L1473.023]
  2. Teach the standard operating procedures. [L1473.023]
  3. Give the new VA a login to the shared Chrome workspace. [L1473.023]
  4. Provide a short video that explains what each bookmark and dashboard is for and how to use it. [L1473.023]
  5. Link to vendor help-centre videos, for example from Shopify and Asana, for general tool training. [L1473.023]
  6. For procedures without training material, record a screen-share video with free software (QuickTime on a Mac) and upload it to the shared drive. [L1473.023]
  7. Use learning by doing: first demonstrate a task, then let the VA do it themselves. When a mistake happens, explain why it is wrong and the logic behind company policy and culture. [L1473.023]

Staged rollout by channel

  1. Start with email only. Process each incoming email together on a call, and explain why each reply is written and who it goes to. [L1000.039]
  2. Once emails run smoothly, introduce phone calls. [L1000.039]
  3. After phone calls, introduce live chat and other tasks. [L1000.039]
  4. Give more responsibility gradually. Do not expect to leave the business unattended right after hiring. [L1000.039]

Three-step procedure for a new VA (Source C)

  1. Invest time at the beginning to show exactly what you expect. Keep expectations low during the first week. [L0765.012]
  2. Be approachable and open to questions. Define all dos and don'ts for the business, including what authority the assistant has and which important policies must be followed. [L0765.012]
  3. Control the work: set deadlines, check completed work, give feedback, and agree on a reporting rhythm (daily, weekly or monthly). [L0765.012]

Two-week plan (older)

(Older: 2021-22.) Use this plan to train a new virtual assistant:

  1. Introduce the company using the corporate information board. [L1396.055]
  2. Have them follow the morning routine with you every day for two weeks. [L1396.055]
  3. Create one draft order yourself, then have them create one while you watch. [L1396.055]
  4. Have them watch the training videos twice. [L1396.055]
  5. Arrange a one-hour training call with a manufacturer for product knowledge. [L1396.055]
  6. Have them write collection page content to learn the products. [L1396.055]
  7. Role-play difficult customer calls. [L1396.055]
  8. Send mystery shoppers to chat or call them to practise answering. [L1396.055]
  9. Monitor their chats and send private corrections during the first months. [L1396.055]
  • Ask manufacturers for product training with a set meeting, because they are often willing to share their expertise. One manufacturer offered a one-hour Zoom training per product category once a week for five weeks, which the owner used to train staff directly. Schedule the session in advance so the expert is prepared. (Older: 2021-22.) [L1396.057]

Skill transfer for a single task

Results

  • Without a structured handover, the trained person often reaches only about 60-70% of the original efficiency. With the skill-transfer process, the target is to keep it at about 95-98%. [L1359.021]

Five-step process

  1. Explain: Give a high-level walkthrough of the SOP, covering why the task matters, what the steps are and what the result should be. Do not demonstrate yet. [L1359.022]
  2. Demonstrate: Perform the whole task yourself, narrating each step, so the trainee sees it done correctly the first time. [L1359.022]
  3. Practise: Have the trainee go through the steps one by one while you sit beside them and correct each step right away. Depending on complexity, this takes 30 minutes to an hour, or several days. [L1359.022]
  4. Observe: Let the trainee complete the entire process alone without interrupting. If they get stuck, finish the step with them before moving on to the next one. [L1359.022]
  5. Feedback: Have the trainee explain what they do at each step and why. This shows whether they understood it and how comfortable they felt with the training. Use that feedback for the next handover. [L1359.022]

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the explain step means the trainee does not understand why a step matters, so they follow it badly or skip it. Even simple tasks need the bigger picture. [L1359.023]
  • If the trainer has never done the process, the demonstration step falls apart and the trainee learns the wrong way. Perform the process yourself first. [L1359.024]

Taking over a task (older)

(Older: 2021.) Use this procedure to train a new hire to take over a task:

  1. Work alongside the new person through the day, covering the tasks that are too hard to put in an SOP. [L0469.107]
  2. Once they are comfortable, have them do the task while you watch. [L0469.107]
  3. Then have them do the task alone while you check in from time to time. [L0469.107]
  • Plan on about a month of doing two jobs while training. (Older: 2021.) [L0469.107]

Example case

  • A candidate from Nigeria with little e-commerce experience but a good attitude and strong writing skills was hired at 200 USD per month. She received one month of training with video lectures, recorded sessions and onboarding. She then took on influencer outreach, research and several other tasks, and her pay was raised to 500 USD per month. [L1431.021]

Customer service and fulfilment hires

(Older: 2020.) Use this procedure when a store outgrows the owner's time and a customer service or fulfilment hire is needed:

  1. Document every customer service and fulfilment step for each supplier and each scenario before the hire starts. [L1277.119]
  2. Set up an open communication channel with the new person, such as Slack or Facebook Messenger. [L1277.119]
  3. Expect a stream of questions for the first few weeks, possibly up to a month, because the hire will not know every answer yet. [L1277.119]
  4. Keep answering those questions until the process runs smoothly without you. [L1277.119]

Scripts and customer reply guidelines

Scripting replies

  1. Work intensively with the new VA for the first few months. [L1437.025]
  2. Script every customer email you would write yourself, and have the VA copy the scripts into a spreadsheet. [L1437.025]
  3. Once the VA drafts emails on their own, edit them and let them see your changes, so they learn where you adjust. [L1437.025]
  4. Adapt wording to the local market. For example, use "mum" instead of "mom" for Australian customers, and check other region-specific terms. [L1437.025]

Reviewing replies

  1. Set aside about 10 minutes per day in the first one to two weeks. [L1431.024]
  2. Open the customer emails that the assistant has sent. [L1431.024]
  3. Check how each reply is worded and whether it fits how you want customers to be addressed. [L1431.024]
  4. Give feedback on anything that does not match, and confirm it is corrected. [L1431.024]

Writing for other countries

  • When hiring for a different country, do not rely on machine translation for customer messages. Write a guideline for each typical situation in the customer's language and politeness level, because a literal translation can sound disrespectful. [L1431.025]

Authority, access and control

Authority and policies

  • Define all dos and don'ts for the business, including what authority the assistant has and which important policies must be followed. [L0765.012]

Access during training

  • Give the new VA a login to the shared workspace and a video explaining each bookmark and dashboard. [L1473.023]
  • Record screen-share videos for procedures without training material and store them on the shared drive. [L1473.023]

Ongoing control and reporting

  • Set deadlines, check completed work, give feedback, and agree on the reporting rhythm: daily, weekly or monthly. [L0765.012]
