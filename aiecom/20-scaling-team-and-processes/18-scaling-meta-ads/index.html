20.18 Scaling Meta ads

Scaling directions and principles

  • Horizontal scaling means finding more audiences: while a product stays profitable, launch 2 to 3 new interests every day, keep testing for as many audiences as possible that work, and reuse them with new creatives. Turn off underperforming ad sets. [L0375.013]
  • Vertical scaling means raising the budget of existing campaigns or ad sets, or duplicating them. Use either method or both to scale aggressively. [L0375.018]
  • Test whether raising budgets or duplicating works better in your own account, because the best method depends on the account. [L0375.015]
  • Budget increase methods differ per ad account. Test doubling the budget, a 25 percent increase, or duplicating the ad set on one campaign first, and roll a method out to other campaigns only if it works there. [L0645.010]
  • Every ad account behaves differently, so test everything instead of copying what worked for another account. Test a structure with a small budget before committing to it. [L0376.024]
  • There is no single strategy that works for every product or ad account. Keep testing broad and interest approaches and scale whichever one proves profitable. [L1161.030]
  • No single formula fits every account. Choose the structure based on what is profitable, and keep the cost per acquisition below your break-even. [L1195.048]
  • Keep scaling campaigns running for the best audiences and creatives, using CBOs where they work. If CBOs do not work in the account, use ABO campaigns that only scale the best audiences and creatives. [L0375.016, L0376.023]
  • Always keep scaling campaigns running. Use CBO for scaling the best audiences with the best creatives, and switch to an ABO campaign dedicated to scaling if CBO does not work for your account. [L0376.023]
  • The testing campaign is a starting structure. Once purchases show which interests and creatives win, move to CBO or other campaign types to scale. [L0647.034]
  • Scale a winning product only once the pixel shows real purchase data. Until then, keep testing. [L1195.046]

Readiness to scale: proof thresholds

Sources disagree on the sales threshold. Use the values below according to the situation:

Source value Threshold Context
Interest or ad set in ABO testing At least 3 purchases with ROAS above break-even Starting a CBO scaling campaign with a $100 budget [L0645.009]
Ad set with consistent sales At least 4 purchases Raising the budget by 20 percent every 24 hours [L0375.014, L0376.017]
Interest proven profitable At least 6 profitable sales Choosing a scaling method [L0639.014]
Audience or ad set About 6 to 7 sales; 5 is too few Most common rule, applied before scaling [L0646.001, L0692.019, L0773.056]
Winning interest About 7 sales Raising its budget or starting a new $50 CBO without interests [L0692.031]
  • Before scaling a Meta ad set, wait for roughly six to seven profitable sales in a consistent pattern. Four or five sales is too little data. A single unusually strong day can distort the picture, so look for steady sales across several days. [L0646.001]
  • Consider scaling an audience only after it has about 6 to 7 sales, since five sales are too few to be confident. Early on, use a small budget rather than a high one. [L0692.019]
  • Do not scale a Facebook ad set until it delivers more than six or seven sales. Before that point, keep collecting data at the current budget. [L0773.056]
  • Once a winning interest reaches about seven sales, you can raise its budget, or start a new $50 CBO without interests to test further. Both options should follow the same ROAS and sales thresholds. [L0692.031]
  • A campaign needs a minimum daily spend of about 10 or 50 pounds per day to be easily scalable. Below that level, scaling is considered difficult. [L0646.003]
  • Once the pixel has 1,000 or more purchases, you can also target a broad audience with no interests. [L0376.025]
  • Do not scale an audience with a ROAS below 1.7 when the break-even ROAS is 1.5. A higher ROAS gives more confidence, and 1.7 is the minimum for scaling. [L0692.020]
  • Advertisers disagree on how far ROAS should sit above break-even before scaling. Some use break-even plus 0.3 to 0.5. A more conservative rule with a break-even ROAS of 1.5 requires a minimum of 1.7, about 0.2 above. Use the margin your own account supports. [L0692.048, L0692.020, L0375.022]
  • Set your own benchmark ROAS and adjust the formula to it, because a 20 percent margin is not every seller's target. [L0375.022]
  • Test any new scaling method on only one winning campaign first. Do not test on your sole profitable campaign. [L0298.014]
  • At low budgets, around $10 per asset, daily sales are very unstable. Even ad sets at about $300 can swing from a 2 ROAS to under 1, though higher budgets tend to stabilize results somewhat. [L1107.009]
  • Scaling is not a day-one activity: segment and scale by profit only after the account has data and standard optimizations are done. [L0224.002]

Testing structure and creative flow

  • Testing campaign structure:
    1. Interest-testing campaign: take winning creatives and test new interests.
    2. Creative-testing campaign: take the winning interest and test new creative variations.
    3. Once winners are identified, combine all winning creatives into one audience targeting for scaling. [L0067.040]
  • Start each new product with $5 per ad set across five to six ad sets, about $25 to $30 per day. Once a test ad set shows a good CTR and consistent sales, choose how to scale: raise the budget on the winning ad sets, or add spend to test ad sets so the winning audience is found faster. [L1184.070]
  • Testing procedure for a new audience:
    1. Test the audience for two to three days.
    2. Remove creatives that perform badly in a consistent way.
    3. Wait until the audience has 6 to 7 sales.
    4. Confirm a ROAS of at least 1.7 when break-even ROAS is 1.5.
    5. Start with a CBO budget of about $50.
    6. Once it is proven, scale using the three-campaign structure below. [L0692.027]
  • A CBO campaign with a daily budget of about $50 is an acceptable starting budget for testing a scaling candidate. [L0692.021]
  • Do not double a budget abruptly. A product that converted above 3 percent in testing dropped to zero add-to-carts the day after the budget was doubled. [L0692.026]
  • Move the winning creative into its own campaign, then duplicate the same creative into multiple ad sets to scale it:
    1. Find the winning creative among the test ad sets.
    2. Move that creative into its own campaign.
    3. Duplicate the same creative into multiple ad sets to scale it. [L1194.017]
  • Relying on a single ad to scale is risky, even if that ad has good metrics. Build more ad variants so the campaign does not depend on one creative. [L1107.005]

CBO and ABO

  • ABO is used for testing interests and CBO for scaling in many accounts. CBO does not work for every account, and ABO can perform better, so the right choice depends on testing the setup. [L0349.020]
  • CBO scaling setup: put a proven interest into a CBO campaign with a budget of 100 to 200 USD. Include 4 to 5 ad sets with the same audience, with about four creatives in each. [L0648.044]
  • Conflict on creative count per CBO ad set. Use about four creatives per ad set in a proven CBO scaling campaign [L0648.044]. Use about three creatives per ad set when a campaign starts with 4 to 5 ad sets [L1107.015]. Use three creatives per ad set in the three-campaign structure [L0692.028]. Use two to three creatives rather than one when building the first scaling CBO [L0645.009].
  • In a CBO, Meta assigns the budget to the ad sets that are reaching the best pockets of the audience, then searches for similar people to grow those pockets. This is why CBO works for scaling once there is a budget of about 100 to 200 USD. [L0648.045]
  • A campaign-level budget (CBO) moves money toward the best-performing ad sets, but it can sometimes allocate spend to a weaker one based on early engagement. Review the allocation regularly and turn off the weak ad sets after enough data has accumulated. [L0639.015]
  • A high-budget CBO does not spend evenly across creatives. On one day one creative gets most of the budget, and on another day a different one does. A scaling campaign therefore needs several working creatives, not one. [L1107.014]
  • Before building a scaling CBO, keep ads that work in other test ad sets even if they fail in one ad set. Remove an ad from the CBO only if it performs badly everywhere, and keep looking for more good creatives. [L1107.013]
  • A CBO scaling campaign starts with 4 to 5 ad sets. Each ad set contains about 3 creatives. [L1107.015]
  • If CBO does not work in an account, scale with ABO instead. Duplicate the winning ad set three times at 100 USD each in one ABO campaign, or create three separate ABO scaling campaigns with 100 USD each. [L0648.046]
  • CBO campaigns with several ads do not work for every account. In the case described, they gave zero sales and rising CPA compared with testing (testing CPA about $10 to $50, CBO CPA about $20). Switching off underperforming ads inside a CBO can disrupt the optimization, so ABO was preferred for scaling in this case. [L0995.026]
  • Move a winning product from an ABO test into its own CBO:
    1. After the ABO test, shortlist the products whose ad sets show strong results.
    2. For each winning product, create a new CBO campaign with three creatives.
    3. Wait until the new CBO is live and running.
    4. Only then turn off the individual ABO ad set for that product. [L0348.034]
  • Once the best cost cap is found, move it into its own individual CBO campaign. Use that cap across other winning audiences and broad campaigns as you scale. [L0977.024]
  • Create three CBO campaigns using the winning interest, then increase budgets there when the data supports it. [L1161.029]

Horizontal scaling procedures

  • Horizontal scaling procedure for a winning ad set:
    1. Move the winning ad set into its own scaling campaign.
    2. Duplicate it, for example 10 times, as ABO campaigns with about $20 in each.
    3. After a short spend, switch off the copies that have no sales; for example, kill an ad after a day without sales.
    4. Keep the copies that perform well and scale them further. [L0995.025]
  • Horizontal scaling means duplicating a winning ad set into a new campaign, often about 10 copies, then keeping the best performer and turning off the rest. It is used when the ROAS stays profitable but no longer supports a large budget jump. [L0646.007]
  • Budget testing with a small ad set: run three copies at 10, 15 and 20 units of budget to find the best budget for the ad, then scale the winner horizontally by duplicating it again. [L0646.008]
  • You can duplicate your top 3 to 5 winning audiences or interests into a CBO campaign with a $150 to $200 budget. Use this when a single ad set's budget increases stop working. [L0376.018, L0375.014]
  • Once a campaign is unprofitable, check it at ad set and ad level first. A single audience or creative can drag down the whole campaign, so kill that bad ad set or creative. [L0375.023]
  • Keeping underperforming ad sets running alongside the winners drains budget, so turn them off consistently. [L0375.033]
  • If an interest or ad set stops working across several days, switch it off and try something else. An interest that stopped can sometimes work again after days or weeks, so a stop does not mean it is dead for good. [L1107.010]

[L0375.021, L0376.016]

  • Scaling an ad set with a 20 percent step: when an ad set consistently produces sales and has at least 4 purchases, raise its budget by 20 percent of the initial budget every 24 hours, as long as it performs 20 percent above BE ROAS. Check whether budget increases or duplication work better for your account. [L0375.014, L0376.017]
  • Scaling by a 20 percent step on a moderate ad: when an ad has only a moderate result but is still profitable, duplicate it at a 20 percent budget step. Run it a few days, and if it holds, raise the budget again by 20 percent. This avoids risking large budgets on an ad whose ROAS may drop. [L0646.009]
  • Scaling a profitable Advantage+ campaign:
    1. Start with a budget such as $100.
    2. Raise the budget by 20% every two to three days while the campaign stays profitable.
    3. If results drop after a few days, return to the previous budget level.
    4. If the campaign is not profitable after about a week, close it and scale a winning interest instead. [L1161.028]
  • Scaling a winning Facebook ad with a slow, step-by-step increase:
    1. Keep the winning ad in an ABO campaign with its own budget.
    2. Raise the budget by about $20 on the same ad, since it already has engagement and Facebook has learned from it.
    3. Run it for a few days.
    4. If the ad holds, add another $20 after about three more days.
    5. Repeat and raise the budget slowly while the return stays acceptable.
    6. Once the ad is strong at a higher budget (e.g. $100), test it against a more aggressive budget, then move to $200 and $400 if it holds. [L0995.024]
  • Scaling an ad that has proven itself:
    1. Keep the original ad set running; do not touch it.
    2. Duplicate the ad set three times.
    3. Put more budget into each copy, for example three ad sets at about $40 per day.
    4. Test under the bigger budgets and see which one holds. [L1315.022]
  • Scale a CBO campaign by raising the budget by 25 percent. If profit margin falls after a 25 percent increase in budget, reduce the budget again (descale). [L0348.013]
  • Scaling by a fixed step on a CBO, with a 100-dollar starting point:
    1. Find an interest in ABO testing that gives at least 3 purchases with a ROAS above break-even.
    2. Launch a CBO campaign with a $100 budget.
    3. Create three duplicate ad sets, each with the same winning interest.
    4. Add the winning ads (creatives) to the ad sets, using two to three creatives rather than one.
    5. If the campaign runs for about 12 hours with a ROAS of 3 or more and at least 3 purchases (2 purchases can be enough), raise the budget by $100, for example from $100 to $200.
    6. Do not raise the budget again on the same day. Check the next day and repeat the +$100 step (to $300, then $400) only while the ROAS holds.
    7. If the CBO is not profitable on day one, cut it. If it is profitable on day one but not on day two, let it run to day three; cut it if day three is also unprofitable.
    8. Keep testing new interests with 15 to 20 percent of the budget so the next winners can be moved into CBO. [L0645.009]
  • Aggressive scaling method: double the CBO budget repeatedly until reaching 500 dollars; thereafter increase budget by only 20% per adjustment. [L0298.008]
  • Budget scaling rule: increase daily ad spend by a maximum of 2x (e.g., $50 per day to $100-150 per day), and only after observing 2 to 3 weeks of consistent profitability. Aggressive scaling without proven consistency leads to losses. [L0321.028]
  • When results are strong, scale aggressively by nearly doubling the budget, for example from 50 to 80 pounds per day, and keep it if performance holds for a few days before the next step. Sustained performance at the higher level then justifies moving to the next budget step, such as 100 pounds per day. [L0646.004]
  • Return on ad spend typically drops sharply after the first aggressive budget increase, then falls by smaller amounts with each further increase. The largest drop comes from the initial jump, so a strategy must allow for that first decline. [L0646.005]
  • Aggressive scaling is safe only if the ROAS after the jump stays about 0.8 above break-even. If it does, the budget can be doubled again and the ad set moved into its own scaling campaign. [L0646.006]
  • A Meta campaign that performs well can be scaled in steps, for example from about $100 per day to $500 per day, and later to the thousands or tens of thousands per day. Each step should follow only a stable result with a strong audience and targeting. Jumping directly to high budgets is a common way to trigger restrictions. [L0637.062]
  • Scaling a test from 5 dollars per day to 100 dollars per day in one jump led to an ad account block. Scale in gradual steps after the product shows profit. [L1131.045]
  • Do not double a budget on one day, and do not copy a 300 dollar CBO to a new 300 dollar campaign. Neither carries a guarantee, and either can break a working setup. Increase in steps across days and watch the ad account's response before duplicating. [L0645.047]
  • Do not change every campaign at once when scaling, because a bad budget change can hurt all campaigns in the account. Apply a change to one CBO campaign, watch the results, and then extend it to others. [L0645.011]
  • Scale a winning campaign by duplicating it at a higher budget only when ROAS is well above break-even. If results are only acceptable, raise the budget of the existing campaign instead. [L0692.029]
  • Example: a $50 CBO campaign with a strong ROAS can be duplicated at $100 while the original stays at $50, bringing total daily spend to $150. If the $100 copy fails, retry the duplicate at $50 or increase the original budget by 20 percent. [L0692.030]
  • On Facebook, smaller daily ad set budgets of roughly £10 to £30 often deliver better and cheaper impressions than large budgets. Scale across several small ad sets first, and raise budgets in steps rather than doubling a single ad set, because big jumps make delivery search harder. [L1426.048]
  • Short event campaigns such as Black Friday or Christmas may be scaled up and down quickly, but evergreen campaigns need a steady 7-day window before any budget change. [L1016.060]
  • Facebook budget steps for a campaign started at $50 per day:
    1. Start the Facebook campaign at $50 per day and make sure the creatives and page are solid.
    2. Let it run for 7 days.
    3. If all target metrics are hitting, raise the budget by 15%.
    4. Let it breathe for about 3 days.
    5. If it still performs well, raise the budget another 15% (a 15-20% step stays safe for the learning phase).
    6. If traffic arrives but there are no sales after 7 days, turn the ads off and refine the website before spending again. [L1016.057]

Duplication methods

  • Triple duplication for a CBO with a $300 total start:
    1. Launch three campaigns with the same ad set at the same budget each.
    2. Start with a total of about $300, for example $100 per campaign, and check that setup is correct.
    3. Optionally start at $200 per scaling campaign.
    4. Add an automatic rule that turns a campaign off when it spends its break-even cost per purchase without producing a sale.
    5. If it fails, relaunch after a couple of weeks at a different budget. [L0349.018]
  • Triple duplication with high budgets, using 200 USD per CBO as the example:
    1. Launch three identical CBO campaigns with the same ad sets and creatives, each on a high budget (the example used 200 USD per CBO).
    2. Let all three run until about 10 to 20 USD of spend.
    3. Turn off any CBO with very high CPCs and no sales.
    4. If one CBO has a sale in this phase, keep it running and turn off the other two.
    5. If no CBO has a sale after about 15 to 20 USD of spend (the break-even), turn off the weakest one and let the other two spend another 15 USD.
    6. If one of the two still has no sale after that, turn off both. [L0648.047]
  • Use triple duplication only after roughly two to three months, once the product works and the budget is high. For the start, a single CBO campaign is enough. [L0648.048]
  • Audience overlap above 15 to 20 percent is poor. More than three duplicated CBOs on the same audiences is likely to add competition with yourself, raise CPM and waste spend, so treat it as an untested risk. [L0648.049]
  • Duplicate the ad set five times into a CBO campaign, creating three CBOs, if the budget increase steps stop working. Keep the one that performs, close the others, then scale the budget of the winning CBO. [L0773.032]
  • Three-campaign scaling structure:
    1. For each winning interest, create one CBO campaign with four ad sets.
    2. Put three creatives in each ad set.
    3. Duplicate that campaign so you have three campaigns.
    4. Launch all three together.
    5. Set an automatic rule that turns off campaigns with no sale after roughly eight to twelve hours or after spend equal to the break-even cost per purchase.
    6. Keep the best-performing campaign and turn off the other two. [L0692.028]
  • Duplicating with one variable changed, used when budget changes and duplication do not work:
    1. Duplicate the ad set 3 to 4 times at the same budget.
    2. Change one thing per ad set, keeping the interest the same. For example, use men only or women only (depending on the product).
    3. Narrow the ad set to the best country.
    4. Narrow the ad set by age.
    5. Narrow the ad set to the best placement and platform. [L0375.024, L0376.019]
  • Ad set variants for the one-variable method:
    1. Ad set 1: men only or women only (depends on the product).
    2. Ad set 2: narrow down to the best country.
    3. Ad set 3: narrow down the age range.
    4. Ad set 4: narrow down to the best placement and platform. [L0375.024]
  • Duplicating an ad set in Ads Manager:
    1. Open the campaign, ad set or ad that should be reused.
    2. Press Duplicate and then Duplicate again to create a copy inside the same level.
    3. For a new ad, rename the copy named copy and swap in the new creative.
    4. For a new ad set, duplicate the ad set and then change the name and the ads.
    5. For a new campaign, duplicate the campaign because it carries over all ad sets and ads inside it. [L0865.055]
  • Copying ads between ad sets:
    1. Select the ads to copy with a click on the first one and Shift-click on the last one.
    2. Press Command C on a Mac to copy them.
    3. Open the target ad set and press Command V to paste the ads.
    4. While the pasted ads are still selected, remove the word copy at the end of their names.
    5. Use Edit > Find and replace, type dash copy in the find field, leave the replace field empty, press Replace and then save the draft. [L0865.056]
  • Budget testing and duplication: a case where the first sales were about 1,700 in revenue (currency not stated). The budget was raised three times within 10 days, and each time Facebook cut it back the next day, while support staff could not explain the cap. Horizontal scaling with separate interest-based ad sets was used as a workaround. [L0644.016]

Automated rules and loss control

  • For campaigns with a 100,000+ dollar budget, implement automated rules to prevent losses at each scaling stage. [L0298.007]
  • Set an automatic rule that switches off a scaling campaign once its spend reaches the break-even cost per purchase without a single sale. Automatic rules matter because campaigns may launch overnight and run unattended. [L0349.019]
  • Set an automatic rule that turns off campaigns with no sale after roughly eight to twelve hours, or after spend equal to the break-even cost per purchase. [L0692.028]
  • Losses on individual days are normal under about $1,000 daily spend, so a profitable day can absorb one or two losing days. Consistent results usually appear once a campaign has spent above $500, and a bigger budget can show inconsistent CBO days that the other campaigns offset. [L0645.046]
  • Review the allocation of a campaign regularly and turn off the weak ad sets after enough data has accumulated. [L0639.015]
  • Scaling a winning interest: confirm the interest has at least 6 profitable sales, then choose a scaling method:
    1. Raise the ad set budget (example: from $20 to $40 per day), or duplicate the winning interest into a CBO campaign.
    2. For a CBO duplication, use a $100 campaign budget with the winning interest copied 4 times, so each copy reaches a different pocket.
    3. Test both methods, since one may work better than the other in a given account.
    4. Check which ad sets receive budget and cut underperformers once you have enough data. [L0639.014]

Self-scaling, events and refresh

  • Use self-scaling (automated budget increases every 2 to 4 hours) only during specific high-intent events: Halloween, BFCM, Cyber Monday, or with very aggressive offers (60% off vs. typical 40%). [L0298.012]
  • Self-scaling works during events because people have high purchase intent and expect sales. During normal days, frequent budget increases hurt performance. [L0298.013]
  • Winning Facebook campaigns often fade after about one to two months. Plan to refresh by changing the creative, the hook or the audience, or by duplicating into a new campaign before results drop. [L1213.034]

Creative testing allocation and budget split

  • Allocate 20% of daily ad budget to creative testing. If the daily budget is 500 dollars, dedicate 100 dollars to testing. [L0298.047]
  • Keep spending 15 to 20 percent of the budget on testing new audiences and creatives, even for products that have worked for months. Keep a separate audience testing sandbox and creative testing sandbox to do this. [L0648.051]
  • Split a daily ad budget into 20% for testing, 10% for retargeting and 70% for existing winning audiences and scaling campaigns. Example: on $100 per day, allocate $20 to testing, $10 to retargeting and $70 to scaling. [L1213.030]
  • Keep testing new interests with 15 to 20 percent of the budget so the next winners can be moved into CBO. [L0645.009]
  • Testing across formats, such as bottom-of-funnel, UGC, natives and whitelist pages, maximizes the chance that something works. A format that fails still gives information, and a winner in one format is still a win. [L1476.054]
  • Aggressive scaling to very high monthly revenue needs winning audiences, winning creatives, strong email and SMS marketing, and Google Ads in place. Without these assets, scaling spend usually causes losses. [L1213.035]

Advantage+ and campaign build

  • Advantage+ campaign setup under the Sales objective:
    1. Click Create and choose the Sales objective.
    2. Keep Buying type on Auction and click Continue.
    3. Enter the campaign name with product, budget type, creative type and date or year.
    4. Under Budget, choose Advantage+ campaign budget (CBO) and enter the daily budget.
    5. Set Campaign bid strategy to Highest volume or value.
    6. Name the ad set after its creative.
    7. Under Conversion, set Conversion location to Website, Performance goal to Maximize number of conversions, Dataset to the default pixel and Conversion event to Purchase.
    8. Under Show more settings, open Attribution setting, choose Standard attribution and set the selections to 7-day click only, with engaged-view and view-through set to None.
    9. Check the start date, end date and time zone.
    10. Set ad set spending limits only if needed.
    11. Under Audience, keep Broad, 18 to 65+ and all genders, and keep Advantage+ audience on.
    12. Keep Placements on Automatic with Advantage+ placements on.
    13. Name the ad.
    14. Under Identity, select the Facebook Page and check the Instagram account.
    15. Under Ad setup, choose Create ad, Manual upload and Single image or video.
    16. Enter the destination URL to a collection or product page and keep Use shop default off.
    17. Under Creative, leave Source URL empty, turn off the catalog if it is not optimized and crop the vertical 9:16 version.
    18. Enter primary text, a headline if wanted and choose Shop Now.
    19. Skip Meta AI options and turn off Enhancements except Show products when the catalog is optimized.
    20. Set URL parameters, check the preview, then press Publish. [L0865.054]
  • There is no single strategy that works for every product or ad account. Keep testing broad and interest approaches and scale whichever one proves profitable. [L1161.030]

Bottom-of-funnel scaling

  • BOF has a hard scaling ceiling due to a limited warm audience size. For solution-aware products, BOF remains profitable indefinitely but cannot scale beyond the fixed daily impression supply. BOF harvests warm conversions rather than generating the volume needed for $1M+ monthly revenue. [L0315.013]
  • Attempting to scale BOF to large monthly revenue targets is a mistake. The warm audience pool is inherently small, imposing a strict ceiling on impressions and spend. Though BOF delivers high ROAS on available inventory, the limited scale makes it unsuitable as a primary growth driver. [L0315.014]
  • Introduce new bottom-of-funnel concepts weekly (e.g., new testimonials, new benefits angles, new social proof). Rotate fatigued ad sets out. Do not constantly refresh if your video campaign is working well, because the inflating warm audience keeps ROAS high even on existing ads. [L0316.019]
  • Scale bottom-of-funnel budget only until ROAS drops to your comfort threshold. If your breakeven ROAS is 1.5X, scale until you reach 2.5X ROAS, then hold budget steady. Do not overscale. Monitor ROAS and reduce if it drops below your target. [L0316.020]
  • Set bottom-of-funnel budget at roughly 30% of what your video ads are spending. This is a guideline, not a fixed rule. Adjust based on ROAS and available inventory. If your video campaign is still strong, the warm pool keeps inflating, and you can maintain steady bottom-of-funnel budget without constant increases. [L0316.021]
  • Adding new ads is better than reducing budget. If an account is underperforming, introduce new ad concepts and variations to give the algorithm more material to work with, rather than cutting spend. Reducing spend only shrinks the business; growing requires adding new creative constantly. [L0316.036]

Targeting, demographics and ceilings

  • Choose a single winning gender and age from the breakdown rather than mixing all demographics. For a product aimed at both genders, check whether men or women perform better and focus budget on the stronger group. [L0773.028]
  • Facebook groups audience targeting at the ad account level, so ads in different campaigns can still reach the same people. Separate campaigns per format are still worth it to control budget and scale each format on its own. [L1476.037]
  • Every ad has a scaling ceiling where spend stops being profitable. Example from the lesson: one ad can take about $2,000 a day, another dies at about $100 a day, images may cap near $200 a day, and strong videos can scale to about $3,000 a day. Separate formats let each one run at its own ceiling. [L1476.038]
  • Facebook's campaign budget optimization shifts budget by percentage and can push about 95 percent of spend into one favored ad, whatever the total budget. Putting different formats into one campaign lets a favored video starve the other ads. [L1476.039]
  • If an ad in a campaign gets no spend, move it to its own separate campaign to give it a fair test. The point of a new campaign is to isolate the variable. [L1476.040]
  • The copy-analysis method for finding winning ads is most useful once you are scaling. Apply it when you are moving beyond testing. [L0772.035]
  • Scale a CBO campaign with a low-volume test before extending it: a budget step only follows a stable result with a strong audience and targeting. [L0637.062]

Team, time and staffing

  • After switching to the Power Five approach, a whole team needs about 5 hours per week for Facebook ads, down from more than 50 hours per week with three people. [L0649.005]
  • If lead generation is not running yet, focus first on hot dynamic, hot static, customers goodwill and promotions. Lead generation comes after these are working. [L0649.051]
  • Quick wins come first: set up dynamic remarketing with varied ads, add a short video ad for hot traffic, run a goodwill video, promote to warm audiences and match email promotions. Lead magnets, opt-in pages and designed assets can wait. [L0649.052]
  • At the business-owner stage, a trained virtual assistant can set up and manage Facebook ad campaigns while the founder reviews them, analyzes results and makes the final spending and offer decisions. Hire an ad manager only if they are trained on your account or show a strong CV with past client results. [L1432.039]

Segmentation by profit margin

  • Segment alpha-beta campaigns by profit margin only after campaigns have generated enough historical data to identify which products drive the most sales with the highest profit margins. This requires completing all standard optimizations (negative keywords, site conversion optimization) and is not a day-one activity for new stores. [L0224.002]
