Owner role and team growth

  • Older advice (2021-22): Growing the team multiplies positive impact by improving team members' lives through meaningful work, enhancing customer service, and creating broader community benefit. [L0107.007]
  • Older advice (2021-22): Sustainable growth comes from repeatedly identifying and reducing weaknesses over time, not from a single fix. [L0443.007]
  • Older advice (2021-22): Assign someone on the team to monitor the online spaces your customers use. They are a steady source of pain points, desires and market research for your avatars. [L0907.018]
  • Running a store passively through a virtual assistant is not permanent. The assistant may quit or stop answering, and competitors may add bundles or redesign their sites, so the store must keep being monitored and improved. [L0826.017]
  • Case: a dropshipping store started in 2014 made its first sale within a few weeks of opening, by Christmas. [L1473.036]
    • It ran as a side business while the owner worked as a bookkeeper, and it helped pay off about 70,000 in debt built up with a husband from buying a house, in just under two years. [L1473.036]
    • After the owner quit the bookkeeping job, the store tripled its revenue in the first full-time year. [L1473.036]
    • The owner still worked about 50 hours a week on customer service, orders and fires, and only removed themselves from business operations in 2018, about four years after starting. [L1473.036]
    • The turning point was a pregnancy in 2017. The owner built the business system and found a virtual assistant within about three months. [L1473.036]
    • Training lasted two weeks, after which customer service and order management were handed over. The owner later could travel for months while the business ran, working on the business rather than in it. [L1473.036]
    • The owner later hired a second assistant and raised pay after one year. [L1473.036]

Delegation and done-for-you services

  • Done-for-you services are listed at the bottom of the members area page and can be booked at any time. [L1496.014]
    • Most of them carry an extra fee and are optional. [L1496.014]
    • They suit those who want faster progress or who dislike tasks such as website design, product uploads or market verification. [L1496.014]
  • Done-for-you store building typically takes two to five business days, depending on team workload. Ask for the team's current turnaround before committing. [L0806.095]

Working setup and tools

  • Use a screen split or external monitor for supplier and product research so you do not switch between windows constantly, which saves time. [L0665.110]
  • Do not open a normal chat or create a new Cowork task without first selecting the project. Work started outside the project does not get the project's files, instructions or memory. [L1506.013]
  • Downloaded course files belong in one dedicated folder inside the project location. You can then drag them into a chat to install skills or to reference them. [L1506.014]

Business identity and accounts

  • Use the exact same email address for the Google Merchant Center account, Google Analytics, Google Ads and the other Google products. This lets Google link them and confirm domain ownership automatically. [L0728.001]
  • Set the same time zone across all Google products so data shows consistently everywhere. [L0728.001]
  • Older advice (2021): Use an existing store's customer support email, at the store's own domain, for business email. Avoid email from another business domain. [L0834.107]

Store build and technical checks

  • Older advice (2021-22): When implementing image optimization, start with the high-priority pages and products you want to rank most, then gradually work through lower-priority pages. This staged approach makes the task manageable without needing to optimize everything at once. [L0301.031]
  • Older advice (2021-22): Clicking through every link on a page by hand is too time consuming on pages with dozens of outgoing links. Use a link checker extension or outsource the scan instead. [L0332.022]
  • Build single-product stores, add relatable products at the bottom of the page, and use upsells in the cart as a way to grow average order value. [L0577.064]

Offers and average order value

  • Quantity discounts (buy one, buy two, buy three) can outperform a complex bundle for a first test. [L0773.041]
    • They are simple to set up. [L0773.041]
    • They lift average order value without needing to pick related products. [L0773.041]
  • Older advice (2021-22): Consistency drives upsell results. Keep offering the upsells across all three channels every time, rather than trying once and stopping. [L1409.027]
  • Older advice (2021-22): Upsell action items: [L1409.029]
    1. Decide which on-site upsell positions to use (product page, cart page, post-purchase).
    2. If no post-purchase upsell exists yet, add one; keep existing product-page upsells and add later upsells in the sequence.
    3. Add an upsell email sequence for each core product from the planner list into your email system.
    4. Customize the email templates with your specific products.
    5. Pass the script and instructions to the customer service team and discuss the planner list with them, so they offer the related upsell when a customer calls about a core product.

Product research and validation

  • Find someone already achieving the results you want, then model their process instead of reinventing it. Starting from a proven model can compress years of trial and error into a short period. [L0523.044]
    • Test and improve the model only after it makes money. [L0523.044]
  • Product review workflow used by the team: [L0530.027]
    1. Watch the TikTok video and judge whether it is good.
    2. Read the comments for buying intent.
    3. Check the seller's account for upload dates and posting consistency.
    4. Open the store on mobile and desktop and review structure, product page and payment options.
    5. Estimate cost and selling price.
    6. Score the product and decide whether to test it.
  • Spending more does not beat a competitor's higher budget if your targeting is sharper. [L0664.066]
    • Older advice (2020): A focused store with a few thousand dollars a month can outperform a competitor spending tens of thousands by cutting weak products. [L0664.066]
  • Roughly 80% of sales often come from about 20% of products. Focus effort on identifying and adding the few suppliers whose products drive most of the revenue. [L0835.125]
  • Validation and profit timing:
    • A store that passes all validation tests can usually become profitable. [L0806.092]
    • How large it grows (hundreds of thousands, millions or more in annual sales) is learned over the first year or two. [L0806.092]
    • Profit does not appear in the first week or the first six months. [L0806.092]

Competitor signals

  • When a competitor switches off a large number of active ads, for example 22 to 30 ads after about three weeks of running, this can signal a stock problem around the holiday rather than a failed product. [L0694.014]
    • If the product meets your criteria, the wait for stock may be worth it. [L0694.014]

Product range and rebranding

  • Launch a new product regularly to give existing customers a reason to buy again. [L0790.083]
    • The target was one new product per month, though one per quarter may be enough in some niches. [L0790.083]
  • Keep a stable store with the same products over time. A single winner can keep a business running, and the goal is to keep the store alive by reselling and upgrading the best product, not chasing one-off hits. [L1440.052]
  • Rebrand a store with a mixed product line: [L0806.094]
    1. Remove the products that do not fit the target niche.
    2. Change the domain name and logo in Shopify.
    3. Keep the remaining products and rename the store to match the niche (for example a carport store).
    4. Keep the removed products only for a future second store if there are enough suppliers to justify it.

Sub-niches and multi-niche stores

  • Older advice (2022): Use the same workflow for multiple sub-niches. Build keyword silos for each sub-niche in one store rather than separate stores. [L0665.107]
    • One store is easier to rank with one keyword silo. [L0665.107]

Launch sequence and growth

  • Older advice (2021): Start paid traffic and suppliers right away instead of waiting to build content first. Make money from paid traffic, then reinvest that money into content and SEO. [L0801.158]
  • Older advice (2022): A store can grow to seven-figure revenue if it stays focused on one niche, becomes a leading store there, and keeps scaling ad budgets. [L0803.074]
    • The main limit is how far the owner pushes, not the product. [L0803.074]
  • Older advice (2021-22): A customer multiplier funnel consists of about six to seven main actions plus some smaller supporting actions. [L0482.004]
    • The main actions work best when they all run together, one after another, to move customers toward a second purchase or toward new customers. [L0482.004]
  • Older advice (2021-22): Follow-up is the practice of keeping prospects who visited but did not buy, and customers who already bought, engaged so they move toward a second or third purchase. [L1497.001]
    • Its value builds over time as traffic sources bring in many top- and middle-of-funnel visitors. [L1497.001]
  • Older advice (2021-22): High-ticket stores need far fewer orders than low-ticket stores for the same profit. [L0771.052]
    • Five saunas a day can be fulfilled by one owner in a few hours. [L0771.052]
    • Equal profit from 40 USD items needs hundreds of orders and a team. [L0771.052]

Copy, direct mail and product arguments

  • Older advice... no year given: When one argument works for one buyer group, apply it to every group with the same need. For example, use it for all gas companies within several hundred miles, then for all stove makers within 500 miles. [L1369.107]
    • Expanding a proven argument to new segments is faster than inventing a new one. [L1369.107]
  • Test changes to the product itself, such as the screening of coal, the cooking time of coke or the size of oven charges, to stay ahead of competitors. [L1369.109]
    • Improving the product keeps the argument for it strong over time. [L1369.109]
  • Study proven direct mail and letter-writing references and apply their principles to your own mailings on a regular basis. [L1369.110]
    • A systematic study routine produces better results than relying on instinct alone. [L1369.110]

Owner income and reinvestment

  • An online business can be scaled far beyond a salary because revenue is not tied to hours worked, and it can run from any location. [L0863.009]
    • This makes it a stronger base for reinvesting income into other assets than a fixed-salary job. [L0863.009]
  • A large share of business income can be reinvested. The lesson cites 80% of earnings invested from 2013 until COVID. [L0863.011]
    • The reinvestment should be spread across diversified assets such as index funds and stable stocks, with only a small part in speculative assets. [L0863.011]

Income beyond the own stores

  • Monthly income for a freelancer who serves multiple dropshipping stores = monthly fee per client × number of clients. Example: 150 dollars × 10 clients = 1,500 dollars per month. [L0910.054]
  • Older advice (2021-22): Skills gained from running your own store, such as paid traffic management, can be sold as a service to other store owners who will pay for them. [L1014.010]
    • Early client work of this kind can grow into a separate income stream alongside the own stores. [L1014.010]
  • Older advice (2021-22): When the same questions and problems keep coming from clients, package the answers into a structured course or program instead of repeating one-on-one advice. [L1014.011]
    • Courses can deliver the same knowledge to more people in less time, and one-on-one coaching can continue alongside. [L1014.011]

Personal environment and idea generation

  • Being in a different environment or culture, even within your own country, shifts how your brain works and generates new ideas. Visit other places, meet people who do things differently, and bring back what you learn. [L1343.033]

Case studies: store design and launch

  • Case: a jewelry store was the sixth product tested after several earlier products without a winner, and it was launched when buyers wanted to show identity. [L1131.057]
    • Ads pointed to a collection page with about 20 designs of an African map necklace, priced at 30 with a buy-one-get-second-free offer, so two cost 30 instead of 60. [L1131.057]
    • A later collection of African country necklaces let buyers choose their country free with the map necklace. [L1131.057]
    • The store made about 60,000 in revenue within two months. [L1131.057]
    • The store was closed after two months because the operator lacked ads experience. Money went into unprofitable ads, the daily budget was raised from 5 to 100 dollars in one step, which got the ad account blocked, and negative comments were left unanswered, which led to the Facebook page being blocked. [L1131.057]
  • Case: a student's store sold a mix of unrelated products, including hair wax, pet grooming gloves, men's jewelry, pet accessories, hats, car accessories and a bathroom apron. Its traffic came from a Facebook ad for a baby hammock sleeping bed. [L1346.034]
    • The homepage showed unrelated lifestyle photos. The product pages used default featured images and had no reviews, no urgency, no estimated delivery dates, no live chat link and no social media accounts. [L1346.034]
    • The reviewer judged the store to look like a burner store. [L1346.034]
    • Recommended changes: choose one product type, theme the site around it, replace the images, add a review system, scarcity counts, urgency offers, estimated delivery, a live chat link, tabbed product pages, a fuller footer and active social media accounts. [L1346.034]
    • The lesson gives no sales figures or results for the store. [L1346.034]
