20.25 Preparing a store for sale or shutdown

Making a store sellable without the owner

  • A buyer acquires a business, not a job. The store must run on its own without your daily involvement, so document all critical processes in standard operating procedures and remove yourself from essential functions. [L0231.010]
  • Most buyers do not want a 40-hour-a-week job. Hire a team and step out of daily operations before selling. The team also shows which tasks still need documentation. [L0554.031]
  • A store that runs without the seller, with documented SOPs, training documents, and processes, is worth more, because the buyer can take over the daily work without you. [L0554.030]
  • Services that depend on the seller reduce valuation. A service tied to the product can add value if a team runs it, but some buyers dislike the extra complexity. [L0554.033]
  • Portfolio investors want passive income, so virtual assistants (VAs) who run the site without the owner matter a lot to them. Buyers who want to tinker will prefer a more hands-on setup. [L1468.038]
  • A store can be sold while it is still early. List it with its key details and either a price or a "best offer" note in a for-sale channel so buyers can make offers. [L0360.083]

Documentation and SOPs

  • Document all processes as SOPs before listing. Clear documentation lets a buyer take over with confidence. Missing documentation makes buyers hold back money or discount the price. [L1468.037]
  • SOPs and a documented, trained team (for example VAs) raise a store's attractiveness, especially to newer buyers. They can decide between a completed sale and no sale. [L0553.020]
  • Document critical processes in SOPs so the buyer can run them without you. [L0231.010]

Building and handing over the team

Procedure: prepare a store team for sale [L0553.022]

  1. Hire and train new VAs about 3 to 6 months before selling so they can run the operations.
  2. Document SOPs.
  3. Hand over the trained team and processes to the buyer as part of the sale. Do not try to train after the sale closes.
  • Using VAs to run day-to-day operations makes a store more attractive, because the owner does not need to be involved. This raises buyer interest and can improve the multiple. [L0553.023]
  • VAs usually transfer with the business. If they do not, you must train replacement hires before closing. Buyers will contractually require you to handle the handover and may hold back part of the payment until training is complete. [L0553.021]
  • You can write a sale-linked bonus into each VA's contract: each gets 1% of the profit when a site is sold. This aligns the team with the long-term value of the business. [L1437.031]

Case: owner-dependent production [L1468.039]

A seller's wine-glass store made about $1,000 per month in net profit. The owner said it needed about two hours per week. However, the product was hand-made by a family member working 30 to 40 hours per week. This made the business dependent on one unpaid producer who might not transfer, so a buyer would discount it.

Contracts with staff and suppliers

  • Formal written contracts with VAs and suppliers are not required to sell a business. They are helpful because they define expectations and protect both parties during the transition. [L0231.026, L0231.030]
  • Contracts with staff and suppliers are not required for a sale, but they add buyer confidence. A contract may be needed before a kickback arrangement with a supplier counts as profit. [L0554.032]

Notifying suppliers and staff

  • Tell your suppliers that you are selling. Clarify what terms the new owner expects, so they understand the changes before the handover. [L0231.019]
  • Inform your staff of the pending sale as early as your circumstances allow. Most buyers retain existing employees, which eases continuity concerns. [L0231.020]

Post-signing handover

  • After the asset purchase agreement is signed, the broker's team can migrate the accounts and run the handover. The level of help depends on what the buyer and seller each want. [L0553.044]
  • Some sellers prefer that the broker does not touch the accounts. Less experienced sellers are guided step by step. [L0553.044]
  • Cross-border handovers, such as a buyer in another country who needs a local company, are handled case by case and can take months. [L0553.044]

Valuation factors

  • A store's valuation is based on profit and success. More suppliers, more revenue, and better profit mean a higher sale price. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0962.136]
  • A buyer will not trust a P&L that is only typed into a spreadsheet. The seller must prove the figures with proper accounting records, so accounting must be upgraded well before an exit. The higher the expected sale price, the more important this proof becomes. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1516.007]
  • Dropshipping businesses can be sold for six, seven, or even eight figures, so a buyer will expect audited, verifiable financial records before paying that kind of price. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1516.008]

Portfolio buyers

  • Portfolio buyers typically set up a separate LLC for each acquisition. This compartmentalizes liability and lets them manage tax reporting independently. [L0231.025]
  • Portfolio buyers want stores that keep running without the seller. They often keep the existing team and systems for a while after purchase rather than shutting everything off at once. Continuity in the first months after the sale is part of what the buyer pays for. [L0554.056]

Sale channels and brokers

  • Store sale options depend on age and profit. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0962.135]
Store profile Route
Under one year old, modest net profit Member marketplace listing [L0962.135]
Over one year old, significant net profit Offer to a broker such as Empire Flippers [L0962.135]
  • Selling a store means transferring its ownership inside Shopify. The store can be sold with or without a full supplier set. (Older advice, 2021.) [L0962.137]

Closing a store instead of selling

  • If a store cannot be actively managed for a long period, such as 18 months, it is better to sell it and restart later than to let it sit idle. (Older advice, 2019.) [L1069.074]

Procedure: shut down a store you no longer need [L0804.102]

  1. Export all products and data with Rewind backups or the product CSV export.
  2. Stop paying for the store once the export is saved.
  3. Contact any supplier you approved with, thank them, and explain that the store is closing.
