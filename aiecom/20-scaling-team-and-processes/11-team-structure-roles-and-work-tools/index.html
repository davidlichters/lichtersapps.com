20.11 Team structure, roles and work tools

Ownership and accountability

  • The RACI matrix assigns four role types to every task and function:
Role Meaning
Responsible The person who does the work
Accountable The person accountable for the outcome
Consulted People whose input and ideas are sought
Informed People kept in the loop

Map every deliverable across your team to these roles. Simplify and review whenever you add a channel, make a new hire, or change a process. [L0260.011]

  • A RACI matrix prevents confusion and competing priorities by creating clear ownership and structured handoff sequences. Without it, multiple team members pursue conflicting directions for the same task. [L0260.012]
  • Each new hire takes over the backup role and tasks of the previous hire. This frees the owner and gives the team a clear chain of responsibility. Continue handing out tasks until the owner is only doing sales and high-level work. [L0337.019]
  • Shared tools make the business transparent. The owner can see order status, check whether emails are answered, read reports in Asana and the help desk, and reach the VA directly on Slack when something is open. [L1473.031]

Team structure by revenue phase

  • Phase guidance, with both thresholds from the sources and when each applies:
Monthly sales What applies Ids
Up to roughly $500,000 A virtual team is fine for drop shipping. An office and an in-house team are not needed. L0413.009
$20,000 to $100,000 The business moves toward a real brand and marketing gains weight. A project manager takes over leading the team toward performance goals. L1129.008, L1129.009
Roughly $500,000 and above The source gives the $500,000 point as the threshold for an office and an in-house team. It gives no separate rule above that level. L0413.009

  • Both thresholds apply together. A virtual team with a project manager is the expected setup at $20,000 to $100,000 per month, and the office threshold of about $500,000 comes later. [L0413.009, L1129.008, L1129.009]

  • At $20,000 to $100,000 per month, a project manager monitors the customer service and influencer outreach staff against performance goals and can help hire new people. Expect to pay about $8 to $12 per hour on Monazar or Upwork. [L1129.009]

  • Virtual teams stay connected by adding everyone to the same Slack channel, including virtual assistants doing different tasks such as product research and product image sourcing. This shows them that they are part of one company. [L0413.010]

  • Define which team roles the business will need, such as ads managers, customer service reps, graphic designers and videographers. Decide whether these staff are based overseas or local in an office. (Older advice: 2021-22) [L0439.022]

  • An operations manager can run the daily business, so the owner only checks in weekly. In a larger setup, one operations manager can oversee a network of businesses while the owner sits at the top. (Older advice: 2021-22) [L0439.023]

  • A typical team structure, (older advice: 2022):

    • The owner handles research and supplier connections plus ad management, 1 to 5 hours per week.
    • A business or operations manager oversees support and sales leads (sales lead and support lead).
    • Design and copywriting teams report directly to the business manager. [L0665.104]

  • Keep one team across all stores rather than separate teams per store. Operations and marketing teams can serve every store. (Older advice: 2022) [L0665.105]

  • A team of 10 virtual assistants and freelancers can cover supplier sourcing for high-ticket dropshipping, website design, product uploads, product optimization, product descriptions and graphic design, plus occasional smaller tasks. (Older advice: 2021-22) [L0499.004]

  • A content-led brand can run with a small team. The owner films the videos, a video editor handles editing, a part-time social media person posts for about three hours per day, and a full-time designer handles visuals. Divide the work so the owner focuses on creating content. [L0487.064]

  • When scaling a dropshipping business, split responsibilities across distinct roles:

    • Product research and scaling strategy
    • Branding and creative testing
    • Paid ads and funnel optimization
    • Operations, fulfillment and backend systems

    Each role covers a different part of the business, so the whole operation gets full coverage. [L0873.001]

  • Operations-focused people who handle fulfillment and backend systems are also well placed to negotiate with suppliers and designers and to keep stores streamlined for sustainable growth. Assigning supplier negotiation to the person who runs operations keeps the fulfillment side and the supplier side aligned. [L0873.002]

  • One company grew to about 15 people, split between the US and the Philippines. Roles covered web design, operations, social media and support, which was divided into live chat, phone and email. (Older advice: 2020) [L0852.102]

  • As the business grows, managers handle daily tasks. The owner checks the business at a high level by tracking the main drivers of revenue, ad spend, return on ad spend, support response times and reviews. (Older advice: 2021) [L0962.131]

Accountability chart and org structure

(Older advice: 2021-22, Source B)

  • An accountability chart is an org chart that shows each role's responsibilities and who holds each person accountable. Draw it bigger than the current team so it shows the structure the business is growing into, and treat it as a living document. [L1334.017]

  • In a Traction-style accountability chart, the top roles are called:

    • Visionary, who owns ideas, R&D, culture and market fit.
    • Integrator, who owns systems, the business plan and targets.

    These labels replace CEO and COO titles. [L1334.018]

  • Every business has three components: sales and marketing, operations, and finance and admin. Each has a director, and the top leader should have only these three direct reports. [L1334.019]

  • Split a combined role into two hires when the workload becomes too big for one person:

    • Customer communication goes to customer service.
    • Supplier work, order management and order processing go to order processing.

    A later split can separate sales from customer service when the business gets busy. [L1334.022]

  • Once several marketing specialists such as SEO, PPC and content writers are in place, hire a marketing manager to coordinate them. This takes the owner out of implementing training material and day-to-day marketing work. [L1334.050]
  • Add the expected team size to the business description for each horizon, because it is a concrete marker of growth. A 1-year picture might show a few employees, while a 3-year picture might show around 10 staff. Keep the description to a short overview. [L1519.019]

Dividing roles and delegating

  • Assign each team member the roles that match their strengths. One VA handled support and operations, while another with graphics and Shopify back-end skills built additional stores. (Older advice: source year not stated) [L1437.028]
  • Split the workload across assistants rather than giving one person everything. For example, two assistants can each manage the same two stores, so no individual is overloaded. [L1431.030]
  • Virtual assistants for live chat, phone calls and order shipping should be separated from social media and content roles, because one person handling all of them quickly breaks down. [L0970.153]
  • Do not expect one virtual assistant to handle every task well. Group related tasks, for example email plus live chat plus phone, and define clear goals and tracking for each role. (Older advice: 2022) [L0803.082]
  • Split influencer work across dedicated roles: one person for micro-influencers, one for mid-level and high-level deals, and one for content curation. Three people covered the influencer function at one brand. [L0790.081]
  • A B2B sales VA with real estate experience was close to closing an order worth about 18,000 for a government buyer. The owner had planned to offer an extra 200 discount, but the VA reduced the discount and still closed the deal. Give experienced sales staff authority to set final pricing within agreed limits. [L0832.062]

Hiring with managers

  • Involve team managers and leaders in the hiring process from the beginning:
    1. Have them help create screening questions, read candidate answers, and push people through the hiring funnel in Asana.
    2. Have them conduct the first 10 to 15 minute interview call (short call).
    3. Keep the final decision for yourself.
  • This gives leaders ownership of their team, involvement in culture, and investment in new hires. It is more effective than hiring a manager for an existing team. [L0263.024]
  • A manager who has learned a documented hiring process can run a hire on their own, with the owner supplying the content. Expect to coach the manager closely on the first hire, and expect the second hire to be run without the owner. (Older advice: 2021-22) [L1334.049]

Capacity measurement

  • Measure operating capacity by asking the team how busy they are and whether orders, customer questions or supplier orders are slipping through. Late orders and forgotten supplier orders are signs that the current capacity limit has been reached. (Older advice: 2021-22) [L1334.045]
  • Realistic capacity can turn out well above the first estimate once a strong team is in place. Reassess capacity as the team grows instead of relying on the initial number. (Older advice: 2021-22) [L1334.047]
  • Measuring what a virtual assistant actually does reveals how much capacity they have. It is common to underestimate it and overload them. Track their workload with time data before assigning more tasks, and review the results when hiring additional assistants. [L1441.030]
  • Time tracking tools such as Hubstaff and the Upwork time tracker log and monitor the working hours of remote team members. [L0926.009]

Workflow boards and task management

  • Create separate boards for workflows with different timelines, such as regular quotes, white glove installation quotes, product upload and content. White glove installation requests can come in at three or four per day and need separate follow-up with both the manufacturer and the customer. Keep a separate board for each workflow to avoid mixing them. (Older advice: 2021-22) [L1396.058]
  • Monday.com (alternatives: Asana or Trello) is used for task management. It stores social media planners, works-in-progress, OKRs and daily deliverables in one accessible location. [L0260.015]
  • Source A uses Asana for the hiring funnel and for team reports. The weakest part of the system is the manual updating of statuses, notes and due dates, because it depends on the VA remembering each update. Where possible, choose tools that update statuses, notes and due dates automatically. [L0263.024, L1473.034]
  • Trello is a kanban-style project board used to track tasks, assign owners and manage recurring processes when a team or virtual assistants are involved. [L0817.005]
  • Basecamp is a project management tool for team tasks. Its small plans are affordable for a startup team. It can track monthly promotions with assigned roles and responsibilities. (Older advice: 2021) [L0853.093, L0247.121]
  • Google Drive stores day-to-day files and shared documents. (Older advice: 2021) [L0853.093]
  • Grammarly is a free grammar and formatting tool for Gmail and other writing platforms. It catches repetitions, run-on sentences and spelling errors in professional email communication with suppliers. (Older advice: 2021) [L0270.020]
  • Working roadmap prompt:
Turn the 30-day roadmap into a shorter working version. Give one line per action, grouped by week one, week two, week three and week four. Separate fixes to do now from testing ideas, and keep only the metric each action should improve. Then assign every action to a named team member with their responsibility.

[L0886.036]

Communication

  • Slack is used for informal team communication and rapid information sharing. [L0260.016]
  • Slack keeps distributed team members connected. Add everyone to the same channel, including virtual assistants doing different tasks, so they see that they are part of one company. [L0413.010]

ClickUp for personal and team task management

  • Set up ClickUp (free plan) for personal task management:

    1. Create separate Spaces for each area, such as life management, the e-commerce brand, and mentoring, with the brands kept in separate folders.
    2. In each list, add tasks with an assignee, a priority, a due date, and a description.
    3. Break tasks into subtasks and sub-steps.
    4. Use the List view as the default for simple to-do/done lists.
    5. Use the Board view for status-based pipelines such as creatives (Ideas, Scripting, Editing, Done).
    6. Use the Calendar view to see all tasks with due dates across the workspace.
    7. Filter the Everything view by priority to see the daily top tasks.
    8. Save a priority view and work from it when time is free.

    [L1181.018]

  • ClickUp is a free task management tool with list, board, calendar and whiteboard views. It also has a Docs feature for dynamic documents that can turn text into tasks, custom fields, threaded comments, and a Gmail extension for turning emails into tasks. [L1181.019]

  • ClickUp custom fields: add a custom field of type Drop Down with options such as strategy call, PDF, case study and video library so each deliverable shows its type. Add fields via the list's column settings or from the field library. [L1181.020]

  • When a team starts producing creatives or deliverables, use a status pipeline to track each item from idea to shortlist, scripting, storyboarding, editing and final. Move items across stages as they progress. [L1181.022]
  • ClickUp Docs can turn a text item into a task. Select the text, create a task from it, add it to a board such as brand creatives, then set priority and due date. [L1181.023]
  • Organize work by separating areas (brand, mentoring, life management). When a calendar block is set for one area, only tasks of that area appear, which avoids distraction from other work. [L1181.024]
