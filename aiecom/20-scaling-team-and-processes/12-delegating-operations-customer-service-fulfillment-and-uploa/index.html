20.12 Delegating operations: customer service, fulfillment and uploads

Principles for delegating operations

  • Operational problems are unavoidable in dropshipping and e-commerce. Competitive advantage comes from solving them faster than competitors, which makes support responsiveness as strategically important as platform features. [L0216.011]
  • Outsourcing is acceptable when the owner understands the process and can spot-check the work. Check that each product row matches its title, description and price before it goes live. [L1111.161] (Source A, 2020, older)
  • Speed of response determines conversion in custom-product and B2B quote scenarios. Customers contact several competitors at once, and whoever responds first builds the relationship and wins the deal. [L0284.001]
  • Outsourcing repetitive uploads to a freelancer is a reasonable way to save time, because uploading takes a long time, especially when suppliers send data in different formats. [L0966.089]
  • Outsourcing does not remove upload time. Upload time grows with the number of products, so start early and have the catalogue ready before launch. [L1166.031] (Source B, 2021-22, older)
  • Do not treat one lesson as a complete outsourcing plan. Outsource a task such as product uploads only if it takes too much of your time, and first upload existing approved brands to go live before adding new suppliers. [L1115.076]
  • Outsourced work that needs no deep local or cultural knowledge can go offshore. Work that depends on knowing the culture should stay with someone from that market. Example: a campaign that phoned households in rural Australia was staffed by offshore virtual assistants (VAs) working from a very basic script. [L1359.044]
  • Outsourced product review or approval work may give unsatisfactory results. Verify quality before relying on an outsourced process. [L1094.109] (2023)
  • Outsourcing customer service is among the first delegations to consider, because customer inquiries are a classic time drain for new owners and the workload grows with the business. [L1514.015] (Source B, 2021-22, older)
  • Concern that outsourced customer service will be worse is unfounded if the right people are hired. Outsourcing also protects the owner's stress level and brainpower. [L1247.007] (Source B, 2021-22, older)

Staffing order and roles

When to hand over customer service. Sources differ on the trigger. Use both tests:

Stage Who handles support Source
First months Owner handles customer service for at least the first few months, because outsourcing is hard to do well without having done the work yourself (Source B, 2021-22, older) [L0909.002]
Early phase, situations still unknown Owner handles messages personally to learn which situations occur (Source C) [L0911.003]
Live chat, early phase Owner answers live chats personally to learn what customers ask and to respond quickly [L0948.022]
Sales generate enough cash flow to pay for support Outsource customer service; until then, keep answering calls, emails and chats yourself or through the callback system (Source B, 2021-22, older) [L0909.017]
Owner is exhausted and sales are stuck Owner handled support personally, which was mentally taxing and caused burnout. Hire a VA for email support as the first priority before scaling further (Source C) [L1127.036]
Store is profitable and can afford a hire Outsource live chat to a staff member [L0948.022]
Main product is private-labelled Owner should not handle customer service personally. Hire a team member to answer support contacts and log every customer request, complaint and feature idea (Source B, 2021-22, older) [L1155.018]
  • Use cash flow as the test for whether you can afford the hire, and burnout or stuck sales as the signal that support is urgent. [L0909.017, L1127.036]
  • A full-time customer service agent in the Philippines can cost as little as $400 per month (Source B, 2021-22, older). Even with an agent, you may still need to speak to some customers yourself. [L0909.018]

First hire: one person for support and orders.

  • Hire one person for both customer service and order management. The tasks are closely linked; one person reduces time lag, can cancel orders directly, and has access to both suppliers and customers. [L1473.016]
  • A single VA can often cover email, live chat and order processing for a young store, so one hire is usually the starting point. Phone support tasks can be handled by the same person, though roles are often split as the business grows. [L1430.001]
  • Once order volume grows, hire a VA for phone calls, emails and daily operations. Train the assistant on the supplier notes spreadsheet before handing over the work. [L0468.171] (Source A, 2020, older)
  • Hire an operational VA, for example for support tickets, phone calls and order processing, once business profits can pay for them. Test a new VA with a small project of 50 to 100 products before a large batch. [L1112.072] (2021, older)
  • Assign duties in stages and check performance with time-tracking data. The assistant should work in the same hours as the team to reduce handover delays. [L1441.043]
  • Route multi-unit requests, such as a customer asking for a dozen units, directly to the VA who handles sales. These deals take a long time and need one person to own them. [L0832.050]
  • A VA can cover order fulfilment, phone contact with customers and product uploads, but only after meeting English and speed requirements. [L1441.043]

Equipping the VA before the first day. [L1430.003]

  1. Ability to submit orders to suppliers.
  2. Access to product information, so they can answer live chat and email questions.
  3. Access to stock levels through weekly inventory reports and dealer logins, to check availability for potential customers.
  4. Limited access to the Shopify admin panel, to look up order status.
  5. A document with all supplier contact details, so they know whom to contact when an order arrives.
  6. A standard order submission email template for suppliers.
  7. Ability to enter tracking numbers in the Shopify back end when a supplier confirms shipment.

Owner's role and roles for the team.

  • Owners should hear about a customer problem after it has been resolved, as long as it was resolved quickly. A customer service manager should handle post-sale problems so the owner does not answer the team's questions. [L1334.021] (Source B, 2021-22, older)
  • A catalog manager keeps product pages, listings and stock information current, adding new products and removing discontinued ones. Outdated information causes customer questions about wrong products and out-of-stock items, which pulls the service team off track. [L1334.023] (Source B, 2021-22, older)
  • Assign the thank-you task to the team member most relevant to it, usually the person who processes orders or a phone customer service team member. The two roles can be held by the same person or by different people. [L0406.027] (Source B, 2021-22, older)
  • Marketing and SEO are roles to hire for later, after profit thresholds are met. [L1446.033]

Customer service and phone answering

Phone staff requirements.

  • Staff who answer calls for an English-speaking store should be native English speakers when customers are in the US. Accents from some outsourcing countries can make US customers lose trust. [L0910.014]
  • A phone VA must be a native English speaker and work during US business hours, for example 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Assistants from other locations suit back-office tasks but not live calls with US customers. [L0910.045]
  • The main requirement for phone staff is trust and friendliness. A warm, caring voice keeps customers calm even when the agent cannot answer every question. Agents still need written answer templates for common questions, such as shipping times. [L0910.015]
  • Plan the first major phone-coverage milestone as 8 AM Eastern through late Pacific time (roughly 12 hours). This requires at least 2 people to share the shift. As volume grows, add a second shift with 2 people in the morning and 2 in the evening, which also gives backup when someone takes a day off. [L0263.009]
  • A business number can later be set up so several staff members or VAs answer the same line. A single-owner setup is enough at launch. [L1198.011] (Source B, 2021-22, older)

Commission-based phone VA (older model). [L0245.052] (Source A, 2020, older)

  1. On Craigslist or Fiverr, find a virtual assistant willing to answer phone calls on a commission basis.
  2. Explain that your business phone number rings through to their personal phone, so they are doing other activities at the same time.
  3. They answer incoming calls and attempt to make sales.
  4. If they cannot answer a question, they take a message.
  5. Pay commission on sales originating from phone calls (approximately 1 to 2% of sale value), not an hourly wage.

Prevention before handling complaints.

  • A better strategy than handling more complaints is to prevent the failure that causes them. A small loss rate that is handled well can still create angry customers, so focus on the root cause. [L0910.043]

Remote VA tasks.

  • Tasks that can be done remotely include invoicing, accounting updates, sending tracking notifications and fulfilling orders. A Philippines-based assistant can update accounting once a week, especially at month end. [L0910.044]

Email, social and chat.

  • Hand email, social media messages and store chat to a VA only after you know exactly how to handle each situation and your budget allows it. [L0911.003]
  • Set up a separate customer support email address that the assistant can access directly, so the founder is not replying to routine order questions. [L1432.006]
  • Live chat should be installed on the store. Shopify Inbox is now a native Shopify option that replaces a separate live chat tool such as Tidio. Its companion phone app sends a notification whenever a customer writes, so a team member can reply quickly. [L1432.007]
  • Give the customer support assistant login access to the live chat tool, either Tidio or Shopify Inbox, so they can answer visitors in real time. Fast chat replies are part of the support setup. [L1358.030]

Why outsource fulfilment and support.

  • Once a store generates sales, outsource order fulfilment and customer support. Answering repeated "where is my order" emails consumes time better spent on new offers, brand building and creatives. [L1432.003]

Order processing and fulfilment

Order placement.

  • Keep order placement manual until stock is confirmed reliably. Order processing should move to a VA once volume grows. [L0834.105] (Source A, 2021, older)
  • Submit first orders to the supplier as an email purchase order (PO) including supplier name, SKU, quantity and customer shipping address. For first orders, confirm the format with the supplier: "This is my first order; please let me know if you need anything else." [L0249.084] (Source A, 2022, older)
  • Fulfilment work requires no special skill, so hire someone to place orders with the supplier. At high order volume, this person may also negotiate lower unit prices. [L1432.004]

Order management workflow. [L0230.007]

  1. New order received → added to the Asana board → notification in Reamaze → fraud check by the Clearsale service → forwarded to supplier.
  2. Order confirmed → credit card charged → updated in Asana.
  3. Order shipped → tracking number added → status updated in Asana → marked incomplete until delivery.
  4. Backorder → team communicates with customer and supplier → order moved to the backorder column in Asana.
  5. Damages or returns → team communicates with customer and supplier → status updated in Asana.

Access for fulfilment.

  • For order fulfilment, the assistant needs access to the payment providers and the supplier account, which should be set up at the start. Once the process is stable, the owner can stop checking every order and check periodically instead. [L1358.028]

Daily fulfilment check. [L1358.029]

  1. At the end of each day, check whether the team has fulfilled all orders.
  2. In the first weeks, check this closely.
  3. When the routine runs as planned, reduce the check to a regular review.

Backorders and stock-outs (case). [L0359.104]

  • A store owner had 12 sales in the first week, but the main supplier could not fulfil the orders.
  • The response: update the product pages with a backorder notice, send confirmation emails, stop ads for backordered products, restructure the store to show in-stock best-margin products, and keep searching for more supplier approvals.
  • The lesson: the problem was temporary, and the niche did not need to be abandoned.

Supplier price and stock changes.

  • Review supplier price and product changes as they come in. Set stock to zero in the Shopify admin for products the supplier discontinued, and list new products that suppliers add. [L0962.132] (Source A, 2021, older)
  • Keep stock and pricing under your own control. Update prices only when a supplier changes its MAP policy or wholesale cost, not daily. [L1090.137] (Source A, 2021, older)

Sourcing a winning product through a VA. [L0678.047] (Source C)

  1. Find a winning product on a competitor's store.
  2. Send the assistant the competitor's product link.
  3. Ask the assistant to contact the supplier, for example CJ Dropshipping, and place the order for you.
  4. Allow a few days for the supplier to reply, since supplier agents are slow to respond.

Daily routines and board management

Morning routine. [L1396.015] (Source B, 2021-22, older; takes about one to two hours at a busy store)

  1. Open email, chat, phone, Shopify and Trello.
  2. Review the inbox first for urgent messages, such as customers who want to cancel an order.
  3. Send the new orders to the manufacturers.
  4. Contact abandoned checkouts.
  5. Check all emails for tracking numbers, follow-ups and customer questions, and create quote requests where needed.
  6. Finish with the Order Processing board: check backorders that are ready to ship, request tracking numbers, and check manufacturer confirmations.

Board management. [L0292.016] (Source A)

  1. Review the board to identify cards delayed in any list longer than expected.
  2. Monitor carrier tracking and move confirmed delivered orders to delivered and pending review.
  3. Follow up with suppliers on orders in sent to supplier that have not shipped.
  4. Contact customers in delivered and pending review to request reviews.
  5. Move completed reviews to the reviewed column.

Follow-up on quotes and delays.

  • When a quote depends on the manufacturer, follow up with the manufacturer as well as the customer. If the response does not arrive within two or three days, keep checking. Track both follow-ups on the same card so delays are not forgotten on either side. [L1396.059] (Source B, 2021-22, older)

Inbox discipline.

  • Aim for inbox zero by the end of each day or as soon as possible, so that the assistant and owner know all messages have been handled. Clearing a full inbox daily is the goal of the labelling system. [L1000.069]
  • A large unmanaged inbox, with thousands of emails read once and then forgotten, causes missed orders and customer complaints. Labels and a daily clear-out prevent this. [L1000.070]

Post-sale calls, reviews and thank-you messages

Post-sale review calls.

  • A customer service VA is well suited to make these calls, because support staff have quiet periods when no inbound calls come in. Calls can be made in these slots. [L1011.014] (Source B, 2021-22, older)
  • Post-purchase review calls are a simple, scripted task that VAs or customer service staff can handle during quiet periods. This makes them a good first task for a new VA, who can learn the customers and the market before taking live support calls. [L1012.011] (Source B, 2021-22, older)
  • A customer service VA usually needs only about an hour of free time per day to work through the post-sale call list. [L1011.024] (Source B, 2021-22, older)
  • If the founder makes the calls, each call takes about 10 minutes. With about one new customer per day, that means roughly one call per day, which fits easily into a daily to-do list. [L1011.018] (Source B, 2021-22, older)
  • Set a fixed time slot for review calls, daily or weekly, and treat it as a recurring task. Start as soon as possible, even if the calls feel uncomfortable at first. [L1012.024] (Source B, 2021-22, older)

Delegating post-sale calls. [L1011.015] (Source B, 2021-22, older)

  1. Give the VA the call script and ask them to read it first.
  2. Have a short conversation and answer any questions they have.
  3. Walk them through the process step by step.
  4. Instruct them to make the calls during downtime.
  5. Each day, have them check the list of customers who have reached the call point, meaning about 10 days after purchase or after delivery, and call those customers.
  6. Add the calls to the standard operating procedure for customer service.

Implementation steps. [L1011.019] (Source B, 2021-22, older)

  1. Develop your call script.
  2. Decide who makes the calls: yourself or a VA.
  3. Add the calls to your daily to-do list, or to the SOPs and to-do lists of your employees or VA.

Reviews and feedback.

  • When delivery volume justifies it, hire one full-time employee dedicated to calling delivered customers for reviews and testimonials. In early-stage growth phases with inconsistent order volume, use the downtime periods to call every delivered order for feedback, photos or videos, rather than leaving gaps in the follow-up. [L0286.041] (Source A)
  • Assign a team member to read reviews of your product regularly on Amazon and on competitor sites, and record the recurring complaints and wishes. Do this alongside your own store reviews so the findings cover the whole market. [L1155.019] (Source B, 2021-22, older)

Thank-you messages.

  • Personal thank-yous can be scaled in two ways: with tools that keep a personal touch, or by delegating to team members. Video and handwritten notes are both viable, and a business can use either or both, since customers receive them at different times. [L0406.008] (Source B, 2021-22, older)
  • Start with individually recorded videos for each order when volume is low. Switch to one pre-recorded video once order volume grows too high to record daily. Individual videos can be sent daily, twice a week, or weekly while the business is still small. [L0406.016] (Source B, 2021-22, older)
  • When a store gets many orders per day, recording an individual thank-you video for each customer can take about three hours a day. At that volume, switch to one pre-recorded video that is sent to every new customer. [L0406.031] (Source B, 2021-22, older)
  • Any team member can record thank-you videos, not only the owner. The video should come from a person in the business. A top customer service agent can record them in downtime and send them through Bonjoro with user access. [L0406.025] (Source B, 2021-22, older)
  • If you send thank-yous personally, set calendar reminders or add them to your to-do list to keep consistent contact with new customers. If you delegate, document the process in the team member's standard operating procedures. [L0203.006] (Source B, 2021-22, older)

Delegating the video thank-you. [L0406.026] (Source B, 2021-22, older)

  1. Run through the video thank-you process yourself a few times.
  2. Write it down as an SOP, or fit it into your existing customer service or order-processing SOPs.
  3. Give the task to a team member who has Bonjoro user access.
  4. The team member logs in and sends the pre-recorded video to each new customer, which takes only a couple of minutes per day.

Product uploads and freelancers

Deciding to outsource uploads.

  • Product uploads are the most tedious part of store setup but must be done correctly. Checking the work of anyone you hire is essential. [L0855.165] (Source A, 2023)
  • A supplier with bulk upload needs a CSV, which is a large spreadsheet. One mistake in one row or column can make many product descriptions or prices wrong. [L0855.166] (Source A, 2023)
  • Product upload CSV work can be done by the owner in one evening (about four or five hours), following Shopify's step-by-step CSV import guide. If it fails, post the job with the CSV on Upwork with Shopify product upload experience. [L0856.101] (Source A, 2024)
  • Use CSV uploads to add or update products in bulk. Shopify provides a template, and most product data can be managed in a spreadsheet before uploading. [L0665.106] (Source A, 2022, older)
  • A supplier catalogue that already exists as a spreadsheet but is not formatted for Shopify can be uploaded by a freelancer on Upwork, Fiverr or Storetasker. Outsourcing saves time when a supplier has thousands of SKUs. [L0857.069] (Source A, 2025)
  • Product upload effort depends on the supplier's data format. Ready-made Excel or CSV files upload quickly, while data spread over a supplier's website takes much longer per product. [L0667.053] (Source A, 2025)

Freelance platforms and costs.

  • Outsourcing product uploads can be done on freelance platforms such as onlinejobs.ph or upwork.com, where workers can be hired at roughly $3 to $4 per hour. [L0663.040] (Source A, 2019, older)
  • Experienced Shopify product upload freelancers on Upwork, often based in the Philippines, charge around $4 to $5 per hour. There is no fixed price per product, because it depends on how the supplier sends its data. [L0667.054] (Source A, 2025)
  • Examples on Upwork were around $5 per hour for Shopify product upload freelancers (Source A, 2025). [L0807.050]
  • On Fiverr, typical pricing for Shopify product upload services is around $10 per 50 products. Many freelancers work in different time zones, which enables overnight uploads while you continue supplier outreach. [L0128.005]
  • Hire freelancers for bulk Shopify uploads through Upwork or onlinejobs.ph to build a consistent working relationship that can be reused as the supplier count grows. There is no fixed price; the cost depends on how much work the supplier data needs. [L1069.030] (Source A, 2019, older)
  • For a full-time dedicated upload person, onlinejobs.ph was listed at $99 per month (Source A, 2019, older). [L0243.019]
  • Image resizing has been quoted at about 10 USD for 100 images (Source B, 2021-22, older). The task needs no speaking or writing, so freelancers or VAs in any country can do it. [L1163.014]
  • Fiverr and OnlineJobs.ph can be used to outsource upload tasks such as image resizing or writing product data at affordable rates. [L1163.015] (Source B, 2021-22, older)

Hiring procedure, step by step.

Upwork with a recorded SOP (Source A, 2019, older): [L0243.019]

  1. Time how long you spend uploading a batch of 25 to 100 products.
  2. Record your screen and voice using Loom, showing the complete upload process (20 to 30 seconds minimum).
  3. Post the job on Upwork (free) with the Loom video as the SOP, or hire from OnlineJobs.ph ($99 per month) if you want dedicated full-time help.
  4. Send the Loom SOP to the freelancer.
  5. The freelancer handles the remaining bulk uploads while you focus on other tasks.

Upwork with a test batch (Source A, 2025): [L0667.052]

  1. Record a screen and webcam video with Loom, a free tool, uploading 5 to 10 products from one supplier.
  2. Narrate each step: get the product name, get the description, get the images, enter the price, and enter the SEO meta description.
  3. Post a Shopify product upload job on Upwork, and send a test job of about 100 products to three or four applicants.
  4. Hire the freelancer who does the test best, and give them the rest of the uploads.

Upwork, filtered by track record (Source A, 2025): [L0807.050]

  1. Upload five to ten products yourself first, and confirm the process works.
  2. Record the steps with Loom as a reference video.
  3. Search Upwork for a freelancer with Shopify product upload experience.
  4. Filter for job success rates of 90 percent or higher.
  5. Send the recording and examples of finished products, so the freelancer can follow the same steps.

Posting for free (Source A, 2023): [L0855.163]

  1. Post the job on Upwork for free.
  2. Review each applicant's reviews and portfolio, asking for screenshots or video of previous work.
  3. Hire, then double-check every upload row before publishing.

Handing off across suppliers (Source A, 2021, older): [L0671.082]

  1. Do a few uploads yourself to learn the process.
  2. Record your screen with Loom while uploading 10 to 20 products from one supplier.
  3. Send the recording to whoever will do the uploads.
  4. Treat each supplier as a separate job, because the work differs even when product counts are the same.

Suppliers that give only basic data.

  • When uploading product lists from suppliers that give only UPC, SKU, name and price, upload three to five products yourself, record it, and have a freelancer do the rest. Spot-check the pricing afterwards. [L0852.106] (Source A, 2020, older)
  • A store owner can outsource site design, product uploads and developer tasks to freelancers on Upwork. Record a screen video showing how you want a product uploaded, then have the freelancer upload 100 to 150 products. [L0852.105] (Source A, 2020, older)

Scale and launch.

  • Launch traffic once around 30 to 50 products are live, and keep uploading the rest of the catalogue in parallel. Stores with over 3,000 products exist, and growth to that size happens gradually. [L0663.047] (Source A, 2019, older)
  • There is no fixed maximum number of products. Keep uploading until every quality supplier in your niche is uploaded, then check regularly for new product lines and new suppliers. [L0835.124] (Source A, 2023)
  • Each new supplier adds variety, and more products are better when they all come from suppliers in your niche. Adding suppliers over time is the way to grow the catalogue. [L0835.128] (Source A, 2023)

VA platforms for uploads.

  • The same platform can hire VAs for product uploads too. Screen candidates, because a good VA can upload hundreds of SKUs per day. [L1274.077] (Source A, 2018, older)
  • A store can scale with VAs for customer support and uploads. Full-time support staff can handle the work for an established brand, but a new store can start with a reliable VA platform. [L1274.079] (Source A, 2018, older)

Catalog, content and pricing

Product pages.

  • Maintain a consistent layout standard across all products. Ensure photo ratios are identical (for example, square) so they display properly in collections. Use a standard template for descriptions. Update the SOP template as you improve the design, and let team members contribute suggestions. [L0263.039] (Source A)
  • Each new product added to a multi-product page requires the full cycle: duplicate, set Active, create its template, assign the template and save. Skipping any one of these steps causes content changes to affect other products. [L1013.017] (Source D, 2026)
  • To audit products that are already live, work back through existing product pages and check each one against the on-page SEO checklist. This is simple enough to hand to a team member or VA, and most people find they are missing some items on older pages. [L1191.025] (Source B, 2021-22, older)

Content and images.

  • Product content such as photos and videos usually comes from the supplier, so you rarely need to create it yourself. Free online tools are available for ad overlays and text on images or video. [L0358.054] (Source A, 2020, older)
  • A VA can handle the repetitive work of uploading supplier product content and building product pages. Delegating these tasks frees your time for supplier relationships and strategy. [L0358.055] (Source A, 2020, older)
  • Resizing large batches of images is repetitive, manual work that should be outsourced. [L1163.014] (Source B, 2021-22, older)

Account location.

  • Upload from the team's own accounts and region rather than assigning posting to someone in another country, because account location affects reach. The owner had not yet considered this when outsourcing uploads. [L1077.035] (Source C)

VA tasks in the store.

  • VA tasks in the store included handling pricing errors, updating products when suppliers change prices, and replacing product photos. One VA learned basic Shopify skills on the side to do these updates. [L0921.023] (Source A)
  • Store speed fixes can be outsourced to Shopify experts found on storetasker.com or on Upwork. This is an option when the owner does not want to fix performance problems personally. [L1446.021] (Source A)

Supplier outreach and product integration

The integration cycle.

  • The product integration cycle is: gain supplier approval, upload their products immediately, return to supplier outreach, and repeat until all approved suppliers are live. [L0128.002] (Source A)
  • Once a supplier approves you, upload all of its products immediately and remove the demo products. Continue contacting more suppliers on your list after the first approval. [L0835.127] (Source A, 2023)
  • Upload new products from a supplier as soon as the supplier approves you, then check for new suppliers every six months. [L0665.109] (Source A, 2022, older)

Recruiting suppliers.

  • For a new store, bring on 20 to 25 new suppliers per month. This creates a deep supplier pool to choose from when fulfilling orders. [L0263.036] (Source A)
  • Track every supplier in a supplier tracking sheet with the date of your last contact, the outcome, and the next follow-up date. Use it to schedule follow-ups, such as contacting a rejected supplier again in three months. [L0835.126] (Source A, 2023)
  • Supplier outreach, including contacting new suppliers, following up, and uploading their products, is a continuous task that never fully ends. Once it becomes too much for the owner, it is a candidate for a dedicated outreach person. [L1446.033] (Source A)

Quality checks on delegated work

  • Check every product upload closely, whether you do it yourself or someone else does. Wrong descriptions, measurements or prices from CSV or Excel uploads can go live unnoticed and hurt conversions. [L0470.033] (Source A, 2023)
  • When outsourcing product uploads, check the work regularly. Common errors are the wrong image on a product or the wrong price on a product. [L0663.041] (Source A, 2019, older)
  • Check that each product row matches its title, description and price before going live. [L1111.161] (Source A, 2020, older)
  • If you outsource product uploads, work in small batches and double-check every price, image and product detail before publishing. Mixed-up prices or images are a common and costly error. [L0247.120] (Source A, 2021, older)
  • Before a new VA uploads a full batch, ask for one sample upload per manufacturer and check it. This catches formatting or quality problems on a small scale before they affect the whole catalogue. [L0832.063] (Source A)
  • Verify outsourced work before relying on it. Outsourced product review or approval work may give unsatisfactory results. [L1094.109] (Source A, 2023)
  • Check the daily fulfilment routine closely in the first weeks, then reduce it to periodic review once the routine runs as planned. [L1358.029]

Downtime tasks and customer insight

Downtime ideas for a VA when the inbox is empty. [L1000.044] (Source A)

  1. Research suppliers that sell the same products, checking whether they offer better deals or have lowered prices.
  2. Learn to upload products, so the assistant can handle new supplier listings when they are added.
  3. If the business is mostly B2B, have the assistant look for potential customers and make cold calls to generate more sales.
  4. Keep a shared task board, such as Trello, where the owner adds downtime tasks as they come to mind.
