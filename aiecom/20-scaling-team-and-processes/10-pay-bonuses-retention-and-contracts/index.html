20.10 Pay, bonuses, retention and contracts

Pay levels by role

  • Full-time offshore VA in the Philippines (older: 2019): typically 400 to 500 USD per month. Paying slightly above the common rate attracts candidates with corporate experience and reduces turnover. [L0356.057]
  • Full-time virtual staff via online-jobs.ph (older: 2020): about 300 USD per month to start, rising to about 350, 400 or 500 USD per month for experienced staff. [L0852.103]
  • Full-time VA after proving themselves (no year given): about 200 USD per week, based on the speaker's experience with Philippine VAs. The source gives this figure per week, so it is not directly comparable with the monthly figures above. [L0921.026]
  • Product lister, starting salary (no year given): about 500 USD per month. This is lower than customer service because product listers do not interact with customers and have less variable demand. [L0263.037]
  • Routine VA work at about 10 USD per hour (no year given): tends to produce lower quality and team members who leave. [L0413.011]
  • Where the sources differ: the 2019 range (400 to 500 USD) describes a full-time offshore VA with corporate experience, while the 2020 range starts at 300 USD and rises with experience. Both apply to Philippine full-time hires, at different career stages and with different candidate profiles. [L0356.057, L0852.103]
  • Target level (no year given): pay VAs close to US minimum wage levels, treat them well and build a team culture. [L1437.032]

Pay above the market

  • Pay virtual assistants above market rates to attract candidates with strong English and previous experience, because higher pay reduces the risk of poor performance. [L0230.013]
  • Pay above the cheapest available rate to improve output quality and consistency and to keep people for years. [L0413.011]
  • Pay a good VA somewhat above the typical Philippine rate to secure quality and loyalty; strong, engaged VAs are then willing to take on additional tasks for other businesses, even after the original store is sold. [L0921.019]
  • Pay above the average rate for the role. A second hire at average pay produced disappointing candidates; raising the offer by about 100 USD per month brought much better applicants. [L1473.018]

Pay models: per task, per product, per article and hourly

General rules

  • Pay per task or per deliverable, such as per product description, per word or per batch of 500 images, instead of hourly. Hourly pay gives no incentive to work efficiently. Exception: ongoing work such as social media management. [L1122.019]
  • Offer a bonus for finishing a per-task job within a set time. This motivates the freelancer to work efficiently. [L1122.020]
  • Budget first, then check value: decide what you can afford, then pay more for tasks that deliver high value. [L1122.021]
  • Set budgets by what you can afford rather than by a fixed rule, and judge price by quality tested through the trial process. Higher spending often yields better quality, but some good writers charge less. [L0379.017]

Procedure: working out pay for a freelancer or VA (no year given)

  1. Set the budget first: decide what you can realistically afford. [L1122.021]
  2. Assess the value the task delivers, such as whether it produces a big jump in profit, and pay more for high-value tasks. [L1122.021]
  3. Research the market on platforms like Upwork to see what freelancers ask and what the average pay is, for example for a VA in the Philippines. [L1122.021]
  4. Negotiate with freelancers, who are often willing to split work or adjust scope to fit your budget. [L1122.021]

Written content and video (older: 2021 to 2022 for Source B; 2019 for Source A where marked)

  • Outsourced content work should always be paid per finished project, never per hour, because hourly pay gives no incentive to finish quickly and can encourage dishonest freelancers to slow down. Per-word pay for written content is an accepted alternative. (older: 2021 to 2022) [L0379.002]
  • Treat each content piece as piece work: agree a price for the delivered finished piece and negotiate it before starting. Apply the same logic to video editing by agreeing a price per video based on its length and requirements. (older: 2021 to 2022) [L0379.003]
  • Pay writers a fixed price per article rather than an hourly rate, because hourly pay makes costs hard to predict when some writers are slow. Quality matters more than volume. [L0382.011]
  • Outsourcing a 500-word article to a non-native English writer on Odesk cost about 5 USD per 500 words at the time described. This is a rough rate; verify current freelancer rates before you budget. (older: rate from an earlier period, no year given) [L0380.020]

Product uploads

  • Pay upload staff per completed product or per task instead of a fixed salary when output varies. Some part-time upload staff earned more than a full-time hire in busy months, and pay followed the work completed. This keeps costs tied to output. (older: 2021 to 2022) [L1396.040]
  • One VA was paid per product uploaded, not hourly, and earned roughly 800 to 900 USD per month during a busy period with many new suppliers. In slower months the same VA earned roughly 300 to 400 USD per month. [L0832.045]
  • Calculate the rate per product before setting payment: rate per product = total payment divided by number of products. Multiply the number of products by the rate to get the payout. (older: 2021 to 2022) [L1396.039]
  • Worked example: a 200 USD test task of 179 products gives a rate of about 1.12 USD per product (200 ÷ 179), not 1.16 USD as the lesson stated. Recalculate the rate before setting payment. (older: 2021 to 2022) [L1396.039]

Hourly pay: where it fits

  • For reformatting a supplier CSV into Shopify's import format, a freelancer who already knows CSV files can be paid about 20 USD per hour. The work takes roughly 20 minutes to one hour however many products the file holds, so an hourly rate is cheaper than a fixed price per product. (older: 2019) [L1275.073]
  • Price a VA task by checking whether the data is ready to upload. A CSV that only needs reformatting takes about 20 minutes, while scattered data entered one by one can take dozens of hours. Hire only people with many reviews for the exact workflow. (older: 2021) [L1112.073]

Commissions and profit bonuses

Principles

  • Do not pay staff only a percentage of sales and expect them to drive results alone. Use clear KPIs combined with point-based incentives that can be redeemed for money, time off or products. (older: 2020) [L1111.160]
  • Performance-based bonuses and compensation motivate higher performance, especially for sales teams. Set clear rules about acceptable behaviour, such as collaboration rather than toxic competition, to prevent conflict while keeping the drive for results. [L0263.032]
  • Use a profit-share or incentive model when hiring VAs with a documented process; this lets a small team test products faster. [L1194.020]

Commission bases in the sources

  • A common commission for a VA is 1% of the total sales they are responsible for. Once you hire the right assistant, a small extra bonus in the paycheck helps keep them motivated. [L0829.026]
  • Reward a sales-focused VA with 1% of the sales they generate. Track the commission by sales channel so it is easy to see who generated each sale. [L1000.042]
  • Pay 1% of the total sale price only on customer exchanges that need a quote or multi-unit requests, or that run longer than two back-and-forth emails. Simple questions about stock, colour or shipping are handled as ordinary customer service without commission. [L0832.049, L0832.059] (merged: duplicate rules)
  • Use net profit instead of sales volume where discounting is a risk. A bonus based on sales volume made VAs discount heavily to hit the target, which hurt profit. [L1437.029]
  • Monthly VA bonus = 1% of net profit, paid to each VA. Example: a store with 10,000 USD net profit in a month pays each VA 100 USD as a bonus. [L1437.030]
  • For large B2B orders, the VA can receive a bonus of 1% of the order value. This is a bonus, not a commission on sales. [L1473.029]

Phone answering

  • Commission for phone support aligns agent incentives with sales outcomes. This structure works well for low call-volume businesses (2 to 5 calls per day), where hourly wages would be inefficient. (older: 2020) [L0245.053]
  • Early on, one VA was paid 5 USD for each day the assistant answered the phone. Later the fee was a flat 150 USD per month for answering calls, which is roughly 5 USD per weekday. (older: no year given) [L0910.051]
  • Flat-fee cost per weekday = monthly fee ÷ number of weekdays in the month. The lesson's 150 USD per month is about 5 USD per calendar day; with about 22 weekdays per month the cost is about 6.80 USD per weekday (150 ÷ 22 = 6.82, which the source rounds). [L0910.052]
  • A phone-answering fee is worth paying because each sale-related call can be worth 50 to 300 USD, while the assistant may already be at a computer for other work. The lesson recommends a flat monthly rate rather than hourly or per-call pay after the first months. [L0910.053]

Sales roles

  • Assign a designated representative for B2B deals and scale the commission with deal size. Large corporate deals may take months to close, so the commission should reflect that effort. [L0832.051]
  • Reward strong deals with an extra bonus in the paycheck, in addition to commission. Good pay and recognition make team members work to close better deals. [L0832.052]
  • Drawing account for uncertain sales: an early mail-sales hire started with a 25 USD weekly drawing account plus a 5% commission on mail sales. Once the commission earned far more than the base, the company replaced the arrangement with a fixed salary. Use a drawing account with commission while sales are uncertain, and switch to salary once the commission level is clear. [L1369.125]

Raises and career growth

Timing of the first raise

  • Plan the first salary raise after 6 to 8 months and communicate this during hiring, so the employee knows growth is planned. This gives motivation and prevents a feeling of being stuck at a flat rate. [L0263.031]
  • Give an annual raise after the first year, plus occasional bonuses. [L1473.029, L0230.022]
  • In the 30-day procedure, raise pay if the work is good after 30 days (see the hiring section). [L0852.104]
  • The sources give different first-raise points (30 days, 6 to 8 months, after the first year). None says which to choose; they are presented here as alternatives. [L0852.104, L0263.031, L0230.022, L1473.029]

After the first raise

  • Tie future increases to performance, such as completing new projects or taking on responsibility, rather than to time. [L0263.031]
  • Show each team member where they can grow, such as eventually managing other VAs, and share company goals with them. The speaker set a goal of about 10 employees for those who grow with the company. (older: 2020) [L0832.048]
  • Build long-term relationships with the same staff instead of repeatedly training new people for the same task. Do not start with salaries you cannot afford. [L1431.031]

Procedure: salary progression (no year given)

  1. Set a starting salary that was negotiated with the hire. [L1431.027]
  2. Agree on salary goals with long-term staff and review them every month or year. [L1431.027]
  3. Raise pay when the stores grow and the team member takes on more responsibility. [L1431.027]
  4. If a bad month occurs, a loyal team may accept a short-term salary cut. [L1431.027]
  5. Only pay raises the business can afford, based on calculated monthly profit. [L1431.027]

Example of progression (no year given)

  • A team member started at 100 USD per month two to three years earlier and set goals with the owner each year. Pay reached 800 USD, then 1,500 USD per month, as the stores grew. [L1431.028]

Bonuses, rewards and recognition

Bonus types

  • Pay a small bonus after a strong month, give extra paid days off, fund relevant training and increase responsibility. These cost little relative to the value the team creates. [L1122.026]
  • Reward large milestones with bonuses, such as an extra payment after 1,000 products uploaded, to keep staff motivated. Milestone bonuses increased loyalty without extra management effort. Plan these rewards in advance. (older: 2021 to 2022) [L1396.041]
  • Paying bonuses is fine. (older: 2021 to 2022) [L1054.035]
  • Paid time off is also a good way to recognise people. (older: 2021 to 2022) [L1054.035]

Equipment and workstations

  • Hardware programme (older: 2020 source, no year given): identify tools that slow down team productivity, for example single screens for an operations team processing 100+ orders per month. Purchase hardware such as screens, laptops and chairs and distribute them to the team. Result: a significant morale boost and measurable productivity improvement. This is worth the investment for core teams. [L0263.033]

  • Workstation programme:

    1. Offer 50% company reimbursement, max 1,000 USD total purchase per employee. [L0263.034]
    2. The employee pays 50%. [L0263.034]
    3. The employee applies through the manager and provides the purchase link and price. [L0263.034]
    4. The owner approves each purchase. [L0263.034]
    5. The employee buys a laptop, chair, mouse or headset. [L0263.034]
    6. The company reimburses 50% after purchase. [L0263.034]

    This builds loyalty and ensures the team has proper equipment while sharing the cost. [L0263.034]

  • Reward examples include buying a staff member new equipment such as a laptop, or paying for higher-level training that builds skills they can use later in other jobs and that strengthens their resume. (older: 2021 to 2022) [L1054.037]

Recognition

  • Thank and praise a partner or team member when a task is completed, because recognition motivates them to take the next task. Pair praise with a clear request for the next step. [L0832.014]
  • Recognise and reward team members by giving feedback and listening to concerns. [L1122.026]
  • Regular recognition of good work encourages people to do their best and builds loyalty. Recognise people and tell them directly when they do a good job. (older: 2021 to 2022) [L1054.039]
  • Build a personal relationship with your team, for example by asking about their safety and well-being during a crisis. Staff who feel valued are more willing to go beyond their job and stay longer. [L1122.027]
  • Remote team members were flown to meet the owner in person for two nights during a trip abroad. The owner said this moved the working relationship to a higher level and made the team feel more like a unit. This is a low-cost way to strengthen a remote VA team. [L1437.041]

Rewarding good work (older: 2021 to 2022)

  1. Always pay people on time. [L1054.035]
  2. Where work is provided above your normal standard, find ways to reward it. [L1054.035]
  3. Paying bonuses is fine. [L1054.035]
  4. If you work with Filipino staff, pay the 13th month. [L1054.035]
  5. Paid time off is also a good way to recognise people. [L1054.035]

Warning on large rewards (older: 2021 to 2022)

  • Overly large rewards set expectations you may not be able to keep. Use rewards sparingly so the person does not come to expect the same big reward every time. [L1054.038]

Paying on time and cash flow

  • Pay team members twice per month, at the beginning and middle of the month. This provides consistent cash flow every 2 weeks and is predictable. Use a single system for all payments, for example from spreadsheet to payment processor, to maintain a standard. (older: no year given) [L0263.021]
  • Always pay on time. For per-project work, pay as soon as the project is submitted unless you agreed a different arrangement. For weekly pay, pay on the same day each week at as close to the same time as possible. (older: 2021 to 2022) [L1054.033]
  • Staff with families depend on predictable income, and late or irregular pay makes it unlikely they will stay long term. Your agreement to pay is part of the deal, so you must meet it. (older: 2021 to 2022) [L1054.034]
  • Do not pay a freelancer in full before any work is delivered, especially on a platform without buyer protection, because you can be left with an unfinished task and no recourse. Choose platforms that hold payment until you accept the finished work, or pay in stages tied to deliverables. (older: no year given) [L1418.011]
  • Structure hiring so that payment depends on delivered work. Paying freelancers upfront, or assigning only simple tasks before payment, can lose money when work is never delivered. (older: no year given) [L1433.002]

Statutory obligations

  • In the Philippines, the 13th month is a local requirement, similar to year-end holiday pay. Pay it to staff who have worked for you there for any period and are still with you at year-end. (older: 2021 to 2022) [L1054.036]
  • If you work with Filipino staff, pay the 13th month. (older: 2021 to 2022) [L1054.035]

Hiring terms and trial periods

  • Hire VAs on full-time contracts with exclusivity instead of 3 to 4 hours a day, because part-time staff tend to look for other full-time work and become distracted. State at hiring that they work only for your business. (older: 2019) [L0356.058]
  • Hiring and retaining virtual staff procedure (older: 2020):
    1. Hire the first person on a 30-day contract. [L0852.104]
    2. Monitor their work and give proactive feedback to help them improve. [L0852.104]
    3. If the work is good after 30 days, raise their pay, for example from 300 to 350 USD per month. [L0852.104]
    4. Set shared goals, such as a bonus when monthly sales pass a set amount. [L0852.104]
  • Virtual staff for full-time roles can be found on online-jobs.ph for about 300 USD per month, with pay rising to about 350, 400 or 500 USD per month for experienced staff. (older: 2020) [L0852.103]
  • OnlineJobs.ph is used to hire VAs, including staff in the Philippines. Many hires in the team stayed for five or more years. (older: 2022) [L0803.081]
  • Prefer a long-term assistant who knows the store and processes. The speaker's team had worked together for four years; once trained, the team can run ad campaigns, so the founder only reviews campaigns and makes decisions. [L1432.033]
  • Starting with an assistant you pay out of revenue can work when cash is short. Move to a monthly fixed salary once revenue exists. [L1432.030]
  • Calling outsourced helpers team members or employees instead of virtual assistants supports a culture where people stay long term. The framing matters most when the owner is hiring for ongoing roles rather than short tasks. (older: 2022) [L1281.129]

Contracts

  • Execute a formal written contract before the virtual assistant begins work. [L0230.017]
  • Before a VA starts training, sign a written contract. A generic VA contract can be found online and adapted to the situation. The contract should state pay, weekly work hours, holidays observed, vacation notice, notice period for quitting, and the training obligations. [L1473.038]
  • Use simple contracts for team members in the Philippines, Bangladesh and South America. Standard templates (LegalZoom or similar) are sufficient. Contracts are not always necessary from day one; some team members ask for them later for credit or visa purposes. This is not standard practice but helpful when requested. (older: no year given) [L0263.046]
  • Virtual assistant contracts: a formal contract is easier to set up when a hiring platform is used. A private arrangement should be based on trust and a clear revenue split. [L1432.030]
  • Test products with a VA using a documented process and an incentive model, so a VA can check products against the criteria list and free the operator's time for decisions. [L1194.020]

Employment classification

  • Hiring ongoing staff in the country where the business is registered can lead the government to treat them as employees rather than contractors. This creates obligations that small businesses often cannot manage, which is a reason to start with overseas workers. This applies in the US, Australia and the UK. [L1122.017]
  • Use contract forms and terms that match the worker's status (see Contracts above; simple contracts for Philippine, Bangladeshi and South American team members). [L0263.046, L1122.017]

Keeping staff long-term

  • Retain good team members for as long as possible. Turnover wastes the time invested in training and in knowing the suppliers, market and customers. Keep expectations stable and change them only with a conversation. [L1122.025]
  • Build long-term relationships with the same staff. Do not start with salaries you cannot afford. [L1431.031]
  • Staff who feel valued are more willing to go beyond their job and stay longer. [L1122.027]
  • Pay VAs close to US minimum wage levels, treat them well and build a team culture. Good pay and treatment keep turnover low and produce much more committed work. [L1437.032]
  • Paying a good VA somewhat above the typical Philippine rate secures quality and loyalty. [L0921.019]
  • Predictable pay and timing make staff more likely to stay long term. [L1054.034]
  • A loyal team may accept a short-term salary cut in a bad month. [L1431.027]
  • Show growth paths and share company goals so staff can see a future with the business. [L0832.048]
