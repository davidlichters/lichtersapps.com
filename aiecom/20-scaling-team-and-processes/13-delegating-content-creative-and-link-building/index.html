20.13 Delegating content, creative and link building

Deciding What to Delegate

  • Write the first 10 to 15 articles yourself to confirm that content marketing works before hiring writers. Invest in a writing team only after the channel has proven results. [L0382.005]
  • Additional content writing is not needed at the start. A few authoritative articles beat many generic ones, and outsourcing blog writing to Fiverr or iWriter is not recommended. (Older advice, 2019.) [L0357.061]
  • Write product descriptions and SEO content yourself, or learn them from free training, before paying someone on Fiverr. The training covers SEO in detail. (Older advice, 2022.) [L0665.108]
  • Build the business beyond your own hands by hiring people to replace your daily tasks step by step. Start with writing, the easiest role to delegate, then add email, design, editing and data entry. [L0380.016]
  • Outsource tasks you cannot do well yourself, such as detailed mock-ups and graphics, once cash flow allows. Start with rough mock-ups yourself and bring in specialists to reduce errors as the business grows. [L1438.050]
  • Decide between doing an edit yourself and paying a freelancer by comparing the hours a first attempt takes with the freelance price. Learning a new editing app can take days, so outsourcing simple tasks can cost less than the time they take you. [L1427.055]
  • Outsourcing ad creative to freelancers does not guarantee strong results, and learning video editing from scratch takes a long time. Plan for both before relying on either. [L0341.024]
  • Dropshipping content and product testing should be done by someone who can produce content rapidly, or by a freelancer paid to deliver within 24 hours. [L0577.065]
  • Keep the creative team separate from routine operations. Copywriters need freedom to incubate, and their output drops when they are held to the same visible schedule as operational staff. [L1366.067]
  • Creative and offer quality drive results more than campaign structure. A winning creative makes the strategy matter much less. Test many creatives and offers continuously, since creative testing is one of the largest parts of the job, whether done in-house or outsourced. [L0995.027]
  • Dropshipping sites can use virtual assistants (VAs) for repetitive tasks such as social media, email, customer service and content curation. Use platforms such as AppWork (or alternatives such as OnlineJobsPH) to hire them, and focus your own time on strategy. [L0226.038]
  • Create a clear role split for every task: the owner keeps strategy and final approval, and delegates the rest. [L0380.017, L0380.016]

Hiring and Vetting

Hiring the first content producer (older advice, 2021-22)

  1. Post a job for a freelancer, such as a writer or video editor. [L0381.013]
  2. Give the candidate a test piece of work. [L0381.013]
  3. Hire the best candidate. [L0381.013]
  4. Start producing the first content piece with them. [L0381.013]
  • Hire quickly and imperfectly rather than after a long search. The first producer can be replaced later once output starts. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0381.016]

Hiring freelance writers on Upwork

  1. Write a job post that sets clear expectations: one article per week, a keyword with guidelines, and finishing the article with an editor. [L0382.007]
  2. State the price: $50 to $100 for a 1,000-word article. [L0382.007]
  3. Say you need to hire three freelancers, which attracts more applicants. [L0382.007]
  4. Say that new Upwork freelancers without ratings are welcome, because you will vet them by reading their work. [L0382.007]
  5. Expect roughly 20 to 40 applicants per post. [L0382.007]
  6. Have an editor shortlist the top five applicants. [L0382.007]
  7. Check each shortlisted candidate for a style match with your brand. [L0382.007]
  8. Hire three to five writers and pay for a one-article trial. [L0382.007]
  9. Hire the best trial writers for 10 more articles and keep only the best. [L0382.007]
  • Freelancers abroad can disappear, get sick, or deliver work that does not match their samples. Always test a writer with a paid trial article before committing to more work. [L0382.008]
  • Upwork gives access to writers and editors worldwide, and lower overhead can mean lower rates. Low rates do not automatically mean low quality, but a clear vetting process is needed to find strong candidates. [L0382.009]
  • Writers from lower-cost markets can produce good results at the start. Ranking for bigger keywords requires native-level writing and higher pay, so pay more when competition for the keyword is higher. [L0382.010]
  • Use a clear-writing guide, such as Paula LaRocque's book on writing, as the standard for judging editor candidates. Use it to recognise clear, natural prose before you hire an editor to screen writers. [L0382.059]

Finding writers through industry communities (older advice, 2019)

  1. Join Facebook groups for your industry. [L0357.062]
  2. Post a request for a paid article on a topic that matches the audience. [L0357.062]
  3. Ask members with experience to message you privately. [L0357.062]
  4. Hire a writer who already knows the product and the community. [L0357.062]

Hiring a native English editor

  • Hire a native English editor you respect to review articles and emails written by non-native writers. Judge the editor by reading their own writing for clarity, conciseness and persuasion. [L0380.019]
  • Savings only materialise when the editor works quickly. [L0380.019]
  • A native editor needs about 15 minutes for a 500 to 600 word article, then does a second pass. If an editor spends more than an hour on an article that short, something in the process is wrong. [L0380.021]

Hiring and vetting a content team member

  1. Post the role on freelance or job boards, such as Upwork, Fiverr or a Philippines-based virtual assistant job board. [L1077.033]
  2. Interview candidates. For a video editor, consider a chat over WhatsApp with sample files. [L1077.033]
  3. Have the new hire produce test TikToks. [L1077.033]
  4. Audit those test videos for brand fit, message and compliance risk. [L1077.033]
  5. If the work is good, give the hire posting access, or have a VA handle uploads. [L1077.033]

Hiring the right role for the job

  • Written content needs a content writer. Video content needs an editor, plus a separate person to record if someone else is doing the recording. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0383.006]
  • Higher-quality writers are less likely to do manual publishing tasks, so expect to hand posting to a separate virtual assistant. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0379.013]
  • Name the producer for each piece, whether a writer, freelancer, VA or yourself. If several people work on one piece, such as a writer plus an infographic designer or a video editor hired on Fiverr, record each person so responsibilities stay clear. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0384.016]

Pay, Test Projects and Briefing

General rules for outsourcing content production (older advice, 2021-22)

  1. Always pay per project, never per hour. [L0379.005]
  2. Always start with a test project. [L0379.005]
  3. Use a quality job posting, with a template. [L0379.005]
  4. Provide clear instructions to freelancers on what a piece of content should include. [L0379.005]
  • Give each freelancer written guidelines for every piece: what it should include. The guideline document works as an onboarding guide and SOP, telling the freelancer what to deliver. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0379.007]

Briefing a research or content writer (older advice, 2021-22)

  • When briefing a freelance content writer for research, give them access to your attention matrix document. For an ongoing content writer, also give them access to your content planning document so they can see what you fill in there. Both are part of the instructions you provide. [L0379.021]
  • Hand the finished customer profile to anyone who writes or markets for you, including in-house staff, new hires, outsourced copywriters, ad agencies, email agencies and customer service, so they learn about your customers quickly. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0907.003]

Writer checklist, given to every new writer and editor

  1. Know the customer from the customer profile. [L0380.022]
  2. Write as if speaking to the customer face to face, in a casual tone. [L0380.022]
  3. Use short words, short sentences and short paragraphs. [L0380.022]
  4. Use bullets and lists where they fit. [L0380.022]
  5. Give concrete examples. [L0380.022]
  6. Follow the rule for how often to use each keyword. [L0380.022]
  7. Follow the rule for how often to use subheadings. [L0380.022]
  8. Check the readability score before handing in the article. [L0380.022]

Pricing for specific tasks

  • Custom images, such as blog hero images, YouTube thumbnails, in-post images and infographics, can be outsourced on Fiverr. Do not spend time creating or editing images yourself. A professional custom image costs around 10 USD. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0379.014]
  • A custom-designed image such as a blog hero image or a YouTube thumbnail costs around 10 USD on Fiverr. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0379.015]
  • When outsourcing video editing per project, state the video's running time (for example, a 30-minute video) and list exactly what the editor must do. The editor then quotes one price for that project, and you can negotiate it. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0379.022]
  • A freelancer charged 5 USD per video to translate captions into French and add a French voiceover to short TikTok-style clips. [L1427.056]
  • Hiring a freelancer on Upwork to build a simple calculator page was estimated at $50 to $100 and could be completed in one day. This was used as the cost benchmark for building a tool page for a keyword. [L1120.025]
  • A product-description team of three freelancers working on a project can produce about 350 unique 200-word product descriptions per week, so a complete store can be ready in about one week. [L1122.007]
  • Fiverr can be used to outsource images for blog posts and content pages, while Canva can be used to make basic graphics yourself. Both keep content production affordable. [L1120.026]
  • Do not pay for social media management that offers only a few posts per month, such as 1,500 dollars for four posts. At that level it does not generate meaningful returns. In the example, the owner had already spent over 4,500 dollars. (Source from 2023.) [L1283.022]

Writer rates: sources differ

  • Upwork writers at $50 to $100 per 1,000-word article were the benchmark for a managed article team. [L0382.007]
  • A freelance writer at about $30 per article was too expensive to fill 60 to 80 empty collection pages. Hiring in-house or on a part-time contract was more practical for ongoing content. Estimate the total cost of the content backlog before choosing a freelance rate. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1396.047]
  • Use the Upwork rate for article production, and use a part-time or in-house contract when the backlog is large and ongoing. Pay more for keywords with higher competition. [L0382.007, L1396.047, L0382.010]

Production Roles and Team Structure

Roles when systemising content marketing

  • Writer: drafts articles from keywords. [L0380.017]
  • Email copywriter: writes emails and is paid more than the article writer, because the email list is the most valuable audience. [L0380.017]
  • Editor and project manager: reviews articles and emails, and coordinates everyone. This can be a native US English speaker. [L0380.017]
  • Designer or illustrator: creates keyword images. [L0380.017]
  • Data entry: uploads articles to the blog and enters emails into the email provider. This is a lower-level role, estimated at about three to four hours of freelancer time. [L0380.017]
  • Quality assurance: the owner's final review. [L0380.017]

Reference team size

  • A content team that produced about 7 articles per week consisted of 7 writers, 3 editors, 2 graphic designers and 1 data entry assistant. The data entry assistant prepares search result research for writers. [L0382.006]

Team size by stage (older advice, 2021-22)

  • Once consistent content has produced results for about 12 months, you can grow in three ways: publish more than 2 pieces per month, increase promotion of existing pieces, or hire an additional person. Output and promotion should keep rising rather than being held flat once results are visible. [L0383.014]
  • Once consistent content has produced results for about 12 months, raise output beyond 2 pieces per month or hire an additional person to keep growing. [L0383.008]
  • Use a fixed two-month review to keep the content process running. Spend at most about 30 minutes per review adding research and ideas to the planner. [L0381.015]
  • Spread one content writer across several stores by working one store at a time, for about one to two months per store, until its collection pages are complete. A backlog of 60 to 80 empty collection pages was expected to take about six to eight months at that pace. Estimate the backlog size before assigning the writer to a store. [L1396.069]

Outsourcing the pipeline (older advice, 2021-22)

  • Outsourcing keyword research and the attention matrix to a virtual assistant is acceptable and cheap. The owner does not need to do all content tasks personally. [L0381.014]
  • Outsourcing techniques from Amazon Kindle eBook publishing, where creators build systems around templates and questionnaires to manage third-party writers, can be adapted to dropship store content creation. This lets you scale without expanding the core team. [L0326.003]
  • A VA can take a finished Word document or draft, insert images, videos and other assets, and publish it to the website. An assistant already handling Shopify product uploads can usually learn blog posting from short instructions. [L0379.012]
  • Outsource one task at a time. Perform each task yourself until you understand it, write an SOP, then delegate. [L0945.005]

Article Production Workflow and Trackers

Production hub

  • Use one shared Google Doc per keyword as the production hub. It holds the keyword, the article section where the writer drafts and the editor edits, the image, the article URL, the email subject line and the email body. [L0380.023]

Per-keyword article workflow

  1. Change to the next keyword on the list. [L0380.024]
  2. Writer drafts the article and hands it to the editor. [L0380.024]
  3. Editor edits the draft and returns it to the writer. [L0380.024]
  4. Writer rewrites the article and hands it to the editor. [L0380.024]
  5. Editor finalises the article and hands it to the email writer. [L0380.024]
  6. Designer delivers the initial sketch of the keyword image. [L0380.024]
  7. Owner gives feedback on the sketch. [L0380.024]
  8. Designer delivers the final coloured image. [L0380.024]
  9. Email writer adds the article link, then writes the subject line and the email body. [L0380.024]
  10. Data entry uploads the article to the blog and loads the email into the email provider. [L0380.024]
  11. Quality check: confirm every step is complete, then send. [L0380.024]

Tracker setup

  • Track every keyword in a spreadsheet with one row per keyword and one column per task, with the team member's name in each column header. Mark each completed handoff with an X. [L0380.025]
  • Use the grid to find bottlenecks, for example when ten finished drafts are waiting for the editor. [L0380.025]
  • Track each article in a shared spreadsheet with one column per production step and a checkmark when each step is done. This shows who is responsible for each step and where work is piling up, such as when an editor has a backlog. [L0382.012]
  • Outsourcing without a tracker hides where work is stuck, such as drafts waiting for editing or articles never uploaded to the blog. Without a per-keyword overview, you only notice the problem when the output is already missing. [L0380.026]

Operating principle

  • Set up systems and check results regularly, then revise processes when results fall short. Process, people and product are the core elements of any business, and content is mostly a process and people challenge. [L0382.050]

Video, Creative and File Management

Creative strategy stages (Source E 2025-26)

  • Creative strategy runs through four stages: conception, planning, production and management. An ad has to pass through all four to be produced and tested properly. [L0508.016]
  • Conception: develop the ad concept through research before anything is made. This is where the idea and style are chosen on the basis of evidence. [L0508.017]
  • Planning: define how the ad will be put together and which assets must be produced. A plan is needed before any filming, creator briefing or design work starts. [L0508.018]
  • Production: the actual creation of content, such as filming, obtaining material from creators or designing assets. A plan alone produces no ads. [L0508.019]
  • Management, in order:
    1. Upload the ads and confirm they are live. [L0508.020]
    2. Check that the media buying is set up correctly. [L0508.020]
    3. Assess the performance metrics of each ad. [L0508.020]
    4. Use the learnings to feed back into the system. [L0508.020]
    5. Repeat the cycle, iterating on the ads that worked and stopping the ones that did not. [L0508.020]
  • Most brands do only conception and production, while planning and management are ignored or done poorly. Their ideas often appear sporadically and are launched after scraping material together, which makes it impossible to test the angles methodically. [L0508.021]

Closing the creative loop

  • Measure what is launched and how it performs, use the analysis to suggest the next creatives, then brief them and contact influencers when needed. Each step should feed the next. [L0885.023]
  • Build new creatives by combining hooks and testimonials from a large research list. This lets you scale testing without filming each ad from scratch. Keep the research list growing so new combinations keep coming. [L0901.016]
  • When one concept proves profitable, scale it by making new scripts around it and small hook or detail changes, and keep investing in that concept. Once a fresh creative set is tested weekly or bi-weekly, the winning concept becomes the one to push. [L0902.024]
  • Creating resized static ad variants can be delegated to a VA once the process is documented. The resizing step is repetitive and does not need the owner. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1169.026]
  • Brainstorm sessions should run weekly or biweekly, so creatives can ideate, test new ideas, and identify emerging trends to stay ahead of the curve. [L0260.018]
  • Once one editorial page converts, reuse its structure for other products by changing only the character, the problem, the expert and the product facts. Keep the AIDA order and the 70/30 split fixed so each new page follows a tested pattern. [L0578.039]
  • Once a sales letter or funnel has proven a closing structure, reuse that structure for new offers. Only the opening and the body need fresh creative work. [L0378.096]
  • Use the awareness pyramid as a recurring check across traffic-based work such as content marketing, pay-per-click advertising, retargeting and email marketing, not only for conversion rate optimisation. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0481.015]

Planning before building

  • Before building any new email sequence, retargeting campaign or content-based ad, write the plan first, covering what the message should say and when it should be sent. Repeat this step each time a new email idea, new product or new content piece is considered. The plan makes the build faster and easier to hand to outsourced staff. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0493.023]
  • The target is to raise email volume to two, three or four emails per week. A founder cannot produce that volume by hand, so modular building blocks are needed to make it operationally possible. [L0610.011]
  • Email marketing example: an email marketer hired from onlinejobs.ph, trained on your products and Klaviyo, creates campaigns she never did before. She becomes an expert in your business through hands-on work. This on-the-job training is often more effective than hiring an "experienced" expert who will not adapt to your specific needs. [L0263.042]

Video file management

  1. Create one main project folder with three subfolders: video, audio and assets. [L0344.039]
  2. Store raw footage and product footage in the video folder, including stock footage. [L0344.039]
  3. Store music in the audio folder, and split it into sound effects and songs. [L0344.039]
  4. Store graphics and photos in the assets folder, in an imagery subfolder. [L0344.039]
  5. Keep the final project files in a cloud folder such as Google Drive, and keep a local synced copy, rather than only in Downloads. [L0344.039]
  • Moving or deleting source files after importing them breaks the links in the project, so footage can disappear when reopened. Store all media in a fixed location, such as a cloud folder synced locally, and never rely on the Downloads folder. [L0344.040]
  • Keep raw editing project files backed up in the cloud, so a project can be recovered if the local computer or drive fails. Store source assets in organised folders alongside the project file. [L0343.049]

Link Building Delegation

What can be delegated

  • Backlink building work, such as skyscraper outreach, can be delegated to a VA or freelancer. Written SOPs make it possible to keep building backlinks to the site on an ongoing basis. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0943.006]
  • Resource page link building and broken link building can also be handed to a VA, assistant or freelancer. These processes suit delegation once the niche opportunities have been identified. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0943.007]
  • Link building tasks such as spreadsheet work, email sending and follow-ups are simple and do not need advanced English or specialist skills. They can be outsourced at low cost, unlike coding, development, content writing or creative work. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0945.011]
  • The outreach work is repetitive and manual but needs little specialist skill, so a general VA can usually perform it. There is no need to pay a high-cost freelancer, and the tasks can be added to an existing VA's workload. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0565.010]
  • Compared with classic one-by-one skyscraper link building, this system is highly automated and scalable. After setup, the ongoing time commitment stays small, and most of the work can be outsourced. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0565.009]
  • Broken link checking can be outsourced to a VA at about 5 USD per hour. The assistant runs the checker on every content page of a niche site, lists the in-content broken links with the URL where each was found, and you review the list to decide which links to pursue and which of your pages to offer. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0332.021]
  • Data pulling, merging and cleaning the likely-linker list are repetitive manual tasks that a VA can complete once the topics are chosen. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1443.018]
  • Sort the backlinks column or the referring domains column from highest to lowest. Both sort keys give a useful ordering for choosing which domains to process first. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1443.019]
  • The per-domain backlink exports can be merged into one master sheet by copy-and-paste or by an automated method. The automated method depends on whether you use Mac or Windows and which spreadsheet app you use, so follow instructions that match your setup. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L1443.024]

Resource page outreach procedure (older advice, 2021-22)

  1. Give the virtual assistant your outreach templates and written instructions on the search and filling-in steps. [L1237.020]
  2. The assistant searches for resource pages using the approved search terms and builds a list of candidate sites. [L1237.020]
  3. Review the list, approve or reject entries, and double-check the quality of each site. [L1237.020]
  4. Once the list is approved, the assistant sends the outreach emails and manages the follow-up emails. [L1237.020]
  5. Repeat the search periodically to find new resource pages. [L1237.020]

Ownership and priority

  • If the owner keeps doing repetitive link building tasks personally, they drop down the priority list and are rarely completed. Consistent outsourcing prevents this slide. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0945.003]
  • Outsource link building in this order:
    1. Perform each link building task yourself a few times until you understand it. [L0945.005]
    2. Once the work is affordable, write or record an SOP for the task. [L0945.005]
    3. Delegate the task to a VA or freelancer using that SOP. [L0945.005]
  • Existing VAs with spare capacity are a good first choice for link building tasks, and they can take on part of the work immediately. This avoids hiring for a new role before the workload justifies it. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0945.012]
  • For a serious link building goal in a growing business, a dedicated link building team member, full time or at least part time, is worth hiring. Once content production runs steadily, more link opportunities appear, and a dedicated person speeds up progress. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0945.013]
  • Extensive outreach also builds industry relationships that can lead to business partnerships, co-promotions, guest posting, featured mentions and promotions to their audience. Over a long-term business, these relationships can pay off well beyond the link itself. (Older advice, 2021-22.) [L0565.013]

Social Media and Influencer Outreach

Organic social posting

  • Organic social posting shows that the brand is active and real when customers search for it. It is not a direct sales channel. [L1094.108]
  • Paid social for high-ticket products often has minimal return on ad spend and a long payback, so outsourcing it is not recommended. (Source from 2023.) [L1094.108]
  • A social media VA received a brand kit from the owner and had access to the website images. She made slight changes to each image so every post looked unique, then published across all accounts from Hootsuite. [L0921.024]
  • A VA wrote a blog post every two weeks, which was proofread before publishing. The posts were then shared on social media. [L0921.025]
  • Delegate social media engagement:
    1. Hire a virtual assistant or use automation tools. [L0226.039]
    2. Have them engage on Pinterest (pinning, inviting followers, following accounts). [L0226.039]
    3. Manage Instagram, Facebook and Twitter interactions. [L0226.039]
    4. Post blog content links to social channels. [L0226.039]
    5. Monitor engagement metrics and adjust frequency based on results. [L0226.039]

Posting cadence: sources differ

  • Expected Instagram cadence was two to three posts per day, with Instagram as the primary platform. Creator-made content can supply much of this volume. Use this cadence only when you have creator content to fill it. [L0790.084]
  • In the first phase, post occasionally, such as when a new supplier approval comes in, rather than several times a week. SEO and blogs are a longer-term play than paid traffic. Use this cadence for a new store. [L0856.104]
  • Recurring weekly content formats posted on fixed weekdays build loyalty, because followers learn when to come back. Choose a format simple enough to sustain for the long term, since consistency matters more than complexity. [L0576.020]
  • Examples of recurring series: a Monday myth-busting post, a Wednesday behind-the-scenes post, and a Friday weekly recap. Adjust the day and format to the brand's rhythm. [L0576.021]

Influencer and outreach

  • Use experienced VAs to research influencer lists, because the manual work is cheap compared with the value of the list. A list of a few hundred influencers typically pays back the cost of the work. [L0790.082]
  • After a campaign, record which influencer collaboration formats and messages worked best with your audience. Reuse these proven approaches in future partnerships to improve results over time. [L0787.006]
  • For a technical niche where you are not an expert, find people who already have authority in niche Facebook groups or Reddit communities, and offer them a paid freelance content gig. Influencers can also be asked to contribute to your content. [L1120.027]
  • Outreach can be scaled by hiring a VA who sends messages from several social accounts, funded from about 500 dollars per month of cash flow. The seller used purchased older Instagram accounts for this. That carries account-ban and platform-policy risk, which should be checked before doing the same. [L0789.058]

Outsourcing vs. owner-led

  • Outsource posting and engagement once the brand's tone is set. The owner should still check the brand kit and approve the first posts. [L0226.039, L0921.024]

Further details

  • Aim for at least 50 entries in the attention matrix, with no upper limit. Keep adding entries, remove those that disappear, and review the whole list about once a year. [L0478.028]

  • Building the attention matrix is a simple, repeatable research task that a virtual assistant can do from written instructions. It is a good first task for a new team member, and a founder can do it personally before hiring. [L0478.029]

  • Build the attention matrix in short, regular daily time blocks instead of one long session, and keep working on it until you have a solid number of entries. A virtual assistant can take on this task from written instructions, which makes it a good first onboarding task for a new team member. [L0478.034]

  • Backlinks for product pages are not required to rank; start with product page SEO. Competitive niche keywords need more backlinks and time, while long-tail product pages can rank without them. [L0856.103]
