22.12 Set 90-day Rocks and measurables

Why a 90-day plan matters

  • Success in dropshipping comes from building systems and staying consistent, not from testing random products. A structured 90-day roadmap gives clarity and focus to move from beginner to consistent revenue. [L0199.001]
  • Older advice (2021-22): Rocks are the priorities for the next 90 days and come from the goals set for the business. [L0441.003]
  • Older advice (2021-22): Rocks are 90-day goals that break a one-year target into quarterly chunks. Each quarter, ask which goals must be hit to reach the one-year target. [L0679.001]
  • Older advice (2021-22): Rocks give the business momentum and traction, so the owner feels progress even when growth is slower than hoped. Long periods without visible momentum lead to stagnation, self-doubt and dislike of the business, and recovering from that gets harder over time. Setting Rocks is therefore a proactive way to avoid that spiral. [L0679.002]

Writing a valid Rock

  • Older advice (2021-22): Every Rock must be specific, attainable and measurable, and it must be reviewed every 90 days. [L0679.003]
  • A goal that is neither specific nor measurable, such as more profit next quarter, is not a valid Rock. [L0679.003]
  • Example of a valid Rock: Make an additional $60,000 profit in the next 90 days. It is specific and measurable, so the result can be checked easily. A vague goal about more profit fails both tests. [L0679.004]
  • Keep Rocks realistic for the 90-day window. People tend to overestimate what they can achieve in a short period and underestimate what they can achieve over a long period. A business with $100,000 in total sales to date should not set a Rock of $1,000,000 in one quarter. [L0679.005]
  • Set no more than 7 Rocks per quarter, because more goals make focus difficult. [L0679.021]
  • Use Rocks only for big-impact opportunities, such as:
    • a new product
    • promoting an existing bestseller
    • a new traffic source
    • an email marketing system
    • a hiring plan
    • a new business [L0679.021]
  • Do not use Rocks for routine tasks, such as answering emails. [L0679.021]

Breaking a one-year target into measurables

Older advice (2021-22). Worked chain:

  1. One-year measurable: 600,000 extra organic search visitors. [L0679.023]
  2. 90-day measurable: first publish 10 new pieces of content. [L0679.023]
  3. Each content piece becomes a Rock, such as hiring a writer or writing one piece every two weeks. [L0679.023]
  4. Weekly to-do lists carry these steps: in week one, write the full piece; in week two, find images and upload it. [L0679.023]

Barrier issues

  • Older advice (2021-22): Barrier issues are the obstacles that could derail a plan. Write them down next to the Rocks and measurables. [L0679.024]
  • When a barrier appears, check whether other measurables or Rocks need to change to free up the resources it needs. For example, if there is not enough time for the content, adjust other commitments to make room. [L0679.024]
  • If the owner does not know how to do a required task, that gap is a barrier issue. Solve it by making the acquisition of the missing skill a 90-day Rock, followed by a later Rock for putting that knowledge into practice. [L0679.025]
  • Common mistake: do not schedule an entire Rock into a single week. A complex task such as hiring and training someone cannot be done properly within a week. Only a truly urgent item should be squeezed into one week. [L0679.016]

Rolling 90-day window and quarterly review

  • Set Rocks one quarter at a time, always working inside a rolling 90-day window. [L0679.006]
  • Each Rock set is reviewed every 90 days, and a new set is defined every quarter. [L0441.003, L0679.003]
  • The 90-day window can start at any time and does not have to match the start of a calendar quarter. Standard financial quarters (Q1 to Q4) are optional. [L0679.026]
  • The first set of Rocks should cover the starting point toward the one-year plan. Build the 90-day sheet after the three-year picture and the one-year plan are filled in. [L0679.026]

Linking Rocks to weekly and daily work

  • Once the Rocks for the quarter are set, they define the weekly and daily to-do lists. [L0679.006]
  • Worked example of this link: the content Rock is broken into weekly steps, with the full piece written in week one and images found and uploaded in week two. [L0679.023]

First 90 days: the Source D roadmap

Overall sequence. Run the six phases over 90 days in this order:

  1. Days 1-14: Foundation [L0199.002]
  2. Days 15-30: Product Research & Validation [L0199.002]
  3. Days 31-45: Store Building [L0199.002]
  4. Days 45-60: Creative Production [L0199.002]
  5. Days 60-75: Launching Ads [L0199.002]
  6. Days 75-90: Optimization & Scaling [L0199.002]

Note: as the source lists them, day 45 falls in both Phase 3 and Phase 4, and day 60 falls in both Phase 4 and Phase 5. [L0199.002]

Phase 1 Foundation checklist (Days 1-14).

  1. Understand branded dropshipping as a business model. [L0199.003]
  2. Set up Shopify with a custom domain and email address. [L0199.003]
  3. Configure Facebook Business Manager and install your Pixel. [L0199.003]
  4. Learn the product criteria for dropshipping. [L0199.003]
  5. Learn advertising angles and how to identify testable products. [L0199.003]

Spend zero budget on ads in this phase. Focus entirely on building knowledge and technical infrastructure. [L0199.003]

Rule for Phase 1. Phase 1 requires zero ad spend. All effort and resources go into technical infrastructure and knowledge, so that Phase 5 ad spending becomes efficient rather than wasteful. [L0199.020]

Mistake to avoid. Starting Phase 5 (ad spend) before completing Phases 1-4 wastes budget on untested funnels and unrefined creatives. Each phase builds the knowledge and infrastructure the next one requires, and skipping steps multiplies risk. [L0199.018]

Expected outcomes after the 90-day roadmap.

Area Outcome
Brand Built from scratch
Product One proven winner identified
Creatives 15-30 distinct assets launched
Data capability Ability to read and interpret ad metrics
Profitability First profitable days or weeks
System A repeatable process for future products
