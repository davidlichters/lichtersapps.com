22.6 Know your strengths, values and working style

Start with honest self-reflection

  • Self-reflection exercises about work habits and thinking style have no right or wrong answers. Answering openly and honestly with yourself is what makes them useful. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L0802.008]
  • Skipping self-reflection exercises because they feel uncomfortable reduces their value, since they are often the most useful part of personal business preparation. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L0802.007]
  • Formal education, a strong academic record and exceptional intelligence are not prerequisites for building a profitable e-commerce business. Long-term persistence through slow or losing periods matters more than traditional credentials. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L0419.014]
  • Persistence is the trait that carries successful sellers: checking the numbers every day, generating ideas, and running the team with genuine belief. Persistence does not mean stubbornly trying to solve everything alone. [L0780.015]

Review your past attempts honestly

  • Reviewing past attempts at online business honestly, including what happened and why it ended, reveals recurring patterns such as quitting early or lacking capital. Address those patterns in the new plan rather than repeating them. [L1503.007]
  • Answer each question of this self-assessment form in writing before starting an online business:
Personal self-assessment form to fill in honestly before starting an online business, answer each question in writing:
1. Name, age, and home country or city?
2. What do you currently do for work or study?
3. Why did you decide to start this business path now?
4. What is your strongest reason for wanting to succeed in ecommerce?
5. What is your main goal for the next 90 days?
6. What monthly or yearly income figure are you working toward?
7. Have you tried ecommerce, dropshipping, or any other online business before? If yes, what happened and why did it stop or fail?
8. What do you see as your biggest obstacle right now? Consider time, confidence, product research, ads, capital, and consistency.
9. What do you expect from a coach, course, or mentor, and how should they support you?
10. Imagine it is 12 months from today and everything worked out. Describe in concrete detail what your work, income, time, and daily life look like.
Commitment (sign or confirm): Guidance, support, and accountability can be provided by others, but results depend on taking consistent action yourself.

[L1503.001]

  • Answering the planning questions quickly or vaguely undermines the whole plan, because the answers are the basis for setting realistic goals. Fill in each answer with specifics such as numbers, dates, and real constraints. [L1503.006]

Map your personality and natural strengths

  • Every person has a unique way of thinking and acting that works automatically and cannot be switched off. Trying to act against it drains energy, while working inside it makes tasks feel natural. [L0701.013]
  • Myers-Briggs and DISC are personality assessments that help you understand how you think and deliver value. DISC is also used to read other people in one-on-one conversations so you can choose the right words. Free resources for both are available online. [L0701.018]
  • The StrengthsFinder assessment from Gallup, described in Tom Rath's book, identifies your five natural strengths or talents. The Gallup study behind it reports that people operate about 80% of their lives from these five strengths, so work should be built around them. [L0701.019]
  • Books alone rarely reveal your own strengths and working style. Get feedback from other people who know you well, and combine it with structured assessments such as Gallup's StrengthsFinder. [L0701.039]
  • Many people stop using their natural way of working around ages 8 to 14, when they are told that what they do by default is not enough and that they must add unrelated skills to succeed. Rebuilding confidence starts with returning to that natural way of thinking and acting, not with adding more courses. [L0701.037]

Define values, passion and purpose

  • Values work as market selection: they decide which audience you want to serve and why. When your work fits your values, it becomes more enjoyable and marketing gets easier. [L0701.017]
  • Problems that you notice in others, often before they notice them, are a strong starting point for a business. Those problems are usually linked to your natural way of seeing things. [L0701.014]

Build in the right order

Work through these steps in sequence:

  1. Understand your personality and how you process information. [L0701.016]
  2. Identify your proficiencies, the five natural ways you think and act. [L0701.016]
  3. Define your values, which determine who you want to work with and why. [L0701.016]
  4. Find your passion, the drive to endure pain for the end result. [L0701.016]
  5. Define your purpose, what you offer the world. [L0701.016]
  6. Make a plan based on the steps above. [L0701.016]
  7. Execute. [L0701.016]
  • Plans that look complete on paper but collapse in execution, with about 50% failing right away, usually mean planning was done too early. Planning should come late, after personality, strengths, values and passion are clear. [L0701.005]
  • Constantly second-guessing your marketing (target group, wording, message) usually signals that you do not yet know your own strengths and values well enough to speak clearly to customers. Fix the self-understanding first, then the messaging. [L0701.004]

Check that your work fits your strengths

  • Heavy work that produces small results usually means you are working outside your proficiencies and your natural working style. Re-check which tasks match your strengths before adding more effort. [L0701.021]

Choose partners and direction carefully

  • Repeated partnerships that do not match, where the cause is never clear, often come from unclear personal strengths and values rather than bad luck. Define both before entering a partnership. [L0701.006]
  • Chasing every new opportunity (shiny button syndrome) keeps you from finishing anything and drains money and focus. Stay with one direction until it is defined by your strengths and values. [L0701.007]

Name your obstacles

  • Name your obstacles specifically in categories such as time, confidence, product research, ads, capital, and consistency, and rank the biggest one. Each named obstacle can then be matched to a concrete countermeasure. [L1503.005]

Use the experience you already have

  • Management experience from a corporate job transfers well to running a business, even when the person has no prior business ownership. Those skills in planning, team leadership and handling pressure apply directly to e-commerce. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L1014.001]
  • Prior sales experience makes supplier contact much easier. Without it, expect the first calls to feel daunting, which is normal. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L1248.009]

Case: investing profits in an offline business (around 2009 to 2010)

Around 2009 to 2010, the owner invested profits from an e-commerce business into a digital signage company that installed displays in about 30 coffee shop locations in New York. The costs were very high because of computers, servers, screens, installation staff, and sales staff, and the business made no money. The owner sold it quickly, recovered only part of the cash, and concluded that offline businesses, managing many companies, and large teams were not a fit. [L1039.031]

Case: a low-ticket store with China-shipped products (around 2011 to 2012)

Around 2011 to 2012, the owner built a low-ticket store selling products shipped from China, with an average order value of about $50, and drove traffic to it. Customers quickly complained about long shipping times, and no support systems existed for the volume of customers, so many were upset. The products were delivered, the website was shut down, and the lesson was that this model was not for them. Orders producing about $8 profit do not justify a large support team. [L1039.032]

Feelings of imposter syndrome

  • Feelings of imposter syndrome are normal for entrepreneurs entering a new market. Push through them by taking small actions rather than waiting until you feel fully qualified. (Older advice, 2021-2022.) [L0453.017]
  • The feeling of being a fraud at the start of a business usually comes from an unexplored personality. Clarifying your personality and the value you bring removes most of that feeling. [L0701.020]
  • Feeling like an impostor is normal even for experienced creators, and nobody fully understands where their best ideas come from. Show up and do the work every day instead of waiting until you feel ready. [L1343.024]

Measure your confidence

Repeat this exercise to track how your confidence changes:

  1. On a sheet of paper, rate from 1 to 10 how confident you are in your ability to do your job right now. [L0701.002]
  2. Below the rating, list every task you have to do (for example marketing, setting up the store). [L0701.002]
  3. Keep the sheet and repeat the 1 to 10 rating after the gratitude exercise to measure the change. [L0701.002]
