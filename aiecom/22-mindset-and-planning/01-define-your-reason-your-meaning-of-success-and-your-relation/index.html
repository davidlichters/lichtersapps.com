22.1 Define your reason, your meaning of success and your relationship to money

Your core reason for building the business

Knowing your personal reason keeps you going through the slow early phase, so write it down and revisit it. The reason is private and does not need to satisfy anyone else. [L1039.001]

A personal reason strong enough to sustain effort over months is required. Without it, people tend to give up when facing rejection, customer service problems, or technical hurdles. [L0826.005]

Procedure: finding your core reason [L1039.002]

  1. Write down the first reason that comes to mind for starting the business, such as quitting a job, extra income, more family time, travel, paying off debt, or a better life for your family. [L1039.002]
  2. For each reason, ask why you want that outcome. [L1039.002]
  3. Repeat the question with each answer, going deeper, until you reach the underlying reason, such as health, sleep, or time for a relationship. [L1039.002]
  4. Write the core reason down and keep it where you will see it when you need motivation. [L1039.002]

Procedure: defining your reason for investing time and money in dropshipping [L0519.003]

  1. Write down on paper why you joined the program and why you are doing this, ideally the same week you start. [L0519.003]
  2. Describe your dream outcome: what you want to do, how your daily life looks, where you live and what your number one goal is. [L0519.003]
  3. Use the answers to find the path (skills, products, business model) that leads to that outcome, and revisit the notes when motivation drops. [L0519.003]

Keep your dreamline private. Sharing it with family or friends can lead you to water down your goals to match their opinions. The list only needs to motivate you. [L1039.011]

Define what success means to you

Define your specific vision of success and what it means to you personally. Create a compelling picture of that outcome with no artificial limits. Self-knowledge of what you want is the prerequisite. [L0007.001]

Define success personally by the work that brings you fulfillment, not by arbitrary revenue targets. Consistent meaningful action aligned to your fulfillment makes you successful by your own definition, while business growth follows naturally. Older advice, Source B 2021-2022. [L0107.013]

Success is measured by growth, not absolute revenue. Growing from 0 percent to 1 percent month-over-month is measurable progress and success. [L0280.038]

A goal such as making more money is too vague to keep you going. A concrete picture of the lifestyle you want, such as time freedom, location freedom or financial independence, gives daily work a purpose. [L0519.004]

Set realistic goals that you actually want rather than abstract income targets. Money alone does not create happiness, so tie goals to what the business makes possible in your life. [L1043.002]

Motivation and staying power

Use two motivators together: a pleasurable outcome to move toward (financial freedom, providing for family) and a painful situation to move away from. Keep both visible in daily thinking. [L1041.002]

A goal must be bigger than the obstacles it will meet, or the first hurdle will stop progress. Each goal should be compelling enough that you are willing to climb every obstacle on the way. [L1041.003]

Early on, expect delayed gratification: you will put in unpaid-feeling hours for two to three weeks or longer before results appear. Accepting that present work pays off later is what keeps people working when motivation drops. [L1039.003]

Your core reason is a tool to pull you back to work when distractions or low energy appear, because focus is what produces results. Successful people are not lucky by default; they use their reason to delay short-term comfort. [L1039.004]

A clear personal reason keeps attention on what matters and filters out distractions. Sustained focus is what turns effort into results. [L1043.001]

Feeling overwhelmed during the building process is normal. Understanding the specific end goal beyond abstract income targets, for example paying off debt in 2 years or funding a 3-month Europe trip next summer, provides motivation when daily tasks feel tedious. [L0251.054]

Passion is the ability to endure present pain because the end result is worth the effort, not a constant feeling of happiness. Purpose is what the world buys from you, while passion is what fuels you. [L0701.015]

Losing motivation after a fast start is normal for almost every business owner. Return to the original personal reason you started, such as extra income, leaving a job or more time for travel, and let that motivate you again. Older advice, Source A 2020. [L0961.083]

Spreadsheets and CSV files are only the necessary tools, not the purpose of the business. Keep the personal goal in view when the daily work feels heavy. Older advice, Source A 2020. [L0961.084]

Procedure: staying motivated while working toward business goals (older advice, Source A 2022) [L1092.104]

  1. Write down the real personal goal the profit is for, such as a family trip, a move or school fees. [L1092.104]
  2. Put that written goal somewhere visible while working. [L1092.104]
  3. Measure progress against that goal, not only against a daily task count. [L1092.104]

Money as a tool and scorecard

Money is only a tool. Treat it as a means to reach your goals, not as the goal itself. [L0831.008]

Treat money as a scorecard that shows whether the business is working and whether you are making the right decisions, not as the end goal. Pair the score with what it lets you do, such as a home, a trip, or a car, so progress feels meaningful. [L1039.039]

Running a business only to make money, with the plan to do something else later, keeps confidence low. A better approach is to move the business toward your own strengths so that it becomes the vehicle for what you really want. [L0701.008]

When profits rise quickly, a new seller can develop fear of losing the money and negative beliefs about earning it. Actively challenge these thoughts, because they can hold back decisions as income grows. [L1185.046]

Pursuing status symbols as a measure of success tends to produce frustration and unhappiness. Creative accomplishment toward goals you genuinely care about tends to bring lasting satisfaction and material results. [L1206.026]

Time, money and income

Time and money are not directly linked. Income depends on the value, leverage, and scalability of what you sell, so avoid pricing only by the hour. [L0378.105]

Shift from payment for hours worked to payment for outcomes whenever possible. Income tied to results can keep growing, while hourly work caps your earnings. [L0378.106]

Case: a 12-year-old tennis racket stringing service

  • Starting point: a used stringing machine bought for about $400 to $500. [L0378.108]
  • Local clubs charged $12 to $15 per racket, and the stringer started at $25 with no experience. [L0378.108]
  • A racket first took 45 to 60 minutes; after practice the time dropped to 16 to 20 minutes, with about 2 rackets per hour including setup. [L0378.108]
  • This came to about $50 per hour at that age, more than most adults earned hourly. [L0378.108]
  • Lesson: a focused skill, charged at a premium, can earn far more than the trade-time model. [L0378.108]

Passion for the business, not the product

Passion for your specific niche category is not required. Focus on passion for building your business itself, which determines long-term success more than enthusiasm for the product. [L0031.005]

Channel your motivation toward building a successful business, watching it grow and executing well, rather than toward personal interest in the products you sell. [L0025.001]

Passion for the product is not necessary for a dropshipping business. Focus your passion on building the business, making sales and growing the store. [L0769.017]

Passion for the niche is secondary; passion for business growth and problem-solving is primary. Knowledge comes through experience, not prior expertise. Older advice, Source A 2020. [L0268.061]

Passion cannot be faked, so commitment is required for success, but it should be directed at the business itself rather than at the products sold. Direct your enthusiasm to building the business, not to the product category. [L0924.009]

The aspects of a store worth being passionate about are building a good-looking website, generating traffic, treating customers well, earning repeat purchases, and growing the store month after month. These areas determine long-term success regardless of the product. [L0924.010]

Hobby versus income

A personal passion or hobby does not need to be your income source. If other products are significantly more profitable and easier to sell, building your business around those allows you to pursue your passion as a leisure activity separate from work. [L0025.002]

Your hobbies do not have to become your business. You can keep an interest purely for leisure and fund it from the profits of a business built on a better opportunity. [L0924.008]

Case: a member with a scuba diving hobby

  • He first considered selling diving products because the hobby was strong. [L0924.007]
  • He realized he did not need to earn money from the hobby, so he chose a product with better sales potential and easier selling. [L0924.007]
  • He reinvested the profits into building the business and used part of the income to fund his diving trips. [L0924.007]
  • Result: the hobby stayed a personal pleasure, while the business was built on a stronger opportunity. [L0924.007]

Keep several interests running at once, including one creative hobby with no income goal. Dropping an interest often makes the main work feel flat, while a hobby done only for enjoyment restores energy for the business. [L1343.039]

Choosing what you will do with the income

Money is a tool for getting what you actually want, not a goal in itself. If you cannot name what you would do with the income, resolve that gap before you invest in scaling. Examples of outcomes to name:

  • Giving back [L0701.003]
  • Independence [L0701.003]
  • Caring for family [L0701.003]
  • Experiences [L0701.003]
  • Becoming debt-free [L0701.003]
  • Leaving a job you dislike [L0701.003]
