22.19 Run tests with budgets, hypotheses and kill rules

Testing culture and hypotheses

  • No ad strategy is a silver bullet. Mentors give educated guesses based on experience, so every opinion must be tested. Treat each recommendation as a hypothesis, not a fact. [L0640.044]
  • Testing produces proof for decisions, while advice from tools and mentors only gives guidelines. When you are unsure and the budget allows, test. When the budget is limited, put money where it already converts. [L1426.040]
  • Treat every idea as a hypothesis and test it rather than debate it. Rejecting an untested idea should require a detailed written argument. The default is to run the test. [L1315.057]
  • Treat every ad test as a hypothesis. Decide on the variable to test, keep the others constant, and accept that early numbers are an educated guess until more data arrives. [L1315.050]
  • A hypothesis is a proposed explanation based on limited evidence that must be tested. Formulate it by reading the data, looking at the creative and theorising where the ad falls short. Then test that single hypothesis against a control. [L0640.045]
  • Large companies test everything and do not reject ideas without evidence. Some results surprise you even though they look obvious afterwards, so test against your hypothesis rather than dismissing it. [L0640.046]
  • Research and testing are the two pillars of finding a winning ad. Research shows who the audience is and what language they use. Testing shows what actually sells. [L1315.058]
  • Research before building: look at the brand's ads, page and offers, then estimate its numbers with a simple spreadsheet and conservative inputs. This gives a clear view of the opportunity before you spend money. [L0524.062]
  • No single product research method is perfect. Use the method that has worked in your own tests rather than copying someone else's routine exactly. [L1436.002]
  • Testing as fundamentals, not hacks: no single method is perfect, and the goal is to increase your odds with more proof of concept. Judge each product by the evidence collected, not by intuition. [L0335.055]
  • Constant testing is the approach. Test angles, videos and products repeatedly and keep what converts. Organic growth depends more on repeated testing than on one perfect video. [L0352.053]
  • Find and test products systematically rather than repeating a few products. Repeated testing of a few products without a method wastes time and money. [L0361.049]
  • Facebook ads are not a fixed science with guaranteed rules, so results must be verified with ongoing tests. Market conditions change, which makes continuous testing a standard part of the process. [L0692.045]
  • Intuition and personal taste often misjudge what the market will buy. Check ideas by response, not by how good they seem. Expect to be wrong often and let results correct the plan. [L1261.134]
  • A product can look weak and still sell. Trust the data over your own opinion, and test the product yourself with ads before rejecting it. [L1161.046]
  • Use data, not emotion, for every decision in the ad account and on the store, including changes to ads, pages and budgets. Treat the numbers on the screen as the only signal of whether something works. [L1038.005]
  • Base decisions on factual data rather than gut feeling, and keep learning from mistakes. The speaker presented analytical habits as a major advantage for founders starting in e-commerce. [L1508.034]
  • Only trust an ad when its traced cost per customer is known. Assuming that a long-running ad is the best ad without checking its results wastes money, because some campaigns run on habit rather than data. [L1261.136]
  • Test creatives, the landing page and the offer separately. Change one asset at a time based on its own metric rather than guessing. [L0871.087]
  • Diagnose before changing anything, and change one variable at a time. If several things change together, it is impossible to know which change caused the result. [L1445.014]
  • Before spending real money, test a new store setup or ad idea with a dummy store or dummy ad. Move to a live store with a real paid ad only after each step is understood. Record every test in a notebook or document so results can be compared later. [L1492.001]
  • Compare each new product test with the data from earlier tests. Build a repeatable routine of testing, reading metrics and improving. Over time this routine builds the skills needed to recognise winners faster. [L1038.019]
  • Small, well-chosen changes, such as title keywords, dynamic retargeting and opt-ins, can compound into large results. Focus on these levers before chasing new tactics. [L0510.060]
  • Competitor optimisation happens gradually. Repeat the competitor review about every two weeks and make small improvements to the store over time instead of trying to fix everything at once. [L0894.003]
  • Constant testing trains video skills such as hooks and angles. These skills transfer to the next product, so early tests are practice that makes it faster to scale a winning product later. [L1444.014]
  • A seller should expect to test many ideas, and not every idea will work. Keep testing, measure the results and adjust quickly rather than waiting for a perfect plan. [L1153.078]
  • Test one product at a time, but read the test result honestly. A failed test is feedback, not failure: test, refine and improve until the product or approach wins. [L0631.012]
  • Two approaches exist. Invest deeply in one product that can become a brand, or test many products quickly to find a winner and then build a brand around it. [L1436.085]
  • Winning in dropshipping comes from the volume and speed of testing, not from perfect products or perfect ads. A stalled result shows what does not work and brings you closer to what does. [L0887.016]

Testing budget and capital

  • Set a firm testing budget before you buy traffic for product tests. Avoid spending large amounts on tests that are not based on the research method. In the mentor's example, unfocused tests on other markets and platforms cost about $500 in total, while disciplined testing lost only about $100. [L0335.024]
  • Testing requires spending money, because product results only show up after real spend. Set a fixed test budget in advance and judge the product on that data rather than on the first few clicks. [L0562.036]
  • Only test with budget you can afford to lose, since results from small samples are noisy. The testing period also needs enough spend before the data can be trusted. [L0644.043]
  • Set a fixed testing budget that you can afford to lose completely, and decide in advance that losing it will not upset you. Do not treat the budget as a limit that ends the business once it is spent. [L1394.004]
  • Before seeing any profit, a beginner should expect to lose about $2,000 in testing, or whatever amount their budget is. The lower the starting budget, the smaller the expected loss. Write this expectation down before starting. [L1394.003]
  • Losses scale with business size. A larger operation may lose $30,000 or $200,000 in testing. Losing money is a normal cost of doing business, not a sign to quit. [L1394.005]
  • Giving yourself a budget for the business and quitting once it is spent is the wrong approach. Accept the testing budget as expendable and keep testing. [L1394.012]
  • When testing a new product, spend no more than 50 percent of your available investment capital on the test. For example, with 5,000 USD in capital, keep at least half in reserve. [L1251.035]
  • How long to test a product before judging it depends on your personal budget and how much risk you can take. Products can fail quickly, so keep spare capital rather than going all in. [L1251.036]
  • Test a new product on a small budget first to see whether the market responds. Testing an untested product means risking the full budget on an unknown outcome. [L1177.050]
  • Test one product per week with a budget of about 200 to 300 USD per product until there is enough data to either move on or keep trying it. [L1176.039]
  • Ad spend on a product that makes no sales is learning money, not lost money. Move on after about 300 USD of spend with zero sales. [L1432.023]
  • Spending profits as soon as they arrive, or switching between many new ventures, starves the core business. Keep the budget for the next product and for branding the current store. [L0363.076]
  • Plan a fixed test budget and a schedule in advance for influencer campaigns, rather than spending freely. Influencer campaigns often fail, and some of the first creators contacted will not convert. [L1379.047]
  • Keep the first sales only as proof. The core is a repeatable process of testing products, reading results and moving on. [L1440.053]
  • Spending on research tools and courses is a trap. The main work is testing ads with real budgets: research can show ideas, but only ads prove them. [L1184.058]
  • Early paid testing minimum: about 100 clicks per product is too small a sample for a firm conclusion. Treat the first 100 clicks as a learning and optimisation period. [L0856.112]
  • Test early ads over an extended period (about 25 days at 10 dollars per day for a product) before dropping it, including optimisation time. Do not stop at a fixed dollar amount if sales are missing. [L0856.111]
  • Test before scaling: start with a small test budget, and only scale an approach if it performs. Each ad account is different, so CBO may not work and ABO or other scaling methods may be needed. [L1335.031]
  • Getting comfortable with large ad budgets is part of scaling a profitable store. Spend levels that seem extreme at the start can be necessary to grow tenfold later. [L0934.086]

Kill rules and stop criteria

  • Set kill rules for a product before you test it, and follow them. Keeping a product you like without results wastes time. A product with a strong visual wow factor but no clear problem it solves often gets views without sales. [L0501.039]
  • Set cut rules before the test starts and follow them strictly. A failing product blocks budget that another product could use, while a strong product can make 5-10 sales on day one. [L0648.041]
  • Stop a product that keeps losing money after a set test budget rather than hoping for a turnaround. Fixed stop rules keep the test budget from being spent on unproven products. [L0661.064]
  • Set a clear kill rule before spending on a new product. If a product gets no sales after a fixed test spend and a few days of ads, cut it and move to the next product instead of pouring more money in. [L0950.001]
  • Kill a product early when it shows no signs. Test about $50 to $100 on day one, with a maximum of $200 to $250. On TikTok, consider killing it around $60 if creatives and CTR are poor. [L1436.084]
  • Give a product three to five days after fixing the offer and the store. If about $200 has been spent across 10 to 15 audiences at $5 to $10 per day without any sign of profitability, move on to the next product. [L1345.079]
  • Spend is one of the limits, but the numbers decide. Before running ads, a dropshipping seller should know cost of goods sold, break-even return on ad spend, and break-even cost per purchase. These numbers define the limits within which every later scaling decision is made. [L0875.001]
  • Switch off an ad that spends past its margin without a sale. In the example below, the margin is too thin to scale at the current CPA, so raise average order value as well. [L0995.019]

  • Do not drop a product too early when its videos show some views, because the breakthrough can come unexpectedly after a stretch of low results. In the referenced case the seller was close to quitting just before the product exploded. [L0821.011]
  • Judge a product by its early signals. If it gets views but no sales after the normal test window, extend the test by one more week. If it gets neither views nor engagement, and you doubt it converts, drop it and move to the next product. [L0820.002]
  • Do not close ad sets too early. Some ad sets only convert after the spend passes a certain amount (for example after the 12th dollar), so give ad sets patience while CTR is good. [L0642.048]
  • Be patient with higher-ticket products (for example a $59.99 price). The pixel needs time to collect data, so give the ads more spend before closing them, as long as CTR is above 1.5%. [L0642.047]

On day two they ran six ad sets with $10 each, with a $20 offer running. Advice given: switch off an ad that spends past its margin without a sale, and raise average order value with a bundle, since a margin of about $23 makes scaling hard when CPA is $15 to $20.   • Quality of testing matters more than volume in the early phase. A product tested with only a few attempts gives little information. Set a clear test-count target before deciding a product is not a winner, for example 20 to 30 products rather than three. [L0691.028]
  • Judging a new product after only one week and one sale is too early, especially if several offers have not been tested yet. [L1131.044]
  • Early signals and dropping: a product that shows weak early signals should not be kept out of hope. Early signals decide the next step. [L0820.002]

Early signals and when to judge

  • Procedure for a new product that performs weakly:
    1. Run a new product for the first couple of days.
    2. Check its performance against your KPIs.
    3. If performance is weak, send your data to coaches or mentors for evaluation.
    4. Wait for their assessment and feedback.
    5. Follow their recommendation to either cut the product or continue testing. [L0037.014]
  • Poor early results were held on to for too long in one case. The team saw weak performance within two to three days and changed the background and angle on day three, which is an early enough point to pivot. [L0764.020]
  • Daily results swing between green and red days, so do not cut a campaign after one red day. Add more creatives and let the system keep learning. [L1019.047]
  • A slow week after sales in past weeks can be normal. Check the funnel (queries, add-to-cart, checkout intent) and follow the troubleshooting steps before making changes. [L0666.046]
  • Low ad spend produces slower data, so a small budget will not quickly show whether a product works. Judge test results only after enough spend has produced enough data. [L1038.008]
  • Early traffic behaviour is random at low volume. A new store may make two or three sales in the first days and then go weeks without a sale. Let data accumulate over about three weeks before major changes. [L0856.110]
  • A low number of sales can come from a sample that is too small to read. The first step is to grow traffic before judging a store as failed. [L0855.172]
  • When few sales come in, do not change niches right away. First add more approved suppliers and optimise ads on the products that already get quality clicks. [L0835.131]
  • The first sales depend on traffic rather than on a fixed amount of time. Once a store receives around 100 unique visitors from Google Shopping, the conversion target can be checked. Note: this advice is from 2020 and is older. [L1111.171]
  • Winning products are found through fast, repeated product testing, so a beginner may need several failed products (for example around eight) before finding a winner. [L1394.002]
  • Judge a hook only after repeated uploads of the same format with variations, usually three to six times. Videos that look weak early can still produce a breakout, and one successful video can lift the whole account's follower count. [L1383.026]
  • Influencer results are not always visible immediately. Be patient while the sales funnel works, and use the full funnel to qualify influencers over a longer period rather than judging a single post. [L0790.087]
  • A product that works at first and then stops being profitable when scaled is normal. Drop it, launch the next product, and record what was learned from each product so the next launch is better. [L1107.004]
  • A single viral result does not prove a product will last. Keep testing new products and angles to build a consistent pipeline. [L0706.058]
  • Winning on TikTok is not a single lucky product. Nobody in the referenced group found one product and executed it properly on the first try. The probability of a hit rises with the number of products tested. [L0821.020]
  • Dropshipping results come from testing, not from research alone. Do not give up on a product too early, but keep checking the data. [L1108.026]
  • Ad spend on a product with zero sales is learning money, as noted above. Use the first sales only as proof of the process, not as the goal. [L1440.053]
  • A case with strong early revenue: one operator reached roughly $1,000 in daily revenue on the second day after launching a new product. That result came only after testing more than twelve products before finding the first winner. [L1454.019]
  • Pinterest case: a dropshipping product in another account generated over $2,000 in its first week on Pinterest, with a ROAS of about 4.5 shortly after launch. Testing was done with a small daily budget and the account was still in the learning stage. [L1135.047]

One product at a time

  • Beginners should test one product at a time, with one account per product. Running several products in parallel splits attention and makes the learning curve harder to manage. [L0820.004]
  • Test one product at a time with full focus. Move on to a new product only after the current one fails to get traction within about one to two weeks. Once a product converts, keep it and open more accounts to push it rather than switching products. [L1186.032]
  • When working alone, test one product at a time for about two weeks, with two products at most. Spreading small effort across many products usually fails. Commit fully to one product, then expand once you are more experienced. [L1444.012]
  • Testing one product at a time is the discipline; testing a single product, deciding it is a scam or saturated, and quitting is the most common failure pattern. [L1038.002]
  • Testing multiple different products in rapid succession, rather than focusing on one product through all six phases, disrupts the learning cycle and prevents reaching profitability by Phase 6. Consistency requires staying with one product long enough to gather meaningful data. [L0199.019]
  • Testing sequence for one winning candidate:
    1. Test one product at a time.
    2. Try different store designs.
    3. Try different prices.
    4. Try different bundles.
    5. Try different audiences.
    6. Once a winner is found, scale it.
    7. If the results are not what you need, move on to the next product. [L1176.040]
  • Patience with one product: a store takes months to form its brand, so keep a product in testing until its data confirms it works before adding more products to the store. [L0871.086]
  • A failed brand can be tested again once the operator has improved offers, upsells and creatives. Apply the lesson from the first attempt before retrying, and do not use it as a reason to abandon the brand category. [L1041.036]

Test pace and volume

  • Set a testing schedule of about one or two products per week. Expect to test several products before finding a first winner. Focus on controlling the inputs of testing, because results follow from consistent testing. [L0347.033]
  • Test one product per week with a budget of about 200 to 300 USD per product until there is enough data to move on or keep trying. [L1176.039]
  • Patience decides how many products you can test. Choose a testing pace you can sustain. Testing several products with high effort and all failing kills motivation. [L1171.048]
  • Some operators can test up to 15 products when they have the energy, which raises the chance that one of them becomes a winner. [L1171.049]
  • Spam testing only fits sellers who already know a very specific niche and have a large budget to test many random products. Without that knowledge and budget it depends on luck and fails, since it needs funds for 50 to 60 products. [L1158.046]
  • Winning in dropshipping depends on continuous testing of many products and ads over time. Each attempt has a low hit rate, and more attempts raise the chance of finding a winner. Stopping after a few failures makes a winner very unlikely. [L1038.001]
  • Expect to test many products before finding a winner. In the example, the first winner took about three months and about six tested products. [L1131.043]
  • Test a product or strategy long enough before judging it. A common budget is about 30 products before reassessing whether the approach works. Most people quit after one or two tries, which is usually too early. [L1128.022]
  • Do not wait for the one perfect winner before testing. Keep testing many products until you see real progress, because a winner may not arrive on its own. [L1444.013]
  • Taking action and testing many products matters more than finding the perfect first product. Not every product will be a winner, so keep testing rather than endlessly searching. [L0561.052]
  • Find winners through volume and speed of testing, not through perfect products or perfect ads. [L0887.016]

Patience with data

  • Be patient when a product is higher ticket, and give ads more spend before closing them while CTR is above 1.5%. [L0642.047]
  • The start is the hardest phase because every test costs money before a winner is found. Once a product is making money, spending on testing becomes much easier. [L0692.046]
  • Losing money on ads is a common reason people quit e-commerce, while organic work mainly costs time rather than cash. Starting with organic reduces the risk of spending a large budget without results. [L0819.018]
  • Viewing a product test as time spent rather than money lost is a valid way to keep testing without pressure. Frustration after each failed product is common, and forcing results quickly tends to make testing harder. [L0819.025]
  • Judge a product using the test window and the data, not the first few clicks. [L0562.036]
  • Influencer results are not visible immediately; qualify influencers over a longer period using the full funnel. [L0790.087]
  • Be patient while a product's data accumulates: the early-traffic randomness described above means major changes should wait about three weeks. [L0856.110]
  • In a slow period, take a short break to rest and plan ahead, then use the time to tighten spending on low-converting keywords instead of cutting all paid traffic. Tightening is more effective than a full stop. Note: this advice is from 2021 to 2022 and is older. [L0949.061]
  • A product's kill rule may need more data before it can be judged, but a fixed test spend still decides. [L0950.001]
  • Build a sustainable pace with patience: "Patience decides how many products you can test." [L1171.048]
  • Be patient with the number of products tested; do not expect results from one trial. [L1444.013]
  • Keep spare capital so that a slow product does not force an early decision. [L1251.036]
  • Ad-level patience: Facebook ad sets may need more spend before they show whether they convert. [L0642.048]
  • Hook patience: judge a hook after three to six repeated uploads. [L1383.026]

Data over emotion

  • Emotional attachment to products can cloud optimisation decisions. Do not decide to bid higher or retain a product based on hope that it will improve. Base all pausing and bidding decisions on statistical thresholds and historical data, not on optimism about future performance. [L0299.027]
  • Do not become emotionally attached to a product; decide to kill or keep it based on test data. Failed stores and products should be cut quickly to free budget for new tests. [L0660.043]
  • Treat ad testing as a data-driven game. Decide based on CTR, store conversion rate and profit, rather than falling in love with a product after investing time and money into it. [L0650.052]
  • Do not become emotionally tied to a product you have invested heavily in. The effort put in does not guarantee output, so judge it by the numbers. [L0950.002]
  • Do not let emotional attachment drive decisions. A product that looks good to you may fail the numbers, and a product that looks weak may sell. [L1161.046]
  • Use the numbers on the screen as the only signal of whether something works, including for changes to ads, pages and budgets. [L1038.005]
  • Compare each product test with earlier data, rather than with how the idea feels. [L1038.019]

Letting go of products

  • Pick products you actually enjoy testing. Products you dislike are often dropped after four days to a week and a half of daily uploads. Lack of motivation kills tests before they produce data. [L0706.009]
  • Set a kill rule before you test and follow it. Do not get attached to one dropshipping product, since a failing product blocks budget that another product could use. [L0648.041]
  • After a failed product, the fastest comeback is to reuse a proven creative and page template and switch to a product that is already selling in the market. Do not build fresh creatives from scratch. [L0950.003]
  • When a product works at first and then stops being profitable at scale, drop it and launch the next product. [L1107.004]
  • Keep the next product in the testing queue. A product that flops should be replaced by the next product in the queue, not treated as proof that the model does not work. [L1038.002]
  • A product that has a strong visual wow factor but no clear problem it solves often gets views without sales. Keep the kill rule even when the product looks impressive. [L0501.039]
  • Lean on the kill rule when the product is one you like: "Set kill rules for a product before you test it and follow them, because keeping a product you like without results wastes time." [L0501.039]
  • Do not stay with an idea just because it sounded good; test it and let the result decide. [L1315.057]

Expecting failure

  • Expect many ideas to fail. Some tests will not work, and a few may cause problems such as banned ad accounts. Treat failed tests as part of the process and keep testing. [L0334.055]
  • Enjoy the process of testing, because brand, entry point and offer tests take time and many of them will fail before one works. [L0334.056]
  • Expect many conversion tests to fail and treat each failure as a learning step. Plan tests so that you know what is being tested and what the results are, then change the approach for the next test. Note: this advice is from 2021 to 2022 and is older. [L0400.014]
  • Most products fail in testing, and no formula reliably finds a winner on the first try. Test many products, learn from each failure, and improve the process rather than expecting a shortcut. [L1436.083]
  • Expect many test products to fail. Repeated iteration is part of the process, and a failed launch is not a verdict on the whole business. [L0950.004]
  • There is no shortcut formula that guarantees a winning product on the first try. Winners come from testing many products, learning from losses, improving the process and the branding, and accepting that a few products will fail. [L1382.065]
  • The first and often the second product are likely to fail, so plan for several test rounds. [L1394.019]
  • Losses are a normal part of testing. Before seeing any profit, a beginner should expect to lose about $2,000 or the full budget, as noted above. [L1394.003]
  • Testing a product is a risk: a product can fail quickly, so keep spare capital. [L1251.036]
  • Intuition often misjudges the market, so expect to be wrong often. [L1261.134]
  • Plan for failure as the default and treat each failed product as information. [L1436.083]
  • Failure is feedback, not the end: "A failed test is feedback, not failure. Test, refine and improve until the product or approach wins." [L0631.012]
  • Many ideas fail; keep testing, measure the results and adjust quickly. [L1153.078]
  • Ad-level failure is common: "Some tests will not work, and a few may cause problems such as banned ad accounts." [L0334.055]
  • Influencer campaigns often fail, and some of the first creators contacted will not convert. [L1379.047]
  • The start is the hardest phase, because every test costs money before a winner is found. [L0692.046]
  • Failure in one area does not mean the model fails. Apply what each failure teaches to the next product. [L1107.038]

Systems for steady output

  • The formula for sustained success in dropshipping is the consistent repetition of three elements: a good product, strong creatives and a good funnel, executed repeatedly over time. Without this cycle, sustainable profitability cannot be achieved. [L0199.016]
  • Build a consistent pipeline of products and angles. A single result does not prove a product will last. [L0706.058]
  • Create a routine: test, read metrics, improve, and record. Over time the routine builds the skills to recognise winners faster. [L1038.019]
  • Record what was learned from each product so the next launch is better. [L1107.004]
  • Write down the expected testing loss before starting, and keep the testing budget separate from the core business. [L1394.003]
  • Treat each product as a single cycle in a repeatable system. Judge it by its data, record the result and move to the next product if the data says so. [L1440.053]
  • Winning in dropshipping comes from volume and speed of testing; a stalled result shows what does not work and brings you closer to what does. [L0887.016]
  • Use a fixed testing budget and fixed kill rules as the system, so that each product gets the same treatment. [L0335.024]
  • Test many products in a steady rhythm and do not wait for the one perfect winner. [L1444.013]
  • Store and brand building happen after the product is proven, not before. [L0871.086]
  • Constant testing creates a stream of creatives and products to choose from. [L0352.053]
  • Use a cut rule before the test starts and follow it strictly. [L0648.041]
  • The cycle of testing, measuring and adjusting repeats for each product. [L1038.019]
  • Keep testing new products and angles to build a consistent pipeline. [L0706.058]

Scaling, automation and channel notes

  • Procedure for paid traffic automation work:
    1. Test automated bidding with a controlled experiment.
    2. Improve the product feed to get more traffic.
    3. Use Smart Shopping in controlled tests, not on the whole account at once.
    4. Shift the time saved to higher-leverage activities. [L0454.010]
  • Automating everything at once and checking performance only about once a month lets underperformance run for weeks unnoticed. Introduce automation in steps with regular review. Blind trust in automated results is the most common error. [L0454.011]
  • Automation works like an exoskeleton: it lets the marketer handle much more volume without more strain, but it does not replace judgement. Use it to get more done with the same effort. [L0454.041]
  • The basics of paid advertising stay the same whatever the interface: show the right message to the right person at the right time. If a campaign ignores this, for example by targeting an audience that has no reason to buy the product, it will underperform regardless of tool changes. [L0489.004]
  • Scaling speed differed among operators. One reached 25k plus per month within two months from start. Another needed six months before mentorship and three months after. One scaled within three days once a winning product and offer were found. The bottleneck was finding a working offer, not the scaling itself. [L1469.021]
  • Build the mindset of right traffic, right time and right price before setting up any campaigns, because the later campaign work depends on these concepts. Checking targeting against the funnel stage and the cost of acquisition is the core test for every paid campaign. Paid ad management should be based on profitability rather than raw traffic volume. Note: this advice is from 2021 to 2022 and is older. [L1507.013]
  • Ad testing decisions are made on CTR, store conversion rate and profit. [L0650.052]
  • Ad-account quality: the bigger the account, the more the testing loss can grow. [L1394.005]
  • A winning product found in one test may need an offer change before it scales. [L1469.021]
  • Test before scaling, and do not scale a product that has not shown data. [L1335.031]
  • A single operator scaled one winner to roughly 200k USD in revenue after 65 products. Afterwards the brand, run as a custom brand, was still growing day over day and month over month. [L1038.023]

Further details

  • Test new and unconventional ideas if the risk is low and potential loss is small; assess the downside before rejecting ideas, then measure their impact on the brand. [L0260.024]

The store was put up in about 15 minutes, and the lifestyle and emotional ads were mixed and matched to fit the fall aesthetic. The seller's conversion rate was strong, and the product was scaled for a period before the season ended, with a note that it would not sell in summer and would fade as a trend. [L0347.037]
