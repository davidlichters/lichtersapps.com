22.2 Write the 10-year vision and core values

Why the vision comes first

  • A business without a written destination and plan depends on luck to reach its goals. A clear end point gives the owner a roadmap to follow. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0439.002]
  • Core business fundamentals such as direction, vision and core values guide almost every decision and come before marketing tactics. Most highly successful owners work from them in some form, so they should not be skipped. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0439.001]
  • Skipping personal vision and development work because it seems unrelated to running the business is a mistake. Its absence tends to show up later as underperformance once the business is operating. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1256.003]
  • A vision defines the destination, while the current situation is the starting point. It should cover both the personal life and the business, including why the business is being started at all. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1256.004]
  • A clear picture of the end-point goal makes building the roadmap much easier. The picture should describe how both life and business look at that point, since the two become closely linked. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1256.008]

What a vision is

  • A vision is a detailed picture of the business and the owner's life once goals are reached, not just a statement of intent. A personal vision can be written in the same way alongside the business vision. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0439.004]
  • Owners must be able to picture their success and believe they can reach their goals, because goals they do not believe in are rarely reached. This applies at any revenue level, whether the business earns 1,000 or 1,000,000 dollars per month. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0439.005]
  • The vision does not require knowing how the goals will be reached. Describe the picture first, and work out the how later in the planning process. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0439.011, L1256.012]
  • Set ambitious goals that feel slightly daunting, and do not let uncertainty about how to reach them stop the vision work. The question is where you truly want to be in order to feel happy, comfortable, and secure, not how to get there. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1256.012]

Size, ambition and time horizon

  • Use a 10-year time horizon for the vision. Use it for the vision exercise rather than a short one such as six months, because long-term vision comes first and significant results are built over longer periods; shorter-term targets follow from it. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0439.006, L1256.010]
  • There is no right or wrong size for the vision. It can be a very large company or a small business. It should still feel challenging to achieve, because small long-term goals rarely keep an owner pushing day after day. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0439.007]
  • A good vision excites the owner and may feel slightly scary. If the goals do not cause some fear or excitement, they are probably too small. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0439.008]
  • Write ambitious, deliberately audacious goals rather than goals limited by what seems realistic today. [L1041.001]
  • Set a time frame for long-term goals, for example reaching a strong financial position by about age 40 with five years of focused work. Include family and free time in the timeline. [L0832.020]

Writing the vision

  • Write the vision without outside influence and without copying example visions. Put down what the owner actually wants, without second-guessing it. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0439.029]
  • There are no right or wrong answers in a personal vision. The goal can be very ambitious or modest, as long as it reflects what you want, and the document is private and only for you. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1256.013]
  • The Vision on a Page worksheet contains a completed sample vision as a reference for how much detail each area can have. Write your own answers and do not copy the sample. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1256.016]
  • The vision must be written down in the worksheet, not only held in mind, because the written document is used later as the reference for planning. Even a quick first draft counts as a starting point, and you should not wait for a perfect version before moving on. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1256.017]
  • Keep the vision document simple and not overthought. Use a basic Pages or Word document, and write the answers from memory without external examples. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0439.027]

The personal vision alongside the business

  • A personal goal in the vision can be to live solely off passive income and no longer worry about business cash flow. The business is the means to fund this life. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0439.025]
  • The vision should also describe your personal life in 10 years: family, location, living situation, travel and what you do daily. It can be a few short paragraphs or several pages, depending on how much detail you want. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0439.026]
  • The personal life described in a 10-year vision, such as family, location, living situation and daily activities, often motivates more than the business figures do. Treat the business as the vehicle that funds that life, and connect the two explicitly in the vision. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0439.030]
  • The personal life section can be short or long. A few short paragraphs (about five) or a detailed text of up to around three pages is acceptable, depending on how much you want to specify. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0439.031]
  • In the early stage the solo owner is effectively the business, doing the website setup, supplier contact, and daily processes personally. Because of this, life and business become tightly connected, and the vision must address both. A team can be added later. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1256.009]

Business vision document: ten steps

Keep the document simple. Use a basic Pages or Word document. [L0439.027]

  1. Enter the business name, followed by the title "10 year vision". [L0439.027]
  2. Write a quick overview of one or two paragraphs describing what the business looks like in 10 years and how success is measured, such as revenue, customers, lifestyle or side-hustle status. [L0439.027]
  3. Describe the brand: how well known it is, who knows it, what people think of it, how many reviews it has and whether people recommend it unprompted. [L0439.027]
  4. Describe the marketing: which channels are used, where marketing is done and how it performs. [L0439.027]
  5. Describe the finances: revenue, profit, profit margin, who manages them, and what opportunities the profit creates. [L0439.027]
  6. Describe the sales: who sells, how customers and prospects are reached, and how many people are involved in sales. [L0439.027]
  7. Describe the customers: who they are, what they think of the business and what they do for it. [L0439.027]
  8. Describe the operations and team: what the internal team looks like, which roles exist, where staff are based, and who manages the business day to day. [L0439.027]
  9. Describe the personal life and lifestyle: daily life, family, location, travel and personal financial situation. [L0439.027]
  10. List the 3 to 5 core values. [L0439.027, L0439.015]

Vision on a Page: personal areas

  • Picture yourself 10 years ahead and describe what your business and your life look like at that point. [L1256.011]
  • Cover every area: business, finances, place of residence, family, number of children if desired, investments, what you do with business income, health, and significant relationships. [L1256.011]
  • Take one to two days if needed; there is no need to rush. [L1256.011]
  • Write one to two paragraphs for each area on the one-page vision worksheet. Length does not matter, only that every area is filled in. [L1256.011]

Write one to two paragraphs per area. [L1256.014]

Core values

  • Choose 3 to 5 core values. More than five is not recommended, and the values should describe what the business stands for and what it should be known for. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0439.015]
  • Build the business on five core values: take ownership and act on challenges directly instead of making excuses; work relentlessly to be the best at what you do; stay humble and keep improving by tracking marketing changes and sharing what works; treat teammates and customers as equals; and handle every dealing with the highest level of honesty, integrity, and transparency. These values apply at every revenue level, from a first sale to multi-million-dollar annual sales. [L0365.013]
  • Define your values before choosing the audience you want to market to. A niche and a process that drain you are a sign that your values were never defined. [L0701.022]

Daily review and visualisation

  • Review the vision regularly, ideally first thing each morning, and visualize it with eyes closed. This helps on difficult days when doubt, criticism or setbacks occur. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0439.028]
  • Revisit the written goals every morning so they shape the day's decisions. [L1041.001]
  • Define a clear, personally meaningful vision of success before acting. [L1041.001]

Review and revision

  • Treat the vision as a starting point, not a fixed plan. It is expected to change as progress is made, and it should be reviewed at least once a year. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1256.015]
