22.22 Use mentors, coaches, masterminds and peers

Choosing a mentor and a single strategy

  • Pay for mentorship only when you can ask specific, in-depth questions. Generic questions that a quick web search can answer waste the investment. [L0690.038]
  • Focus mentorship questions on what cannot be found by a basic search, because a mentor can share knowledge that is not easily found online. [L0690.039]
  • Pick a single strategy from one mentor and follow it instead of second-guessing among many approaches. This reduces decision fatigue for beginners. [L0690.040]
  • Learn the basics on your own before getting mentorship. Use the mentor to speed up progress, not to replace learning. [L0690.041]
  • Mentorship from an experienced coach is a practical investment for a beginner. It shortens the path through product research, ad testing and payments compared with self-funded trial and error. [L1440.054]
  • A beginner with no experienced person to ask tends to repeat avoidable mistakes and lose money on them. Active community channels where experienced operators answer questions and give feedback shorten that learning curve. [L1492.003]
  • Mentorship gives an exponential advantage. Successful people usually had a mentor, and a mentor helps you avoid repeating known mistakes. [L1041.020]
  • Free YouTube content is enough to learn ads fundamentals and how to read ad logic. Add a mentor or consultation when you hit a specific problem, such as not knowing why ads fail after a first campaign. [L1213.079]
  • Keep improving and keep working after the mentorship ends. Success often comes months after the programme, because results come from steady daily work rather than the course itself. [L1440.055]
  • The paid mentoring period lasts three months. Within it, a beginner should be able to build the store, launch ads and start making sales. If the student is not yet confident running Facebook ads at the end, support continues until they are. [L1017.001]
  • In a mentorship program, you choose which mentor to book for each call. You can book several calls with the same mentor or with different mentors. [L0934.112]

Preparing questions and surveys

  • Write the questions down before each call so you can check during the session that every topic you need is covered. Making notes is the minimum requirement even if you are unsure there will be enough time. [L0364.004]
  • Ask questions that solve a real problem you cannot work out yourself. Do not ask for things you can look up. Better questions speed up progress more than waiting for answers. [L1454.017]
  • If you are unsure whether you are reasoning about a niche or product correctly, ask a coach on a group or one-to-one call rather than deliberating alone. Bring the specific criteria you are unsure about so the coach can check them. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1059.009]
  • If you feel stuck during market selection, ask for help instead of letting the same doubts loop in your head. Asking questions is a normal and expected part of building a business. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1040.006]
  • Once you have a shortlisted niche, use a second opinion from a coach, a team review, or a market verification check. Outside input helps you test how you are thinking about the market. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1040.007]
  • Asking questions in a community is a good way to get a single stuck step solved. Rephrase the question and ask again later if it goes unanswered. [L0855.174]
  • Pre-coaching survey, in order (older, 2021-22):
    1. Find the survey link below the videos in the resources section, or use the copy sent by email. [L1144.001]
    2. Open the link in a web browser. [L1144.001]
    3. Answer the questions one by one. [L1144.001]
    4. Click submit. [L1144.001]
    5. A confirmation message appears, and the answers are sent automatically to the coaching team. No further action is needed after that. [L1144.001]
  • Complete the pre-coaching survey before the first coaching call (older, 2021-22). The coaching team uses the answers to learn about the person and business and to review the website in advance. The survey asks basic questions about the person and the business. [L1144.002]
  • Coaching calls should not be spent on basic background questions, so the survey handles that information before the call. (Older, 2021-22.) Each coaching session is one hour, so preparing in advance lets the time go straight to useful help. [L1144.003]
  • A single coaching session lasts one hour. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1144.004]

Preparing for paid one-to-one sessions

  • Paid one-to-one sessions only pay off when you plan before the call. Without a plan, the session is spent finding data and deciding what to discuss instead of solving problems. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0364.001]
  • Do not book a one-to-one session without a clear topic and a defined outcome you want to achieve. Booking a call just to have a chat gives uncertain value for the money paid. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0364.002]
  • Before each call, the basic pre-consultation survey must be completed after booking. Preparation for every upcoming call follows the same sequence: book, complete the survey, then email the topics and details. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0364.012]
  • Procedure for preparing a one-to-one coaching session (older, 2021-22):
    1. Book the call. [L0364.003]
    2. Complete the pre-consultation survey. [L0364.003]
    3. Decide exactly which topics you want to cover, which questions you want to ask, and what you need help with. [L0364.003]
    4. Write the questions down as notes on paper, a computer or a phone so you can refer to them during the call. [L0364.003]
    5. Decide which supporting information the reviewer cannot already see, using your own judgment and without worrying about getting it perfect. [L0364.003]
    6. Add links to specific website sections you want to discuss, grant access to Google Analytics and Google Ads if paid traffic data must be reviewed, and copy the emails you want reviewed or paste their body text. [L0364.003]
    7. Send one email to the support address listed under the course videos before each call, containing the list of topics and questions plus any attachments or links. [L0364.003]
    8. If you are unsure what material is needed or lack time, send at least the list of topics and questions. [L0364.003]
    9. If the reviewer replies asking for specific material, provide it before the call. [L0364.003]
  • Before each call, email the support address listed under the course videos with your list of topics, questions, and any attachments or links. This is best practice and should take little time. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0364.007]
  • If you lack time or are unsure what material is needed, send at least the list of topics and questions. The reviewer may reply asking for specific material before the call. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0364.008]
  • Sending material in advance lets the reviewer read it before the call, so session time is used for discussion and advice rather than silent reading. Only material the reviewer cannot already access needs to be sent. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0364.006]
  • If there is more to cover than fits in one session, handle the leftover topics after the call. Do not let fear of running out of time stop you from preparing. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0364.005]
  • Every member has an onboarding call with the program host or a team member, booked through a link sent by email. The call clarifies your stage and which content to prioritise, so book it before starting the course. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0545.006]

Support channels and how to get help

Community rules and conduct

  • The community must stay respectful. Avoid comments that an average person or the law would consider inappropriate, and avoid topics such as politics and religion, because the space is meant for building businesses. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1498.013]
  • Breaking any of the four core rules can lead to immediate removal from the program with no refund and no prior warning. The four core rules cover inappropriate conduct, selling services to members, direct messages for sales, and sharing content. The refund exclusion is part of the terms accepted at sign-up. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1498.017]
  • Sharing wins in the group is encouraged, because entrepreneurs often work alone and feel isolated. Celebrate milestones such as building a first website, making a first sale, or reaching a thousandth sale, and support others who are struggling. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1498.018]
  • Asking questions is expected. A question that feels basic is still better than getting stuck. Being active, including joining calls even as a listener, tends to increase results in the program. (Older, 2021-22.) [L1498.019]

Mastermind setup

  • Small peer groups called masterminds keep members accountable, because committing to tasks before a recurring call makes people more likely to complete them. Look in community channels for people on a similar timeline and agree on a fixed call schedule. (Source A 2023.) [L0804.116]
  • Stay connected with other business owners through masterminds, community groups or small peer calls to keep momentum going. If no mastermind exists, start one or post in the community. [L0359.107]
  • To find a mastermind group, search the community forum for mastermind and join any that fit. If none exist, post an introduction stating your stage in the program and that you want to start one. (Source A 2019.) [L1275.057]
  • Mastermind groups work best when members are at about the same stage. Sharing your current progress makes matching easier. (Source A 2019.) [L1275.058]
  • A mastermind group of peers who meet regularly gives a home-based operator the social contact that an office job would otherwise provide. It also creates synergy, since the group's combined progress beats what each member would reach alone. Peers who hit their targets motivate the others, and members hold each other to their goals. [L1000.001]
  • Mastermind meetings need a fixed schedule to stay active. Without a set schedule and responsibility, the group dissolves. [L0832.041]
  • Mastermind format, standard procedure:
    1. Fix a recurring call every two weeks at the same time. [L1000.004]
    2. Give each member 10 to 15 minutes to speak. [L1000.004]
    3. Assign one person to take notes. [L1000.004]
    4. At the next meeting, review the previous notes, check where each member stands, and touch base on progress. [L1000.004]
  • Three questions to structure each mastermind session:
    1. What are you working on right now? Others may learn from it or find a faster method. [L1000.006]
    2. What do you need help with? Members can ask peers privately instead of posting in public forums. [L1000.006]
    3. What can you share that will help everyone else? This forces each member to contribute and creates the synergy effect. [L1000.006]
  • Join or start a small recurring group call with about five peers in the same field, where members share mistakes and wins openly. Learning from the group's collective mistakes shortens the learning curve, and isolation during the early stage is reduced. [L1041.033]
  • Sharing what worked and what did not over the past month builds familiarity in a peer group of established store owners. It tends to draw out others' experiences in return. Posting an open question with your own results first makes it easier for others to respond. (Source A 2024.) [L1115.106]
  • A peer community such as a Discord group is useful for sharing problems, even when others cannot solve them, because being heard reduces stress. Active members, mentors and students alike, often offer solutions. [L1128.020]

Confidentiality and trust

  • Protect confidentiality in a mastermind group by signing a one-page agreement in which every member promises not to share what is discussed with anyone outside the group. [L1000.005]
  • Keep the talks light and useful rather than social chat, so members look forward to each session. [L1000.005]

Finding peers

  • Peer examples in person help make an ambitious goal feel achievable. Conferences, meetups, and casual conversations with people running similar businesses shift what a person believes is possible. [L0863.008]
  • Operators working in different countries and markets can give market-specific answers that a single-region perspective cannot. Forming masterminds and connecting with peers are ways to keep learning and growing a business. [L0710.007]
  • To find a business partner or mastermind partner, post a short introduction in an online community. State who you are, where you are, what you are looking for, and what kind of partner you want. (Source A 2020.) [L0852.124]
  • Celebrating peers' wins in a mastermind raises your own energy and motivation, because it shows that a goal is reachable. Seeing others succeed also pushes you to work harder. [L0832.022]
  • Accountability works better when goals are told to peers, because unmet goals alone are easy to dismiss. Small stakes or wagers within the group can make goal tracking fun and increase follow-through. [L1000.007]
  • Joining a network of entrepreneurs speeds up learning, because peers share mistakes and resources. Reciprocity is required so that value flows both ways. [L1077.044]

Networking and partner outreach

  • Build relationships before you urgently need traffic or sales, because the work only pays off when it has already been done. Starting late is like digging a water source only after you are already in need. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0412.007]
  • Relationship building produces compounding results with consistency. The more regular effort you invest, the stronger the outcomes, while sporadic effort yields weaker results. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0297.007]
  • Introverts or people who find this outreach daunting can still succeed through small, consistent steps. Results usually turn out better than expected once action is taken. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0453.016]
  • Do the well-digging activity consistently rather than worrying about how long it will take. Spend a little time each day, and expect many days to have little to do, because most prospects publish only occasionally. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0453.019]
  • Do not interact with a Dream 100 prospect without a reason. Only comment when they have published something new, such as a weekly blog post or a YouTube video published once a week. This keeps the process manageable and avoids spamming. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0453.020]
  • A Dream 100 list does not have to reach exactly 100 names. The goal is to get as far as you can, and it can be built gradually in small daily sessions alongside other tasks. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0526.001]
  • Daily action for this stage: work through the whole Dream 100 list and follow, subscribe, like, join groups and forums, create forum accounts, and subscribe to email lists for each contact. (Older, 2021-22.) [L0526.011]
  • Partnership action plan (older, 2021-22):
    1. Keep engaging with each Dream 100 prospect's content. [L0525.035]
    2. Once you reach about 10 touch points, or about 5 for periodic publishers, reach out directly. [L0525.035]
    3. Set up calls and have conversations as your time allows. [L0525.035]
    4. Book any advertising opportunities you uncover that your budget can afford. [L0525.035]

Reciprocity and giving first

  • Build a supportive network that holds you accountable. Be valuable to others, and reciprocity returns. Leverage mentorship and community, because you are social by nature. [L0007.013]
  • Build a network of people you both add value to and learn from. Share mistakes and wins within the group so everyone grows faster. Give value first without expecting a direct return, and use the network for accountability by publicly committing to goals. [L1041.019]
  • Reciprocity is required so that value flows both ways in any entrepreneur network. [L1077.044]
