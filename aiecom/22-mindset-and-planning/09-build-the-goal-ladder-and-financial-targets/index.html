22.9 Build the goal ladder and financial targets

Define your goals before you expect results

  • Define your own goal before you expect results, because the time you need depends on it. [L0438.013] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • A side income that supplements a main income is a much smaller target than building a large business with millions in annual revenue. [L0438.013] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • Set a financial goal for the business and a personal goal for life outside it, then use both to guide decisions about the store and how much time to invest:
    1. Set a financial goal for the business.
    2. Set a personal goal for life outside the business.
    3. Use both goals to guide decisions about the store and how much time to invest. [L0345.006]
  • Start thinking about next year's goals before the year begins, and set the yearly plan in advance by breaking long-term goals into steps. [L0472.067]

Write a vision and build the goal ladder backwards

  • Start business planning with a written vision document that describes the business in 10 years. Derive the 3-year and 1-year goals from it and record them in a tracking document. [L0441.002] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • The 3-year and 1-year goals should be checked against the 10-year vision and can change as progress is made. [L0441.002] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • A 10-year vision is a destination. Planning every step to it in detail far in advance is unrealistic because the online business space changes quickly. Break the path into 3-year and 1-year milestones so that strategy and tactics are easier to plan. [L1519.001] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • Build the roadmap with three horizons: a 10-year vision, a 3-year goal and a 1-year goal. Plan the 3-year and 1-year goals in less detail than the 10-year vision. [L1519.002] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • For each horizon (10 years, 3 years, 1 year), define four primary elements:
    • a revenue goal;
    • a profit goal, stated either as a dollar amount or as a margin percentage;
    • the business measurables needed to reach those numbers;
    • a short, one-paragraph description of what the business looks like at that point. [L1519.003] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • Plan backwards from the 10-year vision:
    1. Decide the 3-year revenue goal, the profit goal, the measurables and a short business description, based on where you think you should be. It is acceptable to be experimental.
    2. Split the 10-year revenue target into roughly equal steps. For example, 10 million dollars in 10 years can be reached through 3 million dollars in 3 years if growth is linear.
    3. Repeat the same questions for the 1-year goal, working back from the 3-year goal. Growth does not need to be linear, but the steps help set a realistic path. [L1519.005] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • Filling in the 10-year vision, 3-year and 1-year goals without working backwards from the 10-year target risks setting unrelated numbers. Each shorter-term goal should visibly move toward the longer-term one. [L1519.017] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • Source A sets business goals on four horizons: 10-year, 3-year, 12-month and one-month. Personal life planning runs on a parallel track of 10-year, 3-year and 12-year plans. The source reads "12-year" for the personal track; given the 12-month horizon above, this is most likely a typo for 12-month. [L1437.036] (Source A, undated)
  • Source D uses a 90-day horizon for the main goal and a 12-month horizon for the long-term vision when setting personal targets for an ecommerce business. [L1503.002] (Source D 2026)
  • The sources differ on horizon length. Use the 10/3/1-year ladder for long-range business planning, and the 90-day main goal with a 12-month vision for near-term personal targets. [L1519.002, L1503.002, L1437.036]

Set revenue and profit targets

  • Revenue and profit targets are a starting point and are expected to be revised, because the relationship between revenue and profit is not known at the beginning. Set them early anyway, since they give direction to work backwards from. [L1246.023] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • Define financial targets for the business as follows:
    1. Add to your written vision a specific revenue target and a specific profit target for the store.
    2. If you plan to exit (sell) the business, write down a specific target exit payout, for example 50,000, 100,000, 500,000, 1,000,000 or 5,000,000 dollars.
    3. Research the typical sale multiples that dropshipping businesses are currently sold for, using other websites or community groups.
    4. Work backwards from the target exit payout and the multiple to a required revenue or profit goal.
    5. Estimate how long reaching these goals would take and write down the ideal outcome. [L1246.014] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • A six-figure sum is not necessarily permanent life-changing money, because it may not generate enough income to live from the interest. Set targets high enough to allow lasting financial independence. [L1246.015] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • Source D describes the first milestone as a benchmark: the difference between students who waste six months and students who reach their first 10,000 dollars per month in revenue. [L1452.014] (Source D 2026)
  • Before considering the first roadmap complete, check these end results:
    1. A fully launched branded dropshipping store.
    2. A validated product or system generating consistent sales.
    3. The knowledge to scale beyond 10,000 dollars per month toward six figures. [L0914.020] (Source D 2026)

Make goals specific and measurable

  • Write goals with a specific target date and a specific profit figure. Vague aims such as having a successful store or making a sale cannot be measured and are easy to abandon. [L0826.001] (Source A, undated)
  • Write a concrete income target and a 12-month vision. Vague goals such as wanting more money are hard to track and easy to abandon. [L1503.004] (Source D 2026)
  • Turn a goal into an executable plan in this order:
    1. Set a specific, realistic target date. For a beginner, about two months ahead.
    2. Set a concrete profit target. For a beginner, 3,000 USD profit.
    3. Write the specific actions needed to reach that target, not only the wish.
    4. Break each action into a countable number with a deadline. For example, 5 supplier approvals obtained by calling 10 suppliers.
    5. Set daily or weekly activity targets for each action. For example, calling suppliers every day for two weeks.
    6. Track each count and add accountability, such as sharing progress with someone, so that progress stays visible. [L0826.002] (Source A, undated)
  • Specific steps combined with accountability make a goal more likely to be achieved. The more concrete the actions and the more they are committed to, the higher the chance of success. [L0826.004] (Source A, undated)
  • Set a few measurable business goals at the start of the year, such as a fixed percentage increase in net profit or a monthly profit figure. Write them down with a check-in date. At each check-in, record which changes moved the number and which did not, so the next decision is based on results instead of repetition. [L0807.001] (Source A 2025)
  • Detailed, specific goals are more likely to be reached than vague ones, and they should be visible to both the owner and the team. A short-term target such as 10 sales in 5 days can be reached by the end of a month. [L1437.038] (Source A, undated)
  • Track these metrics and turn them into measurable targets with rewards:

  • Define success for each store upfront, such as a monthly net profit goal over a set timeframe. A store that hits a smaller but steady net profit with minimal daily work can still be a success. [L1111.172] (Source A 2020, older advice)

Sequence growth and expansion

  • Growth should come from keeping the store focused and expanding in steps, not from launching many products or stores at once. Validate each step with traffic and sales before moving to the next. [L1114.064] (Source A 2023)
  • Speed and impatience are different things. Move fast on execution while planning long-term goals in a macro view. A goal set for one to three years can be broken into a three-month plan. [L1038.006] (Source D 2026)
  • When results are steady, growth requires taking larger risks. Enjoying a break is fine, but follow it by setting a bigger goal. [L0826.022] (Source A, undated)
  • Set a clear next goal before starting a new project, such as reaching 5,000 USD profit per month or selling the existing store. This prevents coasting after early success. [L0826.016] (Source A, undated)

Plan monthly and daily actions

  • Break a yearly revenue goal into monthly goals. Track the current month's goal on a checklist, such as a Trello board. Review progress monthly so that you can spot where you are stuck early. [L0808.054] (Source A 2026)
  • Set an internal deadline a few days before your real goal date, so that delays do not push the real goal back. [L0966.100] (Source A 2025)
  • Use the goal tracking horizons below. Source A's tracking structure uses shorter horizons than his business ladder. [L1039.012, L1043.006] (Source A, undated)

Use the goal tracker

  • Fill in the goal tracker in this order:
    1. Write the vision document describing the business in 10 years.
    2. Enter the 10-year picture in the tracker: date, revenue, profit, measurables and a brief overview taken from the vision document.
    3. Enter the 3-year picture, working backwards from the 10-year figures: date, revenue, profit, measurables and a brief overview.
    4. Enter the 1-year plan, working backwards from the 3-year figures: date, revenue, profit, the same measurables and a brief overview.
    5. Review the three horizons together and check that each shorter-term figure clearly moves toward the longer-term one. [L1519.020] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • Fill in the 10-year picture of the tracker:
    1. Enter the date that is 10 years from today.
    2. Enter the revenue target in dollars, for example 10 million dollars per year.
    3. Enter the profit goal either as a dollar amount or as a margin percentage.
    4. Enter the business measurables you expect at that point, such as revenue, profit, customer numbers, cost per acquisition, repeat business rate, average order value and number of products sold.
    5. Paste in the overview of the business from the first paragraph of your vision document. Keep it to a brief overview, not the full plan. [L1519.012] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • Fill in the 3-year picture of the tracker:
    1. Enter the date that is 3 years from today.
    2. Enter the revenue target, for example 3 million dollars.
    3. Enter the profit goal as a dollar amount or margin percentage.
    4. Define the measurables you need to track, making each one specific and clearly defined.
    5. Write a brief overview of what the business looks like at that point, covering the highlights, such as team size. Example: a team of 20 people in one year could mean a team of 10 staff within a few years. [L1519.013] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • Fill in the 1-year plan of the tracker:
    1. Enter the date that is one year from today.
    2. Enter the revenue target for the year, for example 1 million dollars.
    3. Enter the profit goal for the year, for example 300,000 dollars, or a margin percentage.
    4. Use the same measurables as in the 3-year picture, since this is generally easiest, and set the figures for the one-year horizon, for example 1,000 customers per year.
    5. Write a brief overview of what the business should look like one year from now. [L1519.016] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)

Set personal life goals

  • Track progress against lifestyle outcomes, such as reaching a home, a trip or a car, rather than only against profit. Concrete targets motivate far more than a monthly profit figure. Without a known amount and purpose, results tend to be random. [L1039.013] (Source A, undated)
  • The sources frame profit differently. Source A's personal track uses lifestyle targets, while the store-level figures above are profit targets. Use both: the store goal governs business decisions, and the lifestyle goal governs how much time you invest. [L1039.013, L0345.006] (Source A, undated)
  • Stretch goals gradually. Aim somewhat beyond what you currently believe is possible, but not so far that they set you up for failure. Expand your comfort zone one step at a time so the larger goals become reachable over time. [L1039.038] (Source A, undated)
  • Set goals slightly bigger than what seems possible, but realistic enough to be reached. Define the final destination before planning the path toward it. [L1043.005] (Source A, undated)

Review, share and raise goals

  • At the end of each month, sit down with the team and follow these steps:
    1. Review financial goals.
    2. Review operational goals, including return rates and the number of customer complaints.
    3. Set specific, detailed targets for the next period, including small short-term ones. [L1437.037] (Source A, undated)
  • Share the goals tracker with the team as the business grows. Employees who understand and buy into the vision tend to work toward it more effectively. If the revenue and goal figures feel uncomfortable to share, that is usually a sign the goals are ambitious enough. [L1519.010] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • Goals are living documents. Review and update them as the business grows. If a 3-year goal is reached early, raise it, and revise the 10-year vision upward as well. [L1519.007] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • Never hitting a goal is acceptable when goals are continually raised, as long as the chase keeps pushing the business forward. Goals do not have to stay fixed once they are set. [L1519.008] (older advice, Source B 2021-22)
  • Success is not final. After reaching a goal, reevaluate it, set new long-term targets and keep optimizing your procedures. Failure is not permanent either; treat it as a lesson to apply the next time. [L1039.034] (Source A, undated)
