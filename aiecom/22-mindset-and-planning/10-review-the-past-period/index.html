22.10 Review the past period

Timing and frequency

Interval Rule Source
Annual, ideally in January Review the past year before setting goals and a plan for the coming year (older advice, 2021-22). L0800.001
Six-monthly The same review can be repeated every six months (older advice, 2021-22). L0800.001
Quarterly The same review can be repeated every quarter (older advice, 2021-22). A quarterly review is also set when new Rocks are defined. L0800.001, L0441.007
Shortest allowed interval Do not review more often than once a quarter (older advice, 2021-22). L0800.002

Where the sources differ, the annual review is the baseline, and six-monthly or quarterly reviews are optional add-ons. The quarterly minimum applies to every interval. [L0800.001, L0800.002, L0441.007]

  • Set a recurring calendar time for a quarterly review, held when new Rocks are defined, and for an annual review, when the business and its progress toward the yearly goals are assessed. [L0441.007]
  • At the annual review, check whether the 10-year vision and the 3-year goals still fit, revise them if needed, then set the goals for the next year. [L0441.007]
  • After the first 1-year plan, the next plan is a 2-year plan. [L0441.007]
  • Quarterly reviews become more useful as the business grows and more changes happen in shorter periods (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.002]
  • Most growth actions need longer than a quarter to show results. Shorter review periods make it hard to judge what worked, and the time spent reviewing outweighs the benefit (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.002]
  • Start the review as soon as the period closes instead of postponing it to the end of the month. Complete it before you build the plan for the next period (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.004]
  • At the start of your planning period, review your business performance and results from the previous year, or from the months you were in business, to establish a baseline for improvement. [L0223.004]

Preparing the review

  • Use a spreadsheet annual review template, available as an Excel version for Windows or a Numbers version for Mac (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.008]
  • The template has a business half and a personal half. Each has the same seven questions: top goals, goals reached, what prevented goals, biggest weaknesses, unexploited opportunities, achievements you are proud of, and what you would have done differently (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.008]
  • The questions stay the same whatever the review interval (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.008]
  • Answer both the business and the personal section, since they reflect each other. Answers from one section can belong in the other, and that overlap is acceptable (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.009]
  • Limit each list to a maximum of five items, and never give one-word answers. Each line should be a complete statement with enough detail to explain what happened and why (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.013]

Mindset for the review

  • Review the year objectively and without self-blame. Mistakes, failed experiments and missed goals are normal in every business, and the purpose of the review is to find what to change, not to judge yourself (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.005]
  • Learning systematically from past results is a defining trait of successful business owners, whether the previous year went well or poorly. The review turns past experience into the basis for changes in the next period (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.003]
  • Many people never change their situation because they do not analyze what they did and what did not work. Deliberately reviewing actions is what separates operators who improve from those who repeat the same mistakes (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.006]
  • Goals are targets to aim for, not a pass or fail test. Count progress toward a goal as a useful result even when the goal was not fully reached (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.014]
  • A missed goal does not make you a failure. It shows what to keep working on in the coming period (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.021]
  • Achievements can be listed even if you missed all your goals. Recognizing your wins is an important part of the review (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.018]
  • In a small or medium business, the owner's own performance strongly drives business results, so the review must cover both the business and the owner personally. Ignoring personal needs and personal performance puts the business at risk (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.007]

Business section

Procedure, in this order (older advice, 2021-22):

  1. Write up to five top business goals you had for the past year, one answer per line, in complete sentences. Fewer than five is fine. [L0800.011]
  2. List which of these business goals you reached. Leave the answer blank if you reached none. [L0800.011]
  3. For each business goal, write one line on what prevented you from reaching it. Your own assessment may or may not be exactly right; the point is to think about the reason. [L0800.011]
  4. List the biggest weaknesses in the business that stopped you from reaching your top goals. Write one line per weakness, up to five. [L0800.011]
  5. List the unexploited business opportunities you identified during the year, even if they overlap with your weaknesses. [L0800.011]
  6. List the business achievements you are most proud of in the past year. Include progress and firsts even if you missed your goals. [L0800.011]
  7. List what you would have done differently in the business. Aim for as many items as you can think of, and revisit the list over several days. [L0800.011]

Notes on the business section:

  • Examples of achievements worth listing: the first month with revenue above 100,000 in your currency; organic visits growing from about 500 to 2,500 per month; hiring the first virtual assistant; launching a first business; or taking consistent steps toward launching one (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.017]
  • List unexploited opportunities even if you never acted on them. They often reflect your weaknesses, so listing them alongside weaknesses is fine (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.016]

Personal section

Procedure, in this order (older advice, 2021-22):

  1. Write up to five top personal goals for the past year. They can be non-business goals such as family time, fitness, health, or personal money targets, and they do not need to be recorded before. [L0800.012]
  2. List which of these personal goals you reached. Leave the answer blank if you reached none. [L0800.012]
  3. For each personal goal, write one line on what prevented you from reaching it. [L0800.012]
  4. List your biggest personal weaknesses and bad habits, or the areas where you feel you could have performed better in life or in your work. Write up to five. [L0800.012]
  5. List the unexploited personal opportunities you identified during the year. [L0800.012]
  6. List the personal achievements you are most proud of, including anything reached such as weight targets, distance targets or new skills learned. [L0800.012]
  7. List what you would have done differently personally in the past year. [L0800.012]

Notes on the personal section:

  • Valid personal goals to review include spending time with family, fitness and health, starting a business, earning a personal income target from the business, buying a property, or investing in shares (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.020]
  • Personal weaknesses are often the hardest to identify, so give that question extra time. Negative findings in the review point to the opportunities for the coming year (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.022]

What you would have done differently (both sections)

  • For this question, spend more than five minutes and list as many items as you can (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.019]
  • Include tried actions that failed and things you planned but never did (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.019]
  • Do this over several days, and come back after a break to find new items (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.019]

Turning the review into the next plan

  • Use the answers from the review as the input for planning the next year, quarter or six-month period, because the weaknesses and opportunities identified here drive what gets planned (older advice, 2021-22). [L0800.010]
  • Before building the plan, review the year behind you. List opportunities you have not fully exploited and identify your weaknesses (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.007]
  • Use these findings to set goals, and make sure at least one weakness is fixed within the six months (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.007]
  • Knowing your numbers is not enough by itself. Profit gains come only when you act on what the reports show, even on tasks that are not exciting. Set the next year's priorities from the analysis, not from sales targets alone (older advice, 2021-22). [L1046.048]
  • Schedule a reset period, free of operational tasks, to review goals, business plans and new ideas from a high-level view. A virtual assistant who runs day-to-day tasks makes this possible. [L1000.020]

Turning results into routine changes:

  1. After each result, decide whether it was a success or a weakness. [L1039.030]
  2. If an action worked, strengthen it so it becomes part of your routine. [L1039.030]
  3. If you spot a weakness, optimize that part of your routine before the next cycle. [L1039.030]
