22.11 Write the 180-day plan

Why a written plan matters

  • Business goals are rarely reached by luck or disorganized action. A written plan gives daily to-do lists their direction and reduces wasted time, procrastination and lack of motivation (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.003]
  • Write down a business plan before starting, and treat it as a guide rather than a fixed rule. [L0829.018]
  • Following scattered online marketing tips without a structured foundation leads to poor results, because tips do not show what matters first or how channels connect. A structured plan that sets numbers first and campaign types in order works better. [L1203.029]

Planning horizon and timing

  • Plan the next 180 days (about six months) rather than a full year, because a long horizon in an uncertain environment makes forecasts unreliable (older advice, 2021-22). Review the plan at the halfway point and adjust course if conditions have changed significantly (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.001]
  • Plan in January, after the year's review, when a high-ticket dropshipping business is often quieter. This makes it easier to look ahead and check whether the business is on track (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.024]
  • Time is the most valuable resource, so spend it on what makes the most money. Plan the next three months carefully, because they determine the following year. [L0334.054]
  • In a compressed launch plan for a new branded dropshipping store, product research and validation can start in week 2 and overlap with store setup, because the research step can take only a few days. Ads and testing frameworks then begin around weeks 3 to 4, and the total timeline depends on how many hours per week the operator can give the project. [L0914.021]

Setting priorities

  • Limit goals and priorities to a maximum of five, because more than five are unlikely to be pursued seriously. Priorities should be major business changes for the year, not small recurring chores (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.010]
  • Typical six-month priorities include growing organic traffic, setting up an email marketing system, or getting 10 new suppliers approved and products uploaded (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.011]
  • Big business changes such as email marketing, SEO or widening the product range take a long time, so commit to them consistently even when distractions or new opportunities appear (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.005]
  • Outsourcing should be one of the priorities in a 180-day plan, and owners should check whether their plan already includes the tasks they need to hand off (older advice, 2021-22). [L1247.001]
  • If you have fewer than five priorities, fill only the matching Priority task columns and leave the rest empty (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.025]

Task breakdown

  • Example SEO task breakdown for six months: write 12 blog posts, promote those posts, run a link-building method on six of them, rewrite collection descriptions, run an SEO audit and fix duplicate content (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.015]
  • Use the plan as the daily reference: the top priority and its associated tasks become the day's to-do list, so you do not need to decide each morning what matters most (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.017]

Writing the plan

  • Planning the 180 days can take ten minutes or a day or two. Finish it before starting the next task, and do not overthink it: get the goals and tasks onto the page first (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.023]

Planner sheet and tools

  • The planning tool is a Google Sheets pair: a 180-day plan sheet and a goals and rocks tracker holding the quarter tabs. It follows the Course B structure and mirrors a 10-year, 3-year, 1-year and 90-day rocks planner (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.020]
  • The planner sheet has these sections and fields (older advice, 2021-22):
Sheet Fields
BSGR 180 Day Plan Date (plan end and next review date); Revenue; Profit; 180 Day Goals/Priorities (no more than 5); Measurables; Paint The Picture; Priority 1 Tasks to Priority 5 Tasks
90 DAY ROCKS - 1st Quarter 90 Day Goals/Priorities; 90 Day Rocks (up to 7 rocks)
90 DAY ROCKS - 2nd Quarter 90 Day Goals/Priorities; 90 Day Rocks (up to 7 rocks)

[L0447.019]

  • For the second half of the year, duplicate the original planner and change its quarter tabs to third and fourth quarter instead of editing the first-half version (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.022]

Checking earlier plans

  • Before writing a new 180-day plan, check any earlier long-range plan (10-year, 3-year, one-year and 90-day rocks). Copy across the items that still hold and revise the ones that no longer match the business (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.021]

Holding the plan

  • Do not change the plan during its term. It stays fixed until the six-month end date, and only then is a new plan written (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.018]
  • Once a plan is set, follow it through to its end without changing it mid-way, unless a major personal event occurs. Change course only at the scheduled review (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.004]

Review date in the calendar

  • Fix the review date now by scheduling a full day in the calendar roughly six months ahead to review the past period and decide what must change based on national or international conditions (older advice, 2021-22). [L0447.002]

The planning cycle

  1. Plan your business priorities and goals for the next 180 days (six months). [L0223.009]
  2. Schedule a review date on your calendar for approximately six months ahead, around mid-year (June or July). [L0223.009]
  3. On that date, review what you actually accomplished against your plan. [L0223.009]
  4. Use the same planning tool to create your next 180-day plan based on the results and learnings from the previous period. [L0223.009]
