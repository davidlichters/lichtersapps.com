11.8 Replicating, remixing and refreshing viral content

Principles of Replicating and Remixing

  • Start from what already works. Copy the winning angle and format first, and change angles only after the product shows traction and converts well. [L1380.049]
  • Copying a successful competitor's video closely, even frame by frame, can still produce a viral result, which suggests that video structure drives performance more than timing alone. [L1429.043]
  • A viral video with weak sharpness and audio can still succeed on its concept. You can beat it by keeping the concept and improving image sharpness and sound quality. [L0657.046]
  • Reposting other creators' videos is risky because TikTok increasingly detects reused content, and the account can suffer. Recreate the concept with your own footage instead. [L0657.018]
  • Study the top videos of a successful account closely, including frame changes, splits, sounds, transitions, recording style and editing pace. Then recreate the structure with your own product rather than copying the content. [L0657.017]
  • Use competitor accounts in the same niche as inspiration. Borrow the theme or structure, then improve it or change it entirely. [L0764.045]
  • Study a proven viral video from another account and recreate its structure in your own way, using the same type of music, hook and context. Copying the core format while filming your own version is the approach that produced the first sales. [L0821.004]
  • To learn what works on TikTok, spend regular time on the platform looking at which videos go viral and which sounds they use. Then replicate those sounds and formats in your own videos. [L0821.014]

Remixing a Winning Ad or Viral Video

Remixing a Winning Ad

  1. Start from the winning ad's angle and script. [L0341.035]
  2. Open with a big benefit shot, such as a phone that sticks anywhere. [L0341.035]
  3. Find footage that shows each benefit and cut it into short clips. [L0341.035]
  4. Adjust clip audio and volume as needed. [L0341.035]
  5. Add text in a TikTok-style layout using Open Sans, scale it down to fit, and set the text background to white at 100% opacity. [L0341.035]
  • One clip is rarely enough to modify a winning video ad well, so gather a full set of footage before cutting. This prevents a copy that looks exactly like the original. [L0341.038]
  • On TikTok, change the clip roughly every 2-3 seconds when remixing competitor videos, so the video is not flagged as a duplicate. Mixing hooks from several winning videos into one new edit is a common method. [L0637.051]

Replicating a Viral TikTok Video

  • Keep the same filming style and footage approach, but make slight variations of the spoken text or on-screen words, and cut any weak moments (for example, shorten a 10-second video to 8 seconds). [L0501.006]
  • Judge each variation against the original. If it performs worse, work out whether the words, the angle or the length caused it. [L0501.006]
  • Do not repost the same video. Make a new variation of the same style (same filming approach, different wording or cut), and reuse trending sounds that worked on your best video. [L0501.016]

Repurposing Viral Footage into a Facebook Ad

  1. Write a script with ChatGPT based on the footage and the product's problem and benefits. [L0524.052]
  2. Record a voiceover, or generate one with ElevenLabs. [L0524.052]
  3. Cut the footage to match the voiceover and add on-screen text. [L0524.052]
  4. Test the cut as a Facebook ad against a version with a fresh opening. [L0524.052]

Copying a Viral Product's Video Approach

  1. Identify the viral video and its trending sound while the trend is still active. [L0655.014]
  2. Copy the video's format, structure, and sound closely rather than inventing a new angle. [L0655.014]
  3. Ensure your product link is working before publishing. [L0655.014]
  4. Add a clear call to action and urgency to the video text and description. [L0655.014]
  5. Post while the sound is still trending, ideally within days of the viral peak. [L0655.014]

Recreating Your Product Cleanly

  • Learn the structure of successful competitor videos, then rebuild it with your own footage, so the content is yours even when the format is copied. [L0657.017, L0657.018]
  • Remove watermarks and create original footage. Reused clips with watermarks can signal low quality and lead to lower distribution. [L1384.046]

Content Mix and Testing Volume

  • Keep testing new angles constantly, because the same content gets harder to keep relevant over time. [L0501.017]

Trends, Sounds and Hashtags

  • Trend-based videos are easy to produce and are worth doing even if a trend does not last, as long as the product fits naturally into the trend format. An example is listing benefits over a trending sound on a product-style video. [L0655.013]
  • Trends can be used to reach new audiences from the For You Page. Include your product in a current video trend, filter, or trending sound instead of relying only on your own content format. [L1384.029]
  • A trend can be used without showing your face. Put the product into a current video trend, a filter or a trending sound. Keep the product presentation within normal limits and avoid anything too revealing. [L1384.054]

Organic Replication of a Viral Video

  1. Use the same trending sounds, the same hashtags, and a very similar text-to-speech voiceover as the viral video. [L1429.017]
  2. Upload two to three more similar videos after the viral one. [L1429.017]
  3. Leave at least 2 hours between uploads. [L1429.017]
  4. Vary posting times, including unusual hours such as 2 am, so different time zones and regions see the content. [L1429.017]

Reusing Your Own Old Videos (Repurposing)

Eligibility Rules

  • Only repurpose content on an account that has been posting for several weeks or months and already has a large library of videos. Do not repurpose on a new account. [L0795.008]
  • Only consider re-uploading a video that is at least about one month old, and only when it previously performed well. [L0795.009]
  • Use repurposing sparingly. Repeating it too often stops it from working. [L0795.010]

Procedure

To repurpose an existing video:

  1. Take a video that already performed. [L0820.016]
  2. Change the hook or the opening. [L0820.016]
  3. Slightly alter the video file itself. [L0820.016]
  4. Re-upload it so TikTok does not treat it as spam. [L0820.016]

File Differences

  • A re-uploaded video must differ in its file content, for example through a small trim of a second or a changed frame, even if it looks the same to the viewer. [L0795.011]
  • Re-uploading the identical file is risky, and a viewer who has already seen it will swipe past. [L0795.011]
  • Viewers who have already seen a video on a large account may swipe past it when it reappears, which can lower its performance. This is a reason to avoid identical re-uploads even when a restriction is not triggered. [L0795.012]

Changing the Fingerprint

  • To change a video's fingerprint before re-uploading it as an ad, speed it up by 1.01x to 1.05x in a video editor or speed tool. Combined with swapping the hook of one video onto the body of another, this creates variants that TikTok may treat as new videos, which can lower CPM. [L1377.063]

Metadata and Meta Refresh

  • The aim of a meta refresh is to make TikTok treat a re-uploaded ad as new content rather than an old video, so it gets fresh reach. [L1436.030]
  • A metadata-changing method can be used to refresh a copied video so the platform treats it as a new upload. In one test a third-party website was used for this, and the effect lasted only about one day. [L1177.057]
  • The metadata that can be changed for a refresh includes coding, dimensions, length, timing, and playback speed. Changing these helps the platform treat a reused video as a new ad. [L1382.026]

Meta Refresh Procedure

  1. Start with the video at its original 1080 x 1920 (9:16) dimension. [L1382.024, L1436.029]
  2. Change the dimension to 1920 x 1080 (16:9) in an editor such as CapCut, Sony Vegas Pro or Premiere Pro. [L1382.024, L1436.029]
  3. Export the video and re-import it into the editor. [L1382.024, L1436.029]
  4. Change the dimension back to 1080 x 1920 (9:16). [L1382.024, L1436.029]
  5. Change the coding and other metadata of the video, such as speed or timing. [L1382.024, L1436.029]
  6. Export the final video and use it for the new ad. [L1436.029]

Results Benchmark

  • After using the meta refresh method on competitor videos, a CTR of 1.52 percent was reached on TikTok, which is very high. [L1382.025]
  • A CTR of about 0.7 to 0.8 percent is considered good on TikTok. The 1.52 percent is a single test result, and 0.7 to 0.8 percent is the general benchmark. [L1382.025]

Downloaders, Watermark Removal and Editing Tools

  • Snap Tick (snaptick.app) and SnapTik are web tools that download TikTok videos without the TikTok watermark. The clean file can be posted on Instagram Reels or YouTube Shorts without visible TikTok branding. [L0795.032, L1186.039]
  • Use the downloader to get a clean file before re-uploading, since the watermark suppresses reach on other platforms. [L1186.039]
  • To reuse existing TikTok content, download the source video with SnapTik, then edit it into your own custom ad in CapCut, which is also available as a mobile app for editing on the phone. [L1183.023]

Video Replies

  • For a video reply, pick a high-liked comment as the hook. The selected comment appears in the top left corner of the reply video, where it works as a strong hook. [L1429.040]
  • Video replies that respond to a viral video with traction can compound reach, because each reply can ride the momentum of the earlier viral video. The top comments on a new reply then supply topics for the next replies in the same chain. [L1429.054]

Case: Replication That Produced the Breakthrough

  • A dropshipper started about 20 months before the interview, initially doubting that dropshipping could be profitable until a friend began making sales. [L0821.022]
  • He tested 6 to 7 products before finding a hit. [L0821.022]
  • For the hit, he noticed another account getting tens of millions of views within a day, bought one unit on Amazon that arrived the same day, and filmed many videos that copied that account's format, music and hook in his own execution. [L0821.022]
  • He posted 4 to 5 videos per day for two weeks, was about to stop just before the breakthrough, and then gained 1,000 followers in 15 days, then 10,000, and about 20,000 as one video reached roughly 10 to 15 million views and earned about $1,000 within 2 hours. [L0821.022]
  • The same product then kept getting about 4 million views per day for roughly a month. The overall path from the first dropshipping attempt to the win took about a year. [L0821.022]

Procedures by Source Unit

Competitor Analysis to Recreation

  1. Find the largest accounts selling the product. [L1380.048]
  2. Collect their most viral videos. [L1380.048]
  3. Note the hooks, transitions, and formats such as ASMR. [L1380.048]
  4. Remove dull moments and shorten each video. [L1380.048]
  5. Recreate the best videos using your own footage and improve the weak parts. [L1380.048]

Further details

  • Reusing the same video clip across several posts or across a second account hurts reach. Posts with identical clips tend to get very few views, so new videos should use fresh footage. [L0656.002]
