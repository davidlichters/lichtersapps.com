11.21 Content strategy, brand voice and long-Term growth

Content pillars and weekly plans

  • Assign each weekday one pillar so the audience learns when each format appears. [L1457.023]
  • Sample week for a short-video account:
Day Pillar
Monday Behind-the-scenes routine videos [L1457.023]
Tuesday Practical hack videos [L1457.023]
Thursday Advice videos [L1457.023]
Friday Trend videos [L1457.023]
  • The sample assigns no pillar to Wednesday, Saturday or Sunday. [L1457.023]

Evergreen versus trend content

  • Post content that aligns with trending topics regularly to boost visibility, but do not rely only on trends. [L0915.012]
  • Organic short-form content is not a quick result. Test several content types to see which ones work with your followers. [L0915.015]
  • Create evergreen content that can be repurposed across platforms for years. [L0915.015]
  • Video trends on TikTok last about two to three weeks before momentum fades, so change style and angle on that cycle. [L1077.022]
  • A video that gets a million views can stop growing after a single day. [L1077.022]
  • Keep producing new content and new viral angles to keep results coming. [L1077.022]

Brand voice and format

  • Short-form video (TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts) is now the main way brands get pushed on social media. [L0915.001]
  • Static posts do little on these platforms, so brand content should be video showing the product in use. [L0915.001]
  • Brands that win on social media tend not to take themselves too seriously. [L0915.025]
  • Lighter, playful content usually performs better than polished corporate messaging. [L0915.025]

Personal creator accounts and product accounts

  • Keep one rule apart for personal creator accounts and product accounts. [L0819.027]
  • A personal creator account should keep posting consistently. [L0819.027]
  • A product or brand account that is not converting should be abandoned, and the product moved to a new account after a short test. [L0819.027]
  • Follower count matters little, so a product account with 10,000 to 70,000 followers may be dropped without much loss. [L0819.027]
  • Switching an existing account to a different product can cost followers, because followers follow the account's identity and not only the content. [L0657.027]
  • Start a new account when the product or niche changes significantly. [L0657.027]

Follower thresholds, with each applying to a different situation:

Situation Follower count Action Ids
Early phase, first base About 1,000 Pass this mark with an easy angle first L0352.003
Established base About 10,000 Move to higher-converting product angles and keep testing new ones L0352.003
Product account not converting 10,000 to 70,000 May be dropped without much loss L0819.027
  • The 1,000 and 10,000 marks guide an account that is growing and has a base, while the 10,000 to 70,000 drop rule applies only to a product account that is not converting. [L0352.003, L0819.027]

Case: a creator with a personal account showing their own face, not a product account, passed 15,000 followers in about two weeks. After one video went viral, they uploaded several new videos and re-uploaded their best-performing videos as slightly changed versions with a new hook. [L0820.052]

Growth benchmarks from the same case:

  • 1,000 followers within 15 days of the breakthrough. [L0821.007]
  • Then 10,000 and about 20,000 followers as viral videos continued. [L0821.007]
  • Follower growth followed the viral videos rather than steady daily gains. [L0821.007]

Volume benchmarks

Benchmark Value Ids
Successful account with consistent uploads, minimum About 150 to 200 uploaded videos L0820.022
High-volume posting, over one year About 500 videos L0820.022
  • Organic video can keep generating sales after it goes viral, without new uploads, so a large catalog of videos produces a few sales each day. [L0820.021]
  • Keep uploading anyway to keep traffic growing. [L0820.021]
  • After one viral video, do not stop uploading. Continue posting good quality content, because stopping after an organic success wastes the momentum. [L0820.023]

AI-generated faceless content

  • One approach was to create about 60 faceless AI videos and publish them on YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and TikTok to test organic traffic before selling products through them. [L1017.036]

General social media growth principles

  • Judge an influencer's TikTok performance over several posts rather than a single video, because short-form results rise and fall sharply from one video to the next. [L0479.032]
  • A new account gets a broad mix of content, because algorithms do not know what the user wants at first. [L0915.010]
  • Train the feed by interacting deliberately with the content type you want to create, such as watching and engaging with similar videos. [L0915.010]
  • Buying followers is not recommended, because TikTok can detect purchased or foreign accounts, which can lead to shadowbanning. [L0657.026]
  • Purchased followers on Instagram can affect reels at first, but they do not provide lasting reach. [L0657.026]
  • Keep testing new angles constantly, since a good video can still break out at around 10,000 followers. [L0352.003]

Live streaming

  • Consistent live streaming is described as one of the few reliable ways to perform well on TikTok. [L1504.009]
  • Live streaming takes a lot of effort and does not suit every brand, so weigh it against the team's capacity before committing. [L1504.009]
  • TikTok's creator settings side panel separates shortcuts for Live, Subscriptions and Series, giving creators quicker access to those monetization and content formats. [L1504.008]

Branded challenges

  • Branded challenges were a major format in early TikTok, when the Explore page listed brand challenges. This describes early TikTok and is older advice. [L0915.026]
  • A campaign built around a theme, product or hashtag works the same way: creators join it and the brand gains a pool of content. [L0915.026]
