11.19 Instagram reels and trial reels

Account setup and first weeks

  • Create the Instagram account, ideally as a business account. [L1186.030]
  • Reels content is organic. A business account gives access to analytics and the option to boost posts, and a personal account is not required. [L1186.029]
  • New accounts can show zero views for days or weeks before a video takes off. Keep posting consistently rather than abandoning the account. A watermark suppresses only the single video it is on, not the whole account. [L1186.031]
  • Growth on any platform depends on regular video posting, so post Reels consistently. [L0915.002]
  • When starting, follow accounts similar to your own to see what they post and to find inspiration. Use Instagram's suggested-accounts feature (the plus icon next to a followed account) to find more. [L0915.014]

Reach expectations and platform value

  • Reels can deliver more views than TikTok on new accounts. In the owner's experience, conversion was higher on TikTok, possibly because the emotional content connects more with TikTok viewers. [L1077.021]
  • Instagram Reels is useful for brand building and for reaching viewers who need to see credibility before buying. [L1077.021]
  • An Instagram following is valued more than an equal TikTok following. For problem-solving products, Instagram may bring more sales, and going viral there is about as easy using the same concept. [L1429.034]
  • Follower counts are not interchangeable between platforms. Reach comparison:
TikTok followers Approximate Instagram followers (similar reach) Source
about 30,000 5,000 to 10,000 [L1379.001]
100,000 15,000 to 20,000 [L1379.001]

Cross-posting TikTok videos to Reels

  • Instagram can take four to five videos per day, and reposting existing TikTok videos there can produce views. [L0352.044]
  • The routine example uses three per day, reposting each day's TikTok videos on Instagram the next day and keeping the same daily count. The two figures do not conflict: four to five is the reported daily capacity, and three is the example routine. [L1186.030, L0352.044]
  • Videos that did not go viral on TikTok sometimes perform on Instagram. [L0352.044]
  • Cross-posting does not fix a weak angle. In one case, a late cross-post of TikTok content reached only about 500 followers and did not convert sales. [L0764.046]
  • Repurpose strong TikTok videos to Reels. [L1077.021]
  • Re-uploading a clip to a personal account with a trending sound set to zero was not restricted in the reported case, including after hundreds of uploads. Restriction risk is still possible, so check account notices after each new upload type. [L0795.036]

Procedure: moving a TikTok video to Reels with a trending sound

  1. Download the TikTok video without watermark using SnapTik. [L0795.033]
  2. Upload the file to Instagram Reels. [L0795.033]
  3. Open Instagram's audio library and choose a trending sound. [L0795.033]
  4. Add that sound and lower its volume to 0. [L0795.033]
  5. Keep the video's own audio (the original TikTok sound) audible. [L0795.033]
  6. The trending sound is used only to signal the format to Instagram, so it does not need to match the TikTok audio. [L0795.033]

Note on conflicting audio advice: the detailed procedure keeps the original TikTok audio audible under a muted trending sound. [L0795.033] The shorter routine says to keep the video unchanged but replace the original audio with a trending Instagram sound. [L1186.030] Use the detailed procedure when the original audio matters to the video; the sources do not say which applies in other cases.

Caption and reply prompt

  • For Reels, write a short description with trending hashtags, and end it with a prompt that invites a reply, such as asking viewers to comment a number or post a funny answer. [L0795.034]
  • Contest-style prompts such as "funniest comment wins" attract many jokes unrelated to buying intent, so judge comments by quality rather than count. [L0795.034]

Downloading and removing watermarks

  • To reuse your own TikTok videos, save them with a TikTok video downloader such as SnapTik, then upload the files to Instagram. This avoids re-filming content that already exists. [L0352.056]
  • Remove the TikTok watermark before posting to Reels, because watermarked videos get suppressed. In one test, a watermarked repost got almost no views. The same video without the watermark reached a few thousand views, compared with a few hundred for other posts. [L1186.028]
  • Third-party watermarks reduce reel performance. This includes TikTok watermarks and a brand logo placed in a corner, so remove them before uploading. [L0796.010]
  • Posting content you did not create, or without the rights to it, is a serious flag for Instagram. If Instagram suspects it, the account can be taken down immediately. [L0796.016]

Upload quality and format

  • Upload videos in the highest resolution available, because Instagram can compress uploaded files. Edits is recommended for this. [L0796.005]
  • InShot is a mobile video editor that can be used to export reels. Export at 1080 resolution where possible. [L0796.006]
  • Instagram favors high-quality video, so low-quality videos get less distribution. Film and edit with quality in mind. [L0796.015]
  • Reels longer than 3 minutes hurt reel performance. Longer reels can still work below that limit. [L0796.014]
  • Test several formats before you know what works for your page, such as a reel of about 2.5 minutes, shorter reels and carousel posts. Let the results guide which formats you keep. [L0796.025]
  • When publishing a carousel, add a song, because carousels with music can also appear in the Reels tab. This gives the carousel an extra discovery path. [L0796.026]

Edits app

Recording and editing

  • Record in the Edits app only for footage you can easily reshoot. For time-sensitive or non-repeatable shots, film with the phone's regular camera, because a long in-app recording can be lost if the phone overheats or crashes. [L1453.010]
  • The Edits camera has a green screen function, a timer, and speed settings, along with the usual camera controls. [L1453.016]
  • Timeline layers: Original audio, video clips, Text, Captions, Overlay, Sound FX, and Cutout each get their own timeline track. Each track can be toggled on or off to preview the video without that element. [L1453.018]
  • Basic clip editing: drag the clip edges to trim, and use slip to shift the visible portion within a clip without changing its length. [L1453.019]
  • Overlay adds a second clip on top of the main video, like picture-in-picture. Add it from the Overlay option on the timeline. [L1453.020]
  • Filters are the same as Instagram Story filters: None, Simple, Boost, Oslo, Paris, and Jakarta. The "Apply filter to all clips" option applies the selected filter to every clip in the project. [L1453.022]
  • Edits has AI tools that restyle or reimagine a video using its editing features. An "animate" AI feature was mentioned as newly released, but its location may have changed, so check the current menu before planning around it. [L1453.021]
  • The Inspiration tab is a Reels-style feed that mixes content popular with your followers and audio that is currently trending. It offers no like, comment, follow, or send actions, so use it only as a browsing source for ideas. [L1453.026]

Export and sharing

  1. Finish the edit on the timeline. [L1453.023]
  2. Tap Export. [L1453.023]
  3. Keep the app open and the screen unlocked until the export finishes. [L1453.023]
  4. The exported video is saved to the camera roll automatically. [L1453.023]
  5. Tap Share to Instagram and choose where to share the video next. [L1453.023]

Edits watermark and share button

  • Videos shared directly from Edits to Instagram, instead of uploaded from the camera roll, carry a glowing "made with edits" watermark. This is claimed to help distribution, but no hard evidence was given, so treat it as a hypothesis to test. [L1453.003]
  • Compare the reach of Edits-shared videos against camera-roll uploads before relying on the claim. [L1453.025]
  • Instagram tends to push its newest in-app features, so adopting a new feature early can give a video extra distribution. The boost from posting through Edits is not proven. [L1453.025]
  • Videos shared from Edits are optimized for high-quality playback on Instagram. [L1453.024]
  • The direct share button with the glowing "made with edits" icon is being phased out and may already be unavailable on some accounts, so use it while it is still offered. [L1453.024]

Trial Reels: principle

  • Instagram Trial Reels shows a short video only to non-followers and keeps it off the profile grid at first, so existing followers never see it. It is useful for testing content styles outside your usual niche without risking your main audience. [L1397.001]
  • According to the source, a Trial Reel is shown 100% to non-followers and 0% to existing followers, so every upload reaches people who have not seen the account before. Brands that struggle to reach non-followers can use this to get a guaranteed share of new viewers. [L1397.002]
  • Re-uploading the same proven video as a new Trial Reel gives it a fresh non-follower audience each time. The main feed does not reveal that the same video was posted repeatedly. [L1397.003]
  • Some creators repost a top video up to 10 times per day. [L1397.003]
  • A Trial Reel can stay in trial without ever being shared to the profile, which is optional. You share it to the main feed later only if it performs well. [L1397.004]
  • Because a Trial Reel is a new feature, it usually gets an early visibility advantage. This boost is not guaranteed to last, since new feature pushes come and go. [L1397.005]

Trial Reels: upload steps

  1. Open the new reel upload flow and select the video. [L1397.007]
  2. Add the caption and the usual settings (tags, location, audio, AI label if needed). [L1397.007]
  3. Under Audience, select Trial. [L1397.007]
  4. Leave the option to automatically share the reel to everyone in trial settings unchecked. [L1397.007]
  5. Under Post to, uncheck the cross-post options for Threads and Facebook (and any other destination listed there). When the dialog "Stop sharing on Threads?" appears, choose "Don't share this reel" so only this reel is affected, not all reels. [L1397.007]
  6. Tap Save draft or Share. [L1397.007]

Trial Reels: settings

  • Audience > Trial: In feed and Reels, the reel is shown only to non-followers at first and does not appear on the profile. If it performs well, the reel can be shared to everyone later. [L1397.008]
  • Trial settings > "Share to everyone" checkbox (automatic sharing): Keep it unchecked when you post the same video repeatedly, otherwise the reel is automatically added to your profile when it performs well. [L1397.009]
  • Post to: The Threads (Public), Facebook (Public) and Your story options are listed next to the account name. Any destination left checked receives the same Trial Reel with every upload, so uncheck them for repeated posts. [L1397.010]
  • Add AI Label: Instagram requires a label on certain realistic content made with AI, so set it during upload when it applies. [L1397.011]
  • "Stop sharing on Threads?" prompt: When it appears while you publish a Reel, choose "Don't share this reel" to stop cross-posting for that one upload only. Avoid "Stop sharing all reels," because it applies to every reel on the account. [L1397.022]

Trial Reels: mistakes to avoid

  • Leaving the Threads and Facebook cross-post boxes checked means every repeat upload of the same video also goes to those other accounts. Uncheck them, or choose "Don't share this reel," before publishing. [L1397.012]
  • Turning on automatic sharing to the profile while re-posting the same video makes the main feed show repeated copies. Keep that option off for repeat uploads. [L1397.013]

Trial Reels: starter routine

  1. List your top-performing videos from the main feed. [L1397.021]
  2. Re-upload one of them as a Trial Reel each day, starting with one per day. [L1397.021]
  3. Leave the reel in trial, and do not share it to the profile unless its insights beat your typical reel. [L1397.021]
  4. Check the Trial Reel insights after several days, then compare them with your typical reel. [L1397.021]

Note on upload frequency: some creators repost a top video up to 10 times per day, while the starter routine begins with one per day. [L1397.003, L1397.021]

Engagement drivers

Early replies and conversation

  • Replying to comments during the first few days after a reel is posted is one of the strongest drivers of reel distribution on Instagram. Follow-up replies help further. [L0796.001]
  • Steps to engage with a new reel in its first days:
    1. Right after posting, open the notifications section. [L0796.002]
    2. Filter the notifications by comments. [L0796.002]
    3. Reply to every comment you can. [L0796.002]
    4. When a commenter responds with another question, keep answering so the dialogue stays open. [L0796.002]
    5. Keep addressing new comments throughout the first few days. [L0796.002]
  • The core Instagram recommendations combine early engagement, including replies to comments and DMs, with timing and quality. [L0796.027]

Timing and consistency

  • After finding the hour when your audience is most active, post at that same time or very close to it on every upload. Consistency in timing is part of the recommendation. [L0796.004]

Commenting on other creators

  • Commenting on other creators' content is rewarded by the algorithm. A relevant or funny comment on a larger account's post can send significant traffic to your own page. [L0796.020]

Engagement groups and trends

  • Engagement groups with other creators, where members support each other's content, are a legitimate way to get early engagement. They do not replace genuine engagement with your audience. [L0796.012]
  • Trending audio and pop culture formats are worth using when they fit your brand, because they often drive short-term viral reach. Do not let trends take over the whole posting plan. [L0796.022]

"More like this" and "less like this"

  • On Instagram Reels, tap "more like this" on a video that matches the content you want more of, or "less like this" on one you don't. Each tap adjusts your personal algorithm. [L1504.027]
  • The commentary described these buttons as coming soon, so check whether they appear in your app before planning around them. [L1504.027]

Threads and Facebook cross-posting

  • Turn on the cross-posting option for Threads and Facebook Reels inside Instagram before publishing a reel, so the same post goes to those platforms as well. [L0796.007]
  • Publish the same video with the same caption on all platforms at the same time. Budget no more than 10 minutes for this upload work. [L0796.008]
  • Enable the Threads option on Instagram, and every reel, carousel or photo you post is automatically shared to Threads as well. [L0796.017]
  • The Threads audience differs from the Instagram audience, so posting there adds reach and gives a place for two-way conversation. Treat it as an additional channel, not a copy-only destination. [L0796.019]
  • Threads-to-Reels growth checklist, from the lesson's slide, which is titled as nine strategies:
    1. Post regularly; cross-posting is okay. [L0796.018]
    2. Engage like it is a conversation. [L0796.018]
    3. Comment on other people's content. [L0796.018]
    4. Jump on trending audio and pop culture. [L0796.018]
    5. Use niche-relevant hashtags. [L0796.018]
    6. Experiment with formats. [L0796.018]
    • These points apply to both Threads and Reels. [L0796.018]

  • Instagram Reels can deliver more views than TikTok, but conversion on TikTok was higher in the owner's experience. Reels is useful for brand building and for buyers who need credibility first. [L1077.021]
